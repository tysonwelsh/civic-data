STUDY MEETING

    A. Welcome, Roll Call and Introduction: By Mayor Dawn R. Ramsey

Mayor Ramsey welcomed everyone present. She excused Council Member Shelton. She
introduced the electronic meeting.

    B. Invocation: By Council Member Brad Marlor

Council Member Marlor offered the invocation.

    C. Mayor and Council Coordination

Mayor Ramsey said the ULCT Board is working with the state and county on a third round of
Cares Act funding. The state will be distributing money. In the past 2 rounds, Salt Lake County
and Utah County distributed funds to the municipalities in their counties and the state distributed
to the rest of the municipalities. Salt Lake County is not on board with that plan this time. They
would like to use the money to put together an economic recovery package. She said they are
working with staff, legislative leadership, and Salt Lake County leadership on what the Cares
Act money can be spent on. She said they will continue to work on the issue, and she will keep
the Council informed.

City Attorney Loose said the money is distributed federally, per person. They do not think that
communities in Salt Lake County should be left out.

Mayor Ramsey noted that Salt Lake County has no legal obligation to share the money. She
reiterated that the state would like the third distribution to be done like the other two, but that is

    D. Discussion/Review of Regular Council Meeting

The Council briefly discussed Action Item G. on the agenda. CM Whatcott said the state officials
said their water conservation plan is one of the best that they have received, and they wish it could
be a model for other communities in the state.

CM Whatcott noted that item G. on the work session agenda is an update from Daybreak, including
home sales this year and an update on what is happening there.

    E. Staff Item: Budget Review (By CFO, Sunil Naidu)

CFO Naidu reviewed a prepared presentation (Attachment A).

Council Member Harris said he was surprised at the figures for restaurants. CFO Naidu said
restaurants were able to adjust how they provided their services to customers. He said South
Jordan has a lot of fast food restaurants, and that may be why it continued to do better.

Council Member Marlor asked if they have an idea how many businesses went under this year
due to Covid? City Commerce Director Preece said they don’t know of any that went directly out
of business, but some are in trouble. He also knows of some businesses that received Cares relief
funding.

Council Member Harris asked if the city was getting their online sales money? Mr. Naidu said
the city has been receiving their portion; those taxes flow through the sales tax commission. CM
Whatcott said the distribution is not as equal as if they bought locally.

CM Whatcott said the restaurants with drive-up, curb side pickup, or delivery services have done
well. South Jordan does not have a lot of high end sit down restaurants. Council Member Zander
said Zao in South Jordan is doing the best in the state for that chain. Their sales have increased.
It was noted that ma and pa type restaurants and those that are dependent on the office crowds
are struggling more. Chain restaurants are doing better.

Council Member Harris asked about the sales tax budget vs the actual numbers. Mr. Naidu said
they did not spend their full expenditures and they were able to generate enough revenue.

Council Member Harris asked for the overall impact of Covid to the health of the city. Mr. Naidu
said they did not see an impact. They are monitoring the new fiscal year; July’s numbers were
normal. They hope it continues.

Council Member Harris said it is remarkable that the city has been able to weather this so well.
Mayor Ramsey concurred. She said they have had challenges, but things are going well. The
businesses are faring better than anticipated.

Council Member Zander said the residents have been blessed generally financially. When they
were told to eat out and support local businesses, they took it seriously. There was a concerted
effort to support those businesses.

Council Member Marlor said staff also budgeted conservatively and that also contributed.

CM Whatcott said the employees have picked up extra loads when all of the jobs were not filled.
He concurred that when people shop locally, it helps the community.

City Commerce Director Preece said they are blessed with a diversified portfolio of businesses.
That has helped them through several downturns.

Mayor Ramsey also recognized the hard work of the South Jordan Chamber of Commerce.

Council Member Marlor asked how other cities are faring? CFO Naidu said he has not seen
numbers, but has heard other communities complaining about being short on money and looking
for ways to get more money. He said growth cities such as Lehi are faring well. Mayor Ramsey
said resort communities like Park City and Moab have been hit hard. St. George has also
experienced a shortfall.

CM Whatcott said South Jordan’s high median income also helps. Mayor Ramsey concurred.

    F. Discussion Item: Redeveloping the Draper Prison Site. (By Point of Mountain State Land
       Authority, Alan Matheson)

Mayor Ramsey introduced Alan Matheson, Executive Director of the Point of Mountain State
Land Authority. He said they have 700 acres, and this is a great development opportunity. He
said this is one of the top development opportunities in the country. They want to make sure they
do it right. He said this is a statewide project, and is intended to benefit the entire state. He said
they had a press conference a couple of weeks ago. They are transitioning from the vision phase
to planning phase. They have hired a planning director and planning firm. They have 5 working
groups looking at various development aspects for this project. They have established 6 guiding
principles of the development 1– Promote the public good 2-Set the standard 3- Think regionally
4-Take the long view 5-Be open and transparent 6- Act with integrity.

Mr. Matheson noted a survey that is available for the development. He said they are anxious to
get going and to do this right. Mayor Ramsey said the survey is open through October 16th. They
want to make sure the land will be beneficial to the residents.

Mr. Matheson said all 700 acres are part of the city of Draper. He outlined when their board and
working group meetings are held. Mayor Ramsey said the meeting schedule is outlined on the
website.

Council Member Zander asked that they get an update on this project in the early part of next
year.

CM Whatcott asked how long the planning phase will last. When do they anticipate
construction? Mr. Matheson said prisoners are scheduled to be moved in the middle of 2022. He
said they want the master plan done July 2021. He said this will be a phased project.

    G. Presentation: Daybreak Annual Update. (By Rulon Dutson)

Rulon Dutson, Daybreak Representative, introduced Cameron Jackson, Director of Marketing in
Daybreak. Mr. Jackson reviewed a prepared presentation (Attachment B). He thanked the city for
keeping their business moving. It has had huge implications for them.

Mr. Jackson indicated that they are starting to see residents of Daybreak bring business to the
area. He noted that lack of housing drives the price of housing up. They are looking for
opportunities for smaller, more affordable homes.

Council Member McGuire asked for an update on the north shore commercial/condominium
project, and an update on the County library and the commercial center along Mt. View Corridor.

Mr. Jackson said the north shore commercial project is underway currently. The Harmons and
the first housing is planned to open Fall 2021. He said the library is also under way and
scheduled to open late next year. He said regarding downtown Daybreak, housing is demand
driven. Commercial development is dependent on third parties. He said they are trying to create a
new urban center with downtown Daybreak. He said commercial development looks at rooftops
so that growth also contributes.

Mr. Dutson said support from the city is helpful in situations like attracting the Salt Lake County
library. Also engaging in conversations with UTA regarding trax stations.

Council Member Zander said a few weeks ago, Council members, staff, and employees from
around the state took a bike tour through Daybreak as part of the ULCT conference. The
Daybreak bike paths were praised on that tour. She noted that on the northeast corner of the lake,
there is a spot on the trail where the sidewalk goes from wide to narrow. She said she hopes they
will widen that trail to make it more manageable for bikes and pedestrians for the entire
perimeter of the lake.

Council Member Zander has been asked by some residents about delays in Daybreak’s building
approvals. Mr. Jackson said their work has doubled because of the increase in home sales. They
have not added additional staff because they are not sure they will continue growing at the same
rate. He said the builders are also strapped because they are selling so many homes. The builders
are submitting plans that are not complete or something was overlooked, such as a setback.

Council Member Zander expressed an interest in getting the cell towers built in Daybreak. She
said anything they can do to encourage the cell phone providers because the coverage is
horrible.

issue as far down the road as they can. Until the demand is there, the cell phone carriers won’t
make the investment.

Mayor Ramsey clarified that the schools are not funded by the legislature. The School District is
required to bond for needed schools.

ADJOURNMENT

Council Member Zander made a motion to adjourn. Council Member McGuire seconded
the motion. The vote was 4-0 in favor.
