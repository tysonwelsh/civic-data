13   Herriman City Council Member, Sherrie Ohrn thanked the Council for inviting her to make a

16   they and the public are aware of a tax hearing they can attend. The public tax hearing will take
17   place August 9, 2023, at 6:00 p.m. at the JVWCD Offices located at 8215 South 1300 West in
18   West Jordan. The proposed tax increase this year is 9.3%. For homes valued at $700,000, the

27   improvements to the facility and there are new storage reservoirs and aqueducts. Large seismic
28   improvement projects are underway because much of the infrastructure was built in the 1970s
29   before seismic requirements were established. These investments are important for maintaining
30   water in case of an earthquake.

 1          2.3     Resolution 2023-38 – Adopting a Final Operating Budget for the 2023-2024
 2                  Fiscal Year Budget for the Fire and Police Protection Fund. (Staff Presenters,
 3                  Bruce Kartchner and Stephanie Thayer).
 4
 5   Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner, reported that the Truth in Taxation Hearing is
 6   a step in a budgetary process that is only required when there is a proposed tax increase and is
 7   mandated by State law. It is done after the budget has been discussed for a number of months, the
 8   numbers adjusted, and a Tentative Budget passed. The hope was that it would be passed as a final
 9   budget tonight. All of the numbers have been discussed at great length including the proposed tax
10   increase. The hearing is specifically for a Certified Tax Rate for a New Fire and Police Protection
11   Fund that is not part of the General Fund.
12
13   Mr. Kartchner reported that last year the City Council approved a tax increase in the General Fund
14   because there were specific needs consisting of fire improvements in fire services as well as
15   additional costs that were incurred due to a specific, above average, increase in the Police contract
16   that was entered into the previous October. Last year, the tax increase was approved by the City
17   Council. When it was discussed at public hearings, many residents specifically stated that they did
18   not support the tax increase in the General Fund because they could not guarantee that it would be
19   used for Fire and Police Services.
20
21   Once money goes into the General Fund, it becomes fungible and you cannot specify one dollar
22   from another. As staff reviewed what other cities are doing, one of the mechanisms created by
23   State law was that a specific fund could be created within the City that property taxes could be
24   collected for. The law specifies that any taxes collected in the fund can only be spent for the
25   specific services identified. In this case, they can only be used for Fire and Police services.
26
27   Mr. Kartchner reported that there is an ongoing staffing problem in the Fire Department where
28   firefighters are called back to their home agencies and have to work when they are scheduled to
29   work in Bluffdale. During COVID, firefighters were taken out of service after being exposed and
30   forced to quarantine. Issues also occur around holidays when there are staffing shortfalls. When

19   Mr. Kartchner next addressed the question many have regarding why taxes are increasing. He
20   explained that the State of Utah backs into the rate and many feel that when the value of their home
21   increases, the City receives more taxes as a result. Because of the way the State calculates property
22   taxes, that does not happen. The State of Utah specifically has a law that specifies that local
23   governments will not get a windfall of revenue as a result of the revaluation or equalization of
24   property taxes. He explained that when property values rise, property tax rates must fall. For the
25   City as a whole, property value increases do not change the amount received. The only increase
26   the City receives each year is on growth or estimated growth, which is calculated every year.
27
28   Mr. Kartchner reported that the rate does not change relative to inflation but relative to values. For
29   example, when looking at the 2022 tax dollars paid for one specific property, $534.40 was paid.

Recent Notable Case Highlights

Child Abuse and Domestic Violence - In May Detectives arrested an adult male for felony child abuse, domestic
violence, and other offenses associated with three different cases. The suspect was currently on parole at the time of the
incident and is currently back in custody due to this case. The victims and suspect were living at the Bluffs apartment
complex at the time of this incident.

Marijuana Distribution Case - In June Bluffdale Patrol Officers responded to a domestic violence call on McKenna
Road. A suspect was arrested for a domestic violence assault and subsequent to that patrol officers noticed drugs in a
duffle bag the suspect had in his possession. A search warrant was secured to search the residence. The search
revealed an elaborate drug distribution operation in the basement that included several high tech weighing, labeling, and
drying machines used in the distribution of marijuana and psilocybin mushrooms. Approximately 80 pounds of marijuana,
several hundred marijuana gummies, several ounces of mushrooms, a handgun, packaging materials, and the machines
were seized. The street value of the illicit drugs was approximated to be about $500,000 dollars. Felony charges
included: Possession of marijuana and psilocybin mushrooms w/Intent to distribute.

Sex Trafficking – Illegal Narcotics Distribution Case - In July the Police Department served a search warrant on
Early Light Way. This was the result of an on-going investigation of sexual trafficking and the distribution of
narcotics. Two of the trafficking victims are juveniles. The suspect was arrested and is being held in jail without bail. A
search of the residence revealed evidence of sex trafficking and illegal narcotics distribution. A second suspect was
subsequently arrested on a traffic stop and charged with possession of a firearm by a restricted person, being a co-
conspirator of aiding and abetting prostitution, and possession with intent to distribute cocaine and marijuana. Both
suspects have a criminal history and have both served time in federal prison.

Notable Case Highlights

Child Pornography Case – In July Bluffdale Detectives with the assistant of the Internet Crimes Against Children
(ICAC) Task Force served a search warrant in Bluffdale. As a result of the investigation and search one adult male was
arrested for possession of multiple child pornography videos/pictures.

Child Endangerment/Drugs Case - In August Officers and Detectives investigated a case of possible child neglect. A
nine-year-old boy was left unattended at a local park for six hours. A third party contacted the boy and learned of some
possible drug use and environmental neglect at the boy’s home. Officers responded. The boy disclosed that drugs were
being consumed in the home and several neglect/child abuse issues existed. A search warrant was obtained and
methamphetamine, heroin, fentanyl, and drug paraphernalia were located. Three adults were arrested and booked on
numerous drug and child abuse related charges. The home was later closed to occupancy by the health department due
to the methamphetamine exposure and the tenants were evicted. The young boy was placed into foster care by DCFS.

Felony Fraud Case – In August Detectives were contacted by a Bluffdale construction company who reported possible
fraud within their accounting books. Detectives were able to prove it was the company bookkeeper and uncovered a total
of $53,518.50 dollars that had been embezzled. The suspect was interviewed and admitted to the fraud. The suspect
was booked on 11 felony charges.

Bank Fraud Case - In August a fraud ring was committing check fraud crimes at area Goldenwest Banks. On August 3rd,
the suspects attempted to commit additional check fraud crimes at the Bluffdale branch location when bank employees
identified them from previous alerts. Bluffdale Officers were notified of the fraud in progress and responded to the
scene. The suspects had left the scene and were subsequently pulled over by Riverton Officers in coordination with our
officers. Bluffdale Officers responded to the bank and through video and other evidence determined the suspects
stopped by Riverton PD were the suspects responsible for the fraud and several previous frauds at other Salt Lake
valley locations. In addition, it was found that the suspects were felony illegal re-entries from Mexico. Immigration and
Customs Enforcement was contacted and placed a federal hold on the suspects. Felony charges included: Producing
False Identification, Forgery and Possession of Financial Transaction Cards (4 counts).

AN ORDINANCE AMENDING SECTION 5.10 OF THE BLUFFDALE CITY CODE
RELATED TO PUBLIC SAFETY CRIMES AND OFFENSES


WHEREAS the City of Bluffdale maintains criminal ordinances not covered by state code;
WHEREAS the City desires to protect the health and welfare of the community;
WHEREAS the City desires to add an ordinance to address the situation of individuals
interfering with employees performing public duties;
WHEREAS the City Council finds that the proposed amendment will further the public health,
safety, and general welfare;


NOW, THEREFORE, THE BLUFFDALE CITY COUNCIL HEREBY ORDAINS AS
FOLLOWS:

     SECTION 1. Adoption of Bluffdale City Code. Chapter 5.10.080: INTERFERENCE
WITH AN EMPLOYEE is adopted as follows:

…

5.10.080: INTERFERENCE WITH AN EMPLOYEE

A person commits interference with an employee when the person knows, or by the exercise of
reasonable care should have known, that the employee is performing public duties and the person
commits a physical act, or refuses to refrain from committing a physical act, that hinders, obstructs,
impedes, or prevents the employee’s performance. Interference with an employee is a class “B”
misdemeanor.

Employee” means an employee or volunteer, whether through a public entity or a private entity
contracted by a public entity.

        A.      The Provider desires to establish and provide a telecommunications network in
the City’s right-of-way (“City ROW”) primarily through fiber-optic lines.

in Utah Code § 10-1-401 et seq. (the “Act”), and the Provider is subject to the City’s

interest of the public to grant the Provider a nonexclusive franchise to operate a
telecommunications network in the Bluffdale City limits.

RECOMMENDATION:
To approve the proposed resolution 2023-40 which allows the City Manager to sign a proposed real estate
exchange agreement between the City of Bluffdale and CC&L Enterprises, LLC.

BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY :
Over the past several years, the City has acquired property adjacent to Redwood Road, south of Bangerter and
north of approximately 13900 South in order to facilitate economic development and obtain right of way for a
planned modified collector road which will connect 14000 South to 13800 South. Through various
transactions, the City has acquired all parcels north of the Maverik convenience store except for two parcels:
one privately held by CC&L Enterprises, LLC, adjacent to Maverik and a UDOT parcel near the Bangerter off
ramp. To facilitate lot and parcel configurations conducive to economic development and to design the
collector road, the City entered into conversations and negotiations with CCL. The CCL piece is key to creating
a marketable retail or restaurant parcel with the only access off Redwood Road north of Maverik. Thus, CCL
and the City have agreed to the proposed exchange.

The exchange of property will allow the City to retain and market all of the land north of Maverik adjacent to
Redwood Road together. The City will relinquish its interests in Lots 2, 3, and 4 of the proposed 1780 West
subdivision in exchange for control of the Redwood properties owned by CCL. Approximately 1.67 acres of
City parcel are being exchanged for approximately 0.83 acres of CCL property. The proposal is an even
exchange due to the much higher value of the frontage parcels with state highway access versus the locations
of parcels across the new 1780 West road with no Redwood Road frontage. If the agreement is approved, the
City Council will need to proceed with a public hearing and a declaration of surplus of significant real property
as required in Bluffdale City Code before executing the real estate transaction.

PLANNING COMMISSION RECOMMENDATION:
To approve the proposed ordinance amendments to the Bluffdale City Zoning Map, rezoning certain
properties from General Commercial (GC-1) to Destination Retail (DR) and to approve proposed text
amendments to the Destination Retail zone, as recommended by the Planning Commission on July 19, 2023,
based on the following findings and recommendations:

For the rezone:

    1. That the proposed rezone is consistent with the General Plan.
    2. That the property is already zoned GC-1.
    3. That the rezone creates uniformity in design and development expectations in the area.

For the text amendments:
    1. That the DR is intended to more specifically tailor commercial development in the Bangerter/Redwood
        area.
    2. That the DR zone updates will provide more consistency in design.
    3. That the DR zone updates are consistent with the General Plan.

Staff recommends that both ordinances be discussed in one combined public hearing, then acted upon
separately.

BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY :
Over the past several years, beginning with the rezonings to GC-1 for the Maverik convenience store, the City
has been implementing its General Plan for commercial development near the Bangerter and Redwood
intersection. After the initial Maverik rezoning, property to the north of Maverik was also zoned GC-1 and the
City acquired some of it. Additionally, the City created the Destination Retail zone further north to the
Bangerter Highway, and then acquired surplus property from the Utah Department of Transportation which
had already been zoned DR. During the same time, on the east side of Redwood Road, the Woodbury
Corporation has been developing the Ranch. All in all, this area has become a burgeoning commercial
development area. The proposed land use ordinance changes are intended to consolidate zoning provisions
into the DR zone and to update the standards to correlate with the current development activity and interest
in the area.

The City owns all of the property north of Maverik, except for one strip of land with the last remaining home
along Redwood road in this area. The City has been in discussions and negotiations with this owner who has
agreed to participate in these zone changes in conjunction with property exchange agreements with the City.
This exchange is a separate action from the City Council but is essential in creating developable subdivision
lots; the City has prepared a preliminary subdivision and road plat. The exchanges also allow the planned
collector road of 1780 West to be completed from its current terminus on the NW corner of Maverik to connect
with 13800 South near the Bangerter Highway sound wall.

11.90.060.010 Purpose And Zone Characteristics

This district is composed of certain land with excellent transportation access and locations
identified with a primarily retail focus. Allowed uses are limited to general retail,
restaurant, lodging, office, service uses, and indoor recreation and entertainment. The
intent is to create and support commercial retail activity in specific areas without more
intensive heavy commercial, manufacturing, contracting, warehousing, distribution, open
outside storage, and similar uses allowed. High quality commercial design standards are
required to increase overall attractiveness and property values. (Ord. 2018-24, 9-26-
2018)

11.90.060.020 Permitted, Conditional, And Accessory Uses

   1. Permitted And Conditional Uses: Permitted and conditional uses allowed within the
      DR Zone are set forth in section 11-35-2 of this title.
   2. Accessory Uses: Accessory uses allowed within the DR Zone are set forth in
      section 11-35-2 of this title. Any accessory use must be clearly incidental to a
      permitted or conditional use of the property. Accessory uses shall not be allowed
      on a lot or parcel without the establishment of an approved permitted or conditional
      use. (Ord. 2018-24, 9-26-2018)

11.90.060.030 Lot Area

There is no minimum lot area required in the DR Zone. (Ord. 2018-24, 9-26-2018)

11.90.060.040 Lot Width

There is no minimum lot width required in the DR Zone. (Ord. 2018-24, 9-26-2018)

11.90.060.050 Lot Frontage
All parcels in the DR Zone shall abut a public street or approved private street or right-of-
way or have acceptable access to a public street through cross access or private right-
of-way provided through an adjacent lot or parcel. (Ord. 2018-24, 9-26-2018)

11.90.060.060 Setback Requirements

Setbacks shall be determined during site plan review, based on required site design
requirements and adopted Building Codes. The width and location of public utility
easements may be modified as approved by the City Engineer. (Ord. 2018-24, 9-26-
2018)

11.90.060.070 Building Height Requirements

A primary building or structure may not exceed forty five feet (45') in height, nor be lower
than ten feet (10') in height. A hotel may not exceed one hundred ten feet (110') in height.
All buildings and structures must adhere to adopted Building and Fire Codes. (Ord. 2018-
24, 9-26-2018)

11.90.060.080 Distance Between Buildings

The distance between any building or structure and any other building or structure shall
satisfy the Building and Fire Codes in place at the time of site plan approval. (Ord. 2018-
24, 9-26-2018)

11.90.060.090 Lot Coverage

The sum total of all buildings, structures and parking on any parcel or project in the DR
Zone shall not exceed eighty five percent (85%) of the total area of the parcel or project.
(Ord. 2018-24, 9-26-2018)

   1. Requirements: Each project in the DR Zone shall satisfy the off street parking
      requirements found in chapter 12 of this title. The spaces shall be hard surfaced

SUMMARY
Over the past several years, beginning with the rezonings to GC-1 for the Maverik convenience store, the City
has been implementing its General Plan for commercial development near the Bangerter and Redwood
intersection. After the initial Maverik rezoning, property to the north of Maverik was also zoned GC-1 and the
City acquired some of it. Additionally, the City created the Destination Retail zone further north to the
Bangerter Highway, and then acquired surplus property from the Utah Department of Transportation which
had already been zoned DR. During the same time, on the east side of Redwood Road, the Woodbury
Corporation has been developing the Ranch. All in all, this area has become a burgeoning commercial
development area. The proposed land use ordinance changes are intended to consolidate zoning provisions
into the DR zone and to update the standards to correlate with the current development activity and interest
in the area.

The City owns all of the property north of Maverik, except for one strip of land with the last remaining home
along Redwood road in this area. The City has been in discussions and negotiations with this owner who has
agreed to participate in these zone changes in advance of property exchange agreements with the City. This
exchange is a separate action from the City Council but is essential in creating developable subdivision lots;
the City has prepared a preliminary subdivision and road plat. The exchanges also allow the planned
collector road of 1780 West to be completed from its current terminus on the NW corner of Maverik to
connect with 13800 South near the Bangerter Highway sound wall.

The restrictive nature of the proposed zoning use table is deliberate. The City desires to maximize the use
and value of the Redwood Road properties to provide food and potential hospitality services to the
community. The General Retail category, although desirable in most commercial locations, somewhat
dilutes those goals. Other office and services are deemed appropriate on the west side of the DR zone.

Since the adoption of the DR zone, the City has updated its design guidelines with specific emphasis on
quality and additional requirements for the GC-1 zone. These additional requirements are applicable along
Redwood Road GC-1 areas. Staff felt in appropriate to incorporate relevant design standards from the GC-1
zone into the updated DR zone, giving developers clearer expectations to follow. Brick is a primary
emphasized material. The existing column in 11.350 for the DR zone will be deleted in its entirety and
replaced with the updated use table in the DR zone as amended.

Model for a Positive Recommendation (Rezone) – “I move we forward a positive recommendation to the City
Council for the GC-1 to DR zone Zoning Map Amendment, Application 2023-21, based on the following findings
(or as modified below):”

   1. That the proposed rezone is consistent with the General Plan.
   2. That the property is already zoned GC-1.
   3. That the rezone creates uniformity in design and development expectations in the area.

Model for a Positive Recommendation (DR Text Amendments) – “I move we forward a positive
recommendation to the City Council for the DR zone text amendments, Application 2023-21, based on the
following findings (or as modified below):”

   1. That the DR is intended to more specifically tailor commercial development in the Bangerter/Redwood
      area.
   2. That the DR zone updates will provide more consistency in design.
   3. That the DR zone updates are consistent with the General Plan.
