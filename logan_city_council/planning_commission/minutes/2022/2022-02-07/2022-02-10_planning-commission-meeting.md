 PC 22-001 West Pointe Subdivision Rezone -Continued from the January 13th meeting
 [Subdivision Permit & Zone Change] Jeremy S Raymond, Olsen & Hoggan LLC/Julie Stoddard,
 HOA President of West Pointe Subdivision, authorized agent/owner are requesting a zone
 change of the 1.36-acre condominium subdivision from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to
 Mixed Residential Medium (MR-20) in order to amend the existing plat to remove any reference
 to ‘condominiums’ and subdivide into 5 parcels. The project is located at 310 West 1400 North;
 TIN: 05-089-0001; - 0002; -0003; -0004; -0005. (Bridger Neighborhood)


STAFF: T. Rice reviewed the request for a rezone from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to Mixed
Residential Medium (MR-20) in order to subdivide the 1.36-acre complex of buildings. The intent is
to change the nature of the land & improvements from a “condominium” where only the buildings
are individually owned, to a condition where an owner owns both the building and the land
underneath. The purpose of the rezone and subdivision is to legitimize the current division of
improvements & ownership, tie those improvements & ownership to the real property located
underneath, and make the properties easier to market and sell. An HOA, or similar entity, would
still be required to own and maintain the common areas, e.g., driveways, open space, etc. A
rezone is required to legally subdivide the overall site because the current unit count exceeds the
allowable density under the current MR-12 zone. The proposed rezone would permit up to 27 units
on the 1.36-acre site. MR-20 is not compatible with the neighboring areas and staff is
recommending denial based on best planning practices of not up zoning when a property has been
intentionally downzoned over the years.

The subdivision of the condominiums is contingent upon the approval of the rezone. The proposed
subdivision would be inconsistent with the current zoning. If the rezone is denied, the subdivision is
also denied. If the rezone is approved, compliance with the subdivision and zoning standards in
MR-20 shall also be met. As a developed site, the expectation is that the subdivision and resulting
lots will need to meet minimum zoning setbacks, parking requirements for each building, and
minimum open space requirements. As the subdivision is currently proposed, minimum setbacks
and open space requirements are not met but can be conditioned. As conditioned, a subdivision
permit could be granted.

PROPONENT: Jeremy Raymond, Attorney with Olsen and Hoggan, stated that the purpose of
the rezone and subdivision is to allow the property to be packaged in a more marketable manner.
It is difficult to get lending and insurance on condominiums. There will be no impacts to any of
the neighboring properties, this will simply make the property a legal conforming subdivision. He


mentioned the city putting restrictions on the property or submitting a request for a downzone
after the final plat is recorded.

Commissioner Goodlander asked how the ownership of the property is structured. Jeremy
Raymond stated that each of the 5 buildings is separately owned along with 1/5 of the common
area.

Commissioner Guth asked if any of the neighboring property owners are experiencing the same
issues. Jeremy Raymond stated that out of the 5 or 6 parcels along the street there were two
that were able to acquire more land to create a legal subdivision. Kymber Housley, City Attorney,
stated that the owners made a conscious decision at the time they developed this site to develop
the units onto one lot and circumvent the City’s requirements for subdividing property. And over
the years they have realized the disadvantages in doing so. Jeremy Raymond mentioned that it
was not the current owners that made the decision. Commissioner Heare asked if the current
zoning is keeping the condominiums from being sold. K. Housley stated that the zoning is not
keeping them from being sold, it is only restricting the property from being subdivided.

PUBLIC: Nate Welker, owner of one of the buildings, stated that there are simply trying to codify
what is there and better manage the existing units.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Lucero asked about recommending a deed restriction with the rezone
and what that would look like. K. Housley said that the restriction would allow for 16 units per acre
on the parcel. The deed restriction would be proposed to the City Council along with the rezone and
if approved, they would both be filed at the same time.

Commissioner Lewis asked if there is a downside to doing a deed restriction. Mike DeSimone,
Community Development Director, said there aren’t any downsides, this is just a way for them to get
what they want without the possibility of the number of units increasing. R. Holley said that if the
applicant applied for a down zone directly after the subdivision was complete it would prevent a spot
zone in the middle of this neighborhood zone. If the applicant did not apply for the downzone the
city could then initiate it. Commissioner Lucero asked if a deed restriction would carry forward with
a new owner. K. Housley stated that the deed restriction would run with the land.

Commissioner Croshaw asked what happened if the boundary lines are not adjusted for a
subdivision. R. Holley said that if they do not meet the required setbacks and minimum open space
the final plat cannot be recorded.

Commissioner Goodlander stated that she is leaning towards leaving it the way it is. It does not
make sense to go through all the steps. Commissioner Lucero agreed with commissioner
Goodlander. Commissioner Guth stated that she is not opposed if this can also benefit the city.
Commissioner Heare said it is not the commission’s place to solve the organization of their
condominium association. Most of the units have been owned by the same owner since this property
was established. Commissioner Lewis and Commissioner Croshaw agreed.

MOTION: Commissioner Goodlander moved to forward a recommendation of denial to the
Municipal Council on a zone change for PC 22-001. Commissioner Lucero seconded the motion

RECOMMENDED FINDINGS FOR DENIAL
1. The existing zoning (MR-12) is compatible with the surrounding neighborhood. The proposed
   zoning (MR-20) is incompatible with the Bridger Neighborhood.
2. This land was originally developed as a multi-family condominium complex and has functioned
   as such for 28 years.


3. The change in zoning for a single parcel, in this neighborhood, is contrary to the neighborhood
   stabilization goals and policies of the Logan General Plan, especially when there are no other
   parcels zoned MR-20 in the immediate vicinity.
4. Changing the zone from MR-12 to MR-20 is inconsistent with previous zoning actions in this
   area.

Moved: S. Goodlander                  Seconded: J. Lucero            Approved: 7-0

Yea: Guth, Lucero, Goodlander, Croshaw, Heare, Lewis, Dickinson              Nay:           Abstain:


 PC 22-010 Hoggan Property-Subdivision [Subdivision Permit] Bradley Hoggan/Bradley & Cathy
 Hoggan, authorized agent/owner, request a subdivision permit to create 30 single family home
 building lots located at 1800 South 1600 West in the Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6)
 zone; TIN 03-005-0048. (Woodruff Neighborhood)


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a subdivision permit to create 30 single-family building
lots on a 6.61-acre property at the southeast corner of the intersection of 1800 South and 1600
West. Both 1800 South and 1600 West streets are under-developed and will be brought up to City
standards with this proposal. There will be 3 street connections made, one to 1800 South, one to
1600 West, and one connecting to the 1900 South stub from the Rosehill subdivision to the east.

There has been concern about the existing ponds on this property and if they are considered
wetlands. The Army Corps of Engineers will determine that along with any mitigation options or
requirements. All downstream irrigation users will continue to receive their water shares. The
stormwater retention pond on the south side of the project will require common area landscaping
and maintenance.

Lots 1-13 abut 1800 South and 1600 South with rear yards. In these configurations, a rear yard
fence that is 4 feet tall or less may be placed on the property line, but a 6-foot-tall fence must be
setback 10 feet from the rear property line. For consistency, the developer may require uniform
fencing materials and landscaping designs in the rear 10 feet of these lots if 6-foot-tall fencing is
desired. Russ spoke about the comments that were received and addressed the concerns that
were brought up.

PROPONENT: Brad Hoggan sent a chat via the google meeting stating he is available to answer
any questions. Commissioner Lewis asked about the wetlands. Commissioner Goodlander stated
that it will be up to the Army Corps of Engineers, the project will have to comply with their findings.
B. Hoggan messaged that they will comply with all requirements. R. Holley said that if the plans
change substantially to meet the wetland requirements they will have to come back to the
commission for approval.

PUBLIC: Kamilla Schultz, who lives in the Rosewood Subdivision and serves on their HOA, asked
if the HOA requirements will include maintaining the sidewalk along 1600 West and 1800 South.
She also mentioned the public notice received did not state that this meeting would be virtual.

Ed Jenson lives to the South of the project and owns land to the west of 1600 West. He said he is
not willing to give up any of his property in order to develop 1600 West. With any mitigation done to
the springs, he does not want the water to run downhill to his property.


Garrett Hinton lives in Rosehill Subdivision, spoke about the traffic issue on 1800 South and the
difficulty trying to turn onto 1000 West.

COMMISSION: Jordy asked where the right of way will be for 1600 West. Bill Young, City Engineer
said that there is a 33’ easement on the frontage properties along 1600 West and 1800 South and

Commissioner Dickinson addressed the other public comments and mentioned that UDOT would

maintenance of the adjacent sidewalk regardless of whether there is a fence or not. R. Holley also
