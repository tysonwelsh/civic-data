constituents about how Ogden residents stand to benefit from these types of project in terms of getting access to the homes. She
stated that Community Development Manager Ogden had indicated that approximately 67 percent of the homes are purchased by
Ogden City residents and she would like to ensure that residents continue to be given priority access to the homes. She stated she
would also like the CAC to consider the individuals who may potentially be displaced through a community revitalization project.
Mr. Peterson stated that part of the burden Ogden City currently carries is due to bad zoning decisions that were made several
years ago; up-zoning decisions during the City's decline led to a 55-year spiral until City leaders pursued a reimagination of the
City. During that downward spiral, Ogden became a repository for low-income housing; the City was essentially warehousing
disadvantaged people and concentrating too many disadvantaged people in one location only makes the problem worse. It was
frustrating for him as a resident of Ogden to watch the failure of the system to aid these individuals in lifting themselves out of
poverty; this failure allowed a vicious and sad cycle to continue. Everyone needs a place to live and it is important to consider the
appropriate mix of residents in all neighborhoods of the City to ensure the success of those neighborhoods. It is opinion that
sprinkling low-income households throughout the City rather than concentrating them in one are is best for the entire community;
if a disadvantaged person located in an advantaged neighborhood, they would likely receive support from their neighbors, which
will allow them to improve the health of their own household. Chair Choberka thanked Mr. Peterson for his response and noted
that she is supportive of the positive improvements the Department is making in various neighborhoods throughout the City, but
she also wants to be considerate of the concerns other constituents have expressed about access to the homes in those projects. Mr.
Peterson agreed and noted that everyone in the housing market is suffering right now because of the huge influx ofpeople moving
to Utah from other states; this has caused housing prices to inflate drastically and there are many who are unsure of their housing
options in the future.

areas; the goal is to raise people out of poverty, but it is not possible to help everyone. Programs are limited, but other sources are
available to low- and moderate-income residents to aid them in improving their situation.

Proposed Ordinance 2020-60 amending the definitions and standards for
Licensed Family Child Care, which would allow for the care of up to 16
children in a home-based business in the R-2 (Two-Family Residential)
Zone
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to definitions
and standards for licensed family childcare facilities came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Jenny Rogers
Miller has submitted a petition to amend the City's zoning ordinance to allow home-based licensed family childcare facilities in
the City's R-2 (Two-family Residential) zone. A licensed family childcare facility is a child daycare that allows up to 16 children

         Minutes of Regular Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah, December 1, 2020 Page 363


to be cared for in a home setting. CmTently, residential zones allow for home occupation child daycares which limit the number of
children to eight. Non home-based child daycare facilities which care for more than eight children may exist in the R-1 and R-2
zones if they are part ofan educational institution and may be approved as a conditional use in the R-3 and R3EC zones and as a
permitted use in the R-4 and R-5 zones. The licensed family childcare facility would allow for the care of more children than is
permitted with a home occupation but would prohibit non home-based daycare facilities in the R-2 zone.
           Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expom1d on the
implications of the petition. The present regulations for a daycare as a home occupation are that they have eight or less children,
no outside employees, State and City licensure, and compliance with Building and Fire Codes. A daycare is allowed in any
residential zone of the City and can be strictly for childcare purposes or offer a preschool as well. There are two types of daycares:
occupied residence or other facilities. The petitioner's request is to 'fill the gap' between a home occupation day care facility and
commercial day care facilities by creating the licensed family care facility classification. This use would act as a home occupation
in the sense that it takes place in the home but allows up to 16 children and outside personnel who have passed sufficient
background checks or licensure requirements. The facility must have sufficient outdoor play area and satisfy increased inspection
requirements. He expounded on the increased requirements that would be placed on a licensed care facility:
                •    Three yearly inspections, two of which are unannounced;
                •    Yearly fire inspection, quarterly fire and disaster drills;
                • Yearly Health Department inspections;
                • Must hold a current food handlers license and First AID/CPR certification;
                •    Maintain a current Health and Safety Plan;
                • Background checks for all personnel;
                •    20 hours per year of professional training for all personnel required;
                • Maintain State approved administrative and child records; and
                •    Comply with all Licensing codes (200+ pages).
           The petitioner cited several reasons she feels the amendments would benefit the community; she indicated Ogden needs
more quality preschools/daycares as most good schools currently have long wait lists. Parents are looking for small in-home
facilities rather than large commercial centers. This is especially true now with the risks of COVID-19. Preschools are critical to
many children's' success in public school later in life; Ogden parents are having to keep their young children home because there
are not enough quality preschools to enroll in. More Ogden preschools would get State licensed if they are allowed to run at the
State allowed capacity; this would also ensure increased accountability and any code violations would be part of the public
records. There are also benefits to allowing and encouraging the use of non-resident personnel in a licensed care facility; parents
want lower ratios of children to adults to increase safety and interaction. They also want qualified professionals working with their
children rather than unqualified provider family members. Early childhood experts agree that lower child to adult ratios are safer
and healthier for young children regardless of group size. And allowing non-resident personnel would provide jobs for many
qualified early childhood professionals graduating from Weber State University (WSU) every year. These types of providers are
leaving Ogden City to start licensed care facilities in other cities. The petitioner has also indicated that quality licensed care
facilities will improve the neighborhoods in which they are located in; the properties will be nicely kept and will create a positive
daytime presence that will deter criminal activity. Any concerns about increased traffic can be addressed in drop-off and pick-up
procedures for the facility. Mr. Montgomery then provided photographs of the petitioner's property, the interior layout, and the
 exterior conditions to illustrate the improvements that have been made to catered to young child development. The Planning
Commission considered whether the desired use is consistent with the City's General Plan, which encourages neighborhood
 communities to have a variety ofland uses, diverse housing types and densities, and the opportunity for residents to benefit from
being close to public uses, such as daycare centers. They considered whether it is appropriate to add a new definition for the term
 'licensed family childcare' as an allowed land use permitted in the City's R-2 zones. They also debated parking requirements for
such a facility and any need to create additional requirements that exceed State requirements for such a facility. They voted
unanimously to forward a positive recmmnendation to the City Council regarding the petition, including:
                •    Creating a definition of licensed family childcare;
                • Revising parking to only require parking for home and staff;
                •    Set hours of operation from 7:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m.;
                • Provide drop off in staggered times; and
                • Limit the use to the City's R-2 zone; and
                • Require compliance with State and local codes for use.
           Mr. Montgome1y then noted that this amendment would change the facility license type from type one to type two, which
 entails different building and fire code requirements.
           Council member Hyer referenced the petitioner's prope1ty and noted that it has a driveway that is just wide enough for
 one vehicle; he asked if that is problematic for drop-off and pick-up at the day care. Mr. Montgomery stated that parents would
 drop their children off at the curb rather than parking in the driveway.
