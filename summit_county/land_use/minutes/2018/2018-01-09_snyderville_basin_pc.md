Chair Harte asked Attorney Thomas to review how the application came to be at this 
point in the process.  Attorney Thomas said the Gorgoza Tubing Park was approved as a 
Development Agreement in 1999.  That DA expired with various uses being vested.  He 
read the list of the vested activities.  With the DA, certain buildings were approved.  
Buildings that were planned, but not built, were not vested.  
 
Attorney Thomas said when a DA expires, if additional structures are desired, the 
current Code becomes the default.  The default zoning on the property is Hillside 
Stewardship.  When a new application is applied for, the Development Director 
determines the process the applicant has to go through.  Director Putt determined this 
application falls under the category of an accessory building over 10,000 square feet.  
That designation falls under the Conditional Use Permit (CUP).   
 
Attorney Thomas said the reason why it falls under the CUP instead of a commercial 
recreational facility is because the outdoor recreational uses were already vested, 
making this an accessory to the recreational uses.  He said the DA specified that the

near-view buffer must be protected.  Additionally, the 32-foot height requirement must 
be complied with.  Setbacks from streams and wetlands must be met.   
 
Attorney Thomas said during the past year, a Special Exception was applied for and 
granted by the County Council.  The Board of Adjustment granted variances from the 
setbacks to streams and wetlands.  This means the Planning Commission does not have 
purview over the height or the setbacks.   
 
Attorney Thomas said because the application is a CUP, the Commission needs to look 
at the adverse impacts and if these can be reasonably mitigated.  The Commission can 
deny a CUP only if it is determined there is no way the impacts can be mitigated.  If the 
mitigating conditions are reasonable, they can be imposed.
