Findings of Fact:  
1. The proposed project is located at 3910 Woodland Drive, Woodland.  
2. The property is in the Agriculture-10 (AG-10) zone.  
3. Accessory buildings and uses to the principal residential dwelling unit or subdivision, 
exceeding 2,000 square feet are a conditional use in the AP zone.  
4. The lot is 3.54 acres in size.  
5. Access to the property is from Woodland Drive.  
6. The building meets the required setbacks and height in the AG-10 zone.  
7. Height in the AG-10 zone is 32 feet above grade.  
8. The proposed building will be 32 feet above grade.  
9. The application was routed to affected county departments, state agencies and service 
providers, no issues were raised.  
10. The use will neither hinder nor eliminate any existing agricultural operations in the area.  
11. It will not hinder the existing irrigation systems in the area.  
12. The site is generally flat with no streams or wetlands.  
13. Due to the small scale of the use, it is not anticipated that any maintenance or improvements 
will be necessary on Woodland Drive.  
14. The size and design of the building is compatible with development in the immediate area.  
 
Conclusions of Law  
1. The proposed Conditional Use as conditioned complies with all requirements of the Eastern 
Summit County Development Code.  
2. The use is appropriately located with respect to public facilities, it is adjacent to an existing 
county road, and has adequate water and septic.  
3. The modification as conditioned is consistent with the standards established in chapter 2 of the 
Eastern Summit County Development Code.  
4. The use is not detrimental to public health, safety and welfare, as the roads and public services 
in the area are enough to accommodate the increase in intensity of the use.  
5. The effects of any differences in use or scale have been mitigated through careful planning.  
 
Conditions of Approval  
1. The proposed accessory building is for private use by the property owner. No commercial use 
is allowed.  
2. The maximum allowed height of the building is 32 feet as defined by the Eastern Summit 
County Development Code.  
3. The accessory dwelling unit shall be connected by deed or restrictive use covenant to the 
principal dwelling unit or structure on the parcel/lot and shall not be eligible for 
subdivision/condominiumization and conveyance to another person.  
4. The accessory dwelling unit shall not exceed one thousand (1,000) square feet of gross square 
footage, as measured from exterior wall to exterior wall of the dwelling unit itself.  
5. All necessary permits must be obtained, and fees shall be paid prior to the commencement of 
any construction activity, including but not limited to the Summit County Engineering and the 
Summit County Building Departments.

4. Public hearing and possible action on a creation of Agriculture Protection Area; 
8552 N Hwy 32– Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this application. The applicant owns three 
parcels on the east side of Rockport totaling a little less than forty acres, and is asking for 
that land to be placed in Agriculture Protection, for agricultural purposes. There may be 
impacts if approved. The required recommendation from the Eastern Summit County 
Agricultural Protection and Open Space Advisory Committee (ESAP) has been given. The 
property has been used as a farm for the last 100 years. Staff recommends that the 
Planning Commission conduct a public hearing and forward a positive recommendation to 
the County Council for this application. 
Alan Siddoway, the applicant stated that this step is preemptive. The current climate 
suggests there will be a request for more development in the area. 
Chair Clyde stated that language would allow any agricultural activity on the property. 
Planner Milliner stated he will look into a Code Amendment to define agriculture with 
size limitations. 
 
The Public hearing was opened. No comment was made and the Public hearing was closed.

Findings of Fact:  
1. The applicant requests the placement of 3 parcels consisting of approximately 39.54 acres, 
into agricultural protection.  
2. The property has been used for raising hay, pastureland, and range land grazing for cattle and 
horses.  
3. A public hearing notice was published in the Summit County News, and 5 notices of the 
public hearing were posted near the property per the requirements in the Eastern Summit County 
Development Code.  
4. The zoning on the parcels is AG-40.  
5. Agriculture buildings and uses are allowed uses in the AG-40 zone.  
6. Section 11-1-3 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code allows a property owner to 
request an agriculture protection area designation if there are five (5) acres of active agriculture 
production within any area to be designated.  
7. Any person affected by the establishment of an agriculture protection area may file a written 
request for modification of the proposal or written objections to the proposal within fifteen (15) 
days from the date of the required public hearing notice.  
8. No requests for modifications or objections to the proposal have been received.  
9. On September 25, 2019, the Summit County Agricultural Protection Board forwarded a 
positive recommendation to the Planning Commission.  
 
Conclusions of Law  
1. The combined acreage of the parcels proposed for the agriculture protection designation is 
approximately 39.54 acres.  
2. No written objections to the creation of an agricultural protection area have been received.  
3. The parcels are zoned for agricultural use.  
4. The designation of the identified parcels for agriculture protection areas is consistent with 
Goal 2.2 of the General Plan, which states, “Acknowledge the historic rural and agricultural 
character of Eastern Summit County.” 
 
Commissioner Rydalch made a motion to forward a positive recommendation to the

Fact, and Conclusions of Law in the Staff Report. Commissioner Sonntag seconded the

Work Session 
1. Discussion regarding possible amendment to the Eastern Summit County 
Development Code to create a Recreation Commercial Zone – Ray Milliner, 
Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this discussion. The Recreation Commercial 
(RC) Zone was discussed as a zoning district during the discussion of zoning changes that 
were made last year. The RC Zone was removed from the recommendation due to the need 
for further discussion. This Work Session is to reintroduce that discussion. 
An RC Zone would allow recreation and commercial opportunities for mixed uses in areas 
such as Echo and Rockport. Eligibility requirements should be discussed, such as a certain 
minimum size. The RC Zone could be processed concurrently with an MPD (Master Plan 
Development). If an RC Zone is within an annexation declaration area then notice will be

given to municipalities involved. A suggested floor area ration of 2.0 was included in the 
proposal. The specifications for buildings in the RC Zone were discussed. Commissioner 
Wheaton suggested that language for building in the RC Zone should allow for variances 
and exceptions. Commissioner Sonntag suggested that at the time of a rezone, the 
Planning Commission could impose criteria. Chair Clyde argued that the applicant for an 
RC Zone would appreciate having some guidance on criteria before a lot of money was 
spent. There would be too high of a cost and risk even at the concept level.  
Director Putt suggested that the MPD (Master Plan Development) language could be used 
with the RC Zone. The MPD language is not subjective, but would be a legislative act. The 
Planning Commission would be able to apply additional Conditions of Approval. A 
Development Agreement would not be the proper tool for recreational development 
opportunities. If the RC Zone was available when Blue Sky was developed, it would have 
been a better tool. There are areas up Chalk Creek or Weber Canyon that would benefit 
with the RC Zone tool. Some flexibility should be included in the RC Zone. There are not any 
areas designated for the RC Zone, but there are areas that could be problematic for some 
RC Zone projects due to access, intensity, or other concerns. 
Planner Milliner shared a Use Table and asked for input from the Planning Commission. 
There are some land use activities that may work that are not on the current table. Chair 
Clyde stated he is skeptical of having a ski run on the Land Use Table, but suggested adding 
a water park and amusement park. Commissioner Wheaton suggested adding a hunting 
lodge and guide service to the table. Director Putt stated that language for temporary 
structures, such as yurts could be included. Other suggestions to add to the Land Use Table
