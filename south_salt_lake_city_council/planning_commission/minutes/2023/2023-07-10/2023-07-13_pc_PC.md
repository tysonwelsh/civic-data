4.      Public Hearing – A Petition to the Planning Commission for a Recommendation to the
        South Salt Lake City Council, for an Ordinance Amending Sections 17.03 and 17.07 of
        Title 17 of the South Salt Lake City Municipal Code to Correct Technical Errors and
        to Amend Certain Townhome Development and Design Standards.

Action Item

Applicant: South Salt Lake City

Ms. Tuia presented the Staff Report and stated that the request is directed at the Townhome Overlay.
A few sections will be impacted by the change. The recommendation was based on feedback staff
received from the City Council. It was proposed that Section 17.03 be amended to correct typos and
modify the minimum width requirements. Currently, the Townhome Overlay requires 128 feet of
frontage that must extend the entire length of the development. It is rare to find a perfectly
rectangular parcel for development so it was suggested that a narrower width be allowed along public
streets. The intent was to allow for irregularly shaped lots to accommodate townhomes.

Title 17.07-Design Standards was also to be amended. Typos will be corrected in addition to three
additional changes. They included changing the minimum requirement for dwelling units per
building from three to two. Currently, there must be at least three connected units and a maximum
of eight. Staff was proposing to reduce that to two. They would also restrict the number of buildings
that would only be allowed to have two units to two per development. It was also proposed that the
window requirement be reduced for facades not facing a public street or internal primary access road
from 40% to 25%. This will allow for flexibility in design. Staff received a substantial amount of
feedback on this particular requirement in that it is challenging to work with. They also would like
to lower the requirement for vehicular access on a secondary internal access road from 90% to 85%.
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                   14
July 13, 2023


Currently, the primary access road can only have 10% of units with vehicular access or garages
facing the road.

Ms. Tuia explained that there are two types of roads in the Townhome Overlay Zone. There is the
primary access road with roads going off of those being secondary. Most of the garages are required
to face the secondary access road. The result of the proposed change would be to allow a few more
units to be accessed off of the primary road.

Commissioner Slifka asked about the purpose of having two dwellings in one building. Ms. Tuia
explained that in a smaller area, there can only be two buildings. The intent is to allow no more than
two two-unit buildings. The proposed change will allow the developer to have flexibility while
maintaining the neighborhood townhome feel.

In response to a question raised, Ms. Tuia explained that the width requirements remain unchanged.
Only the percentage of access is to be modified. Community Development Director, Jonathan
Weidenhamer, reported that the desire is to increase flexibility. On a smaller site like this one, there
is very little ability to manipulate the site to accomplish more housing. The goal is to develop
additional housing units of alternative sizes and price points.

Chair Pechmann opened the public hearing.

Lorena Org participated via Zoom. She reported that she has been working on a project with the
City for nearly 3 ½ years. She thanked staff for the hard work they have put in over the years. She
understands that developers can leave a bad taste in the City’s mouth but commented that not all
developers are the same and not all are truly for profit. With the Townhome Ordinance and because
the City is so built out, there will be developers who want to bring nice development to a
neighborhood where the lots may not be large. The configurations recommended by staff make that
possible. She has worked with Code Enforcement and the Police Department and recognized the
homeless and other problems that exist in the community. Smaller projects can potentially help
bring more home ownership to the City, which is desired and beneficial. She urged the Planning
Commission to consider what has been presented.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.
