Public Hearings

               Max Johnson, Planner, Salt Lake County Planning and Development Services
Division, reviewed the following application to consider a rezone:

              Application #30700 – Waleska Iglesias is requesting to reclassify property located
at 8973 West 3200 South from an R-1-6 to an R-1-3 zone.

              Sara Vanroosendaal, Chair, Magna Metro Township Planning Commission,
stated the Planning Commission voted to deny the rezone request because of traffic concerns
and the rezone is not consistent with the neighborhood.

               Waleska Inglesias, Children First Orphanage, Applicant, stated 18 townhome
units would be developed. The units would be owner occupied and managed by an HOA and
would limit the number of rentals. The cost of the townhomes will be between $295,000 -
$300,000.

               Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Peel, moved to open the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

              Kathrine Bowthorpe spoke in opposition to the rezone. The lots are too small
compared to the homes that are there now. The neighborhood should maintain consistency.

                  Lois Harmon spoke in opposition to the rezone. The area has nice homes with
big yards and horse property. Twin homes or apartments will not fit into the neighborhood and
traffic will be a concern.

               Council Member Prokopis, seconded by Council Member Ferguson, moved to
close the public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

              Council Member Ferguson, seconded by Council Member Peel, moved to deny
rezone application #30700. The motion passed unanimously.

                                −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
               Max Johnson, Planner, Salt Lake County Planning and Development Services
Division, reviewed the following application to consider a rezone:

             Application #30632 – Byron Prince is requesting to reclassify property located at
8003 West 2700 South and 8401 West 3100 South from an A-1 z/c to an R-1-7 zone.

               Mr. Johnson stated the Planning Commission made a recommendation of
approval with the requirement that the developer enter into a development agreement in order to
commit to a certain percentage of lots that are bigger than the base density of R-1-7.

               Sara Vanroosendaal, Chair, Magna Metro Township Planning Commission,
stated the rezone application came before the planning commission twice. The application was

originally denied due to concerns with larger lots not being cohesive with adjacent lots. The
application came before the planning commission again in June. The planning commission was
concerned that if it did not move forward with a development agreement, then the current owners
could piecemeal the property.

                Bryon Prince, Ivory Homes, Applicant, stated he helped the planning commission
envision what the development would look like and made some compromises to the density. The
lots will range between 7,000 – 10,000 square feet. Each homeowner will receive a certificate for
enough sod to cover the front yard and trees along the street. The average pricing will be
$360,000.

               Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Ferguson, moved to open the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

              JR Sharp stated some of the details being proposed sound good. He asked that
whatever the Council decides, to make sure the developer adheres to rules that Magna sets up.

              Hollie Meza spoke in opposition of the rezone. She is president of the PTA at
Pleasant Green Elementary and is concerned with the amount of traffic a new development will
cause. That type of traffic cannot be support right now.

               Ron Price stated if the property cannot be developed in the way that Mr. White,
the current owner, has envisioned, then the developer needs to compromise. He asked the
Council not to make this about the money, but to make it about the community.

               Mindy Sharp stated many homes have sold for over $300,000. People want to
stay in the community, but need larger homes so are leaving because Magna does not have those
types of homes.

             A Citizen stated that he has been trying to get sidewalks and speed bumps in his
neighborhood and has been told no.

               Council Member Ferguson, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to close the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

              Mr. Prince stated the development will have multiple exit points for traffic to move.
There will be multiple discussions about traffic throughout the process. There have been big
compromises made to accommodate the community.

              Paul Ashton reviewed what options the council has moving forward. The council
can deny the application, approve the application, grant the rezone with conditions, or approve
the rezone subject to approval of a master development agreement.

                Mr. Prince stated he is not interested in getting into a long discussion about the
details of the development. The conditions of the approval would need to be outlined tonight.

Inland Port Authority

                 Paul Ashton stated the Inland Port bill was passed at the end of the last legislative
session. A special legislative session will be held to make amendments to the bill. The inland port
authority will take a large portion of Salt Lake City’s and Magna’s land. Magna does not have a
seat on the Inland Port Authority Board; however, a non-voting advisory committee has been
created and Magna will have a seat on the committee. There is a financial advantage to Magna,
in which it will need to create a financial policy and obtain a checking account. He recommended
reaching out to Dave Sanderson, Certified Public Accountant (CPA), to help with the finances.
