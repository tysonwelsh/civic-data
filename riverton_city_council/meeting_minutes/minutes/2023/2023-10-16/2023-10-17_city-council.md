Council Reports

Councilmember Wells expressed his appreciation for the efforts put into the Halloween Bash
event, which was well attended. He also acknowledged the contributions of volunteers and
highlighted that such events not only benefit Riverton City residents but also attract people from
surrounding areas.

Councilmember McDougal expressed gratitude for the recent renovation of Canyon View Park,
particularly the installation of an all-abilities park. He commended the efficient and professional
work done by the City's staff.

Councilmember McDougal also discussed the closure of the Trans-Jordan dump in the next
seven to eight years, which will result in the City relocating its dump facility. He brought attention
to the growing issue of e-waste (electronic waste) and its disposal in landfills. He stated that
electronic companies were no longer contributing to the proper disposal of e-waste, which has
become an environmental concern. He discussed the need for a collaborative approach,
involving the state and other cities, to address this long-term problem and find sustainable
solutions for e-waste disposal.

Councilmember McCay reported that she attended the Utah League of Cities and Towns
meeting and shared some key points discussed at the event. She highlighted "Local Officials
Day on the Hill" scheduled for January 17 and invited everyone to participate, mentioning that
the youth council would also be involved.

Councilmember McCay noted that the Utah League discussed affordable housing and
upcoming state-related matters. She said that the TRCC meeting would take place on the
October 19th, which would involve the distribution of approximately 3 million dollars in funds.
She also expressed her family's enjoyment of the Halloween Bash.

Lake County Jail would only detain these individuals for a 72-hour hold. Mayor Staggs
mentioned that these detentions used to take two to three months when they had 400 beds

         Bangerter Highway Project and Mountain View Corridor Project

Marwan Farah, Project Manager for UDOT, presented an outline of the timeline for the
Bangerter Highway project:

        September 7, 2023: Meet the Contractor event.
        December 2023 to August 2025: Design work for the three interchanges.
        January 2025 to September 2025: Relocation of Rose Creek and pedestrian bridges
         demolition.
        October 2025 to November 2025: Heavy construction on the full interchange bridges.

Mr. Farah also mentioned early activities, including the demolition of the 13400 South and 2700
West pedestrian bridge and utility investigations. The construction work would begin with the
13400 South and 2700 West interchanges and then progress to the 9800 South interchange.
He provided an overview of the construction process and how traffic would be managed during
the construction phase. The goal was to complete all three interchanges by November 2025.

Mayor Staggs asked if there was a date for taking the bridge off. In response, Mr. Farah said
the bridge relocation was scheduled for November 4.

Mr. Farah mentioned the westward shift of the highway's alignment, which would impact utilities,
such as water lines, gas lines, and sewer lines. The good news was that the design-build team
had come up with innovative solutions to minimize impacts on local streets and businesses. He
highlighted the plan for road closures and detours during the mainline work phase. Northbound
traffic was expected to be free-flowing, with left turns allowed into Herriman. The details of the
detours were still being optimized by the design-build team.

Mr. Farah explained the traffic phasing plan for the Bangerter Highway project. He detailed the
phases, closures, and the impact on traffic during each phase of construction. He discussed the
work involving the relocation of the pedestrian bridge and the extension of Rose Creek. He
highlighted the importance of timing these activities to account for low-flow periods in Rose
Creek and minimize disruptions. The project's goal was to optimize detours and minimize
inconveniences to the community during construction.

Councilmember Pierucci expressed gratitude for the presentation and acknowledged that the
western part of the City, which is within his district, would be significantly affected by the
upcoming project. He highlighted the short-term challenges the project might pose and the
anticipated long-term benefits for regional traffic flows. He inquired about the plans to
collaborate with the CIC, the signals timing team at UDOT, and the measures to monitor and
mitigate impacts on east-west traffic during the construction phase.

Mr. Farah explained their plan for traffic management during the project. He mentioned the
intention to coordinate with the traffic operations centers and traffic engineers to make
adjustments to signal timings. He provided examples of how signals would be monitored and
adjusted to accommodate detours during the construction, emphasizing their commitment to
making necessary signal timing adjustments.

Councilmember McCay expressed concern about increased traffic and congestion, especially in
areas bordering her district. She inquired about the evaluation process and emphasized the
existing congestion issues during rush hours and the need to address these concerns as traffic
increases due to the construction. In response, Mr. Farah explained that traffic monitoring is
conducted through CCTV cameras and they can track delays and traffic congestion, and there
are traffic engineers on the ground to monitor the situation.

Oanh Le-Spradlin, Project Manager for the Mountain View Corridor, presented an update on the
project. She explained that Utah is finishing up phase one of the Mountain View Corridor and is
now focusing on phase two, which involves grade separating the middle portion of the corridor
to turn it into a freeway. The project spans nine miles and affects five cities. The approach is
innovative and designed to minimize impacts on the communities. The phase will result in a new
four-lane concrete freeway with grade-separated interchanges and the installation of 22 bridges
at 16 locations, covering 13 interchanges. The current budget for the project is 490 million
dollars.

Public Hearings

         Ordinance No. 23-15 – Text Amendment to Section 18.235.040(6), Landscape
         Design Standards, Addressing a Single Word Clarification, Amendments
         proposed by Riverton City

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said the text amendment concerns the
waterways landscape design standards that were recently adopted. Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy and the Department of Water Resources reviewed these standards before their
adoption, and they identified a deficiency in one portion of the code. To maintain consistency
with their requirements is essential, especially for making flip your strip funding available to
residents.

Mr. Lethbridge said the proposed change is simple, involving a one-word modification. The
section that currently states "turf or sod may not be used in park strips or landscaped islands" is
suggested to be changed to "turf or sod may not be used in park strips or landscaped areas."
This change does not affect the functionality or interpretation of the code. The Planning
Commission has reviewed this amendment and recommended approval, and the City concurs
with this recommendation. While this is a public hearing, no feedback has been received from
the public, except for Jordan Valley and the Department of Water Resources. The process
should be straightforward.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmembers and staff discussed the changes and asked clarifying questions.

Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 23-15 – Text
Amendment to Section 18.235.040(6), Landscape Design Standards, Addressing a Single
Word Clarification, Amendments proposed by Riverton City. Councilmember Pierucci
SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs
called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes,
Pierucci-yes and Wells-yes. The motion passed unanimously.

Discussion/Action Items

         Artificial Turf Discussion

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the City is looking into potential ordinance amendments related to
artificial turf use, which has become more prevalent even in residential settings. Currently, the
City's ordinance is not explicitly clear on whether artificial turf is allowed. The primary questions
for discussion include:

        Should artificial grass be allowed in commercial and multifamily as well as single-family
         residential situations?
        Should artificial grass count towards required landscaping percentages, especially in
         commercial projects?
        What standards should be applied regarding the quality of artificial turf (e.g., grass blade
         length) and ground preparation?
        Should there be differentiation between commercial and residential uses of artificial turf?
        How should repair and replacement of artificial turf be handled, especially in areas
         where traditional landscaping may be required?

Mr. Lethbridge said he would like to gather feedback from the Council and the planning
commission to address these questions and eventually make informed amendments to the
ordinance.

Councilmembers agreed to have further discussion in a future work session on this topic.
