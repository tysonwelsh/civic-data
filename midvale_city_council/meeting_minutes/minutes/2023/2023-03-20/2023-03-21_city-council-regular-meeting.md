       B.       OPIOIDS SETTLEMENT W|TH ALLERGAN, TEVA, CBS,
              WALGREEN'S, AND WALMART
Garrett Wilcox said Midvale City has been approached by the Utah Attorney General's
Office about participating in another national opioid settlement. This proposed settlement
would resolve opioid litigation between the State of Utah and 27 of 29 of Utah's counties
with Teva and Allergan-manufacturers of opioids-and CVS, Walgreen's, and
Walmart- operators of pharmacy chains. Any settlement amount will be split 50/50
betvveen the State and the counties. The State and the counties will receive these funds
because they provide health services related to the opioid epidemic. Although
municipalities will not be receiving any of the settlement funds, they stand to benefit from
the additional services and resources provided by the counties and the State from the
settlement funds.

The final seftlement amount will be determined based on the amount of participation by
political subdivisions with populations greater than 30,000. Under the formula, Utah and
its counties would split the following:

. With '100% subdivision participation: $2'10 million
. With 95% subdivision participation: $'185 million
. With 75% subdivision participation: $123 million

ln order to maximize the potential settlement amount, Midvale must participate in the
