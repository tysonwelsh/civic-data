 4.        Ordinance 2020-33 amending Provo City Code to correct an inconsistency and clarify
           accessory living spaces as a permitted accessory use in the PRO-R-9 Limited Density
           Residential (LDR) Zone. Citywide Application. (PLOTA20200162) (0:32:22)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2020-33 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Robert Mills, City Planner, presented. He explained Section 14.50(09).020(5)(d) of the Provo City Code,
related to the PRO-R-9 LDR Zone, lists “accessory living spaces” as a permitted accessory use; however,
Section 14.50(9).140(5)(a) which related to occupancy restrictions, states “no accessory apartment or
second kitchens shall be permitted in any dwelling unit.” City staff became aware of the inconsistency in
the text and submitted an amendment application to clarify that accessory dwelling units are permitted
in the RPO-R-9 LDR Zone. There were only two homes in this zone that were eligible for an accessory
apartment.

Chair Handley opened public comment, there was no response. He called for a vote on the implied
motion.

       Vote:                    The motion was approved 6:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                                Hoban, and Shipley in favor. Councilor Sewell was excused.

 5.        Ordinance 2020-34 amending Provo City Code to reduce the minimum lot depth in the Low
           Density Residential (LDR) and Very Low Density Residential (VLDR) Zones. Citywide
           Application. (PLOTA20200208) (0:36:39)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2020-34, as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Brandon Larsen, City Planner, presented. The applicant, Jeff Rands, owned the property located 189
South 500 West. This property had an area of 0.25 acres and the applicant wanted to obtain the
necessary zoning to establish two approximately 5,000 square foot lots. The applicant had submitted a
concept plan for the two lots, but the shape of the subject property would not allow him to establish
two lots that meet the minimum 90-foot lot depth of the VLDR Zone. The lot depth requirement was the
same for the VLDR and LDR zones.

Mr. Larsen noted the R1.10 Zone, which had a 10,000 square foot lot area minimum, had a minimum lot
depth requirement of 90 feet; whereas, the R1.6 and R1.7 zones, which had 6,000 and 7,000 square foot
lot area minimum, had a minimum lot depth requirement of 75 feet.

Councilor Harding was supportive. He thought if the R1.6 and R1.7 zones had a minimum lot depth of 75
feet, then LDR and VLDR could also have a minimum depth of 75 feet.

Councilors Harding and Ellsworth shared a similar concern about the way the lots had been divided. To
achieve the minimum lot size, the proposed boundary line that would divide the two lots was not

 6.        Ordinance 2020-35 amending the zone map classification of approximately 14 acres of real
           property, generally located at 1164 South 1600 West, from Agricultural (A1.5) to Residential
           (R1.7). Sunset Neighborhood. (PLRZ20180029) (0:49:21)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2020-35 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Brandon Larsen, City Planner, presented. The property was located near the Kelshaw Lane development
and included two seven-acre parcels. Mr. Larsen described the general area by presenting a series of
maps and described it as historically being used for agricultural uses, but the area was increasingly being
developed as residential.

The applicant had also submitted a concept plan for a 40-lot subdivision. The R1.7 zone allowed for a
minimum lot area of 7,000 square feet, but the majority of the proposed lots would be more than 9,000
square feet. The concept plan had been approved by the planning commission.

Mr. Larsen said there had been a neighborhood meeting in February and the neighbors were generally
supportive. One of the concerns expressed by neighbors included the narrowness of 1600 West and the
fact that it did not have curb and gutter. There would be children walking to schools without sidewalks,
this was concerning. Planning Commission and staff understood their concerns but felt this rezone was
appropriate.

Councilor Ellsworth was worried about perpetuating a pattern of sprawl that was not compatible with
economic or ecological sustainability. She questioned if building at such a low density, everywhere, was
conducive to the goals of the city. Mr. Larsen thought perhaps in certain situations higher density would
be appropriate, but in this situation the density seemed to agree with the area.

Councilor Fillmore asked if there were future plans for widening 1600 West. David Day, Public Works
Engineer, said as the area is developed, they would continue improvements.

Councilor Fillmore then asked if Councilor Shipley, who lived in this area, was in favor of the proposal.
Councilor Shipley felt this was what the neighborhood wanted and was not opposed, but he lacked
excitement about having more of the same type of development.

Chair Handley opened public comment, there was no response.

Chair Handley noted Becky Bogdin, Neighborhood Chair, sent extensive emails about this development.
Council had received and acknowledged her comments.

Councilor Harding followed up on Councilor Fillmore’s questions regarding 1600 West. The development
policies for this area discussed and discouraged “leapfrog development.” He asked what would
constitute a leapfrog development. This project did not touch any land that was already zoned
residential. The adjacent homes were zoned agricultural, but there was a development a few blocks
away that was residential. He thought one impact would be the way 1600 West gets developed. It was
not currently safe for children to walk to school and could also be dangerous if all parents were driving
at the same time to get their children to school. If the development were more sequential, not leapfrog,
then 1600 West could be developed logically with development.

Chair Handley asked if any councilor preferred to continue the item to the next meeting, there was no
request. He called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:                    The motion was approved 6:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                                Hoban, and Shipley in favor. Councilor Sewell was excused.

Councilor Hoban spoke about leapfrog development. He understood not wanting patchwork
development. But there were people who wanted to build, and the Council could not dictate the
market. They wanted the development, but it did not make sense to deny a development because it was
leapfrog. He thought perhaps some development in this area was better than none.

Mr. Larsen responded that infrastructure was key in this case. There was a nearby development with
proper infrastructure in place. This helps to get the ball rolling with more development the area which
would lead to future improvements.
