         A.    DISCUSS OFFICE OF THE STATE AUDITOR'S FRAUD RISK
               ASSESSMENT
Kyle Maurer said beginning in 2020, all local governments are required to complete a
Fraud Risk AssessmenUQuestionnaire and present it to the governing body. The
Assessment assigns an overall risk level of undetected fraud based upon points earned
for the implementation of identified best practices. This assessmenVquestionnaire will be
certified by the CFO (Finance Director) and CAO (City Manager). This assessment was
designed by the State Auditor's Office to identify best practices in fraud detection and
prevention. lt is important to note the following:

     a   Best practices identified in the Assessment are not required, but highly
         recommended.
     a   All of the best practices in the Assessment may be implemented at a low or no
         cost.
     a   The Assessment is not a replacement or duplication of an internal control
         assessment performed by an independent auditor as part of a financial statement
         audit.

Fraud Risk Assessment - What is it?
   a Developed by Office of the State Auditor.
   a Designed to help measure and reduce the risk of fraud, abuse, and noncompliance
      in local governments.
   a Developed to help local government board members understand best practices in
      fraud detection and prevention.
   a Assessment assigns an overall risk level of undetected fraud based on a "point"
      system.
   a Same assessment is used for small governments (with no staff) to large entities
      (such as Salt Lake City).
   a Assessment should be a "starting point" for regular review of internal controls.


Fraud Risk Assessment - What it is not
   r An internal control assessment performed by an independent auditor as part of a
      financial statement audit.

[A]n establishment primarily engaged in the production, fabrication, or processing of
goods and materials using processes lhaf ordrn arily do not create noise, smoke, fumes,
odors, glare, or health or safety hazards outside of the building or lot where such activities
take place and are located entirely within a building. Ihese uses do not include
refineries, rock crushers, incinerators, and similar uses.

This use is currently conditionally permitted in the Bingham Junction, Jordan Bluffs
Subarea 1- 3, Main Street Form-Based Code (as an "lndustrial/Craftsman use"), and
Clean lndustrial zones. By definition, manufacturing uses are required to be located
entirely within a building and cannot create impacts outside of the building or offsite. From
outside of one of these uses, it would appear no different than an office or commercial
use, both of which are permitted uses in the RC zone.

The second part of this proposal is to remove the "Assembly" use from the RC and Cl
zones. Section 17-2-1 defines an "Assembly use" as

[A] business where finished parls are put together to develop a final product. Ihese uses
include computer and electronic assembly, and similar uses, but do not include vehicle or
manufacturing fype uses.

This use is conditionally permitted in the State Street, Bingham Junction, Jordan Bluffs
Subarea 1-3, Main Street Form-Based Code (as an "lndustrial/Craftsman use"), Regional
Commercial, Clean lndustrial, and State Street Overlay Zones.

lf the change to manufacturing zones is permitted, the assembly use no longer has any
relevance. lt describes the same actions that would be permitted under the manufacturing
use, so retaining both would add confusion to the use table without any benefit. Strangely,
assembly is listed twice in the Cl zone use table, permitted outright in one place and
conditionally permitted in another, so this change also presents an opportunity to clean
up the table.

Finally, the third part is to revise parking standards. Adding manufacturing to the RC zone
requires adding it to the parking table. The current standard of 2.5 stalls per 1000 SF has
become problematic; modern manufacturing processes require larger areas for machines
with fewer employees, so a calculation based on area unnecessarily punishes businesses
by requiring far more parking than they will use. Basing the calculation on employees and
vehicles, as is done for several other uses in both zones, will allow manufacturing
businesses to size their parking areas based on actual usage rather than an oversized
estimate.

Step 1: Adopt a resolution setting the tax rate for calendar year 2021 and levying taxes
on all real and personal property within the corporate boundaries of Midvale City, Utah:

Prior to June 22, 2021 , the City Council is required to set the rate of property tax for all
real and personal property within the corporate boundaries of Midvale City. The Certified
Tax Rate is the tax rate used against the assessed values of properties located in Midvale.

Each year the County Auditor calculates a "Certified Tax Rate." The tax rate formula is
designed to give the City the same amount of property tax revenue received in the prior
year, plus any new growth that happened in the City (such as new homes or businesses).
This rate is then applied to the assessed taxable value for every property within Midvale.
The formula does not include any automatic increases, outside of new growth within the
city (new growth revenue is used to provide services to new residents). Property taxes do
not automatically go up when a property's value increases (in fact, the Certified Tax Rate
Midvale City Meeting Minutes
June 15. 2021

goes down in that circumstance to ensure the City receives the same amount of money
as the prior year). lf the City desires to receive more revenue than initially calculated by
the County Auditor, the city will need to go through a process called Truth in Taxation.
This process involves multiple public notices and a public hearing before the final tax rate
is adopted.

Staff is proposing to accept the County Auditor's Certified Tax Rate for calendar year
2021 (fiscal year 2022). The County Auditor's certified tax rate has not been released as
of the date of this Council report. lt is expected the certified tax rate will yield budgeted
revenue of $2,652,109. Last year's certified tax rate was 0.001043, yielding budgeted
revenue of $2,621 ,894 ($30,215, or 1.2ok increase).
