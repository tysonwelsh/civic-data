Council Reports

Councilmember Buroker reported that the City successfully made it through the flood season
without any issues or problems. She expressed gratitude to the safety and public works
departments for their efforts and said it is crucial to appreciate the preparedness and effective
response of these departments.

Councilmember Buroker said she attended the UFA board meeting held earlier that day. She

approximately $10 million worth of flooding costs.

         Resolution No. 23-47 – Adopting the Strategic Priorities

Josh Lee, Communications Director, said that changes were made to the Strategic Priorities
document in February 2023 but it was never formally adopted by the Council.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal asked if it was necessary to formally adopt the Strategic Priorities
document. In response, Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said that resolutions are the official means
through which the Council expresses its position or makes official statements on matters related
to Utah law. He emphasized that resolutions require a majority vote of the Council to be adopted
and the document should have been formally brought back to the Council for ratification. He
highlighted the need for open and transparent communication between staff and the Council,
ensuring that the final product aligns with the collective understanding and direction of the
Council.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal asked if the Strategic Priorities document was part of the budget.
Mr. Carter said it is a separate document from the budget but should be included on the website
on the same page as the budget.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal summarized that the purpose of the Strategic Priorities document
was for the Council, with input from staff, to establish the direction the City will take. The
document should be adopted by resolution, but can be changed if a majority of the Council
desires to bring up a particular subject. He asked Mr. Lee to review the changes that were made
as of February 2023 and said future adjustments can be made as needed, and a follow-up
strategic priority meeting will be scheduled for January.

Mr. Lee said that the proposed updates to the strategic priorities document were as follows:

    Objective 2.2, bullet 1: The city has a verbal commitment from the landowners, and a review
    of the bridge's architectural design is being conducted. This can be considered as in
    progress, and an update will be included.

    Objective 2.2, bullet 5: This item should be marked as "on radar" or "in progress." Similar
    updates will be made to other items that are not being regularly updated.

    Objective 3.2, bullet 2: More information is needed for this item, and it will be updated
    accordingly.

    Objective 3.2: Regarding the reverse in business participation in groups and events, it is
    recommended to revise the wording to ensure that city policies encourage Riverton
    business participation and representation in events. This broadens the scope and
    encourages involvement.

Councilmember McCay said it is important to incorporate the changes and updates that were
discussed during the sessions held earlier this year. This ensures that the document accurately
reflects the work and decisions made by the Council.

Councilmember Pierucci suggested the language should be broadened to more
comprehensively encourage, prioritize, and engage Riverton businesses. This broader
approach would involve actively promoting and supporting Riverton businesses as part of a
Page | 5 Minutes – City Council Meeting

comprehensive economic development plan. The focus would extend beyond simply
communicating about Riverton city events and opportunities for engagement, and instead seek
to actively involve and support local businesses.

Mr. Carter suggested getting the transcripts from previous meetings to capture all of the
changes that were made.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal agreed and said once Mr. Carter has compiled the information and
provided it to the Council, they can review the document and propose verbiage changes as
needed. It would be beneficial to schedule a work session to thoroughly discuss and finalize the
Strategic Priorities document, ensuring that all necessary updates and revisions are
incorporated. Once the document is finalized, it can be formally approved by the Council. This
approach allows for collaborative input and ensures that the document accurately reflects the
Council's intentions and priorities.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal motioned to table the adoption of the Strategic Priorities document.
Councilmember Buroker seconded this motion. Mayor Pro Tem McDougal asked for discussion
to the Motion.

Councilmember McCay stated that the Strategic Priorities had already been discussed in
previous work sessions, which amounted to around four or five sessions. She questioned the
need to allocate additional work session time for the same topic. She proposed a change and
addition to one objective in the Strategic Priorities document. She emphasized the importance
of discussing and agreeing upon the Strategic Priorities and conducting a vote without further
delay. She suggested that the previous work sessions had already covered the necessary
groundwork for this decision.

Councilmembers McDougal and Buroker expressed confusion because of the Strategic
Priorities document that was included with the budget packet. Kevin Hicks, Administrative
Services Director, said the document included in the original budget book was incorrect, as it
was an outdated version from April 2022. The current version of the document, which is
available on the City's website and was shared last week, is the updated version from February
2023.

Councilmember McKay expressed a desire to make changes to the wording of bullets in
objective 3.2 of the document.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal withdrew his motion to table the adoption of the Strategic Priorities
document.

Mr. Carter discussed the process and principles applied when issuing an RFP for food trucks
and businesses for Town Days. He explained that the RFP included a preference for local
businesses, and once the deadline was reached and the booths were filled, priority was given to
a variety of different types of foods and local businesses. He said the Council acknowledged
that not all businesses on the waiting list would likely be able to secure a booth due to high
competition. With this in mind, the staff proposed notifying local businesses in advance of future
RFPs to ensure they have an opportunity to participate. At the previous meeting, the Council
also examined a visual layout of the event, highlighting the capacity and power supply
limitations for booths. He noted that increasing the number of booths could potentially impact
the quality of the event due to power constraints and noise from generators. He emphasized the
need to consider the implications and limitations before making any changes to the layout or

two businesses in question and suggested modifying the process for future years to ensure
proper priorities.

Councilmember McCay said she did not think she had support for adding the additional vendors

Riverton businesses for a designated period of time, allowing them a better chance to
participate in City events. She said when she reaches out to local businesses, it is disheartening
to hear that the City cannot offer them any support. She suggested reducing any potential
hurdles in the RFP process and emphasized the importance of supporting Riverton businesses
as outlined in the strategic plan.

         Resolution No. 23-48 – Adopting the Non-Reciprocal Utilities Transactions for
         Fiscal Year 2023-2024

Mr. Hicks acknowledged that the wording in the annual notice required by the state auditor's
office for the non-reciprocal utilities may be complex. However, he has chosen to keep the
wording consistent as it has been approved by auditors in the past. The purpose of the notice is
to clarify that the City is not transferring cash between funds, but rather accounting for revenue
within each fund separately. This accounting transaction is necessary to ensure compliance with
state auditing requirements.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal further explained that the dollar amounts assigned to services in the
annual notice are not actual transfers of money but rather a means of transparently showing
residents the costs associated with those services. This practice allows residents to understand
the specific dollar amounts allocated to items such as water usage or secondary services.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal called for public comments;
