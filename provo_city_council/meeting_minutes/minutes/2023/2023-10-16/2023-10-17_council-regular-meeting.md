5.         A presentation of the Quarterly Report for the 4th Quarter of FY23 ending June 30, 2023.
           (23-020) 0:41:30

Mr. Borget presented. He said this report is a good indication of how the audited annual report will be
presented in December. He shared that this report includes financial data compared to last year’s
revenues and expenses. He added that no data is final until the city has completed its financial audit,
which will be presented to council in December. He reviewed the statistics within the report.

Councilor Shipley asked if sales tax comes in about three or four percent lower than projected, would it
be about one-million dollars over a calendar year.

Mr. Borget explained that the potential variance in sales tax revenue could range from a million dollars,
as seen in the previous year, to possibly as much as 2 million dollars, without accounting for interest
earnings. He mentioned that there might be other sources of revenue to help mitigate the impact. He
also highlighted the positive aspect that the city's fund balance remains at a healthy level, around 34%,
just below the state-mandated maximum of 35%. He stressed the importance of being vigilant and
proactive in financial planning to avoid unexpected challenges and to ensure the city remains well-
prepared. He continued to review the report and highlighted a few departments.

Chair MacKay invited questions and council discussion. With none, she moved on to the next item.

7.         A resolution indicating the intent to make an adjustment to the common boundary
           between Provo and Orem along 2000 South in Orem. (23-075) 0:57:30

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-48, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Gordon Haight, City Engineer, presented. He highlighted the street in Orem that straddles the city
boundary between Provo and Orem and pointed out the detention basin that Orem maintains. He
explained the proposal is to adjust the city boundary so that the entire street falls within Orem's
jurisdiction. He added since there are no Provo residents living on that street, it would be more practical
for Orem to manage and maintain it, along with the detention basin, without concerns from Provo's
perspective. He said staff recommends a 60-day public hearing process for this resolution, followed by
City Council approval after the public hearing concludes. Mr. Haight invited any questions regarding this
matter.

Chair MacKay asked if there were any questions from the council. With none, she called for a vote.

      Vote:        The motion was approved 5:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Handley, MacKay, Shipley,
                   and Whipple in favor. Councilors Fillmore and Hoban excused.

8.         A resolution indicating the intent to concur with Utah County on the common boundary
           between Provo and Orem near the intersection of 1030 E in Orem and 2250 North in
           Provo. (23-075) 0:59:30

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-49, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Mr. Haight explained this boundary issue between Provo and Orem tried to be resolved a few years ago.
He said both cities agree on the boundary, but the county does not concur. They are initiating a new
process, including a 60-day public hearing, to align the boundary with the agreement between Provo
and Orem. The property in question currently has a lift station owned by Orem, making it sensible for
Orem to maintain it, as it is public property. He said the recommendation is to proceed with the 60-day
public hearing process and later seek a final vote after considering any comments received during the
public hearing.
Chair MacKay asked if there were any questions from the council. With none, she called for a vote.

      Vote:        The motion was approved 5:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Handley, MacKay, Shipley,
                   and Whipple in favor. Councilors Fillmore and Hoban excused.

9.          A resolution indicating the intent to adjust the common boundary between Provo and
            Orem to accomodate a new residential development near the intersection of University
            Avenue in Provo and 800 North in Orem. (23-075) 1:01:30

       Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-50, as currently constituted, has been
                    made by council rule.

Mr. Haight presented. He said this item includes private property, unlike the previous cases that only
concerned public property boundaries. He stated this adjustment is aimed at accommodating a
development project near the Provo-Orem border in Northern Provo. The development includes
housing near the Provo River. He added staff is collaborating with the developer and Orem to create a
more integrated community that falls within the same city, school district, and voting boundaries. Both
the developer and Orem have requested that this property be moved to Provo. He added that the next
step is to initiate a 60-day public hearing process with the recommendation of supporting this
resolution.

Councilor Ellsworth asked if a riverside trail and easement dedicated to the city is included in this
project.

Mr. Haight stressed that this resolution initiates a public hearing with a subsequent final ordinance for
the council's consideration. He said he is confident that any raised concerns during the hearing will be
adequately addressed. He added that while this item deals with a boundary change, the project in its
entirety is being closely monitored by Bill Peperone, who consistently mentions its significance in
meetings.

With no other comments, Chair MacKay called for a vote.

       Vote:        The motion was approved 5:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Handley, MacKay, Shipley,
                    and Whipple in favor. Councilors Fillmore and Hoban excused.

10.         An ordinance amending Provo City code to strengthen and expand the use of
            administrative citations. (23-071) 1:04:14

       Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-40, as currently constituted, has been
                    made by council rule.

Mr. Haight presented. He explained this ordinance was prompted by hillside violations that the city
struggled to address. He said residents approached the mayor, leading to the formation of a task force,

proposal, and the Legal Department had ensured its compliance.

Councilor Handley shared his excitement and said he wants to make sure these penalties are stiff.

11.         An ordinance amending the General Plan to adopt the Provo Gateway Standards. Citywide
            Application. (PLGPA20230190) 1:07:05

       Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-41, as currently constituted, has been
                    made by council rule.

Jantsen Teuscher, City Planner, presented. He said the goal of the gateway standard is to maximize the
aesthetic and developmental potential of the gateway areas. He introduced and discussed the four
sections in the plan. Section One primarily focuses on the prioritization of gateway locations,
categorizing them into three tiers based on their importance. It also introduces the concept of paying
tribute to Provo's history and industries and suggests that subtle elements can reflect these influences in
specific neighborhoods. In Section Two, a comprehensive toolkit of recommended standards is
presented, encompassing sign standards, landscaping standards, parking standards, pedestrian safety
standards, building appearances and setbacks, and streetscape standards. Section Three groups these
standards by tiers, considering current conditions, opportunities in the area, and relevant policies.
Section Four addresses the applicability of these standards. Additionally, Appendix A offers landscaping
resources, including approved plant lists, for reference. He emphasized this framework aims to enhance
and beautify gateway locations in Provo while ensuring their functionality.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With none, she closed public comment and invited
council discussion. With none, she called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:        The motion was approved 5:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Handley, MacKay, Shipley,
                    and Whipple in favor. Councilors Fillmore and Hoban excused.

13.         An ordinance amending the zone map of approx. 0.18 acres, at 156 W 500 S, from General
            Downtown (DT1) to Interim Transit Oriented Development (ITOD) & authorizing a related
            development agreement. Downtown Neighborhood. (PLRZ20230158) 1:16:45

       Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-43, as currently constituted, has been
                    made by council rule.

Dustin Wright, City Planner, presented. He said the applicant is requesting a rezoning for these parcels,
changing them from DT1 zone to match the adjacent ITOD zone to the south. He shared the purpose of
this rezoning is to facilitate the construction of an apartment building on these parcels. This change
would align the parcels under one zoning category, streamlining the process for construction. Mr.
Wright explained a development agreement had been discussed during design review, addressing
pedestrian amenity space, which is not part of the code but is included in this project. He said the
planning commission has provided a positive recommendation for this rezoning request.

14.         An ordinance amending Provo City Code to remove the step-back requirement in the ITOD
            zone. Citywide application. (PLOTA20230160) 1:19:00

       Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-44, as currently constituted, has been
