Chair Spencer asked Mr. Willardson to introduce Agenda Item 3.3:

Agenda Item 3.3 is a request by Development Services to Amend Section 14-3-3(12) Wall Signs of the Orem City
Code by adding the CM zone as a permitted zone.

Staff Presentation: Current zoning code does not list commercial manufacturing as a zone where wall signs are
permitted while wall signs are permitted in all other commercial and manufacturing zones throughout the city.

A recent applicant had requested for approval of a wall sign in the CM zone, and those in Development Services
noticed the absence of language allowing for wall signs in the zone. In the past, the City has approved wall signs
within the CM zone. This amendment would update the language within the code allowing wall signs in the CM
zone.

Chair Spencer asked if the Planning Commission had any questions for Mr. Willardson.

Chair Spencer opened the public hearing and invited those from the audience who had come to speak to this item to
come forward to the microphone.

When no one came forward, Chair Spencer closed the public hearing and asked if the Planning Commission had any
more questions for the applicant or staff. When none did, he called for a motion on this item.

Planning Commission Action: Mr. Cook moved to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council
Amending Section 14-3-3(12) Wall Signs of the Orem City Code by adding the CM zone as a permitted zone. Mr.
Spencer seconded the motion. Those voting aye: Mickey Cochran, Carl Cook, Camille Jensen, Barry Roberts, Haysam
Sakar, Ross Spencer and Mike Staker. The motion passed unanimously.

Chair Spencer asked Mr. Harding to introduce Agenda Item 4.1:

Agenda Item 4.1 is a request by Brian Wilkes for the City to rezone the property located at 166 North 400 West
from the R7.5 zone to the R6.5 zone.

                                      Staff Presentation: The existing property is approximately 12,196 square
                                      feet (0.28 acres). It currently has one single family dwelling. It is a corner lot
                                      with large yard space.

                                      The applicant has submitted a concept plan to divide the lot into two single
                                      family lots--one being 6,939 square feet, the other being 6,540 square feet.
                                      The current R7.5 zone requires a lot size minimum of 7,500 square feet
                                      meaning that the applicant’s proposed lots will not meet current zoning
                                      requirements. The proposed R6.5 zone would allow for the proposed two lots
                                      and their sizes with the minimum size requirement of 6,500 square feet being
met.

The applicant has also submitted concept plans for the homes to be constructed on the proposed parcels. The homes
would be identical in design and would be mirrored over the property line. Each home would be approximately
2,150 square feet separately in size and would have a 750 square foot one-bedroom, one-bathroom accessory
apartment. The primary units would be approximately 1,400 square feet.

The applicant meets the required parking for both the primary and accessory units. The primary units have a one (1)
car garage and an additional parking stall on the side of the home. The accessory apartments each have a one (1) car
garage with doors that lead into the units.

The Development Review Committee has stressed to the applicant that the primary units must be owner occupied in
order to have accessory apartments.

The applicant held a neighborhood meeting on Friday, September 13th at 6:15pm. After the applicant assured the
neighbors that these homes would not be used for multifamily uses, the neighbors appeared to be in support of the
project.

As specified in the table on the attached page, staff has determined that the applicant’s concept plan is permitted in
the R6.5 zone.

Chair Spencer asked if the Planning Commission had any questions for Mr. Harding. Mr. Spencer said in this area
there are a lot of multi-family dwellings. Mr. Harding said yes. Mr. Bench said back in the 80’s and 90’s, multi-
family units were allowed in the residential zones in PRD’s as an underlying zone. Five years ago the PRD zone was
created so now multi-family units are only allowed in a PRD zone. There are approximately forty or fifty PRD zones
within residential zones approved in the 80’s and 90’s.

Chair Spencer invited the applicant to come forward. Mitch Wilkes introduced himself. Mr. Spencer said you own
the lot and you want to split the lot. Mr. Wilkes said yes. The plan is to remove the existing structure and create two
properties with single family homes with accessory apartments.

Mr. Sakar asked what the reason is for the City to recommend this be approved. Mr. Bench said it is an approved use
in a residential zone, but it is up to the Planning Commission’s discretion to make recommendations and up to the
discretion of the City Council to approve it, but it is a reasonable option.

Ms. Jensen asked if the reason for the request to change the zone is to meet the lot size square footage. Mr. Bench said
yes, currently he could build one single family home with accessory apartment under the current R7.5 zone. Ms.
Jensen asked if the plan is to sell the lots. Mr. Wilkes said yes, they will be sold. Mr. Staker said there may be a
discrepancy in the size of the lot and being able to create two lots to meet the R6.5 requirements and asked for any
details on that. Mr. Wilkes said his engineer went out three weeks ago and measured the lot. Mr. Bench said the City
Engineer would have to make sure the lot is large enough. There are areas in the City that have discrepancies with the
deeds and this is one of those cases. Mr. Spencer asked if this will get resolved before this moves forward. Mr. Bench
said yes.

Chair Spencer opened the public hearing and invited those from the audience who had come to speak to this item to
come forward to the microphone.

Charles Watson. He is not familiar with the ordinances. He said Mr. Sakar asked why the city would recommend
approval. He didn’t understand the reason for this type of change. Mr. Bench said one of the zoning options in the
code is the R6.5 zone where the lots are at least 6,500 square feet. That has been set aside in the past as an option for
a residential medium density zone. R6.5 and R7.5 fit within the general plan designation. The Planning Commission
has the discretion to make a positive or negative recommendation to the City Council. They are not asking for anything
that is not already codified they just want to rezone it to a specific zone that is already on the books. The City Council
will make the final decision.

Mr. Watson asked if someone else came in from the R7.5 zone and wanted to change to R6.5 could they do that. Mr.
Spencer said yes, they could ask to do that. Mr. Bench said single family home owners can come in and make that
request, but it is at the discretion of the City Council. Mr. Cook asked if it is common to have properties with a
