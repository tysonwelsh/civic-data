would be a prefab roper building with a dirt floor for personal private use. A covered lean-

Findings of Fact:  
1. The applicant submitted a Conditional Use Permit for an accessory building greater than 2,000 
square feet in size at property located at 30878 Old Lincoln Hwy, Wanship.  
2. Allen Dan Christensen and Amy Beth Christensen are the owners of parcel RRS1GF-1-AM.  
3. The lot is 4.19 acres in size.  
4. The subject property is currently zoned Agriculture - 5 (AG-5).  
5. Accessory buildings exceeding 2,000 square feet in size are subject to a Conditional Use 
Permit in the AG-5 zone district.  
6. Access to the property is from a private driveway off the Old Lincoln Hwy.  
7. The building meets the required setbacks and height in the AG-5 zone.  
8. Height in the AG-5 zone is 32 feet above grade.

9. The proposed building will be 26.5 feet above grade.  
10. The application was routed to affected county departments, state agencies and service 
providers.  
11. The use will neither hinder nor eliminate any existing agricultural operations in the area.  
12. It will not hinder the existing irrigation systems in the area.  
13. The site is generally flat with no streams, flood plains or wetlands.  
14. Due to the small scale of the use, it is not anticipated that any maintenance or improvements 
will be necessary on Old Lincoln Hwy.  
15. Surrounding land uses are primarily agriculture and single-family homes.  
16. The proposed use does not require any changes to the existing infrastructure, and does not 
propose to install either water or sewage facilities at this time.  
17. The size of the proposed structure is compatible with similar structures that have been 
approved in this zoning district. 
 
Conclusions of Law:  
1. The proposed Conditional Use as conditioned complies with all requirements of the Eastern 
Summit County Development Code.  
2. The use is appropriately located with respect to public facilities, it is adjacent to an existing 
county road, and has adequate water and septic.  
3. The modification as conditioned is consistent with the standards established in chapter 2 of the 
Eastern Summit County Development Code.  
4. The use is not detrimental to public health, safety, and welfare, as the roads and public 
services in the area are enough to accommodate the increase in intensity of the use.  
5. The effects of any differences in use or scale have been mitigated through careful planning.  
6. The application complies with the Conditional Use Permit criteria in Section 11-4-7 of the 
Development Code.  
7. The application complies with the Eastern Summit County General Plan.  
 
Conditions of Approval:  
1. The proposed riding arena accessory building is for private use by the property owner. The 
granting of this conditional use permit does not authorize the property owner to utilize the arena 
for commercial purposes. Any commercial use proposed in the structure must be reviewed and 
approved by the County.  
2. All necessary permits must be obtained, and fees shall be paid prior to the commencement of 
any construction activity, including but not limited to the Summit County Health, Engineering 
and Building Departments.  
3. All service provider conditions shall be met. 
 
Commissioner Sonntag made a motion to approve the Conditional Use Permit as 
proposed according to the Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of 
Approval in the Staff Report. Commissioner Benson seconded the motion.

3. Public hearing regarding a Rezone and 5-lot Master Planned Development (MPD) 
located at 660 E Chalk Creek Rd., parcels CCRKR-1 and CCRKR-2, Courtney Richins, 
Applicant – Amir Caus, AICP, County Planner 
 
Planner Caus shared the background for this application. The last meeting for this 
application focused on public comment and the rezone discussion. Staff is now seeking 
direction on the revised layout and the MPD requirements. 
Chair Sargent asked what the open space will be used for. Courtney Richins, the applicant 
answered that the open space will have space for a trail and may be deeded with Lot 5 to 
stay as open space for everyone to enjoy. Planner Caus stated that the open space is not 
required to be public versus private. There is no standard size for a trail easement, as far as 
Planner Caus is aware. 
Mike Brown, representing the applicant stated that the applicant has met with Staff to 
work on the design of the MPD, and page 8 of the Staff Report talks about the deviations 
from the current development pattern. One of the overall objectives of the new Code 
promotes pushing development off of the County roads and using the MPD process. 
Agriculture is allowed in the R2.5 zone and the current site plan helps enhance and 
promote agricultural use. Each lot will have secondary water for the anticipated continuing 
agricultural use. This proposed MPD promotes the goals of the County and promotes new 
agricultural uses.

Commissioner Wheaton asked what can be done to keep the easement in place. Mike

2. Discussion regarding the affordable housing component of a proposed rezone 
(from AG-80 to Commercial) and Master Planned Development (MPD) for the 
Whisper Ridge development, a 200-unit multi-family project, Highway 248, Parcel 
CD-417-418-D, PC Development LLC, Applicant – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner shared the background for this Work Session. A revised spreadsheet was 
included in the Staff Report. Staff is hoping for feedback on whether to move forward with 
this project. 
Henry Sigg, the applicant stated that he started this application to determine if the site is 
suitable for an up zone for intense use. The site plan was created for a private sector model. 
The determination for the average unit price was discussed. The criteria included being a 
resident of Summit County, and the number of people in the household. The applicant will 
work with the County to determine the housing needs. There is economic viability for this 
project, but is this a suitable site? Service providers have indicated that all utilities can be 
available on this site, as long as there is a secondary fire access. 
Jeff Jones, Summit County Economic Development stated that the Eastern Summit County 
Development Code does not have an inclusionary requirement. HUD (Housing and Urban 
Development) establishes median income for the County. Median income was discussed. 
There is more need for rental units than ownership units. 
Commissioner Wilde stated that the density being requested is cumbersome. There is a 
traffic concern. This will not be considered “affordable housing.” The developer’s 
responsibility is to bring what the developer believes the cost will be. 
Commissioner Wheaton stated that with 200 units the traffic will get worse on SR248, 
which could create a nightmare. 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
June 18, 2020 
Page 12 of 13 
 
Commissioner Sonntag stated that the proposed location is a good location. The units 
should be tied to 60 percent of the County mean income with priority given to Eastern 
Summit County residents and workers. 
Commissioner Benson stated that he cannot think of a better location for the density. The 
use would be consistent with the adjacent townhomes. The (Area Median Income) seems 
off. Jeff Jones explained the AMI calculation. 
Commissioner Rydalch stated she is concerned about the location on SR248 because of 
the speed on the road. This location is the most accessible and reasonable, being somewhat 
removed from higher density areas. The units do not seem affordable. 
Commissioner Clyde stated he is concerned with the location. This project would take all

would not be walkable. There would be a transportation issue. It would be hard to turn 
down future rezones for the area. The affordability needs to be more attractive. 
Chair Sargent stated that the priority needs to be for Eastern Summit County residents 
and workers. Anyone living in these units would need to drive to access any services. This 
may not be the right location. 
Planner Milliner stated he will sit down with Mr. Sigg to discuss the next steps.
