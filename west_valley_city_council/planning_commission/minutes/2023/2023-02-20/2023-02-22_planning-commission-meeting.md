ZT-1-2023
West Valley City

An ordinance change to amend Section 7-2-115 of the zoning ordinance to clarify the
measurement standards for a commercial vehicle from the “gross weight” to the “gross
vehicle weight rating” for storage of vehicles in Agricultural and Residential zones.

West Valley City code 7-2-115 currently prohibits the storage of commercial vehicles within
any residential or agricultural zones. However, the current definition utilizes a measurement
method that is difficult to ascertain and creates no exceptions for recreational vehicles,
government vehicles, or emergency vehicles.
Changes to 7-2-115(1) will change the measurement for a commercial vehicle from the “gross
weight” to the “gross vehicle weight rating”. The gross vehicle weight rating is a more readily
available number, and the change will allow code enforcement officers to be more consistent
and accurate during enforcement. The new definition of a commercial vehicle will include any
vehicle with a gross vehicle weight rating of over 14,000 pounds with exceptions for
recreational vehicles, emergency vehicles, and government vehicles.


Staff Alternatives:

     1. Approval. The ordinance should be approved as proposed by staff.

     2. Continuance.

     3. Denial.

               Planning Commission.

C-3-2023
Mapleleaf Cabinets (Outside Storage)
2554 South 3400 West
M Zone (2.46 acres)

The applicant, Aaron Bleak representing Mapleleaf Cabinets, is requesting conditional use
approval for outside storage at 2554 South 3400 West. This property is zoned M,
manufacturing and the West Valley City General Plan designates this area as Light
Manufacturing. The surrounding zone is Manufacturing on the north and (south)east and R-1-
7 and R-1-6 to the west and southwest. The surrounding uses include warehouse/light
industrial uses as well as single dwelling residential.

Mapleleaf Cabinets would like approval for outside storage at their site. They have plans for a
future expansion, that was approved in 2020 under C-2-20, but those plans have been put on
hold for now. For the time being they would like to use the existing paved areas for storage of
supplies, dumpster containers for waste, as well as vehicle storage. All vehicle parking and
maneuvering areas will also be on the existing pavement areas.

Since the site is being developed in phases the landscaping that was required during the
2020 review has been installed adjacent to the residential uses. This also satisfies the
original development requirements for the site landscaping from 1993 (PR-17-93,
Westwood Mill). Therefore, as the site further develops in the future they can build off this
plan.

There is an existing masonry wall adjacent to the residential uses and no additional
pavement will be added as part of this review. If the outside storage use is expanded in the
future the use will require an updated review to evaluate the standards including surfacing
for the intended use.

Staff Alternatives:


Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as the
following:

           1. Outside storage is only permitted on the improved hard surface portions of the
              site as outlined in the site plan submitted with this application.

           2. All vehicle parking and maneuvering areas will be paved with asphalt or
              concrete.

           3. Landscaping along the residential areas is required as outlined in the
                 approved landscape plan from C-2-20.

              4. Must meet the requirements of all affected departments and agencies.

              5. Subject to review upon valid complaint.

 Continuance, to allow for the resolutions of any issues raised at the public hearing.


Discussion:     Jody Knapp presented the application. Aaron Bleak, the applicant, said he would
                answer any questions the Commission may have, but he has nothing to add to
                Jody’s presentation. There was no further discussion.

C-4-2023
Jones Heating & Air Building
Addition 1352 W 3300 S
C-2 C-2 Zone (0.54 acres)

Lamar Jones with Jones Heating & Air is requesting conditional use approval for an addition
on the north side of his existing building. The property is zoned C-2, General Commercial,
and the West Valley City General Plan designates the area as Mixed use. The parcel is also
located within the Jordan River Overlay Zone. The adjacent properties are zoned C-2 to the
east and west, M to the south, and R-1-12 to the north-west. There is an existing single-
family residence to the west, vacant land to the north and east, and Malone Power sports
across the street to the south.

Jones Heating & Air is operating out of a building that was constructed in 1987. The zoning on
the property was M-1, a manufacturing zone, at the time it was built. Later, the zoning on the
property was changed to C-2, and the Jordan River Overlay Zone was also added to the parcel
and overall area. The building and use are non-conforming to current Title 7 standards. Section
7-14-203 of Title 7 states that “existing businesses within the Jordan River Overlay Zone may
continue and can expand with approval by the Planning Commission”. Mr. Jones is seeking
approval for an addition on the north side of the building. The 15’x40’ addition is for the
storage of two trucks. There is also an 8’x 40’ cargo container on the north side of the addition,
however, cargo containers are prohibited in the overlay zone.

The original building is metal, and the exterior of the addition is an engineered wood siding
product. Mr. Jones indicated he is willing to paint the exterior of the addition to match the
existing building color. To address the cargo container issue, he is also willing to frame around
it and enclose it with the same siding as the addition, and paint it match the building. He will
add a sliding door to the front to be able to access the container. Staff also recommends that all
doors on the east side of the building be color-coordinated.

The applicant is not proposing any new signage on the property at this time, but after
reviewing the site it looks like wall signage on the east side of the building is not compliant
with the code. Mr. Jones needs to work with staff to address building signage and apply for
permits where required.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any issues raised at the public hearing and
the following conditions.

    1. The cargo container shall be enclosed, and the exterior walls shall have the
       same material as the addition.
    2. The addition, including the enclosed cargo container exterior, shall be painted to
       match the existing building color.
    3. All doors on the east side of the building shall be painted a consistent color.
    4. A building permit shall be obtained for the addition and enclosure of the
       cargo container.
    5. All wall signage on the east side of the building shall comply with Title 11 of the
       West Valley City Code and permits shall be obtained where required.
    6. All requirements of effected departments and agencies shall be met.
    7. Subject to review upon valid complaint.

Continuance, to allow for the resolutions of any issues raised at the public hearing.

M-1-2023
Ready Made Concrete (Landscape Exception)
2635 S. Constitution Blvd
M Zone (3.57 acres)

The applicant, Tara Stobbe representing Ready Made Concrete, is requesting an exception to
the standards set forth in 7-10 of the West Valley City Code for Standards for Landscaping
Along High Image Arterial Streets at 2635 S. Constitution Blvd. This property is zoned M,
manufacturing and the West Valley City General Plan designates this area as Light
Manufacturing. The surrounding zone is Manufacturing on the north, east and west sides and
the zone is RMH (Residential Mobile Home) to the south across Parkway Boulevard. The
surrounding uses include trucking (Redman), warehouse and light industrial and Mountain
View Mobile Home Park is located to the south.

Ready Made Concrete has been located at 2635 S. Constitution Blvd since 2007. When they
originally occupied the site the building and landscaping along the 2700 West frontage was
existing. They then developed the remaining property under a conditional use approval (C-
11-2007) which included landscaping requirements along the expanded frontages of 2700
West and Parkway Blvd. In January 2023, Ready Made Concrete submitted a building permit
(B23-00176) for a building modification with a valuation of
$650,000.00. That process triggered a review of the site as well the requirements set forth in 7-
10 of the West Valley City Code for Standards for Landscaping Along High Image Arterial
Streets. Upon review, it was found that some modifications were made from the original plan
and over time additional landscaping has been removed from the site. (see aerial photos and
original landscape plan).

Part of the concern and issues with the site has been maintenance of the area along
Parkway due to the UDOT easement and slope of the area for the I-215 overpass.
Therefore, Ready Made Concrete has proposed the following improvement solution:

       The right-of-way area along this corridor is currently maintained by West Valley City.
       Due to the slope the area is not actually mowed but it is sprayed to maintain the
       weeds. Ready Made Concrete proposes to add additional landscaping to this area and
       will improve the entire slope adjacent to their property up to the sidewalk. The west
       end will remain as is, with the 20’ setback measured from the property line with grass
       and trees to meet 7-10, and then as the property heads east the landscaping will taper,
       as it does currently, and the landscaping will occupy the right-of-way and be improved
       with trees and landscaping to cover 50% of the slope (no lawn) and rock mulch. This
       would require an amendment to the existing maintenance agreement West Valley City
       has with UDOT, and then there would be a subsequent agreement between West
       Valley City and Ready Made Concrete that would be executed if this exception were
       to be granted.

The fencing along the landscape border will remain in place. The fence was also part of the
original requirements so it could provide a buffer and sound barrier from this industrial use
to the adjacent residential neighborhood to the south. It is in need of some maintenance and
repair so it will be replaced, which has already begun.

The landscaping along 2700 West will remain, including the setback area along the frontage
in front of the building that has historically been a 10’ wide area. The trees will remain as
required and all landscape materials will comply with 7-10 (lawn or artificial turf and 50%
live plant material).

Staff Alternatives:
