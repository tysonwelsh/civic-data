Proposal 
The applicant proposes to construct a 2,400 sf Major Accessory Building on Parcel SL-E-278. The 
Major Accessory Building will be 23.2 feet in height and will be used exclusively by the owners 
of the property as a garage and storage space.  
Section 10-2-10 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code contains the Chart of Allowed and 
Permitted uses.  In the Rural Residential zone, a Major Accessory Building requires a Conditional 
Use Permit subject to review and approval by the Planning Commission. The applicant applied 
for a Conditional Use Permit on September 16, 2025.

Background 
Parcel SL-E-278 is a 2.53 acre property in the Silver Creek Estates Unit E Subdivision. This 
subdivision was recorded on March 3, 1965. It contains 63 lots, averaging about 2.5 acres in 
size. Lot 278 contains an existing single family dwelling and an existing detached garage. 
The single-family dwelling (SFD) on the lot was built in 1997 and is approximately 2,523 sq ft 
with a detached garage space of 732 sq ft. The detached garage meets the criteria of a Minor 
Accessory Building being that it is under 2,000 sq ft and not more than seventy five percent of 
the building footprint of the primary structure.   
The proposed Major Accessory Building is 2,400 sq ft and 23.2’ high. The building will be used 
exclusively by the owners of the property as a garage and storage space. The application was 
routed to all service providers for review and comment. The application meets the minimum 
requirements for approval, as conditioned.

Findings of Fact: 
11
10 
1. On September 16, 2025, the Summit County Community Development Department
received a complete application for a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) for a Major
Accessory Building at 7765 Silver Creek Rd., Park City (the property).
2. A Major Accessory Building requires a Conditional Use Permit in the Rural Residential
Zone.
3. The property is identified as Parcel SL-E-278 and is 2.53 acres.
4. The property is in a recorded subdivision, Silver Creek Estates Unit E which was recorded
on March 3, 1965 entry number 100548 and is in the Rural Residential (RR) zone.
5. According to Summit County records, the property is owned by Joel Bakken. Joel Bakken
signed the CUP application form.
6. The property is currently developed with a single-family dwelling (SFD) and a Minor
Accessory Building. A lot may have on Major and one Minor Accessory Building.
7. The intent of this proposal is to permit a new Major Accessory Building on the property.
8. A Major Accessory Building  is defined as a building or structure greater than two
thousand (2,000) square feet but not exceeding twelve thousand five hundred (12,500)
square feet in size which is subordinate to, and the use of which is incidental to that of,
the principal building, structure, or use on the same lot. The building footprint is
approximately 2,400 sq ft.
9. The maximum height for an Accessory Building is twenty-six (26) feet. The height of the
proposed Major Accessory Building is 23.2’.
10. The footprint of the proposed building is 2,400 sf, which is under the maximum
footprint size allowed by the lot size.
11. The setbacks of the proposed Major Accessory Building are as follows: front = 93’,
southwest side = 13’, northeast side = 186’, rear = 302’.
12. The Major Accessory Building will be used as garage and storage space for the property
owners. The Major Accessory Building is not intended for public use.
13. On-site privacy for adjacent land uses will not be affected by this proposal.
14. Neither the design/siding/roof materials of the proposed Major Accessory Building are
prohibited design styles. The longest façade is not greater than 60’ and is therefore not
required to have a façade shift .
15. The building identified as a detached garage is considered a Minor Accessory Building.
The Minor Accessory is 732 sf, and is less than 75% of the building footprint of the SFD.
16. The proposal will not combine accessory structures. There are no signs proposed.
17. The principal use of the lot is residential and has been used as a residence.
18. Staff and service providers reviewed the proposal.
19. The application was routed to relevant county departments and service providers.
20. Property owners within 1,000 feet of SL -A-77 received a postcard notifying them about
the project and the public hearing with the planning commission.
21. Public noticing was published in the Park Record.
