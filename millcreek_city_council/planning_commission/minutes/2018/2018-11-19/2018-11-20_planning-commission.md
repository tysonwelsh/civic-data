1.2 Consideration of ZM-18-013, Rezone from R-1-8 to R-1-21 Location: 4284 S. 900
E. Applicant: Leslie Blackham Planner: Blaine Gehring
Mr. Gehring presented the staff report that included a description of the property as well
as the proposed R-1-21 uses which allowed private ownership of up to 4 horses with the
requirement that they be kept at least 40 feet from residents. He added that currently the
applicant has chickens on the property and would like to keep 3 horses there. He said
feedback from the neighbors had been positive and that the Community Council
recommended unanimously approval of the rezone.
Leslie Blackham, 857 E. 4315 S., the applicant, said she had lived there several years and
watched as the lot behind her filled with crime, trash, and illegal parking of construction
equipment. She said the lot was very secluded, but she had wrought iron fences installed
in addition to removing trees, weed, and brush. She had installed no trespassing signs,
painted the outbuilding, added lights, and had begun boarding her horses for certain
periods of time so her neighbors would get used to them. Ms. Blackham explained her
experience with horses. She said her neighbors were in favor of her keeping the horses
there. She said the County had inspected her chickens and commented they were spoiled
while adding she would be responsible horse owner.
Commissioner Allen asked her about the fencing and whether the chickens are allowed to
roam free. Ms. Blackham said there would be a variety of different fencing such as chain
link and electrical. She added the chickens would be free roaming. Commissioner
Mumford asked about the neighbor to the north and the fence line. Ms. Blackham said
there was already a cinderblock fence in place that was 6 feet tall.

1.3 Consideration of CU-18-005, Conditional Use Permit for a Mixed-Use
Multifamily Building Consisting of 250 Dwellings and 15,000 Square Feet of
Commercial Space Location: 3064 S. Highland Dr. Applicant: Han/Sol LLC
Planner: Erin O’Kelley and Francis Lilly (5:55pm)

Ms. O’ Kelley presented her staff report and noted that the conditional uses had been
previously before the Planning Commission. She said the applicant had finished his
TCOZ amendment a couple of months ago and said the site consisted of 4 parcels with
the area designated as a Town Center Overlay. She said the Canyon Rim Community
Council recommended approval of the conditional use permit while the Millcreek
Community Council added a condition to their approval that issues with the traffic study
be addressed. Ms. O’Kelley explained the issues of the Town Center Overlay Zone that
pertained to the Gunn Avenue developments such as additional design guidelines. She
further explained the details of the development agreement such as the designated money
for pedestrian access and the traffic study.

Commissioner Healey commented about how the 1:4 parking ratio seemed minimal even
before adding the additional 50,000 square feet of retail space and then asked if parking
was adequate. Mr. Lilly explained the ratio had been well vetted by 2 different traffic
consultants though he agreed the parking ratio was low but there were mitigating factors
such as angled parking as well as working with residents east of Highland Drive to create
a residential parking district. He added the residents would have to petition for the
parking district, but the city would support it and the development agreement stipulated
the property owner would need to create a parking regulation scheme written into the
leases. Mr. Lilly said they wanted to create a town center worthy of the name and that
higher parking ratios needed to be let go. He said he supported these numbers and added
other cities had even lower ratios including Salt Lake City’s for the Sugar House area.
Commissioner Healey said there had been 2 other approved projects nearby and asked if
there would be a walking path that continued onto this project, so the area would be
uniform. Mr. Lilly answered yes.

Commissioner Booth asked for more information on the residential parking agreement.
Mr. Lilly explained how any neighborhood was empowered by Millcreek ordinance to
petition the city to do a traffic study in determining the feasibility and desirability of a
residential parking program. He added the development agreement included a provision
in the rental agreement that limited parking and would be enforced by sanctions against
their business license. Chair Stephens asked whether on-street parking would satisfy the
commercial parking requirement in the development agreement which Mr. Lilly
answered it would. Commissioner Healey said he felt more comfortable with the parking
situation after listening the explanations. Commissioner Allen said the development
agreement had studios listed as .05 spaces in parking and asked what family doesn’t own
a car particularly in an area without mass transit which would then results in off-street
parking being rapidly filled. Mr. Lilly said #1 of the parking management plan stated
developers agreed that the city may impose a 2-hour parking limit between the hours of
8am and 6pm, Monday through Friday on parking spaces adjacent to the project.
Commissioner LaMar asked about the traffic pattern from Gunn Ave. going eastbound to
Highland Dr. Mr. Lilly said there was possibility of changing Highland Dr. from 4 lanes
to a 3-lane intersection.

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 20 November 2018 Page 5 of 11
Robert Miiller, 1575 Gilman Street Denver Colorado, the architect of the project,
described the design changes and the endeavor to be compliant with all requirements of
the agreement. He said the 1:4 to 1 was the correct ratio and they were doing more
parking than required by the development agreement and added that it was 1 parking
space per bedroom. He explained how the development agreement allowed them to take a
percentage as shared parking and how a portion of the angled parking would be counted
as the general parking pool. Mr. Miiller added that renters would be informed to not park
in the 2-hour angled parking during daytime hours reserved for businesses. He said the
parking ratio was entirely appropriate for a project of this size and the formula of 1 car
per bedroom worked for other projects in communities in Colorado.

Jeremy Carver, 12747 Whisper Grove Circle, said they managed over 20,000 apartment
units throughout the United States and explained the 1.02 ratio to be their standard
parking ratio in their portfolio. He cited similar projects such as Elements 31 as well as
what they were contributing to the Millcreek city center through the development
agreement. Mr. Carver said they had several neighborhood meetings to make sure they
heard all the feedback. He said that residents would soon figure out that turning left on
1300 E. or Highland Dr. wouldn’t be the smartest approach.

Commissioner Allen asked if off-street parking was part of the 1 bedroom 1 parking
space ratio. Mr. Carver answered the off-street parking was part of the formula and that
they had provided an additional 85 stalls than what the traffic study recommended
because as developers, it was in their best interest to provide ample parking otherwise
their renewal percentage would drop. He said across the county people are using cars less
and cited Seattle as an example, but Salt Lake City wasn’t there yet.

Chair Stephens opened the public hearing at 6:37 p.m.

There were no public comments.

Chair Stephens closed the public hearing at 6:37 p.m.

Commissioner LaMar said he’d rather see the study first to make sure the project fit the
General Plan versus the making the General Plan fit this project. He said it seemed like a
pleasant project, that the City would get some benefits but not as much as he had hoped,
and the project seemed rushed with certain things not being fully vetted. Commissioner
Allen said he still had concerns about the parking ratio and if there was going to be a
vibrant city center then it would be busiest when people were leaving work. He added the
city was too anxious to make this project happen and would leave missing opportunities.
Chair Stephens commented that their concerns had previously been addressed when the
Planning Commission had been contemplating changes to the TCOZ ordinance and they
were far beyond those issues at this point. Commissioner Carlson said this wasn’t the first
time this project had been seen, that there needed to be change for people to use cars less
and incentivize other options. He said it was the right vision for the future of the City and
he’d rather not speculate but leave it to the professionals to do parking and traffic studies.

2.1 Consideration of ZT-18-011, Amendment to the R-1 zones Allowing Dog
Walking and Boarding as a Conditional Use Applicant: Ellen Greeneisen Planner:
Francis Lilly
Mr. Lilly noted that the presentation would be different because the applicant and staff
needed to hear guidance from the Planning Commission. Mr. Lilly said the applicant
Ellen Greeneisen ran a dog walking and boarding business out of her home in an R-1-10
zone and was told that use was not allowed in her current zone. He said she did what any
resident of Millcreek had a right to do which was to petition the City Council to amend
the zone though staff was concerned about the potential adverse impact on adjacent
properties. Mr. Lilly started with what was currently in the Millcreek code and how the
definitions such as for the word “kennel” did not perfectly fit with this applicant’s
situation. He said that Millcreek had adopted the Salt Lake County animal code which
currently didn’t have a limitation on how many cats or dogs a person may own. Mr. Lilly
said that it didn’t matter whether a person owned a dog or boarded a dog as the dog
would be defecating at the same rate. He said while some people were afraid that this
type of business would be opening up next to them, the case was that this type of business
was already there.
Mr. Lilly said he had spoken with an attorney who said theoretically someone could sign
a bill of sale each day they dropped off a dog. Commissioner Allen said he experienced a
similar situation in Summit County but with horses and added it was a serious issue. Mr.
Lilly then explained the new jobs that were emerging out of the gig economy where 57
million Americans work in informal situations like Uber, Airbnb, delivery, and dog
walking/boarding services. He said the Millcreek Community Council issued a denial on |
a vote of 6-2 because they thought kennels should be more in commercial areas and not
residential. He said they were also concerned about dog barking, biting, and attacks. Mr.
Lilly said the Mt. Olympus Community Council was split down the middle in their vote
and that the text amendment needed additional work. East Mill Creek and Canyon Rim
Community Councils were unanimous in their votes of support. He said their position
was more on helping people thrive in their neighborhoods as well as opportunities for
businesses. Mr. Lilly said the applicant had taken feedback from Chair Stephens and had
proposed changes to the text. Chair Stephens said his goal was not to have a long drawn
out process. Mr. Lilly said there needed to be regulation, so accountability could exist, |
