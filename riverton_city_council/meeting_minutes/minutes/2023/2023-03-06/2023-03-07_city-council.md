Discussion Items

       Long Range Plan Discussion

Mayor Staggs and Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, led a discussion about the
Long Range Plan.

Mayor Staggs explained the Council could pass over the police fund at this point to give Chief
Barfuss time to meet with Mr. Hicks to go over his department’s budget.

Councilmembers discussed the following funds in the budget:

Fund 40, REDIIF. Mr. Hicks said there was $600,000 to pay down the 2012 refunding. He has
already sent a letter to the trustee and everything will be processed on June 1, 2023. He said
that Fund 40 and Fund 45 will be used for the Bangerter Highway betterment at 13400 South.
Mayor Staggs said the Senate has committed $2 million and SLR has committed to $1-1.5
million but does not want to pay cash. Mayor Staggs believes the City could pay approximately
$500,000.

Mayor Staggs asked about the revenue line showing franchise fees, gas and power as static.
Mr. Hicks said these fees have stayed the same for the past 10 years and he believes this
number is a safe and conservative estimate.

Mr. Hicks pointed out rows 63-64 which would use REDIIF to help out with culinary and
secondary legacy projects.

Councilmember Buroker would like to include an item for trails in the budget. She believes the
County is interested in betterments on the Rose Creek and Midas Creek trails and would like to
collaborate with them to fund some of these projects. In response, Councilmember McDougal
asked if the Council needed to pick which trails to fund. There are funds in Fund 45 going
towards trails.

Mayor Staggs said that Fund 45 included line items for both Midas Creek and Rose Creek.
Mayor Staggs asked the Council to table this issue for further discussion.

In fund 41, Mayor Staggs said there is a 10 acre park and a 16 acre park for Western
Commercial District which is budgeted in 2025 and will be up to the Council to prioritize these
items. Councilmember Pierucci said the BMX park may be something the Council wants to
budget sooner than later. Councilmember Buroker said there may be a grant the City could
apply for to fund the BMX park.

Mr. Hicks explained the City is due for another impact fees study. The parks impact fee fund
was historically not project driven. Other impact fees are driven by project and money can only
be spent if it is on the capital facilities plan. He recommended adding more onto this plan and
mentioned that staff will be starting the process within the next month to update the plans

Mayor Staggs said there are a lot of legacy projects that are not on the capital facilities plan so
they would need to come from a different fund as they are not impact fee eligible. Mayor Staggs
believes the City is in good shape with the Stormwater capital projects fund. He said they can
tap into this fund as well as HB 244 which goes 15 years with $700,000 per year for road
projects.

In fund 44, Mr. Hicks said the $1.6 million reimbursement to Edge Homes for 13800 South is a
guess and it may be too high or too low. The Welby Jacob box culvert has been the same
number for the past couple of years and is unknown when this will happen. Mayor Staggs
suggested changing this amount to $500,000.

Mr. Hicks said when projects are pulled from the long range plan and added to the budget, staff
does its best to update the dollar amounts with current estimates.

Mayor Staggs said they should start the filtering project at the end of March. Councilmember
McDougal asked if they could fill the canals earlier this year. Mr. Necaise said that typically they
allow Utah Lake to fill up first before they will start to fill the canals. Stacie Olson also clarified

that they will not be filling the canals early because they do not want to encourage more water
use.

Mayor Staggs explained that the City is planning to hire a Project Management Professional
(PMP) because of the escalating costs of projects and the need to keep projects moving forward
on time and on budget. Councilmembers agreed with the need for this position.

Councilmember Buroker asked if there was a budget item for Park Avenue. Mr. Hicks said that
HB 244 (fund 47) would be used for Park Avenue for fiscal year 25-26. Councilmember Buroker
asked about Laurel Chase. Mr. Necaise said they are still designing this to see what the costs
will be.

Councilmember Pierucci asked what the process was for when a left hand turn is needed. Mr.
Necaise said that a traffic study is needed to see if changes are warranted.

Fund 45. Mr. Hicks said they have added a few things to this fund. For example, they have
highlighted deficiencies in street lighting and proposed $100,000 each year starting the year
after next. There are other projects that have been added but do not have numbers. They have
proposals for GPS street lighting and smart street lighting. There is also a contract obligation
with Salt Lake County to upgrade the flooring at the senior center every 12 years.

Mayor Staggs asked if there would be a desire to take over the senior center from the County.
Councilmember Buroker said she does not believe the City could program the senior center as
well as the County does. Sheril Garn, Director of Events and Operations, said she agrees that
she would not want to take over the senior center. She would rather the County take full control
of the senior center. Mayor Staggs said he would have a conversation with Mayor Wilson about
taking the senior center back.

Ms. Garn said the high level flooring cost is estimated at $145,000. She said the furniture is
also 12 years old and may need to be replaced.

Mayor Staggs said the Midas Creek trail and Rose Creek have $250,000 each for the current
year. The Jordan River expense to shore up the river came in much higher than expected.
Councilmember Buroker said this project will need to be done now or later.

Councilmember McDougal said that residents are interested in trails being enhanced. He would
like to invest in trails and finish existing trail projects. He would like to look at the budget next
year once the City determines how much the current trail projects will cost.

Councilmembers agreed to concentrate on current trail projects on Midas Creek, Rose Creek
and along the Jordan River. Councilmembers asked to set aside a work session dedicated to
the trail system and outline a trail plan and outline where enhancements are needed.

Fund 46, Stormwater capital projects. Mr. Hicks said these are legacy projects that are not
eligible for the impact fee. Mayor Staggs asked when all the legacy projects are complete, then
could that the City get rid of the utility fee. In response, Mr. Hicks said in the next few years, the
City could possibly get rid of the stormwater utility fee and raise the sanitation fee.

Fund 47. Mayor Staggs explained this fund includes the $700,000 which the City will receive
for 15 years from HB 244. This year the projects include Majestic Rise, 1830 widening and
Trestle Lane.

Councilmember McDougal said he would like crosswalks budgeted. Mr. Hicks said this could
be budgeted as part of Fund 47 because HB 244 specifically calls out safety and connectivity.

Mr. Hicks said they are trying to budget and complete the roof at Tithing Hill. Ms. Garn said the
building needs additional repairs besides the roof.

Water. Mr. Hicks said that some items in this budget will be moved to next year because of
timing.

Mr. Hicks said they updated some of the estimates for fleet.

Mayor Staggs asked about the water construction/operation line goes down next year because
of the Greenwell. Mr. Hicks estimated they will not go over the contracted amount for water. .
There will be a savings, but the savings will only be the fact that the City will now only pay the
contracted amount instead of the overage that the City has been paying each year.

Fund 52, Impact Fee Fund. Mr. Hicks said there are only a few projects in this area and would
like to update the Capital Improvements Plan.

Mayor Staggs asked why irrigation water sales is increasing over future years. Mr. Hicks said
his projections are built on revenue trends, not specific users.

Mr. Hicks as we transition from new to maintain, that the City be cognizant of the operating
revenue over expenses and make sure there is a healthy working capital. The secondary
system is about 23 years old and some of the lines as they go out will be very expensive.

Mr. Hicks said the City is utilizing the secondary impact fees to pay secondary fund back. With
the new impact fee analysis, they will consider this item because this system was built for future.

Councilmember McCay asked about the cost of the impact fee analysis. In response, Mr. Hicks
said they budget about $50,000 to $75,000 for this analysis.

Fund 55, Sanitation. Mr. Hicks said there is another alternative which is not to do anything and
maintain a subsidy. The budget or next year will include a $425,000 transfer from the general
fund to subsidize if the City does not raise fees.

Councilmember McDougal anticipates an increase this year of $2 per ton. Mayor Staggs said
this would equate to about $40,000.

Mayor Staggs said the options include continuing to subsidize or remove recycling which would
make it even. The no recycling option could be very political.

Councilmember Pierucci said it would be better if residents were allowed to opt in to recycling or
look at regional recycling areas.

Councilmember McDougal said he would like to lock in the contract first and continue to
subsidize for a year while they look at options. All Councilmembers agreed to subsidize for
another year.

Mayor Staggs asked how much notice the City would need to provide Waste Management to
discontinue recycling. Mr. Hicks said they require 180 days.

Mr. Hicks said there is an unforeseen expense for health insurance, however, even with a 12%
increase the overall budget would be in line with the projections from the long-range plan.

Councilmembers discussed adding a 10% increase to costs on the long-range plan for
inflationary costs.

Councilmember Pierucci asked about an additional court clerk position. Mr. Brickey said that an
employee has given notice of retiring on June 1. They would like to take this position and move
it into the court so there is coverage.

Councilmember McCay would like to hear from department heads about specific needs they
may have and things going on in their department. Councilmember Pierucci was supportive of
this idea. Mayor Staggs said that each department head could be asked to report at a work
session.

Mayor Staggs proposed that Mr. Brickey give a City Manager’s Report at each work session
and if there were specific questions, then the Council could ask questions of each of the
directors.

Discussion/Action Items

         Resolution No. 23-19 – Objecting to Implementation of “Environmental, Social and
         Governance” Policies in the Finance Sector

Mayor Staggs asked Kevin Hicks to discuss the information about how bond funding is
provided. Mayor Staggs said there is a Senate concurrent Resolution Number 9 which has
objected to the ESG ratings and political ideology.

Mr. Hicks presented information about bond funding using the ESG policies. This information is
included in the packet.

Mayor Staggs explained that ESG policies will prioritize a political score over the free market.
He believes the City’s resolution is a first step in pushing back on this policy and lends support
to state leaders in their decisions. The City will work with financial agencies that do not focus on
political ideology.

Councilmembers expressed support for this resolution.

Councilmember Pierucci MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 23-19 –
Objecting to Implementation of “Environmental, Social and Governance” Policies in the
Finance Sector. Councilmember McCay SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for
discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows:
Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed unanimously.
