Mr. Kelly gave a brief update about the 1200 South 400 West Roundabout. He said there were
existing and future deficiencies they wanted to address; it was anticipated that the Level of Service
(LOS) for the traffic in the area would be at failing level before 2040, as well as safety and queuing
concerns that would affect the surrounding traffic network. Mr. Kelly said a suggested roundabout
design would have two lanes, and shared an example of this kind of roundabout recently built in
Mesquite, Nevada. He acknowledged that the design could initially be confusing for drivers, but
would improve the LOS to grades A and B and improve the flow

Discussion continued regarding the redesign of the roundabout, including whether the intersection

       DISCUSSION – State Street Zoning & associated master planning

Mr. Bench said they had been working on State Street corridor master planning for the past five
years. It had involved a significant and purposeful public outreach campaign and established the
foundational plan for the five State Street Districts. In February 2015, residents participated in a
survey that identified their priorities for the State Street corridor. Preserving existing
neighborhoods was the highest priority, which meant determining how and where to focus density.
Mr. Bench said the city would continue to grow, so in order to provide housing opportunities for
our kids and grandkids they looked to disperse housing along the State Street corridor. The plan
aimed to preserve single-family neighborhoods while considering the safety, walkability,
livability, and aesthetic of a major corridor.

Mr. Bench shared information regarding the public outreach campaign staff undertook as the State
Street plans were created. He said residents, home and land owners, business owners, and
neighborhood groups were all part of the discussions. There were multiple open houses, three
informational websites launched, thousands of email notices, utility notices, and business mailers
sent, five newspaper articles as well as two newscasts about the State Street master plan project,

     ORDINANCE – Amending Article 22-6-9(I) Accessory Apartments of the Orem City
     Code to clarify language in the accessory apartment ordinance

Mr. Bench said it would be helpful to clarify language in the Accessory Apartments ordinance
pertaining to the definition of “owner’ and “bedroom” and to clarify the requirement for interior
connectivity between an accessory apartment and the primary dwelling.

The first proposed change clarified that when a house with an accessory apartment was owned by
a corporation or limited liability company, the house must be occupied by persons who had at least
a combined 50% ownership interest in the corporation or limited liability company to be
considered “owner occupied”.

The second proposed change would clarify the definition of a bedroom. The current ordinance
stated that an accessory apartment could only have two bedrooms. Staff had recently been
encountering situations where owners were attempting to skirt this requirement by labeling rooms
that could easily be used as bedrooms as dens, offices or playrooms. The proposed change would
clarify that basically any room in an accessory apartment that could reasonably be used as a
bedroom was counted as a bedroom for purposes of the two-bedroom limitation.

The final proposed change would clarify that interior connectivity must be maintained between an
accessory apartment and the primary dwelling by means of an interior door (which may be locked).

Mr. Earl clarified that in a scenario where a trust owned a home, they would still be subject to the
requirement that at least 50% interest of equity in the house lived in the house. It had to be owner-
occupied no matter how it was owned; they were not setting them up as duplexes.

Mayor Brunst opened the public hearing at 7:03 p.m. Hearing no public comments, the Mayor
closed the public hearing and brought the item back to the City Council for further discussion.

Mayor Brunst moved, by ordinance, to amend Article 22-6-9(I) Accessory Apartments of the
Orem City Code to clarify language in the accessory apartment ordinance. Mr. Peterson seconded
the motion. Those voting aye: Richard F. Brunst, Jeff Lambson, Debby Lauret, Tom Macdonald,
Terry Peterson, David Spencer, and Brent Sumner. The motion passed.
