WORK SESSION

Chair Jan Bradshaw called the Work Session to order at 5:34 p.m. The agenda items were
reviewed and discussed.

Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink, presented the Compagna Accessory Garage request and
stated that it includes a Conditional Use Permit. The ordinance provides permitted sizes that can
be approved by staff based on the lot size. In order to exceed that size, Planning Commission
review and approval are required. In this case, the property size exceeds the chart set forth in the
ordinance so there is not much guidance in terms of the allowed minimum square footage. The
site is over two acres in size. The applicants are proposing to raze what appears to be an old guest
house or something similar and replacing it with their own personal garages. Staff recommended
approval with applicable conditions. No comments were received by staff from the public.

The second agenda item involved a public hearing for the proposed Holladay Commons residential
subdivision. Conceptual approval was sought. Mr. Teerlink reported that the request is at the
concept level and involves the minimum lot size for a twin home development that is serviced by
a private driveway. The Technical Review Committee (“TRC”) has reviewed the proposal. With
regard to the dedication of Murray Holladay Road, the property is old metes and bounds ground,
which means it is described to the middle of the road. As subdivisions are created, they omit what
would be the public right-of-way, which involves the travel lane, asphalt, curb and gutter, park
strip, and sidewalk. In this plan, that area is deeded to the public. It can be used, however, in the
gross density calculation. Between the two properties are the limits to Holladay Village Zone.

Commissioner Lloyd was pleased to see a private development being successful and attractive.

Mr. Teerlink stressed the importance of road connectivity. With private development, there is not
as much opportunity. The intent was to connect the interior roads and provide a more circular
drive pattern, which was preferred by the Unified Fire Authority (“UFA”). The applicant was able
to provide all of the Utility Connection Letters and among the three buildings that exist on the
property, there are a total of five units most of which are rental units.

The Clarenden Woods project was next reviewed. Mr. Teerlink stated that subdivision plat
amendments are not seen often. The process involves the establishment of ownership and the
boundary of the subdivision. In this case, they are amending that boundary. Because the land use
authority reviewed and approved the boundary, they must amend it. Clarenden Woods was
established in 2007 for four lots. The applicant has since acquired metes and bounds property next
door and would like to add it to his backyard. In order to do that, the subdivision boundary must
be amended. The intent is to take the additional .9 acres and add it to his backyard and maintain
the rest on its own lot within the subdivision plat.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Teerlink stated that because the applicant owns the abutting
property and is adding it to his own property, the HOA does not need to be involved. The approval
process was described.

The Bermondsey Place Planned Unit Development (“PUD”) subdivision request was next
addressed. Mr. Teerlink stated that the applicant is returning with a preliminary level subdivision
plat review as a PUD. He explained that PUDs are a type of subdivision that allows property to
be reorganized to accommodate open spaces, geographic features, and nuances to a property that
would probably limit or otherwise hinder the way a normal subdivision would be laid out. In the
R-2-10 zone, there are lot width, lot size, and setback requirements. In this case, eight units are
allowed, however, there are fewer requirements pertaining to lot sizes and widths. The only three
items that off-limits are height, use, and density. Mr. Teerlink explained that an irrigation lateral
runs parallel to Highland Drive.

Commissioner Lloyd moved to adjourn the Work Session. Commissioner Banks seconded the
motion. The motion passed with the unanimous consent of the Commission.

2.      Residential Subdivision – “Holladay Commons” Concept – 2382, 2390-2392, 2386-
        2388 Murray Holladay Road (R-2-10).
Mr. Teerlink presented the staff report and stated that subdivision applications are reviewed by the
TRC before the Planning Commission reviews them in a public meeting. The TRC looks for
compliance with the underlying zoning requirements, utility availability and access, and any
relevant land use issues that need to be addressed. The TRC has reviewed the application for three
parcels that were developed with separate structures with multiple rental housing in them. The R-
2-10 is a duplex zone of 10,000 square feet. The applicant has proposed a concept plan that shows
the individual lots being split into 10,000 square-foot increments for residential dwellings. The
subdivision will be accessed by a private driveway along the west property line. The TRC
specifically reviewed the dedication required for Murray Holladay Road. The area must be deeded
to the public to allow for sidewalk, park strip, and curb and gutter can be correctly built. The TRC
recommended approval.

One of the property owners, Kevin Ludlow, gave his address as 9055 South 1300 East in Sandy
and described the benefits of the proposal as improving life safety issues and green space. Chair
Bradshaw liked the transition.

Chair Bradshaw opened the public hearing.

Holly Richards gave her address as 2470 Kentucky Avenue. She liked the project as it is presented
but was concerned about what will happen with the next project. She liked that they comply with
the zoning and ordinance and considered it to be an appropriate transition but was concerned about
what could happen going forward. She wanted all to be aware that there are two more steps
involved.

Mr. Teerlink confirmed that the height restriction is 32 feet. The proposed project will include
two stories.

Roger Reese gave his address as 4685 South Grosvenor Court and asked for clarification on the
actual square footage rather than the number of stories. He considered 32 feet to be very high and
close to what Murray Holladay Road is currently. He also asked for clarification on the access
road. He attended the initial meeting held on the property at which time there was talk of the
access road being constructed of a material that looks like grass but can support a full-sized fire
engine. He wanted more specifics on that.

Mr. Teerlink stated that they are looking for the minimum required road widths to provide access
for fire trucks. The material must be minimum aggregate and the UFA has very rarely if ever,
approved any material other than asphalt. It was clarified that the height allowance is 32 feet to
the ridge. The same rules apply to the neighbors to the east. Mr. Ludlow noted that asphalt is the
only material that has been approved by the UFA.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

3.      Subdivision Plat Amendment – “Clarenden Woods” – 2606 East Casto Lane (R-1-10
        Zone).
Mr. Teerlink presented the staff report and stated that the boundaries of subdivisions are
established by the Planning Commission who is the land use authority for the City. Amending
those boundaries requires a decision from the land use authority. In this case, a property owner
within the subdivision has acquired property outside of the boundary. The City is comprised of
metes and bounds property and subdivided lots of record. Often there are subdivisions that directly
abut older pieces of ground. In this case, the property owner is looking to acquire and add to his
backyard in the form of a property that has been developed next door. To do so, he needs to amend
the subdivision plat boundaries. Lot 4 of the Clarenden Woods project is the subject property.
The ground the applicant has acquired immediately abuts the plat. It is proposed that Lot 4 acquire
a strip of ground from the original parcel of property and leave the remaining as-is for a single-
family home. The result would be for the Clarenden Woods Subdivision to have five lots with the
size of Lot 4 increasing. Mr. Teerlink explained that the City Council is not a reviewing body on
this type of plat amendment because they are not amending any public roads.

Commissioner Banks asked if the large existing parcel requires a subdivision or lot line adjustment.
Mr. Teerlink explained that it is one lot and will not go through the subdivision process. He
indicated that the request does involve a lot line adjustment application. However, because it
involves a plat boundary, the lot line adjustment must be reviewed by the Planning Commission.

Commissioner Banks indicated that he would recuse himself from the vote due to a conflict of
interest.

4.       Residential Subdivision – “Bermondsey Place PUD” Preliminary – 4545 South thru
         4551 South Highland Drive (R-2-10 Zone).
Mr. Teerlink presented the staff report and explained that subdivisions are reviewed in three stages
consisting of concept, preliminary, and final plat. Bermondsey Place received Concept Approval
on May 4, 2021. The applicant was now requesting preliminary review, which covers the
construction and development details on the site. Planned Unit Developments (“PUD”) typically
refer to subdivisions that have small lot sizes than the normal zone requires. The three items that
cannot be changed include height, use, and density. For that reason, concept approval is very
important. The TRC has verified that the request complies with the eight units that were approved
at the concept level but are requesting setbacks and lot sizes modified to accommodate the green
space area around the irrigation lateral. The lateral serves numerous properties north of the subject
property and it is important that the facility remain in place. The applicant has been in contact
with the irrigation company on the details and is proposing to create an open space area around
the irrigation lateral recognizing that it will take away from the setback and area that is available
for development. In order to propose those types of modifications, the open space criterion must
be weighed.

The applicant, Ashley Atkinson, gave her address as 9055 South 1300 East in Sandy. She
explained that the biggest reason for seeking a PUD is to provide a nicer streetscape since this area
