38          Animal Control and Code Enforcement Discussion
39
40   Chief Taylor discussed the promotion of Lieutenant John Arnoldsen who is currently assigned to
41   oversee animal control and code enforcement. Chief Taylor said they would like to add an
42   additional employee who could help with animal control and code enforcement duties. Goals
43   include improving standards and service to the community. While the partnership with
44   Stoneridge is valued, euthanization might be considered for animals incarcerated for too long.
45
46   Councilmember Buroker advocated for hiring the additional animal/code enforcement officer
47   before the new budget year. Councilmembers were all in agreeance.
48
49   Councilmember McDougal recognized the increasing number of animals in the community,
50   which may lead to a busier workload for code enforcement. He suggested establishing clear
51   expectations for property maintenance in Riverton in order to prevent further dilapidation and

 1   associated costs in the future. Additionally, he addressed the need to consider euthanasia as an
 2   option for animals that have been abandoned or left behind, due to the growing animal
 3   population. While preserving animals is important, he acknowledged that euthanasia could be a
 4   humane option in certain circumstances.
 5
 6   Councilmember Buroker wanted the minutes to reflect that Stoneridge is an advocate for the
 7   animal and they do everything to preserve and care for the animals.
 8
 9   Mayor Staggs said the arrangement with Stoneridge was established after moving away from
10   Salt Lake County's services due to poor coverage. He highlighted the clinic's capacity to care for
11   20 to 30 cats and dogs monthly and praised their efforts in facilitating adoptions. He noted that
12   while there have been occasional spikes in animal intake, overall numbers have remained
13   steady. He commended the clinic's 24/7 availability and comprehensive services, emphasizing
14   the cost savings and minimal euthanizations under the arrangement.

16            Review Volunteer Committees
17
18   Mayor Staggs said the Council had expressed a desire to review the descriptions of various
19   volunteer committees, as well as assess the status of committee members and any vacancies
20   or open positions. The committees can be accessed on the City's website under the
21   government tab. These committees were established to encourage citizen involvement, aligning
22   with the City's priorities outlined in the 2018 strategic planning. Committee members were noted
23   to be fully staffed, with the exception of the Historic Preservation Committee, which currently
24   has three vacancies.
25
26   The discussion shifted to the Emergency Preparedness Committee, which works to enhance
27   community emergency preparedness plans based on threats identified in the hazard mitigation
28   plan. The committee promotes safe neighborhoods, family preparedness, provides training, and
29   solicits resident feedback. It was noted that the committee is fully appointed with seven
30   members, and Jason Jones, serves as the staff liaison.
31
32   The Events and Inclusion Committee focused on providing recommendations to City staff
33   regarding events, including new events, advertising strategies, event locations, and fostering
34   inclusivity within the community.
35
36   Sheril Garn, Events and Operations Director, said the Events and Inclusion Committee has
37   undergone a transition and is now known as the Riverton Community Arts Committee, focusing
38   on arts-related initiatives. Scott Reed, who previously chaired the Events and Inclusion
39   Committee, now serves as the president of Riverton Community Arts. Under his leadership,
40   efforts have been made to bring together various art organizations and collaborate with entities
41   like the Riverton Arts Council and the jazz band to develop new events and initiatives.
42
43   Mayor Staggs explained that the Historic Preservation Committee aims to identify, preserve,
44   protect, and enhance historic buildings, sites, and monuments within Riverton. Currently
45   composed of seven members, the committee has three openings. Councilmembers are
46   encouraged to recommend interested individuals to fill the vacancies.
47
48   Mayor Staggs said the Parks, Recreation, and Trails Committee focuses on matters related to
49   parks, recreational facilities, and trail development within Riverton. Currently, the committee is
50   fully staffed and has been involved in reviewing conceptual designs for new parks, including a
51   10-acre park and a larger regional park under the power corridor.

10   Council/City Manager Reports

12   Craig Giles, City Manager, explained that utility shut-offs, which had been paused since July,
13   will resume this month, starting on the March 27.

15   Mayor Staggs raised the topic of installing gates at the entrance to the parking lot of the Main
16   City Park off of 12800 South. The estimated cost for this project is around $18,000. The
17   proposal stemmed from concerns expressed by residents living on that street, as it has been a
18   source of significant police department involvement.
19
20   Chief Taylor shared an update on recent incidents involving the residents of the area in
21   question. Last night, around 10:00 PM, Chief Taylor received a call from a resident reporting
22   about 22 trucks parked in the parking area, prompting the dispatch of officers to address the
23   issue. He believes that installing gates could alleviate the impact experienced by the residents,
24   and suggested that the police department could lock the gates at night, with the parks and
25   recreation department responsible for opening them the following day.
26
27   Councilmember McDougal was supportive of installing the gate. Councilmember Buroker asked
28   if they could wait 30 days and try other avenues to take care of this problem. Councilmember
29   Haymond and Councilmember McCay did not like the idea of limiting resident’s access to the
30   park by installing the gates.

15            he deems appropriate
16
17   Mr. Lethbridge said the current zoning for the Mascaro property is planned commercial center,
18   which aligns with the designation of commercial regional in the general plan.
19
20   Craig Calvert noted that an appraisal of the property was conducted in March of 2023. Before
21   any decision to sell or liquidate the property is made, there will be a review of this previous
22   appraisal. The surplus property process can be executed in two ways. If there are interested

20            Resolution No. 24-36 – Authorizing the Filing of a Complaint to Take Property
21            through Eminent Domain
22
23   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained the property is located on 1300 West, south of a
24   construction firm. The resolution aimed to authorize eminent domain action to acquire a portion
25   of the property for constructing a sidewalk along 1300 West due to safety concerns. He
26   explained that despite attempts at negotiation, an agreement with the property owner had not
27   been reached. The purpose of the eminent domain action was solely to obtain the necessary
28   right-of-way for the sidewalk construction. The City had complied with Utah law by providing the
29   property owner with information about their rights, including the right to receive an appraisal. He
30   said the option of reaching a settlement with the property owner was still preferred, but the
31   resolution was deemed necessary to avoid prolonged delays.
32
33   Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 24-36 –
34   Authorizing the Filing of a Complaint to Take Property through Eminent Domain.
35   Councilmember Buroker SECONDED the motion. Mayor Pro Tem McCay called for discussion
36   on the motion. Mayor Pro Tem McCay called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows:
37   Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, and McDougal-yes,. The motion passed unanimously.
