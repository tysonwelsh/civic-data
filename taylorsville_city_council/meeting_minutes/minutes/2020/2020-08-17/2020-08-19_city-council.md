1.      WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

Chair Meredith Harker called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and welcomed those in
attendance. A roll call was conducted wherein all councilmembers were present.

        1.1      Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Vice Chair Christopherson
                 (Opening Ceremonies for September 2, 2020 to be arranged by
                 Councilmember Burgess)

Vice Chair Christopherson directed the Pledge of Allegiance before taking a moment to say how
confident he was that he was leaving the City in good hands. He believed Taylorsville was moving
forward with amazing leaders and staff in place and he had enjoyed being associated with good
people who had good hearts.

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

Mayor Kristie Overson reported that the working group formed to prepare the City to have its own
police department had already been very productive. Under the guidance of Jay Ziolkowski, a
timeline and fact sheet had been prepared and both would soon be published on the City’s website
and on social media. Mr. Ziolkowski had a good meeting with Unified Police and would work
with that entity to negotiate a fair and amicable departure from UPD.

As Chair of the Unified Fire Service Area, Mayor Overson had met with the rest of the board
earlier in the week. At the meeting, the board adopted a policy regarding member cities who
wished to withdraw from the organization. The timing was perfect as Riverton and Herriman were
both leaving the UFSA. They would still be part of the Unified Fire Authority but would simply
have a different funding mechanism.

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints had recently released a rendering of the future
Taylorsville temple. The Mayor felt it was a good representation of the City’s pioneer heritage.
A small ground-breaking would take place in October.

A ribbon-cutting ceremony had been held the previous week for the new 7-11 located across from
Taylorsville High School. It was owned by Taylorsville residents Cliff and Chelsea Anderson.

Mayor Overson reported that dirt would be turning in front of City Hall the following week as the
new City Center expanded beyond city hall and the performing arts center currently under
construction. Speaking of city hall, the east end of the 2nd floor would be undergoing some
renovations in order to ensure that employees could maintain safe social distancing. City
administration was also examining how city business was being conducted, ensuring it was safe
and appropriate for residents who came to city hall. The reception area on the west side of the 2nd
floor would be made safer and touchless doors would be installed to the exterior of the building as
well. The Mayor hoped the work would be completed by the end of the year.

The first Youth Council meeting of the new school year had taken place earlier in the day. Mayor
Overson stated that she and Youth Council Coordinator Kris Heineman were excited to recruit for
the Youth Council. It was unclear what activities the group would be involved in since COVID
was still an issue, but she looked forward to exploring the possibilities. She also asked that anyone
who knew of high schoolers who were interested in government and leadership please share the
word about this opportunity.

Since the pandemic began, Taylorsville had distributed 8,000 face masks to underserved
community members and small businesses in the city. Fifty gallons of sanitizer had also been
given out. Mayor Overson was grateful the city had those supplies and that it was able to share
them with those in need.

The following day would mark the beginning of the sixth special legislative session of the year.
John Hiskey was keeping a close eye on the CARES Act and other COVID related issues including
Salt Lake County’s Small Business Impact grant. The County had received 23 applications for
financial assistance from Taylorsville businesses which totaled $432,000. Twelve of those
applications were approved, the total of which was $254,000. Mayor Overson mentioned that
there were still some avenues for small business assistance.

In closing, the Mayor expressed her very best wishes to Vice Chair Christopherson as he and his
family began their new adventure outside Taylorsville. She described him as a fierce advocate for
not only District No. 3 but for economic development throughout the City. She also pointed out
that he had been a champion of public safety and that it was under his leadership that the council

voted to support a resolution advocating for improving public safety in Taylorsville, including
increased officer wages. It was in great part due to his efforts that the City was now in the process

        1.3      Citizen Comments

Chair Meredith Harker invited any individuals interested in making public comment about any
topic to fill out a form and step forward to be heard. Although there was no one physically present
who wished to comment, two emailed comments had been received and were read into the record.
The first was submitted by Corey Tenney who wrote, “I would like to officially request that the
council engage UTOPIA for a new cost study and present it to the public. I know that this was
done many years ago, but things have changed, and we need better options. Many other cities are
now doing UTOPIA fiber and depending on cost, I believe it makes sense.”

Christopher Lloyd submitted the following comment: “We have few options for high speed internet
here in Taylorsville. My only choices are Xfinity or Xfinity which leads to major price gouging
since there’s no real competition. Xfinity also imposes data limits on their service unless you’re
willing to pay an egregious amount of money monthly. We are tired of being limited to one service
provider here in Taylorsville and need more options. Utopia is our gateway to better connectivity,
better services, and prices providing more competition to benefit our citizens. Utopia has also
proven time and again that it can provide high quality fiber to other cities here in Utah. We should
take the lead and allow them to build their infrastructure here in Taylorsville.”

There were no additional citizen comments, and Chair Harker closed the citizen comment period.

3.      REPORTS

        3.2      Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling Report – Pam Roberts

Ms. Roberts stepped to the podium and expressed her appreciation to Vice Chair Christopherson
for his leadership as the Chair for the WFWR Board. She stated he had done a great job of
representing Taylorsville and also the District as a whole and in understanding efficiencies of scale.
She thanked him on behalf of WFWR and presented him with a token of the agency’s appreciation.

Ms. Roberts then provided a brief overview of the modifications made to the year’s Area Clean
Up program and some data comparing this year to the previous year.

Vice Chair Christopherson asked Ms. Roberts to explain the correlation between the cost savings
realized as a result of these program changes and the ability to defer a rate increase for waste
removal services. She responded that further information would be provided at a September 9th
meeting but that $268,000 had been saved thus far that year because of changes in this program as
well as other cost saving measures such as reduced labor hours, reduced fuel and vehicle
maintenance costs.

        3.1      Unified Police Department Officer Awards – Chief Tracy Wyant

Chief Wyant asked Lt. Brady Cottam to join him at the podium as he presented three awards.

The Precinct Chief’s Award for July 2020 was presented to Anthony Martinez for the
professionalism, patience and problem-solving skills he displayed in his handling of a domestic
aggravated assault case. Officer Martinez’ effort resulted in charges being filed against the suspect
with no additional investigation needed on the part of detectives.

Steven Ivan was given the Officer of the Month award for July. Chief Wyant stated that Officer
Ivan’s sergeant described him as was one of the most selfless officers he had worked with in a
long time. He performed excellent investigations, wrote comprehensive reports and often stepped
up to fill in wherever needed. The way Officer Ivan handled himself on extremely difficult calls
was a testament to his character.

Det. Scott Lloyd stepped forward to help Chief Wyant recognize Vice Chair Christopherson for
his steadfast service and support of law enforcement from 2013-2020. Chief Wyant stated that the
Vice Chair’s proven leadership and resolve would pay dividends for generations to come. Chief
Wyant and Det. Lloyd stressed how indebted the UPD officers assigned to the Taylorsville precinct
were to Brad Christopherson.

       3.3       Parks and Recreation Committee Report – Jared Smith

Mr. Smith explained that he was currently the chair for the Parks and Recreation committee and
listed the other committee members for the council’s benefit. He described some of the activities
that the committee had been involved in 2020, some of which had been delayed due to the
pandemic. One hundred fifty people participated in a virtual race that had recently been held. Mr.
Smith stated that he had seen more people utilizing the parks and trail system during the pandemic
than he had previously and he hoped the City would continue to improve those amenities so that
residents could recreate in Taylorsville instead of going to other cities to do so.

Councilmember Cochran inquired about the number of residents who participated in the “movie
in the park” events. Mr. Smith responded that it appeared to be over 100 people for one such event
and about 140 for the evening that included the live band De Novo.
