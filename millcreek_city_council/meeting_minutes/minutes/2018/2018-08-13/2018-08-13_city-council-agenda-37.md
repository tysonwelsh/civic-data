3. Planning Matters
3.1 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-46, Amending Chapters 19.44,
19.60, and 19.77 of Millcreek Code with Respect to Building Height and Height
Transition Requirements in Multifamily Residential and Commercial Zones, and
Standards for Reductions in Required Setbacks
Francis Lilly said the Council has seen this ordinance before. The ordinance limits the
height of any commercial and multifamily building to 30 feet if the building is within 100
feet of a single family residential zone. Lilly said the Planning Commission brought the
recommendation to the Council to eliminate the option of reducing a setback to 15 feet.
The Council directed Lilly to go back to the Planning Commission to come up with
language that conditioned setback reductions so that they were not granted automatically
by virtue of enhanced landscaping. Lilly said the Planning Commission came up with a
list of 8 items that would be applied as performance standards:

1. The granting of the setback reduction will not be materially detrimental to the
public welfare or injurious to the property or improvements in the vicinity in which
the property is located.

2. The setback reduction is consistent with the policies and provision of the General
Plan and this Code.

3. Where applicable, the setback reduction results in a building site that
accommodates a wider buffer between the use and adjacent R-1 or R-2 zones than
what is required by this Title.

4. The setback reduction does not create a situation where the development will result
in an increase in the number of residential dwelling units than would occur if the
development was built without the reduction.

5. The setback reduction does not create a situation where the proposed development
will cause objectionable noise, odors, trespass lighting, or similar adverse impacts
on adjacent properties.

6. The setback reduction does not create a situation where the proposed development
substantially reduces the amount of privacy that would be enjoyed by nearby
residents any more than would be available if the development was built without
the reduction.

7. The setback reduction does not create a situation where the proposed development
will create an obstruction of view in excess of two feet in height located within a
triangular area bounded by lines drawn from a point on the centerline of any

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 13 August 2018 Page 8 of 13

driveway, set back fifteen feet from the front property line to points on the
property line at the street in front of the property line teen feet either side of both
edges of the driveway.

8. The setback reduction does not create a situation where it is reasonably anticipated
that the proposed development will result in vehicle stacking on a public right-of-
way where that right-of-way intersects with the driveway of the proposed
development.

Mayor Silvestrini asked if there were members of the public that would like to address

be three stories tall.

Mayor Silvestrini thanked Francis Lilly for going back to the Planning Commission and

Council Member Jackson moved to adopt Ordinance 18-46, Amending Chapters 19.44,

4.5 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 18-40, Approving Entry into the
Central Wasatch Commission Interlocal Agreement

Mayor Silvestrini said the Council previously approved entering into the Central Wasatch
Commission but the resolution did not contain the details of the interlocal agreement.
This resolution enters Millcreek into the agreement and appoints Mayor Silvestrini as the
Millcreek representative. Council Member Jackson asked what the fee is to join. Mayor
Silvestrini said it has not been determined but it is usually done by population. He said
there are 10 board members.

Mayor Silvestrini asked if there was public comment. A resident asked for a brief
overview of the Central Wasatch Commission. Mayor Silvestrini explained the
Commission to the audience. He said this started with the Mountain Accord. Stakeholders
came together who had an in interest in the canyons. They established an agreement
called the Mountain Accord (mountainaccord.org), and it recommended a federal
protection area, adoption of about 6,000 new acres of wilderness, changed the boundaries
of wilderness units, arranged for land exchanges and water sheds protections, involves
transportation in the canyons, and the like. The Commission manages the Accord. The
Commission is comprised of Salt Lake County, Salt Lake City, Sandy, Cottonwood
Heights, Alta, Park City, Summit County, UDOT, UTA, and Millcreek.
