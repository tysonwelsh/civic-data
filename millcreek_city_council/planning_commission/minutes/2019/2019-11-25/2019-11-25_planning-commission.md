1. Continuing Business
1.1 Consideration of ZT-19-003, Adoption of the City Center Overlay Zone Planner:
Francis Lilly.
Francis Lilly explained that the purpose of the meeting was for reviewing the Planning
Commission’s draft for the City Center Overlay Zone. He said the City was creating a code
compliance checklist for every building type contemplating in the code. In the CCOZ there
will be a required site plan review whether the proposed building is part of a conditional use
permit or not. The required fee will be $455 and there will be a review process. Staff will
review the application item by item from the City Code. This form will be required to be
filled out by developers. Mr. Lilly believed this would ensure all obligations and
requirements would be met. Then the document would be presented to the Planning
Commission.

Mr. Lilly edited the proposed ordinance section on commercial recreation by adding
language from City Code that it did not include temporary and seasonal licenses or large
scales events as defined in Title 5 of City Code. Vice Chair Stephens stated that if the
commercial recreation definition is all risk and all is allowed except for items that are
excluded, that perhaps the definition should say commercial recreation does not preclude
seasonal and temporary licenses.

Mr. Lilly explained staff would do a 13-item checklist on development plans even if the use
was a permitted use. He suggested that a conditional use permit shall be required for any
building that meets any of the following criteria, “a total square footage greater than 25,000
square feet, and or, a ground floor footprint greater than 15,000 square feet, and or, a height
greater than 36 feet, not including any element described as a height exception in Table
19.73-4.”

John Janson asked the Planning Commission if the height requirement should be lowered.
Any application that comes in that triggers any one of the criteria would require a conditional
use permit. Commissioner Reid asked how developers would react to the conditional use
process. Mr. Janson replied that the development community does not usually like the
conditional use process, however, development was not as discretionary as it once was.
Largely, the decision would be left up to the City. Mr. Lilly stated that the public and
stakeholders expect a public process. Mr. Lilly said that a larger building with larger parking
demand and multiple uses should have a conditional use. Commissioner Reid said she did not
want to make the requirements too stringent.

Commissioner LaMar asked about detrimental effects and the wording of “that may need to
be addressed.” Commissioner LaMar said the wording was not strong enough since the
problem would need to be solved not just addressed. Mr. Lilly stated he would make an
addition that if staff notes a detrimental effect then it will need to be mitigated or resolved.
Mr. Lilly asked if the Commission was fine with the conditional use thresholds for height and
size. Commissioner LaMar said he would prefer the thresholds be lowered. Vice Chair
Stephens asked for the sentiment of the Planning Commissioners and asked why the relevant
language was duplicated in the draft. Mr. Janson stated it was there to make the requirements
very clear. Mr. Lilly said the section is designed to show how to make an application. Vice
Chair Stephens asked to review the categories by district. Vice Chair Stephens then asked if
the requirement for a maximum 2,500 square foot limit on building size in the neighborhood
district was too small. Mr. Janson replied that it applies to a small office in a building. The
intent for a neighborhood district is more residential. Mr. Lilly explained that neighborhood
retail has a limit of 5,000 square feet of space. Commissioner LaMar asked about size of
offices. Mr. Lilly explained this is by use. Commissioner LaMar clarified asking if an
individual had a storefront and a call center then the call center would have to go elsewhere if
it was 5,000 square feet. Vice Chair Stephens asked if the 2,500 feet is aggregate or only for
ground floor use. Mr. Janson replied that the square footage limits are based on the proposed
use. Vice Chair Stephens would prefer to increase the 2,500 square foot limit. Mr. Janson
stated the intent is to keep the space small in a neighborhood district. Mr. Lilly responded
that in the C-1 zone 5,000 square feet is the limit. Commissioner Booth thought some might
slip through. Mr. Lilly said that any office use would have to be limited to 2,500 square feet.
Mr. Janson stated the City is trying to encourage the larger offices to Mill Center or

Commissioner Mumford asked for a definition of non-stealth telecommunication facilities.
Mr. Lilly explained that those are older style cell towers that are not designed to be styled to
look like something else. Commissioner Mumford asked if Millcreek would want to develop
their own communication system at a future date. Mr. Lilly said it would need to be behind a
parapet or be screened in some way if the City moved to that point.

Mr. Lilly moved to page 6, concerning residential uses. Residential uses are permitted by
district and within the building types specified in section 19.73.050 (14) except that mixed
use buildings located within 300 feet of the northwest corner of Highland Drive on 3300
South and northeast corner of 1300 East and 3300 South. Property owners would prefer 250
feet. Mr. Lilly explained that if you are 250 feet from a corner then no more than 25% of the
building could be used for residential uses. Mr. Lilly explained concerning rooftop gardens
that they will not be, “allowed within 100 feet of a R-1 or R-2 zone boundary in the
Neighborhood or Market Place districts, unless the rooftop garden, patio or amenity space is
not located above the highest occupied floor of a building, or is obstructed from view from
the zone boundary by at least 20 feet of occupied floor space.” John Janson stated that the
surrounding zonings to these areas were not often a R-1 or R-2.

Commissioner Reid asked if there were code requirements for parking structures due to
safety concerns and restricted visibility. Mr. Janson stated there were no ordinances in
Millcreek about parking structures and lighting or visibility. Commissioner Reid said it was a
safety concern, however, if the structures were well lit with clear elevators then they would
be safer. Mr. Lilly replied that there was an idea called crime prevention through
environmental design. There are four principles within this school of thought; natural
surveillance, natural access control through limited and obvious points of entrance and exist,
territorial reinforcement by creating safe spaces by eliminating blind corners, and finally
maintenance. The City can incorporate these ideas in the conditional use ordinance.
Commissioner Sieber stated that parking garages in downtown Salt Lake City were brighter
due to brighter colors. Mr. Lilly said the City would have a major parking garage at some
point. Commissioner LaMar asked if the parking structure could be underneath the common
area. Mr. Lilly replied any parking garage was subject to fault line requirements.

Mr. Lilly said building heights were still a concern. Vice Chair Stephens said the
Commission could make a sub-recommendation to the City Council that height should not
exceed 50 feet at Tuxedo Junction. Mr. Lilly explained that the challenge was in height
transitions and proximity to view corridors in residential areas. Mr. Lilly said that
development in the area was the end-goal and leaving the height the same would limit new
development. Mr. Janson and Commissioner Mumford agreed that the area would be a
gateway to Millcreek. Vice Chair Stephens asked if the Commission would recommend the
Tuxedo Junction area to not exceed 50 feet. Commissioner LaMar said that 50 feet was better
than 74 feet. Commissioner LaMar asked if the height of a 48-foot minimum could be
reduced to a smaller amount for the 48-74 feet area. Commissioner LaMar worried about a
canyon feel and a minimum of 40 feet instead of 48 feet could alleviate that. Vice Chair
Stephens asked if a 50-foot maximum height was okay for Tuxedo Junction. Commissioner
LaMar said making the minimum 40 or 36 feet was better since it did not force a potential

Mr. Lilly said that height restrictions would not allow structures except towers and solar
panels and these cannot be located closer than 10 feet to the edge of the building. The
building and fire code have rigorous standards about putting pools and other things next to
the edge of a building. Mr. Lilly said there would have to be a 10-foot setback from the edge
of the building. Commissioner LaMar worried about turning a 74-foot building into an 84-
foot building. Mr. Lilly said that rooftop gardens, patios, and amenity space are, “not allowed
within 100’ feet of a R-1 or R-2 zone boundary in the Neighborhood or Market Place
districts, unless the rooftop garden, patio or amenity space is not located above the highest

20 feet of occupied floor space.” Mr. Lilly explained if it’s a 74-foot building that has a pool
then there would have to be a wall constructed however, the wall would have to be ten feet

space, it would be for recreation. Commissioner LaMar said that if people are using the space
and it was not a mechanical part of the building then the building was too tall for the area.
The mass of the building has therefore increased. Mr. Lilly said that if the building was
setback 20 feet from the road and the amenities on the roof are set ten feet back you could

put a pool on top of their building then they need to include the height of the safety wall in
their plans and then bring the height of the building down to 74 feet to stay within the
required height. Mr. Janson clarified that they wished to emphasize using the roof tops as
small park spaces. Mr. Lilly stated that a roof-top garden was designed to give a view and
was not perceived from the ground-floor. Vice Chair Stephens stated that these buildings
would be subject to the conditional use site plan requirement.

Francis Lilly began a discussion on trees. Mr. Marv Poulson handed out a sheet to the
Commission on recommended tree types. Commissioner Booth stated the that Commission
could get bogged down discussing trees in depth at that time. Vice Chair Stephens asked if
Mr. Poulson had more input on trees. Mr. Poulson replied that he had more to input.
Commissioner Lar Mar suggested that the code should be more open regarding trees and
experts should pick the trees later. Commissioner Reid said she advocated for a consistent
look; however, she was still worried about disease wiping out the trees if they were the same
type. Mr. Mr. Lilly stated there was a proposal for urban forestry policy looking at canopy
requirements and how trees are maintained, the tree design should be consistent in a block by
block basis. Commissioner Booth said that the process for determining trees should be
deliberate and open to a public hearing. Mr. Janson stated there was a tree planting plan,
however, the choice does not need to be made now. Mr. Lilly stated that the City can use
wording saying to stakeholders planting trees must use a tree on the approved list.
Commissioner LaMar agreed, this would give the City the opportunity to adopt a tree plan
for certain blocks.

Mr. Lilly said that stepbacks are for street facing facades and not the back of buildings. Vice
Chair Stephens noted Russ Sorenson’s application and how stepbacks solve issues there. Mr.
Janson said they added on page 23 a 2-foot variation for the back of buildings. Mr. Lilly went
to page 10 for the wording on stepbacks. Mr. Lilly quoted, “For any building over 40 feet in
height or for any development on properties fronting on the Millcreek Common, 33" South,
Richmond and/or with frontage on Highland Drive, a twenty-foot stepback of the building
facade, facing the street, is required between bottom of the first story and the top of the third
story and upper stories. This stepback shall be segmented, such as, a ten foot initial stepback
between the first and second floors and then an additional ten foot stepback between the
second and third floors or shall create a stepback of 20 feet between the first and third floors,
that should be utilized as a usable space for residents or businesses within the building.”
Vice Chair Stephens asked that if a building height over 40 feet would trigger the stepback
requirements. Mr. Lilly replied yes. Commissioner Reid said that if two buildings were back-
to-back from each other than a stepback was unnecessary. Vice Chair Stephens said the way
it was worded then step backs would be required for a building over 40 feet on the street-
side. Vice Chair Stephens clarified if the wording should be, “facing the street”, or “facing
any street.”” Commissioner LaMar stated that he preferred having more stepbacks than not
having them, although being right up against another building did not make sense to trigger a
stepback.

Mr. Lilly said that if there was a chance for parking next to the open space, the parking
would need to meet the standards in the CCOZ Ordinance. The designers are moving away
from a one-way loop road in the open space. Mr. Lilly quoted, “Opposing residential
bedroom windows located on building walls in adjacent buildings, where such buildings are
closer than 20 feet from each other, shall offset the window a minimum of 5 feet and provide
window blinds for each opposing window, or reduce the opacity of the window to prevent
one side from viewing the other.” Windows on building plans would be offset when they are
closer than 20 feet. Buildings balcony’s must be ten feet from each other. Mr. Lilly said that
stucco was prohibited for use as materials on any facade visible from a right-of-way. Mr.
Lilly said on page 25, 10 feet was the minimum rear yard setback except with abutting an R-
1, R-2, or R-4-8.5 zone, then the setback was 25 feet. Mr. Janson added that guest parking
does not include the driveway.

Francis Lilly stated that if there was a small wireless facility in the right-of-way then there
was city code governing the placement. They must be placed on utility poles that are like
streetlights. Mr. Janson reminded the Commission that the only poles will be streetlight poles
since utilities are underground. Mr. Lilly said small wireless facilities could be required to be
placed on existing poles. Commissioner Reid asked if the streetlight language was changed.
Mr. Lilly and Mr. Janson replied yes. Commissioner LaMar questioned drive-through’s for
food or beverage service as opposed to a restaurant. Commissioner LaMar said the terms
“food or beverage service” was more absolute.

Jake Christensen, 3161 Walnut Park Cove., stated in the 48-74-foot zone on 3300 S. if the
setback was only 10 feet, then you can build another 70-foot building next to it with another
10-foot setback for a total setback of 20 feet. This would limit sunlight to lower residences |

Mr. Janson stated there would be buildings with shadows and by increasing setbacks it would
reduce what could happen in the intensity of the buildings. On lower buildings, a setback like
that was not required. Commissioner LaMar asked what the current minimum setback was.
Mr. Lilly said that if two buildings abut, they must be separated by ten feet on each property
for a total of 20 feet. Mr. Lilly stated that if the buildings have a balcony then the setback
begins from the edge of the balcony and they must be 20 feet apart. Commissioner LaMar
said they want to make this a nice, livable area for the residents who will live there.
Commissioner LaMar added he would like more space than the 20-foot minimum.
Commissioner Booth stated that extra space may be nice, however 20 feet was enough
because density was the goal. Density helps decrease building costs and rent costs.
Commissioner Sieber asked that there be a graduated scale for the setback based on the
height of the building. Commissioner LaMar asked for more separation when buildings are
taller. Commissioner Reid said she was fine with 20 feet.

Vice Chair Stephens said that in their recommendation to City Council, the Planning
Commission could instruct staff to review increasing setbacks for taller buildings. Mr. Lilly
agreed to do so.

LaMar voted no. The motion passed 5-1.
