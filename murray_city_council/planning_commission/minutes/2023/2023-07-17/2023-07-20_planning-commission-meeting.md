Hoggan Haven – 17 East 6150 South – Project #22-072
Final Subdivision Approval One-Year Time Extension

Ms. Nixon presented the application from Suzanne Hogan for a one-year extension, for the
preliminary and final subdivision. The Planning Commission originally granted preliminary and
final flag lot subdivision on July 21, 2022. City code requires that when final subdivision
approval is granted that the plat must be recorded within one year. There is a clause in the
code that allows for the applicant to request a one-year extension. Ms. Hogan has requested
this because of the market and interest rate increases. To reiterate last year’s application, both
lots exceed the requirements of the R-1-8 zone, as far as area and setbacks. If the Planning
Commission grants the approval, the plat must be recorded by July 20, 2024. The same
conditions would apply if the extension is granted.

Chair Pehrson invited the applicant up to speak, asking her if she would be able to comply with
the same conditions as the original agreement.

Suzanne Hoggan, the applicant, responded that yes, she will be able to comply with all the
conditions in the original agreement.

Vice Chair Milkavich asked what happens if they aren’t able to start the project within the
coming year. Ms. Nixon responded that they would just have to reapply for subdivision approval.

Public comments were open and closed with no comments being made.

Commissioner Lowry moved to approve the one-year time extension for subdivision approval for
the Hoggan Haven Flag Lot Subdivision of the property, located at 17 East 6150 South, subject
to the eleven conditions:

1. The applicant shall meet all requirements of the City Engineer, including the following:
   a. Meet City subdivision requirements.
   b. Provide grading, drainage and utility plan.

   d. Meet City utility standards, provide standard front rear and side yard PUE’s on the lot.
   e. Obtain a City Excavation Permit for work within City roadways.

Mercantile Towns – 861 East Winchester Street & 6520, 6550, 6580 South 900 East – Project
#23-038 & 23-039 - Final PUD Subdivision Review

Mr. Smallwood presented the application for Scott Verhaaren from The Boyer Company, who is
requesting final PUD subdivision approval to develop the subject property into 109 townhomes.
Mr. Smallwood addressed items from the preliminary review regarded fencing elevations, the
sidewalk, setback modifications and height. This is a 109-townhome development on nine
acres. The layout has not changed in the final subdivision. There was only one change in the
zoning standards. The front setback to accommodate the additional two feet for the sidewalk
was accommodated. They only needed to reduce the setback by an additional foot. They were
able to move things around so that it stayed within 19 feet. Parking 273 stalls are required. They
provided 407, which puts them at 3.7 stalls per unit. There are 2-foot breaks in the fence about
every 65 feet and provides a significant landscape barrier between those. In the preliminary
discussions, they felt that six feet was more appropriate for backyards. The fencing around the
remaining property is eight feet. The heights of the buildings are proposed to be less than the 30
feet, except for one of the buildings, which is exactly 30 feet. This was a requirement during the
preliminary review because of Fire Department requirements. The applicant addressed issues
with the landscaping grade.

Staff recommends that the Planning Commission grant final PUD Subdivision approval for
Mercantile Towns on the properties addressed 861 East Winchester Street & 6520, 6550, 6580
South 900 East subject to conditions.

Chair Pehrson asked if the applicant had reviewed the nine conditions and if they are able to

GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT & ZONE MAP AMENDMENT

Justin Lang – 770 &780 West 5300 South and 845 West Clover Meadow Drive – Project #23-
070 - General Plan Amendment from Low-Density Residential to Medium-Density Residential

Mr. Smallwood presented the application from Justin Lang for a general plan and zoning map
amendment for the properties addressed 777 & 780 West 5300 South and 845 West Clover
Meadow Drive. It’s currently zoned for single-family residential with a minimum 8,000 square
foot lots. The request is to change the zoning from R-1-8 to R-M-15, which is a multifamily zone
with a base density of 12 units per acre. There are five initiatives in the 2017 General Plan.
Initiative three is about livable and vibrant neighborhoods, which states things that are needed
for success – buffering, visual and auditory between adjacent uses. Staff feels that any
proposed project here would provide a sound barrier to the north. The goal is to create housing
in a more gradient way, as opposed to clustering all the apartments and houses together. The
future land use map indicates that this is to remain low-density residential; however, the General
Plan is a fluid document and was evaluated at the market conditions at the time and what we
expected. That doesn't mean that it cannot be changed or reevaluated. Mr. Lang is requesting
medium-density residential, to provide more of a buffer area. The applicable objectives here are
to encourage revitalization along key transportation corridors in the core of the city. This has
been a vacant piece of property along 5300 South and has very little access around it.
Surprisingly, Mr. Lang was able to get an access approval from UDOT to develop this property.
Objective nine is provide a mix of housing options and residential zones to meet a diverse range
of needs related to lifestyle demographics, including age, household size and income. This
relates back to integrating additional housing types. Objective ten promotes a transition of
development patterns between commercial areas and stable residential neighborhoods.
Transitioning means having higher-density housing along commercial areas to help buffer that
and transition into more of a single-family neighborhood. It provides a bit of transition into a
lower-density area.

The last strategy is to support residential infill projects at a compatible scale. Objective three

allow for higher density or new moderate-income, residential development in commercial or
mixed-use zones near transit-investment corridors.

preventing travel in other directions.
