 PC 21-067 Legacy Landscape Shop [Design Review Permit] Jared Hymas/MTSE Holdings,
 authorized agent/owner, request a design review permit to build a new 4,200 SF warehouse
 building on a .5-acre lot at approximately 1221 West 2200 South in the Commercial (COM) zone;
 TIN 03-005-0019. (Woodruff Neighborhood)


STAFF: T. Rice reviewed the request for a design review permit for a new 4,200 square foot
shop/office building on a .5-acre vacant lot at 1221 W 2200 S in the Commercial zone. This
project meets the required lot coverage, building height, frontage, and setbacks. Condition
number 4 requires a performance landscape plan to be submitted for approval prior to the
issuance of the building permit. Condition Number 5 requires the east and west façades to have
an architectural break every 40 feet. There have been no public comments received, and staff is
recommending approval.

PROPONENT: Jared Hymas, owner of Legacy Landscape Shop, said that they need a bigger shop
space for their expanding business, and will add the offices in the future. There will be no retail from
this location.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Goodlander stated that it is a good project and she would approve
with the conditions as they are.

MOTION: Commissioner Goodlander moved to conditionally approve a design review permit for
PC 21-067 with the conditions and findings for approval as listed below. Commissioner Croshaw
seconded the motion.

1. All standard conditions of approval will be recorded with the Design Review and are available

2. No outdoor storage shall be permitted on site.

          b. Open Space and Useable Outdoor areas shall total a minimum of 20% of the project

9. Exterior lighting shall be concealed source, down-cast and shall not illuminate or cast light

 PC 21-070 Gateway Apartments [Design Review Permit] Jeff Douglas/Craig Adams, authorized
 agent/owner, request a design review permit to build a new 62 unit apartment complex located on
 a 2.07-acre lot at 721 South 100 West in the Mixed Residential High (MR-30) zone; TIN 02-063-
 0035. (Woodruff Neighborhood)

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a design review permit to construct a new 62-unit four-
story apartment building located at 721 South 100 West, just south of the Logan River and west
of the newly completed gateway office building. The project meets the required land use, density,
and setbacks. The submitted project shows the lot coverage at 26% and a building frontage that
equals approximately 50%. The building frontage does not meet the minimum of 60% as
submitted. The Planning Commission may use discretion as per LDC 17.43.080.C.5 and consider
the unique shape of the site due to the river and the proximity of the proposed 100 West road
extension and bridge in determining appropriate building frontage adjustments on this property.
The property owner will not be able to use the north corner of the property, this area will be used
for the new bridge and a switchback in the trail that will allow the option for trail users to go under
the bridge and continue east to Main Street and through the future underground tunnel, or traverse
up to the street and across the new bridge. Condition number 5 states that the trail will continue
south along the Logan River and shall be designed to be at least 10 feet wide. The trail will be
paved with concrete or asphalt and connect to the existing trail located at the southwest corner of
the property. The proposed parking plan is 12 stalls short of the 124 stalls that are required. The
alternate parking plan proposes the 12 stalls be shared with the office parking across the street.
This plan has been approved by the Director of Community Development. The project layout,
pedestrian circulation, and building height are all in compliance. Open space and landscaping are
conditioned for compliance.


Commissioner Lucero asked about the construction of 100 West Street and how it coincides with
this project. Bill Young, Logan City Engineer, said that they are currently bidding out the project.
R. Holley stated that the developer has a timeline of one year to obtain a building permit.

Commissioner Dickinson asked about getting a crosswalk connection to the shared parking across
the street. Mike DeSimone, Director of Community Development, stated that if it became an issue
in the future, Public Works would then investigate a crosswalk.

Commissioner Croshaw asked who owns the portion of the property where the switchback will be
and who will maintain the area. R. Holley said that the developer owns that portion, but the city
has an easement there and will develop the area with the new bridge and will also maintain it.

Commissioner Croshaw also mentioned that there does not seem to be a lot of open space. He
also asked if there would be on-street parking along 100 West. R. Holley said that the project has
more than the required amount of open space.

Commissioner Goodlander asked who will construct and maintain the trail. R. Holley said that the
construction of the trail will be a shared cost between Logan City and the developer. Maintenance
will be handled by the Parks and Rec department.

Kymber Housley, City Attorney, mentioned that Logan City cannot require a trail, this is something
that is handled outside of the planning commission through the RDA.

Commissioner Croshaw asked where the property line is along the river. R. Holley said it runs
along the top of the riverbank.

Commissioner Guth asked if condition number 5 is sufficient for the design of the trail. R. Holley
said that this is something for the developer to address. Additional horizontal space along the river
would be a benefit to everyone. K. Housley stated that this is more of a contractual issue and
should be worked out with the developer outside of the planning commission.

Commissioner Heare asked if there could be a parking buffer required to avoid trail obstruction.
R. Holley said this will be worked out in the contract between Logan City and the developer.

PROPONENT: Michael Jewell, said that the trail is a huge amenity for the apartment. He said that
they are happy to work with the city to keep the trail nice and clear for pedestrian travel.

Commissioner Lucero asked about the composition of the two and three bedrooms. M. Jewell said
there are 25 two bedrooms and 37 three bedrooms.

Commissioner Goodlander asked if a crosswalk to the alternate parking across the street can be
required. K. Housley said that cannot be required from the developer, it would need to be addressed
with Public Works.

PUBLIC: Frank Howe, Logan River Task Force Chairman, said that they commend the developer
for developing this trail, and how this portion will now connect the trail to run from Trapper Park to
100 East. He talked about part of the landscape and parking lot being within the critical lands overlay
zone. They would like to work with the developer on modifying their design to include widening and
terracing the flood plain to provide a beautiful outdoor space similar to The Riverwoods across Main
Street,


COMMISSION: Commissioner Dickinson asked for clarification on this being a riparian area. K.
Housley said this area does not have an overlay applied to it.

M. DeSimone said that the city has not adopted a critical area overlay map. The standards are
applied throughout the city per project. This site is not a riparian area, this is a previously disturbed
site, that was formerly developed by Ellis Equipment. As long as this project meets the riverbank
setback, it is compliant with the critical area component and the riparian area requirements do not
apply.

B. Young talked about the 100-year flood plain and how the riverbank on the East side of Main Street
had to be modified to prevent flooding. The river on the West side of Main Street is not the same, it
is very channelized, and the floodplain footprint is within the riverbank.

There was further discussion on the Riparian area, but it is considered previously disturbed and not
subject to the riparian requirements. The committee also talked about the state of the riverbank and
questioned if any vegetation or trees would be planted.

M. DeSimone stated that the vegetation between the trail and the river should be discussed between
the Logan River Task Force and the Logan City as the area in question is not on the developer’s
property.
