1.     Subdivision ordinance changes
       Mr. Brierley explained Staff is proposing some changes to the Subdivision Code in an
       effort to better align with State law, to clarify language, resolve discrepancies and to
       simplify the subdivision process. He stated the recent State legislature has adopted
       language which requires cities to designate a single administrative land use authority to
       review preliminary subdivisions. While it is unclear what this means, it may eliminate
       either the Planning Commission or Mayoral review of these. Chair Sandau expressed
       concern that the Planning Commission be the final review, and asked if their decision
       could be appealed to the elected official. Mr. Brierley responded if the applicant or other
       citizen desired, the appeal process would then go to the mayor.

       Mr. Brierley then indicated State law also allows the combination of subdivided lots
       without an amendment and plat if allowed by the City’s specific ordinances. While
       Ogden City has been doing these for many years, the process for such is not clearly
       stated in the ordinance and Weber County is now requiring a plat for these. He also
       cited other types of small subdivisions such a parcel boundary and a lot line adjustment
       which typically can be done at a staff level but now require notice to neighborhood
       residents and a public meeting. The current code also allows a small subdivision (under
       10 lots) to be reviewed solely by the Mayor, while if there is a street dedication, a two lot
       subdivision requires both Planning Commission and Mayoral approval. He also
       reviewed the amendment process as well as the establishment of single-lot subdivisions
       and stated it is anticipated this code would be reviewed as part of the regular meeting in
       May.

2.     Short term rentals in downtown
       Mr. Brierley indicated Visit Ogden has approached the City requesting short term rentals
       be allowed in the downtown area. They are finding there is inadequate hotel space in
       the area when events occur. While these are now regulated in residential zones, the
       Ordinance is silent for commercial and downtown zones, which makes them not allowed.
       He stated other overnight services such as a hotel or motel are permitted, and it would
       make sense for these to be allowed. He defined the types of overnight accommodations,
       stating short term rentals are limited to less than 30 days, while long-term rentals have
       no time limit but are longer than 30 days and hotel stay is limited to 90 days. There also
       is concern for contract employees to obtain overnight services for up to a six-month time
       period. The downtown code currently defines a hotel as having at least 16 units for
       overnight accommodation up to 90 days, but there are many loft apartments which are
       available for both long-term or short-term rental which need specific regulations. He
       showed examples of existing advertised short-term rentals, stating as they already exist,
       there should be consistent regulations. The City has now purchased a software program
       which is being used to identify vacation rentals and allowing better enforcement, but
       regulations need to be specified.

       Mr. Adam Nelson, Code Enforcement Manager, indicated these uses are better in
       commercial than in residential zones and much of the concern is to assure these are
       safe and meet current fire code regulations. While hotels have specific safety
       standards, vacation rentals are currently operating which may or may not be safe, and
       the same standards should be applicable to both. There also is concern with the ability
       of units within an apartment building used as vacation rentals, with Staff feeling these
       should not be allowed. Hotels will always have better management with a check-in
       counter and physical access, while much of the management for a short-term rental is
       via the internet and email/app technology, with no one responsible if problems occur.
       The number of guests in a hotel room also is regulated, which may or may not be the
       case for short-term rentals.

       Commission asked about the transient rental taxes for both short term rentals and
       hotel/motels. Mr. Brierley responded the tax for each use is identical, and applicable to
       all of these uses. Commissioner Schade asked if existing hotel owners/operators had
       been included in this conversation and whether their ability to operate effectively would
       be affected if more vacation rentals are allowed in the commercial areas. While they
       may be at capacity during events, there is concern their business could be affected
       throughout the year if there is an abundance of vacation rentals. It was suggested the
       hotel association be contacted for their input. Commission also asked about parking
       standards and whether there is adequate parking available for vacation rentals. M
       r. Brierley responded the downtown ordinance would not require additional parking to
       accommodate vacation rentals.

       Mr. Brierley also informed the Commission a petition has been received which would
       allow multiple uses along the BRT route, eliminating the provision which now limits these
       to one per lineal block. Staff also is considering language similar to what is now being
       used by Weber County which requires owner-occupied units to be owned for a minimum
       of three years before the use may occur.
