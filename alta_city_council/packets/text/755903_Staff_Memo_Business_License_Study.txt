Alta Town Council

Staff Report:
Business License Study

To: Town Council
From: Jen Clancy, Deputy Town Clerk
Date Written: September 2, 2021



Zions Public Finance conducted a business license study for the Town of Alta. The draft
study was presented to the Town Council at the July 2021 meeting and discussed again
at the August meeting and work session. The draft report has been circulated to the
Council for review and is attached as an exhibit to the draft ordinance. If the Town
chooses to adopt the study and set a new fee schedule where disproportionate fees are
charged then an ordinance and a resolution need to be adopted.

Here is some additional information to consider:
   •   The purpose of this study is to inform the Town about the costs to the Town for
       administering business licenses as well as other financial impacts to the Town’s
       budget such as enhanced and disproportionate fees. The study identifies the
       maximum fees that can be charged to each respective category. The Town may
       choose to extend a discount to a category of businesses for in-kind services.
   •   In-kind contributions are not included in business license study reports. The
       report’s purpose is to establish the actual cost to the Town so a benefit received
       from in-kind contributions would be outside of the scope of such a report.
   •   Additional information on comparable fees that was requested by Sheridan was
       added to the draft report.

•   Home occupation businesses: The State regulates these businesses and says we
    can’t charge a business license fee. If however, a home occupation business
    requests a license we are entitled to charge the base admin fee of $62.27.
•   This study did not analyze or consider liquor license applications and fees.
•   Option A in Appendix A: This option was proposed in order to reach the $18,000
    revenue in the approved FY22 budget. In-kind services from businesses in
    Categories 1 and 8 that are provided to our community (such as snow removal
    and search and rescue operations) were considered. These two business
    categories also provide sales tax revenue to Town. We note that the sales tax
    revenue budgeted in the 2022 budget is $1.251 million. If you take the average
    revenue generated by the $16,000 difference between our proposed business
    license fees and the maximum fees we could charge, and divide by the sales tax
    rate of 8.75% you get $183,000. Any sales tax generated above that figure —
    expected to be about $1.06 million — represents that the Town really is not
    subsidizing the businesses (rather, the customers of the business are paying for
    the Town’s operations).
•   Options B and C in Appendix A are being presented to offer additional
    alternatives that broaden the range of fee options for the council if needed for
    discussion and consideration.
