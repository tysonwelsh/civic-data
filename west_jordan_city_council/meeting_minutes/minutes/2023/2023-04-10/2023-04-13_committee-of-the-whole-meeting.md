                                 MINUTES OF THE CITY OF WEST JORDAN
                                     COMMITTEE OF THE WHOLE
                                  Thursday, April 13, 2023 – 6:00 p.m.
                                       Approved May 10, 2023

                                         Thomas M. Rees Justice Center
                                 8040 S Redwood Road • West Jordan, UT 84088



 1. CALL TO ORDER

      COUNCIL: Chair Chris McConnehey, Vice Chair Pamela Bloom, Kelvin Green, Zach
               Jacob (joined at 6:05 pm), David Pack, Kayleen Whitelock, Melissa
               Worthen
      STAFF:   Council Office Director Alan Anderson, Council Office Clerk Cindy Quick,
               Policy Analyst Cassidy Hansen, Public Services Director Isaac Astill,
               Mayor Dirk Burton, Public Works Director Brian Clegg, Assistant City
               Administrator Jamie Davidson, Utilities Manager Greg Davenport, City
               Administrator Korban Lee, Senior Planner Ray McCandless, Assistant
               City Attorney Duncan Murray, Attendee Josh Chandler

Chair McConnehey called the Committee of the Whole meeting to order at 6:00 pm and expressed
appreciation to everyone involved in the General Plan update. He explained the intention to recess
and break into groups that evening to review and discuss the guiding principles included in the draft
General Plan document.

At 6:15 pm, Council Member Green moved to go into breakout sessions. Council Member
Whitelock seconded the motion, which passed by unanimous vote (7-0).

Chair McConnehey reconvened the meeting at 7:23 pm.


 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Review the draft General Plan and discuss potential modifications in preparation for
          final adoption
          Introduction – Council Member Whitelock referred to language about encouraging
          development of senior housing (Chapter 1, page 12) and expressed concern that over
          time, as the Baby Boomer generation passed, the need for senior housing would reduce
          and the City would be left with a lot of units needing to be repurposed. She asked how
          many senior housing units were already entitled. Council Member Green suggested
          changing the language to “encourage development of affordable housing”. Chair
          McConnehey suggested removing the statement altogether and the majority were okay to
          remove the statement, but asked that staff still review the language.

          Chapter 2 Demographics – Chair McConnehey commented that the document was very
          visual and easy to consume and thanked those involved. He didn’t feel that any changes
          were necessary.

          Chapter 3 Growth Management – Under Growth Management Policies and Goals (pages
          28-29), Chair McConnehey suggested removing “Provide for orderly annexation of
          properties into the City”, “Ensure that new development pays its fair share for
          infrastructure and costs for service”, and “Ensure future development is provided with
          adequate public facilities and infrastructure”. He suggested changing the language of the
          first goal. Council Member Green suggested removing pages 28 and 29 entirely and a
          majority of the Council appeared to agree.

          Chapter 4 Urban Design – Chair McConnehey suggested instead of a sunset over the Great
          Salt Lake on page 35 that it be replaced with a view of the Oquirrh Mountains. He
          suggested removing the goals listed on pages 40 and 41, “Promote and foster good urban
          design at the community, neighborhood, and individual project levels” and “Identify and
          enhance gateways into the City”, and adding gateway enhancement to the goal on page
          42, “Strengthen the identity and image of the City of West Jordan”.

          The Council discussed removing subgoals under the policies and goals and placing them
          in a separate document of guiding principles for staff and Council reference. Chair
          McConnehey suggested removing “Establish a comprehensive urban open space system
          in the City”, and changing “Establish areas of transit-oriented development (TOD) that
          exemplify a high level of urban design” and “Create a city center that exemplifies a high
          level of urban design” to subgoals. He suggested changing “Preserve positive
          neighborhood character” to “Support neighborhoods and developments of character”.

          A majority of the Council appeared to agree with the two proposed remaining goals.

          Chapter 5 Land Use – Council Member Green and Vice Chair Bloom presented four guiding
          principles based on the drafted principles for Chapter 5 and suggested moving the drafted
          subgoal detail to a separate executional plan.

              1. Land use decisions made using a regional approach that integrates and
                 participates with programs established to better serve the City as a whole.
              2. Land use decisions shall be guided by the General Plan to protect existing land uses
                 and minimize impacts to existing neighborhoods.
              3. The General Plan is the will of the community and presumed correct. Developers
                 have a burden of proof on why the General Plan should be changed.
              4. Land use designs must promote quality of life, safety, and good urban designs.

          Council Member Green suggested changing the Residential Density Table on page 55 to
          reflect the following designations:
              • Low Density – up to 5.0 units per acre
              • Medium Density – 5.0 to 7.9 units per acre
              • High Density – 8.0 to 11.9 units per acre
              • Very High Density – 12 units per acre or higher.

          He said the suggested changes would provide greater consistency with Code.

          The Council discussed high density versus low density and differing perceptions. Council
          Member Jacob said he wanted to be able to make what the residents, developers, and
          Planning Staff had in mind for density designations line up. Council Member Whitelock
          was uncomfortable with some of the suggested ranges. Chair McConnehey did not object
          to the proposed change.

          Council Member Green suggested:
             • Remove references to schools from the Residential Policies and Goals on page 56.
             • Under Agricultural Policies and Goals on page 76 the goal should read, “Protect
                 productive agricultural lands”. The last subgoal should read “Implement
                 standards that promote sustainable development practices.”
             • Remove the last subgoal on page 77 under Master Planned Community Policies
                 and Goals.

          Chapter 6 Transportation – Chair McConnehey suggested eliminating the first goal under
          Transportation Policies and Goals on page 85, and changing the goal on page 86 to read,
          “Provide a safe and efficient multi-modal transportation system within the City.“ He
          suggested the next three goals listed should be subgoals (“Develop a transportation
          management program”, “Ensure the use of bicycle and pedestrian transportation
          systems”, and “Support residential traffic calming”), with “Improve the aesthetic quality
          of the City’s streets” as the second goal in Chapter 6.

          Chapter 7 Housing – The Council discussed rephrasing language on page 91 referencing
          those with “special needs.” Chair McConnehey suggested those with “different abilities.”
          Council Member Whitelock suggested removing language on page 95 (third paragraph)
          and replacing with “West Jordan will comply with ADU laws set by Utah State Legislature.”
          Chair McConnehey suggested removing the paragraphs beginning “As of October 1,
          2021,” and “Since these new accessory dwelling unit laws were passed…”. A majority of
          the Council indicated agreement.

          Vice Chair Bloom left the meeting at 8:06 pm.

          The Council discussed subgoals on page 101. Chair McConnehey suggested the 83/17
          residential housing ratio did not need to be included in the Housing Guiding Principles
          and Goals. A majority of the Council appeared to agree with the following subgoal:
          “Encourage a balanced variety of housing types that meet the needs of all life stages with
          a mix of opportunities for today and into the future.” Council Member Green suggested a
          thorough review of land use ordinance and balanced housing ordinance over the next
          year.

          Council Member Whitelock suggested “Place high density projects near infrastructure
          which exist to sustain the increased density” as a second goal for Chapter 7, removing the
          drafted goals on page 103. The Council discussed including the following goal:
          “Implement programs to encourage repair, rehabilitation, or replacement of
          deteriorating residential structures.”

          Chapter 8 Moderate Income Housing Plan – No change

          Chapter 9 Environment – Council Member Pack proposed the following overarching goal
          for Chapter 9: “Preserve, protect, and restore hillsides, ridgelines, and foothills of the
          Oquirrh Mountains and along the Jordan River.” The Council discussed adding reference
          to the Big Bend Project in the first paragraph on page 123, and the word “approximately”
          to the reference to Redwood Road and 1100 West in the paragraph under Hydrology.
          Council Member Pack suggested the following as the second goal under Environment:
          “Maximize water conservation, protect and improve air quality, and employ
          environmentally sound building practices.”

          Chapter 10 Water Use & Preservation – Council Member Whitelock said staff would
          submit updated information and graphs for Chapter 10. She suggested removing the last
          page of the Chapter (page 145) and moving it to Chapter 11. Council Member Whitelock
          proposed the following policies and goals:
             1. Implement and encourage principles of sustainable water use and water
                 conserving landscaping.
             2. Encourage water conservation by following the City Water Conservation Plan.
             3. Modify the City’s practices to efficiently manage water and set a positive example
                 for water conservation.

          Chapter 11 Parks, Recreation, Trails, Open Space and Community Gardens – Chair
          McConnehey expressed the opinion that detail already addressed by park master plans
          could be eliminated from the General Plan, leaving reference to the master plans. He
          suggested removing the policies and goals listed under Parks, Recreation, Trails, and
          Open Space. Chair McConnehey requested a reference table of all sub-plans. As a guiding
          principle under Chapter 11, Chair McConnehey suggested, “Provide relief from the
          asphalt, concrete, steel, and vehicular environments of a suburban city.” He reiterated
          that Community Gardens would be added to Chapter 11.

          Chapter 12 Economic Development – For Economic Development Policies and Goals,
          Council Member Whitelock suggested:
             1. Attract, recruit, and retain businesses that benefit and enhance the quality of life
                in West Jordan.
             2. Diversify and strengthen the employment and tax base in the City of West Jordan.
             3. Encourage the creation of planned commercial centers that provide the services
                and amenities residents need, and which reduce the need for extra or lengthy
                vehicle trips.
              4. Provide well-designed, attractive, and aesthetically pleasing professional office
                 and business environments within the City.

          Chapter 13 Historical Preservation – Council Member Green presented the following goal
          for Chapter 13. Council Member Pack mentioned a desire to include language in Chapter
          13 to safeguard and enhance the City Museum.

              •   Preserve the character and significance of historic sites and structures located
                  within the City.

           Future Land Use Map – The Council discussed the possibility of changing the Future Land
           Use Map designation of the Bowman’s Arrow property. City Administrator Korban Lee
           suggested waiting until property owners had more firmly proposed a plan so the City
           could better understand needed infrastructure.

           The Council discussed reviewing and updating the existing density table in terms of units
           per acre. Council Member Whitelock suggested the Welby West property should be
           updated on the Map. Council Member Green suggested specific property on 7800 South
           near 1300 West should be designated Community Commercial on the Future Land Use
           Map and addressed by the RDA.

           Council Member Green said he wanted to remove language stating the Regional Airport
           was the largest generator of noise if such language was still in the draft General Plan. The
           Council discussed use of the term “people with disabilities” versus “people with different
           abilities” in Chapter 7.
 3. ADMINISTRATIVE ITEMS
       Chair McConnehey clarifying comments from the majority of Council regarding next steps
       reported that they would bring back the amended General Plan for a public hearing in May
       with final adoption during the following meeting as a Business item.


 4. ADJOURN
Council Member Green moved to adjourn the Committee of the Whole. Council Member
Worthen seconded the motion, which passed by unanimous vote (6-0).

The meeting adjourned at 8:52 pm
