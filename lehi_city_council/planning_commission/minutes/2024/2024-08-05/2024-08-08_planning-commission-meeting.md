3. Regular Agenda

3.1) Recommendation of CORE Architecture’s request for review of an amendment to the
Skye Area Plan adding design requirements for Civic Land Uses. (tabled from the July 25,
2024)

Applicant Curtis Miner presented some of the items that were discussed from the July 25
meeting.

The Commissioners expressed concerns about the vague language in the code regarding glazing
percentages, suggesting that clearer definitions or removing the requirement altogether would be
more effective. The discussion then covered setbacks, with Mr. Miner proposing the removal of
setback requirements to better fit site design needs, particularly for large civic buildings.
The Commissioners questioned the implications of this approach but appreciated the proposal.

They also discussed the importance of water-wise landscaping, which Mr. Miner agreed to
address by consulting with their landscape architects. Additionally, lighting standards were
reviewed, with concerns raised about consistency and intensity. The meeting concluded with a
proposal to separate civic sites into different categories to better accommodate their specific
requirements.

        Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to approve CORE Architecture’s request for
        review of an amendment to the Skye Area Plan adding design requirements for Civic
        Land Uses including all findings stated in the report and all DRC comments.
        Commissioner Lockhart added that the applicant should continue workshopping the
        water-wise landscaping requirements prior to getting to council, with the intention of
        adhering to as much of them as possible, and then to include layering in the C1 and the
        C2 designation that was just mentioned, so that that secondary site will be held to the
        standards we typically have, and if there were wanting to be exceptions to that, they
        would need to follow the regular application process. Commissioner Eyre seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
        Commissioner Lockhart, yes; Commissioner Roberts, yes. The motion passed
        unanimously.
