Ordinance and Issues

Riverton City has for some time been reviewing potential changes to the current Elderly Housing
Overlay zone. That ordinance as it stands allows for various housing types and care facilities for
the elderly, and establishes general density and development regulations for such uses. The
overlay is applied to an underlying zone. The current ordinance is broadly constructed, and the
City has determined that modification and clarification of the ordinance is necessary to best
implement the City’s goals and policies regarding elderly housing in the City.

The currently ordinance allows within the overlay both ‘independent senior housing’, typically
individual living units or structures, and care facilities such as nursing homes. The overlay can
be applied to both commercial and multi-family residential zones. The overlay carries a maximum
density of 20 units per acre, but does not include any criteria or basis for determining how density
may be allocated or addressed.

One of the most fundamental changes recommended in this amendment is to the allowed
underlying zoning. The proposed amendment removes nursing homes, assisted living facilities,
and similar uses from the overlay, and makes those uses conditional in most commercial zones.
Such uses are essentially commercial in nature, and are most appropriately located in those
zones. Existing facilities in Riverton City are all in commercial areas, and so the proposed
amendment will not result in any non-conforming sites. The amended Elderly Housing Overlay
could only then be applied to residential zones, and only for independent elderly housing. With
this change, the density and development standards would primarily be a function of the
underlying zone. This will help to clarify the application and approval process for all involved, and
will make the record cleaner regarding permitted density and unit types. For example, a property
could be zoned RM-6/EH-OV, which would allow elderly housing to be built at a density of up to
6 units per acre. Alternatively, a property could be zoned R-4/EH-OV, which would allow elderly

There has been discussion regarding how to appropriately incentivize development of elderly
housing in Riverton City, and the amendment proposes allowing a density ‘bonus’ of up to 50%.
However, rather than apply the bonus increasing the density above that allowed in the underlying
zoning, which can lead to confusion in the future, the proposed amendment allows the density
bonus in a different way. The bonus is applied to the General Plan Designation for a property.
For example, if a property is designated for ¼ acre lots under the General Plan, the R-4 zone
would be maximum zoning that could be applied for. However, an application for a rezone to the
Elderly Housing Overlay Zone could qualify for a bonus density of 50% above the General Plan
designation, meaning the City could approve the RM-6 zone with the Elderly Housing Overlay.
The 50% density bonus would allow the change from 4 units per acre (R-4) to 6 units/acre (RM-
6), without an amendment to the General Plan. This provides a reasonable incentive to develop
elderly housing, while insuring the zoning will accurately reflect and regulate the densities
involved. The amendment allows the City to establish appropriate conditions during the rezone
process to mitigate identified impacts.

The proposed amendment requires that elderly housing be limited to a minimum of 80% of
residents of 55 or older. This can be accomplished by “deed restriction”, which means the deed
for each lot or unit will be recorded with the limitation. Other methods, including binding
restrictions under a Home Owners Association, are allowed, and may be expanded over time
consistent with applicable laws. The amendment also requires a minimum of three (3) “age
friendly” features for all dwelling units, to help insure that the units will accommodate elderly
residents and allow for “aging in place”.

The Table of Commercial Uses shows the amendment listing Nursing/Care Facilities and Assisted
Living Facilities as conditional in all commercial zones except Commercial Professional Office.
The amendment also adds to each applicable commercial zone the following language under
Conditional Uses:

Nursing/Care facility, assisted living facility. Number of rooms/beds shall be as determined in site
plan approval, subject to site constraints, including available parking. Individual ownership of
rooms/living space is not permitted.

Staff will present additional information on this amendment at the hearing.

18.120.010 Purpose.
    The elderly housing overlay (OV-EH) zone is established to provide a framework
for elderly housing, including attached and detached units. This overlay zone is not
intended for assisted living facilities, residential care facilities, hospitals, clinics, health
care centers, or like uses. The OV-EH zone may be permitted in any residential zone.
The intent of this overlay zone is to provide adequate and affordable accommodations
for Riverton’s elderly citizens

18.120.020 Permitted uses.
   Permitted uses in the elderly housing overlay zone shall be limited to the following
uses or similar uses as interpreted by the planning commission:
   (1) Independent elderly housing.
   (2) Attached or detached units, subject to underlying zoning requirements and
       allowances.

18.120.030 Permitted accessory uses.
   (1) Accessory Buildings. Garage, shed, etc, subject to Section 18.225 of this code.


 18.120.040 Placement of elderly housing.
   (1) Zones Applicable to Overlay. The elderly housing overlay zone may be used in
conjunction with all residential zones. The overlay may not be applied to commercial,
industrial, or other non-residential zones.

18.120.050 Process.
   An overlay zone must follow the rezoning process to add elderly housing as a
permitted use onto the applicable zone. This process is set forth in Chapter 18.220
RCC.

18.120.060 Density and area requirements.
   (1) Maximum Density. The OV-EH zone shall have be subject to the lot size and/or
density regulations of the underlying zone, unless modified by this chapter.
   (2) Parcel Size. Each application pursuant to this chapter shall be for a site of not
less than one acre and not more than 10 acres.

conditions during the approval of the Elderly Housing Overlay zoning for a property,
which shall “run with the land” upon approval. Conditions may include,
       a) Minimum lot size
       b) Maximum unit count
       c) Unit type, including limitations to height or number of stories
       d) Public vs private infrastructure

    (1) . Development standards, including setbacks, building height, coverage, and
other requirements, shall be as found in the underlying zoning. Where a density bonus
is granted under the terms of this section and underlying zoning standards do not fully
address the proposed density, development standards shall be consistent with the
corresponding Riverton City zone, as established in approval of the rezone applying the
overlay, and at the discretion of the City.
    (2). Dwelling units developed within the Elderly Housing Overlay shall include a
minimum of three (3) “age friendly” features, including but not limited to:
        1. Main level living
        2. No step entries/thresholds
        3. Access ramps
        4. Increased door/hallway width
        5. Non-slip flooring throughout
        6. Accessible bathroom facilities
        7. Age appropriate hardware/fixtures, such as lever style door handles.
        8. Other features as approved by the Development Services Director.

      18.120.010 Purpose.
      The elderly housing overlay (OV‑EH) zone is established to provide an area for elderly housing,
      including independent elderly housing, nursing homes, convalescent centers and assisted living
      centers adjacent to, or in proximity to, commercial centers, mass-transportation access, or
      community and civic centers. This overlay zone is not intended for hospitals, clinics, health care
      centers, or like uses. The OV‑EH zone may be permitted in any commercial or RM zone. The intent
      of this overlay zone is to provide adequate accommodation for Riverton’s elderly citizens, where
      the lifestyle is less burdensome and more convenient for residents to perform daily activities. [Ord.
      2-3-98-2 § 1 (Exh. A § 12-256-005). Code 1997 § 12-340-005.]

      18.120.120 Standards for open space.
      Open space shall be maintained by the owner and shall meet the following minimum requirements:

      (1) All areas shall be properly graded so as to cause no drainage problems to adjacent homes or
      other uses.

      (2) All open space shall be sodded or seeded to provide a visually pleasing as well as functional
      space for appropriate activities.

      (3) Where it is deemed appropriate, native grasses and plants may be used along equestrian trails,
      bicycle paths, etc. Steps will be taken by developers to guard against such spaces becoming a fire
      hazard or haven for insects.

      (4) All areas shall have a minimum number of both deciduous and evergreen trees to provide for
      shade and to provide visual relief and a source of rural atmosphere. The following shall be required:

           (a) A minimum of 20 trees per acre shall be planted and maintained.

           (b) Trees shall be a minimum of one-and-one-half-inch caliper to two-inch caliper or, if
           evergreen, seven feet to nine feet tall.

           (c) All areas shall be watered by an installed irrigation system.

      (5) Open Space Guarantees. Within all areas where condominium ownership is established in the
      RM‑12 zone, adequate assurance in a form approved by the city attorney shall be provided for
      permanent retention and maintenance of all open space and areas of common ownership. A building
      permit shall not be issued until all required guarantees have been reviewed by the planning
      commission and approved by the city council. Such open space guarantees could include
      easements to the city for perpetual use as open space.

      (6) Landscaping Guarantee. Whenever a residential dwelling is constructed, landscaping, in the
      form of lawn, trees, or other plantings or landscape features, except for areas covered by walls,
      driveways, and other structures, shall be installed before issuing a certificate of occupancy unless
      weather forces the developer to bond with the city for the landscaping for a maximum period of six
      months.

      18.120.130 Independent elderly housing.
      (1) Conditions. Independent elderly housing facilities shall be subject to the following conditions
      before being granted a conditional use permit, and said permit shall not be denied where a facility is
      found to meet all conditions set forth in this chapter:

           (a) Code Compliance. The facility shall meet all city and state building, safety and health laws
           and regulations applicable to other dwellings in the zone. Further, the facility shall meet all
           state and federal laws which apply to structures and facilities used by elderly people.

           (b) Residents. No person being treated for alcoholism or drug abuse shall be placed in said
           dwelling. No person who is violent shall be placed in said dwelling. No residents of said
           facilities shall be less than 55 years of age unless a spouse to a resident who is 55 years of
           age or older.

           (c) Volunteer Basis. Placement in said dwelling shall be on a strictly voluntary basis and not
           part of, or in lieu of, confinement, rehabilitation, or treatment in a correctional facility.

           (d) Interior. For convalescent homes, no more than two residents shall sleep in a single
           bedroom. A minimum of 60 square feet per individual shall be provided in a double-occupancy
           bedroom. A minimum of 100 square feet per individual shall be provided in a single occupant
           bedroom.

           (e) Other Conditions. The city council, upon prior recommendation of the planning
           commission, may set other reasonable conditions for any individual applicant which it feels
           will further the intent of Section 10-8-2.6, Utah Code Annotated 1953, as amended, and this
           chapter.

      (2) Application Accompanied by Plot Plan. An application for a residential facility for elderly persons
      pursuant to this chapter shall be accompanied by a plot plan in duplicate, drawn to scale, showing
      the actual dimensions of the property, the size and locations of existing buildings, all hard-surfaced
      areas, landscaped areas and such other information as may be necessary to provide for the
      compliance of this chapter.

      (3) Discrimination Prohibited. Discrimination against elderly persons and against residential
      facilities for elderly persons is prohibited. All decisions regarding an application for a residential
      facility for elderly persons must be based on legitimate land use criteria, and may not be based on
      the age of the facility’s residents.

      18.120.140 Fencing.
      (1) Collector Street Fencing. All residential subdivisions having properties adjacent to or abutting
      onto a collector or arterial residential street shall have decorative fencing, in compliance with
      Chapter 18.155 RCC.

      (2) Incompatible Land Uses. All properties adjacent to or abutting a rural residential or agricultural
      zone shall install fencing of sufficient quality, able to withstand an impact from large animals.
      Fencing for properties adjacent to other uses may also be required by the planning commission or
      city council to provide fencing of quality design, and landscape incorporation.

      (3) Height Requirements. No wall or fence less than six feet in height shall be erected or maintained
      in any rear yard, nor shall any wall, fence, opaque hedge, or screening material be erected or
      maintained in any required front yard in excess of three feet in height. Except that any fence
      erected within 10 feet of any driveway and exceeding two feet in height shall be of visually
      nonobstructive material and shall be used to provide a safe distance for pedestrian and street
      traffic.

      (4) Corner Lots. No wall, fence, opaque hedge, or screening material in excess of two feet shall be
      placed on any corner lot within a triangular area formed by the street property lines and a line
      connecting them at a point equal to the required setback lines. [Amended during 2011
      recodification; Ord. 2-3-98-2 § 1 (Exh. A § 12-256-070). Code 1997 § 12-340-070.]
