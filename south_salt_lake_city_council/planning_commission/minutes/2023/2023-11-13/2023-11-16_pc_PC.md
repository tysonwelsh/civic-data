1.     PUBLIC HEARING - A Petition for Final Plat Approval to Consolidate Two Existing
       Properties into One Lot, for a Restaurant, located at 28 East 2100 South.

Action Item

Applicant:     Cassie Ruiz
Address:       28 East 2100 South and 50 East 2100 South, In-N-Out Burger.

Planning Consultant, Alex White Halleck presented the In-N-Out Burger Final Subdivision Plat
Application and reported that at the last meeting, the Commission reviewed the Preliminary Plat.
This is the third step in the entitlement process for the applicant who has gone through the Petition
for Rezone for 50 East 2100 South and completed the Ordinance Amendment Request. Both were
approved by the City Council. The next step is the Building Permit Application. The request
consists of two properties located at 28 East 2100 South and 50 East 2100 South. A map of the
area was displayed. The properties include the former Mad Greek Building and a vacant lot. At
the end of October, the City Council approved the Rezone Petition for 50 East 2100 South from
Commercial Corridor to Crossing MPMU. The approval included a condition that the applicant
consolidate the unsubdivided parcel, which is Lot 5 of the Crossing Subdivision.

Ms. White Halleck clarified that both zones allow fast food restaurants as a permitted use. What
was being reviewed was the infrastructure portion of the plat application. At the last meeting,
there was discussion of the Public Utility Easement (“PUE”) issue that arose as part of the ALTA
Survey and the Title Report for the subject properties. Around 2008, the South Salt Lake City
Council granted right-of-way vacations to a portion of Major Street, all of Regent Street, and a
number of dedicated alleys that ran through this section of the South Boulevard Subdivision, which
was the original subdivision for the area. As part of the vacation process, they left the PUEs in
place. There were water and sewer lines that ran through the properties. The lines were
discontinued and relocated to Lot 1, which is the WinCo parcel. The pipes were left in the ground
and abandoned. As part of the process, the applicant is petitioning to have the City remove the
two PUEs in exchange for relocating them to a 10-foot PUE around the two consolidated parcels.
The applicant continued to work through the process with the South Salt Lake City Attorney’s
Office. The Final Plat will show the PUE being drawn on, however, to remove the two easements,
the City will have to record something with the Salt Lake County Recorder releasing the title
documents so that they no longer appear on the ALTA Survey and the title in exchange for the
new 10-foot PUE.

Other ALTA Survey items included right-of-way dedications. On Main Street, the applicant is
dedicating two feet of right-of-way to meet the standard cross-section that is required per the Code.
The existing curb and gutter will not shift so there will be no misalignment in the storm drain
system. That dedication will take place on paper. 2100 South is a Utah Department of
Transportation (“UDOT”) road so no right-of-way dedication is proposed there at this time. There
is also no proposed dedication along Major Street. The cross sections comply with the required
width and dimension requirements. The applicant will bury all power lines, specifically along
Main Street and throughout the interior of the property. There are no above-ground power lines
on Major Street but there is a cable or line that the applicant is looking at along 2100 South.

Public improvements along the right-of-way were described. For 2100 South, Main Street, and
Major Street, the applicant is proposing to install a 10½-foot sidewalk with trees and tree grates
and remove all existing park strips along those frontages. Ms. White Halleck noted that on 2100
South, the trees are being pulled behind the sidewalk as they will not be flush with curb and gutter
due to right-of-way widths and the requirements for Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”)
compliance.

There is also a condition of approval that the applicant participate in the Public Infrastructure
District (“PID”) that is part of the Sewer Infrastructure Plan that was adopted by the City Council.
Part of the property is already included in that district. The PID Annexation Map will need to
match the plat being proposed.

The Reagan Outdoor Advertising billboard relocation is a separate application that will be before
the Commission at its December 7 Planning Commission Meeting. For the Site Plan to be
approved, it must be moved approximately 100 feet to the east so that is not within the drive-thru
lane and located in the landscaping between Major Street and 2100 South.

Ms. White Halleck reported that nothing has changed on the Site Plan since it was last reviewed
by the Planning Commission. There are 63 total parking stalls, a 34-vehicle queue drive-thru lane,
and two points of vehicular access. The applicant is currently working with South Salt Lake City
Engineering on a full in-depth technical review, which is in process. The applicant completed a
Traffic Study. Under the assumptions for the proposal, there was no drop in the level of service at
any of the intersections that were studied. There has been no change to the elevations.

With regard to the Preliminary Plat, the applicant is still making technical corrections and a
Technical Review will be conducted by a third party and Salt Lake County. The only potential
change was to the easements shown in red that are being exchanged for the PUE. They will drop
off after the City records the document based on the decision of the South Salt Lake City Attorney
and Salt Lake County. All that will remain on the plat will be the 10-foot PUE around the perimeter
of the property.

Ms. White Halleck reported that the proposal supports many things within the General Plan. The
Planning Commission is the Land Use Authority on all subdivision applications and this is an
administrative process. If the applicant meets all of the criteria within the Subdivision Code, the
Planning Commission should look to approve. It is not necessarily a discretionary process. Staff
recommended approval with the conditions enumerated in the Staff Report. The Commission can
choose to approve, deny, or continue the request if there are issues.

The applicant, Cassie Ruiz commented that what they have done over the last several weeks
addresses the wants and needs of those involved. She stressed that there is a lot of emphasis on
their drive-thru service but they are actually a restaurant that offers drive-thru service. They have
dine-in options with seating for 84 and a patio that seats 24. They want to encourage community
engagement and she stressed that In-N-Out Burger is a family-friendly establishment. They are
excited to be part of the community and give back.

Ms. Ungricht commented that the Staff Report did not include a condition to stipulate the time
period with which the Final Plat must be recorded by. The Code specifies that the Planning
Commission can determine a time period of up to one year.

Chair Pechmann opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.

A question was raised regarding whether Reagan Outdoor Signs is asking to relocate the existing
sign or upgrade the existing sign. City Attorney, Josh Collins stated that the decision on the Final
Subdivision Plat needs to be made based on this application. The Conditional Use Permit issue
will be addressed at the next meeting. There are implications, but whether the applicant meets all
of the Code requirements for the Final Subdivision Plat approval is not related. The decision
tonight needs to pertain to what the application says and what the Code requires for the Final
Subdivision Plat.

Commissioner Bellina asked about timing. Mr. Collins stated that the maximum allowed is one
year. Most often 180 days is suggested. He explained that if the specific timing is not printed in
the Staff Report, there is some question as to the timeframe. Being specific provides an official
timeframe by which the Final Plat must be recorded. It also helps in the event an extension is
sought. The intent was to avoid confusion going forward.

Commissioner Bellina asked if the Final Plat can be recorded with the County if reviews are
ongoing. Mr. Collins stated that it cannot be finalized until all signatures are on the plat.
Commissioner Bellina questioned whether 180 days was enough time to complete all of the
necessary reviews. Ms. Ungricht stated that three to six months should be adequate. The most
timely change will be to have the easement removed and the Final Plat cannot be recorded until
that is accomplished. If an extension is needed, the applicant will need to file before it expires. If
there is concern with the applicant meeting that deadline, Mr. Collins stated that the Commission
could choose to allow up to one year for recording.

Chair Pechmann did not object to allowing one year since he was aware that the applicants want
to move more quickly than that.

              Book 2008P of Plats at Page 70 of the Official Records.

              a portion of Major Street, all of Regent Street, and all Dedicated Alleys located

              as part of the entitlement process for development of the WinCo property

              underground water and sanitary sewer pipes that were owned and operated
              by South Salt Lake City. The 2008 vacation kept public easements (noted
              above in item #8) over a portion of the subject utilities located at 28 East 2100
              South and 50 East 2100 South.
