Stakeholders Updates
Kearns Improvement District (KID)

Greg Anderson, General Manager, Kearns Improvement District, stated crews
will begin reconstruction of the waterline on 4320 W. starting April 1, 2024. The six inch waterline
will be upsized to a 12 inch waterline, and the existing homes will be connected to it. KID will
send letters to the homes affected. Mr. Anderson distributed copies of the letter.

KID has applied to receive the Environmental Protection Agency's WaterSense award and will
receive word of the decision in August.

KID is making good progress on its investigation regarding lead and copper content in drinking
water. The Division of Drinking Water requires that KID identify what the laterals are from the
meter to the home. To that end, it has created door hangers with a QR code for residents to scan
and submit a photo of the line as it enters the home. Residents who participate in this program
will receive a $10 discount on their next utility bill.

KID is continuing to replace water meters throughout the district and the newer meters can be
connected to a user application that will be able to notify residents of water leaks immediately.
This project is about 75 percent complete. KID will be mailing out instructions on how to install
the app.

Mr. Anderson shared an article by the American Waterworks Association that talked about
wastage rates of drinking water throughout the nation. KID is keeping its loss rate between 4.8
and 5.1 percent, which is much better than the national average, by proactively using listening
devices on waterlines that can pinpoint water leaks so they can be repaired as soon as the next
day.

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD)

Legal Updates/Information
Final Legislative Recap

Nathan Bracken, Lega! Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated HB 35, the bill
that will convert the Metro Townships into cities/towns, would go into effect on May 1, 2024. HB
174 would put the Metro Townships in a position to determine how to fund law enforcement. If
law enforcement responsibilities are the same, so should taxing authority be. In two years, the
mayor will need to run for election instead of being appointed.
The passage of HB 330, which will require unincorporated islands to either annex or incorporate
in 2027, will not directly affect Kearns. It raised concerns with the County, though, because the
County wiil still be contributing to the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) but
would not have a weighted vote under the existing formula because it would not have a remaining
population. During the waning hours of the legislative session, many affected parties succeeded
in coming up with a new weighted vote formula.
A couple land use bills that passed. One would have given developers authority to hire their own
building inspectors. There was a lot of pushback from the Utah Association of Counties and the
League of Cities and Towns. This resulted in a compromise bill. Cities currently have three days
to perform inspection. If no inspector is available, the developer can use a state approved list of
inspectors.
Met Tax Discussion

Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated Mayor Bush
asked him to discuss the Municipal Energy and Telecommunications Tax Authority (MET). With
the Sheriff leaving the Unified Police Department (UPD), Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement
Service Area (SLVLESA) has reached its cap on what it can charge for property tax. This tax
only applies to landlines. Once Kearns Metro Township becomes a city, it can pass an ordinance
