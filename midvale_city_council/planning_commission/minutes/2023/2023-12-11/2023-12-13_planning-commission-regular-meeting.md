Chair Liedtke called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney

deliberate and decide.

6.    Midvale Fort Union and Center Street Station Area Plan.

      In 2022 the Utah State Legislature passed HB 462 which, among other things, requires
      municipalities with fixed rail guideways (commuter and light rail) to prepare and adopt
      station area plans (SAP) covering a half mile radius of each fixed rail station. SAP’s may
      be unique to their jurisdiction and setting; however, key aspects are to include strategies
      to increase the availability of affordable housing, promotion of sustainable
      environmental conditions, enhancements of access to employment opportunities and
      increase in transportation choices and connections.

      Midvale has three stations within its boundaries. Two station area plans, Midvale Ft.
      Union and Midvale Center, are complete and ready to proceed toward adoption,
      certification and implementation. A third station, Bingham Junction, is nearing
      completion.

      MHTN, the consultant group commissioned to prepare the SAP will lead the discussion
      and presentation at the December 13th Planning Commission meeting. They will

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION DECEMBER 13, 2023.
       highlight recommendations of the plan and proposed implementation steps.

       Staff recommends approval.

APPLICANT:

Ryan Wallace, representing MHTN as a consultant on the project, proceeds to explain the
development of the transit-oriented development as part of the Station Area Plan. Working with
UTA and Midvale City Staff has been going extremely well. This opportunity to develop will help
the State meet needs for housing and transportation. WFRC wants several things from the
consultants and the City to meet requirements mandated by the State.

Residents have shared concerns regarding areas of stability, which are areas where development
makes sense. Midvale has a great benefit of having three separate Station Area Plans, most cities
do not have these lots available. Working with Horrocks, the engineering firm, there have been
impressive results in the possibilities for these developments.

 Looking at types of transportation there have been concerns for pedestrians and cyclists. The busy
roads do not provide a safe route now, but the new development looks to change this.
Improvements to safety, ease of access, civic spaces, and better amenities were a few suggestions
that came up during discussions. There were three different workshops with Midvale City Council,
in which the support for development was expressed so long as they are well thought out and
incentivized.

Fort Union Station Area is intended to become a more welcoming space. The community will be
able to have a more pedestrian friendly route. Mixed-use is highly preferred with more green space
and larger setbacks.

Center Street Station Area is intended to become more of a downtown area where new uses will
be explored and improving upon existing amenities. This is a key East-West transportation point
in Midvale. Maintaining residential space and protecting existing single-family homes from multi-
level developments is important. A lot of streetscape updates and two or three level mixed-use
buildings would maximize the area and still maintain the neighborhood feeling for current and
future residents.

Horrocks helped to look at first-mile, last-mile connections. In the industry this means that most
people do not live and work right beside a station to easily access public transportation. Enhancing
transportation and safety for pedestrians and cyclists would help this factor and incentivize more
use of public transportation.

Refinements to Zoning Ordinances will be necessary to reach these goals. Specific suggestions
being made will be acquiring land for parks, on and off-street facility improvements, shared
parking areas between commercial and residential, better street connections to ease access to
stations, and the creation of an HTRZ. Midvale City RDA Staff has already initiated this process

chance of acquiring these funds for the development.

Available State-owned properties will require collaboration to create more opportunity for the
development to occur. Major commercial and public infrastructure investment will need to move
forward. Looking at other nearby developments and partnering with similar consultants can help
create a more fluid project. Support from the City is extremely important, and the proactive
planning of staff will be the only way to make this possible.
