3. Regular Agenda

3.1) Public hearing and consideration of Brad Long’s request for approval of the BDL
Structures Concept Plan located at 675 West Main Street, with an exception for the building
elevations not meeting the Urban Village Design District requirements.

Applicant Brad Long was present. He expressed his vision for a unique concept that resembles a
college campus in Mississippi. He aims to create a 1,800 square foot, one-story structure with an
art studio atmosphere, offering upscale products like furniture, water features, and hot tubs. The
design is primarily a facade, and the possibility of reversion or expansion could be considered for
the future.

Chairperson Newall opened the public hearing.

John Karpinski- owns land adjacent to BDL's property, expressed concerns about issues caused by
BDL's activities, including Styrofoam pollution, trespassing, and a fire that damaged his pasture
and irrigation ditch. He requested that the Planning Commission enforce zoning guidelines to
protect his property rights. He also had reservations about how the proposed facade development
might affect the neighborhood's character.

Scott Carlson -expressed disappointment with the proposed facade's lack of reference to downtown
Lehi's historic character and art. He urged against granting an exception for its use in the context
of Main Street.

Chairperson Newall closed the public hearing.

During the discussion, there were concerns raised about a concept plan for a property's facade. Mr.
Long clarified that the property in question was not adjacent to the one experiencing issues. Some
commissioners had reservations about the concept's primitive appearance, expressing a desire for
it to fit better into the downtown area. Suggestions were made to improve the concept, including
the addition of windows and a fountain. Zoning issues were also discussed, emphasizing that any
business use of the property would need to meet zoning requirements. Commissioners suggested
private meetings with the applicant and recommended further discussions with staff for the next
steps.

Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to approve without exception Brad Long’s request for
approval of the BDL Structures Concept Plan located at 675 West Main Street, with an exception
for the building elevations not meeting the Urban Village Design District requirements; including
the DRC comments; with the findings as listed in the staff report. He included that since it was
without exception it would need to adhere to the design standards of the area consistent with the
Lehi Development Code. Commissioner Eyre included at Commissioner Everett’s request the
finding that the applicant has not shown clear and convincing evidence that the standards
significantly negatively impact the ability to conduct the proposed use on the subject. The motion
was seconded by Commissioner Everett.

Vote: Commissioner Newall, yes; Commissioner Bunker, no; Commissioner Everett, yes;
Commissioner Britton, no; Commissioner Eyre, yes. The motion passed with a 3-2 vote.


3.2.) Public hearing and recommendation of Lora Hardman’s request for review of the
Hardman Farms, a 67-unit Planned Unit Development (PUD) Concept Plan located at
approximately 3200 North Indian Ford Drive.

Applicant Troy Sanders representing the applicant felt that they had presented a concept plan for
a property that prioritizes heritage preservation and a private gated community with equestrian
amenities.

Chairperson Newall opened the public hearing.

William Wang is concerned about the proximity of the senior living area to his property and the
lack of a buffer zone. He also questions the compatibility of senior living housing with nearby
young families and the impact of police training activities. He's seeking clarification on how the
senior living development aligns with the existing community and surroundings.

The Camp Williams Director of Facilities and Engineering mentioned that the nearby racetrack is
relocating due to an expired lease. They have concerns about a trail entering their property, which
needs approval due to its proximity to Camp Williams. Some of the land is still controlled by the
U.S. government, which could be a future consideration.

Michelle Stallings is excited about the unique addition the concept plan will bring to Lehi, with
the Hardmans aiming to make it a heritage memorial. She appreciates suggestions for better
connectivity and paths but mainly wanted to voice her support for the project, as she usually speaks
against things and wanted to express her positive view.

Chairperson Newall closed the public hearing.

The discussion revolved around various concerns and aspects of a development plan, including
vehicle and pedestrian connectivity, potential changes in standards over time, and the plan's design.
Commission members expressed appreciation for the plan's uniqueness and suitability for the area
while also noting potential challenges such as property issues and power lines. The applicant
addressed questions about trails and power lines. Overall, there was support for the plan's design
and the benefits it offered to the community, with a focus on preserving open space and providing
diverse housing options.

Motion: Commissioner Bunker moved to approve Lora Hardman’s request for review of the
Hardman Farms, a 67-unit Planned Unit Development (PUD) Concept Plan located at
approximately 3200 North Indian Ford Drive; including the DRC comments; with the findings as
listed in the staff report and included one note to refer to the connectivity in the north end. The
motion was seconded by Commissioner Eyre.

Vote: Commissioner Newall, yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes;
Commissioner Britton, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes. The motion passed unanimously.

3.3.) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of Development
Code Amendments to Chapter 12 and Table 12.260, removing the cumulative requirement
for Animal Rights, and an amendment to the Municipal Code, Sections 6-4A-8 and 6-4C-1,
increasing the number of dogs to three.
Chairperson Newall opened the public hearing.

Rudabeh Horsham expressed concerns about the proposed animal regulations. He found the idea
of keeping animals on a quarter-acre property strange and suggested that horses should require at
least one acre. He raised concerns about the potential size of goats and pigs, emphasizing the
likelihood of people mishandling them, causing issues for neighbors and the city. They
recommended limiting the types of animals based on size to avoid potential problems.

Chairperson Newall closed the public hearing.

The discussion focused on concerns about the proposed animal regulations. Members expressed
reservations about allowing various animals, particularly pigs and llamas, on smaller properties
like quarter-acre lots. They questioned the suitability of such small lots, citing worries about noise
and smell. Some members supported urban farming but had reservations about certain animals on
quarter-acre lots in category three areas, with Commissioner Bunker suggesting the possible
removal of pigs on such properties.

Motion: Commissioner Bunker moved to approve Lehi City’s request for review of Development
Code Amendments to Chapter 12 and Table 12.260, removing the cumulative requirement for
Animal Rights, and an amendment to the Municipal Code, Sections 6-4A-8 and 6-4C-1, increasing
the number of dogs to three; including the DRC comments; with the findings as listed in the staff
report his recommendation also included that they do adjust that category three and not allow any
of those animals on quarter acre lots. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Britton.

Vote: Commissioner Newall, yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes;
Commissioner Britton, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes. The motion passed unanimously.

3.4) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of an amendment
to the Master Transportation Plan adding a future offshore freeway along the north side of
Utah Lake to the plan.

Before the item was discussed Craig Chambers clarified that the general plan must precede the
master transportation plan, addressing confusion from a previous meeting. He emphasized that the
Planning Commission's responsibility is to make recommendations or not, rather than solving
issues. He also noted that the Master Transportation Plan is a long-term framework, and changes
may not result in immediate construction. The discussion focused on the legislative aspect of the
item and the Commission's role in making recommendations.

Mr. Seegmiller and Mr. Dinsdale presented this item. Mr. Seegmiller discussed significant
population growth in Lehi, Saratoga Springs, and Eagle Mountain, projecting a quadrupling of
traffic on Pioneer Crossing. UDOT's corridor study revealed Pioneer Crossing is already over
capacity. Different road development scenarios and their impact on travel times and property were
presented. Emphasizing the importance of considering an offshore freeway, a request was made
for UDOT to evaluate all potential solutions. Lake crossing feasibility and timelines remain
uncertain. Ross Dinsdale highlighted the need to address Lehi's traffic issues, regardless of lake
bridge developments.

Chairperson Newall opened the public hearing.

Greg Duerden, State Chair of the Independent American Party, raised concerns about a proposed
road development concept in Lehi. He highlighted potential environmental and aesthetic impacts
and criticized the approach of agencies like MAG and UDOT. He opposed the idea of building a
road across Utah Lake or the Jordan River, urging the commission to deny the concept.

Gabe Lefort, President of the Utah Valley Aero Modelers Club, expressed concerns about the
proposed road development's safety risks near their model airplane flying area close to the Jordan
River. He emphasized the importance of preserving the area for future generations to enjoy the
hobby. He also highlighted the community and charitable aspects of their club, along with their
recent FAA recognition. He urged consideration of environmental impacts.

Jeremy Schell, a Saratoga Springs resident, and small business owner in Lehi, expressed concerns
about the road development's impact on obstructed views of the lake and potential noise pollution.
He suggested that relocating the road further south from the shore or elevating it could be more
acceptable. Additionally, he proposed connecting the road on the west side of the lake to the east
side as a potential solution.

Jeremy Howlett opposed the road proposal due to its potential impact on views, wildlife, and
neighborhood serenity. He suggested integrating public transit into the plan as a more sustainable
solution.

Todd Robins, a Loch Lomond resident, stressed concern for the omission of public transit from
the Master Transportation Plan and emphasized the need to create more walkable and livable
communities. He advocated for reducing car dependency and exploring alternative solutions to
address congestion and improve residents' quality of life.

Susan Wilson expressed her concern about the environmental impact of having a freeway over the
lake in Utah County. She urged the committee to recommend more creative and sustainable
solutions for transportation instead of simply building more roads.

Angie Park, a Lehi resident, urged the committee to advocate for public transit as a sustainable
and environmentally friendly solution for future transportation. She emphasized the need to reduce
car dependency and promote clean air and public transit for the next generation.

Rudabeh Horsham inquired about the future plans for 2100 North, and Mr. Seegmiller explained
that it is designed, with construction beginning in 2026, to accommodate different traffic
movements, particularly those traveling to and from Salt Lake County, while Pioneer Crossing
serves more trips heading to Orem, Provo, and the south.

Michelle Stallings inquired about the lake bridge project's schedule and expressed concerns about
its proximity to the Jordan River's mouth. She wanted to confirm if its inclusion in the master
transportation plan would make it an option for UDOT to consider. Mr. Seegmiller affirmed this.
She also appreciated that transit options were being explored.


Jeremiah Steenbok supports the freeway due to population growth and traffic congestion but
acknowledges concerns about its placement. He believes public transportation alone may not
suffice as most travelers are heading south, requiring additional solutions.

Ms. Harris summarized the online comments noting that many comments were similar to those
heard previously, with various concerns and suggestions regarding the proposed freeway. The
majority of comments were against it, citing environmental impacts. Some supported the idea of
transit as an alternative, while a few believed the freeway would alleviate traffic issues.

Chairperson Newall closed the public hearing.

The Commissioners inquired about who ultimately decides the final option among various
alternatives once they are presented. The staff indicated that an environmental impact study
conducted by UDOT will select a preferred alternative, but input and constructive feedback from
residents can influence the process. The alignment has not yet considered many factors, and a final
decision will be made based on a set of rules. Removing or updating the master plan based on
study conclusions is also possible. The importance of providing constructive input and
participating in the process is stressed.

As the meeting progressed, Commissioner Britton raised additional questions about the approval
process, potential removal of options from the master plan, and clarifies that the current motion is
about approving a conceptual idea for further study, not a final alignment. Commissioner Bunker
expressed his support for the plan, emphasizing the need for forward-thinking and planning for the
future.

Motion: Commissioner Bunker moved to send a positive recommendation of Lehi City’s request
for review of an amendment to the Master Transportation Plan adding a future offshore freeway
along the north side of Utah Lake to the plan; including the DRC comments; with the findings as
listed in the staff report. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Everett.

Vote: Commissioner Newall, yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes;
Commissioner Britton, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes. The motion passed unanimously.
