7. Ordinance 2022-1 amending Provo City code to adopt policies and procedures for the
dedication of private streets and utilities. Citywide Application. (PLOTA20210360).
(1:15:49)

Motion: Animplied motion to approve Ordinance 2022-1, as currently constituted, has been
made by council rule.

Bill Peperone, Director of Community and Neighborhood Services, presented.

Councilor Shipley asked Mr. Peperone for feedback on the wording of the ordinance regarding strictness
and safety.

Mr. Peperone mentioned that the document has been modified to include suggestions from the public
and is comfortable as it is written.

Councilor Fillmore asked for clarification regarding taxes paid.

Mr. Peperone responded that after a lot of research, taxes would be an insignificant consideration.
Councilor Fillmore also asked for an explanation on a separate ordinance.

Mr. Peperone responded that the language of this ordinance is sufficient.

Councilor Ellsworth discussed some concern from citizens and asked for clarification.

Mr. Peperone said the language was selected to allow some discretion for the CRC executive group to
make suggestions.

Councilor Ellsworth also asked about the roads meeting current standards.

Mr. Peperone clarified that the CRC group could approve roads not up to current standards. He said the
language allows for some flexibility for the group to consider alternatives that have a benefit to the city.

Councilor Handley asked if some private streets would become a bigger safety hazard to the public if the
city didn’t incorporate them.

Gordon Haight, City Engineer, responded.

Brian Jones said this ordinance was intentionally drafted to be an interactive process and provided
examples.

Councilor Fillmore asked a few clarifying questions in which Mr. Jones and Mr. Haight responded.
Chair Hoban opened public comment.

Daniel Snow of Provo stated his concerns with how the ordinance is currently written.

With no other public comment, Chair Hoban brought discussion back to council.

Brian Jones mentioned there are always safety valves for citizens and reiterated the importance of
making this an interactive process.

Councilor Shipley stated that he is concerned that the council will have many lining up to ask for money.
He stated he does not want to open the doors for everyone to come to council because it has the
potential to be an easier process than going through the CRC.

Councilor Ellsworth agreed with Councilor Shipley and said the ordinance may require some future
adjusting and may be trial and error.

Councilor Handley mentioned that asking the council to use taxpayer funds would be a hard ask. He said
he supported the draft as it stands and is comfortable on voting on ordinance tonight.
