       The proposed amendment also includes clarifications to the purpose of notice section
       and variance processes. Underthe purpose of notice section, the changes address public
       notice issues by allowing the reviewing body (Planning Commission or City Council) to
       consider challenges to notice. Previously, the code only allowed the Planning
       Commission to consider challenges to notice, which did not address issues with noticing
       for City Council meetings. The variance process has been updated to provide a public
       hearing, bringing the process in line with other, similar review types (conditional use
       permits, subdivisions, etc.)

       All the proposed changes are intended to update noticing standards to match state code,
       respond to complaints with noticing, and clarify outdated language.

       B.     CONSIDER A PROPOSED TEXT AMENDMENT TO MODIFY THE
              DEVELOPMENT REVIEW PROCEDURES FOR CERTAIN MUNICIPAL
              FACILITIES IN JORDAN BLUFFS SUBAREAS 1-3DEVELOPMENT
              (JB1-3) ZONE
Alex Murphy said in 2019 and 2020, and then amended, a zoning ordinance for Subareas
1-3 of the Jordan Bluffs zone. This ordinance established goals for the district, review
process requirements, and standards for commercial, residential, and mixed-use
development based on a "pod" concept anticipated to guide future development in the
atea.

As projects have been approved, it has become clear that the strict reliance on pod
boundaries to drive review procedures has created challenges. Two recent examples
include a sewer vacuum pump station needed to service Wasatch's Phase 3 project in
Subarea 4 and a boat ramp proposal for the Jordan River Parkway Trail. Under the
current ordinance, these major projects affected pod, adding time and cost to the review
process, and tying their installation to future development proposals that do not currently
exist.

This is an unanticipated impact of the review procedure laid out in the code. To address
the issue, Staff is proposing two amendments to Section 17-7-10.12.2 (Development
review process) that would streamline future projects.

The first amendment would provide an alternate review path for municipal facilities 1
under lz acre in size. These projects would go through standard permitted use/site plan
review process defined in Chapter 17-3 rather than completing the long Large Scale Plan
process described in the zone.

"Municipal facilities" means those improved properties owned by the municipality or
public. (17-2-13) Common examples include parks, public safety facilities, public utilities,
trails, etc.

The second amendment would remove the requirement for all municipal facilities to
complete a Development Agreement. This avoids possible scenarios where the city is
required by its own ordinances to enter into an agreement with itself.

       C,   CONSIDER ADOPTING CULINARY WATER AND SEWER USAGE FEES
Kyle Maurer said ln December 2020, the City Council approved a Water Master Plan,
which identified the need for $26 million in projects and $2.3 million per year in
replacement of aging pipes and facilities. The City, after a competitive bid process,
selected the consulting firm of Lewis, Young, Robertson, and Burningham to complete a
Water and Sewer Comprehensive Financial Sustainability Plan. Since April 2021, City
Staff have discussed with the Mayor and City Council a number of alternatives to fund the
needed projects.

The final proposal is a 4 tier rate structure, with the cost of water usage increasing by tier.
This proposal will encourage conservation and meet the state's requirement of having a
water rate structure that encourages conservation. Overall, the increase will generate an
additional 8olo a year in revenue for the Water Fund. Sewer rates are proposed to increase
10% the first year, and 8Yo thereafter (no change to billing structure). Staff will be
comparing actual revenues and expenses to the financial sustainability plan yearly.

lf approved, the new rate structure will go into effect with billings after September 1,2021.

X.      DISCUSSION ITEM
       A.      DISCUSSION REGARDING THE REACTIVATION OF THE MIDVALE
               MURAL PROJECT
Kate Andrus said on October 15,2019, staff and Council discussed the proposed Midvale
Murals Program. The proposed program's goal was to allow the City to partner with
private property owners to increase the number of public art installations in Midvale. At
this meeting, the Council asked that a pilot program be created to test its efficacy.
On November 19,2019, City Council approved a Resolution to set aside funds in the
amount of $4,500 to implement the discussed Midvale Murals pilot program.
Unfortunately, the COVID-19 pandemic started shortly after it was approved, and the
project was stalled and was not able to come to fruition before the end of the fiscal year.
Despite the events that took place over 2020 there still exists the desire to implement
public art installations throughout the City, therefore staff will restart the Midvale Mural
pilot program with the goal of creating three new murals by the end of the new fiscal year.
As a refresher to the program, the process for implementing this project is as follows:

1. A property owner will submit an application with the following:
   . Property owner's affidavit,
   . Detailed mural description including drawings, dimensions, materials, and artist
     information,
   . A photo of the wall the mural will be painted on,
   . A timeline for the installation of the mural.
2. Applications will be reviewed by the Mural Selection Committee consisting of
   .   Council Member
   .   RDA Representative
   .   City Planner
   .   City Communications Representative
   .   Community Development Director

3. lf approved, a property owner can submit proof of costs (receipts, invoices, etc.) for
reimbursement after the mural is successfully installed.

4. The city will reimburse up to the lower of $30 per square foot of mural or $1,500 per
mural (the City may recommend additional funding based on individual projects and
funding availability)

5. The property owner must maintain the mural for five years.
