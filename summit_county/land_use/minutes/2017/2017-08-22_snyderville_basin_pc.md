5. Continued public hearing and possible action regarding a proposed amendment 
to the Silver Creek Village Development Agreement; Southeast Corner of I-80 and 
US-40; Matt Lowe, applicant – Jennifer Strader, Senior Planner 
 
Planner Strader said the applicant is proposing to amend the Silver Creek Development 
Agreement (DA) by adding language that would allow for the platting of 283 lots that 
would be used for affordable housing on five parcels within the subdivision.  The 
current agreement requires that each development project provide a proportionate 
share of affordable housing to achieve integrated development.  The affordable housing 
units would be spread throughout the project if the current agreement is followed.   
 
It was noted that three Commissioners were absent from the August 8, 2017 meeting.  
At that meeting, the Commission voted to continue the public hearing.  They wanted to 
give the applicant an opportunity to clarify the proposed language, provide further 
information on the development, and to submit an analysis of what demographic is 
being targeted, the unit pricing and sizes, and the types of units being proposed.   
 
Planner Strader said the majority of the language remains as was proposed at the 
August 8
th meeting.  The last sentence has been added.  This sentence essentially states 
that the affordable housing units will be platted and credited to future development 
applications until such time that the overall market-rate to affordable units fails to meet 
the minimum established within the DA. 
 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
August 22, 2017 
Page 10 of 52 
 
The current language requires 35% of the first 271 units to be affordable housing.  That 
number is 95 units.  The language goes on to say that thereafter, each development shall 
provide a minimum of 25%.   
 
Planner Strader said the applicant is proposing to plat 283 lots for affordable housing 
units on five parcels.  Staff calculated the coordinating number of market-rate units 
would be 740.  Because the language proposes to plat the units, and not construct them, 
it is possible for the 740 market-rate units to be constructed without any affordable-
units being built.  A slide showed the location of the parcels.   
 
Planner Strader said with the proposed language, the affordable units would not be 
integrated throughout the development as was intended by the DA.  Concentrating units 
on a limited number of parcels would likely result in a limited number of styles, types, 
and sizes.  The current language requires the affordable units to be located throughout 
the project and integrated within the community.  More than 95 units would be 
provided.  As each plat is developed, the affordable units would continue to be 
provided.  No amendments to the existing DA would be needed.    
 
Planner Strader described the different applications that have been submitted for the 
parcels in this area.  The existing Development Code requires any project to provide 
20% affordable housing.  The total number of units being proposed is 347 in the current 
application.  She said that 35% of this number would be 121 affordable housing units; 
20% would be 69 units.  The applicant is proposing to build 117 units.       
 
Planner Strader said Staff still has concerns about conflicts with the language of the 
existing DA.  She said one of the conflicts is found in the sections that refer to the 
affordable units being consistent with the character of the surrounding neighborhood.  
It states the exterior materials and design shall be the same as the market-rate units.  If 
the affordable units are built before the market-rate units, how will the County oversee 
consistency?   
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
August 22, 2017 
Page 11 of 52 
 
Planner Strader said another example of inconsistency is in Section 5.2.2.17.  It states 
that if a non-profit organization enters into an agreement with the County, it shall 
establish the type of units, the targeted income, the pricing, and the timing of the 
project.  The DA doesn’t allow the transfer of the property until the non-profit has 
provided this information.  That information has not been provided.     
 
Planner Strader said the proposal also includes a request for a height amendment for 
parcel 22.1.  It would amend the height from 32’ to 36’.  Staff hasn’t seen any plans, nor 
seen any justification of why that amendment should be granted.   
 
Planner Strader said that Staff recommends the Planning Commission hold a public 
hearing and vote to forward a negative recommendation to the County Council as 
outlined in the Staff Report.
