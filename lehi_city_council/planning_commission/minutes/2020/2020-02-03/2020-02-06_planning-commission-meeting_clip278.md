Work Session, 5:30 p.m.

1. Call to Order
Commissioner Hemmert welcomed everyone to the meeting and excused Commissioner Ellis.

2. Discussion Item

2.1 Neighborhood Commercial Regulations

Sierra presented information on the City's ordinance that is currently in place. She overviewed the
current code and how it is regulated and specified regulation on the building footprint, building
height, and operating hours. Sierra talked about ways to regulate lot size, building footprint, lot
coverage, and building height. Cities that were compared with Neighborhood Commercial were
South Jordan, Salt Lake City, Draper, Riverton, and Provo.


The Commissioners and Staff discussed what would be appropriate for square footage, setbacks,
buffers, lot sizes, and parking to be considered for Neighborhood Commercial. The
Commissioners and Staff discussed NC Regulations and Ordinance options at length.

Sierra Pierson stated Salt Lake has a 4 acre max and stated that we set some criteria or suggested
a cap. Ross Dinsdale stated we want our commercial areas to have walkability. Sierra asked if the
Commissioners were fine with the building height that is currently in the City Ordinance. The
Commissioners stated they are fine with that height. Mike West asked about architectural element.
Commissioner Peterson stated that some elements should be allowed, but the verbiage would have
to be drafted differently.

2.2 Bed and Breakfast regulations

2.3 Short Term Rental regulations

Brittney Harris presented information on the current Bed and Breakfast and Short Term Rentals
ordinance. The City Council reviewed the item on November 19, 2019 and gave feedback. Council
recommended that Bed and Breakfasts are zoned commercial, are unique or old homes, have a
sing indicating use, and does not need to be owner occupied. Council recommended the use for
Short Term Rentals that they don’t need to be owner-occupied while a guest is staying there, limits
the number of people, and has a building inspection or meets hotel requirements.

The Commissioners and Staff discussed the impact of Bed and Breakfasts, regulating Airbnb's,
and concerns that were City specific. Sandy City had a specific community study that Brittney
discussed. Brittney presented information where Lehi did an ADU versus Bed and Breakfast cost
comparison study. Brittney defined BNB's and STR's.

Commissioner Eyre asked what Planning's thoughts were and what the City wanted to do regarding
the issue. Commissioner Everett asked about the historic district and asked about doing away with
BNB's and only allowing STR's.

Brittney Harris stated they would rather regulate the rentals than ignore them and said the different
zones were designated for different regulations and she would review them.

Brittney presented the proposal and details on Bed and Breakfast. The Commissioners and Staff
discussed BNB’s.

Brittney presented the proposal and details on Short Term Rentals. The Commissioners and Staff
discussed STR’s.

Commissioner Nielsen stated an upfront fee to an ADU in Lehi is too expensive. Kim Struthers
stated the fee may be lowered for a certain period of time. Commissioner Peterson stated one of
the reasons an ADU fee was put in place was affordability, but if we make it so difficult to afford,
we end up losing the data of ADU’s. Commissioner Everett stated the City should lower the fee
for obtaining the ADU permit and raise the fee for not having a permit when required.

The Commissioners and Staff discussed the item and proposals at length.

The applicant, Brad Mackay, was present. He stated that the strip of property to the North is

Commissioner Hemmert closed public hearing on agenda item 5.1

        5.2 Public hearing and recommendation of Ivory Development’s request for review
of the Jonsson Park Towns Preliminary Plat, a 59-unit residential development located at
approximately 700 South 1900 West.

Brittney presented clarifying information on agenda item. She stated there is a development
agreement that is concurrent with this request that will go directly to City Council, and not to the
Planning Commission, that is specific to Ivory Ridge and Holbrook developments as well. The
developments include the trail connections and concrete fence between the Jonsson property and
the adjoining property, which is Utah Power and Light.

Commissioner Peterson asked about the open space requirements.
Brittney stated that there were some multifamily amenities that were required.
Commissioner Peterson asked about open space for multifamily residential in City code and
asked if they were meeting any of that. Brittney stated that it does meet the requirements due to
the connection points of the trail connectivity and the accessibility.

Commissioner Nielsen asked if the City required any low impact development where the City
could utilize the big areas of detention for more useable green and open space, as opposed to
slope side and unusable space. Ross Dinsdale stated that the detention areas and the way that it is
laid out will incorporate more open space and stated the applicant will be able to address that
more. Ross Dinsdale said the State is implementing a new rule regarding LID standards and has
a guide that the City will be incorporating into their standards. Commissioner Eyre asked if
Street B or A is cutting through. Staff clarified that it will be street B. Commissioner Nielsen
asked if there is any architectural variation on the back of the homes or requirement to prevent a
solids line of garage doors. Kim Struthers stated they do require variation from the back.

The applicant, Brad Mackay with Ivory Development, was present. He stated last time he was
present a concept plan had been submitted and has been revamped. The suggestions that were
submitted have been added to the new request. The sidewalk on Johnson park lane has been
added, walks projecting out onto 700 South have been added, sidewalks have been added on both
sides of Street B, and pedestrian access points have been added on to the future trail. Brad has
been working with the Jacklin family for the trail site access near their property so that it is
beneficial for the City and the Jacklin family. The trail will most likely be placed to the South
and hug the southern property line of Utah Power and Light Property. The fence will be placed
on the North side of the trail so it doesn’t disturb the agricultural rights of the Jacklin’s and
serves the best interest of the City. One of the changes of the building setbacks is for building
articulation by creating more flexibility where it engages the pedestrian interface by pulling
some of those units forward. The applicant is working with the Property Rights Coalition to
introduce deep well points as a use, as well as using pervious pavement, where DRC suggested
using pavers for plat A, which would be considered pervious pavement. They are meeting the
City standards as of now, but they could change as of March 1, 2020 with the new State
standards.

Commissioner Nielsen asked if there was any on street parking. The applicant stated that there
was not. Commissioner Hereth asked if the driveways were deep enough to fit two vehicles.
Applicant stated they were. The interior units are single car garage, single car driveways.
Commissioner Nielsen asked if the depth is deep enough where it would not block the sidewalk.
The applicant stated the minimum is 18 feet for each driveway.

Commissioner Hemmert opened public hearing for agenda item 5.2

Thad Pinkerton stated his concern is vehicle traffic. He stated Lehi City puts out a temporary
sign that reduces speed periodically. He is concerned that there is a school where traffic increases
and he is concerned about the kids, not the traffic flow. The units on 2300 West increased traffic
flow. His other concern is fire and secondary water. How are the fires going to be prevented with
the water being so low. He just wants his kids safe.

Brian Houston shares the property line with the detention pond. He stated the last meeting that
was held showed an overlay to tie into Snow Springs Drive. He asked if that was still happening.
He asked what would be replacing the fencing that was coming down. Would it be a 6 foot fence,
and what material. He wanted to know about the pathway that runs just South of the current
detention pond and the direction of the trail. He expressed his concern that 59 units was a lot of
units. He wanted to know if there was an idea of price per unit. Is it similar to the development
on Main Street. He isn't concerned about parking; he just hopes the City is there to help with
that.

Ryan Wright stated he is concerned about the children and animals in his backyard. He would
like a replacement of his fence. He is also concerned of the parking near Willowcreek, but
overall just wants a new fence if it is removed.

(Inaudible) She is concerned about the cement wall for the pasture for Utah Power and Light.
Her property is half mink farm and half pasture and wants to make sure that she gets a cement
wall as part of the agreement. She also asked how there would be trail access from the wall. She
asked if she would get a fence and what the green space would be used for.

Commissioner Hemmert closed public hearing for agenda item 5.2

The applicant responded and answered the questions from the public comments. He stated fire
protection and speed can be addressed by Ross Dinsdale. The fire marshals looked at design and
fire hydrant layout and public works looked at the PI system and found it to be satisfactory. He
stated there will only be a fence along the Jacklin property and they are working with them to
determine how far off the property line it would need to be. He said price for the units has not
been determined, but typically their units are around the three hundred thousand range, but that is
a broad answer. Commissioner Nielsen asked about the connecting road to Snow Springs. The
applicant stated it can’t be done until Patterson Construction develops their property, but Ivory
has stubbed their property line assuming it will tie into Patterson’s property. Commissioner
Nielsen asked about the power pole. The applicant stated it would be up to the developer on how
they chose to design that in the future. Commissioner Nielsen asked about the existing fence to
the North and if it would stay in place. The applicant stated it is on Rocky Mountain Power’s
property and he assumed it would stay in place. He stated the mink farm is currently on Rocky
Mountain Power’s property as well. He stated the demo is being conducted by Mr. Jonsson and
he is relocating his operation out to Cedar Fort. Commissioner Nielsen asked who would be
maintaining the trail area. The applicant stated he would only be negotiating the right of way for
the trail and the maintenance agreement would have to be entered separately.

Ross Dinsdale stated that 700 South 80 foot right of way is planned for 7,000 cars per day and
will add another 590 cars to that. Commissioner Everett asked what the current count is right
now. Ross stated there is not a current count right now and the most recent count is from 2014.
He also stated that 700 South also has a cycle track and this development will also provide the
City with the property to complete the cycle track. Commissioner Hemmert stated that Kerry
Evans didn't have any concerns. Commissioner Hereth asked about the water pressure. Ross
Dinsdale stated that recent improvements have been made and a water line has been installed to
supply water to this area to help with the pressure swings. He stated that Jordan River will also
help supply water to the area.

Commissioner Eyre asked about ecological concerns regarding cleanup or biological waste with
the mink. Commissioner Peterson stated any environmental issues that could be considered was
if there were a fuel tank or something similar. Commissioner Hereth asked about the
developmental agreement and if it will impact the site plan. Kim Struthers stated that all three
areas are covered, and in this specific area it says that there can only be 59 units. He stated there
were minor setbacks to make sure it fits in the PUD.

Motion: Commissioner Bunker made a motion to recommend approval of Ivory Development’s
request for review of the Jonsson Park Towns Preliminary Plat, a 59-unit residential
development located at approximately 700 South 1900 West; including all DRC comments
and the findings stated in the report. Commissioner Eyre seconded.

Vote: Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Everett, no; Commissioner Peterson, no;
Commissioner Hemmert, abstain; Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes;
Commissioner Hereth, yes. Motion passed 4-2.

The applicant addressed the Commission with some updates that are environmentally friendly
and coming standardized with their developments and hoped to have more updates with each
