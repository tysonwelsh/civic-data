  b. Change of Nonconforming use, Single-family dwelling 2526 Monroe Ave.
     The applicant, Ms. Teresa Bramwell, stated that she is trying to rebuild her house, which was
     destroyed, due to a fire. She indicated that the home was built in 1912 and is a nonconforming use
     within the neighborhood commercial zone.

      Mr. Simpson stated that the property had been before the Commission a few months prior for an
      expansion of nonconforming use. Because more changes have been made, after further
      engineering analysis, this is before the Commission again. Mr. Simpson reviewed the previous
      approvals and stated that the new proposal is to increase the roof height and remodel the exterior of
      the home. The request to increase the roof height, by approximately three feet, would allow for more
      living space, creating a master bedroom and bathroom upstairs, with additional storage above the
      garage. The proposal for the exterior of the home is to leave the existing brick exterior, add
      windows, shake shingle, and vertical board and batten exterior finishes. Mr. Simpson stated that
      because the residential zones within the East Central do not allow vertical lines on the main body of
      the home, staff is recommending to only do the gable ends with board and batten if the decision is
      to use it. Staff recommends the approval of the height increase.

      Mr. Simpson stated that there are concerns that the contractor has with being able to repair or clean
      the burnt brick. The contractor is recommending painting the brick. Mr. Simpson indicated this is a
      concern because painted brick is not allowed within the East Central unless it has already been
      painted. Staff recommends that the brick not be painted, rather cleaned as best as possible.

      Ms. Bramwell stated that she is happy to put lap siding in place of the board and batten. She
      indicated that the ash and dirt from the fire is not likely to come clean which is why the contractor is
      recommending painting the brick. Discussion continued among the commissioners regarding
      painting the brick.

      MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to approve the expansion of
             nonconforming use to increase the roof height and remodel the exterior of the existing
             single-family home based on the findings that the expansion of the non-conforming use
             will not adversely impact surrounding properties and the expansion will be compatible
             with the surroundings. Approval is subject to the following conditions.
                   1. Limiting board and batten to the gables and exterior colors be earth-toned
                   2. The applicant providing professionally created site, landscaping, and building
                       construction plans and satisfying staff requirements through a building permit
                       review.
                   3. If the applicant’s contractor determines that the marred brick cannot be cleaned
                       that the brick be painted white with some accents.
             Motion was seconded by Commissioner Aaberg and passed 5-1, with Commissioners
             Aaberg, Blaisdell, Safsten, Shale, and Sandau voting aye and Commissioner Shinoda
             voting no. Commissioner Shinoda felt the brick should remain unpainted.

4. Conditional Use Permit, Change of nonconforming use, church conversion to medical office, 705 23rd
   St.
   The applicant, Mr. Shane Sanders, reviewed the history of the building and stated that cheaper
   materials had been used on the exterior of the building and need replaced. The proposal is to redo the
   exterior finishes with materials that will last longer, utilizing a concrete composite siding, and colors will
   be used that are compatible with the surrounding neighborhood. Site upgrades will also be made
   including removing the existing three approaches and adding a larger one on the west side that will
   provide better access to the parking lot, with landscaping also added. The interior of the building will be
converted to medical suites.

Ms. Morgan explained that the church is currently vacant and located within the R-3 East Central zone.
The proposal is to convert it to professional medical offices which is not an allowed use. However,
because the church is a nonresidential building within a residential zone, a conditional use permit may
be considered. Ms. Morgan reviewed the considerations for the conditional use permit. She stated that
the proposal is to have eight medical suites that would be split between consultation and treatment
areas, with operating hours from 7am to 6pm. She explained that the code states that operating hours
should be within 8am and 6pm. Upgrades of the exterior would be finished with materials consistent to
the neighborhood, the addition of new bay windows on the west side of the building, and new
landscaping with a seating area are also being proposed. Ms. Morgan presented the proposed
elevations and floor plan of the building.

Stating that the reuse will not adversely impact the desirability, stability, and character of the area, she
indicated that within the block there are three prominent vacant buildings, and the proposed use would
enhance the stability of the area by minimizing opportunities for crime, vandalism, and transient
activity, which has been reported by the neighbors. The exterior upgrades and landscaping would add
to the character of the area. Ms. Morgan indicated that the footprint of the building will not be changed,
only the exterior finishes, upgrading the parking lot, and landscaping. Explaining that landscape islands
would need to be added to the parking lot, which would reduce the parking stalls, she stated the
reduction of parking stalls would require that they reduce the interior office space slightly. Ms. Morgan
indicated that the addition of screening on the adjacent property lines would be required with the
current chain link fence needing privacy slats added or the fence would need to be replaced with either
vinyl or wood fencing.

Staff recommends approval for the conditional use permit subject to the following: removing the
existing drive approaches and installing one approach on Madison Avenue, that meets width
requirements; reconfiguring the parking lot by replacing three parking stalls with landscape islands and
in turn reducing the interior office space to meet the reduction in parking spaces; locating a dumpster
with code compliant screening and closure; screening along the property lines abutting adjacent
residential properties; completing a lot combination; adjust operating hours to 8am to 6pm; and satisfy
all staff requirements.

Ms. Teresa Bramwell, resident at 2526 Monroe, inquired what kind of medical office the proposal would
be. She voiced her concern about so many medical office buildings being established within a
residential area. She stated that more residential housing is needed, specifically within the
neighborhood addressed.

Ms. Catalina Ramirez, resident, agreed that the area is residential and suggested that the property be
used as a dog park.

Mr. Sanders addressed the Commission stated that the proposal is for professional offices which would
include specialists, rather than a clinic. The church had several offers but wanted to find a tenant that
would serve the community, which is why the developer purchased the property.

  between the UDOT facilities and single family homes. He stated that when these kind of developments
  arise it is common to have many challenges, including being able to provide space for the fire
  department to turn around. The property is currently zoned R-1-6 and the proposal is to go to an R-2
  zoning to help accommodate a group dwelling project for duplexes. The property is 7.2 acres and is
  made up of multiple parcels, which would require a subdivision plat process as one of the criteria. Mr.

  Wall Ave and has taken special care to meet other provisions of the rezone, such as not isolating

9. Public Hearing, Zoning Ordinance Text Amendment, height limits in R-3 zones and adjacent to
  residential and open space zones.
  Mr. Brierley stated that the request is to amend the zoning ordinance relating to height limits in the R-3
  zones. The proposal is to modify the height limits to set a maximum height of four stories or 50 feet, to
  modify the setback standards adjacent to residential open space zones, and to modify height standards
  for nursing homes in the R-3 zone. He explained that the school district has a piece of property that they
  have declared surplus, within the R-3 zon,e and the City Council has had multiple discussions about the
  potential future uses of the property. When considering what could go on the property, they asked what
  the maximum height limit was in the zone. Because there is not currently a height limit, Council asked
  staff to propose a maximum height limit for the R-3 zone and find out what the Planning Commission felt
  a reasonable maximum height limit would be.

  Mr. Brierley reviewed the current height limits within the Code which include residential densities, height
  of other residences in the block, limit next to residential or open spaces, limit on nursing homes, and
  general plan limits on specific sites. He then reviewed and explained each “limit” with examples from the
  community, pointing out that each project was two to three stories. When looking at what would be a
  reasonable height limit in the R-3 zone it was determined that four stories would allow a developer a lot
  of creativity and design, while maintaining density standards.

  In reviewing OMC 15-4-5J2, Mr. Brierley noted that the diagram and the text do not match. For that
  reason, staff is recommending that it be corrected to read “Any building or structure in a multiple-family,
  commercial, or manufacturing zone proposed to be in excess of thirty five feet (35’) tall that is next to a
  single-family residential or open space zone shall be set back thirty five feet (35’) plus one foot (1’) for
  every foot the building is taller than thirty five feet (35’).” Mr. Brierley stated that the diagram should also
  be corrected to match the text. He also reviewed the height limits for nursing homes, which is currently
  two and a half stories or 35 feet. Staff is recommending that nursing homes meet the same limits of
  other structures.

  Noting that a significant amount of correspondence was received regarding the property at 29th Street
  and Buchanan, which is zoned R-3, Mr. Brierley reviewed the property and how the proposed standards
  would affect it. Stating that the property is very sloped and it is in a sensitive overlay zone, he indicated
  that there are many limits on what can be done on the property. Because the property is also within the
  Mount Ogden Community Plan, it already has a four story height limit. The proposed changes would not
