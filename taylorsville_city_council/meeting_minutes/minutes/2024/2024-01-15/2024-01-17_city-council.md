6:15 P.M. BRIEFING SESSION

As Chair Curt Cochran had been excused from the meeting, Vice Chair Meredith Harker
conducted the briefing session, which convened at 6:00 p.m. A roll call was conducted
wherein all council members were present except Chair Cochran.

1.     Review Agenda

Vice Chair Harker briefly reviewed the agenda for the regular meeting. When asked about
the surplus property, Assistant City Administrator/CFO Scott Harrington explained that the
bicycles would be donated to West Valley Police who would have them refurbished and
Taylorsville City Council Minutes

turned over to a bike collective. The firearms would be sold to a licensed federal dealer and
the vehicles would be disposed of in a variety of ways, depending on what was most
profitable for the city.

Regarding proposed mid-year budget adjustments, Mr. Harrington explained that he would
send some information to the council members electronically and planned to meet with
each of them individually in the next two weeks to go through the various proposed changes.
He would go into more detail at a public hearing that would take place in February.

Councilmember Burgess inquired about some drilling that was taking place along 1300
West. City Engineer Ben White responded that Dominion Energy was replacing a high-
pressure line, and their pipes would go under 4700 South and across the golf course.

Council Member Knudsen reported that a “No U-Turn” sign needed to be re-erected at 4700
South 2700 West .

Vice Chair Harker requested an update on the street light replacement project. Mr. White
responded that all the street lights owned by the city which were west of Bangerter Highway
had been replaced. The next group of replacement lights had been delivered and public
works personnel would install them while also keeping the roads clear of snow, doing crack
seal, etc.

2.     Adjourn

Vice Chair Harker declared the briefing session adjourned at 6:05 p.m.

1.     WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

Vice Chair Harker called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and welcomed those in
attendance. A roll call was conducted wherein all council members were present except
Chair Cochran who was excused due to illness.

1.1    Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Council Member Knudsen (Opening
       Ceremonies for February 7, 2024 to be arranged by Council Member Burgess)

Council Member Knudsen led the Pledge of Allegiance before reciting the following two
quotes of Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr.:
  “We all have the drum major instinct. We all want to be important, to surpass others,
  to achieve distinction, to lead the parade, and the great issue of life is to harness the
  drum major instinct. It is a good instinct if you don’t distort it and pervert it. Don’t give
  it up. Keep feeling the need for being important, keep feeling the need for being first,
  but I want you to be first in love. I want you to be the first in moral excellence. I want
  you to be the first in generosity.”

 “Everybody can be great because anybody can serve. You don’t have to have a college
 degree to serve. You don’t have to make your subject and verb agree to serve. You only
 need a heart full of grace, a soul generated by love.”

       1.2     Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Kristie Overson explained that she had recently attended a ChamberWest
Legislative Affairs Committee meeting where legislative priorities were addressed. The

committee would meet twice weekly during the legislative session which would begin the
following Monday.

The Utah Taxpayers Association had also met the previous week and heard from Governor
Spencer Cox, Speaker of the House Mike Schultz, Congressman Blake Moore, and Zions
Bank Senior Economist Rep. Robert Spendlove. They shared some valuable information
regarding what to expect during the coming session.

After that meeting, she and John Hiskey went to the Utah League of Cities and Town’s
Legislative Policy Committee meeting. The primary topic of discussion was Gov. Cox’s First
Homes Initiative and how it would likely affect local municipalities. The Mayor pointed out
that there were plenty of homes available in Taylorsville and that a first home did not need
to be a new home.

Earlier in the day, she and other elected officials participated in Local Officials Day with the
Youth Council. Among other things, Rep. Jim Dunnigan took the Youth Council on a tour of
both the House and Senate in the Capitol. They also sat in on a committee meeting before
participating in a mock debate about housing. It was a fun event which helped the kids learn
more about Utah’s legislative process.

Scott Brown and the Board of Trustees at the Salt Lake Community College had begun their
search for a new president since Dr. Deneece Hufftalin had announced her intent to retire.
Mayor Overson greatly appreciated that the school had sought the City’s input on their
recruitment effort. The school had 60,000 students so it was a huge member of the
community.

City leaders had recently met with UDOT representatives regarding the new I-215
southbound offramp which would be located roughly midpoint between 4100 South and
4700 South. It would add an additional lane of travel as well as some landscaping. Work was
expected to commence in the summer.

The Mayor offered a big thanks to Unified Fire paramedics and firefighters. She explained
that they did much more than put out fires. Taylorsville’s station no. 117 was the 3rd busiest
among all the UFA stations in the valley. She appreciated everything UFA did for Taylorsville.

Mayor Overson recently had the opportunity to be a part of a planned SWAT operation. She
described it as a well-planned, well-executed event that she found very eye-opening.

3.     REPORTS

             3.1       Historic Preservation Committee Report – Susan Yadeskie

      Committee Chair Susan Yadeskie reported on activities the Committee volunteers had
taken part in since her last report. Those activities and projects included making
improvements to the outdoor grounds of the Heritage Center. Grass was removed and
replaced with used brick, old chicken coops were removed, and fencing was repainted. A
large desk which had been donated was refinished, benches were stripped and repainted,
and a garage door was replaced.

The Committee had participated in the Trick or Treat event and hosted many gatherings at
their facility. Ms. Yadeskie greatly appreciated Kris Heineman and the Youth Council for all
the assistance they provided at various events. She also spoke highly of the kids and leaders
from Odyssey Youth who helped clean the Heritage Center after Christmas.

Ms. Yadeskie had been delighted about the success of the Fall Festival which involved
tractors, goats and sheep, a quilt show displaying the work of Taylorsville residents, tours of
the school house, blacksmith shop and museum. The committee members thoroughly
enjoyed interacting with the community.

As always, Saturday with Santa was a very popular event with games, refreshments and of
course Santa Claus in attendance.

             3.2       Taylorsville Bennion Improvement District Report – Mark Chalk

      General Manager Mark Chalk explained that with the recent storms, the snow totals
listed on the slides in the agenda packet had changed. Therefore, he had brought updated
slides to share. He displayed a slide showing the snow water equivalent from a week ago
and then showed one that was updated which took the snow levels up to an “average” level.

This time last year, Utah’s reservoirs (excluding Lake Powell and Flaming Gorge) were
averaging 82% full. He noted that Lake Powell was extremely low, although Flaming Gorge
was doing alright.

City Administrator John Taylor asked if Tay-Benn monitored ground water. Mr. Chalk
responded that they did and that its level did not dip the way surface water did.

     6.2       Discussion Regarding Mid-Year Budget Adjustments to the 2023-2024
               Fiscal Year Budget – Scott Harrington

      license/land use fees)
    An additional $45,400 in non-departmental expenses (insurance, advertising, and
      network/telephone)
    An additional $49,000 in Government Building and Citizen Committees (stone
      repairs, increased Taylorsville Dayzz funding, and garbage removal)
