Proposed Resolution for Historic Memorial: Presenter: D. Jacob Summers, Deputy City Attorney

Mr. Summers explained that this proposed resolution is because of a previous city council
meeting. He gave an overview of the Historical Preservation Advisory Commission. This
commission works in an advisory role regarding the identification and protection of local historic
and archaeological resources. In 1995, the city adopted a Historic Marker design by A. Dale
Brown. Mr. Summers continued to explain the historical marker issues to resolve are what is
considered historic and an audit needs to be done of current markers in Orem. Mr. Summers is
not proposing any criteria on historical markers. This resolution is to provide city staff on how to
proceed. The proposed actions are to (1) reaffirm that it is in the best interest of the city, (2)
direct the Historic Preservation Advisory Commission to develop criteria, (3) requests the
Historic Preservation Advisory Commission create a process of recommendations, (4) request
the Historic Preservation Advisory Commission prepare a report identifying existing markers, (5)
direct City staff to explore and develop potential options, (6) direct staff to continue efforts to
preserve unique nature of land within the PF zone, and (7) request that City staff works towards
the planning and development of other memorial monuments.

Ms. Lauret asked if we should have a brand or similar look. Mr. Summers thinks it
would be appropriate to have similar markers. Mayor asked how many markers we currently
have. Mr. Bench reported that there are about 20 of the original markers. Mayor Young would
like to see us hire a design firm to give proposals. Mr. Lambson would love to see our
communications team to share these markers with the Orem residents. Mr. Bybee suggested
putting benches at these locations. Mr. Peterson would like to see the City tie this into the
SCERA Heritage Museum. Ms. Millett would like to see the City seek for a location for a
Women’s and Children’s park with a monument.

Metropolitan Water District of Orem Annual Report Presenter: Chris Tschirki, Public Works
Director and Reed Price. Assistant Public Works Director

Mr. Tschirki explained that Mr. Brown is not able to attend. He introduced the current
board and staff. Mr. Tschiki gave some history of the Metropolitan Water District of Orem. The
district was created in 1935 to protect water in the City of Orem. The boundaries of the Water
District have changed over the years. Orem City has an excellent portfolio of water resources,
including groundwater, surface water and spring water. He continued that we have shares with

several irrigation, canal or other water associations. We don’t own the water but we are
shareholders in those companies and that gives us the right to use the water. We also have water
as part of a contract with the Central Utah Water Conservancy District in the late 1970’s. We pay
1.89 million dollars for that water annually and will continue to pay that through 2047 when the
contract will need to be renegotiated.

Mayor Young asked if the water fee is variable. Mr. Tschirki explained that the amount
we pay per acre foot does not vary and will stay the same until 2047. Ms. Lauret asked how our
10 million gallon water tank plays into this. Mr. Tschirki informed the council that this is the
difference between storage and supply. That storage tank is 10 million gallons and could supply
the city for one day. Mr. Tschirki continued and reported that as our population has increased our
water usage has gone down. He believes that our residents are being better water stewards.

Mr. Tschiki moved on to speak about our Deer Creek Intake Project. This is a 98 million
dollar project. This will replace the intake structure that is under about a hundred feet of water.
That is currently under construction today and it will be completed next year. Deer Creek
services about 1.5 million people. If this structure fails there would be no Provo River and no
water for irrigation or drinking water. This makes this project super critical. Orem’s overall cost

for this project is about $400,000. Water impact fees have reduced and that water revenue has
been used to fund water projects.

The board works with the City staff on the following projects: the Provo River Watershed
Council, water supply monitoring, our springs and other water resources available, well projects,
and water tank projects. Mr. Tschirki reported on the adoption of new policies in fraud reporting
and our recent fraud reporting score went up and our audit gave us a bill of clean health. The
Metropolitan Water board has donated millions of dollars to Orem City and has a symbiotic
relationship with Orem. Both have the goal of providing safe drinking water to the city.

Mr. Price spoke about some items that the water division has been working on. There are
3 departments in the water division: supply, distribution and utility. He explained what each
division does. Supply gets the water to our pipes using springs, surface water and wells. The
supply department received a perfect score on the Sanitary Survey conducted by the State of
Utah. Distribution maintains the pipes and gets the water to the meters. They respond to tickets
for water line repairs and meter repairs. They respond 24 hours a day if needed. The utility group
is responsible for finding out how much the customers use. This group is replacing all of the
meters in the city with smart meters.

Ms. Millett asked for clarification on the micro bubbles that were noticed in the northeast
area of Orem. Mr. Tschirki explained that the micro bubbles are air bubbles. The water tastes fine
and is clean but high volume of water causes the air bubbles. The City went out and checked the
lines and the water to make sure it was safe. Ms. Millett thanked the water department for their
service.

Mr. Peterson has first hand knowledge of the dedication of our water department from

our water meets and probably exceeds the water quality of bottle water. Mr. Tschirki reminded
the council that the water department does a yearly water report that can be found on the Orem
website.

Before concluding, Mr. Tschirki wanted to recognize LaDell Gillman for his years of
service. Mr. Gillman spoke about his time with the Metropolitan Water Board. He has loved his
time on the board and gave recognition to Stella Welch, the former Mayor.

Recertification of the Orem Municipal Justice Court. Presenter: D. Jacob Summer, Deputy
City Attorney

Mr. Summers informed the City Council about the need to recertify the Orem Municipal
Justice Court. The Orem City Justice Court opened September 1, 2010. The jurisdiction of the
court is for Class B and Class C misdemeanor, violations of ordinances and infractions
committed within the city limits. The judge is Judge S. Parkin who has served since 2010. There
is a small staff that supports Judge Parkin, as well as public defenders and prosecutors. The court
has thousands of cases each year. The court is expected to recertify every 4 years and currently
our certification will expire January of 2024. Mr. Summers explained that it is required that an
affidavit be completed by Judge Parkin, an opinion letter is submitted from the City Attorney and
a resolution be adopted by the City Council requesting recertification.

Mr. Spencer asked about complaints. Mr. Summers explained that all courts have
complaints and the nature of the case is adversarial and emotional, but that complaints are
handled as needed.

Ms. Millett moved to adopt by resolution the recertification of the Orem Municipal
Justice Court. Ms. Lauret seconded the motion. Those voting yes: David Young, Debby Lauret,
Jeff Lambson, David Spencer, Terry Peterson, Tom Macdonald, and LaNae Millett. The motion
passed.
