Recognizing the late Weber State University Track and Field coach Chick
Hislop for his contributions to the Ogden community
A memo from Council staffregarding recognition of Charles “Chick” Hislop, late Weber State University (WSU) Track
and Field Coach, for his contributions to the Ogden community came before the Council for consideration. Council member Hyer
read the certificate ofrecognition issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Caldwell and presented Mr. Hislop’s son, Lance,
with the certificate.
Lance Hislop thanked the Mayor and Council for recognizing his father; his father loved Ogden City and raised his
family here. He and his own father subdivided the rolling hills area at the mouth of Ogden Canyon. He spent his entire career in
Ogden, starting as a sixth-grade teacher, moving to Ben Lomond High School, and eventually to WSU in 1969, where he stayed
for the remainder of his career. He discussed some of the highlights of his father’s collegiate career and the success of the teams
that were coached by him. He was respected by coaches and teams across the nation, one of which commented on the significant
global changes Mr. Hislop made on the sport that he coached. When his father retired from coaching, Ogden City created a “Chick
Hislop Day” in the community and that was one of the things that he treasured for the remainder of his life.
Former Council member Stephens commented on his relationship with Mr. Hislop and the contributions he made to the
City of Ogden; he was coached by Mr. Hislop and is intimately aware of the impact that he made on many young men. He also
loved his family deeply and that was clear to anyone who knew him. He had high standards, showed respect to all, and
communicated to people how important they were to him. Mr. Stephens concluded he is a better person today for his association
with Coach Hislop and he thanked the Council for giving him the opportunity to provide his comments. |
Vice Chair Richey also commented on his association with Coach Hislop; one thing that many are not familiar with are
his philanthropic efforts toward the athletes he coached. He loved his athletes and placed high value on their academic
performance along with their athletic performance.

Representatives from Ogden Fire and Public Services will review the City’s
preparations for flooding :
Ogden City has been closely monitoring this year’s snowpack and preparing for potential flooding in the city.
Representatives from Ogden Fire and Public Services will review the city’s preparations and provide recommendations and safety
information to residents. Concerns regarding issues related to flooding may be reported by calling 801-629-8271.
Fire Chief Mathieu used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to report to the Council on the events on Douglas Street last
week; a flooding concern was reported to the Fire Department for the Douglas Trailhead area. A captain responded to the area but
saw very little water and did not think the issue would become worse. He dismissed the event and did not notify anyone else.
However, the flooding worsened and around 7:30 p.m., and an engine responded to the area and found over 100 people who had :
come to the area to help divert water to the storm drain. Public Services was eventually notified, and personnel were dispatched to
help clean debris and place sandbags to keep water in the gutters. The scene was monitored overnight, and the situation worsened.
He provided photos taken of work done by canal personnel to help mitigate the flooding damage. He noted that the Mayor’s
office, Chief Administrative Officer, and City Council were notified of frustrations in the community about the City’s response.
He acknowledged the shortcomings of his Department and general City coordination in responding to the situation. Internal
meetings were held to address the lack of communication and coordination and a process was developed to ensure that the same
thing did not happen again on future flooding events. The following day, another area along Harrison Boulevard was flooded and
this process was key in an effective response and mitigation of continued flooding. He shared his theories on the causes of the
Douglas Trailhead flooding and noted it is important to know the cause in order to understand how so much water came down the
area so quickly and to ensure that the City can respond appropriately in the future; he believes an ice dam broke and released a
/ — great deal of water at a very high rate of speed. He played videos showing the flow of the water.
Chair Choberka asked if people were calling 911 or the number listed above, 801-629-8271. Chief Mathieu stated they
were calling 911.
Vice Chair Richey stated he was impressed by the response from the residents in the area. Chief Mathieu agreed and
stated they did a great job of assembling and coordinating mitigation efforts.
Council member Nadolski stated he agrees with Chief Mathieu regarding the cause of the flooding, after which Council
member Hyer referenced a similar event in North Ogden on Fruitland Drive; he asked if the same dam break could have caused
flooding in that area. Chief Mathieu stated that is his assumption and North Ogden contacted Ogden City for assistance by way of
additional sandbags. |
Public Services Director Lowder added that he and Chief Mathieu are working together to ensure open communication
between their Departments in an effort to reduce their response time on future flooding events. He then used the aid of a
PowerPoint presentation to discuss the City’s general flooding preparation efforts. His Department is monitoring rivers and
streams throughout the City and have spent the last couple of months removing debris from the rivers at points of concern; he
provided photographs of high-water areas, drone monitoring exercises, and the establishment of sandbagging locations. He
presented a graph illustrating the increased stream flows over the past week as well as a chart illustrating the snow water
equivalent in the State of Utah this year compared to past years. Pineview Reservoir holds 110,000-acre feet of water and the
water shed currently has 309,000-acre feet, or enough to fill the Reservoir three times over. There will be a point in time when the
water flowing into the Reservoir will exceed the amount of water that can be safely released from the Reservoir and timing of
those two events is very important to avoid overflowing the banks of the Reservoir. This is a very hard thing to manage and as a
result, the watershed is at 222 percent of normal. He discussed his Department's efforts to secure and fill enough sandbags to aid
in flooding response, which includes deployment of sandbags to areas where they are truly needed. He provided photos of areas
throughout the City where sandbags have been placed to increase the height of the bank of the Ogden River at locations
throughout the City.

Mr. Miller noted he is currently receiving daily reports from the State of Utah regarding flooding throughout the State,
resources available from the State, snowpack levels, and weather risks/outlook. He read the following statement that has been
placed on the City’s website, social media channels, and through Weber Consolidated Dispatch: “With the rapid rise in
temperatures, and now more snow, flooding is on everyone’s mind. Ogden City Fire is working diligently with Public Services
and City officials to monitor the rivers and flood situation within our city. We could use the public’s help to be our eyes and ears
for any spontaneous runoff, specifically on the East Bench where there is potential from the snow melt. A spontaneous runoffis a
small stream of water or creek created by rapid snow melt that does not follow the normal water flow. These runoffs could be a
potential hazard to homes and infrastructure. Early notification of abnormal water flow in your neighborhood will help Ogden
City Public Services and Fire Department divert the water flow away from homes and back into the rivers. Report flooding or
areas of concern by calling 801-629-8271 during business hours. For after-hour emergencies, please contact 801-395-8221.”

Discussion among the Council and Mr. Miller centered on the City’s volunteer recruitment process; the use of

notification systems (reverse 911) to notify residents of flooding events that could impact them; the responsibility of private
property owners in responding to flooding; and the State’s declaration of a state of emergency, which will allow the City to be
reimbursed for costs associated with responding to flooding events.

Mayor Caldwell added that every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday for the next two months, all Department Directors are
meeting in a standing meeting to receive a status update on flooding and work collectively on the City’s response to the events.

answers; she thanked Chief Mathicu for his honesty and acknowledgement of shortcomings and the need to adjust a processto |
