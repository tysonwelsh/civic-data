1.     SUBDIVISION, CHRISTI SMITH IS SEEKING APPROVAL OF “THE JORDAN
       ACRE”, A THREE-LOT SINGLE-FAMILY RESIDENTIAL SUBDIVISION IN AN R-4
       ZONE. LOCATED AT 11951 SOUTH 1300 WEST.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the request involves
The Jordan Acre Subdivision, which has been before the Planning Commission on a few
previous occasions. Notice of the public hearing was sent to neighbors within 300 feet. The
subject address is 11971 South 1300 West and is on the east side of 1300 West. The
surrounding zoning to the north and south is zoned R-1-22. The General Plan is Medium-
Density Residential, which corresponds with the R-4 zone. A Conditional Use Permit was
requested and granted earlier in the year to maintain the existing fences on the north and
east. The applicants were also granted a fencing exception on the south to allow for white
vinyl fencing and permission to access lots on a private lane.

Mr. Prestwich displayed a map of the proposed subdivision and informed the Commission
that the front portion of the property is to remain as one lot. Lot 101 comprises the entire
frontage. It is a family property and the family member that lives in the home wants to
maintain the larger lot. The applicant would still be able to subdivide in the future after going
through the subdivision process.

Lots 102 and 103 are accessed from the private lane that runs down the middle of the overall
parcel. The lots were determined to meet the standards of the R4 zone of at least 10,000
square feet. Mr. Prestwich presented the Grading and Drainage Plan that includes a swale
along the side of the hard surface lane. The fire turnaround is also hard surface. Detention

Commissioner Rushton asked about the allowable distance between an animal barn and a
residence. As a result, he did not understand why a plot would be approved that places a
home within 15 feet or less of a barn. He was also concerned about potential conflicts
resulting from the barn being near the south fence because it runs along property lines.
Mr. Prestwich confirmed that this happens often with properties in transition from RR-22 or
R-1 to lower densities and is not a problem with the ordinance. He remarked that existing
animal rights will not be impacted by the proposal.

Commissioner James’ understanding was that it is possible to take the area to the north and
make it a legal lot in the future. With regard to fencing, he asked how the ordinance
addresses two different uses and who is responsible for installing the appropriate type of
fencing. Mr. Prestwich explained that in this instance, even though the General Plan calls
for the area to be single-family residential with one-quarter acre lots, the developer would
construct an eight-foot masonry fence because there are existing animal rights. The
ordinance gives the Planning Commission the ability to make an exception recognizing that
some properties will transition out.

2.     ZONE TEXT CHANGE, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING CHANGES TO TITLE 18
       REGARDING DISTANCES FROM RESIDENTIAL DWELLINGS TO STRUCTURES
       FOR KEEPING ANIMALS.

Mr. Prestwich reported that a complaint was received from a neighbor regarding chickens
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                  5
September 10, 2020

that were being kept on a neighboring property. Code enforcement visited the site and
discovered that there is a conflict regarding how far animals or an animal shed can be from
the home or zoning boundary. The ordinance requires it be 40 feet from a dwelling. The
General Regulations specify that no agricultural animals may be kept or maintained within
75 feet of a dwelling or kept within 40 feet of any zoning district that prohibits such use.
Mr. Prestwich explained that if there is a conflict, staff can only enforce the requirement that
is the least burdensome on the owner.

The proposed change would specify that no agricultural animals or fowl shall be kept within
40 feet of any residential dwelling and no structure for the keeping of agricultural animals or
fowl shall be constructed or maintained within 40 feet of a residential dwelling. Specific
examples were discussed. Mr. Prestwich further stated that if a home is built near an already
existing animal shed, the shed need not be removed.

Mr. Prestwich commented on the issue of backyard chickens and stated that in 2008 it
became an issue. The City Council ultimately settled on an ordinance that allowed chickens
in residential zones. They are also allowed in special districts on lot sizes of 10,000 square
feet or larger. He clarified that the proposal would not impact residential chickens and
resolves the ambiguity.

Mr. Prestwich explained that despite there being an exception for residential chickens, there
is a restriction in the R2, R3, and R4 zones, which allow only six hens to be kept. Roosters
are not allowed in these zones. Mr. Prestwich commented on pet goats, which were
approved as therapy goats for autistic children. He explained that two goats of a special
breed that are licensed by a veterinarian are allowed in any zone.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing. There were no public hearings. The public
hearing was closed.

Commissioner Rushton moved that the Planning Commission recommend approval
of the Zone Text Change to Section 18.135.090 for Agricultural Structures as shown
with an exception to read as follows: “No structure for the keeping of agricultural
animals or fowl shall be constructed within 40 feet of a residential dwelling.”
Commissioner James seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous
consent of the Commission.
