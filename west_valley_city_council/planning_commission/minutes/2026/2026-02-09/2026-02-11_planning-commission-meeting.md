S-1-2026
Daybury North Subdivision – Preliminary Plat
5332 W Highbury Pkwy
R-1-8 Zone, 43 Lots, 12.85 Acres

BACKGROUND:
Chris Toronto, with Wasatch Acquisitions & Capital, is requesting preliminary approval for the
Daybury North Subdivision. The subdivision will be in the R-1-8 zone. Properties to the east and west
are Zoned RM, property to the north is zoned C-2, and property to the south is zoned R-1-8. Adjacent
land uses include the Highbury Pinnacle apartments to the east, the Arbor Square townhomes to
the west, single family homes to the south, and a medical clinic to the north. There is a development
agreement on this parcel. Staff will summarize that later in the staff report.

STAFF/AGENCY CONCERNS:
Fire Department:
    1. Fire hydrants to be installed in accordance with the Uniform Fire Code.

Granger Hunter Improvement District:
   2. Project will need to run availability for water, sewer and fire protection.
   3. Subject to design and review inspections.

Utility Agencies:
    4. Subject to all standard easement locations.

Public Works:
   • Subject to the West Valley City Engineering Standards.
   • Will need to evaluate proposed storm drain system and retention areas as shown on the
       plat.
   • Follow recommendations in the soils report.

Building Inspections:
   • Follow recommendations outlined in the soils report.

ISSUES:
   • The applicant is requesting a subdivision consisting of 43 lots on 12.85 acres. The entire
     parcel is undeveloped. The overall density of the project is approximately 3.3 units per acre.
     Lot sizes range from 8,125 square feet to 18,872 square feet. The average lot size in the
     subdivision has been calculated at 10,09 square feet.

   •   As mentioned previously, there is a development agreement for the parcel, dating back to 2012.
       There are only two items in the agreement:
          o The property shall be developed as single family detached homes.
          o All lots shall be at least 8,000 square feet in area.

       There is nothing regarding housing standards in the agreement. All new homes in the
       subdivision need to meet the current housing standards outlined in 7-11-200P.

   •   Access to the subdivision will be gained from an existing access point off Highbury
       Parkway. An additional access point will be installed off Daybury Drive. No lots will front the
       existing streets, but 15 lots will have double frontages. A new 54-foot public right-of-way will
       serve all the lots and create a loop through the subdivision.

   •   There is a City-owned canal/waterway running along the entire eastern boundary of the
       parcel, which then terminates into a City-owned holding pond on the north end of the site.
       Similar waterways are adjacent to lots in the Highbury Place Subdivision. The ordinance
       requires fencing in these situations. Along the back of affected lots in the Highbury Place
       phases, a black wrought iron fence was installed. To be consistent, staff recommends that a
       similar fence be installed along the backs of the new lots adjacent to the waterways.

   •   As many of the lots will also have two frontages, the rear yards along Highbury Pkwy. and
       Daybury Dr. should also be fenced. There are similar lots in the Highbury Place
       Subdivision and those lots have a Trex-style fence for screening of the back yards. For
       consistency throughout the area, staff recommends that a similar fence be used for these new
       lots.

STAFF ALTERNATIVES:
Grant preliminary subdivision approval for the Daybury North Subdivision subject to the following
conditions:

   1. That compliance be made with Granger Hunter Improvement District regarding water line
      extensions, sewer connections and fire protection.

   2. That the subdivision name and interior street names be approved by Salt Lake County
      Addressing Division.

   3. That recommendations outlined in the soils report be followed. The developer/builder shall
      coordinate groundwater issues with the Building Official and Engineering Division.

   4. That street dedication and improvements be in accordance with plan and profiles approved by
      the City Engineering Division.

   5. That this subdivision comply with all provisions of the development agreement.

   6. That all new homes meet current housing standards.

   7. That wrought iron fencing be used along the backs of lots that border waterways and that Trex-
      style fencing be used along the backs of lots along Highbury Pkwy. and Daybury Dr. The
      fencing shall be similar in style to fencing that was installed for lots in the Highbury Place
      Subdivision.

Continuation, to address issues raised during the public hearing.

Discussion: Brock Anderson presented. Commissioner Drozdek asked if there was a traffic study. Brock
replied that a traffic study wasn’t required.

The applicant, Nate Rowe, was available in person. Chris Toronto was available online. Commissioner
Winters asked if the architecture would match the nearby homes. Nate and Chris confirmed that the
development plans would follow R-1-8 zoning requirements and maintain architectural harmony with
the surrounding area. Chair Lovato clarified that the northern lots along the pond are also adjacent to a
waterway and he is in favor of those lots having the wrought iron fencing in the rear yards as well.

Motion: Commissioner Winters motioned to approve SA-1-2026 subject to the 7 conditions in the Staff
Report. Commissioner Porter seconded. A voice vote was taken, and all were in favor of the motion.

Jonesdale Subdivisions Amended
3565 S 5750 W
R-1-8 Zone

BACKGROUND
Meridian Engineering, on behalf of The Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT), is requesting
consideration to vacate Lots 1-9, a portion of 5750 West Street & a portion of 3575 South Street of
Jonesdale Subdivision, Lot 210 & a portion of 5750 West Street of Jonesdale Subdivision No. 2, Lots
301-303, a portion of 5750 West Street & a portion of Darle Avenue (3610 South) of Jonesdale
Subdivision No. 3, Lots 10-14, a portion OF 5750 West Street &
a portion of Darle Avenue (3610 South) of Jonesdale Subdivision No. 4, Lots 601-610, a portion of 5750
West Street & a portion of Masters Street (5770 WEST) of Jonesdale Subdivision No. 6, Lots 811, 818-828
& a portion of Masters Street (5770 West) of Jonesdale Subdivision No. 8.

ISSUES:
The affected parcels were all acquired by UDOT several years ago for the Mountain View Corridor
project. All Mountain View improvements have been built, and the corridor has been open in this section
since June of 2021. This action will simply clean up all the old parcel lines that exist and officially vacate
rights-of-way.

The amended plat will vacate lots in six different phases of the Jonesdale Subdivisions. It will also vacate
a portion of four streets throughout these phases. These phases of the Jonesdale Subdivisions were
recorded in the office of the Salt Lake County Recorder between 1954 and 1972. Many lots and streets
were previously vacated by UDOT for other areas of Jonesdale Subdivisions. The proposed amendment
will create two parcels. Parcel A will contain most of the mentioned lots and all of the vacated streets.
Lot 9A will be a small parcel that contains a parking lot for the Mountain View Corridor trail.

All the affected rights-of-way will be vacated by ordinance. The ordinance will have no force or effect on
any franchise rights of any public utilities, nor will they be impaired thereby. The ordinance will also
protect any holders of existing franchises, water drainage pipelines, or other such uses as they may
presently exist under, over or upon said property or as are or may be shown on the official plats and records
of Salt Lake County.

STAFF ALTERNATIVES:
  1. Approval of the described Jonesdale Subdivisions Plat Amendment.

   2. Continuance to address issues raised during the Planning Commission meeting.

Discussion: Brock Anderson presented. Commissioner Durfee asked him about the handful of lots to the
south of 3500 South. Brock explained that those lots were not part of the Jonesdale Subdivision and thus
are not part of this application.

The applicant, Travis Williams, representing Meridian Engineering, was available and didn’t have
anything new to add to Brock’s presentation.

Lewis Nielsen asked when Mountain View Corridor will be completed. Steve Pastorik answered that
there isn’t a firm date and UDOT would fit the future lanes within the right-of way property that they
already own. UDOT won’t be taking any more of their properties.
