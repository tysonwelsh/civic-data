Amemo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
approving an honorary street name designation. The memo stated the City has received a request for the honorary street name
“Miles Goodyear Avenue” be added to A Avenue and that the honorary street name “Pomona Avenue” be added to B Avenue.
Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
petition for honorary street name designations. Petitioner Brad Timothy requests that the honorary street name “Miles Goodyear”
' be added to A Avenue from 24" Street south to its terminus south of Lake Street. The petitioner also requests that the honorary
--“ street name “Pomona” be added to B Avenue from 24" Street south to its terminus south of Lake Street. He provided historical
information about Miles Goodyear; he was an early trapper and fur trader in the Utah area. He established Fort Buenaventura near
the confluence of the Ogden River and Weber Rivers. He is considered by many to be the original European descent settler of
Utah. He lived at the fort along with his Ute wife Pomona until the fort was purchased by James Brown at the request of Brigham
Ee
Young. Consistent with previous honorary street name processes, the city would produce and install the honorary street signage,
but the petitioner would be responsible for the financial costs of both. Council staff confirmed this responsibility with the }
petitioner on Thursday, December 1, 2022. The Planning Commission reviewed this item on November 2, 2022. The Commission
found that the proposed honorary street names would serve a public purpose, support General Plan goals and policies, be
consistent with the layout and street plan of the city, and not create negative impacts on emergency services, utilities, services,
deliveries, the traveling public, or the residents with addresses on the street. The Planning Commission recommended:
1. That the City Council adopt the honorary street name “Miles Goodyear Avenue” to A Avenue and “Pomona
Avenue” to B Avenue from 24" Street south to their termini south of Lake Street.
2. That the honorary street name signs not be placed at 24" Street to avoid confusion, since 24" Street has the
honorary street name of “Martin Luther King Jr. Street.”
3. That a creative solution be found to avoid the conflict to allow recognition at 24" Street.
4. That the Council update its policy on honorary street names in general.

Mr. Brierley presented optional signage for the honorary street names and indicated there is some concern about the
intersection of 24 Street and Martin Luther King, Jr. Street because there would be four different street signs in one location,
resulting in a cluttered look and confusion. He then referenced a letter from the Diversity Commission, dated November 22, 2022,
which recommended that there not be an honorary street name “Miles Goodyear Avenue”. Citing the history of Mr. Goodyear and
specifically his implied mvolvement with slavery, the Diversity Commission claimed that supporting a request to name a street
after him is contrary to the Commission’s strategic plan, which is to “promote equity, cultivate positive cultural awareness,
promote cultural traditions, and enhance the well-being and quality-of-life of those living in or visiting Ogden, Utah.” Despite
their strong opposition to Miles Goodyear Avenue, the Commission did not directly address or provide an opinion on the proposed
honorary street name of Pomona Avenue.

‘commemorative street name’ in order to remember the history rather than honoring it. What happened at Fort Buenaventura is

Amemo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider resolutions
authorizing the execution of a sales tax agreement for bonds associated with the WonderBlock project and authorizing a master
lease agreement with the Municipal Building Authority (MBA) for lease revenue bonds for the WonderBlock project. The memo
stated the proposed Interlocal Sales Tax Pledge Agreement with the Ogden Redevelopment Agency (RDA) will provide security
for the Utah Sales Tax and Tax Increment Revenue Bonds proposed to be issued for the WonderBlock project. If approved,
Ogden City would pledge sales tax revenues received by the City as a repayment source for the RDA bond debt service, not to
exceed $75 Million. The Agreement would terminate upon refunding or payoff of the Bonds. Although the City is pledging sales
\ and use tax as debt service, the actual funding source will be tax increment generated by the WonderBlock project and lease
_/ — revenue from Business Depot Ogden (BDO). The memo further explained the proposed Master Lease Agreement with the MBA
will authorize the issuance of Lease Revenue Bonds in an amount not to exceed $85 Million dollars. Proceeds of the bonds will
be used to construct two parking structures in the WonderBlock project. The Master Lease Agreement will commence with
issuance of the bonds.

Ogden School District Property

On December 13, 2022, the Ogden City Council passed a resolution declaring the City’s intent to purchase four
properties deemed surplus by Ogden School District (OSD). Per state law (§53G-4- 902), once the Council passes the resolution
declaring intent to purchase, the City has 90 days to deliver to the school district an earnest money offer to purchase the surplus
property at the purchase price. The initial proposal from the City’s administration was to purchase three of the four properties for
the purpose of developing housing. However, the Council felt that further discussion should be had in order to explore all options
for the future use of the property. If it is determined that not all properties should be purchased and the City does not deliver an
offer to purchase the property, the City forfeits its exclusive right to purchase the property and the property will be sold by the
district on the public real estate market.

Public Services Director Lowder, Recreation Manager Bridge, Parks and Cemetery Manager Stewart, and Deputy ,
Community Development Manager Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss Administration’s presentation
regarding the OSD properties. Mr. Smith reviewed the schedule of the City’s consideration of the properties declared as surplus by
the OSD and summarized the Administration’s evaluation of each of the four properties, including the appraised value of the
properties, recommendations to purchase or waive the purchase of the properties, potential future use of the properties, and
funding sources for the purchases. He presented a map of the City to identify the location of each of the properties, after which he
referenced the documents and plans that Administration relied upon to formulate their recommendations: the Quality
Neighborhoods Initiative, Community Plans, and Parks and Recreation Master Plan. The intent of the Quality Neighborhoods
Initiative is to stabilize and revitalize Ogden’s neighborhoods as “neighborhoods of choice” by establishing a pattern of public
investment that catalyzes desirable and appropriate community development. The Community Plans that were examined for this
matter include the Mt. Lewis Community Plan, Southeast Ogden Community Plan, and the Lynn Community Plan. He discussed |
the assets and goals of each Community Plan areas that relate to parks and open space.

Discussion among the Council and staff centered on the age of the community plans that were consulted; and
connectivity of the neighborhoods and ease of access to the parks and open space within the areas.
