3.1 Public hearing and recommendation of Adam Albrecht’s request for review of the
Yeoman’s General Plan Amendment, changing the land use designation from LDR (low


density residential) to HDR (high density residential) for approximately 5-acres of property
located at 143 South 1100 West.

    Sierra Pierson, City Planner, presented information on the agenda item.

    The Commission discussed rights about vested plans. Commissioner Hemmert asked Legal if
    the Commission should discuss the item as current property use or future property use and
    whether it complies with the future plan. Legal suggested the item should be discussed as a
    vested current plan and how the laws stands as the current meeting is being held. The
    Commission discussed land use designations that would fit under the current general plan.
    Amenities and open space was discussed with Staff. The Commission acknowledged that the
    applicant’s narrative is location based pointing out access to I-15, Redwood Road, and other
    main corridors.

    The applicant, Adam Albrecht, was not present.

    Commissioner Hemmert opened public comment on agenda item 3.1

    Public comment was entered into record.

    Commissioner Hemmert closed public comment on agenda item 3.1

    Commissioner Hemmert does not feel like it fits with the current general plan, but it does fit
    with the updated future plan. Commissioner Hereth stated the applicant is asking for an
    amended plan and it may fit with that plan and the public feels like they are being bypassed if
    we base our decision on the future plan. Ross Dinsdale stated the Mayor has asked that
    Engineering conduct a high level analysis of the proposed general plan to see what the
    impact will be. Commissioner Nielsen stated that a high level analysis traffic report should
    probably be conducted first before an item like this is approved to see what kind of impact
    the general plan will have. Commissioner Everett stated that the Commission should consider
    what the current plan is, and he respects Commissioner Hereth point that the applicant has a
    right to amend, but it takes away the rights and the power of the process, and he appreciates
    the public taking the time to be heard. Commissioner Everett stated that adding increased
    traffic while adding mitigating factors could only be detrimental, and the inconsistency to the
    surrounding area is clear criteria they are trying to tie it to access main traffic corridors. He
    doesn’t feel that it fits in with the surrounding areas or that is a gain to health or welfare to
    the City. Commissioner Nielsen stated the residential fits in with the area, it is just a different
    type of residential and from character standpoint it is just different. Commissioner Nielsen
    stated the neighborhood fits and will act as a buffer to the single family homes and acts as a
    transitional space from Main Street. Commissioner Bunker stated he understands there are
    concerns, but he gets frustrated when he attends trainings that say great neighborhoods are
    made of a good mix of condos, apartments, and single family homes. He stated that all homes
    have great people that live in them. Commissioner Jackson agrees with Commissioner
    Bunker and his greatest frustration as trainings are attended is the value of mixed
    communities and that should be fostered. Commissioner Jackson feels great sympathy for
    those that took the time to write in their public comment and wants to make sure the cart isn’t
    before the horse and feels that HDR infrastructure with single family homes are part of that.
    Commissioner Hemmert stated that Commissioner Bunkers comments really resonated with
    him and that he also does not feel that HDR brings in terrible people. Commissioner Everett
    stated that he might disagree with the characteristic of HDR, not being the people, but the
    look and the feel of the environment of the infrastructure, traffic, and quality of life.

    The applicant, Adam Albrecht, was present to address the Commission. He stated they made
    a general plan amendment request because they would like to keep moving forward with the
    project and figure out what their options are. He would like to mention that 1100 West is
    slated to be a major road and they are able to shift gears if necessary. He stated he would like
    to create a nice community and they have submitted a conceptual design.

    Commissioner Hemmert entertained a motion.

Motion: Commissioner Everett moved to provide a negative recommendation for Adam
Albrecht’s request for review of the Yeoman’s General Plan Amendment, changing the
land use designation from LDR (low density residential) to HDR (high density residential)
for approximately 5-acres of property located at 143 South 1100 West, based on the findings
that the general plan amendment is inconsistent with the current general surrounding area, and
that it hasn’t been demonstrated that there is a positive gain for public health, welfare and
general safety for Lehi City and based on public comment there is a perception that there is a
negative impact, that the justification is consistent to the draft of the general plan land use map
that cannot be considered as it is draft and it would take away the resident’s ability to comment if
this is approved. Commissioner Peterson seconded.

Vote: Commissioner Hemmert, yes; Commissioner Nielsen, no; Commissioner Peterson, yes;
Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Bunker, no; Commissioner Hereth, no;
Commissioner Eyre, no. The motion failed.

The Commission discussed reasons why the vote failed and why they voted the way they did.
Discussion ensued amongst the Commissioners.

Motion: Commissioner Bunker moved to provide a positive recommendation for Adam
Albrecht’s request for review of the Yeoman’s General Plan Amendment, changing the
land use designation from LDR (low density residential) to HDR (high density residential)
for approximately 5-acres of property located at 143 South 1100 West, including all DRC
comments and findings based on the report. Commissioner Nielsen seconded.

Vote: Commissioner Hemmert, no; Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Peterson, no;
Commissioner Everett, no; Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Hereth, yes;
Commissioner Eyre, yes. The motion passed 4-3.

    Everett stated the main haul road pulls out on 2100 North where there isn’t a light and
    wondered if it would be better if they utilized the light. Commissioner Eyre stated his guess
    is they would use the primary route when it isn’t high traffic time, but during higher traffic
    they would use the secondary route. Ross Dinsdale stated 2100 North can accommodate a lot
    of traffic.

    The applicant, Jimmy Nielsen, EYP, and Cory Morris with Great Basin were all present.

    Commissioner Everett asked about the primary and secondary truck routes and suggested
    switching the primary and secondary routes. The applicant stated they are trying to be
    sensitive to the homeowners and the peak traffic flow and decided to utilize Mellor Campus
    Drive. Hour of operation will typically be 7:00 am to 3:00 pm.

    Commissioner Hemmert opened the public hearing on 3.2

    Commissioner Hemmert closed the public hearing on 3.2

    Commissioner Everett asked if the routes would ever be used concurrently and if the hours of
    operation could be changed or considered as viable options.

Motion: Commissioner Nielsen moved to forward a positive recommendation of EYP’s request
for review of a grading permit on approximately 36.34-acres of property for the Primary
Children’s Hospital located at approximately 3000 West 2100 North; including all DRC

Commissioner Eyre, yes. Motion passed unanimously.

3.3 Consideration of EYP’s requests for approval of the Primary Children’s Hospital site
plan, a 36.34-acre children’s hospital and clinic located at approximately 3000 West 2100
North.

    Sierra Pierson, City Planner, presented information on the agenda item.

    Commissioner Bunker asked about the exception for the architecture on the side of the
    building. Kim Struthers stated they had a separate meeting to address this and just the
    grading itself the Central Utility Plant will be hidden below. The bridge obstructs the line of
    sight and they will use material to match the building to screen. They are going to screen
    with landscaping also. After Staff met with their team they felt comfortable with their
    proposal. Commissioner Hereth asked about bicycle parking reduction and if there would be
    enough parking. Kim stated the applicant could elaborate on that.

    The applicant, Braun Sears, was present. He stated the majority of the facility was unique to
    Staff makeup with most of the employees coming from the Utah Valley and South Valley
    area. There is an opportunity to add covered parking if the need arises. In this particular area,
    traditional transportation will most likely be used. Commissioner Hemmert asked if mature
    trees were going to be used for landscaping. The applicant stated Intermountain has really
    gone the extra mile by placing the Central Utility Plant as far South from Holbrook Farms
    area as physically possible on site and allowed the design team to treat the Utility Plant as a
    background building by placing it below grade so the generators and equipment can be as far
    away residential area’s as possible. He stated in regard to landscaping they will be able to
    place mature landscaping on day one so the equipment won’t be seen. Commissioner Nielsen
    stated there may be opportunities to do some landscaping and create a berm so there is year
    round screening given the space along 2100 South.

Motion: Commissioner Bunker recommended approval Primary Children’s Hospital site plan,
a 36.34-acre children’s hospital and clinic located at approximately 3000 West 2100 North;
including all DRC comments and findings stated in the report and Commissioner Nielsen’s
recommendations regarding landscaping around the building. Commissioner Hereth seconded.

Vote: Commissioner Hemmert, yes; Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Peterson, yes;
Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Hereth, yes;
Commissioner Eyre, yes. Motion passed unanimously.
