IV.     Continued Public Hearing on Proposed Amendments to Title 13 Regarding Accessory
        Dwelling Units.
Mayor Dahle reported that the above item related to proposed amendments to Title 13 regarding
Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADU”). This was an issue that the City Council had been working on
for some time and the public hearing had been open for the last several City Council Meetings.
Depending on the comments received, the public hearing may be closed or it may continue to remain
open. The packet included a staff report and other supporting documentation. Mayor Dahle explained
that those items would be discussed in detail during the Work Meeting. He did not feel Community
and Economic Development Director, LaNiece Davenport needed to review those documents now.

Mayor Dahle opened the public hearing.

Lynn Pace gave his address as 2386 Olympus Drive and stated that he was grateful for the hard work
and commitment of the City Council. He shared comments related to Internal Accessory Dwelling
Units (“IADU”) and explained that he had already sent an email to City Staff. Mr. Pace worked at
great length with Representative Ray Ward and a representative from the Utah League of Cities and

Towns (“ULCT”) on the draft of the bill. He was excited about the opportunities the bill presented.
Mr. Pace reported that there is an affordable housing crisis and he had long been concerned that there
would be negative consequences unless housing becomes more affordable to a broader range of
citizens. The bill contained many benefits as it would allow people to age in place, get young families
into housing, make housing more affordable, and increase the school-age population. If implemented
as required by the State, there would be almost no noticeable difference within residential
neighborhoods. Mr. Pace believed the bill could increase the amount of housing in Holladay for no
cost and with no visible impact.

Mr. Pace pointed out that most of the general public did not know about the State mandate or the
difference between an IADU and an External Accessory Dwelling Unit (“EADU”). The ADU process
needed to be implemented in a manner that is as seamless as possible. That was the reason
Representative Ward added a list of shall statements into the bill. He also added a list of statements.
For instance, “you shall allow ADUs as a permitted use in primarily single-family residential zones,”
and “you may require a license and annual permit.” Additional examples were shared in the email
sent by Mr. Pace. He felt it was essential to require an annual license and for the City to have a full
range of remedies. Mr. Pace further discussed the latter and explained that there were tools provided
in the statute if issues arose. The City must give notice and an opportunity to solve the issue. If the
homeowner protested, there would be a hearing. If the homeowner did not solve the issue or protest,
fees could be imposed. Notice would be given to the property owner and their lender that the fee
would be imposed as a lien on the property.

Mr. Pace was excited about the ADUs but expressed concerns related to EADUs. Mayor Dahle asked
Mr. Pace to expand on his concerns. Mr. Pace explained that his concerns pertained to a reduced
amount of homeowner supervision. If an EADU is above a detached garage or if there was a separate
unit in the rear yard, it was less likely that the homeowner would monitor noise levels, traffic, or other
disturbances. There was a certain loss of control when the homeowner was not in the same dwelling
as the renter. Another challenge pertained to privacy for neighbors. Many lots in the City are deep
and an EADU in the rear yard of a primary dwelling could cause friction between neighbors. A
private backyard space could suddenly be right next to a dwelling unit.

Kerri Boyle gave her address as 6508 Canyon Cove Drive and stated that she and her husband became

Ms. Boyle explained that she and her husband had not initially known about requirements related to
second kitchens, locked doors, and unlocked requirements. In reading the new mandate in the

III.    Consideration of a Request from Holladay Hills Developer to Support a Request to the
Salt Lake County Assessor for an Adjustment to the Base Year Tax Value for the Holladay
Hills Project Area.
Chair Gibbons reported that the above item related to a request from the Holladay Hills developer.
He noted that he and Mayor Dahle, who serves as Vice-Chairman of the RDA, Wade Budge from
Snell & Wilmer, and the City Attorney had some meetings related to the topic. Chair Gibbons
explained that one year ago, the developer was expecting an increment payout, but it was not received
because the County was working off of a different number. He asked Mr. Godfrey to explain the
situation further.

Mr. Godfrey reported that the Cottonwood Mall Project Area was created in traditional means using
tax increment financing to support the redevelopment of the project. Tax increment was created by
fixing a base-year property tax value within the project area. When redevelopment occurred, an added
value would come in. The difference between the base year tax and the new valuation was called tax
increment money and that was paid to the RDA through the County.

When the Cottonwood Mall Project Area was created, a base year tax value was set in 2007 and the
budgeted estimated amount was $38 million. Unfortunately, that was not the actual valuation of the
property for the base year. This issue was complicated by an amendment that was made to the project
area budget in 2011. That amendment was done to extend the life of the project area because no
development was occurring between 2008 and 2011 due to the recession. Mr. Godfrey explained that
the actual assessed value from the Assessor’s Office for the project area was $44 million and so there
was a significant difference between the $38 million and $44 million.

        a.       UPD Benefits and Compensation Discussion – Chief Ackerman.
Mayor Dahle reported that the above item related to UPD benefits and compensation. Chiefs Jason
Mazuran and Jason Ackerman were present to discuss the issue and answer any Council Member
questions. Mayor Dahle noted that there was a serious issue pertaining to attracting new officers and
retaining existing officers. There is competition between agencies for officers, which was caused in
part by the fact that Salt Lake City dramatically increased their wages. This information was included
in the packet for review.

The UPD Board discussed the issue in August 2021 and acknowledged the issue. Several agencies
had reacted since the announcement made by Salt Lake City and some agencies were waiting to see
how the UPD would react before making any concrete decisions. Mayor Dahle reported that the UPD
Board Members left the August meeting with the consensus that action was necessary. He did not
believe UPD could be last in the market. He explained that their situation is unique because they
cannot act unilaterally. A consensus was needed from the other member entities that make up UPD.
Mayor Dahle noted that the hope was to reach some kind of consensus decision about the increase at
the UPD Board Meeting in September 2021.

Mayor Dahle was looking for guidance from the Council on this issue. He wanted to know how far
they were willing to go as it related to compensation but noted that there would likely be some back
and forth with other member entities before a final decision was made. The goal for the UPD Board
was to have that discussion in September 2021 and make a definitive announcement in October 2021.
He asked Chief Mazuran to share additional information.

Chief Mazuran noted that there were a number of things that had caused the current issues relating to
benefits and compensation. It was happening throughout the County but also across the nation.
Officers are leaving the profession and others are not entering at the same rate. This has created a
“wage war,” that had happened at an extremely fast rate. Chief Mazuran commented that this is
unprecedented and important to address in a multi-faceted way focusing on short-term, mid-term, and
long-term approaches.

Chief Ackerman reported that some of the numbers included in the packet had changed and new

Sandy City had been very vocal in the media and stated that they were planning to make a substantial

Chief Ackerman reported that they are losing 0.7 to 0.8 officers per week. For an agency of their
size, which was approximately 40 officers per year. That means they are losing officers faster than
they can be replaced. He explained that new officers need to be sent to the Police Academy, which
is regulated by the State. UPD can send approximately 20 officers through the Police Academy per
year. If 40 officers are lost per year there is no way to supplement that with just new officers. Chief
Ackerman explained that if the City of Holladay were to lose an officer to another agency or they
decided to leave the profession, the best-case scenario with the current hiring situation was that it
would take four months to replace that officer. That timeline would only be possible if the
replacement was already experienced. Realistically, it would take 6 to 8 months or 12 to 14 months
if the Police Academy training is required.

UPD was trying to gauge what other departments were currently experiencing as UPD is in a good
position with staffing. Chief Ackerman explained that when Taylorsville departed the UPD many of
those officers decided to remain with the UPD. As a result, they were not facing many of the
challenges other agencies are currently experiencing. However, he noted that one agency of similar
scope and size was down approximately 65 officers and they now are not responding to certain calls
for service. It was a concern that UPD could potentially be in a similar situation at this time next year
if certain measures are not taken.

Council Member Gibbons asked for more information about the first through fifth position options.
Chief Ackerman reported that the UPD Board would like the UPD to be positioned either in the top
third of the market or within the top three. The information in the packet outlined the costs that would
be required to move the UPD into certain positions. Mayor Dahle believed the UPD had achieved
the top three position as of June. Within six weeks, they had gone from a top-three position in many
of the ranks to almost last in those ranks. He found that to be very discouraging. Mayor Dahle
reported that he served on the Benefits and Compensation Committee and had been vocal that they
need to make a clear statement to the officers that something would be done quickly. Generally, the
culture in UPD is very healthy and people enjoy working there. However, Sergeant Nick Renfro,
who heads Employee Engagement, stated that while the culture has kept the group together, it was

       b.      Continued Discussion on ADUs.
Mayor Dahle reported that Ms. Davenport provided a Staff Report in the packet highlighting the
outstanding issues that need further clarification. Ms. Davenport needed to make sure she had clarity
on those issues so that the Planning Department could create a final draft that would be voted on at

Council Member Quinn commented that Mr. Pace mentioned notifying a lienholder when a property
is going to be fined. She wondered whether that could be done with short-term rentals as well.
Mr. Godfrey explained that they did not have specific statutory authorization on short-term rentals,
but they did for IADUs. There were additional enforcement tools related to short-term rentals because
of the concern that IADUs could easily become short-term rentals. However, those enforcement tools
did not extend to the lien authority other than if a permit was issued for an IADU and the homeowner
turned it into a short-term rental, which is in violation of the statute and a lien could be imposed for
that. They would have the authority to tell the lien lender about the violation and this was included
in the draft language.

Council Member Gibbons inquired about the enforcement tools. Ms. Davenport explained that the
information found on Page 5, Lines 28-38 related to the lien language. It was highlighted in yellow
because that information was new. Ms. Davenport stated that it would be revised based on some
additional verbiage suggested by Mr. Godfrey. It was noted that the revised language would be ready
for City Council review next week. Mr. Godfrey explained that the section made clear that the City
could hold a lien for violations of the IADUs and the process that was set forth in the Land Use
Development Management Act (“LUDMA”) must be followed. He reported that someone in
violation would be noticed and a hearing would be granted if requested. If during the hearing, the
Hearing Officer found that the homeowner was in violation, then the City had the lien right. In the
statutory sections that authorized the placement of the lien, a reference was made to LUDMA as well
as the State Lien Act. In the latter, any owner, actual or equitable, could be notified of the lien.

Council Member Durham had a question related to EADUs. Some of the concerns shared by Mr. Pace
stood out to him such as concerns related to privacy. He wondered whether an EADU could be built
to the maximum height requirements for a particular piece of property. Mr. Godfrey confirmed this.
Council Member Durham wondered if it would be possible to state that an EADU could only be as
high as the existing primary unit. Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink reported that an accessory
building could only be as high as the primary residence if it were set in the same setbacks as the
primary home. If an EADU was placed further back on the property, the maximum building height
would be 20 feet.
