           Challenges in the Public Services Department include staffmg; assaults on existing staff; increased graffiti, vandalism,
and homelessness; aged infrastructure; and drought conditions. Staffmg is the Department's most difficult/immediate challenge; it
is difficult to recruit, hire, and retain quality employees because there is so much competition and many companies are paying
higher than normal wages. The Department has had 16 open positions in the past year and has only been able to fill eight of the
positions; some positions have been re-opened as much as four times due to lack of qualified applicants or the inability to hire due
to low wages. He discussed the number of open positions in each division of the Department and reported on the impact these
staffmg issues have on the level of service of each division. The Parks and Recreation divisions have encountered increased
difficulty in securing seasonal and part-time employees, which makes it difficult to administer recreational programming. In many
cases, division managers are performing the work that is typically done by other staffers because it has been impossible to fill
vacancies. He relayed the following quote from GolfDivision Manager Brenkman regarding the staffmg situation: "Ifthere is one
thing we learned from COVID, it was that we run our operation too lean to provide quality service to the customer during peak
busy times. We need to add an additional golf shop person to answer phones while the other is checking players in." Mr. Anderson
noted the Department is grateful the Council supported the implementation of phase one of the recommendations from the City-
wide compensation study, and now requests that the Council proceed with implementing phase two and three and proceed with a
comprehensive staffmg study; this will help the Department address current staffmg conditions.
           Mr. Anderson then reported on occurrences of assaults on City staff; there have been three situations where staff were
assaulted in the past year, one where an employee was physically hurt. Dealing with difficult citizens is expected when working
for the public, but the last year or so, this unfortunate challenge has increased tremendously. The increase seems to be taking a toll
on our field and office staff and especially employees that deal with customer relations. One staff member talked about applying
for other jobs because they could not continue the daily criticism and "yelling". He then reported on the impact increased
homelessness has had on the Department; homeless individuals leave a great deal of garbage and debris on public property and
employees are assuming a great deal of personal risk when engaging in clean-up of these problems. Many employees working in
the Department were hired for things like parks and facilities maintenance and they did not anticipate they would be cleaning up
homeless encampments; this has made it difficult to retain employees. The same is true for graffiti removal or vandalism repair,
which has increased dramatically. These issues cause emotional wear and tear on employees and many employees are burnt out.
Graffiti and vandalism make it difficult to attract and retain good full-time and seasonal employees; during the last month alone,
crews have responded to dozens of damage calls to various park restrooms and pavilions (most recently, Mt. Lewis and Momoe
Parks). Graffiti alone, is occupying all of one maintenance tech time as they have not been able to use inmates for more than a
year due to the COVID-19 pandemic. A recent rash of vandalism has damaged numerous restroom partitions, doors, lighting
power supply and card readers. These are expensive to replace (approximately $2,900.00 for single men's and women's
partitions/doors) and take the Structural Maintenance crew hours to remove and replace. Surveillance cameras have been installed
at many locations, but many vandals have not been deterred by the presence of cameras.
           Mr. Anderson then reported on the challenges the Department is dealing with related to aged infrastructure. Aging
facilities need significant upgrades in order to continue to operate at their current level. There is a lack of adequate facilities space
and program participation is limited as a result. The Department's biggest long-term challenge is the maintenance and replacement
of failing, outdated infrastructure. Infrastructure in need of upgrades and replacements are:

      the responsibility to establish a City-wide salary schedule. Administration is proposing implementation of Phase Two of the
      Classification, Compensation and Benefit Study completed by Evergreen Solutions. The proposed staffmg documents and salary
      schedules align with the recommendations from Evergreen Solutions for all positions that were reviewed, except the
      recommendation for Chief Administrative OfficeL The Administration has proposed a salary range for all elected officials; all
      Council members indicated they are not interested in pursuing any adjustments to the salary scales for elected officials at this time.

      the Study and she reviewed past, current, and proposed rate for the position:

                 Ms. Eller-Smith invited HR Manager Judkins to provide an explanation for the differences between the Evergreen
      recommendation and the HR benchmark. Ms. Judkins stated the chief administrative officer (CAO) position was not part of
      Evergreen's evaluation and benchmark process; however, they did review the job description for the position and provided a mid-
      point recommendation.
                 Council member Lopez asked why the position was not part ofEvergreen 's evaluation and benchmark work. Ms. Judkins
    . stated it is common to leave CAO and elected officials' positions out ofthis type of study; for that reason, the City's HR Division
      performed the benchmark analysis for the CAO and elected officials' positions.
                 Council member Nadolski asked if the CAO's current salary falls within the proposed range. Ms. Judkins stated the
      current salary is $150,848; the position is near the top of the current range but would be below the top end of the FY22 proposed
      range. Ms. Eller-Smith asked if the other cities that were part of the benchmark study have a defmed range for the position or there
      is simply a set compensation amount for the position. Ms. Judkins answered the benchmark cities utilize a combination of both
      methods of compensation. Some cities have a minimum, mid-point, and maximum pay for the position, while others have a more
      detailed pay range with several steps in the range.
                 Council member Hyer inquired as to who determines the CAO pay in the benchmark cities. Ms. Eller-Smith stated that in
      Ogden's form of govermnent, the Mayor detennines the pay; in most other cities, the six-Council member form of government is
      employed, which means the Mayor and Council work together to detennine the pay for the position.
                 Discussion then centered on whether the City should have included the CAO and elected officials' positions in a City-

      is willing to support for both the CAO and elected officials' positions. Council member Nadolski indicated he would prefer to

)     position was left out ofthe Evergreen study as well as the manner in which increases in the CAO's compensation are considered

          Discussion of the CAO salary range continued; the Council debated whether to place a maximum or cap on the pay range
for the position. They also indicated they would like to review the current contract for the position before making fmal decisions
regarding the pay range and opportunities for pay increases for the position. Chief Deputy City Attorney Stratford asked why the
contract is relevant to the Council's discussion of the CAO's salary range. Council member Lopez stated he wants to understand
the terms of the contract and whether it entitles the CAO to receive bonuses or other forms of compensation in excess of the
defmed annual salary. He added he is receiving confusing information about the position and wants to ensure the Council has all
information about the position before making a decision on the salary range; he wants to be sure that the City is not treating one
individual different than everyone else in the City. Council member Choberka agreed and stated that due to the level of
uncertainty regarding many components of the process to determine the proposed FY22 wage scale for the CAO, she would prefer
to maintain the current FY21 scale for the next fiscal year and have a broader conversation about the matter in the coming months.
She feels that the Council is pressed to make a decision about the wage scale in the next two weeks, but they do not have complete
information in which to base their decision.
          Discussion of the matter continued, with Management Services Director Brown noting Administration is willing to
withdraw the HR recommendation and follow the Evergreen recommendation for the CAO position. The Council ultimately
concluded to accept this compromise and support the Evergreen recommended salary range for the CAO.
          Ms. Eller-Smith then refocused on salary ranges for elected officials; changing the basis of elected officials' salaries from
a set salary to a salary range would require ordinance amendments for both the Mayor and Council. Other options for salaries for
elected officials for the FY22 Budget are to:
                •    Adopt the current FY21 salaries; or
                •    Provide a 4% increase over FY21 salaries for all elected officials; or
                •    Provide a 4% increase over FY21 salaries for Council members; only; or
                •    Adopt benchmarked salaries for all elected officials.
          Council members discussed the options and the importance in being transparent in the process of determining salary
ranges; they concluded to accept the benchmarked salaries for all elected officials, which are $123,774 for the Mayor and $22,532
for City Council positions. Ms. Eller-Smith indicated this is equivalent to granting a raise to all Council members on July 1; she
noted the Council could enact the pay increases at any point in the coming FY. Council member Lopez stated that he is not
comfortable voting to give himself a raise; he will vote against that action at this time. He suggested that a commission be created
that could help to determine the appropriate salaries for City Council members. Council member Nadolski stated he is not sure a
commission would come up with a recommendation that is much different than Administration's as their recommendation is based
upon commonly accepted benchmarking practices; he trusts the results ofHR's benchmark process. After continued discussion,
the Council ultimately concluded to accept HR's benchmarked wage range for elected officials' positions, with increases to be
delayed until January 1, 2022.
