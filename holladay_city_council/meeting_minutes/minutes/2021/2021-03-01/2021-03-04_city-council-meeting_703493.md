VIII. City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
       a.     Financial Report.
Manager Chamness presented the Financial Report and commented that the City of Holladay was
doing well in terms of the overall budget in revenue. Through January 31, 2021, 97.1% of the
budgeted property tax had been received. Sales taxes were at 53.5% of the budget goal at five months.
Ms. Chamness felt that the City of Holladay would exceed the sales tax goal significantly as
December is usually a good sales tax month. Franchise taxes were at approximately 33.7% of the
budget number.

Ms. Chamness addressed the intergovernmental revenue, which was at 85.9% due to the CARES Act
funds that came in during the last calendar year. Ms. Chamness also addressed the Justice Court
revenue. With many court hearings being held virtually, there was a delay in Justice Court revenue.
She hoped to see those numbers increase when court resumes in person. She reported that the
expenses were tracking well and she was not concerned.

Mayor Dahle was surprised to see the high percentage for the Building Permits and Plan Check Fees.
He wondered whether there was an explanation for the numbers. Ms. Chamness explained that the
high percentage was primarily due to Block D of the Holladay Hills Project.

       b.      Legislative Update.
Ms. Chamness shared legislative updates with the City Council. SB 244 was a bill that allocated a
portion of the quarter of a quarter funding for transportation and was on its way to the Governor.
When the bill was initially released, it did not include the City of Holladay in its funding allocation.
The Lobbyist worked hard to get the City included in the bill. Holladay ended up with approximately

        c.      Additional Updates.
Ms. Chamness reported that the City of Holladay is participating with other cities throughout the
valley on the Seven Greenways Vision Plan. It is a 100-year plan to explore daylighting and
greenways long seven major creeks in the Salt Lake Valley. It would include Big Cottonwood Creek.
Ms. Chamness reported that in Holladay, most of Big Cottonwood Creek is daylighted. She added
that the group was looking for participation from both elected officials and staff in a visioning process.
Ms. Chamness asked that those interested reach out to her for additional information.

Ms. Chamness reported that Salt Lake County moved from a high transmission risk level to a
moderate transmission risk level. For the City that means there would be fewer staggered schedules
for staff at City Hall. She hoped to keep some of that flexibility in place for staff members who can
work well remotely.

        d.     Racoons.
Assistant City Manager, Holly Smith reported that the City, based on the data received, decided not
to move forward with the program. Over the five-year program period, 101 raccoons were trapped
and euthanized. That was an average of 20 raccoons per year. There were 87 phone consultations
and 22 additional consults. City staff requested additional information about where the animals were
located but the USDA does not currently provide that level of detail. Council Member Durham
wondered about the costs associated with the program. Ms. Smith reported that it was approximately
$7,000 per year. Council Member Durham noted that he heard about raccoons frequently from his
constituents and wondered whether other resources were available. Ms. Smith stated that several
private companies offered different services and that there are several things residents can do to
prevent raccoons from coming onto their property.

       c.      Discussion on Property Tax Timeline.
Ms. Smith shared a more detailed timeline and reported that the document was included in the City
Council cloud folder. In March, a rebroadcast video from the virtual townhalls that were hosted in
February would be released. The focus for the next few City Council Meetings would be on the
preliminary proposal for the potential property tax increase. The Council Retreat would include a
discussion related to priorities for the next fiscal year as well as discussions on the target tax increase.
Once a preliminary number was determined, Ms. Chamness would prepare a recommended budget
that would come back to the City Council in May 2021. Ms. Smith noted that the number could
change but it was a necessary starting point.

Once the preliminary proposal is prepared, the information would be shared with residents in order
to obtain feedback. Ms. Smith reported that there had been discussions about how to handle that
process. Staff recommended district roundtables where each Council Member could host a
neighborhood group or invite a small group of constituents to a roundtable to discuss the preliminary
proposal. Ms. Smith stated that the mailers could be sent out regarding the roundtable discussions or
invitations could be sent out to different groups. She hoped to get the roundtables scheduled during
the week of April 12, 2021. Feedback received from the roundtable discussions would be passed
along to Ms. Chamness to finalize her recommended budget.

Council Member Petersen wondered if holding individual roundtables was necessary. She suggested
that something similar to the Town Hall may be appropriate. Ms. Smith explained that the idea of
the roundtable discussions was for the Council Members to have more intimate conversations with
constituents. Council Member Quinn asked about potential in-person discussions. Ms. Smith
believed that something in person would need to be done by invitation to control the number of people
that participate. Council Member Fotheringham agreed that individual roundtables were needed.
Council Member Gibbons was surprised at how few participants attended the original townhalls. He
suggested a virtual event with an in-person event in the future. Ms. Smith reported that staff would
plan one combined event with the flexibility to have smaller roundtables in the future. An in-person
Open House may be able to take place in May 2021. There were discussions about the best possible
date for the virtual event. It was determined that it would take place on April 15, 2021, at 7:00 p.m.

Ms. Smith introduced Fred Philpot who presented the Financial Sustainability model. This would be
one of the key tools the City Council would use over the next few months to determine an appropriate
number. Several scenarios were presented to the City Council previously as well as a preferred
alternative. However, nothing had been finalized and there was still flexibility to refine numbers and


Holladay City Council Meeting Minutes 03-04-21                                                  6
make changes as needed. Mr. Philpot reported that the following three elements should be considered
when evaluating sustainability: Prioritization; Efficiency; andRevenue Sustainability.

Mr. Philpot reported that the model focuses on revenue sustainability but that was only one
component of the discussion. The City Council had an opportunity to prioritize and look for
efficiencies, which would then influence the model. For instance, the City of Holladay has some
substantial infrastructure needs. As a decision-making body, the City Council could choose whether
to prioritize those needs. Mr. Philpot outlined some of the assumptions included in the model, such
as taxes, the revenue stream based on percentage growth, and expenditures. He reported that there
were also tools in the model that relates to financing. Mr. Philpot explained that financing was a tool
that could be utilized to help amortize a cost over a longer period of time. He overviewed other model
line items, such as General Fund revenues, general operation and maintenance expenditures, debt
service expense, and bond proceeds.

Mr. Philpot explained that typically, there is a five-year window when evaluating rates and tax policy.
However, the model was built well beyond that and could look past that five-year window. He stated
that with the General Fund, the model was established to achieve a 25% fund balance. That
requirement was recently increased so that entities can now have 35% of their fund balance relative
to operating revenues. He had not manipulated the scenarios to reach that level but commented that
it could be reevaluated. The model was currently built to achieve a 25% fund balance within the five-
year planning horizon. Mr. Philpot outlined the high capital improvement needs and mentioned the
Roadways and Pavement Replacement Plan that City Engineer, Jared Bunch had worked on. He
reiterated that there was flexibility to make changes to the scenarios as desired.

Ms. Chamness added that the City Council was free to use any of the scenarios that had been
developed to date. They could also provide instructions to develop additional scenarios based on
other relevant factors. She noted that political and economic concerns could also drive the scenarios.
Mayor Dahle wondered if the General Fund budget projections assumed a certain sales tax growth
rate. Mr. Philpot confirmed this and commented that the model tried to reflect a realistic picture with
regard to revenue. Based on percentage growth, 4% growth in General Sales and Use Taxes was
structured into the model. He discussed concerns related to COVID-19 and changes in spending
patterns. Mayor Dahle felt it was important that the number be as realistic as possible. He suggested
that the $300,000 per year for 15 years, discussed earlier in the meeting, be included.

Mayor Dahle felt it was important to understand what the percentages would mean for the residents.
Ms. Smith reported that those details were included in the Citizen Advisory Group Recommendation
Report. She added that the model was a beneficial ongoing resource that could be looked at annually
as part of the budget preparation process. Mayor Dahle stressed the importance of being able to
communicate effectively with residents about the numbers.

Mayor Dahle reported that the property tax increase would be discussed further at the Council Retreat
on March 25, 2021. Council Members would try to see if they could reach an agreement on a number.
He acknowledged that the number may change between the Council Retreat and the end of June when
the budget is approved.
