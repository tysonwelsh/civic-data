5. STAKEHOLDER REPORTS
A. Unified Police Department
Chief Del Craig presented the September crime report. He highlighted concerning increases in
family offenses and assaults, the latter potentially related to a neighborhood fight club issue.
He noted efforts to address youth gatherings and fights, including working with the district
attorney and school district. Chief Craig also mentioned increased thefts and auto thefts
related to homelessness issues. He offered to work with the council on solutions for the dog
park concerns raised earlier.
Council members asked questions about increases in invasion of privacy incidents and fraud
cases. There was discussion about internet-based crimes and social media influences on
youth behavior. Chief Craig noted some arrests made for vandalism and ongoing efforts to
address youth crime issues.
B. Review and Status of Magna RDA Programs, Dina Blaes, Salt Lake County
Regional Development
Dina Blaes, Director of the Office of Regional Development for Salt Lake County, provided an
overview of the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) programs in Magna and reviewed a prepared
presentation (Attachment B). There was talk about transferring the RDA to Magna City control,
a topic of particular interest to Mayor Barney, who expressed a desire to expedite the process.
Additionally, Ms. Blaes provided an overview of RDA-owned parcels on Main Street and the
accompanying development opportunities, while also acknowledging the challenges posed by
the impending expiration of project areas. In response, council members inquired about
housing set-aside funds, landscaping maintenance obligations, and the method for transferring
RDA control to the city.

B. Discuss Magna Title 18 Subdivision Revisions — Jay Springer, Smith Hartvigsen
Magna City Attorney, Paul Ashton, explained that Title 18 needs to be amended to reflect
statutory changes made by the Utah legislature taking effect November 1, 2024. He reviewed
the proposed changes and additional background information from the meeting packet
published prior to tonight's meeting. The changes limit city review of subdivisions and shift
more authority to the planning commission level. Ashton noted most required changes were
already made in a recent amendment. He recommended scheduling formal action for the next
meeting to meet the November 1 deadline.

C. Discuss 8000 West Corridor Widening Project— Tolin Hessel, Salt Lake County

Engineer & Matthew Starley, Long-Ranger Planner II/MSD
Tolin Hessel, Salt Lake County Engineer, and Matthew Starley, Long Range Planner,
presented an update on the 8000 West corridor widening project, which is primarily federally
funded and extends from 3180 South to 2450 South. The project includes pedestrian ramp
upgrades, sidewalk additions, and bike lanes, with Avenue Consultants having completed 60%
of the design work. There is an opportunity for streetscape improvements in the new park
strips. Council members expressed a strong interest in incorporating street lighting into the
project, even if it requires additional funding. There was a discussion regarding the
maintenance responsibilities for trees and landscaping on arterial roads, as well as a strong
desire to consider incorporating streetlights and landscaping into new streetscapes to avoid
additional costs in the future. The staff committed to researching lighting costs and
maintenance practices used by other municipalities.

E. Discuss Council Fee Waiver or Donation Application — David Brickey, City Manager
David Brickey presented a draft application form and process for council fee waivers or

F. Discuss 2025 Magna City Council Meeting Dates - David Brickey, City Manager
David Brickey highlighted possible scheduling conflicts with the 2025 meeting dates, such as

7. MANAGERICITY ATTORNEY UPDATES
David Brickey provided several updates. He is researching overpressure zone regulations and
reaching out to Northrop Grumman for information. In addressing infrastructure issues, he
mentioned ongoing efforts to resolve drainage problems on Sweet Gum Road. Additionally,

8. COUNCIL REPORTS
Mayor Barney announced that the city has successfully reached an agreement to acquire the
Webster Community Center building. This acquisition is aimed at using the facility as interim
city offices, marking a significant step in providing a dedicated space for city operations and
community engagement. Mayor Barney also reported on recent Municipal Services District
(MSD) board actions; notably, the MSD has taken concrete steps to enhance its operations by
formally terminating service agreements with Salt Lake County for engineering and clerk
services, effectively bringing these functions directly under MSD management. Additionally, he
highlighted the excitement around the hiring of Lizel Allen as the new Director of Engineering
for the MSD. Lizel brings over 20 years of expertise in land development, public works, and
civil engineering, and her addition to the team is expected to be a significant asset as the MSD
transitions into its new facility, which is anticipated to be operational by January.
Council Member Pierce provided updates from the latest mosquito abatement meeting,
mentioning that the seasonal employees have completed their term, and operations are
winding down for the year. She also shared progress on various emergency management
