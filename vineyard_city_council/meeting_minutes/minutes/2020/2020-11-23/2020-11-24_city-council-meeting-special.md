WRITTEN DETERMINATION
This meeting will be an electronic meeting according to Vineyard Municipal Code Section
3.08.030.
I, Julie Fulmer, as the Mayor of the Vineyard City Council, do hereby find and declare as
follows:
     1. Due to the Emergency conditions which currently exist in the State of Utah, and
         specifically in Utah County and Vineyard City as a result of the COVID-19 Pandemic
         and the recent surge in COVID-19 infections across the state and in Utah County, the
         holding of public meetings with an anchor location as defined in the Utah Open and
         Public Meetings Act, presents a substantial risk to the health and safety of those who may
         be present at the anchor location; and

     2. The risk to those who may be present at an anchor location can be substantially mitigated
        by holding public meetings of the City Council pursuant to electronic means that allow
        for public participation via virtual means; and

     3. The City has the means and ability to allow virtual participation in the public meetings in
        accordance with the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act;

2. BUSINESS ITEMS
   2.1       DISCUSSION AND ACTON – Temporary Construction Easement
       Public Works Director/City Engineer Don Overson will present a temporary construction
       easement for Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT)/Utah Transit Authority (UTA).
       The UTA double tracking project is moving forward and is needed to complete and open
       our FrontRunner station. The contractor is waiting for a notice to proceed, and in order to
       do that they need a temporary construction easement from the city on the property. They
       will be making some site improvements for us in exchange for the construction easement.
       The mayor and City Council will take appropriate action.

Mayor Fullmer turned the time over to Public Works Director/City Engineer Don Overson

Mr. Overson explained that UDOT and UTA had been having trouble accessing the double track
project and had requested a temporary easement across Vineyard’s property to the west. He said
that once the double track was completed UDOT/UTA would remove, replace, or regrade
anything the city wanted them to. He said that they had also negotiated an access point on the
south side with UDOT/UTA. He added that they would install a water line from north to south
on the road near the property and install a fire hydrant, allowing the city to connect the water line
into the Public Works property.

Mayor Fullmer called for comments or a motion.

Motion: COUNCILMEMBER FLAKE MOVED TO APPROVE THE TEMPORARY
EASEMENT FOR THE INSTALLATION OF THE DOUBLE TRACKING AS PRESENTED.
COUNCILMEMBER JUDD SECONDED THE MOTION. MAYOR FULLMER,
COUNCILMEMBERS EARNEST, FLAKE, JUDD, AND WELSH VOTED AYE. THE
MOTION CARRIED UNANIMOUSLY.

    2.2         DISCUSSION AND ACTION – Tank Design Contract Approval
          Public Works Director/City Engineer Don Overson will present a proposal from Terracon
          for a geotechnical study on the nine-acre Public Works site, to determine the soil stability
          needed for the design of the water tank. The mayor and City Council will take
          appropriate action.

Mayor Fullmer turned the time over to Public Works Director/City Engineer Don Overson.

Mr. Overson explained that staff had found a location on the Public Works site for the water
tank. He said that they needed to have a geotechnical investigation done for the design of the
tank. He said that Terracon had proposed a cost of $38,420 to complete the investigation. He
mentioned that there was no contingency written into the contract and recommended adding a 20
percent contingency of $7,684. This would bring the total for the project to $46,104. He said that
staff was recommending approval of the costs and allowing City Manager Jacob McHargue to
sign the contract.

Councilmember Judd asked if this tank was in addition to the Orem location. Mr. Overson
replied that they would be moving the tank location to Vineyard. He said that with the

restrictions and increase in costs to build the tank in Orem, staff had determined that it would be
better to move it to Vineyard. He said that the first segment of the tank would be a 6-million-
gallon tank, which would allow development in Downtown Vineyard to determine the need for
more water storage.

Councilmember Judd stated that he was okay with the budget proposal. He asked staff to keep in
mind that the water tank would be the first thing people riding FrontRunner would see when
coming into the city. He felt that how the tank looked was very important. He mentioned his first
impressions when riding Portland, Oregon’s transit line. Mr. Overson explained that the person
designing the water tank had a lot of experience with building them and had suggested burying it
as much as possible and being creative with the skin on it.

Mayor Fullmer asked if the change in location would create upfront and long-term cost savings.
Mr. Overson replied that it would save some money upfront except that they would have to build
a pump station. He said that this would also help with water pressure. He added that they would
be able to use Central Utah Project’s (CUP) water pressure and minimize the need for pumping
as much as possible.

City Manager Jacob McHargue explained that the city was still in negotiations with Orem City
for placement of the tank but had been looking for locations in Vineyard. He added that there
would be additional costs for running the pipeline on existing roads in Orem. He said that they
needed to have the geotechnical study done to make sure the Public Works site would work for
the placement of the tank.

Mayor Fullmer called for further questions or a motion.

Motion: COUNCILMEMBER JUDD MOVED TO APPROVE THE CONTRACT WITH THE
ADDITIONAL CONTINGENCY RECOMMENDED BY STAFF AND ALLOW CITY
MANAGER JACOB MCHARGUE TO SIGN THE CONTRACT. COUNCILMEMBER
WELSH SECONDED THE MOTION. MAYOR FULLMER, COUNCILMEMBERS
EARNEST, FLAKE, JUDD, AND WELSH VOTED AYE. THE MOTION CARRIED
UNANIMOUSLY.
