### Orem Wellbeing Survey Presentation
*Presenter: Dr. Courtney Flint, Utah State University Professor and Researcher*

Ms. Cox introduced Dr. Flint who is a professor and researcher at Utah State University. Dr. Flint presented the results from the Orem Wellbeing survey. The survey project goals are to 1) provide Utah city and town leaders with information on the wellbeing and perspectives of residents to inform local planning processes and decisions making, 2) to support comparative research on community well being and 3) to support statewide and regional policy and planning efforts with data representing the lives of Utahns. The City reports can be found online at www.usu.edu/utah-wellbeing-project. The survey was done online in the spring of 2024. The Orem survey received 1,361 results, which is a project record. The survey was advertised through social media and0 newsletters.

Dr. Flint showed the demographics from the survey results, 98.4% were full time residents of Orem. The average length of residence was 24.3 years. 16.8% lived in Orem 5 years or less. The survey underrepresented ages 18-29, those without a college degree, those not married and renters. There was good coverage for ages 30+, gender, children in household, income and employment.

The results from the survey is that the average personal wellbeing was rated at 4.05 out of 5. The community wellbeing results were 3.63 out of 5. The survey broke things down into domains. The most important domains were mental health, safety and security, physical health, family life, and living standards. The highest rated domains were family life, safety and security, living standards, and education. Dr. Flint explained that another important part of the survey was about community connection, that is how connected an individual feels to their community. Orem results showed that those surveyed averaged a 4 out of 5 on connection to community. This matters because those who feel connected to their community score higher on individual well being. She also discussed other demographics such as age, religion, income, disability, and length of residence. Other survey questions were related to the growth in Orem. The top five concerns from the survey were traffic, air quality, water supply, affordable housing and public safety.

The City Council asked questions about the survey. Dr. Flint explained that the comments and possible further studies could be requested. The Orem staff discussed action items related to the survey results.

### Memorialization and Facility Naming Policy Presentation
*Presenter: Bryce Merrill, Library and Recreation Director and Tyler Peay, Assistant Director of Public Works-Public Services*

Mr. Merrill is here with Mr. Peay to discuss how the City will handle requests for memorializing and facility naming. He presented four opportunities for memorials which are public memorials on public property, street naming policy, facility naming rights and sponsorship, and personal memorialization on public property. Mr. Peay spoke about the public memorials on public property, which include the Veteran's Monument at the cemetery, the planned Gold Star Monument at city center, a memorial area near the Senior Friendship Center, and the Women's and Children Memorial. Mr. Merrill spoke about the other three public memorial options which all require a process and approval from the City Council or City Manager.

Mr. Merrill spoke about the potential concerns of individual memorials on private property that include the purpose of public parks and equitable access for all, maintenance standards and park safety, protected speech, and precedent-future maintenance, tree health, additional memorial. He showed some of the individual memorials that are currently in Orem. The individual memorialization policy will have five steps: application, development review committee, technical review, commission review, and City Council/City Manager review followed by a signed agreement.

#### SCERA Annual Report
*Presenter: Adam Robertson, SCERA President/CEO*

Mr. Robertson is the president/CEO of the SCERA theater. He spoke about SCERA's involvement in the CARE tax renewal last election. The SCERA indoor theater is in its 91st year (1933-2024) and the SCERA shell is in its 40th year (1984-2024). The SCERA mission is to enrich the mind, touch the soul and unify family and community by producing and presenting wholesome and affordable arts, entertainment and education for all ages in quality facilities. The SCERA goals for serving the community are to produce, present, and provide a wide variety of opportunities for citizens of all ages to be enriched, entertained, and educated, whether it be as an audience member, a student, an artist, a teacher, a performer, or a volunteer.

Mr. Robertson reviewed the many parts of the SCERA with pictures. He spoke of the hundreds of performers who have come to the SCERA. He spoke of the new fence that was placed around the SCERA Shell this season that was done in partnership with the City of Orem. Mr. Robertson showed images of the many productions that were held this year.

Other programs that the SCERA participates in are the elementary arts education, Gallery 101, volunteer opportunities, and special events. The special events include Celebration of Veterans, Christmas Maker's Market, the annual Christmas display, parade participation, Colonial Days, workshops, Annual Gala, and the Zion's Indie Film festival. The SCERA also manages the Orem Heritage Museum and the shop building which houses the costumes and props. Year long the SCERA offers arts education to youth, teens and adults. Mr. Robertson spoke about the upcoming shows for the fall of 2024.

### SCHEDULED ITEMS

#### Ordinance - Amending Article 2-20 of the Orem City Code to change the name of the Oremfest Advisory Committee to the Orem Events Advisory Commission and to make various amendments pertaining to the Commission.
*Presenter: Bryce Merrill, Library and Recreation Director*

Mr. Merrill is here as a follow up to the presentation given in the September 17 City Council work session. He is proposing the transition of the Oremfest Advisory Commission to the Orem Events Advisory Commission. This would allow our volunteers to do volunteer functions and the City staff to do City staff functions. They would assist in our many City events which include but are not limited to Oremfest and Lights On. The new code was amended since it was presented in the work session. The commission would help with the events calendar, events support, advice and recommendations on city events, annual reports to the City Council, community connection, and event testing and theming. The Orem staff would focus on staff policies.

**Mr. Killpack moved** to approve the proposed amendment of Article 2-20 of the Orem City Code to change the name of the Oremfest Advisory Committee to the Orem Events Advisory Commission and to make various amendments pertaining to the Commission. **Seconded by** Ms. Gale. Those voting yes: David Young, LaNae Millett, David Spencer, Tom Macdonald, Jenn Gale, Jeff Lambson, and Chris Killpack. The motion **passed.**
