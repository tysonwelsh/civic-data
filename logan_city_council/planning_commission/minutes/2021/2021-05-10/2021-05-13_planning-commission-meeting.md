                                  PLANNING COMMISSION MINUTES
                                              Meeting of May 13, 2021
                            Logan City Council Chambers (290 N 100 W Logan, UT 84321) www.loganutah.org

Minutes of the meeting for the Logan City Planning Commission convened in regular session on
Thursday, May 13, 2021. Chair Dickinson called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.

Commissioners Present: Roylan Croshaw, Regina Dickinson, Jessica Lucero, Sandy Goodlander,
Jordy Guth, Ken Heare, David Lewis

Staff Present: Mike DeSimone, Russ Holley, Tanya Rice, Ben Anderson, Kymber Housley, Mayor
Daines, Teresa Harris, Kirk Jensen, Paul Lindhardt
Teresa Harris swore in new Commissioners, David Lewis, and Ken Heare.

Commissioner Goodlander moved to approve the minutes from the April 22, 2021 meeting as
submitted. Motion seconded by Commissioner Croshaw. The motion was approved unanimously.

Commissioner Guth moved to approve the continuance of projects PC 21-023, 21-024, and 21-025
to the May 27, 2021 meeting. Commissioner Heare seconded the motion.

PUBLIC HEARING

 PC 21-020 Millcreek Phase II [Design Review Permit] Mountain States Property
 Management/BBI Land LLC, authorized agent/owner, request a new 72 new unit, 5 story
 apartment building with amenities, and 111 parking stalls. Located on 1.01 acres at 74
 West 100 South in the Town Center (TC-1) zone; TIN 02-046-0014. (Ellis Neighborhood)

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a design review permit of Millcreek Phase II, a 5-story
residential project with 72 units and two stories of structured parking. Vehicular access to the
parking structure and surface parking area is provided on 100 South and 100 West Other site
amenities include a swimming pool, hot tub, fitness room, and dog park. The historic Thatcher Mill
Ruin is located on this property and will be memorialized via a new plaza to be constructed
adjacent to the front of the structure and along 100 South. There are a total of 118 parking stalls,
which equals 90% of the overall requirement The Planning Commission will need to review the
applicants' alternate parking plan to determine if a deviation from the minimum parking
requirements is warranted. The applicant is also requesting a density bonus of two additional
residential units.

Commissioner Goodlander asked about the proposed alternate parking plan and when that would
be submitted for approval. R. Holley said that the proponent will be proposing the alternate parking
plan tonight and if approved it will be added to the conditions. Commissioner Lucero asked if there
were any requirements for bicycle parking. R. Holley said it is one spot per unit.

PROPONENT: Paul Willie distributed a handout to the commissioners to review (included in the
project file). He spoke about the city’s redevelopment plan and bringing business, commerce, and
people downtown. Paul stated that this is the first major project in downtown Logan to be
constructed in the new TC-1 zone which advances the goals of revitalizing downtown. He spoke
about the relocation of the canal, the water feature, and making sure the water allocation for the

downstream users remains the same. He introduced the alternate parking plan that includes a
total of 259 stalls which is 4.2 stalls short. There are an additional 24 stalls available in the 660
feet of street parking.

Commissioner Croshaw asked about the landscaping plans for the rear of the property and where
the property line is along the canal. P. Willie said that the property line is on the north side of the
canal. There will be plenty of landscaping in the back, along with a patio, BBQ area, pool, and
restrooms. Commissioner Lucero asked what percent of people that live in studio apartments have
more than one vehicle. P. Willie spoke about other projects he has done and what their parking
ratios are compared to this project. Commissioner Goodlander asked how the visitor parking will
work. P. Willie said there is guest parking in the rear parking lot, they can also park on the street.
They will also have package lockers, so that deliveries can be made, placed in a locker and then
residents will get an email letting them know.
Commissioner Goodlander commented about the parking issue in that area surrounding the rec
center.
Commissioner Lewis asked what would happen if the state engineer doesn’t approve the
decorative stream. Paul said that they will not be able to have the stream if it is not approved.

PUBLIC: David Olsen, part-owner of the office across the street to the north said he is in favor of
the project, it’s an improvement from the graffiti and weeds that have been there for years.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Guth asked if similar parking exceptions have been granted in the
past and how that worked out. R. Holley said that the City has moved away from granting parking
exceptions in the CR zone. Mike DeSimone talked about the parking. Commissioner Lucero said
she is fine with the plan and the parking.

MOTION: Commissioner Croshaw moved to conditionally approve a design review permit as
outlined in PC 21-020 with the amended findings for approval as listed below. Commissioner
Goodlander seconded the motion.
CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL
1. All standard conditions of approval will be recorded with the Design Review and are available
    in the Community Development Department.
2. The Planning Commission grants a density bonus for two additional units (72 total units)
    because the project provides a parking structure for at least 75% of the required parking
    stalls.
3. The Planning Commission accepts the presented alternative parking plan that provides 259
    parking stalls for both phase one and phase two at a ratio of 1.76 parking stalls per dwelling
    unit. Bicycle parking shall be provided on-site or inside the building.
4. Because of the additional setback and building angle along 100 South, the 58’ tall building is
    approved.
5. The project shall provide parking stalls in accordance with LDC dimensional size standards
    and specifications.
6. Ground floor transparency may be tinted to better screen parked cars inside the parking
    structure.
7. Street trees shall be planted along all adjacent streets at 30 feet on center.
8. Building materials shall match the submitted architectural drawings.
9. Dumpster and garbage collection areas shall be screened with landscaping and/or walls from
    public view.
10. Exterior lighting shall be concealed source, down-cast and reviewed, and approved prior to
    the issuance of a building permit and shall comply with current LDC regulations.


11. No signs are approved with this Design Review Permit. All signage shall be approved and
    permitted by staff in accordance with the Land Development Code.
12. No fences are approved with this Design Review Permit. All fences shall be approved and
    permitted by staff in accordance with the Land Development Code.
13. Rooftop mechanical and/or building wall mechanical equipment shall be placed out of view
    from the street or screen from view from the street.
14. Weather protection shall be provided above all pedestrian entrances.
15. Prior to issuance of a Building Permit, the Director of Community Development shall receive a
    written memorandum from each of the following departments or agencies indicating that their
    requirements have been satisfied:
      a. Fire – contact 435-716-9515
          • Fire hydrants, fire sprinklers and fire alarms required. Aerial apparatus lane along
               100 S will require the mitigation of overhead obstructions.
      b. Engineering - contact 435-716-9160
          • Provide Storm Water Design in accordance with City design standards.
          • Provide water shares or in-lieu fee for increased demand to City system
          • Property Line Adjustment must be completed to combine the current lots into 1 lot (This
               includes Phase 1 and Phase 2)
          • A 15’ easement from top of canal bank must be maintained for canal access for operation
               and maintenance. Any deviations from this setback must approved by the canal company
               and a copy of the agreement submitted to the City for our records.
          • Provide a Private Water Utility Agreement for all private fire lines to property/building
          • The construction of Phase 2 is part of a larger Common Plan of Development that exceeds
               1 Ac, therefore all requirements for a State Storm Water Construction Discharge Permit
               must be met.
          • Provide a Development Agreement for all infrastructure improvements within the City right
               way with surety.
          • City Engineer has reached out to the State Engineers office to ensure there are not any
               issues with diverting the canal back into the original North Branch of the Logan River.
               They are reviewing this issue and should respond back by the first week in May.
               Developer will need to comply with any requirements imposed by this office.
      c. Water/Cross Connection – contact 716-9627
          • Any landscape irrigation connected to Logan City water must have high hazard backflow
               protection and be tested.
          • Any fire suppression systems connected to Logan City water must have a minimum DC
               (ASSE1015) installed and tested.
          • Water main/s must have a RP (ASSE1013) installed and tested as it enters the building.
               Living units and businesses must have separated water supplies with own backflow
               protection to help prevent cross contamination incidences.
          • All points of use of water must comply with IPC and Utah state amendments during and
               after construction.
FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. The proposed project is compatible with surrounding land uses and will not interfere with the
   use and enjoyment of adjacent properties because of the building design, site layout, materials,
   landscaping, and setbacks.
2. The Design Review Permit conforms to the requirements of Title 17 of the Logan Municipal
   Code.
3. As conditioned, proposed project provides required off-street parking.


4. The project meets the goals and objectives of the TC-1 designation within the Logan General
   Plan by providing services near high-capacity roadways and is designed in a way for easy
   circulation of both pedestrian and vehicles.
5. The project met the minimum public noticing requirements of the Land Development Code and
   the Municipal Code.
6. 100 South and 100 West provide access and are adequate in size and design to sufficiently
   handle all traffic modes and infrastructure related to the land use.

STAFF: T. Rice reviewed the request for a design review permit on a new 15,386 square foot
industrial building that will be divided into seven bays to be used as office and storage warehouse
spaces. T. Rice also pointed out that the proposed access off 600 West will be moved to the
northwest corner of the property. This access can be used until 2200 North is constructed in the
future. There is also an access easement running through the property to the south which
provides a secondary means of access to the site. The committee needs to determine if the 20%
fenestration on the north side of the building is acceptable, code states that it should be at 30% on
the ground floor facing a road. Commissioner Lucero asked if the code specifies a future road for
fenestration. M. DeSimone said that the code does not specify future roads, and this is up to the
Commission’s discretion to approve.

Commissioner Croshaw asked about the stormwater retention pond that exists just outside the
property line next to 600 West and if that will remain. B. Young said that this retention pond was
constructed in the city right of way. It may have to be modified depending on where the property
line ends up after 600 West gets widened.

Commissioner Lucero asked why the entrance was moved to the north end of the property. B.
Young explained that the Land Development Code limits access to 600 West, and temporary
access will be allowed until 2200 North is developed in the future.

PROPONENT: Justin Campbell said that they are willing and able to modify the site plan to
accommodate the future of 600 West.

 PC 21-026 Logan Light and Power Substation 9 [Conditional Use] Mark Montgomery/B &

 with the increasing electrical demand on the south end of Logan, located on a .97-acre lot at

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a new electrical substation, transformer, small accessory

visually buffer the substation elements from public view. The solid 8-foot tall concrete block wall will
be setback from the property with landscaping placed in the front yard areas between the substation
and street.

Goodlander asked if there are high voltage power lines in the area. R. Holley said that there is a
high voltage power line on the west side of 1000 West that will be tied into this substation with a
transmission pole.
