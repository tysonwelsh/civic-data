       4. PUBLIC COMMENTS (Limited to 2 minutes per person)

Anyone who wants to speak on an item that is not on the public hearing agenda must sign up
and will be called in order. Please state your name. Council members will not respond during
public comments, but city staff will follow up if needed. Comments are limited to two (2)
minutes.

Lisa Henry approached the podium and stated she had no comments to offer for the current
month but anticipated providing comments at the next meeting.


Melinda Burrell addressed the Council with concerns regarding the ongoing median
construction on 8400 West. She explained that drivers had been driving over traffic cones in
the construction area and expressed her preference for a full median installation similar to
those on the north side. She stated that without a full median, motorists would continue
attempting illegal maneuvers, creating unsafe conditions, and she hoped the final design would
include attractive landscaping.


Mary Knowles then spoke regarding longstanding safety issues near Matheson Junior High,
particularly on Montclair and Jefferson. She noted that the school-zone speed sign had not
been functioning for years and did not light up, which made it difficult for drivers to identify
when children were crossing. She described situations in which parked cars, narrow travel
lanes, and school buses created dangerous conditions and made it difficult for vehicles to pass
each other safely. She emphasized that these issues occur across multiple streets in the area,
especially during busy times and when service vehicles such as garbage trucks are present.


       5. STAKEHOLDER REPORTS
          A. Unified Police Department – Chief Del Craig

Chief Craig of the Unified Police Department presented the monthly police update. He directed
the Council to the statistical report included in the meeting packet and explained that this
expanded report represented a shift under the new UPD administration toward broader, more
comprehensive pooled-services reporting. Chief Craig reviewed current caseloads, noting that
the Violent Crimes Unit was carrying 199 open cases and had received five new cases in the
prior month, including three death investigations, an assault, and a robbery. He continued with
an update on the Special Victims Unit, which had 88 active cases and had received fourteen
new referrals, including child sex abuse, adult sexual assault, child physical abuse,
pornography-related cases, and two runaway cases. He also reported on specialty team
activity, including nine callouts by the Collision Reconstruction Team, thirteen canine
deployments, and six drone deployments within Magna during the month of March. Chief Craig
emphasized that these centralized services allow major felony investigations to be handled by
specialized teams with focused training and expertise. He concluded by reiterating that
additional quarterly reporting would continue as part of the updated UPD reporting structure.
No questions were raised by the Council.

       7. PRESENTATION ITEMS
          A. MSD Updates – Chad Anderson

Reviewed his prepared presentation (Attachment A).

Assistant City Engineer Chad Anderson presented the monthly MSD Updates and reviewed
the prepared presentation included in the meeting materials. He began by addressing
comments that had been raised during the public comment period, including the
non‑functioning school zone sign near Matheson Junior High, and stated he would look into
the issue. He explained that the MSD was continuing work on the Transportation Master Plan,
noting that the project was ongoing with anticipated completion in fall 2026. He described the
forthcoming online “story map” that would allow the public and the Council to view long‑term
road plans and how these plans would guide future capital improvement funding.

Mr. Anderson then provided updates on several major roadway and infrastructure projects. He
stated that sidewalk construction on 8400 West was progressing, with the west side completed
and the east side approximately halfway finished. He informed the Council that paving would
begin soon and sought direction regarding the construction of the median on 8400 West. After
receiving earlier public feedback and Council discussion, he confirmed that MSD would
proceed with a full median installation rather than studying a gap in the northbound left‑turn
lane.

He also reviewed progress on additional roadway projects including 8000 West, 7200 West,
and the realignment of 2700 South and 2820. He noted that acquisition of remaining parcels
was still required before certain segments could advance, and that work continued on
interlocal agreements with West Valley City for cost‑sharing and design obligations. He

Following the engineering report, Long Range Planning Manager Matt Starley introduced an
informational presentation on the Walking Loops Program, accompanied by Tim Sullivan, the

the presentation was intended to provide an update on a new pedestrian‑oriented initiative
being coordinated with MSD. He highlighted Magna’s designation as a nationally recognized
historic district, which allows participation in the Utah Main Street Program and access to
certain tax incentives and grant opportunities. He described how the Walking Loops project
would complement recent downtown improvements, including murals and façade upgrades.

Tim Sullivan then provided an in‑depth overview of the Walking Loops concept, explaining that
the program encourages residents and visitors to walk through downtown districts, cultural
centers, and historic areas using safe and engaging routes. He described the planned route for
Magna’s downtown loop and showcased sample materials, including postcards, interpretive
signage, sidewalk decals, and QR‑code‑linked digital content that would provide historical and
contextual information about landmarks along the loop. He noted that temporary “pop‑up”
improvements—such as benches or painted crosswalks—may be incorporated depending on
available funding. Installation of program materials was tentatively planned for around the
Fourth of July.

Mr. Starley concluded by stating that the next steps included finalizing design materials,
coordinating with City staff and MSD, building out the program website, and preparing for
installation. The Council thanked both presenters for the update.

       8. COUNCIL BUSINESS
          A. Discussion and Potential Action Regarding Resolution R2026-26, A Resolution
             by the Magna City Council (The “Council”) Approving Funding for an Additional
             Police Sergeant – Chief Del Craig, Unified Police Department

The Council considered Resolution R2026-26, approving funding for an additional police
sergeant allocation within the Unified Police Department. Chief Del Craig reviewed his
prepared presentation and explained that, after evaluating two options, UPD recommended
converting an existing COP detective allocation into a sergeant position. He stated that the
cost difference associated with this conversion totaled $56,940 annually, which would need to
be absorbed by the City. Chief Craig discussed the operational impacts of the change and
noted that UPD intended to offset the reduction in detective allocations by adopting a more
hands-on “working sergeant” model. Chief Jason Mazuran added that Kearns and Brighton
were also expected to contribute to the related funding needs.

Council Member Prokopis described ongoing funding pressures at SLVLESA and emphasized
that the rising costs reflected increased call volume rather than an expansion of service.
Lieutenant Chris Benedict confirmed that the UPD Board had set strict limits on cost increases
and that current projections were tracking below those limits. City Attorney Nathan Bracken

advised the Council that the creation of this position would require an interlocal agreement
between Magna and UPD and recommended that the Council authorize staff to prepare such
an agreement, including a two-year review provision.

Council Member Jensen moved to approve Option Two for the sergeant position and
direct staff to prepare the interlocal agreement. Council Member Prokopis seconded the
motion, and it passed unanimously, 5–0.

             B. Discussion and Potential Action Regarding Authorizing the Mayor to Sign an
                Agreement with Economic Development Corporation (EDC) Utah – Greg
                Bisping and Kelly Bush, City Manager

presented an overview of the organization and its services, explaining that EDCUtah works to
attract and expand businesses in Utah by coordinating with communities, providing economic

one-year membership fee of $5,000 would give Magna access to its project pipeline, reporting

outreach.

Council Members Jensen, George, and Olsen asked questions regarding measurable
outcomes, annual evaluation, and how EDCUtah’s services would coordinate with those
provided by MSD. City Manager Kelly Bush stated that MSD was aware of the City’s interest
and that both agencies could work together. She recommended preparing an agreement for
Council review at a future meeting. No formal action was taken.

             E. Discussion and Potential Action Regarding Ordinance 2026-O-08, An Ordinance
                Imposing a Ban on the Discharge of Fireworks Within Designated Areas of
                Magna City – Nathan Bracken, City Attorney

The Council considered Ordinance 2026-O-08, adopting the annual fireworks restrictions map.
City Attorney Nathan Bracken explained that state law requires municipalities to adopt, each

             M. Discussion and Potential Action Regarding the Purchase and Construction of a
                Large Garage for Storage of City Parade Float – Kelly Bush, City Manager

explained that the float could not be stored at the former Cypress High School due to its

Sudbury reviewed the agreement originally reached with Modern Display for seasonal storage

Council Member George expressed concern about constructing a large storage structure on
the Webster Center property and suggested continuing to explore all other alternatives before
approving construction. Council Members Prokopis and Olsen discussed the possibility of
shrink-wrapping, partial disassembly, or off-site storage in nearby communities. City Manager
Bush reported that multiple entities had been contacted, including Rio Tinto, but that no
suitable storage site had been located. The Mayor invited the Council and community

       9. MANAGER/CITY ATTORNEY UPDATES
          A. City Manager Updates – Kelly Bush, Assistant City Manager

             B. City Attorney Updates – Nathan Bracken, City Attorney

City Attorney Nathan Bracken then presented legal updates. He reported that staff was
continuing to work on the boundary adjustment discussions with West Valley City, but no new
information was available at this time. He stated that he hoped to provide a more substantive
update at the next Council meeting. Mr. Bracken also provided background on the City’s
involvement with the Communities That Care (CTC) program. He explained that the existing
agreement—under which Magna acts as fiscal agent for certain county-provided funds—would
expire in June. He outlined two potential paths forward: the City could decline to renew its
involvement, or it could enter into a new agreement with updated terms reflecting recent
changes in federal law, state auditor requirements, and recommended financial controls. He
noted that when public funds exceeding $25,000 flow to a nonprofit partner, state law requires
a formal reporting and oversight structure, and he emphasized that any updated agreement
would need to incorporate those requirements.

Council Members Prokopis and Olsen expressed support for continuing with the program but
emphasized the need for clear guardrails and an annual review provision. Council Member
George agreed and requested that staff prepare a draft agreement for the Council’s review at a
future meeting. Mr. Bracken stated that staff would begin drafting the updated agreement
immediately so the Council could act before the current arrangement expired.
