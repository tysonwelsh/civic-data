Re: Letter of Intent for Group Home at 3550 S. Highway 23, Wellsville, Cache Co, UT. 84339.

Dear Mr. Harrild,

In accordance with Section(s) 17.16.010 (Group Living Facilities) of the Cache County Code,
Uinta Academy is requesting zoning approval for a Group Home at 3550 S. Highway 23,
Wellsville, UT 84339, parcel number 11-070-0006, ‘The program name is the Achievement
Home. Uinta Academy is requesting reasonable accommodation for twelve (12) disabled
residents, The home is located north of Wellsville in Cache County’s jurisdiction.

The area in which the Achievement Home is located is zoned A10 and contains large homes and
farms on large tracts of land. The property is 4.7 acres and contains a 6,900 square-foot home, a
large landscaped yard, a 40x40 workshop, a chicken coop, and an enclosed gazebo, Uinta
Academy will build a corral for the girls’ horses, Per DHS licensing requirements, there is room
for 12 clients to live in the home,

Proposed Use

Uinta Academy is a residential (group home) program for adolescent girls with mental health
disabilities. The Achievement Home will house adolescent girls ages thirteen (13) to seventeen
(17) with mental health disabilities. All of the proposed residents for the Achievement Home
home have emotional, mental health and behavioral disabilities that require them to be in

treatment in order for them to become functional in society and have a chance at a happy and
healthy life,

Uinta Academy is one of the premier treatment programs, nationally, for treating adolescent girls
with mental health disabilities. This is partly duc to our unique family-style treatment approach
which normalizes a girl’s life and replicates a family-like environment. Uinta is also known for
it’s therapeutic equine program where each girl gets her own horse.

All of the girls that reside in a Uinta Academy Group Home have a mental health and/or learning
disability. Specifically, all the girls have a formal DSM V diagnosis of emotional or mental
illness, The majority of the girls have a mental health diagnosis of PTSD, Depression, or
Anxiety Disorder, As a result of their mental health issues, the girls’ normal life functioning is
impaired. These impairments affect: thinking, mentation/processing, reasoning, judgment,
learning, self care, and basic social, academic, and independent/employment functioning. The
Federal Fair Housing Act Amendment and the American’s with Disabilities Act both recognize
person’(s) with mental health disabilities, such as those we treat, as a protected class of citizens


who have a right to live in any zone designated for single family residences, without restrictions
not placed on other groups or traditional families.

Uinta Academy does not accept girls with criminal or violent histories or actively addicted to
drugs or alcohol. The girls at Uinta Academy pose no risk to the community, of any kind,
whatsoever. Uinta Academy currently has 4 group home operating in Wellsville and Lewiston,
Utah. During the past 14 years of operation, there has never been an act of aggression, property
destruction, or abuse of any kind involving a girl at Uinta Academy and the community

Our goal at Uinta Academy is to provide the necessary mental health treatment for the girls in a
family-like setting. The focus is teaching them the basic social, academic, and independent
living skills necessary for them to eventually function in life. As ina traditional family, the girls
have daily chores, they cook together, eat together, go shopping together, participate in physical
exercise together, tend a garden together, and go on family activities together. In order to create
these family-like dynamics, the group home is operated as a normal large family single
residence, There are no commercial signs on the property and nothing about the home or yard
that would suggest that the group home is anything other than a large single family residence,
Should Uinta Academy decide to eventually sell the home and property, it could be immediately
sold as a single family residence to an independent family,

Hours of Operation

Because this is the girl’s residence, the Achievement Home will be in operation 24-hours a day,
7-days a week. However, the girls will be sleeping between the hours of 10:00 pm and 6:45 am,
so the home will appear as any other single family residence during those hours.

Number of Employees

The Division of Human Services Licensing Office requires that residential treatment home
maintain a minimum 1:4 ratio, or one staff for every four residents, and a minimum of 2 staff on
premises at all times. They also require the program to have a nurse, and mental health and
academic professionals to provide mental health counseling and academic services to the
residents. Uinta Academy has approximately 80 full-time and part-time employees; 12-14 of
these will be dedicated full-time to the Achievement Home. The Achievement Home will
maintain 3 staff (Family Teachers) during the hours of 6:45 am to 10:45 pm. The Family
Teachers are female employees who have bachelor degrees. During the weekdays, there will
also be a licensed therapist, a licensed teacher and a home supervisor who will each be present
approximately 30-40 hours per week. The equine manager will spend approximately 4-6 hours
per week working with the girls and their horses. A nurse will be present 3-4 hours per week.
During the hours of 10:45 pm to 6:45 am when the girls are in bed, there will be 2 awake night
staff.

Traffic and Parking

During weekdays, there will be 4-5 vehicles parked at a time at the Achievement Home. On the
weekends, there will be 3-4 vehicles parked on the property. The home has a two-car garage and
there is room in front of the garage and along the circular driveway to park vehicles. The girls
who live in the home do not have personal vehicles and they are not allowed to drive.


Uinta Academy does not intend to park any junk vehicles, buses, or boats on the property. The
property contains many large mature pine trecs and the house and parking areas are not visible
from Highway 23. Accordingly, the number of vehicles parked at the home at any given time
would be well within the norms for a home in a rural community. A drive through the
neighborhood shows a number of homes with 4 to 6 or more vehicles; including cars, work
trucks, tractors, farm equipment, recreational vehicles, and cven semi tractor/trailer(s). There is
a heavily used gravel pit approximately 4 mile from the residence that permits large dump trucks
and heavy equipment. There is also a home directly across Highway 23, that regularly has large
commercial equipment and dump trucks parked at the residence. The number of vehicles parked
at the Achievement Home will be within the normal number of cars for the area,

Treatment Phases

Uinta believes that long term change cannot be externally imposed. The Achievement
Home uses the Stages of Change Model to assess a girl’s progress in treatment. The idea behind
SCM is that behavior change does not happen in one step. Rather, people tend to progress
through different stages on their way to successful change. Identifying where a girl is at in the
change process allows the therapist to tailor interventions to match the girl’s readiness to change.
Just as important is the girl’s understanding of where she is in the stages of change, Insight into
cognitive distortions and unhealthy behaviors that are getting in the way of progress will help a
girl take ownership of the change process. The Stages of Change are:

* Pre-contemplation - is the stage at which there is no intention to change behavior in the
foreseeable future. The girl is unaware or under-aware of her problem or in denial that
there is a problem at all. Girls in this stage are under constant staff supervision and she
has limited privileges. The use of a point system is used to motivate the girl to use newly
learned skills.

* Contemplation - is the stage in which the girl is aware a problem exists and is seriously
thinking about overcoming it, but she has not yet made a commitment to take action. ‘The
fear of change may cause the girl to remain stuck in this stage. Girls in this stage are
under constant staff supervision, however, they can receive visits from parents,

* Preparation — in this stage the girl is intending to take action and may make small
behavioral changes, but has not yet reached the decision to take effective action. She is

appear to “have one foot in the door” and the other foot out. Girls in this stage may earn

In interpreting the Fair Housing Act the Courts have been consistent in their decisions
that the FHAA, ADA, etc, apply to land use matters. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals
indicated “(T)he Act prohibits local governments from applying land use regulations in a
manner..., that will give disabled people less opportunity to live in certain neighborhoods than
people without disabilities.” Smith & Lee Associates, Inc. v. City of Taylor, 102 F.3d 781, 794-
95 (6" Cir. 1996)

The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals has also weighed in as follows: “the law of a state or
municipality is expressly preempted by the Fair Housing Act if it is a ‘discriminatory housing
practice’ under the Act.” Bangerter v. Orem Cily Corp., 46 F.3d 1491, 1500 (10" Cir, 1995).
The Court further explained: “It is widely accepted that an FHAA violation can be demonstrated
by either disparate treatment or disparate impact,” Id, at 1501. Disparate impact occurs when a
facially neutral statute “actually” or ‘predictably’ results in discrimination”. Corporation of
Episcopal Church in Utah v, West Valley City, 119 F.Supp.2d 1215, 1219 (D. Utah 2000).

A number of courts have made it clear that the reasonable accommodation requirement of
the Federal Fair Housing Act includes requiring communities to waive maximum residency
limitations in their codes for residential facilities for the disabled for financial reasons, In City of
Edmunds y. Oxford House, Inc, 514 U.S, 725 (1995), the United States Supreme Court ruled that
limitations on the number of unrelated disabled persons that could occupy a residential building
was not allowed when the City did not limit the number of persons who could occupy a
residential building if they are related by genetics, adoption or marriage. In the Edmonds case
Oxford House sought an accommodation to the City’s limit of five unrelated persons ina
residential dwelling because its residential facility for recovering alcoholics and drug addicts was
demonstrated under the facts of the case to need a higher number of residents “to be financially
and therapeutically viable”, The Supreme Court ruled that such an accommodation was covered
by the reasonable accommodation requirements of the Federal Fair Housing Act.

Other cases where the Courts have held that financial viability or financial issues are
sufficient basis for requiring an accommodation by waiving a community’s limit on the number
of unrelated client who may occupy a residential building include Oxford House-Evergreen v.
City of Plainfield, 769 F. Supp 1329 (N.J. 1991); Smith & Lee Associates y. City of Taylor, 102
F.3d 781 (6" Cir. 1996); and Dr. Gertrude A. Barber Center, Inc. v Peters Township, 273 F.
Supp.2d 643 (W.D. Pa. 2003), In addition, there are a great number of other cases in which
financial hardship or financial viability served as the basis for requiring accommodation in
zoning and other matters under the Federal Fair Housing Act.

The Federal District Court of Utah ruled in The Corporation of the Episcopal Church y.

accommodation of a zoning limitation because financial considerations made it necessary to

Summary

The objective of a reasonable accommodation is to allow people with disabilities an equal
opportunity to reside in residential neighborhoods. To achieve this result, individuals with
disabilities need services and treatment programs that are not needed by people without
disabilities. State and Federal governments have mandated that local governments provide

heightened protection to these types of group homes so that the results of equal opportunity to
housing can be achieved.

The Acts recognize persons with disabilities as a protected class of citizens who have a
right to live in any zone designated for single family residences, without restrictions that are not
placed on other groups or traditional families. The courts have made it clear that the Acts
impose an affirmative duty to make reasonable accommodation in order to allow persons with
disabilities equal opportunity to live in housing of their choice and that equal results will be
achieved as between the person with a disability requesting the accommodation and a non-
disabled person.

Disabled persons must live together in residential facilities because they are not capable
of independent living, Equal results will be achieved as between the persons who will reside in
the Achievement Home and persons who live together because they are related by genetics,
adoption or marriage since there is no limit on the number of such related persons who may live
together in a single residence. The results may be different for non-disabled persons who are not
related, However, the courts have rejected claims that the number of persons with disabilities
can be limited because the same or similar limits imposed on unrelated persons without
disabilities if the accommodation requested from those limits is reasonable and necessary to
allow persons with disabilities equal opportunity to live in housing of their choice.

Treatment Phases:

Uinta believes that long term change cannot be externally imposed, The Young Adult
Home uses the Stages of Change Model to assess a girl’s progress in treatment. The idea behind
SCM is that behavior change does not happen in one step. Rather, people tend to progress
through different stages on their way to successful change, Identifying where a girl is at in the
change process allows the therapist to tailor interventions to match the girl’s readiness to change,
Just as important is the girl’s understanding of where she is in the stages of change. Insight into
cognitive distortions and unhealthy behaviors that are getting in the way of progress will helpa
girl take ownership of the change process. The Stages of Change are:

* Pre-contemplation - is the stage at which there is no intention to change behavior in the
foreseeable future. The girl is unaware or under-aware of her problem or in denial that
there is a problem at all. Girls in this stage are under constant staff supervision and she

has limited privileges. ‘The use of a point system is used to motivate the girl to use newly
learned skills.

* Contemplation - is the stage in which the girl is aware a problem exists and is seriously
thinking about overcoming it, but she has not yet made a commitment to take action. The

Family Program:

The Young Adult Home takes a family-systems approach to treatment. Lasting change
occurs when the whole family is engaged in a coordinated process of growth. Girls who are
struggling are more likely to make necessary changes if they feel that the entire family system is
cooperatively changing. The girls’ parents/guardians participate in weekly family therapy
sessions, monthly parenting classes, and bi-annual Family Weekends. As girls advance through
the stages of change, they earn home visits where they can practice newly learned skills and
coping mechanisms.

that the FHAA, ADA, etc. apply to land use matters. The Sixth Circuit Court of Appeals
indicated “(T)he Act prohibits local governments from applying land use regulations in a
manner... that will give disabled people less opportunity to live in certain neighborhoods than

95 (6" Cir, 1996)

The Tenth Circuit Court of Appeals has also weighed in as follows: “the law of a state or

facially neutral statute “actually” or ‘predictably’ results in discrimination”, Corporation of

A number of courts have made it clear that the reasonable accommodation requirement of
the Federal Fair Housing Act includes requiring communities to waive maximum residency
limitations in their codes for residential facilities for the disabled for financial reasons, In City of
Edmunds v, Oxford House, Inc. 514 U.S. 725 (1995), the United States Supreme Court ruled that
limitations on the number of unrelated disabled persons that could occupy a residential building
was not allowed when the City did not limit the number of persons who could occupy a
residential building if they are related by genetics, adoption or marriage. In the Edmonds case
Oxford House sought an accommodation to the City’s limit of five unrelated persons in a
residential dwelling because its residential facility for recovering alcoholics and drug addicts was
demonstrated under the facts of the case to need a higher number of residents “to be financially
and therapeutically viable”, The Supreme Court ruled that such an accommodation was covered
by the reasonable accommodation requirements of the Federal Fair Housing Act.

West Valley City, 119F.Supp2d 1215 (Utah 2000) that West Valley City would have to make an
accommodation of a zoning limitation because financial considerations made it necessary to
build a proposed residential facility for persons with disabilities at the requested location. The
Court also ruled that the facility needed to be located where it was proposed because the clients
were best served by living in a residential setting.

In Smith & Lee Associates, Ine. v. City of Taylor, 102 F.3d 781 (6" Cir, 1996), the Sixth
Circuit Court held that the district court was correct in finding reasonable accommodation should
have been granted on the grounds of financial viability, among other reasons. It stated,
“Evidence at trial established that, in Taylor, Michigan, a six-person occupancy limit on for-
profit (adult foster care homes) serving the elderly disabled guarantees a negligible or negative
rate of return for investors.” Id at 797. The Smith & Lee court clearly envisioned that the
financial needs of the home can be a factor in determining necessity.

In Keys Youth Services v. City of Olathe, 75 F. Supp.2d 1235 (D. Kansas 1999), a
nonprofit corporation that operated youth homes brought an action against the city under the Fair
Housing Act. The City had denied the corporation’s request to house more clients than permitted
under the applicable zoning ordinance. On appeal, the corporation claimed that the number of
client requested was essential to the financial viability of the proposed home. Jd at 1246, The
court recognized that, “[iJn determining whether accommodation was necessary, Courts have
considered whether a minimum size may be essential to the success of the venture.”

Summary

The objective of a reasonable accommodation is to allow people with disabilities an equal
opportunity to reside in residential neighborhoods. To achieve this result, individuals with
disabilities need services and treatment programs that are not needed by people without
disabilities. State and Federal governments have mandated that local governments provide

heightened protection to these types of group homes so that the results of equal opportunity to
housing can be achieved,

The Acts recognize persons with disabilities as a protected class of citizens who have a
right to live in any zone designated for single family residences, without restrictions that are not
placed on other groups or traditional families. The courts have made it clear that the Acts
impose an affirmative duty to make reasonable accommodation in order to allow persons with
disabilities equal opportunity to live in housing of their choice and that equal results will be
achieved as between the person with a disability requesting the accommodation and a non-
disabled person.
