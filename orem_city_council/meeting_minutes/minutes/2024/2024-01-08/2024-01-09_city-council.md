Open Public Meeting Act and Ethics Training Presenter: Steve Earl, City Attorney

Mr. Earl explained this is the annual required Open Public Meeting Act and Ethics
Training. This is found in section 52-4 of the State Code. Mr. Earl defined an official meeting. If
four members of council are together then City Council Members should not discuss City
matters. All meetings need to be noticed 24 hours in advance with an agenda and no final actions
can be made on items not on the agenda. The meeting will need to be recorded with minutes
posted after the meeting. If there are mistakes they can be corrected but must represent what
happened in the meeting. If the City Council goes on a tour or field trip, that portion of the
meeting does not need to be recorded. Independent recordings are allowed by those in
attendance.

Mr. Earl continued to explain closed sessions. Closed sessions are on limited topics (ie.
to discuss pending or reasonably imminent litigation; the character or professional competence of

an individual; or the purchase or lease of real property). In the closed session no votes or final
actions can be taken. Closed Sessions are required to be recorded or an affidavit is required to be
signed.

Mr. Earl spoke about electronic meetings. The City of Orem has passed a resolution to
allow for electronic meetings. The City Council Chambers is the anchor location and at least one
member of the City Council needs to be at the anchor location. This is usually the Mayor or
Mayor Pro Tem.

There are penalties for violation of this act. This is a Class B misdemeanor. He reminded
the City Council that if 4 Council Members are together outside of meetings, they should not
speak about City business. Speaking with staff about an issue is allowed.

Mr. Earl moved on to the topic of Ethics. The Orem City code 2-6-8 explains conflicts of
interest. If a council member has a financial or personal interest, then they need to disclose and
possibly recuse themselves. If there is a question, please speak with the legal department. The
Municipal Officers and Employee Ethics Act is found in the Utah State Code 10-3-1301. The
code prohibits the use of office for personal benefit or advantage and it outlines disclosure
requirements. These can be criminal offenses. Improper behaviors include disclosing private or
protected information for their own gain or gain of others, securing personal privileges and
accepting gifts of significant value for you or for someone else you know that could influence
decision making. Some exceptions are campaign contributions, public awards, and the occasional
gifts of less than $50.

Disclosures are needed if you receive compensation for services, if you own or work for
an entity that is regulated by a municipality or you have any interest in a business that may create
a conflict. The code has strict penalties for ethics violations. Ms. Millett asked who enforces this.
Mr. Earl explained that he would like to clean up the City Code to have the State be the ethics
enforcement. Mr. Bybee asked if this training could be used for other commissions. Mr. Earl
believes that this training will work for those other commissions but that the City Council will
want to confirm that with the other commissions.

Station Area Plans Update Presenter: Jason Bench, Assistant Development Services Director;

Grant Allen, Senior Planner; Christie Oostema Brown AICP, people+place; and John Janson
AICP, Planning Solutions

Ms Oostema Brown began with the shared objectives: to increase the availability and
affordability of housing, to promote sustainable environmental conditions, to enhance access to
opportunities and to increase transportation choices and connections. She explained the plan's
origin and that growth is part of our history and future. This growth she is talking about comes
from natural increase. Ms Oostema Brown showed a graph of the Median Home Sales Price and
Household Income for Utah County. It shows that incomes have increased in 10 years by about
$30,000 but homes have increased by $300,000. Ms Millett asked questions about the interest
rate, loan terms and down payment figured into the graph.

Ms. Oostema Brown asked what is affordable housing? She explained that for most
families homes that would be around $300,000. The average renter is spending $1,500 a month
and only 12% of renters can afford to purchase a home. Governor Cox says the answer is more
housing. When growth pressure is high, creating great places to live, in the right locations,
matters a lot.

Ms. Oostema Brown illustrated the public stakeholder process, which is to provide
research and information to the public, seek broad public input, build vision directly from public
input, use transparent methods and build momentum for implementation. The public vision and
station area process has four parts. First, hold a workshop to brainstorm. Second, have an open
house to have the public evaluate the scenarios. Third, get a consensus of the vision scenarios.
Finally, implementation strategy and adoption of the station area plan.

Ms. Oostema Brown quickly reviewed what they learned. Mayor Young asked her to talk
about the slide on who participated in the process. The slide showed that 31% lived in single
family homes inside the study area, 5% lived in apartments inside the study area, 52% lived in
Orem but outside the study area and 11% lived outside of Orem. They looked at the many ideas
that were generated from the public. In a nutshell, this is what they learned. The public wants to
preserve existing residential neighborhoods but add new residential with green space. The public
likes mixed use villages for the FrontRunner Station and UVU. They would like to see retail
added, green space throughout, trails and pathways added, and safe, efficient transportation
modes.

The next step was to explore some options for scenarios. They came up with three
scenarios: Mixed Use Village, UVU Mixed Use Village, and Neighborhood Infill. The Mixed
Use Village scenario would have a mixed use village on the south side near the station,
commercial along Geneva Road and green space added. The UVU Mixed Use Village would
have a mixed use village on the south, on campus student housing, parking garages that are
wrapped with green walls, UVU parking garage services and the addition of new single family
homes with detached ADUs. The Neighborhood Infill would include new residential housing on
undeveloped land in multiple areas of the city, student housing on campus, and new park or
green area. Each plan has the same amount of housing. She showed a slide comparing the three
scenarios by safe pedestrian routes, trails connecting to the station or UVU, parking garages, safe
bike routes, ability to move cars efficiently, passive green space, affordable housing,
placemaking, reduced surface parking lots and day-to-day needs.

Ms. Millett wants to know how this housing will stay affordable. Mr. Allen and Mr.
Bench from the planning department explained that this would be part of the city’s planning
process and zoning codes.

The 2050 Vision Development is to pull concepts that capture public feedback, reflect the
overall input, implement regional goals and meet the demands of Orem. They asked the public
which of the 3 scenarios makes the most sense to meet the needs of the future. The results were
43% chose the Mixed Use Village, 41% chose the UVU Mixed Use Village and 17% chose the

Neighborhood Infill. The public also expressed a need for UVU student housing, affordable

The proposed vision & vision principles are that the Orem Station is an urban
neighborhood that supports Orem residents, UVU students, and commuters who are living,
working, playing, learning, and traveling in the area. It’s a friendly place to call home, an
innovative educational and vocational hub, a launch pad for workdays and school days, and a

outside. 5. Encourage community with public gathering spaces and friendly streets with shops,

Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) Legislative Priorities for Cities Presenter:
Karson Eilers, ULCT

Mr. Eilers is the policy director for the Utah League of Cities and Towns. He is speaking
on legislative policy that will be in the upcoming Legislative Session. He explained the ULCT
purpose as it pertains to cities in Utah. He spoke about the three evaluation processes the ULCT
goes through with upcoming bills. First, does this bill respect the traditional role of local
government? Second, is this bill a one size fits all approach? Finally, will this bill result in
unfunded or unworkable mandates?

Two key things he wants to touch on is that there is no surplus in the State or cities this
year. This could mean that fiscal issues may play a bigger part on bills. Second, there are a lot of
bills and it is important to make sure the legislature is aware of the city's issues, needs and
concerns.

Mr. Eilers moved on to housing and growth. In the last few years the state created the
Unified Economic Commission. They recommend legislation to be expedited through the regular
session. This group has been focused on housing and transportation. This is another process
where policy can move forward. The ULCT has adopted four key principles for the Unified
Economic Commission. First, how does this proposal preserve the quality of life for current and
future residents? Second, does this proposal allocate the current and future infrastructure? Third,
will this proposal result in more housing units that are directly affordable to the buyer or renter?

Utah State Senators and Representatives Legislative Discussion Presenter: Marsha Judkins,
Utah State Representative and Keven Stratton, Utah State Representative

Utah State Representative Marsha Judkin and Keven Stratton have joined us for
discussion of the upcoming legislative session. Rep. Stratton came in person and Rep. Judkins
was available by zoom. Rep. Stratton spoke about some important benchmarks, issues and policy
in the State of Utah. Representative Judkin gave an overview of things she is concerned about,
including reduction of taxes. She would like to see money focused on families and that cutting
taxes can put pressure on local governments. She has recently been put on the Political
Subdivision Committee.

Mayor Young expressed his concerns about the emphasis on real estate development. He
would like them to stay in check and he is concerned about any mandates put on cities that are
not funded. He would also like to see an emphasis on the bills that are most important. Rep.
Stratton spoke about the struggle that the legislature has over dealing with affordable housing.
Rep. Judkins responded that the number of bills doesn’t mean more oversight, because many of
