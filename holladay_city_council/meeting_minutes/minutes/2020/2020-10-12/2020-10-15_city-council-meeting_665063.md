III.    Public Hearing on Proposed Storm Water Utility Fee Ordinance.
Ms. Chamness reported that the above matter has been considered for several years and has been
studied by the Holladay@20 Citizen Committee. Through various discussions, it was determined
that there is approximately $18 million in expenses for stormwater improvement projects and projects
that need to be completed to comply with State and Federal law. The Holladay@20 Citizen
Committee proposed the Storm Water Utility Fee Ordinance to address the needed improvements.

Ms. Chamness reviewed additional upcoming opportunities for public comment during the Ordinance
consideration process in addition to tonight’s meeting. Mayor Dahle confirmed that public comment
will remain open until the Council is comfortable moving forward with a decision. A video regarding
the proposed Storm Water Utility Fee Ordinance was made available on Facebook Live.

Mayor Dahle read into the record the names of Jon Greaves, David McAllister and David Dean who
submitted written comments that will be included with the minutes.

The Mayor opened the public hearing at 6:14 pm.

Lyn Felton - 4477 South Peach St. He reported that he submitted a list of comments entitled “Storm
Water Assessment Questions.” He asked about a discrepancy in the CIP assumption amounts. Ms.
Chamness offered to address the matter with Mr. Felton off-line. Mr. Felton added that he considered
the consultant numbers to be somewhat misleading. He supported the City moving forward with
Scenario #3.

IV.     Public Hearing on Proposed Rezone for Property Located at 4813 South Viewmont Street
        from R-1-21 to R-1-10 (Greg Fabiano, Agent, for Owner, Cheryl Cromar, to amend the City
        of Holladay’s Zoning Map from R-1-21 (one home per half-acre), to R-1-10 (one home per
        10,000 square feet).
Greg Fabiano, applicant -8121 Copper Canyon Way was present on behalf of the property owner,
Cheryl Cromar. The Cromar family wishes to subdivide the property, which is just less than one acre
in size. The property was zoned R-1-10 by Salt Lake County prior to the City’s incorporation. The
City then rezoned the property to R-1-21. In order to subdivide the property, it would need to be
reverted to an R-1-10 zone. The applicant was seeking to subdivide the parcel into three buildable
lots. The applicant has met all of the necessary requirements for open meetings and was reviewed by
the Planning Commission. The project was determined to comply with the City of Holladay’s General
Plan.

Paul Allred, Community Development Dir. Provided the staff report. He noted that the project has
received a great deal of vocal opposition. The R-1-15 zone would allow for two lots rather than three
as requested by the applicant, and would be more appropriate.

Mr. Fabiano explained that there is no intention to include high-density housing within the project
and nothing out of the ordinary is proposed. They are seeking to develop three lots, but if not three,
they would also accept the ability to build on two lots. Mayor Dahle asked about the size of the lots.
Mr. Fabiano described the various lot sizes and stated that all similar in size.

Mayor Dahle opened the public hearing at 6:42 pm. He acknowledged the receipt of written
comments from Bryon Smith who submitted written comments that will be included with the minutes.

Kate Meier - 4810 S Viewmont St. He mentioned that the General Plan principles provide that the
City should support the current zoning. She stated that the neighborhood does not support a rezone.

Tim Begue- 4835 S Viewmont St. He is opposed the project because the neighborhood is already
experiencing the impacts of increased density.

Dan Smith - 4805 S Viewmont St. He echoed the concerns regarding increased neighborhood traffic.
He was more amendable to a rezone to R-1-15.

V.      Public Hearing on Proposed Vacation of Public Right-of-Way – Lynne Lane Walkway (a
        proposal to vacate a portion of Lynne Lane which currently serves as a public walkway
        connection between Lynne Lane and 4500 South).
Ralph Carlson, applicant- -2217 East 4500 South. He explained that the project began several years
ago when Officer Barney was shot in the nearby cul-de-sac. The path is open to the public and has
been the source of numerous problems and criminal activity over the years. The path is a blight and
has not been maintained other than by the neighbors.

Manager Chamness reported that Salt Lake County was the original owner and the walkway was not
transferred to City ownership until recently. The Council is only considering the walkway and not
the right of way aligning 4500 South or Lynne Lane. The Council previously discussed the possibility
of using a surplus process to dispose of the parcel; however, because it is a right-of-way parcel it must
go through the vacation process.

Mayor Dahle clarified that if the parcel were vacated it could be landscaped, but not be built on.
Council Member Durham asked about the practical implications of vacating the property.
Ms. Chamness explained that vacation requires a public hearing process, whereas a surplus process
would allow the action to be taken administratively. City Attorney, Todd Godfrey, described the
vacation process.

Council Member Gibbons asked if eliminating access to the property was considered. Mayor Dahle
suggested discussing that during the Work Session.

Council Member Quinn asked if Holladay Water Company will need ongoing access to the property.
The applicant, Mr. Carlson, was aware that access will continue to be needed by the Holladay Water
Company and intends to provide access in the future.

Mayor Dahle opened the public hearing at 7:08 pm and acknowledged written comments from
Laura Walker, Tara Hills, Tanya Bates & Connie Zaccheo that were made part of the record.

Brett Graham - 4450 Lynne Lane . He stated that criminal activity primarily occurs at night. He
supported the path being closed or controlled.

Bonnie Pace - 4470 South Lynne Lane. She owns the home directly west of the walkway and agrees
that there have been problems with trash and graffiti, however, since she installed a fence, the
problems have been resolved. She supported keeping the path open with controls in place.

VI.     City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness reported that the Governor of the State of Utah has announced a movement from a
color-coded response to COVID-19 and a movement toward a public health data-driven metric called
the Transmission Index that will be based on a number of factors. Currently, Salt Lake County is in
the high transmission index level, which means that masks are required in public settings, indoors
and outdoors, where social distancing cannot be maintained. Ms. Chamness sought additional
clarification but stated that gatherings of more than 10 people are discouraged. City staff is currently
required to wear masks. She believes the new guidelines may present some challenges with upcoming
City matters.

Council Member Gibbons thanked Ms. Chamness and staff for their efforts.

Council Member Quinn asked about a sponsorship sign that blew down and asked if it needed to be

        c.      Discussion on Golf Carts.
Mayor Dahle mentioned that a video was compiled by Chief Hoyal clarifying the issues surrounding
golf carts. He wanted the Council to decide whether to adopt an ordinance regulating golf carts before
the video is released to the general public. By Utah State statute, unless a City specifically authorizes
the driving of golf carts by ordinance, they are illegal.

Council Member Fotheringham stated that the use of golf carts is illegal unless made legal and there
is not a lot of desire to make them legal. Ms. Chamness agreed and asked if the Council was ready
to receive public input on the legality of golf carts. Chief Hoyal reported that the Police Department
currently addresses golf cart use by educating the public on legality and safety. Ms. Chamness
clarified that the law is silent on the legality of golf cart use prior to May 2020. It then changed to be
illegal unless an ordinance was adopted legalizing golf cart use.

Council Member Petersen raised concerns about children driving golf carts and safety issues. She
felt that the Council needs to hear from residents with public input. She was concerned about
regulation if golf carts are legalized.

Council Member Fotheringham asked Chief Hoyal what direction is needed from the Council. Chief
Hoyal agreed that it is a significant issue in the community but stated that the Police Department will
abide by the Council’s direction. The video produced by Chief Hoyal was viewed by the Council.

Mayor Dahle asked the Council for direction on addressing a Golf Cart Ordinance. The Council
discussed potential direction addressing education in the community as well as possible regulation.
The Council agreed to post the video on the City’s website in February and determine next steps based
on public input.
