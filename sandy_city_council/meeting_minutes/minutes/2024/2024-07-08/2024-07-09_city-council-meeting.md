4. 24-248 City Council receiving a presentation on the 2024 General Management
and Operations Study of the Sandy City Fire Department

Attachments: FINAL REPORT

Presentation
Dustin Fratto, introduced Chief Tim Nowak and Chief Steve Noble, consultants with
Public Consulting Group (PCG) who presented their final report and recommendations
from a management study conducted on the Sandy City Fire Department at the Council's
request. PCG is a national fire and EMS consulting team with additional expertise in
EMS/healthcare cost reporting and public policy with offices both nationally and
internationally. The final report presented this evening contains 21 scope of work
elements and 30 key recommendations. In their review of the fire department, they found
the culture to be very positive and one that exceeds traditional expectations with a
promising future. Sandy City has steady population growth with future development and
upward expansion. The Sandy Fire Department organizational structure is a fire-based
EMS Agency with a 5 station static deployment model and a specialty response focus.
The assessment of the city's wildland urban interface was considered a realistic threat
(eastern border) and they reviewed with the Council and the Administration the city's
resources, training and experience, and proactive public outreach. In their review of the
fire stations and fleet, they found 3 stations to be adequate, 1 station that needed
improvement, and identified Station 31 in need of being replaced. The city has addressed
this need and has begun construction of a new Station 31. In their review of the fire fleet,
their findings indicated the following fleet needed to be replaced: 7 engines, 1 truck, and 2
ambulances. Additionally, they considered 2 engines, 1 truck, and 3 ambulances be
considered for replacement. They also reviewed with the Council the following: incident
and call data, comparable assessment locally and nationally, and stakeholder
engagement. Their overall assessment findings are as follows: Administrative
assessment with 10 key findings and 10 key recommendations; Operational assessment
with 12 key findings and 15 key recommendations; and Capital assessment with 6 key
findings and 5 key recommendations. They also reviewed the strategic and master plan
and recommendations for the fire department: Immediate (0-6 months), Near Future (6-12
months) and Short Term (1-3 years), Long Term (3 to 5 years) and Extended Time Period
(5 to 10 years).
Council questions, comments, and discussion followed. Council expressed their thanks
to the consultant. They felt the study was very comprehensive and well worth the
investment and will help guide the Council forward with future budget decisions. Council
Member Houseman felt the study provided clarity for the council. Council Member
Dekeyzer asked for cost and data comparables for capital improvements and for planning.
Consultants continued to respond to Council questions and provided additional insight
and details. They reviewed recommendations for personal protective equipment and the
streamlining of the dispatch center. Council Member Mecham thanked the Fire personnel
who attended this evening's meeting and for their participation in the study. Council
Member Stroud had questions regarding the fire fleet. Mayor Zoltanski spoke on the
positive culture in the fire department and attributes this to the people we hire. We hire
the best people. The Mayor thanked Council Member Houseman for bringing forth this
idea.
Interim Chief Ryan McConaghie thanked the Council for requesting and funding the study.
He is very open to the recommendations and appreciated the support from the Council
and Administration.
