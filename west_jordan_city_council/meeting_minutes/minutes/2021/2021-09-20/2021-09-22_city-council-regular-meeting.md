A.     Wood Ranch, MDA, and PC-Hillside Zone Discussion
On August 11, 2021, the City Council tabled Ordinance No. 21-19 amending the City Code to add 13-
5 Article L Planned Community Hillside (PCH) Zone until the September 22nd meeting for a report
on the Wood Ranch Master Development Agreement (MDA). Community Development Director
Scott Langford reported staff and Daybreak had agreed on Transferred Development Open Space
(TDOS), defined as “that portion of a clustered development plan from which density has been
permanently transferred and the land has been dedicated to the public,” with the following formula:

Acres of TDOS/Density Unit = Entire Project Area (Acres) x .25/Total Project Residential Density

Mr. Langford showed a table of open space proposed to be provided in each of the ten phases of
development of Wood Ranch. Representatives of Daybreak estimated the full Wood Ranch project
would be completed within 12-20 years. Mr. Langford spoke of the slope of acres of proposed TDOS.
The following points were explained and discussed:

       Residential Land Use
       • Number of total residential units: 3,068
       • A maximum of 10% of the single-family residential units may include accessory dwelling
          units (ADUs), approved during the subdivision platting process; the ADUs would not
          count against the total residential cap.
       • Number of stacked flat “apartment style” housing: maximum 600 units
       • Single-family versus multi-family ratio: 50/50 mix (1,534 single-family; 1,534 multi-
          family [inclusive of the 600 stacked flats listed above])
       • Single-family versus multi-family pacing: it was the direction of the City Council that
          single-family detached units should never be outpaced by multi-family units. Staff and
          Daybreak were still working on the specific language of how to implement this direction.

Responding to a question from the Council regarding ADUs, a representative of Daybreak stated
some elements of the MDA would carry forward to the HOA. The group discussed trail connections,
the Conceptual Lotting Plan, and alley widths (standard and fire access). Mr. Langford commented
that alley widths were sufficient for two vehicles to pass each other, but the drive aprons allowed
space for vehicles to move over a little and increase the comfort level when passing. Council Member
Whitelock said she had seen vehicles parked in the drive aprons at Daybreak South Jordan, which
reduced the level of comfort. The alleys would be City-owned but maintained by the HOA.

       Commercial Land Use
       • Once the 500th residential unit was constructed, the Property Owner/Master Developer
         shall construct and shall operate, or cause to be operated, a bistro or restaurant of at least
         3,000 gross square feet in floor area; said bistro would be held open to the general public
         for at least two meals per day, six days per week.
       • Within the first subdivision plat within a Village Center Place type, and as a condition of
         plat approval, Property Owner/Master Developer, would construct, or cause to be
         constructed, a minimum of twelve Live/Work units. A Live/Work Unit is an attached
         building form with a minimum of a 75% glass façade and commercial store front that is
         oriented to a street, has a minimum first floor height of ten feet and a minimum finished
         first floor space (excluding garage) of 400 square feet.
       • Within the first subdivision plat within Phase 9, and as a condition of plat approval,
         Property Owner/Master Developer, shall landscape and dedicate to the City an option to
         develop a parcel of at least 5 acres at the corner of 7400 South and U-111 (“5 Acre
         Parcel”).
       • The 5 Acre Parcel shall be used initially for storm water retention requirements of the
         Project and shall be maintained by the HOA, with access reserved to the City. If the City
         determined to develop the 5 Acre Parcel as a commercial use, the City shall first provide
         to Master Developer/Property Owner at least 30 days written notice of its intent to
         advance commercial development of the 5 Acre Parcel, along with the proposed plans,
         development schedule, design, proposed land sale price and development cost, and the
         City-derived economic benefits analysis. Master Developer shall either commit to
         develop the 5 Acre Parcel, which would generate the same economic benefit to the City,
         under the same development schedule, or deed to the City the 5 Acre Parcel. In either
         case, the development would not exceed 75,000 gross square feet, excluding structured
         parking, in addition to the maximum commercial density provided to the Master
         Developer/Property Owner.

Economic Development Director Chris Pengra said the 5 Acre Parcel would likely not meet site
selection requirements to attract a Trader Joes but suggested the unique space would attract
boutiques or restaurants. City Administrator Korban Lee commented that commercial property 10
acres south at 7800 South and U-111 were designated for grocery store and pad sites.

 6.    PUBLIC HEARINGS
        a. ChamberWest Fee Waiver
Council Office Director Alan Anderson explained the request from ChamberWest for a fee waiver
for rental of the Community Room at City Hall for a Meet the Candidates event on October 12th. He
explained that State Code required a public hearing for potential fee waivers and did not allow fee
waivers to exceed 1% of the budget for the given year. City Code aligned with State Code and placed
the responsibility of granting a waiver on the legislative body. The budget impact of the proposed
fee waiver would be 2.5 hours at $95 per hour, less the approved 25% discount for non-profits, for
a total of $178.13.

Responding to a question from Council Member McConnehey, City Attorney Rob Wall said he
advised the Council Office it would not be appropriate for the City to sponsor a Meet the Candidates
event with current Council Members running for office. The event would be sponsored by
ChamberWest. Mr. Anderson added that the agreement with ChamberWest did not specify a Meet
the Candidates event as a required event but did allow business and networking events. Responding
to a question from Council Member Lamb, Mr. Wall said he did not believe current candidate Council
Members voting on the proposed fee waiver would legally constitute an ethics violation but added
that candidates could recuse themselves from the vote to avoid the public perception of such.

Council Member Worthen questioned whether the room size was appropriate for such an event. She
questioned how the public would perceive the Council waiving the fee for use of the room for a Meet
the Candidates event with three of the current Council Members running for office. She suggested
ChamberWest should pay the fee or hold the event somewhere else.

Council Member Pack commented that the same issue would come up in another two years, with
four Council seats up for election. He asked what had been done in the past and suggested having a
standing policy. Council Member McConnehey briefly explained what had been done historically.
Mr. Anderson commented that Meet the Candidates events were held in city facilities in other cities.

Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 7:56 pm

Citizen Comments:
None

Council Chair Jacob closed the public hearing at 7:57 pm

Council Member McConnehey said he would prefer a venue other than City Hall but added that City
Hall was a public place. He said he did not believe any impropriety would be involved if incumbents
voted in favor of the issue and suggested the appearance of trying to prevent the event would look
worse.

Council Vice Chair Green said a Meet the Candidates event was a community event of community
interest. He stated ChamberWest asked to use the room, and he did not believe it was the Council’s
call to tell them to go somewhere else. He pointed out that the Cultural Society of West Jordan used
the room regularly without a fee waiver. He stated similar non-profits should be treated equally.
Council Vice Chair Green expressed the opinion that if the Council denied the requested fee waiver,
they would be in dangerous territory.

Council Member Whitelock suggested Council Chambers would be a better venue. Council Member
McConnehey responded that incumbents would have a “home court advantage” in Council
Chambers.

Responding to a question from Council Member Worthen, Mr. Anderson stated fees were waived on
a case-by-case basis, with applications judged on their own merit. Council Member Worthen said
she would not want to show favoritism and the appearance of home court advantage. She expressed
concern with the event taking place at City Hall.

Council Member Lamb pointed out the issue had not been put forth by candidate Council Members
Lamb, Whitelock or Green. He stated the request came from ChamberWest and would benefit all
candidates. Council Vice Chair Green said if City Hall was an inappropriate venue for the event, the
planned arts center should be built to house such events. He emphasized that West Jordan City did
not have a community center for community meetings. Mr. Anderson said he did not know if
ChamberWest had considered a different venue.

Council Member McConnehey emphasized the question was whether or not to waive fees for the
use of the room. He said he did not believe approving the fee waiver would set a precedent.
Responding to a question from Council Member Worthen, Mr. Lee commented that not all groups
that used City facilities had contracts with the City. He suggested the contract with ChamberWest
could be amended in the future in a way that would meet the intent of City Code and eliminate the
need for a public hearing in the future.

MOTION:       Council Chair Jacob moved to approve Resolution No. 21-044 providing a fee
              waiver to ChamberWest for the rental of the Community Room.
              Council Member Pack seconded the motion.

Council Member Whitelock said she had voted against fee waivers in the past, with one exception.
She said she would vote against the requested fee waiver because she had a hard time saying yes to
one group and no to others. She stated she was not against the specific event.

 8.    BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Council Advice and Consent Amendments
At a work session on May 12, 2021, the Council requested staff prepare language to add Council
advice and consent for appointments to the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy Board and the South
Salt Lake Valley Mosquito Abatement District and requested terms of service for those
appointments. Council Office Director Alan Anderson oriented the Council with current City Code
regarding Council advice and consent to appointments, and recommended terms of service follow
agency by-laws. If terms of service were not addressed in by-laws, he recommended three-year
terms consistent with current committee terms. Each term would go through the advice and consent
process. Staff answered questions from the Council.

Assistant City Attorney Duncan Murray suggested language regarding multijurisdictional boards.
Council Vice Chair Green said he still did not like the definition of “multijurisdictional boards”, and
said he was confused by circling references. Mr. Murray explained the intent of proposed language.
Council Vice Chair Green said he believed the Code needed to be simpler.

MOTION:       Council Member McConnehey moved to direct Council Leadership to work with

              consent to certain Mayoral appointments.
