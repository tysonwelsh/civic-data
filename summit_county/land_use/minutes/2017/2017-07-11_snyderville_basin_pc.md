2. Discussion and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit for a proposed 
petrified wood park in Silver Creek Village Center Subdivision; SCVC-4; Matt 
Lowe, applicant– Jennifer Strader, Senior Planner  
 
Planner Strader reminded the Commission the Development Agreement (DA) was 
recorded in 2015.  The petrified wood park is one of the community benefits proposed 
by the developer in exchange for their density.  They are allowed 1,290 units and 
50,000 square feet of commercial.   
 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
July 11, 2017 
Page 2 of 34 
 
Planner Strader said the plans included in the packet include a neighborhood garden, 
which is an allowed use.  The park requires a conditional use permit.  At this time, the 
Commission is looking at the petrified wood park only.   
 
The park is proposed to be located on Lot 3 of the subdivision.  The lot was highlighted 
in yellow on the site plan.  It is a small parcel of 0.11 acres.  Petrified wood has been 
found on the property in the past.  The developer is required to ensure that the 
contractors are aware that if they encounter petrified wood it will be preserved.   
 
The DA identifies the items to be included in the park, which includes interpretive 
signage (that describes the petrified wood), benches, shade trees, a crushed stone or 
concrete pathway, along with trash and recycling containers.  All of these items have 
been included in the plan.   
 
Planner Strader displayed the site plan.  The red rectangles indicate the location of the 
benches within the park.  A yellow line shows the boundary of the neighborhood garden 
and the brown squares are the signage panels.  Staff finds the applicant has met the 
requirements of the DA and recommends the Commission vote to approve the 
Conditional Use Permit.      
 
The applicant’s team consists of Matt Lowe, Wade Budge, and the architect, Eric 
Langvardt.  Mr. Langvardt said the panels are in the process of being fabricated.  It is a 
way to tell the story of the petrified wood from this area.   
 
Mr. Lowe said the contractor’s know if any petrified wood is found, the developers are 
to be notified immediately.  This has been placed in their contract.  He added they have 
put in a lot of deep sewer lines.  Up to this point, petrified wood has not been found.  Mr. 
Budge said they believe more of it may be found in future phases of the development.

the Community garden, if they are large enough they won’t wander.

A vicinity map showed the location of the property.  The entire property contains

to be included in the park.  It requires the park to be built in two phases.  Tonight, they

Phase I is to include a multi-purpose play lawn, a multi-purpose play area, a shade 
pavilion, trash and recycling, a dog waste station, and internal trails.  The parking lot 
shall have a minimum of 20 stalls.  All of these elements have been included.  It also 
includes a bike station which will be utilized when the County’s bike program comes 
online.   
 
Planner Strader said there are two other items that were to be provided with Phase I.  
The first item is to rehabilitate abandoned waste water ponds.  These are located within

The second item to be completed during Phase I is the establishment of a temporary

identifies the community benefits.  At the end of the chart is this statement:   
“The foregoing benefits and amenities identify the basic level of amenities that 
shall be planned and built.  Additional amenities may be added if deemed 
appropriate by the developer and approved by the County.”     
 
Planner Strader said that due to the size of the park and the activities there, Staff is of 
the opinion that a restroom is not an amenity but a necessary facility in order to ensure 
the health and welfare of the citizens.  A condition of approval has been recommended 
that a restroom facility be added.  The location and the structure could be reviewed by

APPLICANT COMMENTS 
Mr. Budge said they agree with everything contained within the Staff Report except 
they have an issue with constructing the bathroom at this time.  He said because this is 
a private park, it will be maintained by the HOA.  Residents are needed to have the 
funds needed to maintain the facility.  A bathroom has to be cleaned and secured every 
day.  They think the better time to have this discussion is when they come before the 
Commission seeking Phase II approval.   
 
Mr. Budge said that Phase II will expand the park and add the civic uses that will be

residents that would benefit from having a bathroom in Phase I.     
 
Mr. Budge said when the Development Agreement was crafted, they were very careful 
about what this park would look like.  At no point, did they decide a bathroom would be 
imposed at Phase I.  He recommended the reference to the bathroom in Condition 1 be 
removed.  They hope to convince the Commission that Phase II is the better time to 
have that discussion. 
 
Mr. Langvardt said these parks are designed to walk there and walk home.  They are not 
programmed to host things like soccer games.  He said that ¾ of the park will be open 
space with a trail going through it.  A bathroom presents security issues.

6. Public Hearing and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit for 
commercial horse boarding in the Rural Residential Zone; 4970 North 400 West; 
PP-87-C-1-A; Verna Nielsen, applicant– Ray Milliner, Principal Planner  
 
Planner Milliner said the applicant is requesting a conditional use for commercial horse 
boarding located at 4970 North and 400 West off Old Ranch Road.  An aerial map was 
used to show the location of the property.  The applicant, Verna Nielsen, was 
introduced.   
 
Planner Milliner said there are 22 stalls existing on the property.  They are spread out 
over a number of buildings.  The applicant has the capacity for 25 horses.  Twenty-one 
horses are regular sized; four are mini horses, which live in one larger stall.     
 
Planner Milliner said the request is board 20 horses.  At this time, there are 18 stalls 
available for horses.  On the site, there is a family home, a riding arena, three barns, two 
tack rooms, and paddock areas.  The applicant has four horses.      
 
Planner Milliner said the applicant built the barns and was operating a horse boarding 
business for a number of years before the County knew about it.  The applicant was 
informed they were boarding horses without the proper permits.  A conditional use 
permit and a business license are needed.  Promptly being informed she was in 
violation, the applicant came in and submitted applications.   
 
Planner Milliner said the review considered if the applicant can take care of the horses 
and the associated issues, like manure.  The applicant has submitted a mitigation plan 
to take care of the flies and the odor.  The Health Department asked that a condition of 
approval be added stating if a problem occurs they have the right to have inspections.    
 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission

Planner Milliner said Staff found the application complies with the minimum criteria.

APPLICANT COMMENTS 
The applicant, Verna Nielsen, said they have lived on 400 West for 24 years.  They 
started to board horses in the last five years.  She said her husband retired a few years 
ago and she retired from the Park City School District in January.  They found the need 
to supplement their social security payments.  That is when they started boarding 
horses.  The horses belong to her friends.  She has no intention of starting a large 
commercial boarding business.  They do not have riding lessons, clinics, or horse shows.  
 
Ms. Nielsen said this is for a small group of ladies that want to ride their horses on Park
