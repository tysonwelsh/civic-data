 The Taylorsville Youth Council had recently spent a day on capitol hill and among other things,
 met with Senators Wayne Harper and Karen Kwan. They held a debrief earlier today to discuss
 what they had learned.


Mayor Overson had attended ChamberWest' s annual breakfast meeting the previous week when
the 2025 officers and board members were presented. Council Member Barbieri was one of those
board members and attended the breakfast as well. It was exciting to hear the direction the
organization was taking.


A selection committee had met Friday to finalize their choice of artists to display art on the glass
panels along the MidValley Express route. The selected artists had been assigned specific stations
and would soon be signing contracts. She was excited to see the work added to Taylorsville' s
stations.




A coalition of home schoolers in the community had reached out to learn more about the mayor' s
responsibilities. A group of kids ranging from about eight to fourteen years of age met with her at
city hall to learn about ways the city affected their lives. She said the kids had many great questions
and innovative ideas.


The Mayor listened while Salt Lake County Mayor Jenny Wilson presented her State ofthe County
report at the Clark Planetarium the previous evening. Chair Harker and Council Members Burgess
and Knudsen were in attendance as well.


Earlier in the week she had met with staff and Ken Donarski regarding funding recommendations
for CDBG funds that Taylorsville         would hopefully be receiving.   The proposed budget for these
funds would be presented to the City Council in March.

Mayor Overson and Jen Andrus attended an art exhibit at Intermountain Health, who happened to
be a legacy donor to the City' s Plaza +
                                        Art program. The artist was a cardio patient who told a
story through her art about how it helped her cope with her medical situation. The Mayor was
moved by the pieces and greatly appreciated IHC' s willingness to support the City' s art program.

John Hiskey was in the throes of the legislative session and would have an update for the City
Council in the near future. She greatly appreciated his tireless efforts as well as those of
Taylorsville'       s legislators.

3.         REPORTS


           3. 1    Unified Fire     Authority Report— Captain Richard Rich

 la UFA Captain Richard Rich reported on Quarter No. 4 of 2024 ( October through December
2024), explaining there     were     1, 285 incidents in Taylorsville,   487 of which ( 38%) were classified

as emergent. He also compared Q4 data for the last four years before addressing the top five fire
and EMS calls during the previous quarter.

Captain Rich then moved on to reviewing response times for various levels/ priorities of calls.
Earlier in the month, fifteen probationary firefighters had graduated from Camp 58, while the
Camp 59 class of 36 new recruits had graduated two days earlier.
 The Captain then provided information regarding the recent EMAC ( Emergency Management
 Assistance Compact) deployment of personnel to help with the fires in Los Angeles. The staff and
 equipment deployed were:
            65 personnel ( 8 from Unified Fire)
            17 Departments
            15 Engines ( 1 from Unified Fire)
            4 Support Trucks ( 2 from Unified Fire)
             1 Mechanic with Truck


 Captain Rich concluded his report by presenting a variety of safety tips.
