2.    ADOPT (OR DENY) AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND THE UTAH COUNTY GENERAL PLAN, 2014
      AND ADOPT SECTION 5-18 OF THE UTAH COUNTY LAND USE ORDINANCE TO ADOPT
      GRAZING (G-1) ZONE

(Track A, 05:15)
Bryce Armstrong of the Utah County Community Development Office summarized there had been a request by a
citizen’s group last year to prevent any new gravel or mining operations by re-zoning a Mining and Grazing area to
the CE-2, or Critical Environment, zone. The Utah County Planning Commission heard the proposal and
recommended disapproval; the County Commission agreed and requested recommendations to re-zone private and
public properties in West Mountain. Mr. Armstrong’s staff has been developing a new G-1 Zone specifically for
grazing. This new G-1 Zone follows the residential densities and uses format of the existing mining and grazing zone,
but specifically eliminates gravel operations, mining operations, batch plants, crushers, and associated or other uses
deemed unnecessary for a grazing-specific zone. The Utah County Planning Commission reviewed the G-1 Zone
ordinance in November 2017 and has recommended approval to the Board of Commissioners.

Commissioner Lee asked how this new ordinance might impact east county mountain areas. Bryce Armstrong
explained there are plans to update the Utah County General Plan to include a new G-1 Zone as part of the land use
element within the agricultural watershed element, similar to the mining and grazing Area 5 Zone. This ordinance is
only to create the zone – any application of said zone would go through the normal public hearing process and would
be voted upon for specific areas. Mr. Armstrong mentioned current land use would be allowed to continue according
to county ordinance provisions for the continuation of non-conforming uses. He used watershed areas as an example,
pointing out many of those mountainous areas are part of CE-1 or MG zones. Those underlying properties would be
able to continue whatever permitted uses they currently have, but any new uses in those areas would be held to the
parameters of the new G-1 Zone as applied at that time.

(Track A, 10:30)
Commissioner Lee mentioned the federal government and asked about potential conflict for additional logging and
timber industry in forest lands. Bryce Armstrong talked about exemptions from county land use ordinances related to
federal operations on federal lands. He confirmed some property owners have requested exclusion from the G-1 Zone,
including SITLA (Utah School and Institutional Trust Lands Administration) and the BLM (Bureau of Land
Management). They discussed the Resource Management Plan. Commissioner Ivie clarified those two institutions
are objecting to the area in which the G-1 Zone is being applied, and not necessarily to the creation of the zone.
Commissioner Ivie commented on Utah County’s unique position: leading the state in agriculture receipt production,
considered top in the nation for apricot and tart cherry production, and exporting protein. He stated this ordinance
will help to preserve agricultural heritage and the capacity to produce food on both local and national levels.
Commissioner Lee shared his thoughts on the importance of appropriate grazing being beneficial for land.
Recognizing the large audience presence, Commissioner Ivie opened the floor to public comment on this item.

(Track A, 18:30)
Provo resident Robert Stevens pointed out ordinances such as this have historically been created to make illegal
activities legal. Combined with the following Regular Agenda Item No. 3 on the calendar today, the purpose would
be using the government to create monopolies because there are excavation and mining installations already on the
land which would be allowed to continue while prohibiting new operations. Mr. Stevens mentioned the variables in
finding people to graze their livestock on his 70 acres along 7300 South, and the varying quality of dirt on West
Mountain. He strongly opposes this ordinance.

(Track A, 21:10)
Shauna Phelps said she is in favor of this proposal, stating it would be ridiculous not to adopt this ordinance based on
claims of creating monopolies as new mines can always find other locations. Ms. Phelps noted it could be argued
there is currently a monopoly in favor of gravel pits which have intrusive effects on the well-being of West Mountain
residents and farms.

(Track A, 22:30)
Julie Blainey has a large farm on West Mountain. She spoke of the beauty of West Mountain, and how the expansion
of the mining industry is unbearable for residents who have invested their wealth and livelihood there. Ms. Blainey
supports the zoning change for grazing as it will preserve values and important artifacts whereas the trucks, pollution,
and dust from mining adversely causes dying trees and failing crops.

Voice member Julie Sainsbury supports the creation of this new zone. She talked about the importance of a working

Commissioner Lee talked about the need to use reasonable approaches and maintain balance to avoid ‘pendulum
swings’ from one extreme to another.

4.     ADOPT (OR DENY) AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND THE UTAH COUNTY LAND USE
       ORDINANCE, SECTION 5-12, RELATIVE TO THE APPLICABILITY OF THE REQUIREMENTS
       OF THE NATURAL HAZARDS OVERLAY ZONE, AS WELL AS AN AMENDMENT TO THE
       DEFINITION OF CRITICAL FACILITIES FOUND IN SUBSECTION 2-2-A OF THE UTAH
       COUNTY LAND USE ORDINANCE
(Track A, 1:11:15)
Brandon Larsen of the Utah County Community Development Office mentioned an inquiry about potential
development in Thistle has led staff to review hazard zones. He talked about mapping to identify areas within Natural
Hazards Overlay (NHO) Zones which require hazards assessments for certain uses; those assessments may in turn
require mitigations put in place, but do not restrict development. The proposed ordinance is a general ‘clean up’ to
update language and maps, including landslide maps. It will also require large-scale developments to have natural
hazards assessments except for specific circumstances, such as labelling any portions of developments within NHO
Zones as unbuildable for critical facilities and occupied structures. Mr. Larsen mentioned the County Engineer would
also be allowed to determine whether the expansion of a certain land use or structure could be exempted from the
assessment requirement. He reviewed the definitions for critical structures.

Commissioner Lee expressed concern about being overly assertive and using ‘broad strokes’ to define Critical
Environment (CE-1) Zones across the mountains. He asked how that could be prevented in specifying Natural Hazards
Overlay Zones to allow a common-sense approach. Brandon Larsen replied this ordinance will update maps, but there
is still a public process for including any land in NHO Zones. Commissioner Ivie talked about prime productive
farmland being accidentally designated as hazardous due to proximity near environmentally-contaminated area. Mr.
Larsen explained notes are added to plats to declare potential issues and show assessments; some of those assessments
result in declarations showing there were no problems on the property. Applicants can also dispute the inclusion of
their land into NHO Zones.

(Track A, 1:23:35)
Bryce Armstrong of the Utah County Community Development Office mentioned the county used to employ a full-
time Geologist who was instrumental in creating the existing Natural Hazards Overlay Zone. Barring funding for that
position to return, the best practice is to use available reliable information to determine the areas and follow that with
site-specific studies to identify mitigations as needed. Mr. Armstrong and Brandon Larsen reviewed portions of the
map with Commissioner Lee in relation to landslide risks. Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore clarified
the county has a Geo-Tech Engineer on staff via contract; their recommendations have been incorporated into this
proposal. They continued discussing risks, and Mr. Armstrong commended Mr. Larsen for his work to address
deficiencies in the current application process.

(Track A, 1:40:10)
Referring to the discussion for Regular Agenda Item No. 2 on today’s calendar, West Mountain resident Julie
Sainsbury returned for additional comment. Ms. Sainsbury mentioned the alternative language for the grazing zone
which would allow mining as a conditional use; she is against that proposal. South Utah County Community Voice
already considered classifying West Mountain as CE-1 Zone but decided against it specifically because mining is a
conditional use within that zone. Ms. Sainsbury explained the uniqueness of West Mountain already servicing seven
mines with the current road infrastructure can only be addressed with a unique grazing zone. She reminded
Commissioner Lee of his comments on ‘broad strokes’ within Natural Hazards Overlay Zone and pointed out the
similarities for mining in West Mountain. Ms. Sainsbury stated inserting language to allow mining as a conditional
use in the new grazing zone entirely defeats the purpose of creating said zone.

(Track A, 1:43:40)
Provo resident Robert Stevens mentioned all of Utah County is in a hazardous zone in the case of an earthquake with
7.5 magnitude or higher; most properties along the Wasatch Mountain were built on slippery shale of varying depths.
Mr. Stevens explained the Board has just set a precedent with potential repercussions against landowners. He also
pointed out a pending application for three half-acre lots to have waste-water systems. Mr. Stevens stated if that
application is approved, the Utah County Health Department will be establishing it is acceptable to have treatment
systems on half-acre lots; why, then, is there zoning for 5.25-acre lots? If the goal is urban expansion, clusters of half-
acre lots with appropriate waste-water treatment systems for the soil would make more sense.
