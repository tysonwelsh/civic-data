2.       Review Agenda


Chair Harker briefly reviewed the agenda for the regular City Council meeting.

Although the meeting of the Redevelopment Board had been intended to take place after the
regular City Council meeting, it was decided that it would instead be held immediately prior to it.

Chair Harker reported that the Parks and Recreation Committee was interested in planting a tree
somewhere at city hall and decorating it each year for Christmas. The committee preferred this to
getting a new tree each year and re- planting it in a park. Mayor Overson agreed this was a good
idea, although it would require serious thought to find an appropriate        location that would
accommodate future growth as well as annual tree lighting ceremonies and visitors.

The Parks and Recreation Committee was also working on getting Taylorsville recognized as a
Healthy City. They would soon be asking the Mayor to write a letter of support.

2.       Adjourn



The briefing session concluded at 6: 06 p. m., after which a meeting of the Redevelopment Agency
Board took place.

sang two musical numbers.

         1. 2     Mayor' s Report



     Mayor Kristie Overson reported that a ribbon cutting had been held at El Delicioso which
was now open just north of Harmon' s. It was a family- owned ice cream and deli business. West
Valley Carnitas was the site of another recent ribbon- cutting and the owners were very
complimentary of city staff who assisted them in opening their business.

Target and TVPD recently took part in the " Heroes and Helpers" program, assisting five families
and ten children, providing a nice Christmas for them. For the Harmons Giving Tree, the
Taylorsville community collected 21 gift cards, 46 " Santa Sacks" and more than 30 outfits which
were donated to needy students at Taylorsville High School. The Mayor greatly appreciated
Harmons for their community involvement.

The Saturday with Santa event was a success as always. The weather was beautiful and there was
a great turnout. She wished to thank everyone involved in pulling off the fun event.

The Wreaths Across America event also went very well. It was a somber, moving time with a full
house here at city hall before      the   smooth transition   over   the cemetery.   She   appreciated   Kris
Taylorsville City Council Minutes

Heineman, the Youth Council, Suzie Smith, and Joey Bona and his staff for all the hard work they
put in preparing for this event.

She and several council members spoke to students in an Eisenhower Jr. High School government
class. They enjoyed talking to the youth about the important part that local government played in
their lives, even though they might not realize it.

Mayor Overson attended a press conference at which a check for$ 62. 8 million was presented by
the federal government to the Utah Transit Authority for the Bus Rapid Transit project now known
as the MidValley Express. The project was slowly taking shape and would make a positive
difference in Taylorsville and other nearby communities. She pointed out that it would make access
to Olympic events at the Maverik Center very easy when the 2034 Olympics rolled around.

City Administration had met the day before with congressional leaders to discuss the upcoming
legislative session and share expectations. It was very valuable to sit down with them and share
priorities, and she appreciated that they took the time to meet with Taylorsville' s administration.

Taylorsville had recently been named the most generous city in Utah and the 17th most generous
in the country. This was a great accomplishment, and it was the residents who made the headline
possible.


The Mayor had attended the Salt Lake County inauguration earlier in the week. She enjoyed being
able to congratulate the newly elected and re- elected county officials.

Thursday, January 16th was Religious Freedom Day. Governor Spencer Cox had issued a
proclamation in 2024 regarding the day and the City Council had approved a similar resolution as
well. This year the city was reaffirming the resolution in alignment with the Governor. She
welcomed Mr. Grant Howarth for championing this cause.

          1. 3    Citizen Comments


Chair Harker invited any members of the audience who wished to address the City Council to step
forward and do so.


Cheniel Hill spoke regarding what she described as a growing crisis on Atherton Drive from 1300
West to 4200 South, particularly during the hours of 7am and 9am, 3pm to 7pm, and 9pm to 1 am.
She expressed concern about two pursuits, traffic accidents, speeding, racing, etc. She asked for
6- 8 speed bumps, improved sidewalks and crosswalks, and enhanced lighting near schools and
daycares.


Grant Howarth expressed his appreciation to Mayor Overson and the City Council for approving
Resolution No. 25- 01, explaining that Taylorsville was the third city to do so.

There was no one else who expressed a desire to speak, so Chair Harker closed the citizen comment
period.

5.       FINANCIAL        MATTERS



         6. 1     Fraud Risk Report— Scott Harrington


 E      Asst. City Administrator/ Chief Financial Officer Scott Harrington briefly reviewed the
recently completed Fraud Risk Report. With a score of 370 out of 395, the city' s risk of fraud was
classified as" very low." He explained that the score went down slightly because the value of some

areas had been reduced. Two areas that the City now got credit for were having written policies
