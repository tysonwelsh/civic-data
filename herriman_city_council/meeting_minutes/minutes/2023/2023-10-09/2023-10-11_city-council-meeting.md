          2.2. Discussion pertaining to a requested Religious Freedom Day Proclamation – City
               Council Roundtable
      City Manager Cherpeski stated the City had not done proclamations in four or five years, and
      said this had been added to the agenda at Councilmember Shield’s request. He asked for
      direction about how the Council would like to pursue the proclamation. Councilmember
      Shields spoke about his thoughts on proclamations, and said generally, he considered them
      to be political grandstanding. However, he felt since religious freedom was something
      expressly written in the Constitution and many of their sister cities had adopted similar
      stances, he felt this would be appropriate. He expressed this was a core right of American
      citizens and as it was something they had all sworn to uphold, he thought this was something
      they should consider. He thought they should have a discussion as a Council about how to
      navigate implementation.

      Councilmember Ohrn expressed her strong belief in freedom of religion. However, she asked
      what more this proclamation did since she had already sworn to uphold the Constitution of
      the United States when she took the Oath of Office when elected to represent Herriman as a
      member of Council. She felt it did not do anything additional. Councilmember Shields agreed
      it did not do anything tangible since it was not binding. Councilmember Shields explained
      this was not anything more than the Oath of Office and felt it was mostly symbolic, although
      he felt it was the right kind of thing to symbolize. Councilmember Hodges agreed with
      Councilmember Shields’ views and said it would not change how they governed or interacted
      with the City daily. Councilmember Hodges said he did not see the point in doing something
      like this other than giving themselves a pat on the back. Councilmember Shields expressed
      this was purely ceremonial and was designed to give a moment of focus to the idea of religious
      freedom. Councilmember Shields discussed they had received requests for proclamations
      over things the City had no control over, although it was within their purview to make the
      Herriman a religiously tolerant town since it was written in the Constitution.

      Mayor Palmer stated he believed in the freedom of religion, although he would not like to
      move forward with a proclamation. He noted the large volume of requests for proclamations
      received, and worried it would set a precedent if they went ahead with one. He thought it
      would be easier if their policy was not to issue any proclamations, although he acknowledged
      Councilmember Shields’ comments that this was a straightforward thing to align with.
      Councilmember Ohrn agreed with the Mayor’s comments, and added they should try to
      incorporate religious freedom into their conversations every day rather than taking one day
      out of the year to highlight its significance. Councilmember Henderson echoed these
      sentiments. The Council also addressed concerns that although these seemed to be a
      straightforward concept, it had the risk of becoming politicized.
October 11, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                              Page 5 of 21


      Councilmember Henderson summarized that all proclamations had a political undertone, and
      said the Council and Mayor received a large number of requests for proclamations and they
      did not want to influence voters with adopting a City-wide proclamation. He discussed it was
      different to make a stance as an individual rather than as a City, and said he was happy to
      give out his own positions on issues, although it gave him pause to speak on behalf of the
      entire City. He then discussed how although they had sworn to uphold the Constitution, it
      was a living document and was changing. He spoke about how important religious freedom
      was to him, and expressed gratitude that he was able to freely speak about his beliefs.

          2.3. Blackridge Reservoir Discussion – Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager
      Assistant City Manager Thomas reported she had done some more investigation into this
      topic and had found more alternatives. She recommended the following options: one, they
      lose access to water. Alternatively, people could pay for a parking pass that would cost $15
      per day regardless of how long they were parked there. She explained it would be a cost
      share, split 70/30, and was estimated to bring in $47,000 during the summer season. She
      said if they went with this option, it might push people out further to where they could park
      for free and then hike in. Lastly, she said there was an option to expand the permit program.
      Councilmember Henderson cautioned it would be challenging to enforce it if they only
      offered permits to Herriman residents. Councilmember Shields discussed how it was
      impossible to find a solution which would be amenable to everyone, and no matter what they
      did someone would be frustrated by their decision. Councilmember Ohrn thought if they
      went with the parking pass option, they needed to increase the citation cost to get people to
      take it seriously. The Council ensued discussion and agreed to start charging for parking to
      help eliminate the issues at the amenity.

      Councilmember Shields suggested including a sign which listed everything they had found in
      the reservoir including toxic algae, leeches, and snails and cautioned people to swim at their
      own risk. The group concurred. Councilmember Ohrn asked for specifics about how the
      parking pass option would play out, and Councilmember Shields noted there was not an
      option they could pick which had no collateral damage. Councilmember Henderson worried
      that if they went with the parking pass, people would simply park farther away, and this
      would just expand the issues.

      Assistant City Manager Thomas thought it could work out since there tended to be two waves
      of people; an early morning crowd which was gone by mid-day, and then another group who
      showed up in the later afternoon. Councilmember Hodges opined they hire an independent
      third party to develop their parking system, and pointed out there were options like apps
      which told people in real time how many parking stalls were available so they could know if
      the reservoir was full before they left their house. Councilmember Henderson also thought
      the parking pass option was best, since that way only residents who used the reservoir were

          9.2. Presentation and consideration of a proposed public/private open fiber utility
               partnership – Councilmember Steven Shields
      Councilmember Shields recalled in March of 2020, they had initiated a taskforce to explore
      options for installing fiber utility cables throughout the City. He offered a succinct
      background of the reasons for beginning this process and noted they had largely been
      influenced by COVID-19 as the need for high-speed internet became crucial for consistent
      connection to classes and remote work. He spoke about how the strain on the internet
      infrastructure had been debilitating for residents when they were unable to get connected to
      school or work. He said the taskforce comprised of himself, Councilmember Ohrn, and
      members of staff, and explained the group had begun by defining what the main issues in the
      City were, and what the main goals for the City were. He identified some of those goals as
      being to supply every home and business in the City with reliable internet, remove
      government’s influence from picking winners and losers, increase competition and offer
      multiple internet providers, lower costs, add broadband infrastructure, and minimize or
      eliminate entirely any risk to the City and taxpayers. Councilmember Shields elaborated on
      what he meant by “risk” and clarified they wanted to find a solution that people were not
      forced to accept, but a solution which offered different options for people and allowed them
      to choose what was best.

      Councilmember Shields then presented a timeline of their work so far and noted they had
      held many meetings within the last three years in which they had considered a wide array of
