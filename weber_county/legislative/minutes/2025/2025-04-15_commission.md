            Hartman, Attorney Nicholas Caine, Attorney Derek Malan, and Victim Advocate Jamie Pitt.
            On March 24, 2025, at approximately 2:45 PM, the Riverdale Police Department and Riverdale Fire Department were
            dispatched to 5100 South 1050 West in response to reports of a structure fire. Upon arrival, officers observed smoke
            coming from a trailer. They gained entry and discovered an unresponsive woman inside. Officers carried the woman to

            children were safely located, and the suspect was taken into custody.
            Investigators Steve Haney, Steve Zaccardi and Cameron Hartman with the Weber County Attorney’s Office responded
            to the scene and provided critical support throughout the investigation. Their experience and expertise were
            instrumental in advancing the case and ultimately bringing the suspect to justice. Both investigators worked seamlessly
            alongside the Riverdale Police Department, ensuring a smooth and effective investigative process.
            Weber County Attorneys Nicholas Caine and Derek Malan, along with Victim Advocate Jamie Pitt, also responded to
            the scene. Their early involvement and ongoing commitment to the case will be invaluable as the investigation
            concludes and prosecution proceeds.
            All members of the Weber County Attorney’s Office demonstrated exceptional professionalism and dedication throughout
            the investigation. Their contributions were vital in supporting the Riverdale Police Department and securing the arrest
            and charging of the suspect with Aggravated Murder.

F. PRESENTATIONS:
   1. Presentation of a Proclamation declaring April 21-28, 2025 as International Dark-sky Week in Weber
      County.
      Commissioner Harvey: I want to give special recognition to Sara Toliver from Visit Ogden who helps keep
      us focused. I also want to thank Ron Gleason and Zach Thomas, as well as Marty Smith and Todd Ferrario
      from our Culture, Parks, and Recreation department who help us comply with this Dark Sky rating. The dark
      skies across the County are very important and we have an area east of our mountains that we make sure to
      preserve that Dark Sky class rating. The Proclamation today is about maintaining our important Dark Skies
      accreditation. The Proclamation reads as follows:


                          WHEREAS, the aesthetic beauty and wonder of a natural night sky is a shared heritage of all
                          humankind; and
                  WHEREAS, the experience of standing under a starry night sky inspires feelings of wonder and awe,
                  encouraging a growing interest in science and nature, particularly among young people and visitors
                  from outside the local communities.; and
                  WHEREAS, light pollution has scientifically established economic and environmental consequences,
                  which result in significant impacts on the ecology and human health of all communities; and 80
                  percent of the world’s population, including most people in Weber County, lives under a dome of
                  light pollution and may never experience the visual wonder of living under a dark sky; and
                  WHEREAS, light pollution represents a waste of natural resources amounting to roughly $3 billion
                  per year of wasted energy in the United States and contributes to diminished energy security; and
                  WHEREAS, Weber County is home to North Fork Park, the 21st International Dark Sky Park. North
                  Fork Park is a destination for people who seek out the best, darkest skies. Astronomers and local
                  organizations host star parties, eclipse viewing, and other dark sky events that draw locals and
                  tourists to North Fork Park, and
                  WHEREAS, astrotourism not only provides an awe-inspiring experience but also brings significant
                  economic benefits to Weber County. By attracting stargazers and astronomy enthusiasts, dark skies
                  in Weber County boost local tourism, encourage longer stays, and increase spending in communities
                  near North Fork Park. Additionally, astrotourism promotes environmental awareness and
                  conservation efforts, ensuring that future generations can continue to enjoy the beauty of the night
                  sky, and
                  WHEREAS, North Fork Park is home to dozens of nocturnal wildlife species—including several
                  species of bats, owls, and rodents such as mice and voles —and these species rely on undisturbed
                  night environments to hunt, mate, and thrive; and
                  WHEREAS, DarkSky International, the globally recognized authority on light pollution, recognizes
                  International Dark-Sky Week to raise awareness of the effects of light pollution, provide free
                  education, resources, and solutions to the public, and encourage the protection of and enjoyment of
                  dark skies and responsible outdoor lighting.
                  Now, Therefore, The Weber County Commissioners, do hereby declare April 21 – 28, 2025 as
                  INTERNATIONAL DARK-SKY WEEK in Weber County, Utah and ask each resident to join us not
                  only in observing and pondering upon this important week but also in raising awareness and support
                  for protecting our precious dark sky as a resource.
                  Dated this 15th day of April 2025
         Mr. Zach Thomas: I thank you for your support of Dark Skies over the years, Commissioners. This year,
         2025, is the 10th anniversary of North Fork Park becoming an International Dark Sky park. Weber County is
         joining hundreds of cities, counties, and states across the country in declaring this upcoming week as
         International Dark Sky Week. It’s an important cause form the environmental and health concerns. It’s
         always said that light pollution is the easiest form of pollution to solve because we can flip a switch or put a
         shield on our lights. I want to thank Todd Ferrario and the whole Weber County Parks crew; they always ask
         what they can do to help in the Dark Sky effort.
