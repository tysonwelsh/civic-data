                      Council member Choberka disclosed she has personal connections to this project and will be recusing herself from
             discussion and/or action regarding these items.
                      A memo from the City Comptroller regarding the Arts Plaza came before the Council for consideration. The memo
             stated Administration is proposing a Fiscal Year (FY) 21 budget amendment to appropriate $4,250,000 from grant funds received
             from Recreation, Arts, Museum, Parks (RAMP) and the Dumke Foundation, and Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease Revenue
             fund balance to fund construction of the Arts Plaza.
                      An additional memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council for
             consideration. The memo stated the Council will consider a sponsorship naming agreement with the Dr. Ezekiel R. and Edna
             Wattis Dumke Foundation. Under the terms of the agreement, the Foundation will provide $2.2M in funding to construct and
             develop an Arts Plaza, make improvements to Ogden A venue, and install an art sculpture on the site. In return, the City will grant
             naming rights for the Arts Plaza to the Foundation for a term of75 years or until such time as the site is no longer used for civic
             purposes.
                      Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and stated the budget amendment is intended to fund Capital Improvement
             Plan (CIP) project CDOO 1, Ogden A venue Plaza; the proposed project will transform the site of the former Courtyard Inn into a
             public plaza that will help activate 25th Street, create a stronger connection between the Central Business District and East Central
             neighborhoods, and catalyze revitalization in the Nine Rails Creative District. The Courtyard Inn was in dilapidated condition and
             contributed to blight, vandalism, and other crime in the area. Through previously funded CIP projects, the city has acquired the

           Business Development Manager Meess then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the proposal to enter a
naming agreement for the arts plaza project; design principals for the arts plaza are intended to reflect the community, celebrate
Ogden's natural environment, broadcast ideas, and evolve with Ogden. She presented a rendering for the project and highlighted
the improvements to be completed on Ogden A venue and 25th Street; programming at the plaza will be completed by those in the
public and private sector. Weber State University (WSU) will complete two to three major installations each year and community
program will include arts focused events and activities, such as small concerts, theater, dance, and video art. She reviewed the
anticipated project costs, which total $8,322,580, as well as the current project funding plan; the City's share of the project cost is
3 7.5 percent, or $3,122,5 80. This evening, Administration proposes the Council approve the sponsorship naming agreement via
adoption of Resolution 2021-2; this will establish the plaza name in recognition of the Dr. Ezekiel R. and Edna Wattis Dumke
Foundation contribution. Naming rights would be intact for 75 years or until the plaza is no longer used as a public civic space.
The City will commit to use the plaza name on signate and marketing materials. Construction is expected to be completed by
October 15,2021.
           Vice Chair White inquired as to the amount of money that will be needed to pay for ongoing annual maintenance of the
plaza. She also asked if the tax increment from the Adams A venue Urban Renewal Area (URA) will be sufficient to cover those
ongoing maintenance costs. Ms. Buckley stated the Adams URA is already generating tax increment fmancing (TIF) and some of
that money is unallocated and can be used for ongoing maintenance. She believes it will be sufficient to cover ongoing
maintenance and the City plans to contract maintenance out to a third party that specializes in maintenance of this type of facility.
Vice Chair White asked if the City would need to enter into an agreement with the RDA to secure access to TIF funding for this
project. Ms. Stout stated that the RDA Board would be asked to appropriate an operating budget for the purpose of funding the
costs that have been mentioned.
           Council member Stephens asked if the City is able to accept private donations from residents or local businesses who
would like to support the project. Ms. Buckley stated that a donation program has not been established for this project as
Administration has been focused securing the money needed for the initial and substantial investments for the project; however, if
there is a need for additional contributions and once the design is fmalized, she can turn her focus to creating a program that will
make it possible for citizens or businesses to donate to support the project.

        Proposed Ordinance 2021-9 approving an amended development
        agreement for land generally located at 1825 Washington Boulevard to
        revise elevations and extend the completion date
                 A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to the Mountain
        America Credit Union development agreement came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated in October 2018, the
        Council approved an amendment to the Mountain View Community Plan and a rezone of property with a conditional overlay at
        1825 Washington Boulevard for the development of a Mountain America Credit Union. The overall project included the
        demolition of the River Inn on the property as well as the relocation of a billboard. The development agreement set forth a
        timeline for construction of two (2) years from the approval date and memorialized the design of the proposed credit union with
        architectural exhibits. The proposed amendments include an extension of the timeline for project completion and an update to the
        proposed design of the building. The extension is needed due the additional time it has taken to fmalize the relocation of the
        billboard and the architectural changes are due to a rebranding by the credit union.
                 Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
        proposal; he reviewed the changes that have occurred at the site since the 2018 Council approval of the Mountain View
        Community Plan and zone change. The proposed amendments would extend the timeline for an additional two (2) years and
        would update the concept plan for the building with a new design. The proposed design changes must still meet the standards
        included in the conditional overlay as well as section 5.04 of the development agreement. The proposed building designs are a
        change from what was approved with the original development agreement; however, the Planning staff and the Planning
        Commission have indicated that the new design sufficiently meets the standards approved for the project. The updated design
        maintains the use of the original or similar materials which include natural stone, metal and cement fiberboard siding. The color
    I   scheme is generally the same as well using dark browns and creme colors. Mr. Montgomery presented a rendering of how the
j       building may look if the proposed amendments are approved. He concluded The Planning Commission reviewed the proposal at
        its December 2, 2020 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of approval to the Council with a vote of8-1. The Commission's
        recommendation was made with the fmding that the proposal is consistent with the standards and policies of the initial rezone and
        development agreement approved by the Council on October 3 0, 20 18. Commissioner Schade voted against the recommendation

Mayor Comments
         Mayor Caldwell thanked Vice Chair White for the great deal of effort she has expended throughout the 2021 Utah
Legislative Session. Additionally, many members of City Administration have worked hard to address legislation that was being
considered throughout the session. Because of these collaborative efforts, Ogden City leaders are recognized as being able to pull
together and address matters at the State level that could impact local controls. He also thanked the Dumke Foundation for their
generous support of the Arts Plaza project; there are also many other community partners that have supported the project and he
looks forward to the project coming to fruition. He then addressed Mr. Mackner' s questions about the property owned by the City
in the Ogden Canyon. He noted the City owns a 36-inch water line that runs down the canyon; this water line feeds all critical
infrastructure and properties that were purchased in the Canyon were intended to provide protection for that water line.
