2.1      Ms. Bergeson presented this item. CIR Engineering is requesting Planning Commission review of a

         preliminary non-administrative conditional use permit and site plan to allow the construction of an office-
         warehouse space. The proposal is to build a 58,045 square foot warehouse building with office space to be
         used as a distribution center for Kessimakis Produce on a vacant, 5-acre lot. The land use is
         “warehouse/wholesale” and is permitted in the zone, with the approval of a non-administrative conditional

              2.1.1     Portions of the property are in the 100-year floodplain zone, and other portions in the 500-
                        year zone. The property is in the “Floodplain Overlay Zone” and “Sensitive Area Overlay Zone”
                        due to high liquefaction designation.

              2.1.2     The property is Lot 4 of the Sorenson Research Park Phase 2 Subdivision, at the end of
                        Riverboat Road, with an area of 5 acres (217,800 square feet). The project area is currently
                        vacant, with “non-engineered fill” surface and a variety of trees.

              2.1.3     A Geo-Technical survey and tree survey were required. The geotechnical report identifies
                        ground water as shallow as 4.4 feet and recommends that slabs be placed no closer than 4
                        feet above stabilized groundwater elevations. A more detailed technical review ensuring
                        compliance with all applicable building codes would take place prior to issuance of any
                        development permits on the site. Staff will continue to work with the applicant throughout
                        the site plan review as well as the building permit process to ensure all proper measures are
                        taken to mitigate the liquefaction hazard. The trees on site are mainly Russian Olives,
                        Cottonwoods and Siberian Elms. She showed on the image which trees would be removed,
                        and the nine which would be kept, and where the new trees would be planted. Nine of the
                        existing trees will be retained.

              2.1.4     Ms. Bergeson added that the property is within the City’s Floodplain Overlay Zone and is
                        thereby subject to the standards in Chapter 13.16 of the Land Development Code (LDC). At
                        this point of the development review process, the applicant has demonstrated general
                        compliance with the City’s Floodplain requirements. Development in the floodplain will
                        require the applicant to continue to work with the City Engineer to obtain the proper permit.
                        Throughout the review process Staff will review plans for appropriate fill and elevations to
                        address flood risks.

              2.1.5     Notifications were sent to neighbors within 300’ of the site and one comment was received,
                        however, it dealt with a Home Owner’s Association problem, which the City has no control
                        over.

              2.1.6     Ms. Bergeson showed an aerial image of what the site presently looks like and showed
                        proposed architectural elevations of the proposed building, which meets City Design
                        Standards. Staff has suggested that the side of the building facing the golf course be upgraded
                        by adding more windows to soften the look of the loading docks. She showed the proposed
                        site plan which has 94 parking spaces and 19.5% landscaping. Only 88 parking spaces are
                        required but the Planning Commission can make the determination on parking and
                        landscaping requirements.

 3.        Planning Commission Review of the Taylorsville Land Development Code Update Project – Chapter
           13.04: Development Standards. (Jim Spung/AICP/Senior Planner)

      Mr. Spung conducted a discussion regarding the changes made to Chapter 13.04 Development Standards. He
 explained some of the new verbiage contained therein. Most changes regarded parking requirements for senior
 housing, low-income, affordable housing, ADA, commercial centers, high density units, etc. One of the biggest
 changes forth coming is eliminating the minimum numbers of parking stalls requirement and focusing on the
 maximums allowed. The effects of establishing stalls to accommodate electric vehicle charging stations was also
 discussed, including the existence of Federal funding availability for grants to help with the expense of that. The
 requirement for establishing electric vehicle stalls requires 5% be dedicated thereto. Another point for clarification
 needed is to better define angle parking, including travel directions in and out.        Mr. Spung thanked the
 Commissioners for their input tonight and stated he would incorporate those in the next draft.
