Study Session

Presentation: Utah Risk Management Agency
David Petersen, Executive Director of the Utah Risk Management Agency (URMA),
provided an overview of URMA’s history, outlined the services and support it offers,
and responded to questions from the Council.

Report: Tree Committee
David Cloward, Chair of the Draper Tree Committee, presented the committee’s
vision to the Council. He provided an overview of the results from a survey conducted
during the 2024 Draper Days event, discussed strategies for maximizing the City's
return on investment in trees, and recommended funding for a full-time arborist.
Mayor Walker expressed appreciation for the presentation.

Discussion: Draper Days
Parks and Recreation Director Rhett Ogden and Special Events Coordinator David
Wilks provided an update on the entertainment and event plans for the 2025 Draper
Days. They also responded to questions from the Council.

5.    Items for Council Consideration
5.a   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1635 amending the text of the Draper City
      General Plan to add the Draper Town Center Station Area Plan
      Todd Taylor, Planner, outlined the required components of the Station Area
      Plan as mandated by the 2022 House Bill 462. He explained the process used
      to develop the Draper Town Center Station Area Plan (SAP) and presented the
      preferred concept. He also reported that the Planning Commission reviewed
      the proposed SAP and forwarded a positive recommendation.

      Mayor Walker opened the public hearing.

      Paul Skene, a Draper resident, expressed support for redevelopment and the
      creation of a walkable area and gathering space. However, he voiced concerns
      about transportation, noting that the plan primarily referenced light rail
      without addressing other transportation needs. He pointed out that some
                                                                          2|Page
existing roads in the area were already failing and inquired how transportation
would be addressed. He stated that he could not envision accommodating the
proposed density without improvements to the road infrastructure.

Vivian Gubler, a Draper resident in the Parkstone neighborhood, expressed a
preference for minimum density and raised concerns about traffic impacts.
She inquired whether the proposed four-story apartment buildings adjacent
to Parkstone could be replaced with condominiums or twin homes, as she
believed the taller structures would obstruct western views for Parkstone
residents. She also stated that constructing tall buildings in the area would not
be respectful of the surrounding community.

Shane Vogelsberg, South Jordan resident and future resident of the Bainbridge
neighborhood, said his family was attracted to Draper and the Bainbridge
neighborhood by how clean, peaceful, and family friendly it was. Mr.
Vogelsberg expressed concern with plans to increase traffic in the
neighborhood.

Charlene Pineda, Draper resident in the Parkstone neighborhood, said she did
not see the existing fence between Parkstone and the UTA property in the
renderings for the proposed Station Area Plan. Ms. Pineda said Parkstone was
a private community, and emphasized the residents did not want that end of
the community open to the public. She said she would prefer the proposed
apartment buildings next to Parkstone to be three stories maximum.

Mark Clifford, Draper resident in the Parkstone neighborhood, said he had no
idea the City was planning a massive development in the area when he bought
his home. He said the plans were upsetting, and said the property values of
existing residences would go down because of the Station Area Plan. Mr.
Clifford asked if the City had similar plans for the Kimball Station area.

Ron Smith, Draper resident in the Parkstone neighborhood, expressed
concern about parking in the proposed Station Area Plan. Mr. Smith said he
developed Parkstone 25 years ago. He expressed the opinion that a row of
owner-occupied townhomes should be developed adjacent to the Parkstone
boundary, not tall apartment buildings, and especially not rentals. Mr. Smith
spoke of neighborhood integrity.




                                                                       3|Page
Steve Taylor, Draper resident in the Masters subdivision, said he echoed
concerns expressed about traffic. Mr. Taylor expressed concern about the
height of proposed buildings, and said the Draper Town Center Station had
the lowest ridership of all Trax stations. He said there was no consistency in
the proposed Station Area Plan with the rest of the community. Mr. Taylor
recommended buildings should not be allowed to exceed three-stories.

Louise Craig, Draper resident, said she moved to Draper because it was a rural
community, and was not thrilled when Trax was added. She questioned why
the whole community should change for a transportation option that was not
used very well. Ms. Craig said she would prefer a maximum of three-story
buildings. She asked if it would be possible for Draper to have the law requiring
the Station Area Plan changed.

Reed Gibby, Draper resident, said he was philosophically concerned about the
State making laws and regulations that were imposed on cities. He said he
appreciated the Council and was glad he did not have to make the decision.

Mayor Walker closed the public hearing.

Mayor Walker stated that the State Legislature attributed the current housing
crisis to city governments, a position with which he disagreed. He provided
background on the State’s requirement for the Station Area Plan (SAP) and
emphasized that the proposed densities were preferable to those initially
suggested by developers. He also discussed a bill currently under
consideration by the State Legislature regarding local versus state land use
authority.

Mayor Walker explained that the proposed density around the TRAX station
was intended to increase ridership and noted that the City did not have control
over development on Utah Transit Authority (UTA) property. He highlighted
that all Station Area Plans must be adopted by October 25, 2025, and clarified
that adoption of the SAP would not guarantee that the proposed buildings
would be constructed.

City Attorney, Traci Gundersen addressed the financial consequences the
State would impose if the City failed to meet SAP requirements.

      Councilmember T. Lowery stated the threat to local control was very real.
      Councilmember Vawdrey said the City had pushed back where they could.
      Councilmember Johnson reiterated that the requirements were forced on the
      City, and said the City would continue to work to find solutions for traffic.
      Councilmember Green expressed appreciation for the Mayor’s efforts and
      emphasized the importance of complying with the Station Area Plan (SAP)
      requirements, noting that the consequences of noncompliance would be
      significant. Councilmember F. Lowry described the situation as unfortunate,
      thanked staff for their work, and acknowledged concerns about traffic.
      Councilmember F. Lowry assured that the Council would make efforts to
      mitigate traffic impacts.

      Councilmember T. Lowery inquired whether the Council could modify the
      proposed plan to reduce the maximum building height to three stories for
      developments adjacent to Parkstone. Community Development Director Jen
      Jastremsky confirmed that modifications could be made but advised against
      lowering the allowable height below three stories.

      Mayor Walker asked if the Council would be open to increasing building height
      in another location as a trade-off for reducing height near Parkstone.
      Councilmember T. Lowery responded that such an adjustment should only be
      considered if absolutely necessary.

      Councilmember T. Lowery moved to approve Ordinance #1635, lowering the
      heights adjacent to Parkstone from four to three stories, giving staff latitude
      to make adjustments to meet needed densities, with fencing and
      encouragement for owner occupancy. Councilmember Vawdrey seconded the
      motion.

      A roll call vote was taken. The motion passed unanimously.
