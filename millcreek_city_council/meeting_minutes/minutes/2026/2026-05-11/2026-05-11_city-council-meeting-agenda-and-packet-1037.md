             John     Brems          said the ordinance                  would        make       it unlawful          to operate         street       legal       e-

            to different           types      of electric           vehicles,         including         determining           which        vehicles            were       legal
            and which             were     not. The          updated          ordinance          is intended          to clarify        definitions             and
            enforcement              standards           so that officers              can more          easily      and consistently              apply          the law in

           single-family,                 two-family,          and agricultural                   (R-1,      R-2,     and A-zone)            neighborhoods.                  He
           reviewed             the history          of Millcreek's              "belt      and suspenders"                   approach,         adopted        around
           2008-2009,               which          added     lot coverage               limits,     building           height       controls,       and building
           envelope             standards          to traditional         setback           requirements               in order        to manage        house          size and
           neighborliood                  character        on lots      of varying            sizes.        Currently,          lot and parcel          coverage             is
           measured             by everything              under       a roof         (principal           and accessory             buildings),        excluding
           uncovered               features        such     as driveways,                sidewalks,           and open patios             or decks.          Lilly
           explained             that,     while     some      Utah      cities         also measure               by roof         area, many       jurisdictions                 use
           building            footprint,       and Millcreek              already          more          precisely        regulates      roof      elements           (such        as
           eave projections                  and building             envelopes).            Staff         therefore        recorninends           a technical           but
           minor      change             to calculate         lot and parcel               coverage           using        the total     area of building
           footprints            (measured           at the exterior             walls      or, for covered                 porches,      at the base of
           supporting              coluinns)         divided        by the total           lot area. He noted                   that this       approach        better
           reflects       'functional          concerns         such      as permeability,                    green        space,     air and light,          and
           storinwater             management,               while      still     addressing               aesthetic        and massing            issues.     The       change
           would         modestly            asSiSt applicants-such                       as the resident              who      raised       this issue-in
           designing            small       additions        that fit within              the standards,               though       it may       slightly      increase            the
           cost and complexity                      of neighborhood                   compatibility                analyses        for modifications.                Lilly

           bringing            an ordinance           to the council              on May           26, following              a Planning           Commission
           recommendation                     expected         on May           20.
