               ORDINANCE # 2026-O-10                         DATE: June 9, 2026

  AN ORDINANCE ADOPTING MAGNA CITY’S 2026-2027 FISCAL YEAR BUDGET

                                           RECITALS

       WHEREAS, Magna City (“Magna”) is a municipality and political subdivision of the state
of Utah; and

       WHEREAS, Utah Code § 10-6-105 requires Magna to operate on a fiscal year beginning
on July 1 and ending on June 30 of each year for budgeting purposes; and

        WHEREAS, on May 12, 2026, the Magna City Council (“Council”) adopted a tentative
budget for the 2026-2027 fiscal year and scheduled a public hearing on June 9, 2026, to solicit
public comment on the same; and

       WHEREAS, after providing notice of the public hearing pursuant to Utah Code § 10-6-113
and holding the public hearing pursuant to Utah Code § 10-6-114, the Council desires to adopt the
2026-2027 fiscal year budget.

       NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED by the Magna City Council that:

       1.       Pursuant to Utah Code § 10-6-118, the Council adopts and certifies the attached
budget as the final Magna’s budget for the 2026-2027 fiscal year.

       2.      Magna staff are authorized and directed to file a certified copy of the final budget
with the Utah State Auditor within thirty (30) days of the date of this ordinance.

       3.      Magna staff shall file a certified copy of the final budget with the Magna City
recorder and such budget shall be made available to the public pursuant to Utah Code § 10-6-
119.

       4.      This ordinance shall take effect as soon as it is posted pursuant to Utah Code §10-
3-711, deposited, and recorded in the office of the Magna City Recorder, provided that the fee
schedule shall take effect on July 1, 2026.

CHAPTER 2.39 ADMINISTRATIVE ADVISORY COMMITTEES AND ORGANIZATIONS

2.39.10       PURPOSE.

The purpose of this Title is to provide for the creation, membership, and operation of
Administrative Advisory Committees and Organizations (“AACOS”). AACOS shall provide
reports, information and recommendations to the City Manager and the City Council.
ACCOS created by this Title shall be self-governing, but if any provisions of this Title
conﬂict with any provisions of any AACOS bylaws, rules, or articles of incorporation, this
Title shall govern.

2.39.20 CREATION, MEMBERSHIP, AND DISSOLUTION.

AACOS may be created or dissolved by the City Manager, with the consent of the Mayor, as
may be needed from time to time. AACOS created by the City Manager herein shall be
composed of an appropriate number of members, appointed by the City Manager. The City
Manager may remove or suspend members from AACOS at any time, for any reason.

2.39.30 FUNDS.

AACOS shall not collect or disburse any City funds.

2.39.40 OPERATIONS.

(1) AACOS operate for the exclusive purpose of providing information, data, evidence, and
recommendations to the City Manager and City Council related to:

a. Solving problems and accomplishing goals in the community;

b. Planning and implementing social, educational, cultural and recreational activities for
the community;

c. Identifying opportunities to improve community safety; and

d. Promoting the community pride through activities, events and programs.

(2) AACOS information, data, evidence, and recommendations shall be submitted to the
City Manager in a written report.

(3) AACOS shall not be public bodies. AACOS shall not be supported by, or otherwise
collect, expend or disburse public funds. AACOS are not vested with any legislative or
executive responsibilities or any authority to make decisions on behalf of the City or any

                                         Page 4 of 5
public business. AACOS records shall not be public until submitted to the City. Upon
receipt by the City, AACOS records shall be available to the public pursuant to the
Government Records Access and Management Act under Title 63G of the Utah Code, or
any successor statute.

2.39.50 MEETINGS.

(1) The time, place, and frequency of AACOS meetings shall be determined by the AACOS.

(2) Elected officials and City employees may attend AACOS meetings.

2.39.60 ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE.

AACOS may, but are not required to, elect a chairperson, vice chair, secretary, and
additional officers as AACOS determine necessary. AACOS may, but are not required, to
formally incorporate and become a legal entity.

       THIS INTERLOCAL COOPERATION AGREEMENT (this “Agreement”) is entered
into by and between SALT LAKE COUNTY, a body corporate and politic of the State of Utah,
for and on behalf of its Department of Community Services ("County") and MAGNA, a
municipal corporation of the State of Utah (“City”). County and City may each be referred to
herein as a “Party” and collectively as the “Parties.”

                                        R E C I T A L S:

        A.     The County is a county existing pursuant to Article XI, Section 1 of the Utah
Constitution, and the Department of Community Services is a department of the County pursuant
to Salt Lake County Ordinances, § 2.06B.020.

       B.      The County receives funds (“TRCC Funds”) pursuant to the Tourism, Recreation,
Cultural, Convention, and Airport Facilities Tax Act, Utah Code Ann. §§ 59-12-602 et seq. (the
“TRCC Act”). The TRCC Act provides that TRCC Funds may be used, among other things, for
the development, operation, and maintenance of publicly owned or operated recreation, cultural,
or convention facilities.

        C.    City is a municipality and a political subdivision of the State of Utah as provided
for in Utah Code Ann. §§ 10-1-201 & 202, 1953 as amended.

        D.      City has requested TRCC Funds from the County to help it fund the project
described in its application attached hereto as EXHIBIT A. More specifically, City requested
TRCC Funds to help fund the installation of new pickleball courts at Magna Neighborhood Park
(the “Project”). The County Council appropriated TRCC Funds for this purpose in the Salt Lake
County Budget.

        E.      The Parties are “public agencies” as defined by the Utah Interlocal Cooperation
Act, Utah Code Ann. §§ 11-13-101 et seq. (the “Interlocal Cooperation Act”), and, as such, are
authorized by the Interlocal Cooperation Act to enter into this Agreement to act jointly and
cooperatively in a manner that will enable them to make the most efficient use of their resources
and powers. Additionally, Section 11-13-215 of the Interlocal Cooperation Act authorizes a
county, city, town, or other local political subdivision to share its tax and other revenues with
other counties, cities, towns, local political subdivisions, or the state.
       F.      The Parties have determined that it is mutually advantageous to enter into this
Agreement and believe that the County’s assistance under this Agreement will contribute to the
prosperity, moral well-being, peace, and comfort of Salt Lake County residents.

       B.     Prorating of TRCC Funds. For projects with underspend, the TRCC grant will be
reduced based on the project underspend times the TRCC grant percentage. The TRCC
reimbursement will be reduced accordingly.

19. Scope of Work, including key project components, expected deliverables, timeline, and
estimated project completion date (if approved).
Within your answer to this question, please provide all relevant details that will help reviewers better
understand WHAT is included in the project and HOW you will complete it. Please include projected
start and completion dates.
The Magna Neighborhood Park Pickleball Court project encompasses the complete construction and
installation of a new, state-of-the-art pickleball court, designed to provide a high-quality, dedicated
recreational facility for the Magna community. This initiative will transform an underutilized section of the
park into a vibrant, active space.

The project's key components, as detailed in our contractor's quote, involve several crucial phases. It
begins with site preparation, which includes all necessary groundwork such as earthwork, precise
grading, and ensuring proper drainage to prevent water accumulation and ensure the court's longevity.
This foundational work will be completed to industry standards. Following this, concrete pad construction
will establish the court's playing surface. This phase entails meticulous formwork, the laying of vapor
barriers, and pouring high-strength concrete, which will then be finished to a smooth, uniform texture
essential for a quality sports surface. To ensure player safety and clearly define boundaries, fencing
installation will then enclose the court with durable chain-link or similar sports fencing, complete with
access gates. Next, a specialized sports surface and paint application will be applied to the concrete pad.
This multi-layered acrylic system offers excellent traction, shock absorption, and a consistent ball bounce,
optimizing play. Once the surface is ready, regulation pickleball lines will be precisely painted onto the
court in contrasting colors, adhering to official dimensions. A professional-grade post/net system
installation will securely place durable, weather-resistant posts and a net at the court's center, designed
for sustained use in a public park setting. Finally, to maximize usability and allow for evening play, lighting
installation will involve energy-efficient LED sports lighting around the court. This system will be designed
to provide uniform illumination across the entire playing surface, ensuring player safety and extending
playable hours throughout the year.

The expected deliverables of this project include a fully constructed, regulation-sized pickleball court,
ready for immediate public use, complete with all specified components: a prepared and leveled site, a
durable concrete playing surface with high-performance sports surfacing and painted lines, secure
perimeter fencing, a professional post and net system, and effective lighting for extended play.

directly following the finalization of contracts with Magna City in March 2026. We estimate a construction
