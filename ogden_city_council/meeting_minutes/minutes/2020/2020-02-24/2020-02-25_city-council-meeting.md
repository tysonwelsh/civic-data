Fiscal Year 2021-2025 Consolidated Plan Discussion
           Council Deputy Director Symes explained that when receiving federal funds for economic development and housing
programs, the City is required to submit a Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) to the Department of Housing and Urban Development
(HUD) every five years. The last plan was adopted in 2015 for fiscal years 2016 through 2020. The Consolidated Plan is part of
the City's application to HUD for Community Development Block Grants (CDBG) and HOME money. Over the last few years,
Council members have expressed interest in revisiting the policies and objectives of the City's ConPlan. In preparation for the
development of the upcoming fiscal year 2021-2025 Consolidated Plan, Administrative staff have agreed to participate in several
work session discussions with the Council to review Council members' thoughts and ideas regarding the Administration's
approach to the City's use offederal, state and city dollars for programs related to the ConPlan's goals. The initial work session
discussion took place on November 12, 2019. At that meeting there was robust discussion regarding the priority needs proposed
for the upcoming ConPlan as well as discussion regarding Council member questions and concerns. As a follow-up to that
meeting, Administrative staff will review the priority needs and present information specific to the items discussed at the
November 12 meeting. The work session discussions are intended to inform the development of the upcoming FY2021-2025
ConPlan so any changes the Council desires can be incorporated into the Plan.

the document identifies priority needs and projects aimed at meeting those needs as follows:

           Neighborhood Revitalization Strategy Areas (NRSAs) are strongly encouraged by the Department of Housing and Urban
Development (HUD) to create communities of opportunities in distressed neighborhoods. The NRSA is an incentive-based
program created by HUD to revitalize a community's most distressed neighborhoods. Mr. Ogden then summarized the adoption
process identified for the ConPlan; the first work session was held in November 2019; following tonight's work session, there will
be an additional work session on April 21, 2020. The public comment period for the ConPlan is April 3, 2020 to May 4, 2020,
which will be followed by a public hearing on May 12, 2020. Adoption of the ConPlan and Annual Action Plan can occur after the
public hearing and the documents must be submitted to HUD by May 14, 2020. HUD approval of the plans should occur by July
1, 2020, after which funding should be made available to the City.

     Airport Northeast Fire Flow Improvements
               Fiscal Years 2020-2024 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Amendment & Fiscal Year 2020 Budget Amendment:
     Council Policy Analyst Mabey explained the City Council has been asked to consider a Capital Improvement Plan (CIP)
     amendment to incorporate a new project into the current plan: Airport AR093 - North East Fire Flow Improvements. This project
..   is intended to improve water facilities at the Ogden Airport to ensure adequate fire protection for future development. Prospective
     developments near the Airport are anticipated to result in up to 150,0000 square feet in new facilities within the Northeast
     Commercial Aerospace Project Area. This includes Phase 1 of the Borsight project - a 30,000 square foot hangar and office
     facility that are anticipated to expand local employment opportunities.
               Ms. Mabey added this proposal includes a budget amendment to transfer $250,000 from Airport parking improvement
     funds for Airport water systems engineering and design to ensure adequate water flow for fire suppression needs. The project
     scope is encompassed in Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) project Airport AR093 - North East Fire Flow Improvements; this CIP
     project is currently a proposal that will be considered in conjunction with this budget amendment.
               Public Services Deputy Director Anderson reviewed the scope of CIP Project Airport AR093 as follows: "AR093Ogden
     City is preparing for the expansion of Maintenance, Repair, and Overhaul (MRO) operations at the Airport in the Northeast
     Commercial Aerospace Project Area, including several phases of planned expansion by Borsight. Additional fire flow will be
     needed to support future business expansion and growth in this area. It would be prudent to size infrastructure improvements to
     support this growth. A water tank is needed to provide additional fire flow to support development. At this time, it is anticipated
     that the tank will be approximately 200,000 gallons, although the size may increase based on design considerations. The water
     tank would provide adequate fire flow for the future growth of Borsight and other potential tenants in the project area. The City
     proposes to reduce the budget for a parking lot improvement project at the Airport and fund the water tank infrastructure project.
     The $250,000 funded to AR093 will fund the design costs for the water tank infrastructure and the City will ask for an
     appropriation for the construction costs once the design is complete." He then identified the proposed location of the tank and
)    indicated that project completion is estimated for December 2020.
               Planning Manager Montgomery reported on the Planning Commission's consideration of the proposed CIP amendment;
     they found the project is consistent with the General Plan and protects the safety and integrity of the Airport while allowing for
     improvement and expansion of the facility.
               Council member White inquired as to the project(s) that will not be completed as a result of the CIP amendment and
     subsequent budget amendment. Ms. Mabey stated that the budget transfer is coming from Airport parking improvement fund.
     Chief Administrative Officer Johnson added that funding was allowed in 2017 at the point that City Administration thought it
     would be necessary to expand parking in response to an increase in flight offerings from the Airport. The demand for parking did
     not materialize and the funding is still available. Administration proposes to re-appropriate that money for a study regarding the
     water tank project; the project will make it possible to attract businesses from Hill Air Force Base (HAFB) to the Airport.
                Council discussion centered on long-term plans for the Airport, including other CIP projects to be completed at the
     facility and the concept of attracting a commercial airline to Ogden City.
               Council member White asked if the funding for the proposed project will come from the General Fund or an Enterprise
     Fund. Mr. Johnson stated the proposed funding is General Fund money. Comptroller Stout stated the funding is actually coming
     from the City's CIP fund; she reviewed the summary appropriation schedule, noting the new tank will be a City asset and she
     would like to create an accounting mechanism that will allow the City to save funds to provide for the ongoing maintenance of the
     tank. Funding could be generated via execution ofa common area maintenance (CAM) agreement with the other users who will
     utilize the tank.

     MAKE Ogden Preview
              A memo from Council staff explained that as the Wasatch Front experiences unprecedented growth, the City of Ogden
     continues to position our downtown as a competitive location for businesses to thrive and an excellent place to live and play. The
     Central Business District (CBD) Community Plan is the strategic plan that guides this vision, but it is almost a decade old. The
     time has come to create a new vision of the future of Ogden City. In Spring 2019, Ogden City, with its consultant Design
     Workshop, kicked off a multi-month planning process to create a new Downtown Master Plan that will effectively change
     portions of our General Plan/CBD Community Plan and will guide the future development of our downtown through the next two
     decades. This exciting process has been dubbed MAKE Ogden. Council was encouraged to get engaged with this planning process
     and explore the initial concepts developed to guide the project.
              Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
     the Downtown Ogden Master Plan planning process; it commenced with a summit in the spring of2019, which was followed by
     visioning in the summer and fall of2019, and public outreach- including a public workshop and smvey-through February 2020.

          There was a total of 1,063 respondents to the public survey; translations of the survey were available in English and
Spanish. The City was seeking input on assets, challenges, and priorities for downtown Ogden. He then highlighted the project
dilemma; the City of Ogden needs a comprehensive vision to guide growth and development in its Downtown and to maintain and
grow its tax base-which will improve the long-term fiscal health of the community and ability to meet basic service needs. How
can Ogden strategically evolve its downtown core to take advantage of its historic and natural assets to become a nexus for
business and employment attraction, residential growth, and recreational and cultural activity that increases the value of the land,
contributing to the long-tenn health of the Ogden community? The project approach has been to consider smart growth and social
equity opportunities. People are demanding walkable mixed-use, diverse communities and these places are where the vast
majority of investor dollars are going. Smart growth components include a focus on walkability,job density, housing density, and
getting people in places. Social equity opportunities include transit access, social services, diversity of housing tenure, and
housing/transportation affordability. Smart growth benefits include a 3 8 percent savings on infrastructure, 10 percent savings on
public services, and 10 times the revenue generation. The benefits of social equity include investors being less apprehensive,
keeping people in place, and access to new streams of capital. Mr. Cooper concluded by providing a graph highlighting the
trajectory of population and land values based on multiple triggers; dramatic growth is facilitated by fom episodes identified in the
Downtown Ogden Master Plan: support your downtown, catalyze your downtown, expand your downtown, and connect your
downtown. He indicated a public open house will be held tomorrow evening during which he will expand on this thesis and
provide clear timeframes for each episode.
          The Council then engaged in discussion with Mr. Cooper regarding details of the Master Plan document, including
private versus public sector investment, community feedback, the difference between the Master Plan and other community plans
for the City, and next steps in the process prior to proposed adoption of the Plan document.
