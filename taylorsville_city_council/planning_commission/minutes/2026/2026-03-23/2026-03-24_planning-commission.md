BRIEFING SESSION — 6PM
1. Summary: Attached & Detached Accessory Dwelling Units — Grant Allen

Senior Planner Grant Allen provided a comprehensive briefing on accessory dwelling
units (ADUs), both internal and detached, focusing on recent legislative changes at the
state level and their implications for Taylorsville. He emphasized this was an introductory
discussion with more detailed conversations to follow, rather than an exhaustive treatment
of the topic.
Mr. Allen explained that Taylorsville currently allowed internal ADUs, which included
basement apartments and attached units such as additions to existing homes. The focus
of the evening's discussion was on detached ADUs, which were separate structures from
the main single-family dwelling.
The presentation centered on Senate Bill 284 from the 2026 legislative session, which
mandated that municipalities adopt land use regulations permitting detached ADUs on lots
of 11,000 square feet or larger containing single-family dwellings. Staff noted that while
11,000 square feet was the legislative minimum, cities could choose to allow detached
ADUs on smaller lots.
Mr. Allen outlined the legislative requirements in two categories: mandatory actions
(the city “shall’) and prohibited restrictions (the city “may not’). Under the mandatory
requirements, the city must allow detached ADUs on qualifying lots, require compliance
with building, health, and fire codes, and provide a process for converting existing legal
accessory structures to detached ADUs.

Commissioner Murphy asked about the conversion process, specifically whether property
owners would need to return to the city for approval. Mr. Allen confirmed that it would be
necessary and explained that the specific process would be developed through future
discussions, noting that utility connection requirements would likely significantly impact
these changes.
Regarding restrictions the city was prohibited from imposing, he explained that municipalities
could not require more than one off-street parking space for units smaller than 650 square
feet, could not require two off-street parking spaces for units larger than 650 square feet,
and could not require design standards in the traditional sense.
Commissioner Wright expressed concern about potential quality issues, questioning
whether substandard structures could be built without design oversight. Mr. Allen
addressed this by explaining the permitted regulations (things the city ‘may’ do),
including the ability to require design consistency with single-family dwellings, though he
acknowledged some ambiguity in the legislation prohibiting design standards while
allowing design consistency requirements.
Commissioner Mufioz raised similar concerns about the apparent contradiction
between prohibiting design standards while permitting design consistency requirements,
suggesting the legislation might need clarification in future legislative sessions. Staff
acknowledged this ambiguity and referenced specific statutory language that would
need further analysis.
The discussion covered various regulatory options available to the city, including
restrictions on structure size, dimensions, height, and lot coverage. Mr. Allen explained
that maximum lot — coverage regulations could control how much of a rear yard could
be occupied by structures. Setback requirements from property lines, other structures,
and easements were also discussed as permissible regulations.
Commissioner Murphy inquired about the identification of utility easements, and Mr.
Allen explained these were typically shown on subdivision plats or recorded separately
for older properties predating subdivisions. The city could prohibit ADUs in public utility
easements to protect both property owners and utilities from potential damage.
He outlined additional regulatory options, including prohibiting units larger than the main
dwelling, restricting front yard placement, prohibiting short-term rentals (less than 90 days),
requiring owner occupancy, and limiting properties to one accessory dwelling unit, though
he noted the city could choose to allow multiple units on larger parcels.
A significant discussion point involved utility capacity constraints. Mr. Allen explained that
if utility providers like Taylorsville Bennion Improvement District determined that
additional units would require system improvements such as lift station upgrades, the city
could prohibit ADUs in those specific areas.
Commissioner Wright requested that future discussions include input from the
improvement district, noting earlier mentioned reservations about potential impacts. Mr.
Allen agreed to include utility providers in upcoming conversations.

He presented preliminary data showing Taylorsville had approximately 2,050 existing

lots of 11,000 square feet or larger that would qualify under the legislative mandate. If the

threshold were lowered to 10,000 square feet, the number increased to approximately 3,819

lots. At 8,000 square feet, which would align with existing R-1-8 zoning, the count jumped

dramatically to 8,289 lots, significantly expanding the potential impact across city
neighborhoods.

Staff concluded by indicating plans for future work sessions using a collaborative

format, potentially including maps and draft standards for detailed discussion. They

committed to researching approaches taken by other communities. that had
implemented detached ADU regulations for an extended period, learning from both
successful implementations and necessary adjustments.

The new legislation would take effect in the first week of May. Future discussions could

proceed according to the Commission’s preferences, whether focusing first on lot size

thresholds or other regulatory aspects.

The briefing session adjourned at 6:31 p.m.

hy 3. Public Hearing and Consideration of a Nonadministrative Conditional
Use Permit for Extended Hours of Operation and to Operate a Bar
Establishment at 5418 South 1900 West - Jerryne Bergeson

Ms. Bergeson explained that this was a continuation of the March 10th meeting. The

application sought approval for a bar establishment and extended hours of operation at

5418 South 1900 West, submitted by Jessica Blakeman on behalf of The Break Sports

Bar and Grill.

Ms. Bergeson detailed that the property was designated as high intensity commercial within
the CenterPoint site-specific planning area and was zoned regional commercial. Since the
location was within 250 feet of a residential zone, a nonadministrative conditional use
permit was required for both the bar establishment and operation past 10 PM.

The property consisted of approximately 0.85 acres with a 7,300 square foot building. Ms. Bergeson
noted that no significant changes to the site were proposed, except for slightly larger windows.
The main entrance and exit would be on the south side, with a patio to the north that would serve
only as a fire exit.

Ms. Bergeson explained the role of the Planning Commission in evaluating potential
detrimental impacts and establishing appropriate conditions. The primary concern identified
was the potential impact on peace and quiet of surrounding residential areas. A public notice had
been mailed to property owners within 300 feet of the property on February 27, 2026, and
notice was posted to the city and state websites on March 13, 2026.

Regarding hours of operation, Ms. Bergeson clarified that while the staff report contained
conditions for reduced weeknight hours, the applicant later requested reconsideration for extended
hours. The original request was for operation until 1 AM all nights, with the Department of
Alcoholic Beverage Services (DABS) requiring establishments to remain open one hour after
last call. They would be willing to compromise to stay open until 1AM on Thursday, Friday, and
Saturday nights.

Ms. Bergeson presented the following recommended conditions of approval, which had been
updated from the March 10th meeting:.

1. Existing patio feature may be used only after approval of a building permit that
included the installation of sound mitigating features.

2. While expansion would require nonadministrative site plan review and approval,
future expansion of outdoor patio and dining area was limited to eastward
expansion of the existing patio, or on the east side of the site.

3. Outdoor patio or dining space would not be permitted on the south and west
portions of the site, to mitigate impacts to the peace and comfort of adjacent
residential.

4. Waste pickup was to occur no fewer than four times per week.

5. Hours of Operation to be determined in the motion.

6. Language to discourage loitering was to be printed on signage within the parking
lot

7. In addition to state and federal laws, smoking and vaping could only occur on the
east side of the building. Signs indicating such were to be posted on the building
in a visible location.

8. An administrative review would take place six months after final approval to ensure
compliance with the approved conditions to determine if the identified impacts were
being mitigated. The Director could decide that the findings of the review needed
to be presented to the planning commission to consider modifying or adding
conditions.

Chair Young opened the public hearing. However, there were no members of the public either
in person or online who expressed a desire to speak. The Chair then closed the public hearing.

Commissioner McElreath asked Ms. Bergeson to review the proposed hours of operation. She
reiterated that since the staff report was created, the applicant had requested to either be
allowed to serve until 1 AM every day (closing at 2 AM) or keeping those hours at least on
Thursday through Saturday. Commissioner McElreath was comfortable with either option since
both were within DABS guidelines, knowing there would be a six-month review where hours
could be adjusted if they proved to be problematic for the neighbors.
Commissioner Wright asked what hours of operation were approved for reception. centers in the
city. Ms. Bergeson explained that reception centers typically were required to shut down by 10
PM Sunday through Thursday and operated until midnight on Friday and Saturday nights. She
noted that the city had been requiring reception centers to maintain similar security and supervision
standards as bars, even though they did not have DABS licenses.
Commissioner Willardson expressed concern about serving liquor past midnight, suggesting
that last call should be at midnight to avoid potential trouble. : :
Commissioner Wright was unclear on why reception centers would have different rules than a
bar establishment. Ms. Bergeson responded that they were different uses. Commissioner
Mufioz commented that the applicant sought to operate during hours that were typical
throughout Salt Lake County. It might feel unusual because this would be only the second bar
establishment in Taylorsville.
Commissioner Wright again questioned the difference between complaints of late nights and
rowdiness at a reception center vs. at a bar. Chair Young pointed out that bars and reception
centers had different regulatory requirements. While there had been complaints about loud music
at reception centers, there was no plan for live music at The Break.
Blake Brown, the operations manager for The Break Sports Bar and Grill, was invited to address
the commission. He explained that they were had returned to their original proposal to operate
until 1 AM every night, noting that the other bar in Taylorsville (‘Bout Time Pub) operated from
10 AM to 1 AM daily. Mr. Brown emphasized that their establishment would have DABS-
trained staff who knew how to handle intoxicated patrons safely, unlike some reception
centers. He mentioned they operated five other locations and wanted to maintain consistency
across their establishments.
When asked about the importance of Thursday night hours, Mr. Brown explained that
Thursdays were as busy as Fridays and Saturdays for bars, and they didn't want to lose late-night
business to competitors.
Commissioner Wilkey raised questions about noise ordinance compliance and concerns about
the impact on nearby residents, particularly regarding staff closing procedures until 2 AM and
potential early morning trash pickup. Ms. Bergeson confirmed that the establishment would
need to comply with Salt Lake County's noise ordinances and noted that the trash enclosure had
been relocated to a more appropriate location away from residential fences.
Commissioner Murphy asked whether the applicants could close earlier on certain nights if they
chose to, which Commissioner Young confirmed they could.
