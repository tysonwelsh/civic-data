      3. Discussion and Action Items
          3.1. Discussion and consideration of a Resolution adopting a proposed rate of tax and
               levying taxes upon all real and personal property within the Herriman City Fire
               Service Area – Kyle Maurer, Finance Director
      Director Kyle Maurer presented on the topic of property tax rates for the 2025 tax year. He
      began by reviewing the basics of property tax, noting that the certified tax rate is calculated
      to generate the same revenue as the prior year, plus revenue from new growth. Importantly,
      rising property values do not necessarily equate to increased taxes for homeowners due to
      how Utah's tax system is structured. For 2025, the certified tax rate was set at 0.001329,
      slightly higher than the previous year due to adjustments related to redevelopment project
      areas. This increase was projected to generate approximately $382,000 more in revenue due
      to new growth, not rate increases. The average home in Herriman, valued at $640,000, would
      pay about $468 in Fire District taxes if the certified tax rate were adopted.

      Director Maurer emphasized that the Herriman City Fire Service Area's tax revenues had
      been affected by its participation in redevelopment areas, though this did not reduce overall
      revenue, just redirected a portion. He also explained that proposing a rate higher than the
      certified tax rate would trigger the Truth in Taxation process, requiring formal hearings and
      public notice. Staff recommended a small, incremental increase to the rate to maintain
      purchasing power over time, citing that there is no built-in inflation adjustment in the
      certified tax rate. The primary driver for the increase was a $147,766 rise in the assessment
      paid to Unified Fire Authority (UFA), which provided the fire services. A 1.7% tax rate increase,
      or about $8 more annually for the average homeowner, was proposed to cover this cost. He
      noted that if the Board moved forward with a proposed increase, the Truth in Taxation
      Hearing would be scheduled for August 13, 2025.

      Trustee Jared Henderson supported the proposal, explaining past efforts to exit the larger
      taxing district and realign with more sustainable funding. He pointed out that the City had
      already paid for land and buildings with cash savings and was planning for future fire stations
      and staff to accommodate Herriman’s growing population. Trustee Henderson stressed the
      importance of steady, manageable increases to avoid sudden large tax hikes in the future. He
      asked for a detailed financial outline comparing fund balances over time to aid in
      transparency and long-term planning.

      Trustee Teddy Hodges agreed, emphasizing the importance of preserving purchasing power

      area in the amount of $8,552,067. Trustee Ohrn seconded the motion.
