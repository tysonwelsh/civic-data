       b. Resolution No. 237 authorizing the execution of a Purchase and Sale Agreement
          between the Redevelopment Agency of West Jordan City (seller) and Sawtell
          Properties, LLC (Buyer).
          City Administrator Korban Lee briefly introduced the item noting that the City had been
          speaking in negotiations with Peterson Development regarding selling a portion of
          property. Adjustments had been made to the property disposal. In addition to
          purchasing the property, Peterson would deed a protection strip in a different location
          to the City. He clarified it was a Quit Claim Deed but still an important part of the item.

          Council Office Director Anderson asked for time to take care of a technical issue.

          At 8:39 pm Board Member Green moved to take a five-minute recess. Board
          Member Jacob seconded the motion. All voted in favor and the motion passed
          unanimously (7-0).

          The meeting reconvened at 8:44 pm

          Economic Development Director Chris Pengra gave a brief overview of the Purchase and
          Sale Agreement (PSA). He noted that Dave Clemence and Adam Long were available via
          Zoom to answer any questions. Pertinent provisions were that it governs the sale of
          2.895 acres at $13.25 per square feet.

          Chairperson McConnehey asked where the property was located. Mr. Pengra explained
          it was east of Mountain View Corridor and north of 9000 South, sandwiched in between
          Mountain View Corridor and 5600 West.

          He continued noting that along with the PSA there was a Road and Easement
          Agreement, as well as a Buyback Agreement which would be executed at closing. The
          PSA also required the Buyer and Seller to share the cost of a traffic signal located at
          Frisco Drive and 5600 West if warranted by the development of either party.

          The Road Easement and Buyback Agreements identify the relationship, cost sharing,
          and obligations between the Redevelopment agency and the purchaser at a future date
          when the road is established. The Buyback Agreement was there in case the PSA is
          executed but the property doesn’t close. At that point, the Redevelopment Agency would
          retain ownership of the property and provide access to the buyer.

          Board Member Green noted a difference in the footer of the signed PSA and the one
          attached to the Resolution. Adam Long was not concerned, the difference was not
          substantive. Board Member Green referencing Section 9.7 of the Buyback Agreement
          voiced concern with language addressing 16 units per acre and voiced concern that it
          alluded to promising zoning. Mr. Pengra clarified that the board could not make that
          promise. Board Member Green referencing language at the top of page 10 regarding
          conditions described and not satisfied on or before closing the buyer could terminate
          the agreement. He questioned the language where it was referencing something that
