UPDATE- Hillcrest Park

Mr. Gray introduced Brent Potter and Cory Schupp of Blueline Designs. They
presented an image and explained some of the new updates and layouts of the
park that included the splash pad locations, pathways, and hammocking area
location. Reason for making those changes were to save existing trees that are on
this site. Mr. Potter continued to explain there is a large difference in the site
plan due to fire code and to the architecture of the building. The building had
some slight modifications in an effort to reduce costs.

General discussion of architecture and layout occurred while displaying an
image of the outside of the building. Windows and bathrooms were addressed
and concerns were resolved.

Mr. Potter indicated they are hoping to start demolition very soon, bids were
due on September 29, 2022 and it is estimated 30 days to bring a contractor on
board for the demolition.

PUBLIC HEARING / ORDINANCE — Amending Section
14-1-4 Sign Zones and the Sign Zone map of the Orem
City Code extending Sign Zone E to include a portion of
the CenterPoint Church property located at 1550 S
Sandhill Road.

Mr. Bench stated CenterPointe Church would like to install a double sided, pole
mounted digital sign on their property. This sign would replace the historic use
of hanging 10’ x 10’ banners on the building to advertise for events, such as a
Christmas Eve Service. The pole sign would be installed near the southwest
corner of the building oriented towards the freeway. 1550 S Sandhill Road does

not currently have a sign zone, so it is considered to be in Sign Zone “D.” Sign
Zone “D” allows freestanding monument signs, however is does not allow any
freestanding pole signs (Section 14-3-3). The applicant is requesting that sign
zone “E,” which allows pole signs, be extended to include 1480 S Sandhill Road
and a portion of 1550 S Sandhill Road property

General discussion ensued regarding the proposed rezone, businesses and
residents surrounding that area.
Mayor Young opened the public hearing at 6:48 pm.

Samuel Richardson stated that he is in favor of protecting that neighborhood and
he likes the idea of the sign being parallel along I-15.

Mayor Young closed the public hearing at 6:48 pm.

Mr. Earl suggested that the council inquire to the applicant if they would be
willing to build to the specific dimensions of the approved plan.

Mr. Macdonald moved to approve an Ordinance Amending Section 14-1-4 Sign
Zones and the Sign Zone map of the Orem City Code extending Sign Zone E to
include a portion of the CenterPoint Church property located at 1550 S Sandhill
Road. Mrs. Millett seconded the motion. Those voting aye: David A. Young,
David Spencer, Debby Lauret, Tom Macdonald, Jeff Lambson, Terry Peterson
and LaNae Millett. The motion passed.

PUBLIC HEARING / RESOLUTION - Amending portions of Chapter 4
(Housing) of the Orem General Plan including updating Section 4.1
Population Demographics, Section 4.2 Existing Conditions, and enacting
Section 4.4.2 pertaining to Moderate Income Housing Strategies.

Mr. Bench stated pursuant to Utah Code Annotated (UCA) 10-9a-403(2)(b)(iii),
Orem City is amending portions of Chapter 4 (Housing) of the Orem City

General Plan to adopt the following four moderate income housing strategies and
implementation plan for each item. The proposed amendment and text-strikeout
are included as part of the staff report.

¢ (E) create or allow for, and reduce regulations related to, internal or detached
accessory dwelling units in residential zones;

Accessory apartments are already adopted into Orem’s municipal code

and part of the Development Services process. Orem City continues to

provide all requirements for legal

accessory apartments online. We also continue to advise residents who ask about
their options. Orem monitors the number of legal apartments by annual report of
existing legal apartments. Currently, there are 722 legal accessory apartments in
Orem. It is anticipated that approximately 26 additional accessory units will be
approved in the next year.

* (X) demonstrate implementation of any other program or strategy to address the
housing needs of residents of the municipality who earn less than 80% of the area
median income, including the dedication of a local funding source to moderate
income housing or the adoption of a land use ordinance that requires 10% or
more of new residential development;

In 2012, the City approved the Affordable Senior Housing (ASH) Overlay Zone
to assist income restricted seniors. The City provides all information on our
website and works collaboratively with the Utah County Housing Authority to
provide new affordable senior housing units. Orem plans to continue to educate
developers on regulations for developing in the Affordable Senior Housing
Overlay (ASH) zone. The ASH overlay zone allows up to four units to be
constructed on a single parcel. The tenant of each unit is restricted to those over
the age of 60 and at or less than 80% of the median income of the Orem/Provo
MSA.

* (Q) create a housing and transit reinvestment zone pursuant to Title 63N,
Chapter 3, Part 6, Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zone Act;

A developer has approached the City and has met with Staff, the Mayor and
members of the council as to the feasibility of an HTRZ Zone. The next step in
this process is for the Developer to to hire a consultant for a study of the HTRZ
estimated to take 6-8 months. In 2023, following the study, the Developer,
Consultant and City will consider the next steps as required from the HTRZ Act.

* (V) develop and adopt a station area plan in accordance with Section 10-9a-403.1;

In accordance with Utah Code Annotated (UCA) 10-9a-403.1, Orem City has

outlined Station Area Plans for the transit stations in Orem into two phases:
Phase 1 will begin with the revision and update of the existing station area plan
for the Orem Frontrunner Station. Phase 2 will create a station area plan for the
Lakeview, Main Street and University Place Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) stations.
Additionally, staff is proposing to update Section 4.1 Population Demographics with
updated demographic charts with 2020 Census Data and modify a portion of Section 4.2
Existing Conditions to match current zoning

Ms. Millett asked Mr. Bench to highlight where they added the owner-occupied
housing.

Mr. Bench reviewed the presentation showing the area and stated it is
encouraged to be owner occupied twin home type developments in the station
area plans.

Mayor Young opened the public hearing at 6:54 pm, hearing no comments he
closed the public hearing and brought the item back to the council for further
discussion.

Mr. Lambson moved to approve by Ordinance amending portions of Chapter 4
(Housing) of the Orem General Plan including updating Section 4.1 Population
Demographics, Section 4.2 Existing Conditions, and enacting Section 4.4.2
pertaining to Moderate Income Housing Strategies. Mrs. Lauret seconded the
motion. Those voting aye: David A. Young, David Spencer, Debby Lauret,
Tom Macdonald, Jeff Lambson, Terry Peterson and LaNae Millett. The motion
passed.

RESOLUTION - Expressing Intent to Combine Orem Dispatch
Services with Provo Dispatch Services

Captain Sorenson stated the dispatchers are very short on staffing and the State
Legislative and the State Tax Commission are looking to eventually move to
one PSAPS per County. He explained they are looking for a better opportunity
for employees and better service for the citizens. Mr. Sorenson explained some
of the research and factors that the departments have taken into consideration
while considering this move. He spoke about the shared staffing model and
some of the benefits and opportunities that employee could have with this

process.

General discussion ensued regarding the satisfaction of the officers with
this move and the solutions that have been made based on employee
concerns.

Mrs. Lauret moved to approve a Resolution expressing intent to combine Orem
dispatch services with Provo dispatch services. Mr. Macdonald seconded the
motion. Those voting aye: David A. Young, David Spencer, Debby Lauret, Tom
Macdonald, Jeff Lambson, Terry Peterson and LaNae Millett. The motion
passed.

PERSONAL APPEARANCES

Ben Wheeler thanked the City Council for giving the residents of Orem a voice
on the ballot. He spoke about Alpine School District and the debt that is
associated with the district. He provided information about Alpine School
District bonds, inflation, budgets, proposition 2, and tax debt.

Debi Carlyle spoke about some of the benefits that she sees in a smaller school
district. She explained that as a tax payer she would prefer the best value for her
dollar, and she believes that would be possible with a smaller district.

Michelle Lee stated that Alpine School District has a bond debt and
some large amounts associated with it. She voiced her concern about the
future if the bond was to pass and possibilities that would play into the
predicted outcomes.

Bryn Garcia voiced her concern about the library displays and how it has affected
her as a mother. She enjoyed the convenience of the displays in the library when
they were set up and the knowledge and experience that she believes her children
gained from the books on the displays. Mayor Young expressed his confusion
stating that specific displays had not been banned in the library. Ms. Garcia
stated that the displays are not currently up.

Christine Crane expressed her concern as a mother that she is no longer seeing
specific displays in the library anymore. She commented on some of the
benefits of having the displays up in the past and why she believes they should
be up in the future. She has questions for the library commission in regards to
the displays and will look forward to receiving a response from them.

Amy Karr voiced her frustration and confusion regarding the interaction with
the City Council during the public comment period two weeks prior. She had
not heard back from the council in regards to the Heritage Month displays and
is displeased because the council stated they would look into the issue. Ms.
Karr spoke about previous meetings with the Library Commission and events
that took place in those meetings. She spoke about transparency with the
Director of the Library and discussions they have had.

Annabelle Cruz expressed she is disheartened that Hispanic Heritage Month is
not being celebrated on the library displays. She is frustrated about this issue
and voiced her concerns stating that she believes Orem is moving backwards.
Mayor Young stated that there is no ban on certain displays and there is some
misguided information.

Blyden Loutensack commented on Alpine School District and what he believes
they support. He explained some content in current Alpine School District
libraries that he believes is unacceptable due to warning labels on the books.

Scott Brady spoke about the possible school district split and concerns he has
that may be associated with it. He also explained some potential benefits of
Orem not being in Alpine School District in the future.

Shane Ramirez explained that her daughter is the one who created the pride
display. She expressed the difficulty as a parent in seeing the contention the
display caused and hearing the comments said about it. She believes that there
has been a shift sense the pride display and explained the difficulty in asking
where to find books to feel included instead of them being readily available on
a display.

Lennard Lee spoke about the Alpine School District bond and explained the
process of the bond using different situations as examples. He gave some advice
based off of his personal experience.

RESOLUTION - A Resolution of the City Council of the City of
Orem outlining its opposition to the 2022 General Obligation
Bond for $595 million proposed by Alpine School District.

Mayor Young read the proposed resolution and then opened it up to
statements by the City Council Members.

Mrs. Lauret stated she just found out about this resolution and so she has not
yet had time to look it over. She feels as though the Council should be neutral
when it comes to this bond. She explained she thinks the citizens of Orem will
be able to look at the information and vote accordingly on the ballot. She
stated some values that she lives according to are:

Is it the truth?

Will it be fair to all concerns?
Will it build good will and better friendships?
Will it be beneficial to all concerns?

She stated that in regards to the question of “will it build good will and better
friendships”, she believes that no matter what the outcome of the bond, they
are going to need to work with Alpine School District (ASD). She believes that
by voting yes on this resolution it will create more ill will.

Mr. Spencer stated that he is opposed to high taxes for the city of Orem. He
spoke about signs that he is seeing around town and what they are stating
for proposition 2. He believes that this bond with ASD will increase the taxes
of Orem citizens. He explained the history of ASD bonds, the future of the
new ASD bonds, current seismic concerns, and property taxes. Mr. Spencer
continued to state Orem’s taxes will stay the same, and not increase with the
new school district, if Orem votes to break away from ASD. Orem’s taxes will
stay the same because they will not be responsible for the ASD bond debt.

Mr. Macdonald stated he received notice of this resolution being on the
agenda by a reporter from the Daily Herald. He stated that it was troublesome
to him that four members of the council had discussed while not violating any
open meeting laws, individually about this. He believes that this is a council of
seven people and not four and he would not like to hear about it not from a
reporter. He explained that the same day that he heard about it he was not
able to verify numbers and so he does not know them to be accurate. He said
that the Utah taxpayer’s association elected to remain a neutral position on

the proposed issue of the ASD bond and he found that to be interesting. He
continued the Utah Taxpayers Association said the impending vote on the pro
split of Orem into a new district will not affect this bond issue. If the district is
split corresponding projects within the new district and the dollar amount of
those projects will be pulled out of the total amount and be voted on by the
new implementing body. Mr. Macdonald stated that in the past the council
has had a good pattern of remaining neutral. He concluded that the resolution
is not binding and it is only recommended by some members of the City
Council.

Mrs. Millett stated that as a representative she feels the responsibility to
represent Orem citizens. She said that ASD has announced that they will
not be rebuilding any of Orem’s seismic unsafe schools in their first phase
and she believes that is not protecting the lives of the children. She
expressed her concerns with seismic safety in Orem schools, children’s
safety and property taxes.

Mr. Peterson voiced his concern of residents’ frustrations with increasing
property taxes and inflation. He believes that it is time to keep our taxes in
Orem and expressed concern about possibly getting into more debt with the
ASD bond. He then listed possible positive outcomes of having an Orem only
school district. He continued to state his disappointment with ASD for

not correcting misinformation that is being spread among citizens. Mr.
Peterson concluded by quoting different statements from authors and poets,
focused on lies being brought to the truth. Mr. Lambson stated he had been
on vacation and got home recently so he also just found out about the
resolution a little over 24 hours ago. He wishes that he would have had time
to put together a statement and type out his thoughts and feelings. He is
grateful to hear others’ opinions and have the opportunity to voice his opinion
but he is confused that they had to change the way that they normally do
things, because of something that may be said in the meeting. Mr. Lambson
agrees that he does not prefer to see property taxes rise and he would like
what is best for everybody. He has a hard time as an elected body not being
neutral. Mr. Lambson stated that this has become such a controversial topic
in the community and he is concerned about that. He believes that as an
elected body coming together as a council, things should remain neutral. He
concluded by stating he does not feel that it is his job as an elected official to
encourage the residents what to do, he trusts the citizens of Orem.

Mayor Young clarified some reasons why he felt as though the council
needed to have this conversation about the resolution. He stated that this
resolution was last minute for everybody on the council. He expressed his
feeling on tax increase, seismic issues in schools, and future predictions of
the ASD bond.

General discussion ensued regarding the council members notification of the
resolution.

Mrs. Millett motioned an approval of a resolution of the City Council of
the City of Orem outlining its opposition to the 2022 General Obligation
Bond for $595 million proposed by Alpine School District. Mr. Peterson
seconded the motion. Those voting aye: David A.

Young, David Spencer, Terry Peterson and LaNae Millett. Those voting
nay: Debby Lauret, Tom Macdonald, Jeff Lambson the motion passed.
