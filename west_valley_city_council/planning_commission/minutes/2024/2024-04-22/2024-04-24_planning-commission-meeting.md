Paul and Kareen Swenson have submitted a General Plan/zone change application for a 2.96-acre
parcel located at 4053 South 7200 West. The property is currently zoned A (Agriculture,
minimum lot size ½ acre) and the current General Plan designation is Large Lot Residential (2 to
3 units/acre). The proposed zone is M (Manufacturing) and the proposed General Plan
designation is Light Manufacturing.

Surrounding zones include A to the south, R-1-10 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum
lot size 10,000 square feet) to the east, R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size
8,000 square feet) to the north, and R-1-7 to the west in Magna. Surrounding land uses include a
City owned storage lot to the south, single unit dwellings to the north and east, and a Salt Lake
County park to the west. The property includes two homes – one built in 1978 and the other built
in 1982.

Approximately the west 40% of the property falls within Overpressure Zone A with the remainder
of the property being within Overpressure Zone B. Overpressure Zone A is the innermost circle
(closest to Northrop Grumman) and prohibits residential use.

Development Proposal

If this application is approved, the applicants intend to develop a self-storage facility on the
subject property. Included with this report is a letter from the applicants that describe how they
arrived at the proposed use. In summary, the applicants indicated that self-storage was the
preferred use because residential isn’t viable primarily due to Overpressure Zone A, commercial
uses like retail, office, or a restaurant are unlikely in that there are no other similar businesses
nearby, and most industrial uses would be problematic given the adjoining residential. In the
applicants’ letter they also mention that they will not pursue other uses allowed in the M zone.

The M zone requires a 20’ building setback from a residential zone, agricultural zone, or
residential use. The applicant is requesting that, through a development agreement, this 20’
setback be reduced to 0’ to eliminate a 20’ space that is not visible to the public and could create a
security concern for the neighboring residents. There are several self-storage facilities in the City
that adjoin residential where the City’s Board of Adjustment has approved variances to waive the
20’ setback requirement due to security concerns. A development agreement can be used to
accomplish the same outcome.

A recent variance example is the self-storage development at 5443 South 6055 West. The
buildings along the east side of the property adjacent to residential were built on the property line
and are 10’ tall.

The City has also submitted a General Plan/zone change application on the City-owned parcel to
the south for a General Plan designation of Light Manufacturing and M zoning. If the City’s
application is approved, the 20’ setback would not apply along the south property line.

Staff received a phone call and visit from a resident in opposition to this application. This resident
lives on the street east of the subject property and is concerned about the potential impact of the
proposed project on property values and views.

City staff has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for a 2.0-acre parcel located at
4063 South 7200 West. The property is currently zoned A (Agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre)
and the General Plan designation is Large Lot Residential (2 to 3 units/acre). The proposed zone
is M (Manufacturing) and the proposed General Plan designation is Light Manufacturing.

Surrounding zones include M to the south, R-1-10 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum
lot size 10,000 square feet) to the east, A to the north, and R-1-7 to the west in Magna.
Surrounding land uses include single family homes to the north and east, a Salt Lake County park
to the west and railroad tracks and industrial property to the south. The property is owned by

house larger animals.

Approximately the west 50% of the property falls within Overpressure Zone A with the remainder
of the property being within Overpressure Zone B. Overpressure Zone A is the innermost circle
(closest to Northrop Grumman) and prohibits residential use.

Staff is proposing these changes for several reasons. First, the outside storage of garbage cans is
not an allowed use in the A zone. Second, the housing of animals is still allowed in the M zone.

roughly 50% of this property. Fourth, a General Plan/zone change application was also submitted
on the property directly north for a General Plan designation of Light Manufacturing and M
zoning. If the application on the adjoining property is approved, the applicant intends to develop
storage units on the property. If the zoning on the subject property remains A, the storage units
will be required to meet a 20’ setback from the A zone and a 20’ height limitation at the minimum
setback. These requirements would not apply if the subject property is zoned M.

5431 & 5477 West Lampert Lane
M Zone (4.81 acres)

The applicant, Premier Truck Group, is requesting conditional use approval for outside storage of
heavy trucks, trailers and parts in conjunction with heavy truck service at 5431 & 5477 West
Lampert Lane. This property is zoned M, manufacturing and the West Valley City General Plan
designates this area as Light Manufacturing. The surrounding zone is Manufacturing on all sides.
The surrounding uses are similar trucking related businesses including Sprinter, Intermountain
Trailer and Freightliner.

Premier Truck operates a repair facility in the building to the north of this property and wish to
improve this area for customer trailer and heavy truck parking while their vehicles are being
serviced. The outside storage area would be used for storage of large truck parts like hoods,
bumpers and fenders. The storage lot will not be used for any tires, junk, salvage or wrecked
vehicles. There will also be no customer or employee parking stalls in this area.

Both lots will be completely paved with asphalt or concrete. There is existing fencing and slats
will be added to the north section of the fence and along both sides of the outside storage lot to
provide screening of the storage areas. Staff recommends that the screening be extended along
both sides for approximately 160’, which would be to the building edge of the adjacent lot to the
east.

Landscaping will be completed along the frontage of Lampert Lane. There must be a minimum of
5% landscaping for the site which can all be accommodated along the frontage. This landscaping
will consist of 1 tree for every 30’ of frontage as well as 50% live plant material coverage, not
including tree canopies. The area will also be bermed to provide additional screening. Since this is
a heavy truck and trailer storage lot only and there will be no building or standard vehicle parking
lot no additional landscaping will be required within the site.

Application #:         C-7-2024
Applicant:             Castlewood Development
Address:               2940 West 3650 South
Request:               Conditional use approval for a 330-unit apartment development
Property Size:         3.02 acres

The Planning Commission approved a conditional use permit for this project on June 8, 2022.
However, due to financing issues and other market conditions, the developer did not complete the
conditional use approval process with staff and did not begin the project. Since conditional use
permits automatically terminate within 12 months of Planning Commission approval when a
building permit has not been obtained and construction of at least the foundation has not been
completed, the applicant reapplied for the conditional use permit in May of 2023 and the Planning
Commission granted the new approval on May 24, 2023. However, the developer has still not
obtained a building permit or commenced construction. With expiration of the second conditional
use approval approaching, the applicant is seeking a third approval.

Castlewood Development is requesting conditional use approval to redevelop 3.02 acres at 2940
West 3650 South into a 330-unit, 2 to 7 story, Class A apartment development. The proposed unit
mix for the project is 42 studio units, 201 one-bedroom units, and 87 two-bedroom units. The
majority of the property (2.94 acres) is zoned CC (City Center) and the balance (0.08 acres) is
zoned R-2-8 (Two Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet). The entire
building is within the CC zone. The property is designated as very high density residential (over
20 units/acre) or office for most of the property and promenade/plaza for the remaining north
portion in the Fairbourne Station Vision, which is part of the City’s General Plan. The property is
currently vacant as the office development previously on the property was demolished in 2014.

According to the Public Works Department, 3650 South will be improved between 3200 West
and 2700 West likely in 2025. Planned improvements include consistent curb, gutter, and
sidewalk along both sides of the street as well as a left turn lane in the middle of the street. Once
these improvements are made, no on-street parking will be allowed.

Project Review
The first section below outlines the purposes in the CC zone and how this development
accomplishes these purposes. The second section addresses the main ordinance standards (it is not
a comprehensive list) applicable to this development.

City Center Zone Purposes
The following is a list of purposes from the CC Zone (underlined) as well as a brief description of
how the apartment development meets the stated purpose:

              Create a recognizable center or downtown for West Valley City. At 7 stories, this
           apartment development will add to the unique, urban environment in Fairbourne
           Station.
               Encourage and direct development that supports transit. High density residential
            development is one of the best land uses for supporting transit.
             Encourage infill and redevelopment near the future transit station by City Hall. The
            apartment building replaces an office development demolished in 2014 and is
            approximately 650’ from the West Valley Central TRAX station and 400’ from the

Parking
The parking ordinance allows the Planning Commission to reduce the parking standard for multi-
family residential from 2 spaces per unit to 1.3 spaces per unit for studio units, 1.5 spaces per unit
for one-bedroom units, and 1.75 spaces per unit for two-bedroom units if the Commission finds
that the reduction is not detrimental to the surrounding area and will enhance the proposed
development. The CC Zone also allows a 10% reduction to parking requirements. The applicant
has submitted the attached parking study in the form of a memo from Hales Engineering to
support the proposed amount of parking.

Amenities
For a project of this size, the ordinance requires a minimum of five amenities from the list in the
ordinance with at least three of the five being major amenities. This project has six major
amenities (outdoor swimming pool, courtyards, clubhouse/community room, rooftop patio, fitness
room, and community game room/recreation room) and seven minor amenities (outdoor fire
pit/fireplace, hot tub, building lobby, package locker system, indoor bicycle storage, electric
vehicle charging stations, and outdoor covered pavilion with tables and a grill).

Unit Sizes
The ordinance requires a minimum of 400 square feet for studio units, 600 square feet for 1-
bedroom units, and 850 square feet for 2-bedroom units. The proposed minimum unit sizes are
520 square feet for studio units, 604 square feet for 1-bedroom units, and 930 square feet for 2-
bedroom units. Given the mix of unit layouts, many units will exceed these proposed minimums.

Landscaping and Recreation Space
The ordinance requires a minimum of 10% of the site area as landscaping and a minimum of 40%
of the site area as landscaping and recreation space. The landscaping percentage for this project is
15.0% and the combined landscaping and recreation space percentage is 54.8%.

Architecture
After reviewing the elevations and renderings, staff noted to the applicant that the parapet along
the south elevation needs more variation. The applicant has acknowledged this deficiency and
will have the architect make the correction. Once this comment is addressed, the elevations and
renderings will meet or exceed the City’s design standards.

Other Commitments

In addition to addressing the architectural comment mentioned above, the applicant has
committed to the following improvements:
             Fencing will be installed around all ground floor patios and will include a gate to
            provide residents with private outdoor space. The applicant is working to determine
            the fence material.
             The existing chain link fence along the west side of the property shall be replaced
            with a 6’ vinyl fence.
             The applicant will work with the City’s Parks Department to align the grade of the
            site with that of the adjoining park. This may require some regrading on the City’s
            park property, which the applicant has indicated she is willing to do along with any
            repair of landscaping disturbed because of the regrading.
             A sidewalk connection will be provided on the east side of the property to the
            sidewalk running along the north side of the adjoining library property.
             To avoid having pets relieve themselves on the City’s promenade park, the
            applicant has agreed, at the City’s Parks Department suggestion, to include a pet relief
            area with artificial turf, a garbage can and a plastic bag dispenser. This will hopefully
            help prevent damage to the sod in the park as well as constant waste pickup.

The applicant also secured an easement from the adjoining condo association to the west to allow
access to 3000 West.
