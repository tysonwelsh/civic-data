        The purpose of the work session was to receive presentations/discuss the Fiscal Year 2024-2025 budget specific to
proposed arts grant awards, salary schedules, and schedule A-1; discuss Council business; and hear Council comments.

Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Budget
          Proposed Arts Grant Awards: Each year the Ogden City Council appropriates grant funding to support various local arts
organizations and programs. The Ogden Arts Advisory Committee and Administration review each application and provide
recommendations to the Council. The Fiscal Year 2025 budget for arts grants is expected to be $150,000. This year Ogden City
Arts received 31 applications requesting a total of $248,500 in funding. Of this, 11 applications applied for a total of $130,000 in
general operating support; 20 applicants applied for a total of $118,500 in project support.
          Arts Administrator Buckley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the criteria used by the Arts
Advisory Committee to evaluate and score each arts grant application, provide a two-year comparison of applications received and
funded, and to list the projects and programs for which the Arts Advisory Committee has recommended funding. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council centered on the differences between general support grants and project support grants, as
well as the methodology used to determine the percentage of a project or program an arts grant should fund. Chair Richey
concluded a public hearing has been scheduled for August 6, 2024 regarding this item; the Council can take an action on the
proposed resolution supporting grant funding recommendations following the closure of the public hearing.
          Proposed Salary Schedules: Council Deputy Director Symes explained that tonight is an opportunity for the Council to
continue discussions of the proposed Salary Schedules. The Council has heard from the City’s Administration about proposals
from the Compensation Study as well as from the consultant who performed the work. Now that the Compensation Study has been
completed, the Salary Schedules have been updated to include the recommendations from the study. The Salary Schedules set
salary ranges for Elected Officials, Non-merit Special Employees, Merit Employees, and Sworn Fire and Police Employees. There
is no proposal to increase elected officials’ salaries in the Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Final Budget. Within the Fiscal Year 2024-2025
budget, Administration has set aside a total of $4,657,950 for compensation and benefits adjustments. This includes $3,131,275 in
the General Fund and $1,526,675 in all other funds. The McGrath study is intended to provide recommendations on all salary
ranges and position and/or title changes needed to provide competitive wages for city employees. Full implementation would
require an additional $87,350 over all funds with $85,400 needed in the City’s General Fund. If approved by the Council, this
change would be reflected in a final Schedule A-1 adjustment with the final budget adoption.
          Proposed Schedule A-1: Comptroller Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the adjustments
included in the Mayor’s proposed Schedule A-1; the majority of the changes to the General Fund include adjustments responsive
to the recommendations of the McGrath study relative to position titles and compensation adjustments; the net increase of these
adjustments is $85,000. Additional adjustments in other funds in the City relate to allocating additional funding for the Home
Exterior Loan Program (HELP), from the Water and Sewer Utility Funds, recognizing various state and federal grants, and
additional changes the Council has discussed. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the
meeting.
          The Council discussed items listed in Schedule A-1, after which Council member Choberka inquired as to what will
become of any budget surplus dollars available at the close of the current fiscal year. Mr. Sorensen stated that the Fiscal Year
2023-2024 budget will expire, and any unspent dollars will flow into the City’s fund balance; some may be restricted, but
unrestricted funds will be available to appropriate to other areas of the General Fund. It will be necessary to evaluate whether any
increase in the fund balance will push the total of the fund over the 35 percent allowed by State Law, but this information will not
be finalized until the conclusion of the audit of the current Fiscal Year budget.
          Chair Richey stated that items yet to be determined by the Council include the final property tax rate and any additional
compensation adjustments for City employees; this led to discussion of whether to consider additional cost of living adjustments
(COLAs) for City employees, the proposed new positions, and options for reducing the workload of certain Departments and
positions in the City.

Council Business
         Council staff reviewed the Council calendar for the remainder of the month of July.
         Council member Hyer reported that the Central Weber Sewer District is considering a tax increase and it is required by
State Law that any city that is part of a special service district notice a proposed tax increase by the district. Council Executive
Director Eller-Smith noted Council staff will work with the District to formulate an appropriate notice that can be published on the
City’s website as well as discussed during an upcoming business meeting.


         The meeting adjourned at 4:40 p.m.
