   2. ZONING REGULATION AMENDMENT (ZRA) (Public Hearing) Legislative

      Consider a request regarding proposed changes to the City Code regarding water usage, connections,
      conservation, landscaping, and other procedural changes consistent with implementing the water
      provisions. Proposed City Code changes include changes to Title 8, Chapter 1, regarding water
      connections and water waste; Title 10, Chapter 1 regarding water acknowledgment statements, permit
      process, vesting, and requirement of culinary water "will serve" letters; Title 10 Chapter 2 definitions;
      Title 10, Chapters 4, 5, 7, 8, 11, and 17 regarding landscaping and other conservation measures, certain
      land uses, special standards for some uses, and infrastructure standards; Title 10, Chapter 23 regarding
      landscaping provisions; and Title 10, Chapter 25 regarding subdivision requirements and processes.
      2022-ZRA-002 (Staff – Scott Taylor)

      Scott Taylor presented the following:

      Scott Taylor – We had the discussion last Thursday with the City Council; I’m just going to hit the
      highlights for the ones who weren’t there. Scott described the presentation included at the end of these
      minutes, which was also presented at the Joint Work Meeting on June 9, 2022. We have worked a lot
      with the other communities in the area, we feel like every community needs to be on board with these
      changes. We worked a lot with the Washington County Water Conservancy District and the other
      regional municipalities. Other communities have made little changes in what they have adopted, but the
      meat and potatoes of what has been adopted by other communities is all about the same. Santa Clara,
      Ivins, and Washington have all adopted this ordinance with slight changes. We held four different
      stakeholder meetings with carwashes, homeowners’ associations, The Chamber of Commerce and Home
      Builders and Institutional users just to get some feedback on how these proposed ordinance changes
      would affect their businesses. Scott described the proposed changes to Title 10 and Title 8.

      Commissioner Andrus – What exactly counts as a shade tree?

      Jami Bracken – There will be a list of appropriate shade trees. They will be defined in the ordinance,
      appropriate types of trees that will be approved by the district, and they will be approved by the caliper.

      Scott Taylor – In nonresidential zones they will not be allowed to have turfgrass unless it is for active
      recreation areas. They will submit their landscape plans with their Site Plan Reviews and they will need
      to be prepared by a professional landscape architect. Car washes will be limited to 35 gallons per
      vehicle of fresh water, with limit reduced to 25 gallons per car in 5 years. We’re not requiring recycling,
      we are saying however you choose to get the 35 gallons per vehicle that’s fine, we’re not requiring that
      you recycle. We will require is that the facility is sized and plumbed so that in the future they can
      recycle. Our thought on that is that technology improves and we’re certain in the future with changes in

      technology and the economics and the price of sewer treatment, it will be cost effective to recycle. If the
      facility is already plumbed for it, it will be easy to retrofit and have the mechanics so they can recycle.

      Commissioner West – These are just for new car washes? Existing wouldn’t be affected?

      Jami Bracken – Under the existing code, recycling is required right now. They already have systems.
      With the new car washes we are not going to require it because we are going to put a gallon limit per
      car.

      Scott Taylor - Another change would be the process of development approval. The water is currently
      guaranteed at the issuance of building permit when the WCWCD fee is paid. If you purchase a property
      doesn’t mean there is a guarantee of water. After the amended ordinance water will be guaranteed after
      the City approves the construction drawings, then the WCWCD will issue a will serve letter. They will
      pay the impact fees for the water of the subdivision. They will only have 1 year to build the subdivision.
      The developer will only submit what he can build within a year, and they won’t tie up the water.

      Commissioner West – If he sells that property within the year, does that start over?

      Jami Bracken – The water right will run with the property. If they buy the property and want to do
      something different then that will serve will go away and they will start the process over. If they want to
      keep that approval then they will still need to finish within the year. The developers will have a choice
      they can put in the infrastructure before they record the plat or record the plat and then put it in within a
      year. Either way they have to get it done to get that water right.

      Pro tem Chair Kemp – What happens if they don’t finish in a year?

      Jami Bracken – Worst case scenario, if the subdivision comes in they post a bond to complete the
      infrastructure, then they get half way through and that year expires. There is a provision for a one-time
      extension through the Community Development Director. If they just ignore it and walk away or if they
      go bankrupt the City has two choices, they can vacate the plat and notify the district that those water will
      serve letters are now available again. Or they can call the bond and finish the infrastructure themselves.

      Commissioner Fisher – And that extension by the Community Development Director, is that limited to a
      certain amount of time?

      Jami Bracken – 6 months.

      Commissioner Fisher – But you get discretion, 1 to 6 months?

      Jami Bracken – Yes.

      Commissioner Fisher – With the PD zone change you have to bring in the preliminary plat, right? So,
      all of this would be required at that zone change.

      Jami Bracken – The zone change wouldn’t take effect until the very end. The adoption date and the
      effective date are two different things.

      Commissioner Fisher – So at the end of the year, if they didn’t get it, their rights would pass down?

                               Source Sizing Standard
• In the past, the State DDW set the Source Sizing Standard (the amount of water required to provide
  adequate water service to each Equivalent Residential Connection (ERC)).

• The Source Size Standard was 0.89 acre-ft per ERC

• Recently the DDW has developed a method where each community is able to set their own Source Size
  Standard, based on at least three years of historical data and/or an engineering study.

• Currently, the most recent 3-yr data shows Source Size Standard at about 0.78 AF/ERC.

                                   • A lower source size standard will enable us to “stretch” or existing
                                     water resources.

                                   • A regional goal is to reduce our Source Size Standard to 0.58
                                     AF/ERC through conservation measures.

                        Water Conservation Ordinances
• Regional Cities, working with the WCWCD have drafted model Water Conservation Ordinance.

• The ordinance addresses building standards and landscape standards.

• Each community has taken the draft ordinance and adjusted to meet their needs.

• Santa Clara, Ivins, and Washington have all adopted the ordinances, with slight changes.

• Rather than adopt a “stand alone” water conservation ordinance, St. George is proposing amendments
  to it’s existing zoning ordinances to minimize conflicting ordinances.

                                   • We have held four different Stakeholder Meetings for Car Washes,
                                     Homeowners Associations, Chamber of Commerce / Homebuilders,
                                     and Institutional Users to present the proposed ordinance and
                                     receive feedback from the various users.

             Water Conservation- Construction Standards

•   Energy Star Appliances- All new construction
•   WaterSense Labelled Plumbing Fixtures- All new construction
•   Hot Water Recirculation of other means of providing hot water within 0.6 gallons
•   Each Unit Individually Metered if ground-level access

             Water Conservation- Landscape Standards

• Turfgrass in SF Residential zones limited to 8% of total lot area, with 1,500 SF Max. cap
• Swimming pools count as landscaped areas
• Turfgrass not allowed in park strips, landscape areas less that 8’ in width, or landscaped
  areas that are on slopes greater than 15%.
