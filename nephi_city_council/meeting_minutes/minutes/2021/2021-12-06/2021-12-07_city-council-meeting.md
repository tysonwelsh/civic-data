NEPHI LIONS CLUB PRESENTED CONTRIBUTION FOR PARK BENCH AND FOUNTAIN:

   Donald Eyre Jr. and Robert Painter representing the Nephi Lion’s Club presented the council with a
check for $7,590.00 to help pay for a bench at Canyon View Park and a drinking fountain at Pioneer
Park. The council and Parks Superintendent Gerratt Bethers expressed their appreciation to the Lion’s
Club for the contribution and their service to the community. Mr. Eyre and Mr. Painter thanked the
council for letting them use the park for the Chuck Wagon Breakfast and for supporting the 911
Commemoration Program.

ONE-TIME GRANT OF $10,000 PRESENTED TO GREATER NEPHI CHAMBER OF COMMERCE:

   Abby Ivory, President of the Greater Nephi Chamber of Commerce, accepted a $10,000 one-time
grant check from the city council. The chamber has been reorganized and have hired Kerry Anderson as
an executive director to help the chamber succeed and provide valuable services to area businesses.
Ivory reported that the membership is growing and that the chamber had held a kick-off meeting last
Thursday that was highly successful. Ivory introduced MaKenzie Memmott, Vice President, and Kerry
Anderson, Executive Director.

HUMPHREY PROPERTY MOVED TO LEVEL ONE ON THE ANNEXATION AREA MAP:

  City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council that on September 21st, the council
elected to change the annexation level on a property owned by Joe Humphrey from a level 3 to a level 2
within the annexation area boundary. On the October 26th Work Session, the council reviewed the
Level 2 analysis for this property to potentially classify it as a Level 1.
   The Humphrey property directly adjacent to Highway 132 had already been made a Level 1. Mr.
Humphrey had requested to expand that designation to the full property. He had indicated that he
would like to create several commercial pads on the property. The area would be considered
contiguous with the city limits since the county road shed is in the city directly across the highway.
    The Level 2 analysis showed that, despite a reduction in agricultural land and being adjacent to a
residential area, the change to a Level 1 could be warranted. The change would be warranted because it
is an identified use in the city’s general plan, it complements the adjacent highway, and is in line with
the economic growth encouraged by the city’s general plan.
  Mr. Atkinson advised the council that based on the Level 2 analysis, staff recommends that the whole
parcel be classified as Level 1 in the annexation area map and thus, eligible for an annexation petition.
    Councilor Worwood moved to accept the recommendation and to change the Humphrey property
west of the city to a Level 1 on the Annexation Area Map. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The
motion carried on a unanimous vote.

SAFARI MOTEL AND MOTE 6 PROBATION AGREEMENTS APPROVED:

  City Attorney Kasey Wright presented two probation agreements between the City and Safari Motel
and Motel 6. The two motels have been operating illegally by allowing long-term stays more than 30
consecutive days which violates City code. The agreement spells out terms and conditions including not
allowing long-term stays, keeping easily accessible records of all individuals who stay and the date the
individual checked in and out of the motel, and that law enforcement will be allowed to inspect the
records upon request.
  Councilor Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the agreement with Safari Motel. Councilor
Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
   Councilor Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the agreement with Motel 6. Councilor
Memmott seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

   Councilor Ostler echoed all that had been said and reported that fire mitigation measures were being
done around the Old Pinery.
