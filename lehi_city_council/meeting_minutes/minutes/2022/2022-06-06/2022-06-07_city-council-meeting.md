1. Welcome and Opening Comment
   Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present. Councilor
   Condie and Albrecht were attending virtually. Councilor Southwick offered the opening
   comment.

2. Discussion and Presentation Items
   2.a. Round-Up Stock Parade Discussion- Civic Improvement Association
   John Lewis, representing the Civic Improvement Association, stated that in celebration of the
   85th Round-Up Rodeo, they would like to add a horse drive at the beginning of the stock parade.
   He said that it would be similar to what they did for the 75th celebration with the long-horn
   cattle. He stated that they believe it will be safe and they are insured. The committee wanted to
   be transparent with the Council so that they understood what was happening.

    2.b. COPS Grant Discussion- Police Department
    Chief Darren Paul informed the Council that the federal government offers these grants for
    new Officers. If they are awarded the grant, then it only covers the salary and benefits for 3
    years, but not the equipment that needed for the position. If they were awarded the grant, then
    they would need to make a budget amendment. The Council reached consensus that they would
    like to proceed with the grant application.

    2c. Partnership with Off-Duty Management Discussion- Police Department
    Off-Duty Management is a private company that works with Police Officers to schedule evens.
    The get requests to have officers at Thanksgiving Point or other venues. Right now an officer
    manages the schedule and everything that goes into that. Off-Duty would take care of all the
    scheduling and free up personnel.

   2d. Fiber Network Update
   Shaye Ruitenbeek gave an overview of the timeline for the project. They are working on the
   backbone and design. They plan to have a preregistration link available that can help dictate
   where to begin with construction. They are also working on a Q&A for the website. They help
   to begin connecting people by spring of 2023.

   2e. Parks Update and Discussion
   Steve Marchbanks, Parks Manager, gave a presentation similar to what was done at the Budget
   Retreat in February. He said that they do have more information and updated numbers which
   may help determine the direction of park construction.

   He said that the latest bids show Mellor-Rhodes Park will cost about $34 million to construct.
   That doesn’t include the cost of infill dirt which could be around $5 million. He explained that
   they could save money by slowly adding infill dirt as it’s available. Mr. Marchbanks showed
   the high level concept plan for Family Park, and Dry Creek Lake.

   There was discussion about how to proceed with the limited funds available. Councilor Koivisto
   was prepared to wait on Mellor-Rhodes so they can save money on the infill. She understands
   that there are concerns with water to fill the reservoir at Dry Creek, so her priority would be to
   Family Park. Lorin Powell stated that they need to conduct testing on the reservoir. They will
   lose the grant money for Dry Creek, but they could apply for other grants in the future.

   There is 7 million allocated for all abilities park and 10 million for park projects in the 2023
   budget. Councilors Hancock and Southwick agreed that he would like to use as much money as
   they can right now toward Family Park while they wait for the infill for Mellor-Rhodes and the
   testing on Dry Creek. There was discussion about needing to complete 600 East Road for
   Family Park.

   Councilor Hancock would like to see the engineering design and phasing approach for Family
   Park.

   Mayor Johnson would like to put a call out to get fill dirt brought in for Mellor-Rhodes. They
   need about 100,000 cubic yards.

   Councilor Condie stated that he was frustrated because he didn’t understand that Dry Creek’s
   reservoir was primarily constructed for flood mitigation and then 2nd for PI. It doesn’t make
   sense to put amenities at Dry Creek if the water won’t be there. He said they allocated $5 million
   in the budget for Mellor-Rhodes if the PARC tax passed.

   2f.) PARC Fund Recommendations and Discussion- PARC Committee and Lehi City Staff
    Nicole Kunze, PARC Committee Chair presented the Committee’s findings to the Council.

   McKay Christensen and Brent Lange spoke in favor of allocating funds to Thanksgiving Point.

   Collin Baker, PARC Committee members, also offered his perspective on their
   recommendations.
