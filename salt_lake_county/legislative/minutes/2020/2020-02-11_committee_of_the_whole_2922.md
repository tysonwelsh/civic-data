3.4 Legislative Update 20-0142
Presenter: Kara Trevino (Approx. 1 hr.)
Discussion/Direction
Ms. Kara Trevino ,  Intergovernmental Relations Manager, Council Office, 
reviewed the following legislative bills and proposals.
- HB 305 Impact Fee Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill targets the Olympia Hills development and only applies to 
counties of the first class.
Ms. Darcy Goddard , Deputy District Attorney, stated this bill allows municipalities 
adjacent to a development to impose an impact fee and the primary municipality could not 
issue permits until the neighboring impact fees were paid.
A motion was made by Council Member Newton, seconded by Council Member DeBry, 
to oppose HB 305.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-HB 271 Firearm Preemption Amendment
Ms. Trevino stated this bill gets to regulate everything the County does with guns.  The 
bill is also highly punitive.  
Ms. Goddard stated the County is a political subdivision of the state, so it feels it can 
regulate the County.
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated this bill is in response to the County requiring 
background checks for any guns sold in County facilities.  It was implemented without a 
lot of fanfare or impact.
Mr. Sim Gill , District Attorney, stated the Mayor followed the law in implementing this 
policy.  The policy does not violate any legal state or federal law.  It was solely within her 
discretionary power.
Mr. Ralph Chamness , Deputy District Attorney, stated the sponsor would not be trying 
to do this if they thought there was a violation by the Mayor.  The fact that they are trying 
to end this means they understand the Mayor acted within her rights.
Page 3 of 13

A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member 
Ghorbani, to oppose HB 271.  
A substitute motion was made by Council Member DeBry, seconded by Council Member 
Newton, to remain neutral on HB 271, monitor the bill, and work with the sponsor to 
amend the “remove from office” language.  The motion failed 4 to 5.
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member 
Ghorbani, to oppose HB 271.  The motion passed 5 to 4.
-HB 261  Eminent Domain Revisions
Ms. Trevino stated this bill would not allow the County to use eminent domain without 
justifying it as necessary for public use.  Eminent domain could not be used for 
recreational purposes.
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member 
Bradshaw, to oppose HB 261.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-HB 202 Local Government Nuisance Ordinance Reform
Ms. Goddard stated this bill limits the circumstances under which a municipality or 
County may impose a criminal penalty for violation of an ordinance.
Ms. Trevino stated she recommended opposing this bill as written but letting the lobby 
teamwork with the sponsor.
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member Granato, 
to oppose HB 202 as written, but to allow the lobbying team to work with the sponsor on 
language.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-HB 226 Storm Water Permits
Ms. Trevino stated this bill prohibits government agencies from imposing storm water 
fees.
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member 
Bradshaw, to oppose HB 226.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.

-SB 48 Corrections Officer Certification Pilot Extension
Ms. Trevino stated this bill extends the sunset date to 2027 for the pilot program allowing 
19-year-olds to become corrections officers.
A motion was made by Council Member DeBry, seconded by Council Member Jensen, to 
support SB 48.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-HB 206 Bail and Pretrial Release Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill makes changes to provisions relating to bail.
Mr. William Carlson , Chief Policy Advisor, District Attorney ’s Office, stated this bill 
will address when an arrest is made, the terms of release and what to do with cash bail that 
has been forfeited.
Mr. Richard Mauro , Executive Director, Legal Defender Association, stated his 
organization is mostly in favor of this bill.
Ms. Erin Litvack , Deputy Mayor of County Services, stated CJAC did an analysis of 
the County ’s 2019 jail data, and the County could expect to see up to a 6 percent 
reduction annually in the number of bookings at the jail.  
Mr. Gill stated he is supportive of this bill.  This documentation is already being done in 
Salt Lake County.  This is codification of best practices.
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member 
Ghorbani, to support HB 206.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-HB 265 Government Records Transparency Act
Ms. Trevino stated this bill is troublesome in what it asks state and local governments to 
do in tracking personal identifying information.  
Ms. Goddard stated it may not be possible to comply with this law.
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member 
Ghorbani, to oppose HB 265.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-HB 273  Property Rights Ombudsman Amendments

Ms. Trevino stated the County, Utah Association of Counties, and the Utah League of 
Cities and Towns opposed this bill.
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member 
Bradshaw, to oppose HB 273.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-HB 288 Prosecutor Data Collection Amendments
Ms. Goddard stated many legal agencies worked with the sponsor of this bill.  It collects 
data from prosecutors regarding race, gender and socio -economic status as it relates to 
sentences.  Most offices already collect this information.  The County needs to make clear 
to the sponsor that it needs to be aggregate data without individual names.
Mr. Gill stated he wanted to be sure this did not turn into an unfunded mandate that the 
County would have to pay for. 
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member 
Bradshaw, to support HB 288 with amendments.  The motion passed by a unanimous 
vote.
-HB 245 Dogs in Watershed Areas
Mr. Gary Edwards , Director, Salt Lake County Health Department, stated dogs are 
already allowed in the canyon if they do not get out of the car.  Some people have been 
cited for having dogs in cars.  As this bill is written, that cannot happen.
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member 
Granato, to support HB 245.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-HB 176  Vehicle Emissions Reduction Program
Ms. Trevino stated this bill failed in the legislature last year.  It is known as “cash for 
clunkers.”
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member DeBry, to 
support HB 176.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-HB 118   Retail Tobacco Specialty Business Amendments
Mr. Edwards stated this bill works to accomplish things the Board of Health wants, 
including limiting flavored products to tobacco specialty shops.

A motion was made by Council Member DeBry, seconded by Council Member Jensen, to 
support HB 118.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-SB 40  Youth Electronic Cigarette, Marijuana, and Other Drug Prevention Program
Mr. Edwards stated this bill creates a mechanism to distribute funds for educational 
efforts in communities. 
A motion was made by Council Member DeBry, seconded by Council Member Jensen, to 
support SB 40.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-HB 268  Property Tax Notice Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill allows a person to designate an additional person or address 
to receive a property tax or privilege tax notice.  This would cost the County 
approximately $100,000 in programming costs.
Mr. Scott Tingley , County Auditor, stated his office already does this for a handful of 
property owners.  However, offering it to everyone would require substantial changes to 
the property tax system.  He is okay with this being in electronic formate only.
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member 
Bradshaw, to oppose HB 268 as written, but to support it with electronic notifications 
only.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
-SB 56 Public Safety and Firefighter Tier II Retirement Enhancements.
Ms. Trevino stated this is optional for the County.  It amends provisions relating to the 
New Public Safety and Firefighter Tier II Contributory Retirement System.
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member 
Bradshaw, to support SB 56.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
