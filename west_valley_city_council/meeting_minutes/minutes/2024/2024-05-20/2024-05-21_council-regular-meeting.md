COMMENT PERIOD
A.  PUBLIC COMMENTS
    Wendy Finau introduced herself as a small business owner, writer, single mom, and
    survivor of suicide loss. She explained that she lost her mother to suicide when she
    was nine years old and now volunteers as a suicide prevention advocate within the
    Granite School District. Wendy advocated for those who feel voiceless, like her
    mother, and emphasized the importance of balancing mental and physical health.
    She shared her personal journey of healing and how it taught her this balance.
    Wendy encouraged students to prioritize self-care and find inspiration, noting that
    while many students are inspired by sports, others find deep inspiration in the arts,
    particularly theater. Wendy highlighted the values imparted by theater, such as
    inclusion, cooperation, and responsibility. She referenced Rachel Harry, a
    distinguished theater educator and 2017 Tony Award winner, who emphasized the
    life lessons taught through theater. Wendy urged the Council to consider the
    importance of preserving the arts in their community. She pointed out that Sandy
    City's Hale Center Theater generated significant economic benefits and suggested
    that West Valley City could also create a cultural hub, inspiring its residents and
    boosting the local economy.

       Zachary Garrett, a junior at Hunter High School in West Valley City, expressed his
       concerns about the closing of the West Valley Performing Arts Center. He shared
       that last summer he participated in the Youth Theater Arts summer camp at the
       center, an experience that initially he resisted but ultimately found transformative.
       This camp helped him prepare for his auditions at Hunter High, provided him with
       valuable mentorship, and boosted his confidence. Zachary emphasized that the
       camp's impact was significant enough that he had already enrolled for the upcoming
       summer, along with some friends. He praised the beautiful, historic building and its
       educational facilities, arguing that closing it would be detrimental to the
       community, particularly for students like him who seek local theater education
       opportunities. He highlighted the importance of keeping the center open to provide
       a local venue for growth, development, and the pursuit of their passions.
       Additionally, Zachary mentioned being inspired as an audience member at the
       center's shows and expressed his aspiration to perform on the same stage one day.
       He concluded by urging the Council to make the right decision and keep the West
       Valley Performing Arts Center open, noting its role in inspiring and providing
       opportunities to the community.

       Laura Leavitt, the school director at American Preparatory Academy, West Valley,
       spoke about her deep connection with the West Valley Performing Arts Center. She
       shared her personal history with the building, beginning from her youth when her
       family moved to Utah and regularly visited the center, which she described as a

magical place. She expressed her sadness when it closed and her delight when West
Valley City created the new Performing Arts Center. Laura emphasized the
significant role West Valley City played in providing valuable resources for its
citizens, highlighting the center as one of those contributions. She pointed out that

Sawyer Dunroe shared her experience as a seventh grader given the opportunity to
intern on the show "Shakespeare in Love" at the West Valley Performing Arts
Center in February. For three months, she dedicated every available hour to the
center, despite a busy schedule, which included being on a swim team, playing
water polo, taking dance and acting classes, learning to play drums, and being a
vocalist in a rock band. Sawyer expressed her love for theater, emphasizing how it
builds community, fosters friendships, and teaches essential skills like public
speaking. She highlighted that although she had performed at various theaters, the
West Valley Theater was her favorite due to the inclusive and respectful
environment provided by the director, producer, performers, and other
professionals. She recounted spending over 40 hours at the theater during her spring
break, enjoying every moment and learning new skills daily, particularly in theater
technology. Sawyer shared how she assisted the sound engineer and helped run
sound during the actual show, contributing to the production team and performers
to create something beautiful. Sawyer concluded by expressing her pride and
gratitude for the opportunity, noting that none of this would have been possible
without the West Valley Performing Arts Center.

Shelley Pace from District 3 implored the Council to protect the West Valley
Performing Arts Center. She shared her first memory of West Valley City, which
involved visiting the beautiful theater about 20 years ago, sparking her love for
theater. When the theater reopened under city management, she felt immense pride
in her city's investment in the arts. Shelley expressed her admiration for the high-
quality productions at the center, stating that she is a proud season ticket holder and
monthly donor. She cited a city audit that praised the performing arts center for
providing the highest quality community theater in Utah and noted that it was

scheduled for a major overhaul to ensure its longevity. She emphasized the
importance of the strategic plan for West Valley Arts, highlighting the long-term
benefits of building and maintaining such a program. She mentioned the
community's remarkable willingness to support the theater through funding and
volunteer work, which gave her hope that the center's future could be secured.
Shelley also shared her personal safety concerns, recounting incidents of break-ins,
car crashes, and shootings in her neighborhood. Despite these challenges, attending
events at the arts center gave her a sense of hope and belief in the community's

city and her willingness to accept certain inconveniences for the greater good.
However, she passionately urged the preservation of the theater, which gives her
hope, strength, and a belief in her ability to contribute positively to her community.

Walt Sorenson, a lifelong resident of West Valley City from the Highbury
neighborhood, voiced his opposition to the proposed GPZ-5-2023 surf park in
Highbury. He characterized speculative developments like the surf park as
detrimental, citing examples of similar projects across the West that have failed to
benefit local communities. He argued that the developer's projections and impact
studies for the surf park were flawed, potentially leaving the city with an empty
venue. Walt contrasted the surf park proposal with the efforts to save the West
Valley Performing Arts Center, which he described as a positive development. He
emphasized the importance of the performing arts center as a foundation for the
city's economic value and community engagement. Walt asserted that strong
anchors like the arts center are essential for attracting and retaining residents, and
for fostering economic development. He acknowledged the challenges in
preserving the performing arts center but called for collective effort to find
solutions. He urged the city to tackle immediate problems while setting the stage
for long-term success. Walt appealed to the mayor and the city council to
demonstrate pride, unity, and progress by supporting the arts center. He concluded
by asking the city to rally behind the cause and preserve what he considers a jewel
of West Valley City.

intention to cause offense. He admitted that he would avoid such terminology in
the future and expressed a willingness to listen to others and perhaps gain more
cultural sensitivity. Despite his apology, he also conveyed a sense of bewilderment

Randy Lucero spoke about noise issues caused by Western Gardens' events, which
have been disturbing the peace in his neighborhood near 4100 South and 4000
West. Despite wearing hearing aids, Randy described how he had to resort to
earplugs due to the loud noise late at night, particularly on Saturday evenings. He
mentioned that the noise starts around 7-8 PM and continues until 10 PM,
disrupting the tranquility of the community. Randy expressed frustration that his
complaints to West Valley officials had not resulted in any action, even though the
noise was loud enough to be heard blocks away and vibrated the windows of his
house. He highlighted the discrepancy between state and city noise ordinances and
his difficulty in finding a resolution. Despite his concerns about the noise, Randy
reminisced about his early memories of West Valley City, recalling its
transformation from dry farms to the developed community it is today. He
concluded his remarks by reaffirming his fondness for West Valley City and
thanking the council for their time.

Sarah King Flinton, a social worker specializing in at-risk adolescents in West
Valley City, emphasized the profound impact of community theater on mental
health. She highlighted that traditional clinical office visits often fail to effectively
engage teenagers and adults struggling with depression, anxiety, ADHD,
behavioral challenges, and suicidal ideation. Sarah cited numerous research studies
that support the therapeutic benefits of participatory theater. Sarah shared personal
anecdotes from her clients who participated in community theater, recounting their
significant positive transformations. She stressed the importance of maintaining
community theater in West Valley City, especially in a state where suicide is the
leading cause of death for individuals aged 10 to 24. Sarah referenced research from
the University of Pennsylvania, which found that arts and culture, particularly in
diverse communities, significantly improve health, safety, and well-being. Benefits
included reductions in child abuse, obesity, and serious crime rates, as well as
improvements in academic performance. Sarah concluded by emphasizing that
community theater is more than just entertainment; it is a vital tool for resilience,
healing, and positive social change. She urged the council to prioritize supporting
the West Valley Performing Arts Center, as it plays a crucial role in fostering a
healthier, safer, and more inclusive community.

Scott Bohn expressed concern regarding traffic safety in his neighborhood. He
reported that despite submitting a request for a traffic and speed survey in March,
the survey has not yet been conducted. Scott highlighted the increased traffic
congestion on his street, due to the opening of an auto mall. This has led to
numerous cars parking on the street, creating hazardous conditions. Scott recounted

a recent incident where he narrowly avoided hitting a child who ran into the street
chasing a ball. Despite driving under the speed limit, he encountered speeding
vehicles and individuals racing minibikes on the street. Scott pleaded with the
council to expedite the traffic survey process to address the safety concerns in his

Eddie Garcia expressed his strong opposition to the closure of the West Valley
Performing Arts Center. He shared deeply personal reasons for his protest, stating
that the theater had been instrumental in helping him through difficult times. Eddie
recounted losing two close friends to car accidents and feeling on the verge of
suicide. However, attending shows at the Performing Arts Center with his
stepmother provided him with solace and support during those dark moments.
Eddie emphasized the profound impact that theater has on people, especially in
providing comfort and teaching valuable lessons that cannot be easily conveyed
through other means.

Eve Garcia emphasized the significant impact of the West Valley Performing Arts
Center on local businesses. She highlighted conversations she had with business
owners while putting up posters for an event at the theater. According to Eve, these
discussions revealed that when people attend shows at the theater, they also visit
nearby restaurants, contributing to the local economy. Eve stressed the importance
of the theater as a reason for people to come to West Valley, which ultimately
translates into profits for businesses in the area. She expressed her intention to
continue these conversations with business owners to understand the extent of the
theater's impact on their bottom line. In conclusion, Eve underscored the
importance of the theater to the community and pledged ongoing support for its
preservation.

Councilmember Will Whetstone expressed gratitude for the numerous emails,
phone calls, and comments received regarding the West Valley Performing Arts
Center. He acknowledged that the message from the community was loud and clear,
stating that he heard what they were saying. Councilmember Whetstone described
the issue as a "math problem" that needed to be solved, indicating the complexity
of finding a solution. Despite not knowing what the solution might be, he expressed
his enjoyment of attending productions at the theater over the past two years,

anecdote about his daughter's involvement in theater at Hunter High School,
