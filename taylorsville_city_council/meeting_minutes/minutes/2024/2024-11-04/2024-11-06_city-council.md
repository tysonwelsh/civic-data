3.         REPORTS


         3. 1        Unified Fire Authority Report— Captain Richard Rich


 ID   UFA Captain Richard Rich presented his report for Q3 which was typically the busiest
quarter of the year. They had a very busy fire season during the summer, with three structure
fires in Taylorsville alone. Some Taylorsville statistics of note were:
           1, 297 total     incidents,    470 of which ( 36%)           were emergent.

           1, 008 total ambulance responses, 43. 5%                  of which came from Station No. 117 while 35. 5%
           of them     came     from     Station    No.   118.   17. 2%  of the ambulances responded from Kearns
           Station    No.    109   while     the    remaining     3. 9% were from Millcreek, Riverton and Magna
           stations combined.


Captain Rich displayed a heat map showing various emergency service responses throughout
the quarter. He explained that the Wildland team wrapped up their year recently after assisting
with fires in California, Oregon and Nevada. UFA was in the process of getting that team qualified
as a hot shot crew which was the highest level of training possible.

UFA' s recruiting efforts continued. They had a recruit camp currently in session that involved
fifteen candidates in their 4th week of training. He anticipated an additional camp being held the
following winter and they had been able to fast- track five individuals who had been part- time UFA
employees       previously.


Captain Matt Hambleton' s name was one of 306 from the U. S. and Canada added to the Fallen
Firefighter Memorial in Colorado Springs, Colorado in September. Captain Hambleton had been
stationed at Station No. 117 and passed away in 2023.

UFA's Annual Pancake Breakfast was a huge success. Although attendance was down slightly
from previous years, there were still over 400 attendees.

Karina McLellan of Ability Inclusion Services explained that the growing demand for housing
accessibility was driven by an aging population and a heightened awareness of the needs of
people with disabilities. This had produced a significant shortage of ADA- compliant housing in
Utah. To help address that gap, her agency had just purchased property near their community
day center in West Valley, where they planned to build a 12- unit ADA-compliant residential
complex. This would allow them to create more opportunities for disabled people to live
independently in a supportive environment. Ms. McLellan hoped that her agency would be
prioritized as she indicated that it served the Taylorsville community.
Rosie    Ortega   of the   YMCA   LIT ( Leaders    in   Training)     program addressed the City Council,
explaining that their focus was on youth development, including after school programs which
provided a safe and structured space for children to spend their time after school. They focused
on positive reinforcement, and the program fostered a welcoming and secure environment
supporting both academic and personal growth. The LIT program was designed to cultivate
leadership skills in students through group activities, team projects and opportunities to mentor
younger children. Program participants gained confidence, developed interpersonal skills and
learned the value of responsibility and community involvement.

There was no one else who expressed a desire to speak, so Chair Cochran closed the public
hearing.
