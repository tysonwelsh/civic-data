RTP Small Area Meetings Results Tim Hereth, MAG (5:11) Tim Hereth reported that during the month of September, MAG hosted four small area meetings in different regions of the county to gather feedback on projects for the upcoming RTP 2023 update. A presentation was also given at each meeting explaining the purpose of the plan and the RTP 2023 goals. The meetings were attended by commissioners, legislators, mayors, city staff and other regional partners including UTA, UDOT, ULCT, and Utah County Chamber of Commerce. A summary of the feedback from over 250 responses received at the meetings was provided. Details found at: https://arcg.is/1vOXzr RTP Draft Projects List Approval Tim Hereth, MAG (14:42) Every four years the Regional Transportation Plan (RTP) is updated to reflect the changing needs of Utah County and to be in compliance with federal regulations. The RTP list includes Highway, Transit, and Active Transportation Projects. Currently, highway and transit projects are listed. Active transportation will be finished and added pending the completion of a study by the end of the year. Tim Hereth provided an overview of the draft projects list using data and maps found at https://mountainland.org/rtp/. He reported that although the list is not final, approval is needed to move forward with further analysis on initial projects. The draft project list is open for change, taking into consideration the input received and air quality analysis. Public comment will be accepted through November 11, 2022. Concerns were expressed by Orem City and Provo City regarding the freeway crossing line over Utah Lake. Sam Kelly stated that his preference is to leave the freeway crossing line over Utah Lake a vision rather than a phase until an actual location is identified and agreed upon. MPO Technical Advisory Committee October 24, 2022 Page 3 TAC members requested time to collect data before the RTP projects list is finalized. A special meeting will be held December 12, 2022 to allow further discussion. UPWP Annual Performance and Expenditure Report Bob Allen, MAG (47:42) The annual Unified Planning Work Program describes the transportation planning work within the Mountainland MPO. The program gives a general overview of the planning work to be accomplished over the planning year by describing the planned work, budget, and funding sources. The Annual Performance and Expenditure Report describes the planning activities conducted and the funding expended for FY 2022 (July 2021- June 2022). Overall, MAG budgeted $2,851,655 for the year and expended $2,483,967, leaving a balance of $367,689, which is reprogrammed in the following year's Work Program. 2023 Proposed TAC/RPC Meeting Dates and Bylaws Update Bob Allen, MAG (51:07) Bob Allen reported that Mountainland MPO Bylaws state that MPO RPC and the MPO TAC meet monthly. In reviewing past meeting and cancelations, and reviewing workload, MAG staff recommends that both committees go to a bi-monthly schedule. On average, four of twelve meetings annually are canceled. Staff believes that changing to a bi-monthly schedule and allowing the chair to call for non-scheduled meetings, committee members time can be better used. In proposing this change, the Mountainland MPO Bylaws were reviewed. Changes, suggestions, and clarifications were presented in detail. Concerns were raised about potential projects missing the opportunity to receive funding to move forward due to the bi-monthly schedule and difficulty getting meetings back on people’s schedules. Aaron Wilson moved to recommend Mountainland MPO Board Bylaws be changed as proposed; that Section 2.3 (e) be changed to clarify that a local government alternate MPO Technical Advisory Committee October 24, 2022 Page 4 member to the MPO Board be a senior staff person; that MPO Board determine their leader's terms (stay as is or change to every year); that virtual meetings can be held as needed; and that TAC recommends monthly meetings. Rob Hunter seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Socioeconomic Forecast (TAZ Data) Viewer Request for Input Tim Hereth, MAG (1:25:20) Tim Hereth reported that each Regional Transportation Plan 4-year cycle, the data, models, and tools for the plan must be updated. Along with this update is the socioeconomic forecast by traffic analysis zone (TAZ). TAC members are encouraged to provide feedback to ensure that the forecast is plausible throughout the jurisdictions, densities and the residential/commercial distribution follow expectations, and city totals and growth make sense. MAG has built a web application to interact with this forecast and to accept comments. Comments need to be submitted by November 14, 2022. www.mountainland.org/se-forecast-viewer-draft Corridor Preservation Request 820 N Provo Bob Allen, MAG (1:31:15) Provo City is requesting funds to purchase an existing residence located at 1280 W Camelot Dr. Corridor #38, 820 N Provo Appraised Value: $665,000 Estimated Closing Costs: $20,000 Total Request: $685,000 This request is within the purpose and policies of the Corridor Preservation Fund Program; the fund has an adequate balance, and the property is apparently needed for future widening of 820 N. While this appraisal is revised from an earlier one and reflects an increase in valuation to close a gap between the original appraisal and one provided by the property owner, the adjustment looks reasonable on its face. This is not an apparent request for relief from financial hardship and the parcel is already developed. Staff proposes that TAC recommend to the Regional Planning Committee approval of this request. UTA Long-Range Transit Plan 2023-2050 Update Alex Beim, UTA (1:37:00) Alex Beim reported that UTA is kicking off a long-range transit plan (LRTP). UTA’s planning process phases include strategic planning, service planning, operations planning, and implementation. He explained that UTA’s LRTP is complimentary, but different from the MPO’s RTP planning processes and that it is community and data driven 30-year vision.
