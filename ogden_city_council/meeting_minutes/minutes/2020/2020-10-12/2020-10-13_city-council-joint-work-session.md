Storm Water Low Impact Development Standards
          Ogden City Planning is proposing to amend the City's zoning ordinance to include low impact development for storm
water filtration. Low impact development includes landscaping elements like bioswales to allow natural filtration of storm water
runoff from parking lots and large impervious surface. The proposal is the result of changes to the Utah State Department of
Environmental Quality's storm water management practices for municipalities.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint to summarize the proposal to amend zoning regulations to
accommodate low impact design (LID) standards for storm water management. LID standards are currently not supported by the
City's zoning ordinance; ordinances need to be updated to reflect State Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) storm water
management regulations. LID components are defined by the DEQ as follows: "Systems, either structural/ natural, that use or
mimic natural process to promote infiltration, evapotranspiration, or harvest/ reuse of storm water as close to its source as possible
to protect water quality and aquatic habitat." The State has required each City to implement LID standards requiring new
development projects to include infrastructure capable of managing an 80 th percentile rain event. The ordinance amendments
would apply to projects greater than an acre or less than an acre that are part of a common plan that encompasses more than an
acre, as well as redevelopment projects that increase the impervious surface by 10 percent. LID standards must be feasible; in
some cases, the standards are not practical due to high ground water table, slope, soil type, drinking water source protections, and
accessibility. The City must provide at least five types of LID standards and the proposed ordinance amendments include rain
gardens, bioswales, green roofs, minimization of impervious surfaces, vegetative strips, and infiltration basins. The goal of each of

    Marshall N. White Center Advisory Committee Ordinance Amendments
              Ogden City Public Services is recommending the Marshall N. White Advisory Committee to be reinstated for three years
    and to adopt the proposed changes to Title Three, Chapter 14 of the Ogden Municipal Code.
              Chief Deputy City Attorney Brown explained the proposed ordinance would make minor amendments throughout the
    section of the City Code governing the Advisory Committee; however, there are some significant changes to the membership and
    tenn appointments guidelines. The Committee would be comprised of not fewer than nine members, but not more than 11 voting
    members, and five ex-officio nonvoting members as follows:
                   1. One member from the Parks and Recreation Advisory Cmmnittee.
                   2. One member will be the Ogden City Recreation Manager.
                   3. One member will be the Marshall N. White Supervisor.
                   4. One member will be a representative from the Division of Alts and Special Events.
                   5. One member will be a representative from an Ogden-based youth service organization.
              Voting members should have experience in the areas of recreation, fitness, education, cultural heritage, arts and special
    events, business, finance, human services, or community assistance. Insofar as possible, Committee members should be residents
    of Ogden city or Weber County, with representation from the four Municipal Districts. A majority of the voting members should
    be Ogden City residents. Relative to Committee appointments, the tenn will change from three years to two calendar years, or
    until the member's successor is appointed. For voting members appointed in 2020, six members shall be appointed for an initial
    tenn of one year, and five members shall be appointed to an initial term of two years and may serve a maximum of five years.
    Ongoing, no member may serve for more than two consecutive two-year terms. Additionally, the Committee may recommend to
    the Mayor that a Committee member be removed for unexcused absence for three consecutive meetings or attendance below 7 5
    percent of scheduled meetings over an 18-month period. The Marshall N. White Center Advisory Cmmnittee sunset on March 31,
    2009 and has been inactive since. If reinstated, the new sunset date for the committee would be renewed for three years to April
    30, 2023.
              Council discussion of the proposed ordinance amendments centered on term limits, removal of a member for missing a
    defined number of meetings; Council member Hyer indicated he feels the ordinance should be amended to indicate a member will
    be removed if they miss more than three meetings and he feels that same removal guideline should apply to all advisory
    co1mnittees of the City.
