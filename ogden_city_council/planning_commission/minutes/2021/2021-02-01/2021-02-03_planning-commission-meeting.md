3. CBD Site Plan, for apartments at 441 20th Street.

  Mr. Steve Peterson stated this is a request to convert the previous Lil Audrey’s spa to high-end
  apartments, anticipating 14-15 units. He stated most work will be on the interior, while there will be
  some masonry and stone repair on the exterior. Some windows will be replaced for fire safety, but the
  appearance of the building will remain as it currently exists. A separate covered carport with solar
  panels is also provided for the tenants.

  Commissioner Schade asked if the applicant might consider removing the paint and restoring the
  original masonry appearance of the building. He also suggested a plaque be placed on the building to
  identify it’s history and tell the story of the original Dee School. Mr. Peterson indicated he is willing to
  explore those options.

  Ms. Olson stated this building was constructed in 1904 as a school and is three stories in height. There
  are existing entrances on the north, east and west sides of the building, with the south side functioning
  as a paved parking lot. Due to the steep topography, there are masonry retaining walls on the south,
  west and east property lines. An existing 15’ right-of-way also exists along the west property line which
  continues through the length of the block. The request is to convert the existing site to a 15-unit
  apartment complex. The site plan proposes to stripe 15 parking spaces as well as two additional ADA
  parking spaces, with each row of parking to be covered with a carport canopy containing solar panels.
  In addition, a planting island is proposed, and the existing stairs at the west entrance are to be replaced
  with a new ADA ramp with handrails.

  The proposed use and density is appropriate for the CBD, and there will be no significant changes to the
  exterior of the building. The Ordinance requires 20% on-site landscaping for multi-family uses, and the
  proposed landscaping is 14%. Staff has provided an alternate site plan which shows an additional 4,025
  square feet of landscaping along the southeast portion of the site, which migiht include trees, turf and a
  walking path as well as a 4’ sidewalk access on the south side of the building to provide access to the
  landscaped area. She stated the parking requirement is 22 on-site spaces while the site plan shows 26.
  She suggested some parking be removed to provide additional landscaping as well as a location for a
  dumpster with appropriate screening to serve the residents. She indicated the proposed parking layout
  eliminates any potential use of the existing right-of-way, suggesting an option might be to vacate the
  right-of-way. She recommended the right-of-way remain free of structures unless it is legally vacated,
  stating the carports are required to have a minimum of 1’ setback from the property line. She also
  stated no building materials have been identified for the structure of the carports, and Staff suggests
  any beams and posts be painted to match the building. Staff recommendation is for approval, subject
  to the following conditions:
    1. Install 4,025 sq. ft. of landscaping on the south east portion of the site, as shown in the staff

    3. Decrease the number of parking stalls onsite to twenty-three (23) parking stalls, including two (2)
        accessible parking stalls to meet onsite landscaping and dumpster enclosure requirements.
    4. Install a gated, masonry dumpster screening enclosure adjacent to the south wall of the building
        as shown in the staff recommended site plan.

4. Public Hearing, Preliminary Subdivision Amendment, for Ogden Business Exchange Subdivision at
   approximately 552-558 Stockman Way. (Attachment C)

  Mr. Martin McFadden indicated this is a request to allow construction of a tilt-up building on Stockman
  Way.

  Ms. Olson this request is to combine Lots 3 and 11 to create a new, larger lot as well as to vacate
  recorded public utility easements dividing the existing lots. She reviewed the surrounding area, stating
  the lots are part of the Ogden Business Exchange Subdivision. A portion of the property is in the BEI
  zone, with the east portion in the BEH zone. The original subdivision had smaller lots, but as
  development has occulted several have been modified to accommodate larger buildings. In considering
  a subdivision amendment, the Commission is to determine whether there is good cause for the
  amendment and whether the amendment complies with both zoning and subdivision codes. The cause
  for the amendment is to allow more desirable lots for future development and provides opportunities to
  develop vacant property. The lots as proposed are compliant with the minimum lot area requirements
  of the zone and meet the subdivision standards. She noted if there are any existing utilities within
  recorded easements, the utility company requirements must be satisfied. Staff recommendation is for
  approval subject to the following conditions:
  1. Clearly identifying new modifications on the final plat and removing labels of previous lots as well as
      easements to be vacated.
  2. Obtaining and satisfying all department staff and private utility requirements.

  Commissioner Schade asked if there is a rear setback requirement and whether access to the trail
  system would be comfortable if the building were too massive. Mr. Montgomery answered the rear
  setback is 40’ plus an additional foot for each foot any building is above 35’ in height.

As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Garner to close the public
hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously.

  MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the subdivision

  easement being recorded on Private Drive A to allow existing five property owners continued access

Mr. Ian Franklin, representing City Engineering, stated the width of 9th Street in this area is 20-28’ of
pavement which is typical of a local road. He felt the road would support anticipated traffic from the
new use. He stated he is not aware of any plans for the installation of new sidewalk in the area, which
typically is required as part of new development. He stated whether posted or not, the speed limit on
local residential streets is 25mph. He suggested the townhome project would likely generate less than
100 vehicle trips per day which would not be noticeable to the road system as it exists. He also
indicated the private lane/shared driveway should not be allowed to have parking as it is not wide
enough.

Mr. Stratford reviewed land use rights, stating a property owner hasw the right to develop so long as the
standards of the Ordinance are satisfied. He stated if legal access to existing properties currently
exists, it cannot be removed by a new development, but the Commission has no ability to create or
reduce these rights as part of the approval process. He also indicated while the developer is obligated
to contact irrigation companies when water lines cross their property, the irrigation company cannot
block development. The developer should work with the companies to assure water rights are not lost,
and if details cannot be worked out, an opinion from the land use ombudsman may be considered or
court action pursued. He also indicated the conditional use process allows the Planning Commission to
identify potential impacts of new development and consider whether these impacts can be mitigated.
While not required to be eliminated, if these impacts can be mitigated, the Commission is obligated to
approve the use. The Planning Commission is bound by the existing ordinances, which can be changed
only by the City Council.

Mr. Strong stated the proposal is for a 6’ vinyl fence along the property lines, and does not anticipate
taking down any existing fences, but is willing to work with adjacent property owners. He stated no
parking is anticipated for the private roads.

Commissioner Southwick asked about the adequacy of 9th Street. Mr. Franklin stated the minimum
lane width for roads is 10’, and a 20’ road is suitable for two-way traffic. As there are no improvements
such as curb, gutter, sidewalk or parkstrip, the asphalt is available for vehicle traffic only. While the
minimum with for construction of new roads is 28’ there is no minimum standard for existing roads. He
stated if residents are concerned about parking in the right-of-way, consideration might be given to
establishing a parking by permit only in the area. Discussion continued relating to potential trips
generated by the 15-units, sidewalks and standards of the Federal Highway Administration. As this
project has no frontage onto 9th Street, the developer is not responsible for additional improvements.

Review of Meeting
Mr. Stratford stated the Commission typically listens to all public comments and addresses questions after all
comments are made. This evening questions were answered after each comment which appears more laborious, but
as the Commission has no specific rule, the process can be changed as desired by the Chair. He stated some items
brought up by the public are not based on the item before the Commission and while it may be important information,
the consideration of the Commission should be based on the Ordinance governing the particular request. He stated
the number of existing multi-family units in an area is not reason to deny a project so long as the project meets the
requirements of the zone, but may be an impetus to consider plan amendments which may better direct future
development. He felt citizens appear to be frustrated as their comments cannot stop development, and he tries to
explain the Planning Commission is bound by the existing land use codes. Mr. Montgomery suggested more
information might be made available during the notification process to assure residents that while their input is
valuable, the Commission cannot always respond as they desire. Commissioner Southwick expressed concern with
the number of units proposed to be rentals, stating there are a limited number of residential units available for sale.
Mr. Stratford indicated the sight triangle distance is a standard of the Engineering Division and can be changed
according to the speed of vehicles and the location where cars stop. While the minimum is 20’, it can be increased to
protect the safety of vehicles and pedestrians. Mr. Stratford also stated a question had arisen relating to building
codes of the old Dee School, and as the use changes, building services will be completing a code compliance analysis.
