Study Session
      Mayor Pro Tempore Bryn Heather Johnson called the meeting to order.

      Report: Major Roads CIP Update
      Public Works Director Scott Cooley reported that the 12150 South signal
      installations had been completed. Councilmember F. Lowry stated that
      complaints had been received regarding the timing of the southbound left-
      turn signal at the intersection, and Mr. Cooley stated that he would review the
      timing. Mr. Cooley also reported that Zone 4 sidewalk repairs were expected
      to be completed by the end of June, at which time Zone 5 sidewalk repairs
      would begin. He also provided updates on the 1700 East storm drain
      improvement project and the Pump Station 3 upgrade project.

      Mr. Cooley reported on planned capital projects for 2026, including pavement
      maintenance; Pioneer Road resurfacing and storm drain, curb, and gutter
      improvements from 100 East to 700 East; construction of dual northbound
      left-turn lanes at the 14600 South and Minuteman intersection; installation of
      sidewalk, curb, and gutter improvements along 700 West from 11400 South to
      12300 South on the west side; an asphalt addition on 13400 South; a sidewalk
      gap project on Manfield Way; expansion of the City Hall parking lot;

Report: Parks CIP Update
Parks and Recreation Director Rhett Ogden reported on completed projects,
and said Jenson Farm Park upgrades were ahead of schedule. He provided an
update on: Porter Rockwell Trail bridge expansion; North Canary Hills access
easement; Sunrise Station Park Expansion; Bonneville Shoreline Trail; Spring
Hollow bridge replacement; and West Red Rock Access Loop Trail. Proposed
projects included flyover crossings at Vertigo Trail/Levitate Trail intersection
and Maple Hollow Downhill Bike Trail/Ann’s Trail intersection.

Report: Fuel Mitigation Grants Funding Update
Fuels Crew Supervisor Parker Hansen reported on projects completed over
the previous three years, noting that some had been funded through a
$119,000 grant. In response to a question from Councilmember F. Lowry, Mr.
Hansen stated that ongoing maintenance of fire breaks would be manageable
due to the significant clearing work that had already been completed. Mr.
Hansen also explained that the City’s fuel mitigation efforts had helped
homeowners maintain their insurance coverage.

Mr. Hansen emphasized that chipper events had been an effective method for
reducing wildfire risk in Draper and explained plans to expand chipper days in
the upcoming season with the assistance of a $200,000 grant. The Council and
staff discussed County-owned land within the Wildland Urban Interface (WUI)
area and the coordination involved in mitigation efforts.

Council / Manager Reports
Tree Committee
Councilmember Johnson said she met with the Tree Committee that morning,
and the Committee had been open to new goals of beautification and
landscaping. She said the Committee suggested adding community education
to their scope as well.

Historic Plaque
Community Development Director Jennifer Jastremsky said the Historic
Preservation Commission had been working on a historic plaque for the
Widowmaker Trail, and had been working with Salt Lake County to put the
plaque on County land. She said an interlocal agreement had been drafted,
and asked for Council feedback. She reported that the City would pay for
installation and maintenance. A majority of the Council indicated support.

Legislative Update
Mayor Walker provided an update on the current Legislative Session,
highlighting bills concerning property tax, municipal fund balances, gas tax,
and elimination of voting by mail. He said he personally appreciated voting by
mail, commented that eliminating the vote-by-mail system would be costly for
every municipality, and reported the bill died in committee earlier that day.
City Manager Mike Barker said the alcohol bill would allow for municipal
discretion regarding proximity requirements.

Rocket Launchers
Councilmember F. Lowry asked if use of rocket launchers was legal in Draper.
Mr. Ogden said explosives restrictions in Code prohibited use of rocket
launchers in City parks, and said he would reach out to Mr. Lund about the
possibility of placing a sign adjacent to his property. Mayor Walker asked if the
Council would be willing to create Code language that specifically called out
rockets, and a majority of the Council appeared to indicate support.

Draper Amphitheatre
Mayor Walker said the City typically did not rent out the Draper Amphitheatre,
and said no policy for rental was in place. He expressed hope that a policy
would be adopted prior to renting out the facility to ensure sufficient recovery
of costs, including police and fire expenses. Mayor Walker cautioned that if the

                                                                       5|Page
     facility were opened for rental, the City would not be able to discriminate
     based on message or content. Councilmember Johnson expressed concern
     that making the facility available to rent would open the door to frequent use.
     She pointed out the facility was in a residential neighborhood, and said she
     would not want to turn the area into a regular event center.

     Councilmember F. Lowry asked if the City could sponsor or partner with a
     group for an event at the Draper Amphitheatre, such as the America 250 event
     mentioned during Public Comments. The Council and staff discussed
     considerations, including funding for overtime for police and fire and other
     services, and how to determine which organizations with whom the City would
     partner. Councilmember Dahlin said she was not opposed to the idea of
     renting the facility, but agreed a policy needed to first be in place to mitigate
     concerns.

6.   Adjournment
     Councilmember T. Lowery moved to adjourn the meeting. Councilmember
     Johnson seconded the motion, which passed by unanimous vote (4-0).

     The meeting adjourned at 7:50 pm.
