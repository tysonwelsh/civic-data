The motion passed unanimously. 5. 2024 Legislative Summary LaNiece Davenport provided a brief summary of the 2024 legislative session with a summary of bills and appropriations MAG staff tracked. The summary can be found on the “Resources” page of MAG’s Planning website. She summarized the following bills and appropriations. House Bill 430 sponsored by Representative Perucci and Senator Cullimore. This bill came out pretty late in the session and does a variety of things that essentially benefit local governments when it comes to transit. The idea on this bill is to fund transit innovation projects for local governments. This bill would allow municipalities to propose a public transit innovation pilot program and then UDOT Transportation Commission would actually administer this program. UDOT would receive the applications from the local governments, set up criteria to look through the applications and then provide funding thereafter. Trustee Acerson was asked if he had anything to add, he noted that LaNiece hit the salient points and that it is trying to build transit ridership and to do something local that would tie into the regional system. House Bill 449 Pedestrian Safety Facilities Act sponsored by Representative Abbott and Senator Riebe. This bill requires UDOT projects to also consider bicycle safety in addition to pedestrian safety. House Bill 488 Transportation Funding Amendments sponsored by Representative Spendlove. The bill provides ongoing funding to the TTIP for commuter rail improvements, larger capital expenses for commuter rail projects. This bill also allows any county to impose a 0.30 local option sales tax and if they do, this allows them to use some of the imposition on public safety in addition to transportation. This is the first time that we are opening up the local option sales taxes to items outside of transportation. Page | 2 Senate Bill 51 Road Construction Bid Limit Amendments sponsored by Senator Winterton and Representative Watkins. The bill increases the bid limit for B and C road construction projects and ties it to the National Highway Construction costs price index instead of using consumer price index. It removes the cap and it does a few other provisions. Senate Bill 208 Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zone Amendments sponsored by Senator Harper and Representative Whyte. The bill fixes HTRZ issues, adds objectives, housing requirements and density requirements for an HTRZ. Please read the summary if you are interested in this bill. One bill I don't have on this list is Senate Bill 235. This was a railroad ombudsman bill that creates a railroad ombudsman position and provides ongoing funding in the amount of $800,000 for railroad crossing maintenance. So if you have any issues with railroad crossings, this bill will help with that. I think it is also important to highlight bills that didn’t pass and there are 2 bills I want to highlight. First is HB65 which is the Active Transportation and Canal Trail Amendment Bill. The idea was to identify and prioritize canals and UDOT would have a first right of refusal any time a private property owner wanted to sell some land, so that UDOT could build trails around the town. It had a few noticing and inventory and planning and reporting requirements. This bill did not ultimately pass, but I imagine we will see this one again next year. The second bill that did not pass was the TUF bill, HB367 Local Government Fee Amendments. We talked a lot about this bill last meeting. Essentially, it places some parameters on transportation utility fees. Moving on to Housing and Homelessness, there are essentially 2 really big homelessness-related bills. House Bill 298 and House Bill 421. I didn’t spend a ton of time on these 2 bills but they change the definition of code blue and the requirements that we have on response plans and a variety of other things related to homelessness or vulnerable populations. House Bill 465 is the Housing Affordability Revisions bill. Essentially, it does change some of the requirements on your moderate housing report, it also opens up the ability to use redevelopment agency or community reinvestment agency monies and changes what those entities can spend money on when it comes to housing. They can now pay for things like acquisition, construction, rehabilitation or income target housing. It defines what affordable housing is and outlines other parameters. It encourages the Point of the Mountain State Land Authority, if appropriate, to utilize land use authority to increase the supply of housing in the State. Senate Bill 268 is the First Home Investment or FHIZ bill by Senator Harper. It did pass. The idea on this one is if you want to encourage mixed use development in a city or town center and you want to provide housing opportunities within that center or in areas close to the center, you can apply for this tax increment financing tool to help cover some of the expenses for a project that would do that. There are a whole host of parameters that are part of this bill. So take a peek at those summaries that are linked if this is something you are interested in. It is important to note that this is not a requirement. It allows a municipality to create one of these zones but it is not a requirement. House Bill 13 Infrastructure Financing Districts (IFDs) bill passed. Essentially, it creates these infrastructure financing districts providing another opportunity for developers who create affordable housing to pay for some of these expenses along with infrastructure. There are a few appropriation bills that had appropriations or intent language for funding within the MAG region. I need to make this very clear, these are not items that MAG lobbied for. Beginning with the first one, this actually came out of language from last year and we’ve been talking about this one a bunch which ended up appropriating $775 million in one time dollars and $330 million dollars in ongoing dollars to UDOT for the TIF or Transportation Investment Fund. The monies pay down the outstanding debt service which allows Page | 3 UDOT the ability to fund programs and projects out of the TIF program project list. So that money will come from the general fund into the TIF. The next item is also one we talked a lot about here as well. The 1% transfer of the state sales tax from the TIF to the TTIF (the Transit Transportation Investment Fund) which equates to about $45 million dollars a year in ongoing and stable revenue to fund transit projects. The next one is $50 million dollar one time appropriation for the Point of the Mountain transit stop. This would be for the Frontrunner commuter rail station at the Point of the Mountain and would come out of the TTIF. Also, Vineyard City and Orem City got some money, Vineyard got $10 million for the 12th Street overpass and Orem got $1 million for the Orem Center Street Railroad crossing. The Huntsman Cancer Institute received about $663,000 in ongoing money for their new Cancer Research Center in Vineyard and some additional one-time money. $5 million for Provo airport. $2.4 million (rounding up) for Meals on Wheels which is statewide appropriation for all of the area agencies on housing. Not sure of the exact amount going to MAG, but approximately $375,000. This was a big win for Meals on Wheels. So overall, a good session. New RTP Process Overview Shawn Eliot, Transportation Manager, stated that w e are updating our RTP process policies and procedures from years ago to reflect what we're going for the next RTP. We update the plan every 4 years. We just updated our plan in 2023. For the next plan, we want to have unified goals and outcomes, and engagement and outreach to engage with you and with our stakeholders or transportation stakeholders and the public and others. And then have multimodal transportation strategies, a balanced approach to consider both land use, road, transit, safety, and active transportation projects. We are going to enhance our project selection process. And we're also going to clarify our amendment process. We will update how projects get added to the plan in between updates and how we get them in the plan. Through this process we are proposing lots of meetings with our committees, with you and the Tech Committee over that 4 year timeframe plus meetings with local agencies and staff like UDOT and UTA as well as community groups. We're updating the project selection process and amendment process and using best practices. We propose to have this process done this summer. So in the first half of 2024. Goals and requirements will be the next step, and that will be working with our committees to develop the RTP goals of what we want for our transportation plan. We want to ensure that we're within the Federal guidelines and emphasis areas. And then also consider our local and statewide goals. We're updating data and land use to model what projects are needed in the future because of our growth in jobs and in people. It's a large process, a lot of data. We start with creating a 2023 baseline for building units, for housing, units for oﬃce and so forth. And then also employment inventory. From that we created 2050 data forecasts of projections out to 2050 of households, population and jobs. We meet with local governments, agency staff and our committees to gain feedback on that work, and to also make adjustments on that feedback and part of that is allowing our city staff and support to look over that web-based maps to be able to give us electronic feedback. Page | 4 We will hire a consultant to update the Utah County Wasatch Choice Vision. This study will allow for lots of feedback and input at multiple workshops. We will develop key land use and transportation themes from members, partners and the public within the Wasatch Choice framework. Basically, how do we create a project needs list and what are the needs based off the growth that we are projecting out to 2050. This is used to model our project needs for every 10 years in the plan. It includes capacity, safety, operational and active transportation and transit projects. We will develop performance measures using RTP goals and federal emphasis areas and use performance measures to rank projects for fiscal constraint and create a draft project ranked list for MPO Committees to review. We are working with our transportation partners to create a revised financial model. We are developing costs and revenue forecasts. We used to create a fiscally constrained project list. We will create the draft 2027 - 2050 RTP and air quality conformity determination. We will hold a 30-day public comment period and finally, we will seek approval in June 2027. What’s next, we will be seeking the summer adoption of RTP Policies and Procedure in Summer 2024 so that we can move forward and everybody will know what to expect. Our team will collect land use and datasets for models in Summer 2024. And lastly, we will start developing RTP Goals in Summer 2024. Are there any questions? Utah County Corridor Preservation Program Process Overview Calvin Clark, Transportation Project Manager, noted that it had been a few years since we have done an overview of our Corridor Preservation fund. We just want to make sure that everyone is up to speed. This is meant to be a refresher and/or an introduction. This is State Code 72-2-117.5 and assigned to County COG, delegated to MPO and funded by vehicle registration fees paid in Utah County - this is important because these funds cannot be used for anything outside of what is in State code. Regarding approved Projects: the current list adopted Dec 2019. MAG intends to adopt a new project list later this year based on amended RTP-we are strictly limited to only updating the map once per year and because of this, we have decided to wait until we are through the amendment process before recommending the adoption of a new map. MPO TAC - recommended for approval, then it will go to the MPO Board, which has assigned the COG to be over this. From there, on to the County Commission for the final buy off. All acquisitions, the seller must be willing. After Acquisition: properties may be temporarily utilized in any capacity until needed for the project. Any funds generated from properties in excess of the maintenance costs must be returned to the fund. Upon project construction, excess property is sold with proceeds being returned to the fund. Current status, 2023 Year-End Balances: Total Balance: $19.7m, 1 year of debt service $1.6m, Obligated but unspent $9.0m, Total Unobligated $9.2m, Average yearly obligation is $3.2m. I’m showing all these details just to let you know that this is what we’ll be keeping track of. Question: What obligations do we have? Where do these bonds come from? What are they for? A few years ago, the County Commission came to this group and asked to refund it and we took 10 million dollars out of our balance and it was refunded at a much lower rate. It goes until 2030.
