31   2.1) Public hearing and recommendation of Edge Homes’ request for review of the Auburn
32   Springs Grading Permit on approximately 34 acres located at 400 South 2500 West.

34   Katie Bussell presented the item. The intent behind the grading is to make utilities installation
35   easier, and to provide buffer to raise the neighborhood to the same level. There will be an
36   estimated 25-35 truckloads of fill coming in daily over a period of six months. There are two
37   proposed haul routes, one along 700 South, and one along 2300 West.
38
39   Commissioner Everett asked about a third potential haul route he had heard about that would
40   cross through city property. Jed Stuart, representing Edge Homes, shared that the property to the
41   west of the proposed plan is currently under contract, and they are working on a haul route that
42   will go through that property and be less intrusive to neighborhoods and residential areas.
43
44   Commissioner Lockhart asked where the road stub was. Stuart explained that there are plans to
45   build the road through. Commissioner Lockhart asked about the hours of operation. Stuart says
46   the site will operate normal business hours, 8-5. Edge Homes will be spraying water for dust
47   mitigation. Commissioner Everett asked about the trailer size. Trailers will be 30 feet long. The
48   fill will come from another project in Saratoga Springs, to the west of West Lake High.

195   3. City Business
196
197   Craig Chambers did a presentation on deed restrictions. A deed restriction is terms and
198   conditions attached to the transfer of property. They can restrict use, future sale, potential
199   improvements, rentability, and other aspects of property rights. Deed restrictions cannot add
200   property rights, only restrict them.
201
202   Deed restrictions must be imposed by the property owner. Developers may put deed restrictions
203   on properties in order to get benefits from the city, such as added density. Restrictions must be
204   committed to writing and legally recorded. Deed restrictions are contracts between individuals,
205   however they are written. The main difference between deed restrictions and development
206   agreements is that deed restrictions move with the land. Deed restrictions are often attached to
207   subdivisions and are meant to preserve and enhance communities and amenities. They reduce
208   risk for future owners and make land more valuable. They may not be illegal, immoral, or
209   contrary to public policy. A deed restriction shouldn’t come as a surprise if a proper title search
210   is conducted.
211
212   Deed restrictions are a practice outside of common law. Buying property with a restriction is
213   similar to buying a property within a city: the owner is beholden to certain rules such as zoning
214   laws. Restrictions only terminate upon written agreement by all parties involved, or if an
215   expiration date is part of the agreement. Commissioner Everett asked if a developer who put a
216   deed restriction would be involved with the property indefinitely. Chambers explained that
217   developers waive rights to the property once it is sold, so the only interested parties would be the
218   city and the landowner. Commissioner Everett asked who is in charge of enforcing deed
219   restrictions. It would be up to the HOA, or if there is no HOA, responsibility lies with the city.
220
221   Chambers concluded by discussing the rule of perpetuities and how it doesn’t necessarily apply
222   to deed restrictions.
223
224   The Commission discussed potential particulars of deed restrictions, as well as the difference
225   between deed restrictions and development agreements.
