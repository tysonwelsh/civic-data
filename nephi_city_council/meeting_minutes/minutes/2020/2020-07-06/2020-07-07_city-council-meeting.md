Mayor Glade R. Nielson called the meeting to order and welcomed all present and then Councilor Seely
offered a prayer.

PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE:
  The Pledge of Allegiance was led by the Nephi City Police Department.

LETTER OF SUPPORT PRESENTED TO THE NEPHI CITY POLICE DEPARTMENT:

  Mayor Nielson read a letter of support from the City Council to demonstrate the confidence the
council has in the Nephi Police Department. The letter stated: “In the past several weeks, police
departments across the nation have been the target of misplaced animosity due to isolated instances of
racial injustice. As officers continue to do their job to serve and protect the residents of their
communities, they have also received varying levels of support from their own governing bodies.”
Mayor Nielson expressed the council’s appreciation and support for the police department’s
professionalism and work ethic in all the duties they perform.

2020 MISS NEPHI ROYALTY CROWNED FOR SECOND YEAR:

   Mayor Nielson stated that the Miss Nephi annual pageant was canceled this year to avoid the
spreading of COVID-19. The Miss Nephi royalty from 2019
 graciously agreed to serve as royalty again for 2020. The Mayor stated that the council is grateful for
their willingness to serve an extra year. Miss Nephi Pageant Director, Jamie John came forward and
crowned Jaida Stephenson Miss Nephi 2020 with Maddison Webster 1st Attendant and Taizlee Ostler 2nd
Attendant.

CONSENT AGENDA APPROVED:

  Councilor Memmott moved to approve the consent agenda which included the approval of the
minutes of the regular council meeting held on 6-16-20, the work session minutes 6-16-20, work
session minutes 6-23-20, and a list of claims on the warrant register dated 7-7-20. Councilor Ostler
seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

BUSINESS LICENSES APPROVED:

  Councilor Jones reviewed and moved to approve the business license applications of Revolution Land
and Sea and Open Hearth. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous
vote.

PUBLIC COMMENT:

   Mr. Blaine Malquist spoke to the draft of the nuisance and abatement ordinance. He expressed that
he thought the ordinance was somewhat harsh. He stated that we all have rights to do what we want
on our own property and he hoped this ordinance would not limit those rights. He went on to express
his concerns about the amount of pesticide and herbicide spraying that occurs by city, county, and state
officials. He said that studies show that we are living in a “toxic sewer” that is destroying our health.

  JD Parady expressed his appreciation for the work the council had done on the nuisance and
abatement ordinance. He is concerned about the protests and happenings up north coming to Nephi
with the celebrations that will be held within the next month or two. He suggested that the formal
protest process be looked at very carefully so that we have a stake in our community and to not let
people be imported in with harmful demonstrations.

JESSICA DESCHAMPS HIRING AS OFFICE SPECIALIST I RATIFIED:

   Councilor Memmott moved to ratify the hiring of Jessica Deschamps as Office Specialist I. Councilor
Worwood seconded the motion the motion passed on a unanimous vote. Ms. Deschamps stood and
told a little about herself and said she appreciated the opportunity to work for Nephi City.

GUN RANGE ANNEXATION AREA CHANGED TO A LEVEL 1:

   Councilor Ostler moved to change the gun range annexation area from a level 2 to a level 1. Mr.
Atkinson stated the main reason for doing this was not necessarily to provide city services but to bring
the area under municipal law. Councilor Jones seconded the motion. The motion passed on a
unanimous vote.

CERTIFIED TAX RATE FOR CALENDAR YEAR 2020 ADOPTED:

   Mr. Atkinson gave a presentation on how property taxes are collected and calculated. Council
members discussed the Nephi City certified tax rate for calendar year 2020 that had been prepared by
the Juab County Assessor and the Utah State Tax Commission. The 2020 rate is .000872 down from
2019 rate of .0011. Councilor Memmott moved to adopt the 2020 certified tax rate of .000872.
Councilor Seely seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

NUISANCE AND ABATEMENT ORDINANCE 07-07-2020 TABLED FOR FURTHER REVIEW:

  Mr. Atkinson reviewed the final draft of Ordinance 07-07-2020 with the council. The ordinance had
been an item of discussion and review for several months. The ordinance would assist the city’s code
enforcement officer by providing additional tools that can be used to enforce the code. Councilor
Memmott suggested that the public have more time to read and digest and offer suggestions to the
ordinance. Councilor Jones moved to table the ordinance. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion.
The motion passed on unanimous vote.

WATER/SEWER UTILITY TRUCK DECLARED SURPLUS PROPERTY:

  Mr. Atkinson updated the council that in the 2020 fiscal year budget, the Water/Sewer department
purchased a new utility vehicle to replace a 2009 Dodge Ram. The old utility truck had been in
operation for 11 years with almost 144,000 miles. The department is requesting to have the City Council
declare the old truck as surplus and authorize the truck to be sold. The estimated value of the truck is
around $18,000. Councilor Jones moved to declare the 2009 Dodge Ram Water/Sewer utility truck
surplus property. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

ARTS GRANT APPLICATION REVIEWED:

  Mr. Atkinson presented a draft of arts grant application. Arts grants are funded through the City’s
Parks, Arts, and Recreation (PAR) Tax passed by voters in November of 2018. Collection of this tax
began in the City’s fiscal year beginning in July of 2019. Each fiscal year the collections will be totaled.
The City Council has designated 5% of the PAR Tax revenue to be available for grants dedicated to local
arts organizations.

   In fiscal year 2021, there is approximately $3,900 available for an arts grant. This amount may be
divided between grant applicants. Grant funds can only be used for operating expenses of non-profit
cultural organizations.
    A discussion ensued about what type of projects the Arts Grant could fund. Councilor Seely
expressed his desire to have the money used for an art project within the city to add interest for
travelers to visit and to take pictures with. Seely also questioned whether we could require a
percentage of public buildings to add aesthetic enhancements. Councilor Memmott suggested that the
grant application deadline be extended to August 14, 2020. Mr. Atkinson said he would change the
deadline date and contact possible art grant applicant organizations to inform them about the Art Grant
available.

AUDIT ENGAGEMENT LETTER FROM LARSON AND COMPANY APPROVED:

   Councilor Seely moved to authorize the mayor to sign the engagement letter with Larson & Company
for fiscal year ending June 30, 2020. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion, which passed on a
unanimous vote.

CITY ADMINISTRATOR UPDATES:

   Mr. Atkinson reported that a couple of years ago the city began working on growing the city’s social
media presence. He reported that this past week the city reached 2000 followers on the city’s social
media sites.
  Mr. Atkinson reported that the 4th of July firework show was conducted without any problems. He
said that one improvement could be to shut off the Main Street lights during the show. Mayor Nielson
reported that the firemen really liked the new location.

NO CITY COUNCIL UPDATES:

   Councilor Seely reported on the JRDA meeting. He said that a high-voltage powerline company had
approached JRDA for an easement to improve a road.
   Councilor Ostler said that he had been approached by an American Legion member wanting to put a
larger flag at the Vine Bluff Cemetery.
    Councilor Jones reported that the public works building is going up. He also said that more street
signs needed to be placed on 300 West.

COUNCIL MEETING ADJOURNED:

    Councilor Seely moved to adjourn regular council meeting and convene into executive session to
discuss the competency of an individual. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion, which passed on a
unanimous vote.

   The meeting adjourned at 8:45 p.m.
