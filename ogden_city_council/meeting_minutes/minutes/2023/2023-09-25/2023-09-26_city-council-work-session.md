Proposed Zone Text Amendment— Use and Development Standards in M-1
and M-2 Zones

Planning Manager Brierley explained Administration is proposing several amendments to the allowable uses and

development standards of certain uses in the M-1 and M-2 zones. The amendments would do the following:
e Prohibit new junk and salvage yards, rebuildable car lots, recycling collection centers, and limit the area
vehicles could be stored in new auto repair shops. |
¢ Limit new entertainment and fitness facilities, except motor vehicle related uses.
e Allow manufactured home sales displays and RV storage lots with offices.
e Limit new personal storage facilities.
* Modify setbacks, height, and lot coverage standards.
e Establish design standards for new industrial buildings.
establish landscape buffer standards between industrial and residential uses.

The proposal focuses almost entirely on the M-1 (Limited Manufacturing) and M-2 (Manufacturing and Industrial) zones
and would not generally be applicable to the City’s other manufacturing zones, which include the MRD and DDR zones at
Business Depot Ogden (BDO), the OCIP zone in west Ogden, or the BEI and BEH zones within the Trackline Economic
Development Project Area in west Ogden. Administration posted a notice of pending ordinance on April 20, 2023, which limited
the new applications for most permitted and conditional uses in the M-1 and M-2 zones, prohibited new junk and salvage yards in
the M-2 zone and prohibited new recycling and collection centers and towing and impound lots in the M-1 and M-2 zones. The
pending ordinance restrictions expire on October 17, 2023. He noted that during the September 19, 2023, work session, the
Council expressed several concerns about the proposed amendments and the purpose of tonight’s discussion is to try to address
and respond to those concerns. He used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the intent and purpose of
Administration’s proposal; his first slide included a graph that charted the growth industrial land construction in Weber and Davis
Counties from 2010 to 2023, the net absorption of the industrial square footage, total vacancy rates, and lot term average vacancy

| rates. The conclusion that can be drawn from this data is that vacancy rates have decreased, which is an indicator of a need that is
~~” not being met and more construction should be anticipated. He noted the Economic Development Corporation of Utah (EDCUtah)
works to attract projects to the State of Utah and most of the projects they see are manufacturing-type projects. Job growth in the
manufacturing sector has outpaced growth in other sectors by more than eight percent; Business Depot Ogden (BDO) is a prime
example of these trends; over the past four years, the acres available or development has decreased from nearly 400 to under 100.

The supply in Weber County is dwindling. He added that the average annual wages in manufacturing are $74,201, compared to

$54,154 in other sectors. The manufacturing industry has a significant impact on the State’s economy, including travel and ae)
tourism, real estate, and services, all of which contribute to the gross domestic product (GDP). The manufacturing sector is also a
‘job multiplier’; each manufacturing job creates .92 jobs in other sectors. Key industries the City has focused on in the
manufacturing sector include aerospace and defense, outdoor products, life sciences, software and technology, and advanced
materials and manufacturing. He called out a random M-2 zoned property in the community to be considered as a case study; it is
2.8 acres and is very near another property for which a salvage yard has recently been proposed. He offered a comparison of the
benefits of a salvage yard versus a common manufacturing use. The annual property and sales tax generated by a salvage yard
would be $7,500 and $30,000, respectively. A yard on a property of this size would have approximately four to five employees.
However, a salvage yard use can result in a significant amount of environmental contamination. If the property were used as a
personal storage facility, the annual property and sales tax revenue would be $27,000 and $11,000, respectively and there would
be approximately three employees. He noted there are currently 15 personal storage facilities in Ogden, equating to 11 square feet
of storage space per capita, which is commensurate with the market rate of 10 to 13 square feet per capita. If the property were
used for a manufacturing purpose, the annual property tax would be $25,000, but the use would employ 50 employees directly and
46 indirectly and would have an impact on the GDP of $15 million. He noted the proposed ordinance would prohibit new junk and
salvage yards, used and rebuildable car lots. The basis for this prohibition is that the uses are associated with low employment,
low property value, and potential environmental issues. He asked if the Council wishes to consider amendments to the junk and
salvage yards regulations on its own or as part of a larger ordinance amendment relating to manufacturing zones. Mr. Brierley
added the proposal relative to personal storage facilities would be to limit new personal storage unites to:
1. Replacement of units from downtown
2. Infill of existing
3. Upper floor
4. Population growth
This proposal is based upon low employment rates, modest land value, and the fact that manufacturing zoned land is
_ better suited for higher value uses. He also asked if the Council would like to vote on this matter independent of other proposed
amendments to the manufacturing zone ordinances.

The Council contemplated the questions regarding the proposals relating to salvage yards and personal storage units and
ultimately concluded by directing Mr. Brierley and Council staff to provide the Council with an opportunity to consider
amendments pertaining to these two use categories separately from the broader amendments to the manufacturing zones. : i

Mr. Brierley then concluded with is presentation, touching on the matter of athletic facilities in manufacturing zones;
athletic facilities are using space that industrial businesses could use, and Planning staff feels the uses are better suited to
commercial zones. The proposal is to limit recreation facilities to those that are motor vehicle related, prohibit athletic clubs/gyms,
and prohibit adult live entertainment in the M-2 zone. He asked if the Council would also like to vote on this separate from other
amendments to the manufacturing zones. Council member Nadolski noted that youth in the Ogden community are being forced to
travel to other communities that have large athletic facilities that can accommodate all types of sports in wide open spaces. He
stated that he does not want to prevent landowners in Ogden from providing those types of uses that are in very high demand. He
added that ifthe City were able to provide a large recreation space for residents, he would not be as concerned about prohibiting
the use in the manufacturing zones. Council member Hyer agreed and suggested that it may be possible to create anew zone for
these types of uses and to concentrate them in one area of the community.

Mr. Brierley then touched on proposed amendments to development standards in the manufacturing zones; this includes
elimination of the impervious coverage standard; elimination of the rear yard setback in the M-1 zone, which staff does not see a
purpose for unless a manufacturing use abuts a residential use; capping maximum heights at 90 feet in the M-2 zone (there is
currently no maximum height restriction in the M-2 zone); defining buffering standards for manufacturing uses that abut
residential properties; including regulations that provide for building durability; and landscaping requirements aimed at improving
aesthetics of a manufacturing use. Relative to architectural elements, a developer can pick three items from a list of 11 that have
been developed for consideration. Council member Hyer expressed concern about allowing a developer to pick three options as
many of them may not be cohesive with one another. He would like to visit with Mr. Brierley more about his concerns regarding
the list of architectural elements. Council member Nadolski added he would like to understand what other communities are doing
in terms of imposing architectural and landscaping standards for manufacturing uses. Mr. Brierley stated he is unsure of the

requirements in other communities.

In conclusion, Council Executive Director Eller-Smith indicated Council staff will meet with Mr. Brierley to attempt to
craft a proposal based upon the feedback provided by the Council this evening.
