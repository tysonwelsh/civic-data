(Project CUP-20-017) - Overview of Project – Samantha DeSeelhorst

Associate City Planner, Samantha DeSeelhorst, shared slides on the signage application and
reported that it is a basic signage application for a property along Highland Drive, requesting
conditional use approval for wall and monument signing. The applicant is Wasatch Sign and
Lighting on behalf of the property owner, Lynn Curtis.

Ms. DeSeelhorst gave a brief overview of the site context and reported that the approval is for a
single monument sign located at the front of the site, and two wall signs, one located on each of
the existing buildings. The applicant felt that the lack of signage creates confusion for clients,
both in finding the property from Highland Drive and in navigating the site. The two different
buildings each serve a different age group of children. There is the Children’s Academy Preschool
at the rear of the site, which is specifically childcare for younger kids, age infant through preschool.
The Children’s Academy Creek is for older school-aged children.

Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that as far as Code goes, signage does not normally go to the Planning
Commission, however, City Code requires all signage in the Residential Office Zone to obtain a
Certificate of Design from the Arts Council and conditional use approval from the Planning
Commission as well as a standard Building Permit. The Arts Council voted unanimously to issue
a Certificate of Compliance at their last meeting on November 19. The item was currently under
the review of the Commission.

Ms. DeSeelhorst stated that the same chapter of City Code gives guidelines of signage, as outlined
in the project memo and staff report. The application met and followed all guidelines. She
reported that the monument sign is compliant with City Code at 32 square feet per side and is four
feet tall. It will be constructed using aluminum and steel and located in an existing planter box
and will be parallel to the driveway. The wall signs are nearly identical but are different in terms
of the name denoting the different age groups. They are also Code compliant at less than the
allotted six square feet and are proposed to be constructed of aluminum and Sintra.
Ms. DeSeelhorst presented renderings showing the sign placements above the exterior entrances,
which are also compliant with City Code. Staff recommended approval of the project.

(Project ZTA-19-003) Overview – Michael Johnson

Mr. Johnson reported Project ZTA-19-003 is a City ordinance that can be applied in specific City
Redevelopment Areas, with the gravel pit or a portion of the gravel pit being the newest. Specific
areas where it may apply were identified. It allows a specific development plan that only applies
to a particular property. He noted that two projects are currently going through the process. The
proposal involves efforts by the City to clean the area up and remove gray areas. Mr. Johnson
stated that nearly one year ago the City contracted with Landmark Design to assist with the draft.
For all new projects moving forward, the amendment will apply.

Mr. Johnson gave a brief overview and stated that the approach was to use and assess the current
ordinance. The Consultant chosen by the Commission met with staff, the Planning Commission,
and the developer. He did not meet with Lockwood but they reached out. Mr. Johnson reported
that after all of the consultations, some Code modifications were presented to the Commission.
Mr. Johnson presented the following Code changes:

   •   Several terms were eliminated that were similar such as PDD, PDZ, and Development Plan.
       To clarify the various connotations, some definitions were added.




Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 12/02/2020                                         3
   •   It was proposed that Tier 3 be excluded from the ordinance. Because the approval process
       is more strict, it would not have been included and there is no variation in the standards
       that need to be met. It was noted that there was no reason for anyone to apply for a Tier 3
       PDD and then seek to rezone a site.

   •   Clear language was added that includes participation in each project by the Architectural
       Review Commission (“ARC”). The ARC will conduct the first review after which it would
       go before the Planning Commission and the City Council.

   •   Clarification was added to the section pertaining to Affordable Housing. It will be held
       until the update to the Lockwood text is reviewed by the Council. Flexibility was added
       including allowing 15% of the units to be at 80% of the Area Median Income (“AMI”) and
       using HUD data rather than City data.

   •   Significant differentiation was added. One section in the ordinance pertains to
       development standards and submittal requirements. Regardless of the size or scale of a
       property, the same level of detail must be provided. Projects were divided into single
       and multi-phased, with an additional step added to multi-phased projects. A flow chart
       was added to help visualize the approval process.

Mr. Johnson reported that the targets were updated to make them flow better and make it clear that
the goals for PDD are City-wide. With every project, all four points cannot apply, but each project
should at least contribute to the objectives. To explain the plan on a case-by-case basis, certain
uses were reaffirmed. There were also differentiated application criteria for single and multi-phase
developments. Single-phase technologies, such as planting and lighting plans and precise sign
information and diagrams, will include the stricter requirements. Building elevations will be as
they appear and multi-phase implementations were broken out. There will be several requirements
such as a traffic study, market research, a proposal for affordable housing units, and a plan for the
concept site or a master site plan showing the open space, houses, and parking.

Mr. Johnson explained that because of the architectural designs, a site photometric plan showing
the location of each light bulb will not be needed. This is similar to what was done with the gravel
pit. When each phase was reviewed by the Architectural Review Commission and the Planning
Commission, they established a design theme and criteria so that the two bodies could determine
whether each phase was in compliance. At various stages in the process, the information will
be broken down to make it more usable. The City Ordinance for parking and lighting standards
will also be referred to as the basis for development.

It was reported that Tier 3 was modified. The building heights are the same in Tiers 1 and 2, but
an adjustment was needed. The current ordinance specifies that to construct a Tier 2 project with
a height of 50 feet, it must have commercial on the first two floors. According to different market
studies and initiatives in other cities, two-level mixed tiers do not always work. Mr. Johnson
commented that they did not want only mixed-use on the ground floor but also did not consider
two levels of mixed-use to be appropriate. He suggested there be further discussion on the plan
for a single mixed-use of 50 feet at ground level. All other information was to remain the same.

       1.2     (Project GPA-20-001) Cottonwood Heights Parks, Trails, and Open Space
               Master Plan.

Mr. Johnson reported that staff had been working for some time on an Open Space Master Plan
with a significant amount of input having been provided by the Parks, Trails, and Open Space
Committee. Several members were also serving on the Steering Committee. Senior City Planner,
Andrew Hulka, provided an overview of the feedback, what the plan seeks to accomplish, and
public input. It was noted that at some point, a structured draft plan will be prepared. It was
anticipated that a public hearing on the proposal will be conducted in January or February of 2021.

Mr. Hulka reiterated that the plan is still a rough draft and staff continued to collaborate with the
Parks, Trails, and Open Space Committee. The plan's definition is a long-range vision for parks,
trails, and open space through maintenance and preservation of existing amenities, the addition of
new parks, new trails, and continued public education. There are also long-range plans that provide
a basis to build off of.

The original 2005 General Plan included feedback on parks, trails, and open space. The County
also has a Natural Areas Plan. Mr. Hulka reported that in 2008 the City adopted an Open Space
Plan that addressed concerns. The County also has a Parks and Recreation Master Plan and an
East-West Recreational Trails Master Plan. Mountain Accord and Wasatch Choice are regional
plans that encompass more than just parks, trails, and open space. Some of the goals and insights
that are deemed relevant could be adopted.

Existing City plans include the City Bike Plan, which calls for bike lanes and trails throughout the
City. The Fort Union Boulevard Master Plan calls for mixed-use pathways or separated bike lanes
throughout the entirety of the corridor. The Wasatch Boulevard Master Plan calls for trails on
Wasatch Boulevard.

Mr. Hulka reported that they gathered demographic data showing that thousands of residents will
need amenities in the coming decades. The population will include people with varying interests

Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 12/02/2020                                        5
and expectations to use open space in different ways. It was suggested that this be considered in
future plans. An inventory was available of parks and open spaces in the City. It includes parks,
schools, the Skate Park, the Recreation Center, trails, open public spaces, and regional facilities
near the City boundaries. A more comprehensive inventory was being prepared by the Parks
Committee.

Mr. Hulka reported that in 2017, they began taking public comment. In September 2017, an Open
House was held. Maps were created to highlight desired changes. Surveys were also conducted.
Another open house took place in 2018 to get additional feedback from residents. Mr. Hulka
described the vision of the plan. Research on the benefits of parks and trails was conducted. It
was noted that there are health benefits to the use of parks and trails. With regard to economic
benefits, studies have shown that parks and trails increase the economic value of surrounding
properties.

Mr. Hulka explained that there have been a number of reports on facilities that people want to see
fixed, upgraded, or installed. These include a bike track, a stage for live music, frisbee golf, an
all-skills park, a playground, and a multi-use Fort Union path. It was noted that the open space
images for Fort Union were prepared by University of Utah students.

The Wasatch Boulevard Plan envisions a trail that would run north to south along Wasatch
Boulevard. There had also been a recurring request for a Dog Park for which a plan was included.
Overflow trailhead parking in Ferguson Canyon was also something that staff believed would
benefit the City.

A path along I-15 close to Parleys along I-80 was also proposed that would require working closely
with the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”) on the right-of-way. A drawing was
provided showing the connection between Wheeler Farm and the City and Little Cottonwood
Canyon.

Mr. Hulka stated that the East Jordan Canal Trail is an existing trail that was extended to Mountain
View Park from Fort Union Boulevard. Another new trail being proposed was the Union View
Trail that connects the subdivision above Home Depot and Target and the industrial area to the
community core. Other connection efforts were described. In the open houses, Deaf Smith
Canyon was identified as a priority. Mr. Hulka displayed an image showing the proposal for
Bonneville Shoreline access and will look like a neighborhood trailhead with no parking. Foothill
open space preservation was another priority identified.

Mr. Hulka reported that the City was divided into areas for planning and are neighborhoods that
center around activity nodes. That information was included on a map. To see how existing and
new parks and trails, bike lanes, and open space facilities will operate they looked at each
neighborhood. The planning areas were identified.

Mr. Hulka reported that several criteria were implemented to identify possible future open space
acquisitions and to determine which sites were preferred, which should be prioritized, and potential
funding strategies.

       3.2     (Project ZTA-19-003) – A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Take
               Possible Action on a City-Initiated Zoning Text Amendment to Chapter
               19.51 – “Planned Development District”.

CED Director, Michael Johnson gave a brief overview of the item and reported that the City-
initiated amendment was developed in 2016 to incentivize redevelopment in certain targeted areas.
It gives the City the flexibility to design ordinance standards, development standards, and actual
development projects that fit in the location it is being applied to. The ordinance was posted on
the City's website. He identified the following changes made:

   •   Addition of a specific definition to clarify the terms and acronyms used.

   •   Removal of the Tier 3 area from the ordinance.




Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 12/02/2020                                         9
   •   The addition of clear language that requires the participation of the Architectural Review
       Commission (“ARC”) with every project. The first official step in a process will be
       review by the ARC before being presented to the Planning Commission and ultimately
       the City Council.

   •   Modifications to the Affordable Housing Provision.

   •   Clarification of the approval process and requirements for single and multi-phase
       projects. A flow chart was included to provide a graphic of the various steps as part of
       the approval process.

Staff recommended no action be taken.          A Work Session was to be held to implement
recommended changes.

Chair Coutts opened the public hearing.

Patricia Dennis raised concerns regarding the 300 feet versus the 50 feet written on the plan.
Mr. Johnson clarified that the 50 and 300-foot limits are available at two different levels for
different areas. Tier 1 is only applicable at the gravel pit site. The Tier 2 area along Fort Union
Boulevard was identified. The maximum building height is 50 feet with mixed-use development.
The maximum building height is 300 feet in for Tier 1 at the gravel pit site if there is a 500-foot
setback from Wasatch Boulevard.

Commissioner Bevan commended Mr. Johnson for his excellent work on the amendment.

Chair Coutts was concerned about the disparity between how public space is defined by the
Commission and planning groups. She remarked that the Commission looks more closely at public
space. In an apartment building, for example, public space is open to all tenants and not necessarily
to the general public. She asked if there was a way to describe this to simplify the process.
Mr. Johnson offered to look into it further.

Commissioner Chappell noticed that the 50-foot height was not altered and was already included
in the provision. She asked if the City had inherited a cap. Mr. Johnson clarified that except for
office parks, the standard height is 50 feet throughout the City and must be 25 feet from residential
zones. It was suggested that there be language stressing the importance of how a project interfaces
with the surrounding area. Mr. Johnson stated that the plans could be supplemented with a clear
reference to the requirements.

Chair Coutts pointed out that there is a density bonus for developers to provide certain things, such
as connectivity to the existing community, as the reason for having the PDD option. In addition
to the retail and other things provided by developers, developers get a slightly greater height in
their buildings and the City gets additional amenities. Chair Coutts felt that there will always be
a push and pull with developers in that regard. The Commission should clarify its intent.
Mr. Johnson explained that the intent is for the developer and the City to benefit.
