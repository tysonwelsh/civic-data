2.      A presentation of all funds revenue forecast. (22-016) (0:18:45)

John Magness, Interim Council Executive Director, presented. For the last several budget cycles,
staff have done an analysis of all the funds coming into the city to assist with broader revenue
projections. Mr. Magness reviewed the categories included in the analysis: sales tax, utility
revenue, property tax, fees, chargebacks, RAP (recreation, arts, and parks) tax, franchise fees,
grants, telecom charges, B&C road funds, fines, miscellaneous, other, licensing fees, P&I
(principal and interest), land sales, bonds, and transfers (such as utility transfers).

He noted that the economic impacts of the pandemic created a few anomalies during fiscal year
2020, but the remaining data still allows for helpful projections. Staff received the actual figures
from the current fiscal year (which includes 8 months of data) earlier in the day. Mr. Magness
displayed these figures and explained how this fit into the modeling staff have done. Staff will be
doing a more detailed analysis on fees and will present that to the Council in the future.

Councilor George Handley asked for clarification on what constitutes chargebacks. Mr. Magness
explained chargebacks worked as an internal service fund, to compensate certain city
departments for services performed for other city departments. Continuing with a review of the
different revenue sources, Mr. Magness noted that some years had been slightly lower for actuals
but overall the city revenue and budgets evened out. Regarding grants, Mr. Magness noted these
included a number of different grant-related funding sources. If a planned grant is not received,
then the corresponding project is either delayed or does not get completed; the grants do not have
a bearing on city services. Councilor Rachel Whipple asked what B&C roads stood for; Mr.
Magness explained it was not an acronym but two different classes of roads for which the city
receives funding. Mr. Magness displayed the total with and without bonds (since the bonds are
essentially debt on specific projects). The total without bonds was about $349,929.

Mr. Magness reviewed projections regarding the general fund and he reviewed each of the
funding sources or categories (which were the same categories as in the all funds revenue
forecast listed above). Councilor Katrice MacKay asked if these fees were increased already; Mr.
Magness indicated those fees had not yet gone into effect but were included in the tentative
budget. For property tax, if property values increase, the mill rate falls; consequently the only
property tax increase the city receives is for new development. Ms. MacKay asked if the A, B, &
C road tax comes from gas; Mr. Magness indicated that it did. There has been a slight decrease
on licensing fees in recent years as licensing fees have not changed in some time. Some of the
fines in the general fund have been impacted in recent years by the pandemic and they hope
these will continue to recover. Transfers in (such as from other utility funds) will see a small
increase. Some departments’ transfers are decreasing over time. The general fund has
experienced approximately 5% growth year-over-year. Mr. Magness invited the Council to
submit any further questions they had for analysis. Ms. Whipple and Ms. MacKay indicated they

4.      A presentation regarding property tax. (22-016) (0:56:29)

John Magness, Interim Council Executive Director, presented. As property values increase in
Provo, the mill rate decreases. The City will still receive increased revenue from new
construction in Provo. Provo has three property tax streams, which include general operations,
bond obligations, and library operations (which is a dedicated revenue source for the library).
Since 2011, the mill rate for general operations has decreased 34%. In response to a question
from Councilor George Handley, Mr. Magness indicated that the increased bond revenue in 2018
was related to the Provo City Center construction bond. Mr. Magness showed the changes to the
property values in Provo from Fiscal Year 2011 to 2021.

Councilor Katrice MacKay asked about the County’s role in the process of setting the certified
tax rate versus the Council’s role. Wayne Parker, CAO, responded and shared some details to
answer Ms. MacKay’s question. The County is required to calculate the certified tax rate, which
they deliver to the city. The County’s rate is not discretionary—rather, the County is required by
state law to calculate the certified tax rate so that it brings in the same dollar amount, excluding
new growth. The effect is that as property values increase, the mill rate is driven down; any
increase to property tax revenue for the City is a result of new construction/growth. In the budget
ordinance the Council will set the certified tax rate for the next year. They can either set it as the

Councilor David Shipley supported making an inflationary adjustment to the property tax rate.

and water rate changes, those were going to be more aggressive in the last few years—there were
significant makeup adjustments on those fees. If they can get past the bulk of those fee changes,
it makes more sense now to look at property tax as an annual slight adjustment. Mr. Handley
asked Mr. Shipley if he recommended waiting one year until the fees would drop down. Mr.
Shipley did not think the fees dropped down, but the increases dropped down and were not as
severe. John Borget, Administrative Services Director, indicated that under the five-year plan for
wastewater, the coming fiscal year is the last year of those changes. The water fees have some
additional increases still to come.

Mr. Magness showed the revenue history for the general fund including property tax and sales
tax revenues. Data generally shows that property tax is a more stable funding source through a
recession, compared to sales tax which tends to drop off under those economic circumstances.
Fees are also typically pretty stable during a recession. Councilor Travis Hoban asked how the
city’s reserves are. Mr. Borget said that they increased the general fund balance last year to 28%
(by law they can go up to 35% in general fund balance). Mr. Borget anticipated they would see
another increase to the general fund balance in the coming fiscal year. If the city gets to that
limit, they are as fiscally conservative as the state allows them to be.

Mr. Magness indicated that if the Council wanted to move forward with indexing the property
tax rate to the CPI, he did not recommend using this year’s CPI as it is very inflated—he would
recommend some calculation such as an average of the CPI over the last 10 years. Mr. Handley
commented about the Council’s ability to plan over the next several years; any council at any
point can decide they did not want to continue doing small increases every year. Mr. Handley
thought this was one problematic approach to doing smaller incremental approaches, when there
may not be the political will in the future to do continual smaller increases. Ms. MacKay asked
for clarification about the truth in taxation process. Brian Jones, City Attorney, responded and
clarified that one of the limitations is that to begin the process, the Council must publish a notice
that includes the highest rate they are considering; after doing so, the Council can pass anything
from 0 up to that stated maximum rate.

5.      A presentation regarding non-General Fund expenditures (22-016) (1:46:17)

Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, presented. Non-General Fund expenditures include all other city
funds: enterprise, special revenue, capital improvement project (CIP), internal service, and debt
service. Ms. Dayley referred to information in the published materials which provided additional
context. She highlighted the summary of major changes to each fund from the last fiscal year to
the next year’s tentative budget. Enterprise Funds include the Provo Airport, Provo Power, Peaks
Ice Arena, sanitation, water, wastewater, utility transportation fund, and Timpanogos Golf
Course. Ms. Dayley offered a clarification about the materials in response to a question from
Councilor Rachel Whipple. Most categories in the Special Revenue Fund included increases for
the cost-of-living and market rate adjustments. The CIP Funds has large changes—this reflects
the large projects happening in the current and coming fiscal years. Internal Service Funds
include employee benefits and insurance as well as other internal inter-departmental services.
Ms. Dayley noted she is awaiting more information about computer replacement and would
follow up with the Council when she had received it. Council staff are also working on a report
on fleet services. Ms. Dayley reviewed the Debt Service Fund. She noted staff would be
preparing a separate analysis on the Airport budget, in light of recent announcements about
expanded services at the Provo Airport. Councilor Katrice MacKay was interested in seeing
more details on the wages for airport employees required by the FAA. Staff received several
other requests from Councilors and responded to these questions. The May 24 budget meeting
will be cancelled, with budget presentations to come on May 17 and in June. Presentation only.
