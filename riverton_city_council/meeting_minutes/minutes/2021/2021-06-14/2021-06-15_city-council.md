       Fireworks Restrictions and Water Conditions – Mayor Trent Staggs

Mayor Staggs said that he had asked Chief Wade Watkins and Deputy Fire Marshal Dan
DeVoogd to update the citizens on fire restrictions.

Chief Wade Watkins presented some of the challenges during the holiday season with
fireworks. He said there will be more staff so that they can attack any fire that may start. He
said that first responders will be directly dispatched so that there is no delay in being
dispatched. Councilmember Buroker asked if the citizens should still call 911 for assistance.

Chief Watkins said that the citizens will still call 911 but the dispatching will happen directly for a

year will be very similar to last year. Councilmember Buroker said she appreciates them
actively patrolling. She said that residents near the Midas Creek trail are concerned about this
area because it is very dry.

compliance with water restrictions. He said that once Utah Lake reaches the compromised level
then residents will not have access to the secondary water.

Stacie Olson, Water Department Manager, said that historically the City has been using more
culinary and secondary water than the previous year. She presented the slides showing the
historical data.

Mayor Staggs said that in the code, it says that the Mayor and Council can enact restrictions

an emergency meeting if there was ever a need to restrict water.

Councilmember Wells said that he has had many calls and emails about water running at the
parks. He said the residents don’t understand that the City runs the water for pressure reasons.
He said there are a lot of ways that citizens can look for abuses and try to get a hold of the right
person to help fix the problem. He said that everyone needs to do their part to help with water
conservation. Stacie Olson said that they are sending out employees to knock on doors to try to
educate residents that they have received complaints about.

which ones need more care. He said they are looking at other areas in the City and said that
they can lower the pressure at splash pads or limit the hours.

Councilmember Stewart said that he worries about water pressure and has had residents
complaining about the water pressure in his area. He said that he wanted to make sure that the
pressure is being maintained. Ms. Olson said that they could put some meters in his area to
track the water pressure. Councilmember Stewart said that the secondary meters and reports
would be helpful for his area. Trace Robinson said they are getting reports from these meters
already. Casey Saxton said that the City gets criticized for watering and that the City should be
proactive on educating residents about watering.

Councilmember Buroker said that the City should be communicating on this water issue daily.
She said that residents should be looking at smart controllers. She would like the graphics
posted weekly. She says that the City should have a goal for reducing water consumption in the
coming years.

Mayor Staggs summarized the water discussion and said the City should lead out on the steps
to conserve water.

         Year 2021-2022

Discussion/Action Item

      Ordinance No. 21-09 – Amending the Stormwater Fees – Ryan Carter, City Attorney

Ryan Carter explained that he circulated a memorandum on April 20, 2021 and a preliminary
draft of the amendments to adjust the stormwater fees. He said that the Jordan River is
deemed to be regulated by the Clean Water Act and the City must regulate the stormwater
discharge. He said that years ago the City started the Stormwater Utility program. He said
there was a uniform rate of measurement called an ERU with a fee attached to that unit.

Mr. Carter said that the City had been studying what the costs associated with the Stormwater
program are as it compares to residential and commercial properties. He said there is also a
roadway use component. He said that in studying this further, regular residential use is very
low. He said as a general rule, commercial use is going up and residential use is going down.
He says this is a much more scientific approach to determining stormwater use.

Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 21-09 –
Amending the Stormwater Fees. Councilmember Buroker SECONDED the motion. Mayor
Staggs called for discussion on the motion; there being none, he called for a roll call vote and
the vote was as follows: Councilmembers Buroker-Yes, McCay-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-
Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
