housing authority formation process was and asked for direction on the goals of the
housing authority. Dan Nackerman, an advisor, also joined the discussion.

County Council Member Harte was overwhelmingly in favor of pursuing the authority as
there was interest in the community. He felt if there was no housing authority in place,
they wouldn't be in a position to benefit from vouchers if there were changes to that
program in the future, plus there were plenty of other things a housing authority could
benefit from. He knew the budget was the big question.

Park City Council Member Toly agreed that this was the right direction and concurred
that budget was the big question, along with the model; either the county umbrella or
the multi-entity housing authority. County Council Member Harte clarified that the
housing authority would have its own mission statement and townships would not be
forced into joining or contributing to the housing authority. Park City Mayor Worel
inquired if there was any interest from other municipalities in joining and it was indicated
other entities were learning and watching.

Park City Council Member Gerber stated that she had been cautioned about going
down this path because it was one more hoop to jump through administratively. She
suggested an overall strategic housing plan would be beneficial, and pointed out that
housing authorities generally always needed funding and didn't fund themselves.
Nackerman revealed that most mid- to large-sized housing authorities didn't get direct
city or county funding and operated autonomously because they were a separate entity,
but smaller agencies relied on special funding at times.

County Council Member Stevens asked for definitions of small and medium housing
authorities and Nackerman indicated the HUD defined a small agency as one that
managed up to 400 units. He predicted this authority would start out small and grow
over the years. Jones suggested that a difference between the two models would be
how the boards were structured, however, the attorneys would need to advise on
tradeoffs.

Park City Council Member Dickey requested information at a future meeting about how
a board could be structured in Summit County versus each town/city structuring their
own board with an interlocal agreement to work together. County Chair Armstrong
questioned the benefit for a large authority being able to apply for larger amounts of
funding and asked if there would be competition with each other as small individual
housing authorities applied for funding. Nackerman reported it wouldn't impact the
qualifications to apply for certain funding. Further, he believed that being a true regional
partnership might make the entity more qualified to apply for certain funding.

County Council Member Robinson was in favor of a housing authority but believed the
County ought to have some way to co-develop, own and benefit from appreciation, cash
flow and long-term return of affordable housing. He was concerned about creating
another layer of organization and duplicating efforts with other agencies that were doing

the same thing, and questioned what the real benefits would be. Jones responded that
school districts felt like they were pushed into being their own developer because there
wasn't a way for them to secure units for their employees. Interviews with private
developers gave them a lot of information and he noted the benefit depended on the
kind of expertise of the regional housing authority executive director. If the focus was
specific to development, long term ownership and the ability to capture investment, then
that expertise would be necessary to help structure that authority.

Park City Council Member Doilney remarked that there was a regional need for housing
that was not being met and the reason they were there was because it was decided
they needed a group with a single purpose. He believed that they would be going
backward if they said they already had a group doing this work. He heard a housing
authority could give clear direction to all the different entities and there could be
efficiency and consistency there. He thought the umbrella model made the most sense.
To answer Park City Council Member Toly's request, he answered 100% in the rental
unit market and 90% employment assisted housing. They needed a single purpose, and
a housing authority was the best option on the table.

County Council Member Stephens wondered what it would cost each organization to
bring this work in-house, produce housing plans and focus on the work with the
developers, and thought the answer could change the conversation. County Council
Member Harte clarified that the primary focus of this process had been the formation of
a housing authority, its structure and funding. At the same time, they found
overwhelming support from the stakeholders for the focused, regional housing authority
plan and the ability to harness the power of all the organizations.

County Council Member Robinson asked for a list of stakeholders. The following
stakeholders participated: Mountainlands Community Housing Trust, Habitat for
Humanity, Park City Community Foundation (PCCF), Christian Center of Park City
(CCPC), Garbett Homes, J Fisher, Henry Sigg, Tony Tyler, Park City Board of Realtors,
Cowboy Partners, the Chamber of Commerce, Vail, Utah Preservation Fund, Peter
Tomai, Rory Murphy, and the three school districts.

City Council Member Gerber asked if there were additional federal funds, besides the
federal vouchers, worth pursuing with a housing authority if there were no federal
vouchers available. County Council Member Harte indicated that the choice vouchers
were the only ones not available, but all the other funding mechanisms would still be
available. Park City Council Member Dickey queried if we were building more
bureaucracy without much benefit. County Chair Armstrong was not quite ready to
support the remaining funding questions. Further, he wondered if the school districts
had a source of funding at the legislative level to provide employee housing and
whether those were relationships that could be leveraged. He asked for direction to
continue to move forward with this proposal with its feasibility and budget questions. He
also asked if there was support for one authority or focused internal operations.

County Council Member Stephens urged a pros and cons list to help guide this
discussion in the future and County Council Member Robinson agreed. Park City Mayor
Worel asked what would happen to the existing deed restricted housing stock each
entity had if a regional authority was formed. County Council Member Harte asserted
each entity would retain them and they would not be transferred to the housing
authority. Furthermore, if a housing authority existed, it would not preclude entities from
pursuing internal projects and noted not every housing project had to be within the
regional housing authority boundaries. It was decided to continue to move forward with
this process.

CHILDCARE UPDATE
Michelle Downard, Park City Municipal Resident Advocate, along with Park City Human
Resources Director Sarah Mangano, presented this item. Downard indicated that they
were currently working on a draft childcare recommendation for City Council for
November, put forth by the working group which would include the recommendation to
establish a task force to decide next steps to build capacity and address affordability.
Park City Mayor Nann Worel proposed that this working group be expanded county-
wide to include school districts, the Chamber and Summit County to partner with and
look at long-range goals to increase capacity and a sustainable funding source, since it
was not only a Park City boundary issue.

County Chair Armstrong stated the County discovered that their impact fees for
childcare facilities came in extremely high so they cut those a lot to make it easier to get
a facility open and noted that there were things the County could do in the permitting
process as well to encourage new facilities to open. He also emphasized the push for
businesses to provide childcare, whether onsite if they were able, or to subsidize
childcare for their employees. He directed the County Manager to look at subsidizing
childcare and/or providing childcare for their own employees as a benefit and to include
it in the budget process. He noted that there were a lot of big developments coming and
suggested getting them to think about developing a childcare space.

Park City Council Member Gerber noted federal funding for childcare was cut and the
childcare centers were feeling that pinch right now. There were two phases in the City
plan: stabilization and capacity building. It was always more expensive to create
something than to stabilize it, however, the $1,000,000 in funding was not enough.
Many childcare providers she had spoken with were approaching retirement age and
they noted that it was too hard to continue. She conceded that there were some large
businesses who might be able to provide childcare, however, it was the small
businesses that would struggle to provide it.
