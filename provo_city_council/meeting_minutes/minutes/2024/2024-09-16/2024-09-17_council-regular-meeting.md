1.          A recognition of Provo City’s September 2024 Employee of the Month (24-008) 0:08:43

Mayor Kaufusi expressed her privilege in recognizing an outstanding employee whose exceptional
dedication benefits Provo's community. She introduced Doug Robins, the Parks and Recreation Director,
to present the September 2024 Employee of the Month award to a remarkable member of the Parks
and Recreation Department.

Doug Robins recognized Rylee Snelson being selected for the award. He highlighted Rylee’s remarkable
18-year tenure with the Parks and Recreation Department. Rylee currently serves as a project manager,
overseeing projects ranging from minor facility upgrades to significant recreational initiatives, including
the adaptive Universal Play Center at North Park and the massive Regional Sports Park. Robins praised
Rylee’s dedication, problem-solving skills, and commitment to excellence, describing him as a “go-to”
person who delivers high-quality results consistently. Rylee’s proactive attitude, teamwork, and
extensive knowledge of construction have significantly reduced costs and conserved public resources for
the city. His efficient work ethic and ability to maintain positive relationships foster a strong culture of
collaboration. While demanding yet fair as a leader, Rylee sets an exemplary standard for those who
work with him. Robins shared anecdotes of Rylee briefly leaving the department but always returning,
humorously tying this to his ongoing involvement in Provo’s green spaces. He also extended gratitude to
Rylee’s family for their support of his contributions to Provo’s Parks and Recreation success, recognizing
him as a cornerstone of their nationally renowned team.

Chair MacKay read the public comment preamble and opened the public comment period.

Charlene Grecki addressed the council, sharing her struggles with homelessness since 2022. She
explained that she lives in a van with her children and three cats, facing significant hardships daily.
Charlene expressed frustration over her wait time for housing assistance through Utah County Housing,
which was initially estimated to take three years but has now extended to six years.
At 69 years old and battling stage four kidney disease, diabetes, and other health issues, Charlene
highlighted the challenges of her situation, including the lack of support from the community. She
recounted experiences of being ignored when seeking help, such as needing a jump-start for her car.
Despite her efforts to manage and care for her family under these conditions, she described feeling
unseen and unsupported.

Angela (unknown last name) shared her experience as a Provo resident since 2006 and as someone who
has faced homelessness. She recounted her decision to attend a potluck she discovered through a small,
taped-up flyer about homelessness, despite uncertain weather. Angela expressed confusion about
whether the event was city-sponsored and apologized for her misunderstanding.
She highlighted the challenges of homelessness, including her personal experience living at the Food and
Care Coalition and the temporary nature of available assistance. Angela voiced frustration over federal
funding cuts, sharing that her case with the Provo Housing Authority was closed due to a lack of funds.
She criticized the system for leaving people like her in despair while those in charge have the power to
prioritize who receives assistance. Her comments underscored the need for more reliable and equitable
support for those experiencing homelessness.

Jacob (unknown last name), a BYU student and Provo resident, raised concerns about the safety of the
intersection at 500 North and 100 East. He described the intersection as dangerous due to its limited
traffic control, with only two stop signs and reliance on drivers to yield to pedestrians—something they
often fail to do. Jacob shared personal experiences of being in the crosswalk while cars proceeded
through it, emphasizing the risks to pedestrians. He respectfully requested that the city reconsider the
intersection's design to improve safety.

Chad Kimball, accompanied by his wife Adrienne, of Provo, addressed the council about ongoing issues
near their home across from Timpview High School, which has been under construction for an extended
period. Chad explained that their cul-de-sac is frequently used by students and staff for overflow
parking, creating significant problems, particularly when school lets out. He described how the area
becomes congested with double and triple-parked cars, blocking driveways and idling while waiting for
passengers. This situation creates a hazardous environment, especially for children returning home from
school. Chad also noted that some teachers park in the cul-de-sac, sometimes partially blocking
driveways, because it is more convenient than using the designated teacher parking area that requires a
shuttle. He respectfully requested that the city consider measures to address the situation, such as
limiting parking, restricting access, or implementing designated no-parking times, to improve safety and
reduce congestion in the small cul-de-sac.

Miguel Garcia, of Provo, expressed serious concerns about alleged police corruption and misconduct
involving an officer named Justin North. He claimed that he and his spouse have been targeted by secret
policing activities, including being followed by police and federal vehicles with advanced capabilities,
which he alleged emitting harmful radiation. Miguel described experiencing harassment and
surveillance, stating that similar activities occurred at his previous residence, leading to their eviction.

5.       An ordinance updating the Urban Forestry Chapter. (24-077) 0:32:48

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-46, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

*Items 5 and 6 were discussed together*

Scott Henderson, Chief Administrative Officer, presented a proposal for consolidating the city’s forestry
and arboriculture services, which are currently divided between the Parks and Recreation and Power
departments. He explained that while both departments have dedicated, hardworking crews,
consolidating these services under one city forester in the Power department would improve efficiency.
This consolidation would enhance emergency preparedness, ensure all city crews are certified to work
around power lines, and streamline the process for addressing tree-related issues. The move is also
expected to save around $250,000 by shifting the funding from the general fund to an enterprise fund.
Additionally, it would bring salary and training balance for the crews and provide clearer lines of
responsibility, especially when tree issues are reported through the city’s 311 service. Henderson
emphasized that the consolidation aligns with the city's focus on efficiency, clarity, and better service
delivery. The proposal is in the process of budget and code adjustments, and if approved, the
consolidation could take effect by October 1.

8.       An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding signs permitted in the downtown zones.
         Downtown Neighborhood. (PLOTA20240191) 0:46:40

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2024-47, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented. He explained that this amendment was developed in
collaboration with the Downtown Business Alliance, aiming to reintroduce marquee signs as a permitted
sign type on Center Street, between 500 West and 200 East. The proposal permits up to three marquee
signs per block face, with limitations on their size, thickness, and lighting. The lighting would be
restricted to internal or external lighting only, with no neon lights allowed. This initiative is designed to
support economic development and promote local businesses along Center Street while preserving the
historic character of the area. The Planning Commission has reviewed and recommended approval of
the proposal.

Chair MacKay opened public comment.

Jan Ward, of Salem, commented that while observing the downtown area, he noticed that the trees
have grown tall, obscuring visibility of potential signs. Although the area is beautiful, the trees make it
difficult to see businesses, which may affect their ability to be noticed. He suggested that this could be
addressed to help business visibility.

With no other comments, Chair MacKay closed public comment and invited a council discussion.

Councilor Christensen expressed that discussions about signs always involve finding a balance. After
reviewing the staff proposal, he believes it strikes the right balance for downtown businesses. He
emphasized that signage is important in a downtown environment and supports the proposal to help
businesses thrive.

Char MacKay called for a vote.

      Vote:         The motion passed 7:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley, Hoban,
                    MacKay, and Whipple in favor.

Councilor Christensen expressed concern about ensuring that the stretch of the river near the
development remains a beautiful and well-maintained area. He asked what assurances citizens have
that this section will be properly cared for and enhanced.

Mr. Ardmore acknowledged that maintaining the river's beauty has been a key concern throughout the

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With no comments, she invited a council discussion.

Councilor Christensen how many rental units in phase 1 compared to phase 2.

Councilor Christensen inquired about the number of rental units in Phase One, to which Mr. Ardmore
clarified there were 195 rental units, with an additional 24 planned for Phase Two. Councilor
Christensen expressed concern about the housing crisis and emphasized the Council's responsibility to
balance rental availability with opportunities for homeownership. While acknowledging developers’
justifications for rental-heavy projects, he argued for greater efforts to promote homeownership and
voiced his intent to vote against the proposal due to the lack of balance in housing options.

Councilor Garrett acknowledged the Council’s priority to increase homeownership but emphasized the
importance of supporting a developer bringing in entrepreneurs and contributors to Provo's economic

show appreciation for the developer's efforts and contributions to the city.

expense to benefit the city by providing a well-maintained trail, potentially with benches or other

Councilor Whipple highlighted that the developer is building housing to support his tech business by

Mr. Jones provided additional historical context for the project, clarifying that Phase Two was originally
located in Orem and entitled for the same development now proposed in Provo. The developer sought
to consolidate the project under one city’s authority, prompting a request for annexation into Provo.

for the River Trail and public trail easement. Mr. Jones emphasized that the developer is requesting
approval for a project that could have been built in Orem but was moved to Provo due to the

11.      A resolution approving the appropriation of $13,743 in the General Fund for a contractual
         sales tax increment post-performance payment to Day’s Market. (24-011) 1:02:23

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2024-47, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Keith Morey, Economic Development Division Director, presented. He discussed the success of sales tax
reimbursement agreements with various shopping areas in the community, including the Days Market
area. These agreements aimed to stabilize the environment and increase traffic to these areas. In
exchange for improvements made by the shopping areas, the city agreed to share a portion of the
increased sales tax for a limited time, as a demonstration of partnership and support for economic
development. The current contribution for this project is $13,743, bringing the total reimbursement
over five years to $64,000. This initiative has been successful, and while the reimbursements will
eventually end, it has proven to be a valuable tool for supporting local development.

Councilor Garrett asked about termination of payments due to changes in legislation.

Mr. Morey explained that the agreement for sales tax reimbursement has a limited time and cannot be
renewed due to a state legislative change made two years ago. While the existing agreements, like this
one, can continue until their term ends, no new agreements can be created. He clarified that the state's
decision to eliminate these agreements was not due to legality concerns but to prevent cities from
incentivizing retailers to move from one community to another, which could negatively impact local
economies. He expressed that while he understood the state's intent, it had eliminated beneficial
opportunities like this one.

12.      A resolution approving and adopting the Amended Project Area Plan for the Freedom Plaza
         Community Development Project Area. (24-078) 1:06:56

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2024-RDA-09-17-1, as currently constituted,
                   has been made by council rule.

Mr. Morey explained that when a city creates a redevelopment project area, it defines a map to notify
taxing entities of potential changes in property tax distribution. The original project area boundary was
set in 2010, but after the court designated the area for a new courthouse, the boundary shifted.
Although everyone agreed to this shift, the formal process to update the boundary was never
completed. Mr. Morey clarified that the notice had been sent to all taxing entities, and now they are
seeking approval from the Redevelopment Agency to officially approve the corrected project area
boundary.

Chair Whipple opened the item for public comment. With no comments, she invited a discussion from
the board.

Board Member Christensen emphasized that prior to City Council and Redevelopment Agency meetings,
members receive a binder with detailed information to review. They also have a work session to go
through the details thoroughly. He pointed out that the information is publicly available online, not a
secret, and that a lot of thought goes into the decisions rather than simply "rubber stamping" them.

Chair Whipple stated that this is the final step in a lengthy process that involved extensive conversations
and research. With no further board discussion, she called for a vote on the resolution.

      Vote:         The motion passed 7:0 with Board Members Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, Handley,
                    Hoban, MacKay, and Whipple in favor.
