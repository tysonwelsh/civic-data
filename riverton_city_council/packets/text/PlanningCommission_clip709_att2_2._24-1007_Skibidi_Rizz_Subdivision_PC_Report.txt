Background

The applicant did not return updated drawings and documents before the PC packets were published. The
updated submittal is critical to show a key piece of information which the PC must evaluate and decide.

This is a public hearing for a preliminary subdivision of property located at 13341 South Trestle
Lane, located on the north side of 13400 South. This property is in the southern center of the city
and has approximately 117-feet of frontage on Trestle Lane.

The existing parcel is about 1.25-acres. The property is zoned RR-22, which is minimum ½-acre
lots with animal rights. All adjoining and nearby properties are similarly zoned. The proposed lots
meet the zoning ordinance for size and dimensions, thus making a rezone unnecessary. The
General Plan for the area is Estate Density Residential (minimum 21,000-sf lots).

The applicant is proposing to create a second lot, with each lot being 0.50 acres or more. The
existing house will remain on Lot 2 and access Trestle Lane. A new home will be built on Lot 1.

The development will be served by a new private lane on the south side of the parcel. The
Planning Commission approved the private lane to service both lots at the June 13, 2024 PC
meeting. The paved width of the private lane will be 20-feet. A fire turnaround will be located
near the western boundary of lot 1. The lane will be marked with a street sign identifying it as a
private lane and no city maintenance will occur on the private lane or on infrastructure in the
private lane. Utilities in this subdivision will be privately owned and maintained.

      A.     CHECKETTS PRIVATE LANE, PLZ-24-2021, A CONDITIONAL USE
             PERMIT APPLICATION FOR A PRIVATE LANE TO ACCOMMODATE TWO
             LOTS LOCATED AT 13341 SOUTH TRESTLE LANE. APPLICANT – KELLY
             CHECKETTS.

City Planner, Lisa Halversen presented the Staff Report and stated that the request is for a
1.25-acre property situated in the south-central area of the City, bordering Trestle Lane and
abutting the Utah Salt Lake Canal. The proposal involves subdividing the property, which
currently includes a house on the west side with access to Trestle Lane, to create an
additional lot in the eastern section.

The zoning is RR-22 as are the neighboring properties. The land meets current zoning
regulations for subdivision. An Owner's Affidavit was provided. No responses were received
from the public from the mailed notices. The original plan was presented with a proposed
configuration for the private lane during the Design Review Committee (“DRC”) Meeting, it
may be adjusted slightly. The lane, which would service a maximum of two lots, needs to
be at least 20 feet wide and extend 216 feet in depth. The turnaround is in compliance with
Fire Code regulations.

Ms. Halversen believed that the property meets the qualifications for a private lane due to
the absence of planned City roads in the area, the lack of existing local streets extending
towards the property, and the fact that access for neighboring properties would not be
impacted. Staff recommended approval with the conditions enumerated in the Staff Report.
Commissioner Rushton asked if Trestle Lane was designated as a public road, as opposed
to a private lane. It was confirmed that that was the case. He further sought clarification

regarding the configuration of the private lane and the potential impact on access for
Sandstone, LLC. He noted that while the applicant appeared to own the majority of the land,

Commissioner Rushton addressed animal rights for both the original parcel and the
subdivided parcel. Staff confirmed that both parcels will retain animal rights even without
including the acreage of the private lane, both parcels exceed the minimum of 0.5 acres,
granting them animal rights under the RR 22 Zoning Code. A concern was raised that if the
lane exceeds 150 feet in length, it might need to be wider than 20 feet. Ms. Halversen
responded that the width requirement was solely based on the number of lots accessing the
lane. According to Code a wider lane is mandated for situations exceeding two lots or 150
feet in length. The original application specified a planned width of 23 feet with the final
width being determined according to the City standards and Fire Department requirements.
If approved, the lane would need to meet the revised width requirements, potentially
necessitating an increase in width from the originally proposed 23 feet.

Commissioner Cluff asked about a narrow strip of property situated in front of the subject
property. She sought clarification on whether it was an easement, a potential concern, or
simply an inaccuracy on the map. It was reported that the strip in question is part of an
ongoing City project on Trestle Lane and has already been acquired by the City. It was
noted that the map had not yet been updated to reflect this change.

A question was raised regarding the structure located behind the property that was believed
to be owned by Sandstone, LLC. Clarification was sought on whether the structure was a
residence, a barn, or a garage and if there had been any discussions concerning the
possibility of the two property owners combining their entrances into a single access point,
eliminating one of the entrances. It was clarified that the outbuilding on the Sandstone
property served as a business entrance. The applicant, Kelly Checketts, stated that the
opening being constructed by the City in the road would be a full 40 feet wide, eliminating
the need for two separate entrances. He further stated that shared access onto Trestle Lane
would be possible due to the absence of a fence between their property and the neighboring
property line.

Mr. Checketts elaborated on the project, explaining that the property had been recently
purchased. His brother resides across the street. Due to allergies to most large animals,
he saw no need to maintain the entire 1.25-acre property. However, a desirable feature was
a sizeable lot bordering the canal. Research was conducted to confirm compliance with City
regulations. It appeared that the subdivision plan largely met the requirements. The
intention was to subdivide the property, sell the existing home on the front portion, and
construct a new home on the rear portion.

       B.     RIVER RIDGE AMENDED SUBDIVISION, PLZ-24-1003, AN APPLICATION
              TO COMBINE TWO LOTS INTO ONE, LOCATED AT 13214 AND 13198
              SOUTH ARIAT COVE. APPLICANT - DAVID FREISS.

Ms. Halversen presented the Staff Report and stated that the request involved a lot line
adjustment within the River Ridge Lane Subdivision. The original 16-lot subdivision was
approved in 2019. They are estate-density lots with a minimum lot size of one-half acre.
The applicant, David Freiss, proposed merging Lots 104 and 105 to create a single, one-
acre lot suitable for constructing a large house. Rumors suggest that the home could
potentially be part of the Parade of Homes.

It was noted that the request involves a simple combination of the two lots with no
modifications planned for the common lot lines or utility connections. According to the DRC
Meeting conducted with the applicant and the Fire Department, a comment was received
from the neighbor to the north, who expressed a preference for a more southerly placement
of the turnaround relative to their gate. Staff clarified that the specific location of the
turnaround within Code specifications remained at the applicant's discretion.

Documents were presented for reference, including the mailing sent to residents,
Mr. Freiss's application, and the subdivision plat. The plat depicted the two lots along with
dotted lines illustrating the proposed combination. The preliminary location for the temporary
turnaround is positioned slightly south. The temporary turnaround would be removed upon
completion of the road. Staff reiterated that it could not be used as part of a driveway and
that parking on it would be prohibited. Staff recommended approval with the conditions
enumerated in the Staff Report.

Commissioner Rushton expressed confusion due to the presence of a gated entrance,
suggesting that road construction might never occur. He sought clarification on whether the
turnaround should be considered permanent. Ms. Halversen confirmed that the designation
of the turnaround is temporary until the road is built. It was acknowledged that this could
take a significant amount of time, potentially 20, 25, or even 30 years but will likely be
removed in the future.
