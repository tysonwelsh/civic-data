MRD Zone Text Amendments
          Deputy Planning Manager Brierley reported a petition has been filed by The Boyer Company to amend the warehousing
size limit and food manufacturing restrictions in the Manufacturing, Research, and Development (MRD) zone. Due to shifts in
industry requirements, the petitioner feels it is necessary to increase the maximum warehouse size above the current limit, which
is 50,000 square feet. He presented an aerial image to identify the current uses in BDO, including the MRD zone and DDR zone.
The MRD zone was transitional in nature and located on vacant lands. Warehousing is not as heavy a use as other uses that are
allowed in the MRD zone, so the Planning Commission found the increased limit to be acceptable under the guidelines of the
General Plan. The Planning Commission also recommended setting a 50-foot height limit for buildings along 1200 West, which is
the separating line between homes and BDO. Additionally, the petitioner feels that food manufacturing would be an acceptable
use in the MRD zone, and the Planning Commission concurred, with the condition that noise, smell, and fumes need to be self-
contained.
          There was brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierly regarding permitted uses in the MRD zone and other areas
of the City in which the zoning designation would be appropriate.
          Council Deputy Director Symes stated that ifthere are no objections from the Council, staff can schedule public input
and an action item on a future meeting agenda. No objections were voiced.                                                                        )

Parking Violations Fee Amendments
          Senior Accountant Johns reported Administration is proposing a change in parking citation fees. The proposed
amendment is designed to increase fees to cover projected costs of running the parking citation program. When compared to
similar governing entities, Ogden's parking fees are quite low, but this proposal would increase minimum fees to help sustain the
citation program. This amendment would also simplify the fee schedule, clarify where parking enforcement may occur in the city,
and streamline the process for citation appeals. The impetus for these changes is the implementation of new software, the need to
consolidate and simply fee schedules, and the need to cover costs and increase revenues. The scope of the proposed amendments
includes addressing infractions of existing parking ordinances, and not addressing City parking needs or general parking policies.
He then presented the current parking citation fee structure and offered a comparison with the proposed adjustments. The current
ordinance provides for infractions on certain types of public property and the proposed change would clarify that all public
property is subject to enforcement. The current ordinance allows for a reduction of penalties based on the appeal process and the
proposed change would remove the penalty reduction in favor of citation dismissal if an appeal is successful.
          There was brief discussion among the Council, Mr. Johns, and Police Department staff regarding the various citation
categories, time frames for resolving a citation, and escalating fees for unpaid citations. There was a brief focus on the areas of the
City where parking infractions are more prevalent than others.

Subdivision Recording Fees
         City Recorder Hansen explained the City's current process for recording fmal subdivision plats is burdensome for both
the Recorder's office and the applicant. The Recorder's Office is seeking to simplify the recording process for fmal subdivision
plats. The following is a summary of Ogden City's current process for accepting and recording fmal subdivision plats:
                1. Final subdivision plat is approved and is ready to be recorded at the county offices.
               2. The City Recorder's Office sends the plat with a staff member to the Weber Center.
               3. The city staff member must meet with the applicant at the Weber Center to pay recording fees.
         It is inconvenient for the applicant to have to meet with City staff at the County offices just to pay the recording fees. It is
also inconvenient for staff because they must schedule their visit to the County building based on the applicant's availability. Ms.
Hansen noted she is proposing a change in fee structure so the applicant can pay the County's recording fees at the City
Recorder's Office. The Recorder's Office would then pass the applicant's payment through to the County offices. This would save
the applicant from having to meet the Recorder at both the City building and the County building. Recording fees are standardized

Fiscal Year 2022 Arts Grants
          Council Policy Analyst Watkins explained each year the Ogden City Council appropriates grant funding to various local
arts organizations and programs. The Ogden Arts Advisory Committee and Administration review each application and provide
recommendations to the Council. The FY2022 budget includes $1 00,000-plus there is a leftover amount of$7 ,000 from FY2021,
for a total of $107,000.
          Arts, Culture, and Events Manager McBride stated Administration, along with the Ogden City Arts Advisory Committee,
has submitted its recommendations for arts grants. The recommendations include summaries and analyses from the cmmnittee that
satisfy state law. There were 32 total applications this year, and the Administration is recommending that each organization
receive some level of funding except for three, which were deemed ineligible.
          Arts Coordinator Buckley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the Arts Grants recommendations; for
FY 2021/2022, $100,000 has been allocated for arts grants. An additional $7,000 ofFY 2020/2021rollover grant funds have been
added for a total of $107,000. The City Council's role in the grant process is to make the detennination that the value of the
tangible and intangible public benefits that will result from the proposed grants are equal to or exceed the monetary value of the
grants. This year 32 grant applications were received. Upon review the Arts Grants Review Sub-Committee, and the full Arts
Advisory Cmmnittee, recommends that 29 grant applications merit funding. Ten general support grants totaling $43,500, and 19
project support grants totaling $63,500 for a total of$107,000 in Fiscal Year 2021-22. She presented a list of the entities that will
receive General Support grants as well as a list ofthose that will receive Project Support grants. The total requested for General
Support was $87,275 and the total requested for Project Support was $123,676, for a grand total of $210,951. The amount of
funding available ($1 07 ,000) will be divided with a 40/60 split for General Support/Project Support, respectively. She then noted
grants are reviewed with a rigorous scoring process, using the following review criteria:
               •   Quality of the artistic mission and programs offered.
               • Number of creatives and community members served.
               • Access for underserved populations. Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion.
               • Applicant shows a strong ability to implement project.
               • Applicant shows a strong marketing plan and a solid budget.
               • Project aligns with goals and objectives from the Arts Master Plan.
          The Ogden City Arts Grants are competitive. Some applications are not chosen for funding. General reasons for funding
denial are: application scored below funding threshold using review criteria; project does not serve the cmmnunity of Ogden;
incomplete application, or budget issues.
          Council member Stephens commended the Arts Advisory Committee for their efforts to evaluate arts grants applications
and provide these recommendations to the City Council. There was a brief discussion about efforts to advertise the Arts Grants
program to increase the number of applicants each year. There was also a brief discussion about the structure of the Arts Advisory
Committee. In conclusion, there was brief discussion about public arts projects that have been completed in the community with
the help of Arts Grants.
