RESOLUTION 21-42: AUTHORIZE EXPENDITURES FOR THE UTAH
VETERANS MEMORIAL PARK
Wayne Pyle, City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 21-42 that would authorize
expenditures for the Utah Veterans Memorial Park.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The West Valley City (Utah Veterans Memorial Park) located at 3234 South
       Cultural Center Drive. This will be Phase 1 for the Utah Veterans Memorial Center.
       The Phase 1 Project will include the garden and memorial park area as depicted in
       the concept drawings. The construction budget is projected to be about
       $1,400,000.00. The anticipated schedule for a grand opening on November 11th
       2021. This resolution will provide partial funding from the City General funds. The
       other funding to complete Phase 1 will include $300,000 from the foundation
       created by the Mayor and participants’ efforts, as well as matching funds to the
       $500,000 that will come from other potential sources as previously discussed.

The City Council will consider Resolution 21-42 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
March 23, 2021 at 6:30 P.M.

RESOLUTION 21-43: APPROVE AMENDMENT #2 TO THE INTERLOCAL
AGREEMENT BETWEEN WEST VALLEY CITY AND SALT LAKE COUNTY
FOR SECURITY SERVICES AT THE WEST VALLEY COUNTY LIBRARY
Scott Buchanan, Deputy Police Chief, presented proposed Resolution 21-43 that would
approve Amendment #2 to the Interlocal Agreement between West Valley City and Salt Lake
County for security services at the West Valley County Library.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West Valley City Police Department first entered into this agreement in 2018 and
       extended in 2020.

       The Salt Lake County library provides a benefit to the people of West Valley
       City. However, calls for service to a library are common. Assigning on-duty
       officers to the library as a dedicated assignment will alleviate the burden the
       library places on the West Valley City Police Department and provide a
       continuous police presence to assist the library in protecting its property and
       insuring order.

Mayor Bigelow noted that there have been issues at this library and this is an important
agreement for the City to have. Councilmember Christensen asked if there is any concern
at the Hunter Library. Deputy Chief Buchanan replied that this was requested by the West
Valley Library and Hunter Library has not reached out with concern. He noted that the
Police Department has a great relationship with the libraries in the City.

The City Council will consider Resolution 21-43 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
March 23, 2021 at 6:30 P.M.

CONSIDER APPLICATION PUD-1-2020, FILED BY RUSSELL PLATT,
REQUESTING FINAL PLAT APPROVAL FOR THE ALPIZAR PLANNED UNIT
DEVELOPMENT
Steve Lehman, CED, presented application PUD-1-2020, filed by Russell Platt, requesting
final plat approval for the Alpizar Planned Unit Development.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Mr. Russ Platt, representing the property owner, is requesting final approval for a
       planned residential development to be known as Alpizar PUD. The property was
       recently rezoned from the C-2 Zone to the RM Zone.

       The Alpizar Planned Unit Development will be a residential community consisting
       of 50 townhome units on just over 3 acres. Proposed townhomes will be 3 stories
       with two different building types. Proposed unit sizes are 1,750 square feet for the
       west units and 1,950 square feet for the east units. Each unit will have 3 bedrooms
       and a bonus room that could be used for an office or similar.

       The architecture is very urban. All units will have a roof top patio which has been
       used successfully in the Cobalt Landing development on Redwood Road. Proposed
       building materials are stucco, fiber cement siding and tile. All townhome units will
       have a two-car garage for parking. Units on the east side will have driveways for
       additional parking. 34 guest spaces will be provided for the west units which do
       not have driveways. Overall, the site will be parked at 3.5 spaces per unit.

             Access to the subdivision will be gained from 3500 South. All internal roads will
             be private. The project has a large central open space on the west side of the
             development. During the rezone, the applicant agreed to install courtyards, rooftop
             patios for all units, a tot lot/playground, a walking path and BBQ and picnic areas,
             some of which will be located within the central open space.

      Councilmember Lang asked if the units to the south are three-story. Steve replied yes but
      noted that there is a healthy setback from the residential properties. Steve detailed the
      elevation and the location of the property lines. Councilmember Lang indicated she is
      concerned with the height of the buildings and their proximity to residential.
      Councilmember Nordfelt asked if the Council can place restrictions on a PUD. Steve
      replied yes. He indicated that he will speak with the developer to see if there is flexibility
      on building height.

      The City Council will consider PUD-1-2020 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
      March 23, 2021 at 6:30 P.M.

B.     STREET LIGHTING FEE FOLLOW-UP DISCUSSION
       Mayor Bigelow stated he revisited the discussion and is up to speed.
       Councilmember Huynh thanked the Council for their streetlight conversation. He
       indicated this is an important conversation to have and can be detrimental to the
       community in both safety and appearance. Mayor Bigelow stated that streetlights
       provide a quality of life and safety for residents. He indicated that the City is falling
       behind as time passes and a $3 increase would cover the cost of maintenance and
       repair. Mayor Bigelow stated that the problem won’t be solved in a short amount
       of time. He indicated that Councilmember Lang was concerned about sidewalks
       and noted that staff suggested using the general fund balance that is currently being
       used for streetlights and applying that to sidewalks. Councilmember Buhler stated
       that he feels the City is committed to sidewalks and lighting and the question is
       how and when to fund it. Councilmember Lang asked if this money would be in its
       own enterprise fund that is only dedicated to streetlight repair, maintenance, etc.
       She stated that she would like to ensure that the funds are used in the way they are
       meant to. Wayne replied that this is something that the City can do. Councilmember
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – MARCH 16, 2021

      Lang indicated that she would be supportive of that and noted that this would
      provide a benchmark to measure progress. Councilmember Nordfelt asked if all
      properties would be charged. Russ replied all properties with streetlights which is
      almost the entire City with the exception of a few commercial properties.
      Councilmember Nordfelt stated that he feels it may be more feasible to take this fee
      from a property tax raise. He expressed concern that smaller properties and homes
      will be paying the same rate as larger more expensive ones. Russ stated that the
      intent with a streetlight fee would be that the City could charge all properties,
      including schools, churches, government, etc. If this was removed, it may change
      the revenue significantly. Mayor Bigelow stated that the options are to do nothing,
      get funding from taxes, or charge a monthly fee. Mayor Bigelow stated it can be
      easy to get support on new buildings or roads but can be difficult to get funding for
      maintenance and repair. He indicated that it is important for the City to maintain its
      appearance for both aesthetics and cost effectiveness. Councilmember Huynh
      stated that he feels it’s important to do something. Councilmember Buhler agreed
      and stated that he feels the City has a responsibility to maintain infastructure.
      Councilmember Christensen agreed and stated that things will continue to get
      worse. Councilmember Lang indicated that she is in favor of a change when staff
      is prepared. Russ stated that it could be several months before work can be planned
      but the ordinance could be prepared in several weeks. Councilmember Lang stated
      that she is in favor of a fee as opposed to a tax increase. Councilmember Fitisemanu
      suggested a proportional fee. The Mayor and Council continued to discuss options
      of funding and how fees could be applied. The Council agreed to move forward
      with a fee and would like an ordinance to be proposed with an effective date.
