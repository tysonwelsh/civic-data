ZONE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATIONS
ZT-3-2024 Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting amendments to Chapters 7-1, 7-2,
          7-6, 7-7, 7-9, 7-11, 7-13, and 7-14 of the zoning ordinances to define the term
          masonry wall, require a certain type of masonry wall for double frontage lots
          along arterial streets, and update the standards on exterior building materials.
          (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:    Steve presented. In summary, the amendment is not a substantial change, it's
               mainly trying to use the same language across all the different places in the code
               where it refers to materials, and allowing surfaces like fiber, cement and
               architectural concrete. An exception to that would be in a multi-family situation.
               We feel architectural concrete is not a great fit for a multi-family building.


ZT-4-2024      Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting an amendment to Section 11-5-
               106 of the sign ordinance to prohibit billboards in the Decker Lake Station
               Overlay Zone, Jordan River Overlay Zone, Bangerter Highway Overlay Zone,
               and 5600 West Overlay Zone. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or
               steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)
Discussion:
               Steve presented. Currently, in the sign code, there is one standard that billboards
               shall not be allowed in gateway-oriented overlay zones, regardless of the
               underlying zone. The problem with that language is that it's too vague. We don't
               notice the gateway. “Orient overlay zones” isn't capitalized. And it doesn't
               callout a specific overlay zone. To be clear, the code would state that billboards
               can't be in the names of the overlay zones. There's no ambiguity.

               Brandon Hill clarified that because of what the State law is, it’s possible that a
               billboard could be relocated into one of those zones under certain circumstances.
               That it’s not a blanket guarantee that a billboard will ever be there, but it limits
               billboards to only circumstances where it's required by state code.

CONDITIONAL USE APPLICATIONS
C-6-2024  Petition by JK INVESTMENTS LLC requesting conditional use approval for an
          Outside storage yard. The property is located on three parcels at 2125/2145 S
          7200 W and 7165 W 2100 S on 7.92 acres and is zoned M (Manufacturing).
          (Staff – Brock Anderson at 801-963-3361or brock.anderson@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:    Steve presented. This is one will look familiar to the Commission. It was
               approved last year, but they let their approval lapse and never got their
               improvements in. This is a conditional use from JK investments. They’re
               requesting conditional use approval for outside storage of construction,
               equipment, and materials. The plans are essentially the same as the previous
               application. There will be an access on 7200 West and 2100 South. On the far
               north edge of the city there will be a paved area with some parking and a small
               building, but most of the area is where they will have storage for construction,
               equipment and staging. In terms of requirements, they are required to put in
               improvements along 7200 West 2400 South, landscaping along the front edge,
               surfacing in the parking lot, screening the storage areas, and getting a permit for
               the structure. There were no questions or comments from commissioners.

 DISCUSSION ITEMS
           Report items that were reviewed by PC and took a different direction after
           Council.

               The first one was a property just south of the river canal and west of Mountain
               View Corridor, about 38 acres, where there was a request to rezone from
               agricultural zone to manufacturing. Nick Mingo was the applicant, and the
               property was owned by Ivory. Planning Commission recommended approval, but
               then Council denied that request, with no further discussion or actions by the
               Council. The Council went in a different direction.
              The second one was a zone change within the salvage yard area just west of 5600
              West and about 2300 South. Carlson distributing was the applicant. They had an
              older metal building that they had acquired and wanted to use it for their truck
              repair. The Planning Commission recommended approval and when it went to
              Council, they denied it. Several months later they came back with an amended
              proposal and asked if the Council would reconsider. They did approve after the
              developer committed to more improvements on the property. The council was
              favorable to the change in zoning, but just wanted them to do a little more in
              terms of improving the property. There was only one they just outright denied it,
              and there's no further changes. The second one was initially denied, but then
              approved.

              Martell asked if Steve knew what the difference of opinion was. Steve recalled
              that some of the Council felt the area should be residential. Historically, the
              canal has been the dividing line between industrial and residential, meaning
              residential south of the Canal Industrial on the north side.

              The third one is the north section of 5600 West and 3100 South. There is about
              872 acres of ground that could be developed in the city as residential. It’s not all
              vacant property, as there are areas planned for industrial or commercial. If that
              property were developed as we have it, either zoned or planned, that would lead
              to about another 4,200 units of housing. West Valley City would get about a 10%
              bump in housing units. At that point there'd be no more land left and the only
              way to get more housing would be to redevelop or change industrial and
              commercial to residential. Steve asked if the Commissioners feel West Valley
              City needs more, or less housing.

              Cindy asked how the Council feels about it. Steve replied that the same question
              was asked of Council and about 10 responses said they were for growth and 5
              members said they don’t want more growth of residents. Steve does know that
              the State does want West Valley City to have more housing built. Brandon
              agreed that the State might move in a mandated direction to require more
              housing to be built. Steve mentions the Council has said they want a lot of the
              remaining single-family homes to be low density, like 2 to 3 units to the acre, So
              even though you've got a lot more land reserved for single family, because it's
              low density you don’t that many units. Martell would like the Council to
              reconsider the zone requirement to increase the RE threshold to 3 or 5 units to
              the acre before requiring bigger single-family home lots or have a mixture of lot
              sizes.
