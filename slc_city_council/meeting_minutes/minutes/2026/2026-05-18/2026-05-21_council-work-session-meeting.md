**Fiscal Year 2026-27 Budget: Department of Public Lands**   ~ 1:00 p.m.
  45 min.
The Council will receive a briefing about the proposed Department of Public Lands budget for Fiscal Year 2026-27.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Thursday, May 21, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date -  Tuesday, April 21, 2026 and Tuesday, May 19, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, May 19, 2026 and Tuesday, June 2, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - TBD

**Summary:**

Allison Rowland introduced the proposed Fiscal Year 2026-27 budget for the Department of Public Lands and noted that, excluding the Golf Fund, the department budget would increase modestly while continuing to absorb utility, contract, and personnel cost increases.

Kim Shelley, alongside Tyler Mudock, then presented the department’s updated mission, recent accomplishments, operational efficiencies, requested budget increases, and proposed reductions. The presentation highlighted Jordan River Trail vegetation removal funded through a state grant, free community events and volunteer efforts, Glendale Park and pickleball court improvements, asset management planning, additional maintenance obligations tied to new properties and amenities, and a request for wildland-urban interface fire mitigation funding. Department representatives also described proposed reductions affecting administrative costs, operations, charges and services, park security, and Park Ranger staffing.

Council Members and staff discussed:

* Whether proposed reductions would affect timelines for projects funded through the 2022 general obligation bond
* The city’s sycamore tree health after a recent late frost/freeze
* Water conservation and irrigation upgrades and updates, including maintenance implications for new properties
* The role and expected impacts of reducing Park Ranger staffing
* The native plant propagation program and addition of native plants into public parks
* The Regional Athletic Complex fee structure and a need for further analysis of economic growth opportunities
* Overall improved maintenance and responsiveness in parks and public spaces

**Council Requests:**

* Council Member Puy requested additional information on the Folsom Trail and what nearby residents should expect from the project.
* Council Member Puy requested follow-up regarding tree losses on North Temple, maintenance expectations for newer tree plantings along 300 West, and additional information about the Thousand Trees Program. Staff responded that they would follow up with responses to Council Member Puy’s requests.
* Council Member Wharton requested a follow-up on the proposed Foothills subcommittee within the Parks, Natural Lands, Urban Forestry & Trails Advisory Board (PNUT) and emphasized the importance of including non partisan members in that work. Staff confirmed a follow-up and estimated they would provide that information in June.
* Council Member Petro requested clearer performance measures for the Park Ranger program, including objective measures of success and how the program should be evaluated as a use of public funds.

*Council Member Puy left during this item.*

**Summary:**

Allison Rowland introduced the proposed Golf Fund budget and explained that revenues were projected to continue increasing, allowing the fund to support a larger capital improvement program while reducing reliance on General Fund support.

Matt Kammeyer described six years of financial improvement, growth in play and revenue, planned capital investments, and the request to add one full-time position. The presestation also highlighted recent division accomplishments, including golf path repairs, continued design of the Rose Park irrigation project, and installation of golf simulators at the Forest Dale golf course. Staff said the division’s long-term goal remained self-sufficiency and that user fees were helping fund major deferred capital needs.

Council Members and staff discussed:

* The Golf division’s financial improvement over the last six years and its continued progress toward becoming self-sustaining
* Revenue growth, increased demand, and how playable days and utilization affect budget projections
* Water conservation practices at golf courses, including irrigation upgrades, drought-response measures, reduced cart washing, and dry areas residents may notice on course properties
* Council Member appreciation for the Golf division’s operational and financial turnaround and for ongoing improvements at City golf courses

**Summary:**

Kate Werrett introduced the proposed Department of Public Services budget and identified key policy issues, including staffing changes street maintenance funding, and a proposed public electric vehicle charging fee.

Jorge Chamorro, alongside Julie Crookston and JP Goats, outlined the department’s organization, recent accomplishments, operational efficiencies, overall proposed budget reduction, and specific reductions and requests. The discussion included assumption of responsibility for the Ballpark and Old Library (The Leonardo) building, consolidation of cleaning functions into the Urban Services Division, improved response times for occupied recreational vehicles and bioclean requests, roadway condition improvements, and reductions in contracted cleanup services and vacant crossing guard positions.

Council Members and staff discussed:

* The proposed public electric vehicle charging station fee and the logistics of implementing a metered charging fee
* Roadway condition improvements following the Mill and Overlay Program and how roadway eligibility affects the Overall Condition Index
* Department reductions and savings, including appreciation for efforts to identify operational efficiencies
* The proposed Ballpark administrator position and the timeline for that position
* Engineering coordination and projections needed to continue maintaining roadway conditions over the next several years
* Department responsibility for the Ballpark and Old Library building, along with broader maintenance obligations for City facilities

**Fiscal Year 2026-27 Budget: Fire Department**   ~ 4:05 p.m.
  45 min.
The Council will receive a briefing about the proposed Fire Department budget for Fiscal Year 2026-27.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Thursday, May 21, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date -  Tuesday, April 21, 2026 and Tuesday, May 19, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, May 19, 2026 and Tuesday, June 2, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - TBD

**Summary:**

Michael Sanders introduced the proposed Fire Department budget, including the proposed increase, the request for 10 new full-time positions, and the use of proposed property tax revenue for staffing and operational costs.

Chief Karl Lieb and Alicea Caccavo presented department accomplishments, including retention of the department’s ISO Class 1 rating, completion of the Station 1 bay extension, a memorandum of understanding with the Salt Lake City School District, elimination of toxic aircraft firefighting foam, and several operational efficiencies. The presentation also addressed service demand, apparatus needs, and the department’s resource allocation strategy.

Council Members and staff discussed:

* Reallocation of resources to improve responsiveness and the department’s data-driven approach to deployment
* The importance of placing personnel in the right locations at the right time
* Whether the department would need another CHAT team and whether increased deployment would create more wear on vehicles
* How to ensure residents remain safe as population and service demand continue to increase in concentrated areas
* Possible overtime needs or whether additional funds should be set aside
* The department’s work in the Avenues and near the University of Utah

**Council Request:**

Council Member Puy requested staff explore ways to reduce calls associated with the University of Utah and whether there is a path to recoup some related response costs.

*Council Member Young arrived during this item.*

**Tentative Closed Meeting**   -

The Council will consider a motion to enter into closed meeting. A closed meeting described under Utah Code Annotated (UCA) Section §52-4-205 may be held for specific purposes including, but not limited to discussing:

a.     discussion of the character, professional competence, or physical or mental health of an individual.

b.     strategy sessions to discuss collective bargaining.
c.    strategy sessions to discuss pending or reasonably imminent litigation.
d.    strategy sessions to discuss the purchase, exchange, or lease of real property, including any form of a water right or water shares, if public discussion of the transaction would:

(i)    disclose the appraisal or estimated value of the property under consideration, or
(ii)     prevent the public body from completing the transaction on the best possible terms.

e.    strategy sessions to discuss the sale of real property, including any form of a water right or water shares, if:

(i) public discussion of the transaction would:

(A) disclose the appraisal or estimated value of the property under consideration, or
(B) prevent the public body from completing the transaction on the best possible terms.

(ii)     the public body previously gave public notice that the property would be offered for sale, and
(iii)     the terms of the sale are publicly disclosed before the public body approves the sale.

f.    discussion regarding deployment of security personnel, devices, or systems.
g.     investigative proceedings regarding allegations of criminal misconduct.

A closed meeting may also be held for attorney-client matters that are privileged pursuant to Utah Code § 78B-1-137, and for other lawful purposes that satisfy the pertinent requirements of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act.
