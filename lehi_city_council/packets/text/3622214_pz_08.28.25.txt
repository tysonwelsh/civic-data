 96   3.3) Public hearing and recommendation of Fox Brother Properties’ request for review of
 97   an amendment to the Grant’s Corner Development Agreement allowing automotive
 98   accessories installation and service.
 99
100   Brittney Harris presented the item. A previous development agreement from last year allowed
101   the property to function as an office warehouse. The new tenant wants to do a window-tinting
102   business. DRC recommended the automotive accessories installation and service category as
103   they think it is narrow enough.
104
105   Samuel Johnston, the tenant, was present. He clarified that heavy machinery would not be left on
106   the lot, and that any chemicals used would be nontoxic.
107
108   Commissioner Everett asked about hours of operation for the business, and about the parking
109   situation. Johnston stated that the business would run from 8-5. Any cars on the lot overnight
110   would be stored in the warehouse, so there would be no overnight parking. There is an agreement
111   to have no cars in the lot during the rodeo so they can be used by rodeo attendees.
112
113   Commissioner Everett asked if the definition was narrow enough, or if the city could get more
114   specific. Harris said that more specificity could be a part of the motion. The category
115   “automotive accessories installation and service” isn’t defined in city code, but the usual
116   procedure is to use the common definition as found online. Commissioner Everett shared that he
117   would like to narrow the agreement to the particular use being requested.
118
119   Commissioner Jackson asked legal counsel on whether to recuse himself, as he is coworkers with
120   Johnston’s wife. Craig Chambers said that as they are only passingly acquainted, he could
121   remain as part of the quorum.
122
123   Commissioner Eyre asked Johnston if he was comfortable with the Commission narrowing the
124   agreement. Johnston asked for stereo enhancements to be included as an option, as the business
125   he is running is part of a franchise that takes care of multiple aspects of vehicle improvement.
126
127   Britnney Harris looked up the category and explained that the definition is fairly narrow to after-
128   market car enhancements like new tires, stereo enhancements, window-tinting, and other similar
129   processes. The Commission shared opinions that the term “after-market” would be too broad.
130
131   Commissioner Lockhart asked if the development agreement is specific to the parcel of land or
132   only to the applying tenant. It would be tied to the parcel. Commissioner Lockhart clarified that
133   as there are several offices up for rent, other tenants would also be allowed to do the services
134   specified in the agreement. Johnston explained that in his lease he has an exclusivity agreement.
135   Commissioner Jackson asked if the development agreement could be attached to Johnston’s
136   lease. Commissioner Everett pointed out that that wouldn’t be possible as the Commission is not
137   privy to lease specifics.
138
139   Commissioner Lockhart clarified that the added uses would be specifically for this development
140   agreement and not added to the table of allowed uses in a Mixed Use zone.
141
142   Commissioner Jackson opened and closed public hearing.
143
      Lehi City Planning Commission                    3                                   August 28, 2025
144   Commissioner Everett still wanted to narrow the agreement but was open to all of the jobs
145   mentioned by Johnston. He explained that he is uncomfortable with the idea of outsourcing
146   definitions from the internet.
147
148                   Motion: Commissioner Everett moved to give a positive recommendation to the
149                   city council with the recommendation that it be narrowed in the terminology
150                   under the development agreement to keep it limited to window tinting, paint

152                   all DRC comments with the findings that this is a niche service that hasn’t been

155                   intent, and provisions of the General Plan and its various elements.
156
157   Commissioner Jackson asked if the development agreement could be expanded later on if

197   3.6) Public hearing and recommendation of Bob Perdue’s request for review of the Water’s
198   Edge General Plan Amendment on approximately 13.02 acres located at approximately
199   1300 East 175 South, changing the land use designation from Commercial to Mixed-Use.
200
201   Brittney Harris presented the item. The General Plan has the area designated as a Transit
202   Oriented District (TOD), but the actual plot is currently zoned for commercial development.
203   There was a site plan approved in 2017, which has since expired. There are currently two
204   resolutions from City Council causing issues with the project, one that prevents any development
205   until UTA provides a schedule for transit construction, and one that allows sanctuary to come in
206   for a TOD, however this property is not where the city would want a transit station, so the plot
207   doesn’t qualify. Changing the designation to Mixed Use would require them to come in with a
208   land use plan before development could begin.
209
210   Commissioner Everett clarified that the land would still be within the half-mile radius that
211   defines a TOD. His worry is losing control over how much residential and commercial
212   development happens on the property.
213
214   The applicant, Bob Perdue, was present. He explained that the area is currently surrounded by
215   commercial property, vacant land, and apartment buildings, which is why he’s opting for Mixed
216   Use. No initial plans have been submitted yet, as there are several different plans in the works.
217   The current plan is to have some hospitality development and smaller commercial projects
218   intermixed with residential properties. He has noticed some homeless encampments and
219   dumping happening on the property.
220
221   Commissioner Jackson asked about the ownership history of the property and what happened to
222   the previous site plan. Perdue explained that it was previously owned by Pat Nelson, who had
223   several different plans over the years that were prevented due to significant financial issues,
224   which put the property in foreclosure. It was purchased by the current owner about a year ago.
225
226   Commissioner Everett asked what percentage of the parcel is buildable, as it contains a large
227   section of wetlands. There are about 9.5 developable acres according to an engineering study
228   which was just completed. Commissioner Everett asked if there would be one access point to the
229   development or two. There are currently two, but the developer is working on third and fourth
230   access points, which would reach adjacent property, and cross the wetlands to provide access to
231   the Walmart parking lot. It is a large project, requiring approval from a variety of people and
232   organizations, which is what the property owner is doing now. Commissioner Eyre mentioned
233   that the last site plan contained a pedestrian access point. Perdue explained that this project
234   includes both pedestrian and auto access.
235
236   Commissioner Jackson opened and closed public comment.
237

      Lehi City Planning Commission                    5                                   August 28, 2025
238   Commissioner Everett asked about how many residential units would be allowed in a Mixed Use
239   zone as opposed to a TOD, and if there would be a limitation set on homes or rentals built. Harris
240   explained that 330 residential units would be allowed regardless of whether the lot was zoned
241   Mixed Use or TOD. The city won’t know how many units will actually be built until plans are
242   submitted to engineering. The main change that would happen if the zone changed would be the
243   city losing some amount of review over what uses would be allowed, and a change in design
244   standards.
245
246   Commissioner Everett asked if any other TODs have been developed in Lehi. There have been
247   two. Commissioner Lockhart asked why the city would be okay redesignating land that is in a
248   TOD, as they are already a contested topic municipally. Harris explained that the property has
249   been in limbo due to the two resolutions in play. Mike West explained that the purpose of the
250   resolutions was partly to pressure UTA into letting the city know where and when transit is
251   coming to avoid developing high density in areas where there may or may not be stations in the
252   future. He also shared that this anticipated station location hasn’t changed over the years, so it
253   feels slightly safer to redesignate this for high density than it does for others. Commissioner
254   Lockhart worried that changing the land use designation for the plot would undermine City
255   Council’s decision to put the resolutions in play. Commissioner Jackson shared frustration over
256   the lack of effort on UTA’s part. He has no issues with the development, as he worries more
257   about the city being left high and dry by UTA. Mike West further clarified that the resolution is
258   broad-reaching while each individual TOD is unique. The TOD being discussed today currently
259   has 15 minute bus service, so it is already a transportation hub. Harris shared that the
260   Commission could suggest repealing the resolutions in their motion.
261
262   Commissioner Everett shared that he can see this being a functioning TOD with development,
263   but worried about losing any hold the city may have over UTA by skirting the resolution. He also
264   shared worries over passing over the development and losing out on amenities that will come
265   with the project. He thinks the end result is going to be similar either way, it’s just a matter of
266   how soon the development is allowed to happen.
267
268   Commissioner Lockhart emphasized her stance of not wanting to undermine City Council, as
269   they have made their goals clear with the passed resolutions. The position of the Commission is
270   to investigate whether new development meets the city’s current established goals.
271   Commissioner Jackson agreed; he didn’t like the idea of becoming a body that dismisses City
272   Council’s decisions. The Commission agreed that while they liked the project, they generally felt
273   uncomfortable with suggesting overturning current legislation.
274
275   Commissioner Eyre clarified that the biggest difference in changing the zone would be that the
276   amount of commercial development in the area would be limited. Harris further clarified that a
277   Mixed Use zone doesn’t require as much commercial development as a TOD, but that the
278   number of residential units would remain the same. Additionally, the developer will likely want
279   to develop the non-residential land as well. The city also has ways of minimizing commercial
280   properties within the TOD, if needed.
281
282   Perdue shared that his architect is closely connected to the City Council and has been back and
283   forth with them for a long time trying to get a waiver of moratorium for the TOD. The developer
284   would prefer to develop under the TOD zoning, but they have been unable to get feedback from

      Lehi City Planning Commission                    6                                   August 28, 2025
285   either UTA or Lehi City Council. Since there has been no movement, they submitted the General
286   Plan Amendment.
287
288   Commissioner Lockhart shared that the resolutions are working as they are intended by keeping
289   developers stuck and maintaining the TODs as they are.
290
291                   Motion: Commissioner Everett moved to give a positive recommendation to the
292                   city council with the findings that the proposed amendment is not detrimental to
293                   the health, safety, and public welfare of Lehi City, and that the proposed General
294                   Plan Amendment is consistent with the land uses and zoning of nearby and
295                   adjoining properties. He included all DRC comments. Commissioner Eyre
296                   seconded.
297
298                   Vote: Commissioner Lockhart, no. Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner
299                   Eyre, yes. Commissioner Jackson, no. Commissioner Kunze, no.
300
301                   Motion failed with two in favor, three against.
302
303   Commissioner Jackson said that he would prefer to send a neutral recommendation.
304   Commissioner Eyre is against neutral recommendations as he finds them milquetoast.
305   Commissioner Lockhart worried about the potential domino effect of rezoning the TOD and
306   opening doors for other TOD plots to be rezoned. Commissioner Kunze clarified that the
307   resolution has been in place for five years without any action from UTA.
308
309                   Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to give a negative recommendation to
310                   the Lehi City Council with the recommendation that they strongly consider their
311                   resolution halting TOD development, and identify that this application is an
312                   example of how that resolution is going to undermine the concept of a TOD
313                   moving forward with or without UTA. She included all DRC comments.
314
315   Commissioner Jackson asked for the motion to be amended to include that the Commission liked
316   the project as a possible benefit for Lehi.
317
318                   Amended Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to give a negative
319                   recommendation to the Lehi City Council with the recommendation that they
320                   strongly consider their resolution halting TOD development, and identify that this
321                   application is an example of how that resolution is going to undermine the
322                   concept of a TOD moving forward with or without UTA. The verbal ideas
323                   presented for the use of the land are appealing and sound like a good concept
324                   going forward, but there is an issue with having a resolution that forces applicants
325                   to look at other designations that are inconsistent with the general plan and
326                   undermine the health, wellbeing, and future longevity of a TOD for the city as a
327                   whole in the future. She included all DRC comments. Commissioner Eyre
328                   seconded.
329
330                   Vote: Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner Everett, no. Commissioner
331                   Eyre, yes. Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes.
