 Any comments received prior to completion of the Staff Report are addressed in the Staff Report to the Planning
 Commission. The item was opened for public comment at the hearing and no comments were received.
APPLICANT RESPONSE
Key points addressed in the applicant's presentation to the Planning Commission included the following:
• The reason that the code would apply to projects with 20 units or more is so that space can be properly maintained.
   Projects with fewer than this could struggle cover the cost of upkeep for the amenity space.
• Limiting the amenity space to fifty percent outdoors at ground level is what we have done in the other zones that are
   more urban in nature to encourage more efficient use of land and to help provide more amenity space within the
   structure that can be used throughout the year.
• To help add clarity to the proposed text about what amenity space should be, a sentence could be added that describes
   what would not be desired. Developers often want to use small remnants that are not being used for building or parking
   to count for the amenity space requirement.
• The interim zone was intended to be temporary and that a new zone would replace it. The ITOD zone will have an
   overhaul in the near future where more substantial changes can be made.

PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
• Land values would drive the intensity of development and therefor eliminate the need to limit the amount of outdoor
   ground level amenity space to fifty percent.
• There could be a few changes made that would help clarify the text regarding “massing or accumulating”.
• It could be beneficial to have a minimum square footage of amenity space that would be needed to qualify as amenity
   space.
• There was a discussion about this amenity space being only being required for residential uses and not commercial
   uses. The ITOD zone has a clear

FINDINGS / BASIS OF PLANNING COMMISSION DETERMINATION
The Planning Commission identified the following findings as the basis of this decision or recommendation.
See Exhibit A for following changes to be forward on to the City Council:
• In subsection (b) replace “Only” with “Up to”
• Consider using “usable open space”
• In subsection (c) replace “massed’ with “contiguous” and add a sentence about not using remnants or unusable spaces
    for amenities

 LEGAL DESCRIPTION FOR PROPERTY TO BE REZONED
 The property to be rezoned to the MDR Zone is described in the attached Exhibit A.

 STAFF PRESENTATION
 The Staff Report to the Planning Commission provides details of the facts of the case and the Staff's analysis, conclusions,
 and recommendations. Key points addressed in the Staff's presentation to the Planning Commission included the
 following: Staff gave an overview of the request, the history of the site, and the reasons for proposing the zone change.
 Staff also updated the Planning Commission on meeting with the property owner and requesting a continuance on the
 proposal.

 NEIGHBORHOOD MEETING DATE
 • A neighborhood meeting was held on 09/03/2020.

 NEIGHBORHOOD AND PUBLIC COMMENT
 • The Neighborhood Chair addressed the Planning Commission during the public hearing.
 • Neighbors or other interested parties addressed the Planning Commission.
CONCERNS RAISED BY PUBLIC
Any comments received prior to completion of the Staff Report are addressed in the Staff Report to the Planning
Commission. Key issues raised in written comments received subsequent to the Staff Report or public comment during
the public hearing included the following:
    • Jonathon Hill reported on the neighborhood meeting, stating that those in attendance were split on supporting a
        zone change on the property. Some of the concerns were not getting a grocery store, issues with apartments on
        the property, and needing more information on what the zone change would mean for the area. He also wondered
        if additional incentives would help to bring in a grocery store on the site.
    • Rick Cox described frustration with the process for rezoning his property. He has a lease with Smiths for eight
        and half more years and cannot commit to a grocery store because Smith’s may renew the lease. He wants a
        continuance to review his options with his family and to reach out to Smith’s.
    • Mary Ann Perkins, a co-owner in the property, echoed Mr. Cox’s comments regarding a continuance and
        concerns.

APPLICANT RESPONSE
Key points addressed in the applicant's presentation to the Planning Commission included the following:
  • Staff agreed with the Planning Commission that the subject site would be best for a grocery store, but due to the
      lack of desire of Smiths to build there and the chilling effect it is having for other possibilities, the City needs to
      try something else to encourage a grocery store in west Provo.
  • Staff responded to questions of eminent domain and other possible locations for a grocery store on the west side
      of Provo and gave a comparison of the SC2 zone and the MDR zone.

PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
   • Dave Anderson wondered if any other location was viable for a grocery store on the west side of Provo and
       reviewed things that grocers look for including traffic, access, visibility, and receipt totals. He stated he would
       hate to give up this potential site. He also noted that in today’s market a MDR zone could be more value than a
       SC2 zone.
   • Deborah Jensen thought if the current owners are not doing anything, maybe a zone change is appropriate.
   • Laurie Urquiaga asked the staff if eminent domain had been considered or if the Cox family could develop on
       their own. She stated she would support a zone change here if there was another site ready for a store.
   • Ally Jones noted that it makes sense for Smiths not to build a grocery store at this location.
   • Lisa Jensen asked what initiates a taking with zone changes, it would have to lose all economic value. She also
       asked what mixed-use zones would be available to use instead. Thinks that more study is needed before deciding.
   • The Planning Commission discussed options at length and were not confident that this would be the best option
       for the property, stating that more study and background would be helpful to justify the zone change.
