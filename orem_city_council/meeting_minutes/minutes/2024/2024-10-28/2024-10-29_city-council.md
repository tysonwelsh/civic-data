Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Consolidation Plan Presentation Presenter:

Heather Cox, Management Analyst and Taylor Draney, MAG Program Administrator

Ms. Cox introduced Ms. Draney who is with MAG (Mountainland Association of
Government) and is the administrator of our CDBG program. Ms. Cox explained that every five
years HUD requires a regional consolidated plan. This plan is due in 2025.

Ms. Draney explained that last October MAG took over administration of the City of
Orem CDBG program, which is a federal grant distributed according to population. The City of
Orem gets about $600,000 each year, with an additional $350,000 unused previous year funding.
The funding categories include housing, public services, public facilities, and economic
development. An annual action plan is required and every four years a consolidated plan is
required. The projects outlined in each annual action plan must fit in the broader goals and
objectives outlined in the consolidated plan. This next consolidation plan will cover 2025-2029.
The purpose of today’s meeting is to gather high level feedback about the City Council’s
priorities.

Ms. Draney spoke about the community survey results. The citizen survey was created
and distributed throughout Utah County. The survey asked citizens to select priorities for CDBG
funds. There were 214 respondents and 59 were from Orem. The results were broken into the
funding categories. For housing the survey found that affordable housing, making homebuying
affordable and making renting more affordable were high priorities. For Community Services the
top priorities were libraries, parks/open spaces, walking/biking paths, and senior centers were the
top priorities. For streets and utilities the priorities were street improvements, sidewalk
improvements, sewer/water improvements and accessibility improvements on public streets. For
business and community support the top priorities were crime victim services, senior services,
neighborhood cleanups, and support for existing local businesses. The survey had a section for
write-in priorities and the most popular were bike lanes, utility/rental/homeowner assistance,
disability services and mental health services.

Ms. Draney talked about past Orem CDBG projects which include neighborhood
cleanups, code enforcement, critical home repair, facade improvements programs for local
businesses, homelessness services, and victim services for survivors of domestic violence. She
asked that each council member select their top 5 priorities. MAG will use the results from the
survey and the City Council to complete the CDBG consolidated Plan.

Phase 3 Bike Park Presentation Presenter: Bryce Merrill, Library and Recreation Director and
Tyler Peay, Assistant Director of Public Works-Public Services

Mr. Merrill and Mr. Peay presented the Bike Skills Park Phase 3 update. Mr. Peay
thanked Councilmember Gale for her help in this project. He gave a short history of the project.
It was conceptualized in July 2022, the Central Utah Water Conservancy District's(CUWCD) has
given approval, it will be funded in FY2025, the project should begin construction in May 2025,
an UORG grant was awarded for the project, and Avid Trails has been hired to design the park.
The next steps are to determine the design options, finalize agreements, and begin construction in
spring 2025 with a completed date of July or August 2025.

Mr. Peay presented two options for the council to review. Option A is Earthen Features
and a map of the area and the proposed trails was shown. This is essentially a three trail system.
Option B is Original scope + prefabricated features. Option B is just like option A but with the
addition of implementing some prefabricated features, including wooden ramps and aluminum
frames. This is the option that staff is recommending. A drawing was displayed to show the
addition of prefabricated features and pictures were shown of the prefabricated ramps and
features. This area could add on a lookout destination. Mr. Merrill spoke about other locations he
has visited in preparation for this project and about the direction this area could go next.

Mr. Peay presented a preliminary design of a pump track for phase 4. A pump track is a
circular track that doesn’t require one to pump or kick to go around, the momentum is gained by
the track design. The pump track has become popular in the last few years. Currently the City

Citizen Connect Presentation Presenter: Keri Rugg, Deputy City Manager/Management Services
Director and Cody Steggell, GIS Division Manager

Ms. Rugg introduced the new Citizen Connect portal for Orem residents. Mr. Steggell
gave a brief overview of the Citizen Connect portal. This is a citizen engagement portal. He
showed an overview of the portal for the City Council. The portal can be found at
connect.orem.gov. This is a live site but has not been advertised to the public. This portal allows
for citizens to report issues throughout the city including damaged garbage cans, streetlights,
water leaks, etc. The portal will allow citizens to monitor the progress of their ticket. It will also
connect the citizens to the utility billing website. Citizens will need to create an account. Mr.
Steggell logged in and showed the City Council how to navigate the portal. The City Council
asked questions about the process and different elements of the portal.

Orem Neighborhood Advisory Commission Report Presenter: Debi D’Amico, Community
Connection Supervisor and Orem Neighborhood Advisory Commission Members

Ms. D’Amico introduced Amy Green who serves on the Orem Neighborhood Advisory
Commission. Ms. Green presented the 2024 annual report and shared the highlights of the year.
The commission was expanded from 9 members to 18 members. That is 2 members from each of
the 9 districts. This year, the commission made efforts to increase communication within our
individual neighborhoods. This is based on their goal to connect neighbors and develop
neighborhood communication networks. Ms. Green shared the following neighborhood
resources: orem.org/neighborhoods, orem.org/residentresources, and orem.org/planning.

Other 2024 highlights were (1) learned from and worked with City departments, (2)
shared information about Kindness Week and selected Kindness Award winners, (3) provided
feedback on, distributed information about, and served with the new myHometown initiative, (4)
reviewed neighborhood plans in preparation to provide feedback for the City’s General Plan
update, (5) provided input on and support for City grant applications, (6) staffed booths and
served at City events to share information about City projects, resources and events. Looking
ahead to 2025 the commission will work to build neighborhood connection and unity, continue to
improve key communicator network neighborhood webpages, give service at city events,
expansion of myHometown, and assist with Kindness Week.

Mr. Gardner is here to present this year’s Veteran’s Day program and give an invitation to
the City Council and Orem citizens to attend this special event. Mr. Gardner is a member of the
Orem Post 72, which was chartered in 1948 and now has over 80 members of which about 14 are
active on the Honor Guard. The Honor Guard has served military rights to over 100 people
annually. They perform flag ceremonies for the city senior living centers and schools. This year
the Veteran’s Day Program is on November 11, 2024 at 11 am at the Orem City Cemetery. The
program will include Mayor Young, Miss Orem Royalty, the Orem Unified Band, and the feature
speaker will be Lt. Col. Dan Patterson. Lt. Patterson is the keynote speaker of the event.
