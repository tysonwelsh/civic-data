        ACTION ITEM
        Applicant: Shelter the Homeless, Inc.
        Address:      315 West 3300 South

Community and Economic Development Director, Jonathan Weidenhamer, presented the Staff
Report beginning with an abbreviated review of progress to date on the Family Interim Non-
Congregate Children Housing Facility (“FINCH”). Shelter the Homeless Executive Director,
Laurie Hopkins, and The Road Home Executive Director, Michelle Flynn were present
representing the applicants along with various members of staff. The request is to approve a
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                        5
January 16, 2025

Conditional Use Permit to operate a FINCH at 315 West 3300 South. Mr. Weidenhamer discussed
the population to be served and how the impact of protecting and serving them aligned with the
community and its residents. The required Zone Change was consummated by the City Council
on December 11, 2024, along with an associated Ordinance amendment. The plat was since
completed administratively, and the mylar is being circulated for completion. The final step before
the Commission was to review the application to ensure mitigation of off-site impacts.

Ms. Hopkins commented on the journey this project has taken in response to a lack of adequate
resources for families falling into homelessness which had been identified in 2022. She expressed
her appreciation for the support of the South Salt Lake Community and its leadership. Ms. Flynn
added that it would be an honor and a privilege to partner on the program and to be an asset to the
community.

Chair Carter asked if personal pets would be allowed. Ms. Hopkins confirmed. She noted that it
has been found to be a barrier to entry to a program if pets are not allowed. Chair Carter asked if
a partnership would be in place to address vaccinations and licensing requirements. Ms. Ungricht
stated that compliance with the Animal Services Code would be required.

Commissioner Ewell asked about the reference to outdoor storage. Mr. Weidenhamer stated that
there was no outdoor storage permitted on the Site Plan as provided but the issue may require a
future amendment. Commissioner Ewell explained that no set parameter was identified for
loitering and asked if it would be advantageous to set one. Ms. Hopkins stated that there had been
concern that waiting at the TRAX station should not be restricted. She noted that the organization
is committed to working with its partners and the South Salt Lake Police Department to make sure
the neighborhood is safe.

Motion to APPROVE the Application for Conditional Use Permit to Operate a 562,795
Square-Foot Family Interim Non-Congregate Children Housing Facility located at 315 West

Findings of Fact:
       1.      In 2023, Shelter the Homeless, Inc. (the “Applicant”) purchased property
               located at 315 West 3300 South in South Salt Lake City, on which an
               abandoned Motel complex is located, (the “Motel Property”), to renovate the
               four-structure complex, expand homeless services for families with school-
               aged children, and operate a new, Family Interim Non-congregate Children
               Housing Facility (“FINCH”).

       2.      At the time of the Applicant’s purchase, the Motel Property was in the Transit
               Oriented Development– Core land use district. The Transit Oriented
               Development – Core land use district prohibits all Uses that are not specifically
               listed in the City’s land use matrix.
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                    6
January 16, 2025

       3.     South Salt Lake City’s municipal code requires all “Development Lots” to be
              located within a recorded subdivision plat and to be served concurrently by
              adequate public infrastructure.

       4.     The Motel Property had originally been developed in unincorporated Salt
              Lake County and is not served by public infrastructure that meets City
              standards applied to the Transit Oriented Development – Core or any existing
              zone within the City.

       5.     In 1998, prior owners had annexed the Motel 6 property into South Salt Lake
              City under a state law, which required the City to annex substandard
              development upon its owner’s request.

       6.     At the time it was originally developed, the Motel Property was not included
              in a legal subdivision plat.

       7.     Historically, Motel uses adjacent to the I-15 corridor are occupied for less than
              24 hours at a time, are not designed to support long-term occupancy, are not
              designed to accommodate pets, and on average, enjoy no more than a 50%
              occupancy on an annual basis.

       8.     In June 2023, after the Applicant purchased the Motel Property, the Applicant
              submitted a predevelopment application for review of its proposal to convert
              the abandoned hotel complex into a new FINCH Facility.

       9.     Based on the Applicant’s predevelopment application, City staff determined
              that the Applicant’s proposal would require the following path to achieve full
              development entitlements and operation permits:

              a.      Petition for a Zone Map Change

              b.      Petition for an Ordinance Text Amendment

              c.      Application for a Subdivision Plat

              d.      Application for a Conditional Use Permit

              e.      Application for a Building Permit

              f.      Application for a Certificate of Occupancy

       10.    On September 1, 2023, the Applicant submitted a petition for an ordinance
              text amendment for modifications to the South Salt Lake Municipal Code to
              create a Family Interim Non-congregate Children Housing “FINCH” zone.

       11.    On September 1, 2023, the Applicant submitted a petition for a zone map
              change to rezone the Motel Property to the FINCH zone.

       12.    The Applicant submitted to City staff descriptions of the characteristics of the
              families it proposed to serve, proposed maximum occupancy, maximum
              intensity of use, adverse off-site impacts mitigation, infrastructure
              improvements, various operations plans and protocols, a specific perimeter
              fence design, public funding aspirations, and health and safety commitments,
              all of which have informed City staff’s recommendations regarding the
              Conditions of Approval contained herein.

       13.    The Applicant proposes to limit the intensity of use of the Motel Property to
              400 qualified guests, in 85 rooms, whom it expects will be housed every day on
              the Motel Property, 365 days per year, with up to a total of 45 employees,
              volunteers, service providers, private safety personnel, etc. on site.

       14.    Such densely inhabited space presents an intensification of use of the Motel
              Property, by a population that is experiencing public health and safety
              challenges, such that the City expects that the proposed use will require a
              substantially larger public safety response than was typical of public safety
              responses to the former Motel 6 use.

       15.    Mitigating the adverse impacts of the proposed use on City personnel and the
              neighboring properties will require at least two private security personnel or
              off-duty police officers, on-site 24 hours per day/7 days per week, closed circuit
              monitoring, specific resident behavior search provisions, firearms and
              contraband response policies, enhanced public safety personnel access to the
              FINCH facility, and coordinated response efforts with private security

       16.    The Applicant proposes that to partially mitigate the potential adverse impacts

       22.    A FINCH facility is a facility that is owned, operated, or contracted for
              operations by a government entity or a charitable, nonprofit organization that
              provides free, temporary (on average, less than one year), non-congregate
              housing to families, which must include at least one minor child or one young
              - adult child who is still engaged as a student in the K-12 public school system,
              who are experiencing homelessness while the parent(s) or guardian(s) obtain
              work, job skills, or otherwise take steps to stabilize the family’s long-term
              housing circumstances.

       23.    The proposed FINCH facility would provide temporary, non-congregate,
              emergency shelter for a maximum of 85 families and no more than 400
              individuals.

       24.    The proposed FINCH facility has a maximum of 85 individual residential
              rooms with occupancy of each residential room limited to individuals in the
              same familial household.

              of qualified families who will be prioritized for occupancy within the zone,
              based on the following attributes:

              a.      Families with young children;

              c.      Families with children already attending school in the Granite School
                      District; or

                                    BYLAWS & RULES OF PROCEDURE


I.          INTRODUCTION.

         These bylaws are designed and adopted to provide guidance and direction for the South Salt
Lake Planning Commission (“Commission”) in the performance of their duties. The Planning
Commission shall be governed by the provisions of all applicable State Statutes, City Ordinances, as
well as these bylaws.




                                                     T
II.         COMMISSION MEMBERSHIP.

      A. Members. The Commission is comprised of seven members (“member”) and two alternate
         members (“alternate”). All members and alternates shall be appointed by the Mayor, subject to
         the approval of the City Council. All members shall be addressed as “Commissioner”.

      B. Alternates. Alternates may act in the place of any absent member at any meeting of the

             AF
         Commission. Members are responsible for coordinating to have an alternate act in their place and
         shall notify the Community Development Department of the circumstance.

      C. Terms. Commission members are appointed for a period of four (4) years, with terms staggered
         to allow for continuity on the Commission.

      D. Resignation/Removal of Commissioners. All resignations must be in writing. Commissioners
         may be removed for cause.

      E. Quorum. Four (4) members shall constitute a Quorum to conduct business. All actions taken
         shall be by majority vote.

      F. Attendance. Attendance of members at regularly scheduled meetings may be enforced by the
         Chairperson of the Commission. Any member desiring to be excused shall notify Staff and
R
         coordinate to have an alternate act in their place.

      G. Clerk. The Community Development Department shall provide a person to act as Clerk of the
         Commission.

      H. Staff. The Community Development Department shall act as Staff for the Commission and shall
         provide staff reviews or reports, administer zoning ordinances, provide drafts of ordinances or
         master plans for consideration coordinate and provide training for the Commission, and process
D

         all applications required by city ordinances.

      I.   Member Responsibilities. Each member shall be responsible to:

           1. Read and study the agenda, staff reports, and all attached documents prepared by Staff so that
              they are fully informed about each application prior to the scheduled Commission meeting.
           2. Act in a courteous and respectful manner towards other members, Staff, applicants, and the
              public during all meetings.
           3. Attend Commission meetings and arrive on time.
           4. Follow the Rules of Order and Procedure as outlined in Section V.


                                                       1
       J. Training. Commissioners shall obtain training as required by Utah Code. A newly appointed
          member may not participate in a public meeting as an appointed member until the member
          completes the training as required by Utah Code. Failure to comply with the training
          requirements may result in removal from the Commission.

   K. Compensation. Members shall serve without pay, except for reasonable and legitimate expenses
      as approved by the City Council.

III.       POWERS AND DUTIES OF COMMISSION.

   A. Commission Member's Duties. The Planning Commission shall:




                                                  T
      1. Prepare and recommend a general plan and amendments to the general plan to the legislative
         body.
      2. Recommend zoning ordinances and maps, and amendments to zoning ordinances and maps,
         to the legislative body.
      3. Administer provisions of the zoning and development ordinances, where specifically
         provided for in those ordinances.
      4. Recommend action or take action on development applications.

            AF
      5. Advise the legislative body on matters as the legislative body directs.
      6. Hear or decide any matters that the legislative body designates, including the approval or
         denial of, conditional use permits and subdivision plat approvals and amendments to
         subdivision plats.
      7. Exercise any other powers that are necessary to enable it to perform its function, or are
         delegated to it by the legislative body.

   B. Chairperson. The members shall elect a Chairperson from its members at the first regularly
      scheduled meeting of the Commission each year. The Chairperson shall preside at all meetings of
      the Commission. The Chairperson shall vote but shall not make or second motions. The
      Chairperson shall maintain order at all meetings.

   C. Vice-Chairperson. The members shall elect a Vice-Chairperson from its members at the first
      regularly scheduled meeting of the Commission each year. In the temporary absence or disability
R
      of the Chairperson, the Vice-Chairperson shall preside at the meetings in the Chair’s absence and
      perform the role and responsibilities of the Chair. In the event of the resignation or removal of the
      Chairperson, the Vice-Chairperson shall serve the remainder of the term as Chairperson and the
      Commission shall elect a new Vice-Chairperson to serve the remainder of the term.

   D. Election of Chairperson and Vice-Chairperson. At the first regularly scheduled meeting of the
      year or in the event of the resignation or removal of the Chairperson or Vice-Chairperson the next
      practicable meeting thereafter, a new Chairperson and Vice-Person shall be elected by receiving a
D

      nomination from a Commission member and by receiving the majority of the roll call vote of the
      Commission members.

   E. Chairperson and Vice-Person's Absence. During the temporary absence or disability of the
      Chairperson and Vice-Chairperson, the Commission member with the most seniority shall
      perform the role and responsibilities of the Chair.

   F. Conflict of Interest. Members of the Commission who feel they or any other member of the
      Commission have an actual, apparent, or reasonably foreseeable conflict of interest on any matter
      that is on the Commission agenda shall explain the apparent conflict to the Commission. If there
      is a conflict of interest, as determined by the Commission, that person shall not participate in the

                                                    2
      discussion and voting of that matter, nor attempt to use his/her influence with other
      Commissioners. An alternate may sit in place of the member stepping down but the member may
      resume the seat for other items on the agenda.

  G. Use of Power. A Commissioner shall not use the power as Commissioner to seek or obtain a
     special advantage that is not in the public interest nor any special advantage that is not a matter of
     public knowledge.

     change this schedule provided that such change is posted and members provided with a copy, as
     required by Utah Code. The Commission is anticipated to meet twice monthly.

  B. Regular Meetings. Meeting locations shall be publicly noticed and held each month. Annual

        AF
     notice of meeting dates shall be noticed as required by Utah Code. In addition, dates and times of
     the meetings shall be posted as required by Utah Code.

  C. Field Trips. The Commission may, at their discretion, schedule field trips for the purpose of
     investigating certain matters. Field trips shall be for the purpose of gathering information, not
     discussing decisions nor conducting business.

  D. Special Meetings. Special meetings may be called by the Chairperson, Staff, or a majority of the
     Commission members at any time. Absent urgency, Commission members must be notified at
     least seventy-two (72) hours in advance by telephone or written notice before the meeting is held
     and public notice is given as required by Utah Code. If the meeting is of an urgent nature,
     Commission members must be notified at least twenty-four (24) hours in advance by telephone or
     written notice. Other special meetings, including work meetings, may be called by the
     Commission, the Chair, the Mayor, or Staff. The Commission must be given at least one week of

     advance notice of these meetings.

     Code. If a meeting is rescheduled, the new meeting time, date and location shall be posted as
     required by Utah Code.
