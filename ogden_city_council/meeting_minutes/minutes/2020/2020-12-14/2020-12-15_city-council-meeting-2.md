Proposed Ordinance 2020-62 amending the Ogden Municipal Code by
amending the definition of open space and other provisions pertaining to
open space and landscaping
          A memo from the Community and Economic Devel~pment Deparhnent regarding proposed amendments to the open
space definition came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Ogden City Planning Division is proposing a
change to the zoning ordinance clarifying the term "open space or open green space" specific to site plan requirements. Thetenns
"open space" and "landscaping" have been used differently at different times, and at times interchangeably, in the zoning
ordinance. The proposal would clarify that open space is to be used as a tenn for areas outside of a given setback that are planned
for or programmed for use by residents of the property. This would differ from required landscaped areas, which are areas with
living plant materials or features that include living plant materials.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and made a presentation explaining the text amendment proposal.
According to current ordinances, only side and rear yards of a property are defined as open space; yard areas are typically referred
to as setbacks. Open spaces are planned landscape areas for activity or relaxation. He presented a site plan to illustrate the
presence of open spaces in a commercial development. There are several similar terms used in the ordinance, but they have
different intents:
               •   Setbacks - Required minimum distance from property lines to structures;
               • Yards -The open area between building and property line (all yards and setbacks shall be landscaped);
               • Landscaping - The placement of plant material in all areas not occupied by building or parking (this is
                   what is placed in the yards created by the setbacks); and
               •   Open (Green) Space -Areas of landscaping with specific intent for usage.
          Additionally, there are similar words with different meanings; open space and open green space are both zoning
regulation terms for landscaped areas with specific intent. Open space and open green space zone are categories of land use for
undeveloped land or name of uses of land that are outdoor related. In certain zones, there is a general definition of what open
space is - a planned area. These zones include Planned Residential Unit Development (PRUD), group dwellings, 12 th Street
Overlay Zone, mixed-use, and Neighborhood Commercial (NCl and NC2). In other zones, the definition of open space needs to
be updated. Currently in these zones, open space is referred to as a percentage ofland allocated to landscaping: R-3, R-3EC, R-4,
R-5, Professional Institutional (Pl), and Central Business District (CBD). It is not necessary to change definitions for the
commercial and mixed-use zones where open space refers to a percent of a site to be landscaped. He summarized the suggested
amendments to the open space/open green space definition as follows: "A planned outdoor landscaped area designed and installed
or retained and enhanced for uses of the residents of the property for gatherings, activities, or visual enjoyment outside the
required zone setbacks." The Planning Commission voted unanimously (9-0) to recommend approval of amendments to
definitions and chapters where open space is used to refer to a percentage of landscaping coverage.
          Chair Choberka introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2020-62, entitled:
                   "An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Sections 15-2-16 and
                   15-33-3 to amend the definition of open space and amend other provisions pertaining to open space; amending
                   subsections 15-17-4.F, 15-18-4.F, 15-19-4.F, 15-34-3.B, and 15-36-4.F to revise provisions pertaining to open
                   space and landscaping; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting
                   after final passage."
