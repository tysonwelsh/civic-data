1.       A presentation regarding Council budget priorities in regard to FY 22 proposed
         budget. (21-015) (0:14:20)

Cliff Strachan, Council Executive Director, presented. He reviewed elements of the tentative
FY2022 budget as they related to the Council’s stated priorities. He noted that the greatest
challenge was simply in the limited funds available. Mr. Strachan and others responded to
questions from Councilors during the presentation.

Councilor David Harding asked about the increase for benefits. John Borget, Administrative
Services Director, explained that the Milliman index determines the increase for medical
insurance rates. This was reflected in the budget for the calendar year (or plan year) effective in
January 2022. Mr. Borget also explained that sick leave buyout, travel, and training budgets had
been restored in the budget going forward. He noted that the City did give merit increases last
year. Councilor David Sewell asked for clarification regarding sick leave buyout; Mr. Borget
explained that the City “buys” back half of unused yearly sick leave accrual from employees.
Councilors also asked how public safety staffing, as well as a long-term plan, had been addressed
in the budget. Several Councilors expressed that additional guidance from the Police Department

2.      A presentation on FY 22 Budget All Fund Revenue Forecast. (21-015) (0:57:14)

John Magness, Policy Analyst, presented. He shared revenue projections and comparisons staff
had used for the forecast and he reviewed each revenue category. He invited questions and
discussion from Councilors during and afterwards. Councilor David Sewell thanked Mr.
Magness for his analysis. Mr. Sewell restated his takeaways from the presentation as a means of
confirming that he understood the presentation and its conclusions. Mr. Magness offered further
clarification of the methods used for analysis, comparing year-over-year growth as well as the
difference between projected and actual revenues. Councilor David Shipley also thanked Mr.
Magness for this helpful presentation. Mr. Shipley suggested it would be helpful to see trends for
different departments over the last four years, in order to get a better sense of where the revenue
growth was being programmed. He also thought it would be a helpful exercise in order to more
clearly communicate to the public how tax revenues were being used. Presentation only.

4.      A presentation from the Community and Neighborhood Services Department
        regarding their FY22 tentative proposed budget. (21-015) (1:34:44)

Gary McGinn, Community and Neighborhood Services Director, and Sandy Bussio, Parking
Enforcement Supervisor, presented. Mr. McGinn elaborated on the supplemental request for
additional parking enforcement officers and administrative support. He also wanted to outline
their revenue projections with two additional enforcement officers in place. Ms. Bussio shared
data based on the current staffing level, including personnel and equipment costs. She also
reviewed the revenue based on standard ticketing and citations in a typical year. Ms. Bussio also
shared details about the training for enforcement officers and how they intended to utilize
additional officers to be more responsive to requests coming through customer service. Ms.
Bussio also highlighted details for the various parking permit areas throughout the city. Mr.
McGinn noted that they were at capacity right now and without additional staffing, could not
support extensions to the permit areas and increased customer service requests. The intent was
that the revenue resulting from the additional capacity of additional enforcement staff would
provide the necessary budget to fund those added positions.

Councilor David Harding asked several questions. He wondered where they were at in the
process of hiring a parking coordinator. Mr. McGinn hoped they would open the posting the
following week. He noted that parking enforcement needs would still exist regardless of having a
parking coordinator. Mr. Harding also asked if this supplemental request had been funded as part
of the tentative budget. Wayne Parker, CAO, indicated that it was not funded at this time. Mr.
Harding wondered whether it would be possible to pursue creating an enterprise fund to
encapsulate all parking management activities, as revenues and expenditures would be easily
identified within the fund. Mr. Parker issued a caution regarding the revenue projection; he
agreed with Mr. McGinn and Ms. Bussio on their strategy, but that doubling staff was not a
guarantee that revenue would necessarily double. Mr. Parker suggested other factors that could
impact the projection. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked for clarification on the projection; Ms.
Bussio indicated the numbers were based on current revenue with the current staffing. Mr. Parker
also noted that some expenses were first-year expenses but not ongoing, so that would likely help
them to reach more of a break-even scenario. Mr. Harding shared additional comments and noted
his appreciation for the discussion. Presentation only.

Councilor David Shipley asked about the tiered water rates and how this system could further
motivate and incentivize conservation among Provo residents. Mr. Decker shared more details
from Bowen Collins, the city’s consultant on developing the tiered rate structure, on the process
by which they reached the recommended tiered rates. Mr. Decker explained that in order to make
the change revenue-neutral, they had to decrease the number of tiers from the original
recommendation. Mr. Shipley thanked Mr. Decker for the explanation and while he was curious
about the other options, he indicated he trusted Mr. Decker’s assessment.

Mr. Shipley asked about the City’s new wastewater treatment technology and whether it could
serve as a backup system for potable water if necessary. Mr. Decker explained that membrane
bio-reactor systems were the most advanced of their kind and were common in California,
Arizona, Texas, and elsewhere. Reverse osmosis also takes out some of additional minerals in
the effluent. He noted this process is added at end of treatment plants in locations where the
resulting effluent is returned to the culinary water system. Mr. Decker explained that Provo does
not currently have this reverse osmosis process added at the end of our treatment plant, however,
Provo’s resulting effluent could be used for secondary water sources. Mr. Shipley was interested
in learning the cost to add reverse osmosis treatment as a contingency plan. Mr. Decker indicated
that he could check the costs of what that might be for Provo’s system. Mr. Shipley also asked
whether the potential of receiving funds from the American Rescue Plan Act jeopardized any of
the City’s opportunities for grant funding on ASR and other projects. Mr. Decker did not believe
it hindered the City’s ability to secure funds from other sources.

Councilor David Harding suggested implementing a small increase for the utility rates to
compensate for the two-month delay in implementation. He worried if the City was hampering
their future ability to have necessary funding by shorting the initial revenues with the two-month
