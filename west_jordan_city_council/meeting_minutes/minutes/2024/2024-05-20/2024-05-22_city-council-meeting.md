 5. PUBLIC COMMENT
     Janet Blackburn said she lived at a bend in Grizzly Way across from the high school football field
     where multiple vehicles had run off the road into a cinderblock wall causing damage. She said
     residents depended on accident reports to file for insurance from the parties who hit the wall.
     She asked what would happen in situations of damage with no witnesses or no insurance, and
     expressed the opinion it was not fair for residents to have to pay for the negligence of others.
     Ms. Blackburn said residents were originally told by the builder of the homes that the
     cinderblock wall belonged to the City, but recently learned the City deeded the wall to the
     property owners not long after the homes were built without the property owners’ knowledge
     or permission. Ms. Blackburn said traffic on Grizzly Way had increased dramatically, and
     vehicles traveled the road too fast in all weather conditions and at all times of day. She said
     residents had proposed many solutions to the traffic problem but had been told by the City the
     proposals were not possible. Ms. Blackburn stated barriers were needed to protect their wall
     from attack. She said residents paid their taxes and expected protection.

     Ann Marie Barret, Bingham Creek Library Manager, spoke of upcoming library events and
     activities.

     Gage Broadhead expressed a desire to bring awareness to a need for culture in the community
     for youth. He spoke of a program called “Local,” and said he hoped to get started on the program
     in June.
