 1.       Reconsideration following mayoral veto of an ordinance related to COVID-19
          pandemic responses, including face coverings and public gatherings. (20-120)

 Cliff Strachan, Council Executive Director, presented on the reconsideration request. The
 Mayor vetoed the previously passed mask ordinance citing three main concerns: she has not
 received a request from any community partners asking for a mandate, she was concerned about
 the enforcement burden on the Police Department, and the mandate conflicted with her
 ideological principles about limited government and a “don’t tread on me” perspective.

 Brian Jones, Council Attorney, highlighted several minor tweaks to the ordinance. The actual
 changes proposed to the City Code were identical to those proposed and approved on Tuesday,
 August 25, 2020. The only changes were made to the preamble to provide the background and
 proper context to the Council’s reconsideration of the ordinance. Mr. Jones briefly highlighted
 several provisions of the proposed changes to the City Code text. Specifically, he addressed

 Council Chair George Handley expressed appreciation and respect for Mayor Kaufusi and her
 relationship with the Council. He felt that they were unified in a common desire to minimize
 risk and promote health and safety during the pandemic. He expressed disappointment in the
 lack of civility in the public dialogue surrounding a mask mandate. As elected officials in
 Provo, they were all committed to modeling civility about this and other issues in Provo. The
 Council was committed to an ongoing and continuing dialogue about pandemic precautions. He
 thanked members of the public who have engaged on this issue, staff who have spent many
 hours behind-the-scenes to make the meetings possible and prepare drafts of the ordinance, and
 to the Administration for their cooperation throughout the process. Mr. Handley then invited
 comments and discussion from the Council on the implied motion to approve the ordinance.

 Councilor Bill Fillmore shared his comments on the proposed ordinance. He highlighted some
 of his lingering concerns about items not addressed by the ordinance but indicated his intent to
 vote in favor of the ordinance that evening. Specifically regarding his concerns, he felt some
 language could be further clarified to consider disability or health challenges, expanding
 outreach and education efforts, concerns about the individual mandate being punitive, and a
 desire to find some leeway for organizations to implement precautions to allow outdoor
 gatherings. He suggested removing the fine from the proposed ordinance, to focus on
 enforcement or compliance in public spaces and retail settings.

 Mr. Handley reminded the Council that time has been set aside at the upcoming Council
 Meeting on Tuesday, September 1, 2020, to discuss ongoing concerns related to the mask
 ordinance. He noted that the intent for this vote was simply a reconsideration of the ordinance
 after the Mayor’s veto.

 Councilor Shannon Ellsworth acknowledged the difficulties the city and its residents and
 businesses have faced in the last six months. She expressed her hope that civility and respect
 could continue to be demonstrated in all areas.

       Motion:          An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2020-36R, as currently constituted,
                        has been made by council rule.
       Vote:            Approved 6:1, with Travis Hoban opposed.
