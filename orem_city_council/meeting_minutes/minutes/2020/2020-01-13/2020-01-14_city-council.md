     REVIEW – Officer-Involved Critical Incident Protocol (OICI)

Before he began his review of the OICI, Chief Giles introduced the new vests that Orem police
officers were wearing while on duty. He said duty belts, which had been the standard for many
years, carried equipment weighing around 20 to 30 pounds and often led to hip and lower-back
issues for officers. The new load-bearing vest distributed the weight of the equipment better, and
made equipment easier to reach when needed. Chief Giles reviewed the improvements of the
materials and design, including panels for bullet-proof plates to be inserted. He said most officers
were already wearing the more functional vest, and he passed his own vest around for those that
wanted to see the vest up close.

Chief Giles said the OICI was a new protocol that was developed by all the police chiefs in Utah
County. The intention was to improve on the current protocol that was erroneously called “Officer-
Involved Shooting” protocol. The new OICI protocol addressed all officer-involved critical
incidents, and would be conducted simultaneous to administrative investigations of incidents.
Chief Giles shared the work flow of the OICI protocol, and explained that the venue agency would
be whatever agency the involved officer was a part of. The Task Force Manager/Commanders

          PUBLIC HEARING
          ORDINANCE – Amending Orem City Code by enacting Section 22-14-21(C)
          Requirements for Cannabis Production Establishment and Medical Cannabis Pharmacy
          and amending Appendix A by enacting Standard Land Use Codes 2140 Cannabis
          Production Establishment (Subject to the requirements of Section 22-14-21(C)) and 5920
          Medical Cannabis Pharmacy (Subject to the requirements of Section 22-14-21(C))

Mr. Bench said that in November 2018, Utah voters approved Proposition 2 which made medical
marijuana legal in the state. The state legislature passed House Bill 3001 on December 3, 2018
which was signed by the Governor and became law. Among the elements of the bill specific to
cities was a requirement that cities provide in their zoning code a place for cannabis cultivation
facilities and cannabis pharmacies. The licensing for the cultivation facilities and the pharmacies
were regulated by the state; the cities must allow for the use in their code.

The proposed amendments to Appendix A included enacting two new Standard Land Use Codes
2140 Cannabis Production Establishment (Subject to the requirements of Section 22-14-21(C))
and 5920 Medical Cannabis Pharmacy (Subject to the requirements of Section 22-14-21(C)). SLU
Code 2140 would allow a cannabis production establishment (cultivation) in the M2
manufacturing zone only. This would meet the requirement stipulated in state law. In addition,
SLU Code 5920 would allow a medical cannabis pharmacy in all zones that were not primarily
residential zones subject to a separation requirement. These zones included the PO, C1, C2, C3,
HS, M1, M2, CM, and BP zones. This would also meet the requirement stipulated in state law.

Additionally, Section 22-14-21(C) Requirements for Cannabis Production Establishment and
Medical Cannabis Pharmacy would provide additional requirements pertaining to both the
production and sale of medical cannabis.

Mr. Bench reviewed the additional requirement the City was proposing, including that the Orem
Police Department could review the criminal backgrounds of applicants and that cannabis
production must be done in an indoor facility. Mayor Brunst wanted to clarify that the proposal
would not only comply with state law, but allowed the City more control by including specific
requirements in Orem’s code. Mr. Bench said that was correct, and commented that state law
already had strict controls in place but the city was able to supplement that in their code.

Mr. Macdonald asked the process by which staff determined the additional requirements in the
proposal. Mr. Bench said Mr. Earl had done extensive research on the subject. Mr. Earl said he
reviewed the bill passed by the state, as well as the detailed analysis the Utah League of Cities and
Towns had prepared. He also reviewed ordinances in other Utah cities to see what limitations they

Mr. Spencer asked for clarification on the zoning area they were proposing to allow the cannabis-
related land uses. Mr. Bench showed an aerial map, and said that zoning would not be the only
factor in limiting where these facilities could be. There were restrictions set by the state regarding
proximity to schools, which would limit options for these facilities.

Mrs. Lauret said she understood that the State had licensed three cities in Utah County (Lindon,
Provo, and Springville) of the 14 licenses they issued to allow for cannabis cultivation facilities
and cannabis pharmacies. She was curious about the interest in Orem for these kinds of facilities.
Mr. Bench said there had been a handful of phone calls before the end of the year, but they were
only speculative inquiries and the City gave no confirmations on anything other than Orem was
not a state-licensed city at this time.

Mr. Sumner asked if multiple facilities would be opened in cities with a cannabis license. Mr.
Bench said it was possible, but would depend on distance requirements. The state had identified
manufacturing zones as acceptable zones for cultivation facilities, but none were allowed in
residential or commercial zones. Cannabis pharmacies would be allowed in commercial zones and
the designated M2 zone if the ordinance were passed.

Mayor Brunst opened the public hearing at 6:17 p.m. No one came forward to speak, so he closed
the public hearing.

Mr. Macdonald said he appreciated the work staff had put into this proposal to ensure that they
were meeting state requirements but also protection Orem for the future. Mr. Clark showed the
Zoning Map of Orem to clarify the allowed zoning location. He said of the three zones the state
said these facilities could go, the proposal was to only allow them in one zone. He added that
growth in Vineyard would also affect the distance requirements for cannabis facilities. Mayor
Brunst pointed out the location for the announced temple for the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-
day Saints, and said he thought that would also be a factor in distance requirements.

Mr. Peterson commented that living with chronic pain was a difficult way to go through life; on
the other hand, living with addiction was also a difficult way to go through life. He did not want
to appear to be in favor on any level of allowing these kinds of facilities in Orem, so he planned to
vote against the proposed ordinance. While he understood that state law prohibited the outright
denial of such proposals, he felt they could send a message by not changing their code to
accommodate these cannabis facilities.

Mr. Macdonald said that if the City Council did nothing, these cannabis facilities could go
anywhere in the state-identified zoning areas. By adding restrictions in the city code they were
making those facilities more expensive to operate, which would work in favor of discouraging
cannabis facilities to move into Orem.
