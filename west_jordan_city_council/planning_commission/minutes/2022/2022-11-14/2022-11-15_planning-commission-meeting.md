3. West Jordan Oil Changers; 4782 West 7800 South; Preliminary Site Plan, Administrative
Conditional Use Permit for Vehicle and Equipment Repair, and Minor Subdivision
Amendment (Wheatland Estates Phase 1 Lot 2); SC-2 Zone; West Jordan Retail

Management (applicant) (#28384, 28381, 28271; parcel 21-30-352-032]

Tayler Jensen stated that the lot is within an existing development. The request is to split the lot into

two and develop one lot with a quick oil change business, which also requires a conditional use permit.

The Design Review Committee recommended approval of the design and placement. There are no

changes proposed to the existing access points.

Based on the analysis contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission

approve the Conditional Use Permit, and Site Plan for West Jordan Oil Changers and the Wheatland

Estates Phase | Lot 2 Amended Minor Subdivision on approximately 1.02 acres located at 4782 West

7800 South, with the requirements of approval.

There are no conditions of approval suggested by staff because staff has not identified any reasonably

anticipated detrimental effects of this use. If the Planning Commission disagrees with the staff’s

analysis and/or identifies any reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of this use, reasonable

conditions should be identified, which, if imposed, would reasonably relate to and substantially mitigate
each of the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of the use, and which would comply with existing
law. Such information should be clearly included in any motion made by and subsequent decision of the

Planning Commission relative to the application. Any Planning Commission decision will supersede

any conflicting conclusions or recommendations of the staff stated in the report or otherwise presented

to the Planning Commission.

Requirements of approval (existing law:)

1. Comply with all the requirements of the West Jordan City Code (“City Code”) and other
existing law, including but not limited to City Code Sections 13-13-1 through 13-13-10
inclusive, with all existing trees to be retained and with the requirement that the
applicant/developer shall replace any landscaping disturbed or destroyed through the
construction of the site.

The reason for the requirements to retain existing trees and to replace any disturbed or destroyed

landscaping is to comply with the cited City Code Sections and other applicable law, especially the

provisions of City Code Subsection 13-13-6C.

George Winn asked for an explanation regarding a drainage ditch on the property.

Tayler Jensen said he was not aware of a drainage ditch; however, the Engineering department is still

working out drainage for the site.

Ammon Allen asked where the basin will be located.

Tayler Jensen understood that they may be connecting into the master drainage system for the overall

commercial subdivision without the need for a basin.

Ammon Allen said stormwater retention is needed in this area because it pools during a rainstorm. He

pointed out an access and utility easement and asked if it belonged to the city or a third party, and what

obligation the city had to protect it since the footprint of the building is right next to it.

Tayler Jensen said it is not a city easement. He said there were no concerns from the engineering or

utilities departments during the review, and the property owner signed both project applications.

Ammon Allen said that one of the plans showed an 8-foot perimeter landscape to the north in the

asphalt. That area borders an asphalt parking lot, and he did not think it should be installed there.

Tayler Jensen said the landscaping is located between the drive aisle and the building.

Ammon Allen said if that is the location then he is okay. He still had concerns with the question of
storm drainage, but staff said there are no conditions recommended by the engineering staff and
everything should move forward.

Tayler Jensen said the utility engineer said the project was okay to go to the planning commission and
that anything that needs to be addressed can be done through engineering.

Kent Shelton asked if this project would create any impact to the existing parking lot.

Tayler Jensen said there will be no impact to the existing parking lot.

Mitchell Harvey, applicant attending remotely, said this site was part of a larger drainage plan that they
have not been able to locate. They will put together a revised drainage report that will likely result in an
underground basin using oversized pipes on their site. They have included a note on the plan that says
they will work with city staff to provide a report that meets all city requirements.

Jay Thomas opened the public hearing.

Paul Stringham, Peterson Development, said they have been working with the applicant for a year. The
applicant has been doing the civil work and Peterson Development believes in their review and working
with the city, that the application complies. This location is in a drainage area and the applicant needs to
meet city requirements. The parking situation is good according to the code and based on this type of
use that is really a drive through oil change without a heavy parking requirement. Peterson
Development understands the cross access with the other properties and supports the application for a
use that is appropriate for the area.

Tayler Jensen said that the site has about 50% more parking than is required.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Duncan Murray said that city staff conducted some analysis on the conditional use permit and found no
detrimental effects. Many of the nearby uses are other commercial uses. Mr. Murray indicated that
during deliberation, if the Planning Commission finds any detrimental effects, then those need to be
identified, along with any conditions to reasonably mitigate the identified detrimental effects.

Kent Shelton said the project is directly across the street to the south and to the west of residential
neighborhoods, but there are walls in place. With the existing walls in place, he does not think that there
would be any detrimental effects for the nearby residential neighborhoods.

Ammon Allen has reached the conclusion that the use matches well with the community, and he did not
see any detrimental effects to consider, since the drainage is being resolved. Also, since the perimeter
landscaping is being located where staff indicated and is being changed on the site plan, then he is
satisfied with the conditional use permit and site plan, as modified.

None of the other planning commissioners identified any detrimental effects (or conditions to
reasonably mitigate any detrimental effects) pertaining to the proposed conditional use.

4, Copper Rim Town Center IOZ; approximately 5950 West 7800 South; Amend the

Preliminary Master Development Plan to revise approved fencing in the commercial area
along Mountain View Corridor; P-C (IOZ) Zone; CW Land Co./CW Copper Rim 1,
LLC/Matthew Dean (applicant) [#28784; parcel 20-26-457-001]

Matt Dean, Vice President of CW Land Co., was representing the Copper Rim master developer and
Furniture Row USA. In 2021 the master development plan included an 8-foot-tall concrete sound wall
on commercial and residential land uses next to Mountain View Corridor. The purpose of the
amendment is to modify the building material from an 8-foot concrete wall to an 8-foot decorative
fence that fronts the commercial area. Reasons for the amendment are that a solid wall would block the
majority of the storefronts in the retail center and does not allow for visibility from Mountain View
Corridor. Retail businesses within the Town Center want that visibility. The proposed 8-foot-tall
aluminum decorative fence will provide transparency. The solid concrete wall is appropriate next to
residential uses and should remain. The proposed amendment is important to retain current and attract
future commercial businesses in Copper Rim and is limited only to the commercial retail portions that
front Mountain View Corridor. Furniture Row said they intend for the fence to be painted a flat black
color, which will be tasteful and attractive.

Ray McCandless pointed out the orientation of the Furniture Row building that faces southwest toward

the intersection. Visibility is important for commercial uses and staff was in favor of the change to the

Copper Rim Town Center IOZ Preliminary Master Development Plan.

Ammon Allen noted that the adjoining fence along the water tank is not as tall, so he suggested that the

applicant be allowed to choose whether they want to have six feet in height for consistency or keep it at

eight feet.

Ray McCandless said staff did not see a problem with allowing that.

Jay Thomas opened the public hearing.

5. Text Amendment — Development Procedures; Amend the West Jordan 2009 Code Sections

13-7C-1 through 13-7C-9, Sections 13-7D-1 to 13-7D-9, and Section 13-71-2; General Plan
Amendments and Zoning Ordinance Amendments; City-wide applicability; City of West
Jordan, (applicant)

Alan Anderson, Council Director, gave a synopsis of the proposed text amendments, which include

clarifications and changes at the request of the City Council. It is important for all affected departments

to participate in the development process. He recommended that the Planning Commission forward a

positive recommendation to the City Council for the application.

A brief discussion was held. The Council would like to compile data in a way that allows them to make

objective determinations on how the proposal will impact all services and to provide for good budgeting

and planning. It was also pointed out in another provision that the Commission should stipulate reasons
for any negative recommendations to the Council.

Jay Thomas opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

MOTION: Matt Quinney moved, based on the information and findings set forth in the staff
report and upon the evidence and explanations received today, to forward a
positive recommendation to the City Council to Amend the West Jordan 2009 Code
Sections 13-7C-1 through 13-7C-9, Sections 13-7D-1 to 13-7D-9, and Section 13-7I-
2. The motion was seconded by Jay Thomas and passed 6-0 in favor. McKenna
Marchant was absent.
