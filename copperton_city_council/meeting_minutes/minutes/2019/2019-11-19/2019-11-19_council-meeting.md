pursuant to Section 19.050.060 of the Copperton Metro Township Code, per diem compensation
for planning commission members in the amount of fifty dollars ($50.00) per member for each
meeting attended is hereby established effective January 1, 2020.

Medical Cannabis Act/Industrial Hemp Act

               Nathan Bracken gave an overview of both acts, noting that neither activity is
currently allowed under the acts and that changes will need to be made to the zoning ordinances
in Copperton.

The Medical Cannabis Act authorizes the growing, sale and dispensing of medical cannabis. It
goes into great detail about where these facilities can be located. They are considered permitted
uses in agricultural or industrial zones that already exist. Cities can designate one industrial zone
or one agricultural zone for growing. If this is not done, anybody can grow cannabis in any of
those zones.

The second aspect of the act is the cannabis dispensary. That is a permitted use in any zone
except something that is primarily residential. Other restrictions apply. Because Copperton is
primarily residential, you would have to make a change to allow growing or dispensing.

The Industrial Hemp Act is different. Hemp is different than cannabis. The act authorizes the
Department of Agriculture to come up with a program. It also indicates that the enterprise must
comply with local regulations.

               Council Member Pazell stated he would like to take a look at urban agriculture.

                 Mr. Bracken stated the Council does not want large numbers of people bringing
industrial activity into residential neighborhoods.

Social Media Policy

                Council Member Bailey stated government agencies cannot remove comments
off Facebook that they find inappropriate. First amendment rights cannot be violated. Comments
can only be removed if they are slanderous, libelous, or obscene. The County proposed letting
their attorneys handle any situation regarding social media comments.

               Council Member Pazell stated elected officials cannot block or censor people
from their page unless they are being threatened with violence or the post is obscene.

               Nathan Bracken stated the challenge for Council Members is when running for
reelection an opponent could post something on the township’s Facebook page, and it could not
be removed. Private Facebook pages for individual members are different and posts could be
removed from them.

             Mayor Clayton stated the Council will draft a policy, read it, adopt it, and post it
on the page. The Council will soon be sharing an administrative assistant who can do these
postings.
