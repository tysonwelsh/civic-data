4. Discussion and possible action regarding a Conditional Use permit for a private 
recreation and athletic facility with a training room and accessory apartment 
attached; 532 East Westwood Road; LS-B-124; Van with I-Build, representing the 
applicant- Ray Milliner, Principal Planner   
 
The applicant’s team introduced themselves.  They were: Van Kelly and Jared Higgins 
with I-Build Utah and Bill Van Sickle with Van Sickle Design and Drafting.  Elizabeth and 
Matthew Gilmour are the property owners. 
 
Planner Milliner said this is a Conditional Use Permit for a 14,000 square foot accessory 
building.  This application was reviewed by the Planning Commission in October.  At 
that meeting, concerns were expressed by the Planning Commission.   
 
Planner Milliner said the applicant is ready to address the Commission’s concerns.  
These were centered on the building’s height, the commercial nature of the building, 
and the impact on a local elk herd.  A slide of the structure was shown.  Planner Milliner 
said the cupolas have been removed from the roof; therefore, the building now 
complies with height limits.   
 
Planner Milliner said he contacted the State of Utah’s Natural Resource Department 
about the elk herd.  After reviewing the site plan, the DNR representative said there will 
be limited impact on the elk.  Only a minimal amount of grazing area will be removed.

Planner Milliner said the applicants have prepared a video and some maps.  These 
address the size of the building and the compatibility of the structure to the 
neighborhood.  The video and the maps were shown.  Highlights of the video were: 
 There are 108 properties with 114 outbuildings. 
 The outbuildings range in size between 4 and 18,000 square feet. 
 Different aerial views of the location were shown. 
 The design of the building fits in with the rural nature of the community. 
 
Chair Peck asked for an explanation of the map.  She was told the orange buildings are 
structures above 10,000 feet.  These figures include each structure’s overhangs.  These 
figures were obtained by using Google Earth.  Planner Milliner said his research agrees 
with what the applicant’s.  Within the vicinity, the largest buildings are approximately 
22,000, 15,000, and 12,000 square feet.  He believes this shows there is precedent for a 
building of this size.  Most residents have some kind of accessory structure, but most 
average around 5,000-6,000 square feet.

FINDINGS OF FACT 
1. The applicant is requesting conditional use approval for an Accessory Building 
Greater than 10,000 square feet and a Low Impact Permit for a Recreation and 
Athletic Facility. 
2. The use is proposed as a 14,000 accessory building with a large soccer arena, 
accessory apartment/living area and a warm-up/storage area. 
3. The property is a 9.3 acre lot in the Silver Creek Estates Unit B Subdivision. 
4. It is located in the Rural Residential (RR) zone.

5. Access to the site is from East Westwood Road via a standard driveway. 
6. The building would be approximately 32 feet in height measured from existing 
grade to the highest ridge. 
7. Section 10-2-10 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code states that an 
Accessory Building greater than 10,000 square feet is a Conditional Use in the 
RR zone and that a Private Recreation and Athletic Facility is a Low Impact Use 
in the RR zone. 
8. Section 10-3-2 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code states that the 
Community Development Director may, “combine the processing and hearing of 
development permits,” to simplify the process. 
9. This permit has been combined into one CUP. 
10. The building would be accessory to an existing single family home. 
11. The accessory unit will be 879 square feet in size. 
12. Chapter 10-2-10 of the Development Code lists accessory dwelling units as an 
allowed use in the RR zone, provided certain criteria are met. 
13. The accessory building will share a septic sewer system with the single family 
home. 
14. Information from the Health Department indicates it will be able to 
accommodate all affluent from the barn. 
15. All parking from the project is contained within the proposed building site. 
16. All necessary public facilities are available on site, including water, septic, and 
roads. 
17. The barn is compatible with the existing neighborhood character.  It is 
architecturally compatible with the surrounding architecture as well as the 
adjacent single family home. 
18. The Snyderville Basin Planning Commission conducted a public hearing for the 
project on October 9, 2018.  The hearing was closed at that meeting.   
 
CONCLUSIONS OF LAW 
1. There is good cause for this conditional use.

2. The proposed Conditional Use Permit, as conditioned, complies with all 
requirements of the Snyderville Basin Development Code. 
3. The modification, as conditioned, is consistent with the Snyderville Basin 
General Plan, as amended.   
4. The use is not detrimental to public health, safety, and welfare, as the roads and 
public services in the area are sufficient to accommodate the increase in 
intensity of the use. 
5. The use is compatible with the existing neighborhood character and will not 
adversely affect surrounding land uses.     
6. The effects of any difference in use or scale have been mitigated through careful 
planning. 
 
CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL 
1. This building is only for private use by the property owner.  No commercial use 
is allowed on the site. 
2. The proposed accessory apartment shall meet all criteria specified in Section 
10-8-5 of the Development Code prior to issue. 
3. Any fencing placed on the property shall be built compliant with the wildlife 
fencing standards as recommended by the Utah Division of Natural Resources. 
4. All associated landscaping shall be native, drought-tolerant plants.  
5. The standing seam metal roof shall be non-reflective with a muted earth tone. 
6. Staff shall review all exterior lighting for compliance with the standards in 
Section 10-4-21 of the Development Code prior to the issuance of a building 
permit.   
7. Prior to the issuance of a building permit, the applicant shall work with 
representatives from the Summit County Health Department to ensure that the 
septic system is sufficient to accommodate the impacts of the barn. 
8. All necessary permits must be obtained and fees shall be paid prior to the 
commencement of any construction activity, including but limited to the Summit 
County Engineering and the Summit County Building Departments.
