         2. PUBLIC HEARING TO DISCUSS AMENDMENTS TO THE OPERATING AND CAPITAL BUDGET OF WEBER
            COUNTY FOR THE 2019 CALENDAR YEAR.
             Scott Parke, County Comptroller, presented the resolution with the summary of adjustments. The highlights
             included $300,000 for a road grader, the county received several large grants, including $750,000 from the
             State for the Archery Range, an additional $230,000 is needed to cover workers’ compensation increased
             costs, and the Library will spend some of the fund balance that they have saved over the years for
             improvements, Chair Jenkins invited public comments and none were offered.
         3. PUBLIC HEARING TO CONSIDER AND TAKE ACTION ON ZTA 2019-05, A PROPOSAL TO AMEND THE LAND
            USE CODE, TITLES 101 AND 106, TO REMOVE ANTIQUATED SLOPE AND ROAD AREA REQUIREMENTS
            APPLICABLE TO CLUSTER SUBDIVISIONS, PRUD’S, AND MASTER PLANNED DEVELOPMENTS.
             Charles Ewert, of the County Planning Division, stated that when the Cluster Subdivision Code was amended
             in 2018 the county amended the method of calculating density for nontraditional subdivisions (e.g., cluster
             subdivisions) to overcome some issues and enhance incentives of clustering by eliminating the slope
             reduction from the density calculation. In the amendment process, a section that was buried in the code was
             missed and this item corrects the oversight. The current calculation of density for a cluster subdivision
             requires an exclusion of 10% of the gross land because of assumed area required for streets. In reviewing
             this item with the Ogden Valley Planning Commission, they wanted to see the definition of net developable
             acreage to also change to say that it is just the area of road space that the developer actually proposes, not an
             assumed static or constant number, and the county removed the 10%. Commissioner Froerer declared conflict
             of interest because he just realized that the applicant is one of his current clients. Christopher Crockett,
             Deputy County Attorney, said that as a legislative item he did not believe it prohibited Commissioner Froerer
             from voting unless the two other commissioners believed it was a conflict.
