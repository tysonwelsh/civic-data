Unified Police Department Report



                                              1

               Chief Del Craig, Unified Police Department, reported that theft has been a big
issue recently. There have been issues at Copper Park, burglaries at construction sites, junction
boxes being broken into with the intent to pull out the wire, and catalytic converters stolen from
cars. Right now, metal prices are high.

             Council Member Hull stated Chamber West has a bill in the Legislature’s
upcoming session to try to make it hard for people to turn around catalytic converters.

                Chief Craig continued the report stating the UPD is currently working on
reunification drills with the school districts to practice reunifying children with their families, in the
event of an emergency. Then, on October 26th, the UPD did a search warrant of five places, two
of which were in Magna where there are gang members living with whom the UPD has had issues
with before. The search warrant resulted in a large amount of drugs and money seized, primarily
from a target in Salt Lake City. There were also some gang related homicides earlier in the year.

              Council Member Hull asked if the UPD was seeing gang activity in the junior high
schools and high schools.

              Chief Craig stated it has not seen a lot of that there, but the gang world has
changed substantially. Gang members are not as flagrant as they used to be. There is now a
low incidence of graffiti, which was something the UPD used to use as an indicator. That is
another reason it is trying to reach out to the schools – it wants to start building a relationship with
the youth and build some trust.

Citizens

               James Stanley Pappas asked if the Council would expand its draft noise
ordinance to include harassment, stalking, and fireworks. He has a neighbor who is doing all
these things to him. Current laws do not allow law enforcement to stop the harassment. If that
was incorporated into the ordinance, it might give law enforcement the ability to take action on
this.

              Paul Ashton stated the Council’s draft ordinance only pertains to noise. Nuisance
ordinances are very hard to draft and enforce because unless they are specific, the courts do not
enforce them.

            Chief Craig stated the UPD has been trying to get these neighbors to come to
some agreement on this. The hard part is being able to monitor the conduct without observing
it.

                Council Member Hull asked if having something on camera would work.

             Chief Craig stated capturing harassment with camera footage would be good.
That would be the best thing to use to make a case without it happening in law enforcement’s

Council Reports

Unified Police Department (UPD) / Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

               Council Member Prokopis stated at the last UPD Board of Directors meeting, the
board approved a tax increase, and that will be in the tentative budget. One hundred percent of
the new money generated will go towards employee compensation. All sworn officers will get
between 6 and 16 percent raises, depending on where they fall in the market. This will go a long
way in helping to address ongoing recruiting challenges. There will be about a 16 percent tax
increase for SLVLESA. Its budget does not start until January, but the raises will be retroactive
to November 1st provided the tax increase passes in December.

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

              Council Member Prokopis stated the UFA has hired four paramedics who started
yesterday, and it is hiring another 35 firefighters in January. It is hoping to have the old building
demolished by the end of October. There has been some trouble with getting right of ways

through one of the neighboring properties to run power. Then, the new station in Midvale has
been under construction for several months. Also, the UFA is working on getting booster shots
for all of its employees, as they were vaccinated in December and January. About 90-92 percent

Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD)

               Mayor Peay stated the MSD is working on its budget now and discussing the bond.
The Board also discusses other things, but he does not report on most of what it does because it
does not affect the Magna Metro Township.

              Paul Ashton gave an update on the status of the bond. The interlocal agreement
that the Council approved went to the District Attorney’s Office, and it made some immaterial
changes. However, Mayor Jennifer Wilson wants to look at it one last time. The bond attorneys
have also looked at it and everyone is on board with it.

Magna Mosquito Abatement District

               Council Member Pierce stated the Mosquito Abatement District recently attended
a conference in Park City. There were a lot of informative presentations. The Magna Mosquito
Abatement District presented on the difference between the effects of two different chemicals that
it used in Magna last year.

                Paul Ashton stated there will be a bill at the Legislature at its next session
regarding canals and irrigation ditches, and it might impact mosquito districts because of the
chemicals they use. He asked Council Member Pierce to inform the Magna Mosquito Abatement
District about that.
