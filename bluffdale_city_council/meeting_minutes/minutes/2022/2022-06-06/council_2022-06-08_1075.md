       7.4     Budget Discussion Relating to Various Budget and Finance Topics.                    (Staff
               Presenters, Stephanie Thayer and Bruce Kartchner).

Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner overviewed the 2023 Fiscal Year Tentative
Budget and stated that adjustments have been made. The latest version was provided as of the
previous Friday. The previous day, preliminary numbers were received from the County on the
Certified Tax Rate. Once the final numbers are received they will be added to the model. The intent
was to adopt the budget on June 22, which is the deadline put forward by the State. Due to inflation,
an increase in the Certified Tax Rate was recommended for Property Taxes. He described the
rationale and why they believe it is impo1iant for the City to consider an increase.

Mr. Kartchner reported that the underlying premise of Utah State Law is that as property values
increase, municipalities should not receive a windfall of revenue as a result of the revaluation of
properties. It is called an equalization project. As property values rise each year, the property tax
rate that is part of the City budget must of necessity decrease. That adjusted rate each year is known
as the Certified Tax Rate. After looking at the budget and analyzing the needs of the City, it was
recommended that this year the Certified Tax Rate remain the same as last year at .001519. Staff
acknowledged that they will be raising additional revenues as a result and a Truth in Taxation Hearing
will be required to be held in August.

Mr. Kartchner explained that Public Safety costs are one of the most fundamental City services.
Property taxes are the most stable revenue source and help pay for fundamentally important services.
Comparisons since 2008 were presented showing costs and property taxes collected each year.
Around 2008, they were close to the same amount but over the years the increase in Public Safety
costs has exceeded the increase in property tax revenues. If the same graph were continued through
fiscal year 2023 and accepted the County's preliminary rates, their coverage for Public Safety costs
would drop from 44% this year to 36% next year. By imposing the proposed increase, the result will
be to cover 47% of the costs, which is close to what it has been for the last five years.

Mr. Kartchner investigated what other cities are doing and stated that neighboring cities cover Public
Safety costs in a variety of ways. For the current fiscal year, Bluffdale has the lowest coverage of
surrounding cities. He pointed out that not all of the taxes paid come to Bluffdale City. The portion
of the total taxes paid that the City collects is less than 14% for the current tax year. For the proposed
tax year, it is less than 14%. It was estimated that the tax increase for the average homeowner will be
$11.43 per month based on the average home value of $691 ,200.

Mr. Kartchner explained that different revenue has different characteristics. Sales taxes, for example,
are highly dependent on inflation and the economy and fluctuates significantly. From 2009 through
2011, cities that relied heavily on sales taxes were struggling because it is not a stable revenue source.
He explained that the State law in Utah allows no windfall for rising prices, which has the opposite
effect when home prices are falling in that the rate increases. The result is stabilization of revenue
source. In looking at different revenue sources, the intent was to look at the situation from the
standpoint that the services are impo1iant and the revenues are stable. As a result, they ought to be
matched up.

Mr. Reid pointed out that Bluffdale used to have the lowest property tax rate other than Sandy City
and the Town of Alta in the County. When he first came to work for the City, they had no franchise
tax, no economic development, and very little sales tax. The Council was extremely pleased that they
were able to run a City with no expenses. At that time, they had just gone from a volunteer Fire
Department to full-time and moved from Salt Lake County Police to the Unified Police Department
("UPD"). The intent behind the creation of the UPD was to bring in a stable revenue source. The
decision was made by the City to enter into a joint contract with Saratoga Springs because the Council
was not comfortable imposing a greater tax obligation on the citizens. The addition of Franchise Tax
and the work done by the Council to promote economic development helped them recover other costs.
He confirmed that stable revenue sources are the most important. He noted that the two largest
expense categories are Police and Fire.

Mr. Kartchner displayed a chart of neighboring cities and showed where Bluffdale falls with respect
to the Certified Tax Rate. He repo1ied that historically and currently, Bluffdale is largely a bedroom
