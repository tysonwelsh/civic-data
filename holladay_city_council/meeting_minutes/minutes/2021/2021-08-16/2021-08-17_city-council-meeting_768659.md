        a.      Consideration of Ordinance 2021-21 Determining the Rate of Tax for 2021/2022
                Tax Year and Levying Taxes Upon All Real and Personal Property within the City.
Mayor Dahle shared opening remarks with those present. He explained that he had been on the
Holladay City Council for 7 ½ years and had been through seven budgets. During each budget
presentation, there had always been a statement to indicate that no property tax increase was proposed
at that time, however, this approach was not sustainable long-term. This decision resulted in a
reduction of the Capital Improvement portion of the budget due to inflation. Mayor Dahle explained
that he was committed to bringing these issues to the public in order to hear what they had to say
about the challenges that currently face the City of Holladay.

Three years ago, the property tax increase process began. It began with the Citizen Advisory Group,
which included eight citizens of various professional capacities. That group worked with consultants
to scope the public infrastructure in the City and determine what condition it was in. The Pavement
Condition Index was created. It rated each of the roads and priced out what it would cost to bring the
roads to at least an average level. Between the roads and storm drains, the estimated costs were $75
million. The Citizen Advisory Group worked with consultants to come up with a plan that they
thought Holladay residents would want. This was blended with surveys that were conducted along
the way through a company called Y2 Analytics. The surveys asked residents to share their thoughts
about the City of Holladay as well as what they wanted to see in the City.

The Citizen Advisory Group eventually passed a recommendation onto the City Council to raise
property taxes to a point where a strategic plan could be funded. That plan would take the City out
10 to 20 years and would start to address the shortfall in infrastructure investment. Mayor Dahle

website and through public engagement. It had been a long process and the City tried to provide as

make sure the process was transparent. Mayor Dahle was proud of everything that had been rolled
out in terms of the data and opportunities for comment.

Mayor Dahle reported that property taxes had not been raised since the City of Holladay incorporated
21 years earlier. He noted that there had been some frustration trying to communicate certain things
to the public. For instance, numerous complaints were received from residents who indicated that
property values had increased and that increase should be used to cover expenses. Mayor Dahle
clarified that due to the Truth in Taxation Legislation, the mill rate dropped to maintain the property
tax level. It could not be raised until the City Council went through the current process. Ms. Smith
shared a considerable amount of information online related to this misconception but it had been a
difficult issue to explain. The only way the City received new property tax was through new growth
or through the annexations that had taken place over the last 21 years.

Mayor Dahle opened the public hearing. He reported that 45 comments were submitted via email.
The Council Members had reviewed those comments. Copies of the emails would be included with
the City Council Meeting Minutes.

An unknown gentleman reported that he paid $200,000 for a condominium nine years ago. Over the
summer, three different real estate agents contacted him and offered $450,000 for his remodeled
condominium. Some of the remodeled condominiums had sold for $500,000. If his condominium
was worth $500,000, he wondered why the property taxes would need to increase. He would be
paying a great deal more than what he paid on the original $200,000. Property home values in the
Holladay area had increased over 20% in the last year and he did not understand why taxes would
need to be raised.

Council Member Gibbons reported that he was a first-term Council Member but he served on the first
elected Holladay City Council in 1999. At that time, he agreed to not raise property taxes because
back then, the City was in a very different situation financially. The Cottonwood Mall was a huge
financial boon to the City and sales taxes from the Mall added an extra $500,000 or more each year.
Property taxes were not increased back then because the City did not need the money. When the Mall
closed in 2008, the hope was that it would quickly reopen and the City would be able to regain some
sales tax revenue. That was not the case.

Truth in Taxation was passed as a statute in 1985 and meant that property taxes could not be raised
by inflation. The City receives a dollar amount that is set every year. The only way that number is
increased is due to new growth or annexation. Council Member Gibbons explained that the property
tax revenue had been frozen at 1999 levels for the past 22 years. The City decided that due to fiscal
needs they should take a step forward to partially recoup inflationary losses the City had experienced
over the last 22 years. He noted that residents will be unable to live off of incomes they earned in
1999 and the same was true for the City of Holladay. He did not believe it was reasonable to expect
the City to continue to live on 1999 level property tax revenue.

Council Member Gibbons was unsure about the property tax increase until he dove into the details of
the Truth in Taxation statute. It was unsustainable for the City of Holladay to survive off of that
revenue indefinitely. As a result, he planned to vote in support of the property tax increase. Council
Member Gibbons felt it was the right thing for the City. It was important to get things in order with
respect to the infrastructure needs of the City.

Council Member Quinn reported that even though property values had increased, the share of property
tax that comes to the City remains the same. For individual property tax, the share that a certain
property was charged can change slightly. For example, if one property tax bill goes up then another
property tax bill would go down. The City receives the same amount every year. It depends on how
the properties are assessed by the County Assessor. Ms. Chamness explained that the County Auditor
calculated a rate based on valuations determined by the County Assessor. They are based on
valuations as of January 1 of any given year. As a result, the taxes paid in November are based on
the valuations from January 1. The County Assessor sets the valuation and the County Auditor
calculates a rate that generates the same amount of revenue for the City based on all of those changes
in valuation.

Council Member Quinn commented that most people she had spoken to understand that the City needs
more revenue. However, some felt that 50% was too much at one time. She believed that this was
more about people than numbers. One of her neighbors had a gutter on one side of his driveway that
had sunk below the level of the street and as a result, he was unable to drive on that side of his
driveway without scraping the bottom of his car. This neighbor had been waiting several years for
this to be fixed. It was on the list of City projects but there had not been money to pay for the repair.
People in her district email her regularly to state that the road is in terrible shape and want to know
when they will be repaved. While the tax increase could be done more gradually, it was important to
focus on solutions and not have to pick and choose which constituents were able to have their gutters
fixed and their streets resurfaced.

Council Member Durham thanked the Citizen Advisory Group. He noted that they did a lot of work
in putting together a proposal and recommendation. The Citizen Advisory Group initially
recommended an increase that was significantly higher than what was being proposed currently but
did a lot of work to look into different options and alternatives. Council Member Durham also
thanked staff and the residents who took the time to share feedback. It is a very important decision
and merited a lot of discussion and input from all those affected. He assured those present that
Council Members had read through all of the comments. There had been town meetings and open
houses and the City Council had listened to many different residents. Council Member Durham
explained that this was not a decision that anyone on the Council took lightly.

Council Member Durham noted that some residents had expressed concerns that the money coming
in was not being spent efficiently. He stated that this was not an issue. Many needs had gone unmet
because the City had been stuck at a 1999 funding level while the infrastructure continued to
deteriorate. Holladay had not increased the property tax rate in many years and even if the proposed
property tax increase happened, Holladay would still have a lower tax rate than many neighboring
cities. Rejecting this proposal would endanger the quality of life in the City. Council Member
Durham sympathized with those struggling to understand how the property tax system worked.
However, he noted that while it was a complicated system, it was the option that the State Legislature
had given the City to generate money for what needed to be done.

Something Council Member Durham had learned through this process was that as the City
infrastructure deteriorates, it becomes more and more expensive to repair. If repairs can be made
early on, it saves the City a considerable amount of money. He felt it was good stewardship to
consider the repairs that need to be made and focus on making sure those repairs take place efficiently
and cost-effectively. Council Member Durham would likely vote in favor of the proposed tax increase
because he felt it was important to provide a certain quality of life in the City of Holladay.

Council Member Petersen felt similar to her fellow Council Members. She expressed appreciation to
both staff and the City Council as well as the citizens who shared their comments. There were several
comments that she wanted to address. Annexation had been mentioned several times. Some residents
wondered why a property tax increase is necessary when there had been annexing in different areas.
Council Member Petersen explained that it was important to keep in mind that while more tax income
was received due to annexing in new areas, many of those areas were in worse condition than some
areas in Holladay. For example, Council Member Quinn’s district had been neglected by the County,
and some costly repairs needed to be done there. Although taxes came in from the area when it was
annexed, far more went out to ensure that the roads were at an appropriate level of service.

There was discussion regarding the Holla Dollars program. Council Member Petersen explained that
this program was intended to incentivize residents to frequent restaurants and stores in the City. That
money had not been taken out of the City funds. Federal funding due to the COVID-19 pandemic
was received and staff used it wisely. Mayor Dahle added that COVID-19 funds were restrictive in
terms of how they could be spent. The money that had gone into the business grants and Holla Dollar
program were meant to help the businesses that had been most impacted by the events of the last year.
Those funds could only be spent in certain ways.

Council Member Fotheringham thanked the Citizen Advisory Group for their dedication. He had
served as the Council Liaison and explained that the group was made up of professional problem
solvers including a professional engineer, chemist, business strategist, and rocket scientist. The
Citizen Advisory Group helped gather data, analyze problems, look at costs and revenue and explore
the impacts of the recommendations. They provided a number of different options as well as a final
recommendation. The decision was made to choose the recommendation that had the biggest impact
because, in the long run, it would cost the least. This had to do with the Pavement Condition Index.
The longer the City waits to repair a road, the more costs will increase. Council Member
Fotheringham expressed his appreciation to staff and Ms. Smith in particular. He intended to vote in
favor of the proposed property tax increase because he felt it was the right thing to do for the City.

Mayor Dahle discussed the condominiums that were being built in the City. That was new growth
and would account for additional tax revenue coming into the City. However, he pointed out that the
growth in the City was approximately 1%, which is relatively small. There was a graph on the
Holladay@20 website that showed the property tax line. There are bumps when annexation happens
and a very gradual increase. This accounted for a flat property tax rate and the 1% new growth.
Mayor Dahle reported that the Utah Taxpayers Association stated that they would not oppose the
property tax increase based on the information they received and their review of what had happened
in the City. Their recommendation was that every five years there should be an incremental increase
of 10 to 15 percent to take care of inflationary periods. That was a recommendation he felt should be
passed along to future City Councils. Mayor Dahle noted that he would support the proposed property
tax increase because he felt it would give the future City Councils a long-term strategic toolbox to
plan and implement changes within the City.
