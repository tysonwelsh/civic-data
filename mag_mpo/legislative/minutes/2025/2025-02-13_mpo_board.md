Advanced Air Mobility (00:14:58) Mayor Julie Fullmer introduced Paul Damron from UDOT's Advanced Air Mobility department to discuss new opportunities in the state related to airports and advanced air mobility. Paul Damron presented a comprehensive overview of advanced air mobility, explaining its evolution from drones to potential air taxis. He detailed Utah's progress in developing advanced air mobility, including legislative support, regulatory changes, and infrastructure planning. The presentation covered milestones such as defining vertiports, addressing drone regulations, and conducting an economic impact study that projected 11,000 new jobs and $8 billion in business activity. Paul Damron discussed the state's phased approach to implementing advanced air mobility, starting with small drones and progressing towards potential medical support and passenger transportation. The presentation highlighted Utah's proactive stance in creating a supportive ecosystem for this emerging technology, including working with legislators, developing test sites, and preparing for future transportation innovations. Attendees asked questions about safety, cost, and practical implementation of these new transportation technologies. TIP Modifications - Orem 1600 N/400 E Roundabout - Scope Change (00:43:19) Bob Allen presented the modification request from Orem City for the 1600 North and 400 East intersection project. Originally awarded $1.4 million for a roundabout, Orem proposed two key changes: implementing short-term active transportation safety improvements and using remaining funds to develop and design a grade-separated pedestrian crossing. Commissioner Skyler Beltran moved to deny the Orem 1600 N 400 E Roundabout project scope change. Mayor Steve Staheli seconded the motion, and the motion passed. Councilmember Chris Killpack, Commissioner Jim Evans, and Mayor Dan Olson voted nay. Page 3 of 5 After the motion, concerns were raised about spending $800,000 on design work for a project without guaranteed future funding. It was suggested potentially separating the two components - short-term improvements and design work. The board recommended that Orem City return with a more specific proposal, particularly for the short-term improvements, and clarify potential funding sources for the long-term grade-separated crossing. The consensus was that while safety improvements were important, the Board wanted more clarity on the project's feasibility and funding. Data Modeling (01:08:53) Tim Hereth presented the data modeling process for the Regional Transportation Plan. The presentation focused on how MAG estimates current and future population and job locations using sophisticated modeling techniques. They use two primary models: the Real Estate Market (REM) model and a traﬃc modeling system. The process involves collecting detailed building stock data from the county assessor, using near-map imagery, and incorporating city general plans to create accurate socioeconomic estimates. Key points included: ● Models are updated every four years (soon to be every two years) ● They collect housing inventory and non-residential square footage ● Population and job estimates are created in collaboration with the Kem C. Gardner Policy Institute ● Forecasting extends to 2055 ● The models use multiple data sources including American Community Survey, travel surveys, and UDOT counts ● A peer review by experts from other MPOs validated their modeling approach The goal is to produce accurate information for transportation planning, particularly the Regional Transportation Plan. Tim Hereth emphasized the complexity of predicting job-housing balance and noted the challenges of build-out scenarios. The next steps involve adjusting forecasts with new housing data, continuing work with the Kem C. Gardner Policy Institute, and preparing estimates to be reviewed in spring and draft plans to be completed in fall and winter.
