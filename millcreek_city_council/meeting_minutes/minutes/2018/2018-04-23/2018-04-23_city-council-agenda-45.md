2. Proposed Millcreek Canyon Transportation trial program; Laynee Jones

Mayor Silvestrini said the Central Wasatch Commission is an entity that will oversee
regulating the canyons in Salt Lake County. The Commission currently has 7 members, but
the Mayor has filed an application for Millcreek to be added. There may be a financial
commitment to it in the future if Millcreek is accepted. The contribution goes towards
funding the Commission, which has a budget of about $1.6 million. There are 2 positions
available and Alta, Summit County, and Millcreek have applied to be on it.

Laynee Jones said she approached the Mayor about a trial project in Millcreek Canyon to run
shuttles to alleviate parking. She is proposing a $50,000 pilot project to run shuttles into
Millcreek Canyon for a couple of months this summer. She hopes to refine a series of pilot
projects over the next couple of years so that one day when there is a toll that there will be a
system of shuttle providers that pay for themselves. The reasons she thinks Millcreek Canyon
would be a good place to start is because it is a shorter canyon, she thinks the Council would
support it, and in 2011 the approved master plan for the Canyon has a shuttle program. She
suggested sending out an RFP and said UTA would provide free vehicles. Jones said they
could use Skyline High School for parking. Council Member Jackson pointed out that the
Skyline High School parking lot would work for this year, but with the rebuild happening
next year, that location would not be available. Mayor Silvestrini suggested Churchill Jr.
High, Eastwood Elementary, or Wasatch Jr. High could be an option. He has spoken to
Mayor McAdams at the County about putting up $30,000 and the City puts up $20,000 for
the project. Council Member Uipi asked how far west the parking and shuttling would go to
service more residents of Millcreek. Jones said she is exploring is an on-demand ride share
program that carries multiple passengers, that is why she is suggesting putting out an RFP to
get innovative providers involved. Council Member Jackson asked where in the budget this
program could come from. Laurie Johnson said the City could reduce the fund balance for
this fiscal year and make a line item for the next fiscal year. Council Member Uipi suggested
engaging the public at Venture Outdoors. Laynee Jones said she could oversee the project.
The Council was interested in looking at a project proposal. Mayor Silvestrini will talk to
Mayor McAdams about putting together an RFP that the County will write but the City will
distribute. Council Member Uipi asked about the sustainability of the project. The Mayor
said the project is a partnership and the idea is to find out the interest for the project through
the pilot program.

2.5 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 18-_, Approving Supporting the
HB 362 (2015) authorized 0.25% Local Option General Sales Tax Dedicated to
Transportation, and Encouraging Salt Lake County to, Per SB 136 (2018),
Impose the Tax in 2018

Mayor Silvestrini said a new law was enacted in the legislature this year and this bill
strongly encourage counties to adopt the quarter percent local option sales tax to
support transportation. The sales tax was presented on the ballot in 2015 and it
nearly failed countywide. The legislature has now asked counties to enact the quarter
percent sales tax to support transportation funding. The County Council has
requested support from the municipalities in passing the local option sales tax. The
benefit to passing it now before 2019, is that cities will receive .1 percent of money
that is collected. If the counties do not pass it by then, the State has mentioned that
they will levy the tax and keep the money. This is a way for Millcreek to get about
an extra million dollars a year. The initiative is supported by the Utah League of
Cities and Towns.

Council Member Uipi moved to approve item 2.5. Council Member Jackson seconded.
Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.
