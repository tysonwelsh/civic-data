         The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.,
with a focus on the certified tax rate; discuss a proposed rezone at 155 West 14th Street; discuss a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-
2026 budget amendment pertaining to Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) funding; discuss a proposed FY 2025-2026 staffing
document amendment; discuss proposed Arts Grants funding; discuss Council business; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review
           Chair White and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.
           Certified Tax Rate: Council Assistant Executive Director Symes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the
certified tax rate for Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2026. According to Utah Code 59-2-924(1)(g), certified tax rate means a tax rate that
will provide the same ad valorum property tax revenue for a taxing entity as was budgeted by that taxing entity for the prior year.
The certified tax rate allows taxing entities to receive the same property tax revenue from the previous year plus new growth and
as the City’s total taxable value increases, the certified tax rate decreases. If the City’s total taxable value decreases, the certified
tax rate increases. No inflationary adjustment is built into the calculation. Mr. Symes presented a graphic to illustrate the manner
in which the rate is calculated; the City’s total budgeted revenue is divided by the total property valuation and new growth is then
added in. He also presented a chart illustrating the manner in which the City’s certified tax rate has adjusted from year to year
based upon the formula used by the County. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the
meeting.
           Finance Director Sorensen explained when the FY 2025–2026 Budget was adopted on June 17, 2025, the final certified
tax rate had not yet been provided by the State Tax Commission. The adopted budget did not propose a property tax increase,
other than the natural growth in revenue and the increment associated with the expiration of two Redevelopment Agency (RDA)
project areas—Lincoln and Fairmount. The proposed certified tax rate for FY 2025–2026 is 0.002193, a slight decrease due to
increased assessed valuations across the City. This rate will allow Ogden City to collect the same amount of property tax revenue
as it did in FY 2024–2025, meeting the statutory requirement for revenue neutrality. According to Weber County data, the average
residential property value in Ogden increased by 2.4 percent, rising from $421,800 to $431,940. Residential properties are taxed at
55 percent of their assessed value, while non-residential properties are taxed at 100 percent. At the proposed certified tax rate, the
annual tax on a primary residential property valued at $431,940 would be approximately $947.24. Mr. Sorensen noted that
Administration’s property tax revenue projections were higher than what will be generated based upon the certified tax rate by
approximately $900,000; Administration recommends keeping that revenue projection in the budget and the staff in his Division
will monitor revenues throughout the FY to determine if any adjustments are needed. He stated the City does have a fund balance
to rely upon in the event there is a revenue shortfall at the end of the FY. This is the second year that the revenue projection has
been higher than actual revenue based upon the certified tax rate and this matter should be carefully evaluated prior to adoption of
the FY 2026-2027 budget.
          The Council debated whether Mr. Sorenson’s recommendation is best practice or whether a budget amendment should be
considered at this time rather than later in the fiscal year to correct the revenue projection.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026 Budget Amendment – CIP Funding
         Finance Director Sorensen stated Administration is proposing to allocate $8 million from the Capital Improvement Plan
(CIP) Fund to three projects: $5 million for the acquisition and demolition of the Weber County jail building, $2.5 million for
Union Station Museum and campus improvements, and $500,000 to General Park improvements for an all-abilities park. The $8
million is from ARPA funds the City received but had to encumber by December 31, 2024. The City received a total of $29.6
million in federal ARPA and state ARPA funds. The Council had previously approved the appropriation of approximately $20
million in previous actions. The Council approved a budget amendment on December 17, 2024, to appropriate the remaining
$9,042,825 of ARPA funds. In that budget action, the Council approved an ordinance reducing public safety wages and replacing
them with the ARPA funds and in turn placed $8 million of the offset into a restricted CIP account. Any use of funds from this
account would need additional Council action. The additional $1,042,825 from the ARPA funds was allocated to Lester Park
improvements. While the $8 million was set aside with the budget action on December 17, 2024, the Fiscal Year 2026 budget
included the appropriation of the funds to the three projects discussed with the initial budget amendment proposal in December
2024. Though included in the budget, the proposed allocation was not discussed in detail during the review of the budget. With the
approval of the Fiscal Year 2026 budget on June 17, 2025, the Council amended the final Schedule A-1 document to remove the
allocation of the $8 million for the three projects and place the funds back into the restricted CIP fund.

Proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026 Staffing Document Amendments
          Finance Director Sorensen stated Administration proposes to amend the Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2026 Staffing Document
to add five unfunded firefighter positions. These additional positions will provide the Fire Department with more flexibility in the
hiring process and enable proactive hiring in anticipation of known or expected vacancies. Adding the positions will have no fiscal
impact on the Fiscal Year 2025-2026 budget.
          Council member Graf asked why this is necessary; he asked if there is some type of Human Resources regulation that
prevents the Fire Department from interviewing and hiring a new employee predicated upon an existing employment leaving the
City. Chief Mathieu stated he is not able to extend an offer of employment to an individual until the position they will fill has
actually been vacated; he stated that two individuals are retiring from the Department this summer and he had the opportunity to
hire a highly qualified individual to fill one of those positions, but he could not extend the offer until the position was actually
vacant. Creating floating positions will help to address this issue, but it is not something he envisions being utilized frequently.
Mr. Sorensen noted that this practice has been used successfully in the Police Department for several years.

Proposed Rezone at 155 West 14th Street
          Planning Director Brierley explained G R I G, LLC has submitted a petition to rezone 0.73 acres from M-1 (Limited
Manufacturing) to C-3 (Regional Commercial) or FLEX Industrial. The subject property is the Iron House Gym property located
on 14th Street, south of WinCo near Wall Avenue. The Iron House Gym has a 3,000 square foot office/showroom section that the
owner has recently turned into a tattoo parlor that the Planning Division considers to be a personal services-type business. The
property is currently zoned M-1 which allows a fitness center/gym but does not allow a personal service business. The C-3 and
FLEX zones allow both a fitness center and a personal services-type business. On May 7, 2025, the Planning Commission
considered the proposal and voted unanimously to recommend approval of the rezone to C-3.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on potential impacts the proposal could have on adjacent
properties, after which Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported action on the petition is scheduled for July 15.

Proposed Arts Grants Funding
         Arts Administrator Buckley explained the Ogden City Arts Advisory Committee (OCAAC) has conducted the arts grant
process including advertising to recruit applicants and reviewing and grading the submitted applications. Ogden City staff
assembled an arts grants review subcommittee in accordance with Ordinance 2015-41 and formed a process for the grading
system for the subcommittee in compliance with the Arts Advisory Committee Ordinance allowing each grant application to be
measured equally using the grant criteria. The individual scores for each grant were averaged to determine a final overall score for
each application. Those scores were then used to rank the grants in both the General Support and Project Support categories. The
application review subcommittee included seven members of the OCAAC, Ogden City’s Community Engagement Administrator,
and a Policy Analyst representative from City Council. The Committee’s recommendations for disbursement are attached.
Twenty-nine applications are recommended for funding at varying amounts. The Administration recommends that the City
Council approve funding based on the grant review committee’s recommendations. Ms. Buckley used the aid of a PowerPoint
