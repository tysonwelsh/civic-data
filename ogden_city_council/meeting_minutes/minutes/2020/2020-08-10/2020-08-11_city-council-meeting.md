         Disposition of Unclaimed Property
                   Council Policy Analyst Mabey explained the City Council has received two requests to consider the disposition of
         unclaimed or legally confiscated property acquired by the Ogden Police Deparhnent and Weber Morgan Narcotics Strike Force.
         Utah law provides for the disposition of firearms no longer needed as evidence and outlines specific standards for the disposal of
         unclaimed/confiscated firearms. An agency may choose to dispose of unclaimed/confiscated fireanns, but in doing so is required
         to sell the firearms to a federally licensed firearms dealer and apply the proceeds from the sale to a public interest use, or to
         destroy the firearm ifunfit for sale or if the firearm is associated with a notorious crime. Before an agency applies the proceeds to
         a public interest use, the legislative body must provide permission to do so and designate and approve the public interest use.
         Agencies are also required to comply with Utah Procurement Code.
                   Deputy City Attorney Brown explained the Weber Morgan Narcotics Strike Force is requesting that the City Council
         adopt a resolution to authorize the disposition of unclaimed/confiscated fireanns and currency that are currently in possession of
         the Strike Force. The Strike Force intends to sell the firearms and appropriate the proceeds and currency for building maintenance
         and repair. Pursuant to an interlocal agreement, Ogden City is responsible for fiscal operations of the Strike Force, including
         budgeting, purchasing, and contracting functions. As a result of this agreement, the Weber County Attorney's Office has
         determined that the Ogden City Council consider this resolution as proposed.
                   Deputy Police Chief Young explained Police Captain Sube oversees the Field Operations Bureau, which is where Police
         Deparhnent evidence is housed. He noted that Weber Morgan Narcotics Strike Force Commander Lieutenant Kovalski oversees
         collected evidence for that agency. The three men responded to the questions included in the City Council's meeting packet
         regarding this subject matter. This included an overview of policies that govern the disposition of unclaimed and confiscated
         property, detailed information about the items to be disposed of, and the use of funds to potentially be secured through the
         disposition of the firearms. The City should not be receiving a great deal of money through the disposition of the items, but any

Agenda Review
          Chair Choberka and members of Council staff briefly reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council
meeting scheduled to begin at 6 :00 p.m. Council Deputy Director Symes reported on developments related to agenda item dealing
with adoption of the Southeast Ogden Community Plan; the item was tabled during the July 14 meeting to allow for continued
consideration and discussion with the entire City Council present. Planning staff has provided an optional amendment for the
Council to consider when acting on adoption of the Plan. This language provides more detail regarding unique characteristics in
the Southeast Ogden Community Plan area that differentiates it from other neighborhoods in the community and can be
considered as the basis for limiting the number of accessory dwelling units (ADUs) in specific neighborhoods ofthe Plan area.
The main unique characteristic relates to the area's proximity to Weber State University (WSU) and the housing pressures on the
neighborhood based on this relationship.
          Discussion among the Council centered on the differences between the ADU land use and typical rental homes and
whether residents of the Plan area understand those differences. There was also a focus on the claims made by residents of other
areas of the City that feel that the residents of the Plan area are being given preferential difference based upon the consideration of
limiting the number of ADUs in neighborhoods there. Mr. Spencer indicated that he understands that claim but referred back to
the unique characteristics that have been identified for this Plan area that cause staff to support a request from the community to
limit ADUs. Discussion then shifted to the City's ability to enforce ordinances against illegal rental units in the community and
whether residents can initiate a code enforcement action. Mr. Spencer stated that the City is developing a page for the website that
will give public information about legally licensed rental units in the City and offer residents the ability to submit an electronic
request for enforcement against illegal rental units.
          Council member Hyer stated it is important to provide the distinction that Community Plan steering committees are
advisory bodies and do not have the ability to make binding decisions for their community; there is a perception that the Council
would be doing something wrong if they made a decision that is different from what the steering committee has recommended.
Council member Nadolski agreed but noted that individuals who participate in these types of processes can become
disenfranchised if all the work they have done and the recommendations they have made are completely disregarded. Chair
Choberka stated that she hopes that those who have participated in the public planning process understand that a Council action
that may vary somewhat from the recommendation provided by the steering committee should not be cause for offense; it is
important for the Council to weigh all options and consider the greater good of the community. She is very focused on creating an
environment of inclusivity throughout the entire City; it is acceptable for the Council to disagree with part of the recommendation
provided by the steering committee and that should not be interpreted as the Council not thinking about the issue in its entirety.
Council member Nadolski stated that ifhe felt the recommendation from the steering committee were irresponsible or perpetuated
bias in any way, he would not accept it; however, he feels that the recommendation is based on what is good for the Southeast
Ogden Community Plan area as well as the entire community as a whole. He indicated he would not be opposed to considering
similar limitations for other Plan areas in the City.

    Council Business
              Consideration ofMask Policy: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated that she has learned that if a City wants to
    opt-in to a State mandate on mask wearing, the City needs to let the State Department of Health know of those wishes. However,
    the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) has indicated that this must be done in the fonn of an ordinance adoption. She has
    reached out to general counsel for the Governor's Office regarding the process for opting into a program and she will report back
    to the Council when she has more clear infonnation. If ordinance adoption is necessary and the Council wants to mandate masks
    in the City, it would take a majority of the Council to adopt an ordinance. The Council engaged in discussion ofa mandate and
    ultimately concluded they do not want to support a mandate because it would be very difficult to enforce; however, Council
    member Lopez indicated that the Council can lead by example and wear masks in their daily lives and especially when they are
    engaged in City business at a City facility. He also wondered if mask wearing could be required by employees or visitors in City
    buildings. Ms. Eller-Smith stated that is an administrative decision for the Mayor to make and if the Council wishes to pursue that,
    they could request the Mayor consider such a policy. Council members Stephens and Nadolski suggested that the Council and
    Mayor consider a joint resolution supporting mask wearing in the community. The Council discussed this idea and agreed it would
    be meaningful to adopt a joint resolution and work to spread the message to the community that both the Council and City
    Administration encourage mask wearing in Ogden City. Ms. Eller-Smith stated she will work with Administration to coordinate a
    joint action on this issue.
