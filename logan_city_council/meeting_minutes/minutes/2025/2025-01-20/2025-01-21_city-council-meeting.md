Gail Hansen, a resident of Logan spoke in regard to the importance of celebrating and

Joshua Molitor, a resident of Logan suggested alternative ventures to address the housing

Jeff & Diana King, residents of Logan thanked the Council for the opportunity to speak
and expressed their gratitude for all the Council does for the City. They spoke in regard to
the need for and importance of a community recreation center as the recreation center will
be handed over to the Logan School District. Recreation content available to the public is
crucial as a means of education and public health. They requested in the meantime the
agreement between the City and the Logan School District extend the use of the agreement
of the recreation center. They do not believe it is in the best interest of the citizens of Logan
for the agreement to be terminated without having something else in place.
Mayor Daines would respond fully to the Kings in an email, but part of the reason is that
Logan High School has two additional sanctioned sports along with additional sports needs.
Many of the programs in place are used by Cache County patrons rather than Logan
residents. The strategy is to work with the County to create a rec center that the entire
county can use, and the City contributed funding for the ongoing feasibility study. A
number of communities have expressed interest.
Over fifty years, the needs of the center have changed. 50 years ago, patrons did not need
to be separated from the students but here are laws that require patrons and students be
separated. An ending date has been negotiated, there is a memorandum of understanding
on how it will occur.
Richard Anderson, Finance Director remarked that the subject has been discussed multiple
times as of three years ago and as part of the budget hearings. The Logan School District
has also given presentations. This has been a long process, the school was requesting more
time, and there simply wasn’t enough time for citizens to fully utilize the facility. The
agreement has been in place for 50 years, but the agreement has not worked well in the last
15 years. Logan School District also requested that the facilities be taken over by them.
Councilmember Simmonds said that Mayor Daines will correspond wit Mr. King and Mrs.
King in further detail in this regard.

PUBLIC HEARING – Project Year 2025 Annual Action Plan (AAP) Initiation –
Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director (33:05)

Community Development Director Mike DeSimone addressed the Council regarding the
Project Year 2025 Annual Action Plan

He stated that this public hearing is to initiate the CDBG Annual Action Plan cycle for
Project Year 2025. This hearing does not require formal action by the Council.

The purpose of the public hearing is to help inform the CDBG Steering Committee when
they start meeting on determining what areas of the City should be emphasized, the types
of projects that should be accomplished this year, what services or programs may be
lacking, what particular groups of people may need services, and what specific planning
projects the City may consider.

The 2025 AAP will define the funding priorities for 2025. This will be the second year of
the 2024 - 2028 Consolidated Plan and he anticipates receiving approximately $560,000
in CDBG funding. Based on the Con Plan's priorities, the funding outcome should be
approximately 60% to provide a suitable living environment (50% to infrastructure and
10% to access and mobility), 20% to improve the capacity of public service providers
that serve LMI residents (10% to public facilities and 10% to public service
improvements), and 20% to invest in CDBG planning and administration (5% to planning
and 15% to admin).

Priority 1: Contribute to a Suitable Living Environment
Funding Goal is 60%
       50% towards neighborhood infrastructure in LMI areas
       10% towards the remediation of access/mobility barriers is all areas
Sidewalks, pedestrians crossings, ADA Corners, park improvements, public art,
community gardening or gathering areas, improved lighting and traffic calming measures.

Priority 2: Improve Capacity of Community Service Providers
Funding Goal is 20%
        10% towards facility improvements
        10% towards public service activities
Domestic abuse programs, volunteerism programs, integration of the immigrant
community, senior home care programs, job training initiatives, transitional housing
units, public transit services, affordable programming for kids and basic financial
management classes.
