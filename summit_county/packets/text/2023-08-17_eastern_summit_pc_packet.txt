To: Eastern Summit County Planning Commission
From: Jennifer Leslie, County Planner and Amir Caus, AICP, County Planner
Date of Meeting: August 17, 2023
Type of Item: Low Impact Permit – Recommendation to the Community Development 
Director
Process: Administrative Review
RECOMMENDATION: Staff recommends the Planning Commission review the proposed Low 
Impact Permit application for an accessory dwelling unit and forward a positive 
recommendation to the Community Development Director, pursuant to the findings of fact, 
conclusions of law, and conditions of approval as stated in this staff report. 
Project Description
Project Name: Payan Accessory Dwelling Unit
File Number: #22-234
Applicant(s): Hiram and Maygrei Payan
Property Owner(s): Hiram and Maygrei Payan (JT)
Location: 1920 S West Hoytsville Road, Wanship
Zone District: AG-10
Parcel Number and Size: NS-152-B (1 acre)
Final Land Use Authority: Community Development Director
Proposal
The applicants, Hiram and Maygrei Payan, are proposing an attached internal accessory 
dwelling unit within their two-story single-family home with an attached garage. The property 
is located at 1920 S West Hoytsville Road. The tax id number is NS-152-B. The proposal includes 
connecting existing second story bedrooms, located above the garage area, to the lower garage 
area with a new interior staircase. An upper hallway connection into the main dwelling would 
be walled off. No exterior modifications are required. The proposed accessory dwelling unit 
would be approximately 986 ft2.
See Exhibits A, B, and C for proposed floor plans, vicinity maps, and site photos.

Background
According to County records, the single-family house at 1920 S West Hoytsville Road was 
constructed in 1978. Access to the property is from West Hoytsville Road via a recorded 20 ft 
wide road easement from 1972 (Entry # 116549, Book 40 Page 114). The easement crosses a
portion of parcel NS-152-2-A, owned by Larry Vernon.
On December 3, 2015, building permit #15-954 was issued for an interior remodel of the home, 
allowing the attached garage to be converted to living space with a bathroom and canning 
kitchen. Garage doors were removed, and windows and an entry door were installed. See 
Exhibit D for building permit documents.
In the spring of 2016, building inspections were conducted by the Summit County Building 
Department. An inspection report indicates that some construction did not match the plans 
submitted for the building permit, including stairs that connect the garage area to the 
bedrooms above and a wall separating these bedrooms from the rest of the home. Those
modifications classify the garage area as an internal attached accessory dwelling unit (ADU), 
which triggered the need for a Low Impact Permit. A Low Impact Permit was not pursued while 
building permit #15-954 was valid.
On June 1, 2020, Larry Vernon, an adjacent property owner, filed complaints in the Third 
District Court against Hiram and Maygrei Payan. Some complaints were related to the
construction of the ADU. On March 17, 2022, a bench trial was held. See Exhibit E for findings 
of fact and conclusions of law from the trial. An evidentiary hearing was scheduled to further 
discuss the matter. 
On September 2, 2022, Hiram Payan submitted a new building permit (#22-1309) for the 
construction of the ADU within the primary residence. Review from Planning Staff indicated 
that a Low Impact Permit would need to be obtained before any building permit could be 
issued. On October 7, 2022, a Low Impact Permit application was submitted. 
Service providers were sent the application for review. Their only concerns would need to be 
addressed in the building permit phase, including an inspection and possible expansion of the 
septic system. A Restrictive Use Covenant was recorded (Exhibit G).
During a site visit on November 15, 2022, staff from the Health, Building, and Planning 
Departments noticed that interior construction of the project was complete. The Chief Building 
Official said that the property owners would be issued a notice of violation for inadequate 
inspections prior to drywalling and painting. The Official also noted that the stairs were not 
code compliant. The Health Department couldn’t review the septic system because the drain 
field and line to the tank had been buried. Additionally, the ADU had been occupied without 
receiving a Certificate of Occupancy. 
8
4
Due to public comment received for this Low Impact Permit, the Community Development 
Director elected to bring the item for a public hearing and review by the Eastern Summit 
County Planning Commission. Public comment included an email with an attached letter from 
Larry Vernon (Exhibit H). Mr. Vernon’s primary concerns related to the quality of the site plans
and the belief that the current zoning does not permit an additional dwelling unit.   
On January 5, 2023, the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission held a public hearing for 
the Low Impact Permit. During the hearing, only Larry Vernon expressed concerns.
Following Mr. Vernon’s comments, Commissioner Wilde closed the public hearing. While the 
Commission appeared ready for a decision, Commissioner Wheaton moved to table the 
recommendation until after the scheduled evidentiary hearing in the Third District Court, which 
revisits the civil dispute between Mr. Vernon and the Payans. Commissioner Clyde seconded 
the motion. All commissioners voted in favor. No date for the next commission meeting was 
scheduled. 
The evidentiary hearing took place on March 14, 2023 (Case No. 200500214). Of the sanctions 
and orders related to the ADU (Exhibit F), the Payans are to vacate the converted garage of all 
occupants until they obtain a Low Impact Permit.
Following the ruling, Mr. Vernon submitted additional comments (Exhibit I) to the Summit 
County legal team regarding the Low Impact Permit. The comments addressed potential 
pollution from the septic tank on the Payan property and concern for the loss of development 
rights on his 40-acre property behind his home, which is also accessed via the driveway shared 
by the Payans.
Since the evidentiary hearing, Maygrei Payan has reached out to the planning team to comply 
with the District Court’s orders by continuing to pursue this Low Impact Permit. All service 
provider comments have been re-sent to the applicant for clarity when pursuing a building 
permit, pending approval of this Low Impact Permit.
Analysis and Findings
The Planning Commission may recommend approval, approval with conditions, or denial of the 
low impact permit based upon written findings of fact according to each of the following
standards. It is the responsibility of the applicant to provide written and graphic evidence 
demonstrating compliance with the following Standards of Review: 
Standard 1: The use conforms to all applicable requirements of this Chapter and State and 
Federal regulations. COMPLIES.
Analysis: Appendix A of the Eastern Summit County Development Code states that an 
accessory dwelling unit (ADU) is “an area used by the owner of the primary residence or

primary tenant/business as a dwelling unit for the private use of the property owner's 
relatives, domestic help, caretakers, nursing staff, or similar users. An accessory dwelling 
unit shall contain cooking, sanitation and sleeping facilities.”
The proposed ADU contains 986 square feet of gross floor area, consisting of a 564 ft2 of 
ground level living area, kitchen, and bathroom and 423 ft2 of three upper-level
bedrooms accessed by an interior staircase. The ADU is contained within the building 
footprint and envelope of an existing primary dwelling. No exterior changes are 
proposed. Parking is sufficient for the existing house and the accessory dwelling unit is 
provided on the property with adequate circulation.
Standard 2: The use is consistent with the goals and policies of the General Plan. COMPLIES.
Analysis: The General Plan includes a goal to promote a mix of housing types and 
availability, and includes the following statement:
“Consider mechanisms to provide a realistic opportunity to meet estimated housing
needs within Eastern Summit County, including a variety of housing types and
affordability.”
ADUs are allowed in all zone districts, either within a primary dwelling, or in a detached 
structure on the same lot as a primary dwelling unit. This is one mechanism that creates
housing units at varied levels of affordability.

Findings of Fact:
1. Parcel NS-152-B, described as 1 acre, is a conforming parcel zoned Agriculture Grazing 
10 (AG-10) located at 1920 S West Hoytsville Rd. 
2. According to the current assessor’s parcel information, Hiram Payan is the owner of 
record for parcel NS-152-B. 
3. The property is currently developed with a single- family dwelling built in 1978, 
according to County records. The house consists of 2,481 square feet of living area and 
1,090 square feet of basement area. The records indicate that there is an attached 
garage consisting of 637 square feet (outside dimensions), and a detached tool shed 
consisting of 204 square feet.
4. There is a 20’ wide road access easement for the benefit of parcel NS-152-B over parcel
NS-152-2-A, recorded at Summit County as entry #116549, Book 40, Page 114. Larry 
Vernon is the owner of record for parcel NS-152-A, according to the current assessor’s 
parcel information.
5. On December 3, 2015, building permit #15-954 was issued to Hiram Payan as owner 
builder for an interior remodel, allowing 564 square feet of the garage area to be 
converted to finished living space, with a bathroom and a canning kitchen.
6. On March 23, 2016, a plumbing, electrical, and framing inspection of the property was 
conducted by the Summit County Building Department. A list of items to be completed 
was provided to the property owner. 
7. On April 19, 2016, the property was inspected for a 4-way and insulation. The inspection 
report indicated that all items on the previous inspection report needed to be 
completed, including an inspection of the insulation prior to drywall installation.
Additionally, the plans needed to be amended to show the additional work that was
under construction but not shown on the plans. This included framing information for 
stairs to access bedrooms above.
8. By connecting the bedrooms to the kitchen, bathroom and living area of the converted
garage, an accessory dwelling unit (ADU) was created.
9. The proposed ADU is 986 ft 2 as measured from the inside of the exterior walls. The
lower floor area consists of 564 ft2 and the upper floor area consists of 423 ft2. The 
upper floor area does not include the stairs, open areas to below, or attic areas, as 
allowed by the code.
10. Appendix A of the Eastern Summit County Development Code states that an ADU is “an 
area used by the owner of the primary residence or primary tenant/business as a 
dwelling unit for the private use of the property owner's relatives, domestic help, 
caretakers, nursing staff, or similar users. An accessory dwelling unit shall contain 
cooking, sanitation and sleeping facilities.”
12
8
11. Section 11-3-16 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code states that states that 
ADUs are low impact uses within the AG-10 zoning district.
12. ADUs are subject to the requirements of Section 11-6-5 (B) of the Eastern Summit
County Development Code.
13. Section 11-6-5 (B) of the Eastern Summit County Development Code stipulates that an 
ADU shall not exceed 1,000 square feet of gross square footage. Summit County code 
does not restrict ADU based on lot size or street frontage.
14. A Low Impact Permit for the ADU was not applied for during the time that building 
permit #15-954 was valid. 
15. On August 20, 2020, Maygrei Payan requested a copy of materials for building permit
#15-954. The information was sent to her by email, and she was advised that no 
certificate of occupancy had been issued, no final inspections had occurred.   
16. On September 2, 2022, Hiram Payan submitted a new building permit (#22-1309) for the 
construction of the ADU within the primary residence. Review from Planning Staff 
indicated that a Low Impact Permit would need to be obtained before any building 
permit could be issued. On October 7, 2022, a Low Impact Permit application was
submitted. 
17. The purpose of this Low Impact Permit application is to allow the property owner to 
apply for a new building permit to create a lawful ADU.
18. Low Impact Permits are subject to requirements of Section 11-4-8 of the Eastern 
Summit County Development Code, which establishes that the Community 
Development Director is the Land Use Authority for Low Impact Permits. 
19. Section 11-4-8 states that before a Low Impact Permit is approved, the Community 
Development Director must conclude that factual evidence exists to verify the following 
findings:
· The use conforms to all applicable requirements and State and Federal regulations.
· The use is consistent with the goals and policies of the General Plan.
· The use conforms to all requirements in chapter 2, "Development Evaluation 
Standards".
· The use is not detrimental to public health, safety, and welfare.
· The use is appropriately located with respect to public facilities and services.
· The natural topography, ridgelines, soils, critical areas, watercourses, and vegetation 
shall be preserved where possible through careful site planning and design of access 
routes, circulation areas, buildings and other structures, parking areas, utilities, 
drainage facilities and other features.
20. Findings of fact related to Section 11-4-8 are as follows: 
· The proposed ADU complies with all applicable requirements and regulations. 
· A Restrictive Use Covenant for the ADU was recorded on November 1, 2022.
· The proposed ADU will provide diversity in housing types and levels of affordability 
within Summit County.
· Service providers reviewed the proposal for compliance with applicable Building,
Fire, and Health code standards and their issues have been addressed with revisions 
to the plans and/or with conditions of approval.

existing house has parking for five or more cars on gravel parking areas on the 
property.
· Roads in the vicinity of the proposed use are sufficient to accommodate traffic to
and from the site without endangering motorists or requiring upgrades and/or 
improvements.
· The proposed use, as conditioned, will not intensify the likelihood of pedestrian and 
vehicular conflicts on the site.
· The proposed use will not intensify noise levels or odors on the site.
· The proposed use, as conditioned, will not create unmitigated dust or dirt conditions 
on the site.
· Adjacent property owners will not see a significant change in privacy with the 
proposed use.
21. A driveway is defined in the Eastern Summit County Development Code as “a means of 
access to one (1) but not more than five (5) one-family dwelling units. Without 
assurances that only five (5) dwellings will use a driveway, it shall otherwise be 
designated as a local road”.
22. Construction of the subject ADU will not create an additional one-family (single-family) 
dwelling unit. Therefore, construction of the ADU will not affect the number of one-
family dwelling units accessible via the driveway that runs through the Payan lot and the 
neighboring residence owned by Larry Vernon. If the 40-acre lot behind Mr. Vernon’s 
residence, which is also accessible via the same driveway, is subdivided and individually 
developed, the driveway could be improved to meet criteria to be designated as a local 
road. Local road designation would provide access to more dwelling units than 
permitted with a driveway designation. Therefore, no loss of development rights is
associated with the construction of the Payan ADU. 
23. Section 11-4-8(C)(2) of the Eastern Summit County Development Code allows the 
Community Development Director to determine whether a public hearing and 
discussion should be scheduled with the Planning Commission due to potential issues or 
community comments. In the case of this application, an adjacent property owner, Larry 
Vernon, provided input objecting to the Low Impact Permit for the ADU. Therefore, the 
Community Development Director determined that a public hearing with the Planning 
Commission was recommended.
24. On January 5, 2023, the Planning Commission meeting and public hearing was held. 
Notices of the public hearing were published in Summit County News and public notice 
websites on December 23, 2022. Courtesy postcards were sent to all property owners 
within 1,000 feet of the subject parcel. 
25. During the commission meeting, the public hearing was closed. According to Draft 
Minutes from the meeting, the Planning Commission decided to table the
recommendation to the Community Development Director until the Judge’s orders had 
been issued for an ongoing case in the Third District Court involving the applicant and
Larry Vernon. No date for the next commission meeting was scheduled.

26. The Third District Court evidentiary hearing took place on March 14, 2023 (Case No. 
200500214). The Judge ordered Hiram and Maygrei Payan to vacate the converted
garage until a Low Impact Permit is obtained for the ADU. 
27. There have not been any changes to the plans for the ADU since the public hearing with 
the previous Planning Commission meeting. Therefore, the Staff still seeks a 
recommendation from the Planning Commission to the Community Development
Director for the Low Impact Permit.

3. The application as conditioned is consistent with the Eastern Summit County General

4. The proposed use, as conditioned is not detrimental to public health, safety, and

5. The use is compatible with the existing neighborhood character and will not adversely

Conditions: 
1. All necessary permits, approvals, and fees must be obtained and paid prior to issuance 
of a building permit for the ADU, including but not limited to the Summit County Health, 
Summit County Engineering, and the Summit County Building Departments.
2. All requirements from service providers shall be met prior to issuance of a building 
permit for the ADU.
3. All construction shall comply with applicable regulations of the Eastern Summit County 
Development Code, as well as the Building, Fire and Summit County Health Codes in 
effect at the time of building permit issuance.
4. All unresolved inspection report items shall be resolved to the satisfaction of the Chief 
Building Official prior to issuance of a certificate of occupancy for the ADU.
5. Future modifications of the ADU require an amended low impact permit unless prior 
written approval from the Summit County Community Development Department is 
obtained. 
6. Current Development Code allows no more than one accessory dwelling unit on this lot.
7. Consistent with the State Code definition of an internal accessory dwelling unit, the use 
of this ADU is limited to a long-term rental of 30 consecutive days or longer.
8. A Restrictive Use Covenant shall be signed and recorded with the Summit County 
Recorder prior to issuance of a building permit for the ADU and shall include reference 
to these conditions of approval.

Type of Item:  Code Amendment – Public Hearing, Possible Action 
Process:  Legislative 
 
 
RECOMMENDATION:  Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the proposed 
language to amend section 11-4-5: Subdivisions in the Eastern Summit County Development 
Code, conduct a public hearing and forward a positive recommendation to the County Council 
per the findings of fact and conclusions of law in this staff report.  
 
Proposal 
 
Staff is proposing to amend Section 11-4-5 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code to 
provide an alternate method for a property owner to obtain lien holder consent when 
amending a subdivision plat, which is currently required via a signature on a mylar or certificate 
of consent prior to recordation.  
 
Background 
 
Over the past few years, there have been plats that have been reviewed and approved by the 
County but have not been recorded at the Recorder’s Office because the property owner was 
not able to obtain lien holder consent.  
 
This amendment creates a process where if a property owner has demonstrated that 
reasonable efforts were made to obtain the signature, the plat can be recorded. The process 
would require:  
 
1. Applicant mails a certificate of consent to lien holder (the certificate must be in an 
approved format from the County).  
71
Code Amendments     
2. If the lien holder does not respond within 30 days, then the applicant may provide the 
County with a list of names and addresses of lien holders who didn’t respond and,  
3. Request that the County:  
a. Mail a certificate via certified mail to the signers and publish notice of the 
amendment in the newspaper.  
4. If the County receives no response, consent would be considered given by the lien 
holders and the applicant could move forward and have the mylar recorded.  
 
Analysis 
 
Section 11-5-3 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code establishes a process for 
amendments to the text of the Code, it states that whenever an amendment to the Code is 
initiated, it must be reviewed by the Planning Commission who will deliver a recommendation 
to the County Council. The County Council, after holding a public hearing, can approve, approve 
with modifications, or deny the amendment. There is no criterion. 
 
The Eastern Summit County General Plan has specific goals related to future amendments.  
 
Goal 2.1. g States: 
 
“Enact ordinances, resolutions, codes and other forms of land use controls to reduce 
nuisances and land use incompatibilities.” 
 
Goal 2.1.i States:  
 
“Create appropriate and predictable development procedures in the Development Code 
to ensure that all land use and development is adequately reviewed and determined to 
be consistent with the goals of this Plan before any approvals are granted.” 
 
This process will not circumvent approvals from the Land Use Authority. It creates a process 
where a property owner can record the approved plat in cases where lien holder consent is not 
obtainable.  
 
Recommendation 
 
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the proposed language to amend 
section 11-4-5: Subdivisions in the Eastern Summit County Development Code, conduct a public 
hearing and forward a positive recommendation to the County Council per the findings of fact 
and conclusions of law in this staff report.
