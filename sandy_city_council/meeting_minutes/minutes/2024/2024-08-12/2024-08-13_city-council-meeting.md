4. 24-280 Second Reading: Council Member Dekeyzer recommending that the City
participate in the Community Renewable Energy Program

Sponsors: Dekeyzer
Attachments: Dekeyzer Memorandum Community Renewable Energy Program

Final
Exhibit A May 21, 2024 Presentation
Exhibit B_ Interlocal Cooperation Agreement
Exhibit C Executed Prospective Party Application
Exhibit D Utility Agreement URC
Exhibit E Draft Resolution 24-36C
CREP Resolution 24-05 (signed)
Community Renewable Energy Act
Second Reading: Council Member Aaron Dekeyzer sponsored this item and spoke to the
Council about the Utah Renewable Communities. In this second reading. Council Member
Dekeyzer asked the Council to formally consider moving forward with participation in the
program, prior to the August 31, 2024 deadline.
The Utah Renewable Communities is a coalition of cities, counties, and other
municipalities that are pioneering renewable energy solutions in Utah. In April 2019, the
Utah Legislature passed the Community Renewable Energy Act. Currently, there are
eighteen communities participating in the program. In 2024 the Utah Legislature opted to
re-open the program allowing additional municipalities to join the coalition. The intention
of the program is to launch a default net-100% renewable electricity option for customers
of Rocky Mountain Power in the communities that have opted to participate. Mr.
Dekeyzer reviewed the action timeline for the application and participating in the program.
He requested Council feedback and consideration of his proposal and adoption of
Resolution 24-36C.
A lengthy Council discussion, comments, and questions followed. Discussion included
the costs of the program and the financial impact to residents, the program goals, the
projected time frame for the program to be fully operational, the relatively short timeline
for residents to ‘opt out" of the program, and the small number of participating
communities. There are only 18 communities participating in the program out of a total of
283 communities state-wide. Shane Pace, CAO, spoke on behalf of the Mayor and
expressed the Mayor's support of the program.
Public comment opened:
Mr. David Ryser is a retired physician and expressed support for the program. He hopes
Sandy City will participate in the program.
Ms. Julie Morgan expressed support of the program. Utah does not have the choice to
ignore air quality. We need to begin the process of change.
Mr. David Follawd is a retired physician. Air pollution is a major trigger of asthma. Every
action matters. Every year matters. Every choice matters. He spoke in favor of the
proposal.

Mr. Andrew Watt spoke about his love of the outdoors and doing his part to make a
difference. He shared that he had asthma and thinks this program will help improve the air
quality in Utah. He express support of the proposal. We have to start somewhere.
Mr. Marc Peterson retired from General Electric. He spoke about his experience with
renewables and spoke about costs associated with coal plants. He asked the Council to
look at this proposal from a cost perspective.
Ms. Pat Jones applauded Council Member Sharkey for her prior comments. Fees always
go up and she spoke about the financial impact to residents on a fixed income as a result
of rising fees. She asked the Council to wait and see how the program proceeds. She is
not supportive of the program.
Mr. Mike Shackelford expressed support for Sandy to participate in this program. He has
solar panels at his home and just recently purchased an EV car. Residents want this
program and these programs are needed. He felt this was a good program and he asked
the Council to match the financial commitment made by Cottonwood Heights.
Mr. Steve Wise has been a resident for 33 years. He has solar panels. He thinks it is an
overreach for cities to ask their citizens to give money to Rocky Mountain Power to go
green. Residents should not be made to pay a company to go green.
Ms. Dawn Bates said she believed in clean air and the freedom to choose. Please make
it a choice.
Mr. Dave Egelund is against this proposal. There is too much unknown. He was not
willing to join because others are joining the program. There are too many questions and
unknowns at this time.
Ms. Sara Kenney lives in Lehi. She is a member of Sierra Club and expressed support for
the program. It makes sense and will impact people all over the state.
Mr. Craig Ulrich appreciated both the pros and cons. He is in favor of renewable energy
but this program is not the path to get there. There are too many unknowns and too many
paths to failure. We live in a natural bowl and Rocky Mountain Power cannot guarantee
that we will end up with clean air. He was not supportive of this resolution.
Public comment closed.
A lengthy Council discussion continued. Many Council Members expressed appreciation
to Council Member Dekeyzer for bringing forth this proposal but also expressed their
concerns about the city's participation in the program. A motion was made to adopt
Resolution 24-36. Motion on the discussion followed. Council spoke about how little had
been accomplished in the last five years since this program had been implemented and
waiting until the program had a more defined outlook. Lynn Pace, City Attorney, spoke
about the program funds and the legal requirement involved with this program and
expressed confidence that the law will be followed and the funds would be used as
required by law. Many on the Council felt that not enough progress had been made in the
past five years and that there was no track record. Many were not in favor of participation
in the program. Council Member Robinson spoke in favor of the program and thought it
was a good step moving forward but could appreciate both sides of the issue and the
robust conversation this evening.
Following the vote on the motion, Council moved to General Citizen Comment.

Second Reading: Council Member Dekeyzer presented Resolution 24-35C, a resolution
adopting a council policy regarding committee assignments. The goal of the proposed
policy is to address the timing of certain appointments and create a new procedure in the
event of any stalemates involving council committee assignments. He reviewed the
proposed changes with the Council. A lengthy Council discussion followed. Council
members expressed concerns with the varying timeline for service on certain committees
and using a coin toss to end a stalemate. There was discussion that this felt like a
personal grievance and that the proposal was unnecessarily complicated. Other Council
Members expressed support for the proposal.
Public comment opened.
Public comment closed.
A motion was made by Ryan Mecham seconded by Aaron Dekeyzer to adopt Resolution
24-35C. Council discussion on the motion continued. Some council members were not
supportive of changing the timing of service for the LPC committee. Council Member
Stroud offered a friendly amendment to keep all the committee assignments service from
January to December. Ryan Mecham accepted the friendly anendment. Aaron Dekeyzer
did not accept the friendly amendment. The motion with the friendly amendment failed to
get a second so the motion failed. A new motion was made to table the item.
A motion was made by Ryan Mecham, seconded by Aaron Dekeyzer to table the
