2021. Meeting was conducted by Chair Graf and began at 6:09pm.

1.   Discussion, Petition to remove the restriction of prohibiting painting brick in the East Central
     Community.
                                                                                                         1
     Mr. Montgomery reminded the Commission they had entertained a petition to remove the
     restriction for painting brick in the East Central Community during their regular meeting in July
     and had tabled action and asked it be scheduled for a work session. At that time the petitioner
     had claimed it is impossible to remove the; paint without damaging the brick.

     He reviewed the history of the East Bench Historic District, indicating it had been created in an
     effort to preserve the character of existing homes in the area. At the same time, consideration
     had been given to create a local district, where each home in the area would be required to
     obtain a certificate of historic appropriateness for any work done to the exterior of the
     buildings, it was determined the National designation is more appropriate as it provides
     opportunity for tax credits for rehabilitation of buildings.

     In determining how to act on the petition, a hierarchy of consideration may be appropriate, with
     the first being whether the historic character of the East Central Community should be
     preserved, and whether painting brick affects the integrity of that historic district. Is there a
     place for historic preservation of the East Central Community and does preserving the brick
     protect the character of the National Historic District. He cited the National Register
     Nomination document, indicating brick was an identifying feature of the area, and there has
     been a history of local brick yards in the community. He stated there is documentation that
     50-60% of the East Central community is brick, and it is a unique feature of the area. The brick
     and mortar appearance of buildings tells a story of the community. While some homes had
     been painted prior to the Ordinance being adopted in 2009, the petition seeks to allow brick to
     continue to be painted. The Commission should determine whether brick and the dimension
it creates with mortar an important historic feature that should be retained. He stated once a
brick building is painted it does not read as original and often become an upkeep problem for
future homeowners. He stated while some brick was not meant to be exterior brick but was
used to connect buildings and may warrant being painted, most brick on single-family homes
was adequately treated to withstand weather and meant to be surface material.

He stated at the time the National Historic District was created, consideration had been given
for the area to also be on the local register, and while there are many existing individual
buildings on the local register, it was felt it would be a burden to both Staff and residents of the
area, and it was felt zoning regulations could be adopted as a tool to protect buildings in the
area.

Chair Graf stated while he feels it is important to retain brick, there is concern that homeowners
are not being aware of the restriction and suggested there may be a petition method whereby
an exception to the ordinance might be considered in order to seal brick that has begun to
deteriorate. He noted the material Staff had provided indicates some communities have
installed a badge into the sidewalk or front door frame to identify the area which would also
inform existing and future owners of the design restrictions of the community. He also
suggested a separate website might be created about the East Central Community to inform
owners of the regulations as well as benefits of the area.

Mr. Montgomery stated the intent of the National Register nomination was to give incentive to
property owners by making historic tax credits available which would then preserve the
                                                                                                       2
integrity of the community.

Commissioner Southwick felt there is merit to preserving the appearance of existing brick
exteriors on homes, indicating it does not increase the sales price of home and is often done
when a quick turnover of a home is desired. Commissioner Sandau noted while some
owners may be aware of restrictions, there should be a better way to educate new owners.

Mr. Montgomery suggested the City might work with the real estate community so they are
educated and can inform potential owners of the zoning regulations of the area. Owners
often assume it is allowed as there are existing painted homes in the area. Other methods of
educating the public may be to place a note on the title, place notices in the City water bill of
utilizing social media, or wayfinding signs placed in the area identifying the historic area, or
medallions on the sidewalk. Any of these could come with a QR code which would give more
detail about regulations of the area. Commission felt a multi-prong approach towards
education is warranted so that the majority of the community members would be educated.
Commissioner Southwick noted he could work with the local association of realtors but felt
the most effective means would be to record a note on the deed so it would be available for
title work. A map could also be placed on Realtor.com which would identify the area. It also
was suggested a catalog be created to identify homes which had been painted before the
regulations were adopted, and are legally grandfathered.
Commission then discussed penalties for zoning violations, stating while some property
owners have removed paint which was in violation, others prefer paying citations as the
removal of paint is so costly.

Commissioner Schade stated there is value in retaining the historic character of this
community, stating paint does not improve the appearance of homes, but expressed concern
as to how it can be stopped. Commissioner Safsten asked if colors are regulated and
suggested some particular colors might be identified which fit with the area. He expressed
concern about existing grandfathered buildings which might be repainted to a hue that better
fits the community. Commissioner Sandau noted this is not a wealthy community, and
expressed concern that the cost of removing paint is prohibitive to many new homeowners.
She asked if a funding source might be made available to assist with the removal of paint.
Chair Graf felt there are many homes in this community and while many have the attitude it is
deteriorating due to its age, others have invested as long-term residents. Mr. Montgomery
responded Ogden City has created redevelopment districts in this community with the intent
to stabilize neighborhoods and create home ownership. The City has purchased buildings,
removed units and restored single-family homes in the area. Because of these investments,
the value of the community has improved. On the flip side are investors trying to make a
quick sale and improve curb appeal by painting the brick, which then becomes a burden to
the new homeowner as the brick bubbles and peals over time and further maintenance is
required than if the original brick were retained. Commissioner Southwick noted he has seen
some improvement in most neighborhoods as owners invest in upkeep of their homes.
                                                                                                  3
Mr. Montgomery indicated the State has a list of contractors recommended for masonry and
paint removal. Staff has contacted Abstract Masonry and found they can remove paint from
any brick without damaging the brick. Their process includes an analysis to determine the
most appropriate method of removal, and then a test patch is done with three different
methods to determine which is the most effective without damaging the brick. In addition,
the mortar is often damaged by the paint, and often repointing of the mortar is required in
addition to the removal of the paint. While the City is not aware of the cost others have paid
to remove paint, there are methods available which would not damage the underlying brick.
When notice of the zoning violation is given, the owner begs for the regulation to not be
enforced due to the cost of paint removal. The consideration of this petition is to determine
whether this area is important enough to preserve that the violation remains and the
buildings are restored to their original appearance. Mr. Montgomery also asked if the
Commission felt the regulation should be expanded to include both the NC and CBD sections
of the community. Commission felt these buildings also are important to the overall
community and should be included in the regulation.

Discussion continued relating to action on the petition, timing, and consideration of education
methods as well as whether criteria could be established to allow exemptions or exceptions.
It was suggested consideration be given to identify existing brick homes and their condition
as well as identifying the area with wayfinding signs, medallions in the sidewalk or a notice
recorded on the title of each of these properties to protect the existing brick. Commission
directed Staff to consider their workload for the September meeting when determining
   whether this item should be on that agenda or whether additional research is warranted. It
   was felt benefits of being on the National Register should be better publicized and continued
   efforts made to improve the East Central community. It was felt the value of living in the area
   can be improved.

   Mr. Montgomery summarized the discussion stating the Commission has indicated there is
   value in retaining the regulation for the East Central Community to not paint or cover existing
   brick structures but that better education needs to occur. Staff will do additional research on
   whether there might be unique situations where brick can be painted and create exemption
   criteria as well as creating a database to identify existing brick structures within the
   community, independent of the zoning designation. The potential is that action on the
   petition would then trigger other amendments and improved education efforts.

There being no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at
7:25pm.
