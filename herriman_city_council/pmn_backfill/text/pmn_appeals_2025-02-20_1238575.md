                         APPEAL AUTHORITY
                       PUBLIC MEETING AGENDA
                               Thursday, February 20, 2025

The following are the minutes of the Herriman Planning Commission meeting held on Thursday,
February 20, 2025, at 2:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Courtroom, 5355 West Herriman Main Street,
Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the City Hall, on
the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Commission, media, and interested citizens.


Presiding: Appeals Authority David Church


Attendance: Applicant Representative John Brunt, Planning Director Michael Maloy, Deputy City
Recorder Angela Hansen

Note: The Appeal Authority Officer did not make a decision on the following matter during the public
meeting; however, a written decision on the request will be published at a later date.

2:00 PM:

1.   Call to Order

Appeal Authority David Church called the meeting to order at 2:07 p.m.

He explained this was a variance hearing on the request from Grieg and Tamarin Youlton for a
property located at 12128 South Tower Arch Lane. Mr. Church noted that the applicants were not
present but were represented by their architect, John Brunt. The city was represented by Michael
Maloy, City Planning Director.

Mr. Church outlined the procedure for the hearing, emphasizing that the burden of proof for the
variance was on the applicants. He explained that he would allow the applicant to present first,
followed by a response from the city, and then the applicant could reply to that response.

Mr. Church stated that he had reviewed the original application, the original city staff report, the
amended application from the applicants, the slightly changed city staff report, and a letter from the
homeowners addressed to Herriman City Planning. He asked if there were any other documents that
should be part of the record, to which both parties responded that there were none at that time.

2. Review and consider a variance request (City File No. V2024-139) submitted by John Brunt, AIA
   (authorized agent, project architect) on behalf of Grieg and Tamarin Youlton, property owners,
   to reduce the minimum rear yard (i.e., building setback) from 15′-0″ to 5′-0″ to construct an
   attached covered deck within the rear yard of a single-family dwelling located at 12128 S Tower
   Arch Lane in the R-2-10 Multi Family Residential Zone of Herriman City.

Mr. Brunt presented the case for the variance request. He explained that the property had a unique
topographical situation due to an upslope, which required the developer to put a retaining wall along
the back of the property. This retaining wall used up a significant portion of the level area in the
backyard, making it difficult to install a detached covered porch large enough for family gatherings.

Mr. Brunt noted that the footprint of the house was deeper than other houses on that side of the
fence, resulting in the shortest rear yard from the main house to the property line among the houses
in the area. He emphasized that the retaining wall significantly impacted the use of the rear yard.

Mr. Brunt also mentioned that Tamarin's father, who lives with the family, has mobility problems
and uses a walker. The proposed attached covered patio would provide easier access for him
compared to a detached structure.

Mr. Church asked for clarification on whether the argument was about the size of the attached
structure or the inability to build one at all within the setbacks. Mr. Brunt explained that they could
build an attached shaded structure within the setbacks, but it would be only 10 feet deep, which they
felt was too narrow to be functional. The proposed structure would be about 18 feet deep.

Mr. Maloy presented the city's position. He pointed out the location of the retaining wall on the site
plan and cross-section provided in the packet. He explained that the retaining wall extends beyond
the subject property to the neighbors' properties on both sides and was built to create a more
usable backyard with a flatter surface.

Mr. Maloy emphasized that the city's variance standards are verbatim from state code and that all
five approval standards must be met for a variance to be granted. He stated that the city's position
was that the applicant did not meet any of the five standards.

Mr. Maloy also noted that there is no minimum square footage for an accessory structure
guaranteed as a property right in the Herriman City Code. He explained that while the applicant's
design reflects the dimensional space required for their desired outdoor use, there is nothing in the
ordinance that guarantees a minimum size accessory structure.

Mr. Brunt suggested that if they were allowed a 10-foot setback instead of a 15-foot setback, they
could probably make a usable deck area. He expressed willingness to go back to the owner and work
on a revised proposal if it tips the scale in favor of approval.

Mr. Church focused the discussion on the first three criteria for granting a variance, particularly
whether literal enforcement creates an unreasonable hardship, whether there are special
circumstances attached to the property, and whether granting the variance is essential to the
enjoyment of a substantial property right.

Mr. Church expressed concern about whether the hardship was self-imposed, given that the
homeowners chose the lot and house design. He also questioned whether having a deck like the
neighbors constitutes a substantial property right.

Mr. Church decided to leave the hearing open for two weeks to allow both parties to submit
additional written arguments. He requested that both sides copy each other on any submissions and
ensure that he receives a copy. Mr. Church emphasized that he was particularly interested in
arguments addressing the first three criteria for granting a variance.

3. Adjournment
Appeals Authority David Church adjourned the hearing at 3:01 p.m. with the deadline for additional
submissions by March 6, 2025.
