Public Hearing – 6:30 (Or as soon thereafter as possible)
To receive public comment regarding the annual budget of the Redevelopment Agency for fiscal year
July 1, 2026, to June 30, 2027.

Community and Economic Development Director, Jonathan Weidenhamer, presented the
Redevelopment Agency’s budget and recommended that the Board schedule an introductory workshop
later in the summer to provide a comprehensive, foundational overview of how the Redevelopment
Agency functions and utilizes its various economic development tools. He noted that the City typically
collaborates with external consultants, such as Rob Sant, to assist with these educational sessions and
strategic tools.

Mr. Weidenhamer explained that South Salt Lake is unique compared to some larger municipalities
because it blends its Redevelopment Agency efforts directly with traditional economic development
activities. This comprehensive approach targets large project areas, such as the downtown Housing
Transit Reinvestment Zone (HTRZ), while simultaneously funding ongoing business outreach,
communication plans, and community partnerships handled by Senior Program Manager, Jenny Diersen.

Mr. Weidenhamer acknowledged that tracking the funding can be unintuitive due to necessary
accounting transfers, loans between funds, and debt service allocations, and he expressed gratitude to
Interim Finance Director, Joy Manwiller, for her assistance in preparing the financial details.

Mr. Weidenhamer emphasized a general tone of fiscal conservatism for the upcoming fiscal year,
explaining that the Redevelopment Agency had successfully tightened its belt to reduce its reliance on
the City general fund dollars to zero. By relying exclusively on Redevelopment Agency funds for its
operations, the agency aimed to remain mindful of unpredictable national and state economic
conditions. He stated that economic uncertainty often slows down traditional redevelopment projects,
making it crucial for the City to fulfill its existing downtown commitments and maintain the momentum
generated by the Housing Transit Reinvestment Zone bond funds.

Detailing specific funds, Mr. Weidenhamer highlighted that Fund 71, which governs Operations,
included a $250,000 reduction in professional services. This reduction would not decrease the agency's
level of service because a two percent administration allowance from the Housing Transit Reinvestment
Zone bonds would offset the costs. Fund 71 also featured a $135,000 allocation for capital expenditures
to preserve two existing agency facilities. Specifically, approximately $40,000 was designated for
xeriscaping and general maintenance of the Square Kitchen roof, asphalt, and HVAC systems.
Additionally, funds were allocated to clean up falling trees at the Millcreek Pond facility to mitigate
property damage risks before potentially transferring the property to a conservation easement holder.

For Fund 72, which covers Debt Service, Mr. Weidenhamer explained that revenue from the Market
Station project area would be pledged to cover obligations previously paid by the general fund. This
shifting of revenue provides a two-year window of financial flexibility before those funds must be

redirected toward Housing Transit Reinvestment Zone debt service.

Regarding Fund 73, the Housing Fund, Mr. Weidenhamer recalled that the agency received one million
dollars from the general fund in fiscal year 2026, which was primarily used to match a $500,000 county
grant for community development programs. Because the agency was carrying forward approximately
$600,000 into fiscal year 2027, staff did not request any additional general fund allocations from the
City. He reported that the City had successfully acquired five houses and was in the process of securing a
sixth property. Within the next few months, staff plans to introduce a community land trust framework
to partner with organizations like Habitat for Humanity. This mechanism allows the City to retain land
ownership through long-term leases while selling the residential structures, thereby ensuring
permanent housing affordability while allowing buyers to build equity in the buildings.

Mr. Weidenhamer stated that Fund 74, representing the old streetcar project associated with the
Zellerbach development, had exhausted its financial obligations and would be closed out. Consequently,
the property tax increments previously captured by this fund will return to the standard taxing entities,
including the school district, the county, and the City.

Fund 76, which tracks the Market Station, will continue to pledge its revenues to debt service until its
scheduled expiration in 2038.

Finally, Mr. Weidenhamer discussed Fund 78, noting that Ms. Manwiller helped reconcile the $20.4
million in bond funds secured in December. These funds are actively backing existing developments such
as Bowers, One Burton, and Blazer. While participation agreements are in place for the aQui 2194 and
Utopia developments, the exact timing of the fund disbursements across the current or upcoming fiscal
years remains to be finalized. Upcoming initiatives under this fund include the Belhaven development on
3rd West and the Haven project on the corner of Southwest Temple and Haven Avenue, which is a
planned 300-unit residential complex that recently submitted concept designs to the fire department.
Construction on the Haven project is projected to begin late next summer.

Mr. Weidenhamer concluded by requesting that the Board hold the Public Hearing, take feedback, and
continue the item to the June 17th meeting for final action.

Director Thomas inquired about the City contract with the Chamber of Commerce, asking what specific
benefits the City receives in return for the $70,000 contractual expenditure.

Mr. Weidenhamer replied that the contract is divided into several portions, primarily covering a piece of
the salary for the Chamber president and Chief Executive Officer, as well as allocating $25,000 toward
the Utah Microloan Fund to support local businesses within South Salt Lake. He noted he could provide
a more detailed breakdown via email to the Board.
