Mr. Jason Binks stated that Charles Hammon, representing Copper Flats LC, requests a
conditional use permit and preliminary site plan for a 37-unit multifamily residential project at
8018 S Main Street in the Multifamily - Medium to High Density (RM-25) zone. The single lot
is roughly 1.5 acres, mid-block, with 179 feet of frontage on the west side of Main Street. The
subject site has 2 existing single-family homes on a lot that was consolidated in early 2020. The
lot is adjacent to 3 other zones: Multifamily (RM-25) to the north and west, Jordan Bluffs (JB) to
the south, and Single Family (SF-2) to the east. All the surrounding uses are residential with
single-family homes to the north and east and multifamily structures to the south and west.
The proposal is a single 3-story structure with 37 units (29,203 SF total) consisting of studios
and 1- and 2-bedroom units. There is a single drive aisle from Main St into a parking lot along
the entire south property line with 68 parking stalls, about half with carports. There is a
pedestrian network that connects the structure with the street, parking lot, and several amenity
areas. The proposed plans meet lot standards, setback, height, and build-to requirements.

CITY STAFF (APPLICANT); JANA WARD, PLANNER I (STAFF).
Ms. Jana Ward stated that LuAron Foster is proposing a text amendment to update the Regional
Commercial zone to modify the impervious surface requirement in the front yard. The ordinance
currently limits impervious material coverage to 15% of the front yard! and Ms. Foster’s request
proposes to limit the impervious requirement to the front yard setback only. As the RM-12, RM-
25, 7200 S Overlay, and CI zones contain similar language regarding impervious surface
coverage, Staff recommends extending this request to include those zones as well. Additionally,
while working with the applicant on this proposed amendment, Staff identified a couple tables in
the RM-12 and RM-25 zones where building height had not been updated in an earlier text
amendment and is proposing to address that oversight in this request.
The Regional Commercial (RC) and Clean Industrial (CI) zones both include the following
provision:
Impervious Surface. Impervious surfaces shall cover no more than fifieen percent of the
front yard. ($$17-7-12.3(B) and 17-7-13.3(B))
Both zones are intended to allow for a wide variety of uses, utilizing landscape buffers and other
requirements to mitigate potential impacts on neighboring properties. Both zones have suffered a
loss of development opportunities and variance requests due to this requirement. Past ordinances
in the RC zone once allowed parking lots and drive-throughs in the front yard. Based on current
code, however, parking lots and drive throughs are not allowed in the front yard. This has
resulted in numerous lots that have access management and dimensional issues that make it
difficult to develop regionally significant commercial projects that increasingly rely on drive 1
throughs and visible parking lots. Narrow lots have also suffered as the simple installation of a
driveway can result in exceeding the impervious surface requirement. The proposed text
amendment would change the language in each zone to limit impervious surfaces in the front
yard setback, instead of the front yard, thereby resolving these issues while maintaining the
overall objectives of the zones.
The Multifamily Residential — Medium Density Zone (RM-12), Multifamily Residential —
Medium to High Density Zone (RM-25) and 7200 S Overlay Zone (7200 S) contain similar
language limiting impervious surface in the rear yard’:
RM-12: Impervious Surfaces. For new multifamily development, up to forty percent of the
rear yard may be an impervious surface. (§17-7-3.4(A)(3)(c))
RM-25: Impervious Surfaces. For new multifamily development, a maximum of forty
percent of the rear yard may be an impervious surface. (§17-7-4.4(A)(3)(d))

Ms. Ward stated that as part of the preliminary site plan review for a new general office
development project located at 6820 S 900 E, Ms. Bishop is requesting a reduction in the
residential landscape buffer along the western property line. The property is zoned Regional
Commercial (RC), currently undeveloped, and is approximately 2 acres in size.
General office uses are permitted in the RC zone and the site plan application will be reviewed
by Planning Staff. However, the applicant has requested the Planning Commission allow a
reduction to the Residential Landscape Buffer required by Midvale City Municipal Code Section
17-7-12.6(A)(8) between residential and commercial uses.
Section 17-7-12.6(A)(8) of the zoning ordinance requires new development to provide a
landscaped buffer to separate residential uses from non-residential uses. The standard
requirement for this buffer is 30° and is intended to provide adequate screening, buffering, and
separation of these uses. When utilized, the buffer should use a combination of distance and low-
| level screening to separate the uses to soften the visual impact of the non-residential use. In
circumstances where a 30’ buffer is not practical, the ordinance allows the Planning Commission

Bylaws Staff presented a draft of proposed changes to the bylaws in the October 28, 2020 and
November 18, 2020 meetings. These changes are intended to clarify points of confusion within
the existing bylaws, strengthen the ethics and training requirements, adopt new policies for the
Planning Commission, and make technical corrections. Proposed changes include:

e Updating references to State and City Code;

e Removing language duplicated from the City’s Municipal Code;

communication, media requests, and public impartiality;

e Setting an earlier meeting time (6:00 p.m. instead of 7:00 p.m.);

e A new policy allowing Commissioners to place items on meeting agendas;

e Clarifications to the abstention policy;

e Clarifications regarding electronic meetings; and

vote on amendments.
