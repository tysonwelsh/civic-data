68   3.2) Public hearing and recommendation of Brad Tronson's request for review of the
69   Tronson General Plan Amendment on 0.66 acres located at 635 West State Street, changing
70   the designation from Commercial to Heavy Commercial.
71   Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item. General Plan Amendment will be
72   consistent of properties. DRC has one comment, which does not recommend commercial
73   designation on this property.
74
75   Commissioner Eyre asked when General Plan Amendments can be submitted. Commissioner
76   Kunze asked if this was already being used as commercial, which it is.
77
78   The applicant, Brad Tronson, was present. There is no light or heavy commercial designation. He
79   would like to build a building for commercial use with interior office warehouse. He would like
80   to build something like Dennis Schoonover's establishment.
81
82   Commission Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.
83
84   Teresa Gonzalez, who is adjacent to the property, expressed her opposition to the GPA zone
85   change. She stated that it went against the adopted general plan, was adjacent to residential areas,
86   and would impact planned residential developments. She mentioned that the barrier and buffer
87   zone should have been addressed years ago but had not yet been resolved, despite previous
88   commitments. Ms. Gonzalez requested that the property remain designated as light industrial and
89   based on the recommendations of the planning committee and the general plan goals and
     Lehi City Planning Commission                    2                                September 12, 2024
 90   policies, urged that the item not be approved for a designation change. While she acknowledged
 91   the potential for noise, she did not feel the area was appropriate for the new designation.
 92
 93   Katie read one online comment which was also left by Teresa Gonzalez.
 94
 95   Commission Chair Jackson closed the public hearing.
 96
 97   Brad Tronson mentioned that two nearby residents had spoken to him, and neither had an issue
 98   with the construction of a building and the enclosure of materials. However, he noted that the
 99   building they plan to construct does not meet the light industrial designation. He also stated that
100   there will be multiple tenants.
101
102   Commissioner Jones asked Katie to clarify the definitions of heavy commercial, commercial, and
103   light commercial use. Commissioner Jones expressed concerns that the proposed development
104   might not meet the criteria for conforming with the neighboring areas.
105
106   The Commissioners inquired whether the property currently meets the buffering requirements.
107   Katie Bussell noted that if the requirements were approved, they would be reviewed during the
108   site plan process. The commissioners questioned why the property had previously been approved
109   as an island. Kim Struthers explained that it originally matched the surrounding properties.
110   While the site plan had been approved for a previous applicant, the property was never
111   developed as planned.
112
113   Commissioner Eyre expressed concern that approving this could set a precedent for further
114   exceptions, especially since it does not comply with the current code.
115
116   Commissioner Jackson agreed with Commissioners Eyre and Jones. He sympathized with the
117   applicant, noting that the property is a prime piece of real estate.
118
119                   Motion: Commissioner Jones moved to recommend denial of Brad Tronson’s
120                   request for review of the Tronson General Plan Amendment on 0.66 acres
121                   located at 635 West State Street, changing the designation from Commercial
122                   to Heavy Commercial; including all DRC comments, noting the development
123                   did not meet the findings listed in the report. Commissioner Everett seconded the
124                   motion.

130   3.3) Consideration of Texas Instruments request for approval of the LFAB 2 Site Plan
131   including exceptions to the design standards located at 4000 North Flash Drive.
132   Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item, noting that the DRC had made four
133   redline comments. Kim Struthers stated that if the exceptions were not approved, the applicant
134   would need to revise their site plan for future approval.
135
136   Commissioner Everett inquired about the traffic calming survey and whether it was
137   discretionary, based on a recommendation from the City Traffic Engineer.
      Lehi City Planning Commission                    3                                 September 12, 2024
138
139   Commissioner Kunze asked if the proposal was intended to match the existing building. She
140   commented that it seemed like a lot of exceptions were being requested but noted that the
141   proposal matched what was already in place. Kim agreed with her assessment.
142
143   Commissioner Eyre asked about the landscaping and whether an appropriate amount was
144   planned for planting. Kim confirmed that the plan still met the code requirements and that it
145   included native trees and bushes.
146
147   Commissioner Everett asked about the reduction in bike parking, indicating that it did not seem
148   like an unreasonable request. TI had provided numbers for current pedestrians who were riding
149   bikes and using the parking. Everett also noted that the utility screening appeared to include the
150   planting of trees and a berm and asked if this required an exception. Kim stated that the situation
151   was unusual but that it still met the level of development required.
152
153   The applicant, Barry Mccoin from Page Supplement Architectural firm, acknowledged that there
154   were numerous exceptions but stated that they had reviewed the green space and buffering. He
155   noted that they had tried to address all concerns within reason. He explained that the building is
156   large, with varying elevations and facades that are stepped back, while maintaining the required
157   height. The utility boxes, which carry massive voltage, are located behind the building, serving
158   as a natural buffer. The building also functions as a sound barrier, with some of the dock
159   operations positioned on the east side to minimize disruption to nearby residential areas.
160
161   Barry mentioned that the glass designation was not feasible for the building’s intended use.
162   However, they were following the LEED requirements for bike parking, though the current
163   facility's bike parking is not heavily used. He clarified that the setback had been modified to
164   avoid the need for an additional request, and they were not encroaching on the setback as the
165   zoning lot line had been adjusted. He explained that the building could not be more than 50 feet
166   away, but without the exception, they would not be able to connect to the existing building.
167
168   Regarding landscaping, Barry stated that they proposed planting 1,200 trees, though they would
169   prefer to plant fewer. Kim Struthers noted that the code would require approximately 2,200 trees.
170   Commissioner Everett remarked that even 1,200 trees seemed excessive, especially since the
171   project was connected to an existing property. The applicant added that much of the property
172   would remain in its native state. After hearing the rationale behind the exceptions, the
173   Commissioners agreed that the applicant was making a good faith effort to meet and address the
174   code requirements.
175
176   Commissioner Eyre reviewed the possible findings on the item, and finding number two allows
177   the exceptions. Kim Stated all the exceptions are acceptable and can be allowed.
178
179   Commission Chair asked for further discussion.

182                   for approval of the LFAB 2 Site Plan including exceptions to the design
183                   standards located at 4000 North Flash Drive; including all DRC comments and
184                   findings stated in the report, also noting the exceptions for the design standard.
185                   Commissioner Eyre seconded the motion.

191   3.4) Public hearing and consideration of Compass Billboards? request for conditional use
192   approval of pylon signs with electronic message displays for EV Auto located at 1060 West
193   State Street.
194   Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item. There were 8 redline DRC comments.
195
196   Commissioner Everett asked for clarification on the total sign height.
197
198   The applicant, Braxton Shank from Compass Billboard, was present. He stated that they were
199   comfortable with a 40-foot height due to the freeway’s grade. Commissioner Everett inquired
200   about the electronic features of the sign, and the applicant explained that it was a standard sign
201   with automatic dimming to reduce light pollution. When asked by Commissioner Everett if the
202   sign could be turned off during non-operating hours, the applicant responded that the sign’s
203   location made it barely visible. Commissioner Eyre asked whether charging services would be
204   offered, as that could affect the hours of operation. He also mentioned that the hours of operation
205   could influence the conditional use. Commissioner Kunze raised concerns about light pollution
206   affecting nearby residents, but the applicant clarified that there were no homes currently on the
207   property. Kim Struthers noted that the site plan included charging stations, although they were
208   likely intended for inventory purposes. The applicant then inquired whether operational hours
209   were addressed in the code, and the commissioners clarified that they could impose regulations if
210   light pollution became an issue.
211
212   Commission Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.
213
214   Adam Olsen expressed concern about the automated sign, fearing it would impact his residence.
215   While he had no objection to the sign being installed, he was worried about potential light
216   pollution, particularly from flashing and animated elements, noting that an existing sign already
217   affected their homes.
218
219   Commission Chair Jackson then closed the public hearing.
220
221   Commissioner Everett suggested that a reasonable condition might be to reduce the sign’s
222   operating hours to mitigate the impact on residents. Commissioner Eyre agreed with the idea of
223   turning off the sign but questioned whether it was reasonable to impose such a condition.
224   Commission Chair Jackson pointed out that the Ken Garff sign, though taller, was located farther
225   away.
226
227   Mr. Shank acknowledged the concerns about how signs could affect residences and emphasized
228   that he did not want to be a nuisance to the surrounding community. He asked the commissioners
229   to consider either shielding the sign or setting conditions on hours of operation, noting that
230   another option could be to keep the sign static during non-operating hours.
231
232   Commissioner Eyre expressed his personal dislike of signs, viewing them as a blight on
233   communities, but also recognized their value for business advertising. He felt that requiring the
      Lehi City Planning Commission                    5                                September 12, 2024
234   sign to be turned off entirely might be excessive but suggested that offering the option of either
235   shielding or turning off the sign could be left to the developer.
236
237   Katie Bussell clarified that the code specified 10:00 p.m. as the cutoff time for operating hours.
238
239                   Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to approve Compass Billboards request for
240                   conditional use approval of pylon signs with electronic message displays for
241                   EV Auto located at 1060 West State Street; including all DRC comments and
242                   findings stated in the report, noting the exception for the height due to the grade
243                   of the freeway. Commissioner Kunze seconded the motion.

315   3.8) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a
316   Development Code Amendment to Chapters 12-A and 12-B, adding some additional
317   requirements to grading.
318   Katie Bussell presented information on the agenda item.
319
320   Katie pulled up the map to show some of the occurrences. Commission Chair Jackson asked for
321   clarification on misdemeanor charges and the associated penalties. Craig explained that the code
322   enforcement team worked with residents who had encroached on City property. He outlined the
323   justification for keeping these violations as misdemeanors, noting that it was not feasible for
      Lehi City Planning Commission                   7                                September 12, 2024
324   every code enforcement violation to address all issues. The cases that were brought to
325   prosecution typically involved ongoing problems. Katie added that most complaints were
326   initially sent to the planning staff, who would issue a letter asking the resident to contact the City
327   for compliance. If the resident failed to comply, the case would then be forwarded to
328   prosecution.
329
330   Commission Chair Jackson opened the public hearing.
331
332   Brandon Rudolph spoke about the seriousness of each misdemeanor, noting that most cases were
333   sent to prosecution and resolved there. He confirmed that his experiences aligned with what
334   Craig had stated.
335
336   Commission Chair Jackson then closed the public hearing.
337
338   Commissioner Everett emphasized the seriousness of residents developing on City property.
339   Commissioner Eyre asked for clarification regarding the Jordan River Trail. Kim explained that
340   the trail was on Utah County property, so the county would need to take a position on the matter.
341   The Commission Chair inquired about the possibility of charging daily misdemeanors. Craig and
342   the Commission discussed the process of notifying the tenant and allowing them a grace period
343   to respond or contact the City. Commissioner Everett pointed out that while there was discretion
344   in enforcement, compliance often could not be achieved without penalties.
345
346                   Motion: Commissioner Jones moved to recommend approval of Lehi City’s
347                   request for review of a Development Code Amendment to Chapters 12-A and
348                   12-B, including all DRC comments, with the findings stated in the report. Noting
349                   a grace period of 30 days of notice before a misdemeanor Class B charge can be
350                   brought to prosecution. Commissioner Everett seconded the motion.
351
352                   Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner
353                   Eyre, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes; Commissioner Jones, yes. The motion
354                   passed unanimously.
