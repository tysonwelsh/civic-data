1. Discussion Items
1.1 Discussion on Public Works Operations Contract; John Miller, Public Works

Director
John Miller introduced Brandon Johnson, Public Works Inspector, to the Council.
Brandon Johnson used to plow streets for Salt Lake County before coming to work
for Millcreek. Mayor Silvestrini said staff has had conversations about renewing the
Salt Lake County public works contract which would be a 44% increase for the same
level of service. He said Holladay and Taylorsville will also be getting increases and
it is not sustainable. Council Member Marchant said every city’s contract cost was
increasing and the Municipal Services District (MSD) was staying the same, so the
numbers did not add up. Laurie Johnson said when the County added in the
administration costs, MSD had already been paying it and the cities had not been.
John Miller said the MSD is on a calendar budget year. Council Member Marchant
requested a spreadsheet of costs. Miller provided the Council with a handout on
public works operations costs, but it was not the detail that Council Member
Marchant wanted. Council Member Uipi asked if the administration costs were the

the public works budget.

1.2 Discussion on FY 2019-20 Budget; Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director
Laurie Johnson said the budget building blocks are people, policy, and place. She said
there would not be any additional employees added to Millcreek staff next fiscal year.
In the budget, she budgeted for a 1.6% cost of living adjustment (COLA) and 2.5%
merit for employees, a 6.68% medical insurance rate increase, and the dental, vision,
and life, and disability rates will stay the same. She said at the next Council meeting

she would propose a flex schedule proposal and paid time off (PTO) accrual change

recommended staying with HDME as the auditor for four or five years. Mayor
Silvestrini said Millcreek’s contribution to the Central Wasatch Commission is
estimated to be about $75,000, which is based on population. Johnson then went over
the upcoming FY 18-19 and FY19-20 budget schedules. Mayor Silvestrini said
Millcreek shares 3 Unified Police Department (UPD) sergeants with Holladay. He
said Chief DeBry would like to restructure the Millcreek precinct which would be
about a $50,000 impact to Millcreek, and that will be built into the contract. Johnson
said Millcreek received more in property tax this year than what was needed for the
UPD contract, so she recommended moving the excess funds into fund balance to
offset costs for the next time. She said if a truth in taxation hearing was needed for the
next fiscal year, she anticipated the amount of increase being minimal.

She asked when the Council wanted to continue the strategic planning. Council
Member Uipi suggested doing planning once a month during work meetings.

1.3 Discussion on Planning Priorities for 2019; Francis Lilly, Community
Development Director
Francis Lilly handed out a memo on community development priorities for 2019,
which were:

1) Commission a study to analyze critical views and recommend/implement changes to
Millcreek’s ordinance to protect identified viewsheds.

2) Study and adopt an ordinance relating to accessory dwelling units (ADU).

3) Conduct studies to identify specific neighborhood characteristics and write standards in
the code that are reflective of each neighborhood rather than the one-size-fits-all RCOZ
approach.

4) Complete a village center study for the Olympus Hills neighborhood.

5) Tweak or redo the Canyon Rim Village Center study.

6) Adopt the flag lot policy as an ordinance.

7) Recodify/reorganize Title 18 and Title 19.

8) Tweak FCOZ — move some of the slope and terracing/retaining requirements into Title
18 to make them applicable to all developments.
9) Focus efforts on City Center initiatives.

He also provided a list of potential ordinance updates, and goals and strategies from
the General Plan. He said ordinance updates coming to the Council in the near future
are on condominium conversions, public noticing amendments, billboard code
amendments, and sign code updates. He said each priority would require staff time
and some had fiscal implications. Mayor Silvestrini suggested staff reach out to the
Salt Lake Chamber of Commerce on priority item 2. Lilly said Murray and Sandy had
ordinance on ADU needing conditional use permits. Mayor Silvestrini asked what
Lilly wanted for a flag lot policy. Lilly said the current policy is convoluted and he
wanted to codify something. He requested guidance from the Council on two-three
priority items. Council Member Jackson said viewsheds are not the highest priority,
but ADUs, RCOZ, and FCOZ are. Council Member Uipi said reorganizing Title 18
and Title 19 was a high priority. John Brems said Herriman did the same thing to
their subdivision and zoning titles and it took about a year to do and cost about
$75,000 for consultants. Council Member Uipi said items 2, 3, 8, and 7 were her top
priorities. Mayor Silvestrini and Council Member Marchant said 9 should be the top
priority. Mayor Silvestrini said the city center has momentum and he wanted to keep
it going. He said acquiring park space in the city center would keep good faith with
the public. Council Member Uipi said 9 was more of an Economic Development
Department priority than a Community Development one. Council Member Jackson
asked if the City should address ADUs sooner than later. Mayor Silvestrini said yes.
Mayor Silvestrini said he had not heard too many comments about the RCOZ from
residents. Council Member Uipi said she does when it gets misapplied. Lilly said he
could field complaints about RCOZ. Mayor Silvestrini said not many lots are affected
by FCOZ. Lilly said the slope and terracing provisions should be removed from the
FCOZ ordinance and added to the regular code. Mayor Silvestrini relayed an example
about an FCOZ issue and a building permit violation in Olympus Cove.

John Geilmann recommended each Council Member list their priorities for the 9
items. Council Member Catten asked which item would be the least consuming on
time and resources. Lilly said item 8 and he removed it from the list. Council Member
Marchant asked for Francis Lilly’s priorities, which were: 2, 4, and 1. Council
Member Jackson asked about the Canyon Rim Village Center study. Lilly said he
wanted to take it to the community and ask what they liked or did not like about it.
While the Council listed their priorities, Mike Winder discussed drafting a potential
economic development plan. The totaled Council priorities showed item 9 (City
Center initiatives) being the first priority, item 2 (ADUs ordinance) second, item 4
(Olympus Hills study) third, item 6 (flag lot ordinance) fourth, and item 5 (Canyon
Rim study) fifth.
