PUBLIC COMMENT:

   Abigail Hansen addressed the council about how chickens are not allowed in her neighborhood. She
said that she had come to the meeting to ask the council if they would be allowed to have chickens in
their area. She said that because of the high price of food chickens could help because they produce
food.
   Ruth Hansen spoke and echoed what her daughter had said about allowing chickens in their
neighborhood. She stated that chickens help control bugs, they provide food, they make good pets and
are much quieter than her neighbor’s dogs and car alarm. She said that she could go a block down the
street and buy a plot in the cemetery that allows animal rights.
    Jamie Gilbert spoke and said that she lives close to Main Street, and she is not allowed to have
chickens. She said she believes that chickens have a minimal impact on a neighborhood. She stated
that Nephi’s goal is to be a rural community and by allowing property owners to have chickens would
help add to the rural feel of our community.
   Terry Cook spoke and said that he lives in a neighborhood where chickens are allowed, and he thinks
they are a benefit to the community. Mr. Cook agrees with having chicken and residents should have
personal property rights to have chickens.
   Elsha Penrod lives on second south and as you look at the zoning map, directly across the street from
her home her neighbors have animal rights. Her kids would love to have rabbits and they are not
allowed and everyone around them have animal rights.
   Larry Ostler is not against people having chickens and rabbits. People have dogs. The price of grain is
very expensive to feed animals right now.
   Ruth Hansen said that chickens and rabbits can eat from scraps.

RODERICK NEPHI BUSINESS PARK PETITION (1) REJECTED AND RODERICK NEPHI BUSINESS PARK
PETITION (2) ACCEPTED:

  City Recorder Lisa Brough presented a rejection letter for Roderick Nephi Business Park Petition that
included the northern part of the Worthington property. The petition was rejected because of the lack
of a recordable map.
   An annexation petition called Roderick Nephi Business Park (2) was present to the council. This
petition included the entire parcel of the Worthington property. Councilor Worwood moved to accept
the petition and to allow it to move forward in the annexation process. Councilor Ostler seconded the
motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

UDOT AGREEMENTS FOR FAA GRANTS APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council two FAA grant agreements. The two grants were the Airport
Coronavirus Response Grant and the Airport Rescue Grant. UDOT would be the state agency
administering these grants. Councilor Parady moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the two FAA Grant
agreements. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

QUITCLAIM DEED FOR PROPERTY NEAR HOSPITAL:

   Mr. Atkinson updated the council that when the hospital was originally constructed, there was a
three-way deal between the city, the hospital administration, and the school district. At the time, the
city owned property where the hospital is located and there was a small parcel that shows on the county
records still being owned by the city. The parcel is on the north side of the hospital properties and is
near their stormwater pond. Mr. Atkinson stated that it is highly unlikely the city would ever have a
need for the property and will provide a benefit for the hospital.
   Councilor Memmott moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the quitclaim deed for the property near
the hospital. Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

PROPERTY SURPLUS DECLARATION PROCESS DISCUSSED:

  Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council the process of declaring real property surplus property. The
council has been discussing developing or selling some property known as the “Greenhalgh Property” on
the southwest end near the 222-freeway exit. To allow this property to be sold, it must first be declared
surplus. Most small properties owned by the city have not fallen into this category. The state requires
that prior to “...disposing of a significant parcel of real property the municipality shall:”
                  Provide 14 days’ notice for public comment
                  Provide an opportunity for public comment
The city’s code defines a significant parcel in Title 1-12-1 as: “a parcel of real property owned by the city
that has a fair market value of one hundred thousand dollars ($100,000.00) or more or an annual fair

WORK SESSION:

Work Session convened at 9:06 p.m.

    1. The council discussed draft revisions to the Combined Use zone, the ID-3 zone, and the
       definition for "open space.” The council had some concerns about the height requirements in
       the ID-3 zone.

    2. The council discussed grants available for trail systems and the idea of putting mountain bike
       trails in Rees Flat property owned by the city.

    3. Mayor Seely brought up the idea of using high school interns for the summer.

    4. The council agreed to direct the Planning Commission to discuss the chicken/animal rights issue
       and bring back some recommendations to the council.

    5. Councilor Parady moved to adjourn the work session meeting. Councilor Ostler seconded the
       motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

   Meeting adjourned at 10:10 p.m.
