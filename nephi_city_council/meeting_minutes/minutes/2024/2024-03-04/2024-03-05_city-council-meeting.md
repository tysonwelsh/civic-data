PUBLIC COMMENT:

  Blaine Malquist addressed the council and said that he and his family members had attended the last
three Planning and Zoning meetings where the Planning Commission were discussing being able to use
your RV on your own property. He said that at the first meeting the Planning Commission discussed that
you couldn’t use your RV for any days on your own property and then at the second meeting they were
going to allow using your RV on your own property if you obtained a permit. He thought that would be
a great inconvenience. Then he said, at the third meeting on February 21st they said that they were back
to zero days unless you were building a home on the lot where the RV would be used. He said that he
did a survey and had talked to over 50 families and 47 families said that they all use their RVs at their
homes and would prefer this still be allowed. He said the constitution and the Declaration of
Independence gives us the right to decide what we do on our own property. Mr. Malquist said that it is
a hardship and costly to families if they are not allowed to use their RVs on their own property.

  Paul Keddington spoke briefly and introduced himself as a Messianic Jew. He asked the council if they
knew what Sukkot is. He answered that it is a biblical commandment to stay in a tent or an RV for a
week. He said that millions of people around the country observe this ritual. He said this has been
going on since the time of Moses to help people remember the exodus from Egypt. The practice is
found in Leviticus 23 in the Bible. He said that he is a veteran and served for 8 years. He was grateful
for the system of government that people can come and discuss issues. He asked the council to allow

222 BUSINESS PARK COMMERCIAL SUBDIVISION FINAL PLAT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson presented a final plat for the 7 lot 222 Business Park Commercial Subdivision. The
development consists of one parcel and is located approximately 618 W Sheep Lane. The development
was previously rezoned to HC-1 from the ID-1 zone. There is a bond in place at the city for curb and
gutter that was a condition of the property being annexed into the city. The developer has reviewed
approval from both UDOT and the railroad regarding this project.
  Councilor Travis Worwood moved to approve the final plat for the 222 Business Park Subdivision.
Councilor Jeramie Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

MARTY BARBER WITH BARCO CONSTRUCTION REZONE REQUEST APPROVED:

    Mr. Atkinson explained that several years ago, the city entered into an agreement with Directrix
Corporation (Mad Dog Knives) to sell a city-owned property for the purpose of a business expansion for
a light manufacturing company, Mad Dog Knives. The parcel is a triangle piece adjacent to I-15 and
Sheep Lane. The parcel was originally annexed as part of the Biglow Annexation and was zoned Highway
Commercial 1 (HC-1) at annexation. The lot was subsequently rezoned to the ID-3 (Light Industrial) zone
in June of 2023 allowing for the light industrial use needed by Mad Dog Knives. Since that time Mad Dog
Knives has sold the property to Marty Barber (Barco Construction) who is assuming the building permit
of the property now under construction. When the building is completed, Mr. Barber has a buyer
interested in buying the building to use it as an auto-repair/maintenance shop. To have that use
available to the buyer, the parcel must be rezoned to HC-1.
    Councilor Cowan moved to approve Ordinance 03-05-2024 changing the property owned by Marty
Barber from an ID-3 zone to ID-1 zone. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion passed o a
unanimous vote.

WATER RIGHTS CONSULTING CONTRACT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson explained that Nephi City originally contracted with Sunrise Engineering to engage a
consultant to proof water rights with the state, compile and track the city’s water right inventory,
examine the exchange agreement Nephi City has with the Nephi Irrigation Company, and to complete
the city’s 40-year water rights plan. Most of those services have been completed and the original
contract is substantially fulfilled. The city’s 40-year water rights plan was adopted by the City Council
on May 2, 2023.
  Mr. Atkinson stated that there were still some additional services the City would like to complete with
the help of the consultant from Sunrise, Ken Tuttle. Mr. Tuttle has retired from Sunrise Engineering but
has agreed to assist Nephi City as an independent consultant. Staff is recommending that we engage in
a contract with Mr. Tuttle for the following services:
    ● Draft, prepare, and review water right purchase/dedication documents for purchased water
         rights and change new water rights from their current use to a municipal use.
    ● Manage and track progress of Nephi City water right inventory, update current water right
         inventory as needed from “Approved” status to “Certified” use status.
    ● Consulting services associated with new annexations or development and the resulting water
         right requirements.
    ● Consulting services associated with water right purchasing, exactions, title transfers, deeds, and
         associated water right protections.
    ● Water right proof services, drafting proofs for the water rights that are deemed ready for
         proofing, equal to the current water right use as reported to the Utah Division of Water Rights
         (DWRi).

NORTH RIDGE ESTATES PLAT F ANNEXATION PETITION APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson explained the annexation process and said that the annexation petition before the
council tonight was a small piece of property that would add to the North Ridge Estates Subdivision. that
includes ½ of 1600 North. This would allow another row of houses on the north side of 1600 North.
Mayor Seely asked that he thought originally the plan for this subdivision was to have larger lots on the
east end of the subdivision. The Council discussed entering into an annexation agreement specifying the
kind of density and zoning they would allow with the annexed property.
  Councilor Travis Worwood moved to accept the annexation petition and allow the annexation process
to move forward. Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
