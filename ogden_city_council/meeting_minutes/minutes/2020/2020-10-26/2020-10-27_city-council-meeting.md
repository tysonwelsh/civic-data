       Housing Policy Discussion
                 Council Executive Director Eller-Smith used the aid ofa PowerPoint presentation to review the City's current housing
       policies as well as the results of the City's efforts to address housing affordability. The pmpose of the City's current policies is to
       create healthy neighborhoods with a broad range of housing options. A strong core builds on strength and enhances the image of
       the City. Targeting of the policies are focused, coordinated efforts to achieve the best results. Block level action planning is the
       most effective type of targeting; it involves City resources and departments, neighborhood residents, owners, and partners. The
       City has pursued several low-moderate income housing projects, including the Lincoln Cottages, Oak Den Bungalows, individual
       units at scattered sites, and housing rehab/resale projects. In these projects, 373 homes have been sold, with 335 of them
       purchased by low-moderate income buyers. Low-moderate income is defined as being up to 80 percent of the area median income
       (AMI) for a family of four, which translates to $68,000 per year or $32 per hour for wages.
                 Ms. Eller-Smith then facilitated discussion among the Council regarding their desires for additional data pertaining to
       housing or policy adjushnents to be considered jointly by Adminish·ation and the Council. There was a focus on the appropriate
       ratio ofrental units versus owner-occupied units in the City; partnering with other communities in Weber County to develop a
       comprehensive housing policy; and the concept ofinclusionary housing requirements that could be included in a specific zone in
       the City. Council member Hyer suggested that the Council solicit input from Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC) and the
       Weber-Morgan Health Department regarding this matter because they have a lot of data regarding growth projections, wage data

       with Council member Nadolski noting that while that information would be valuable, the Council needs to set a goal for future

       Council member White noting she feels that the Council has enough data to begin moving on developing a comprehensive

housing policy; however, the City has already done a lot of work and adjusted regulations to address housing affordability. It is
important to document existing policies in order to respond to complaints and concerns the Council has received about the City's
lack of activity to respond to current housing market conditions. The Council agreed there is a housing affordability issue in the

          Council member Nadolski suggested developing an affordability plan for the City that will include new ideas and
strategies to add to the City's already robust approach to addressing the housing matter. This will give the Council the opportunity
to discuss individual ideas that could become strategies in the plan. Chair Choberka supported that idea, but indicated that
developing a comprehensive plan could take a number of years. Council member Lopez referenced the difficulty in engaging in
dialogue regarding such a sensitive issue, and he suggested that a survey be developed that asks pointed questions regarding each
Council member's feelings about housing issues in the communitY,.
          Ms. Eller-Smith stated that responsive to State Legislation, the City has developed an affordable housing plan and
submitted it to the State. Council member White acknowledged the existence of that plan, but indicated she feels that Ogden needs
a more comprehensive plan that considers different variables, such as owner-occupancy versus rental units; the relationship
between housing affordability and housing quality; the impact that growth will have on the availability of housing and
affordability; and actions that can be employed to achieve targets defined in the plan. Additionally, the City's AMI is lower than
the AMI for the region and it may be appropriate to consider a companion policy that addresses wages in the community. The City
has been focused on business recruitment and job creation, but that has a direct impact on housing availability in the City. Several
Council members agreed with Council member White's ideas for a housing affordability plan and discussed the next steps for the
body and Council staff to take to begin working on the initiative. They acknowledged that the workload of Council staff and
Administration is currently very heavy and it would be prudent to consider hiring a consultant that can dedicate more time and
resources to developing of a comprehensive plan. Council member White referenced existing policies in other communities and
suggested that the Council review them before beginning the process of developing a scope of work for a consultant that will be
tasked with formulating a draft plan; she indicated that an important early step in the process of developing a plan is to evaluate all
existing policies to determine how to build or improve upon those policies in a comprehensive housing policy.
          Ms. Eller-Smith indicated that staff will prepare a survey that can be sent to each Council member to provide their input
regarding specific housing issues in the community; they will also begin to develop a scope of work that can be used to inform a
formal request for proposals (RFP) seeking a consultant to develop a comprehensive housing plan.
