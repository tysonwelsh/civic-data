2) Presentations and Reports

2.1) Lehi East-West Transportation Study Presentation
     Josh Gibbons from Hales Engineering put together a presentation on future transportation
demands in Lehi. He explained that the current MAG plan projects out to 2050, by which point
the population is predicted to double in the valley. Lehi is anticipated to be at a population of
185,000 at full build-out. Mayor Johnson explained that the city has been operating with the
build-out figures for a while now. He emphasized the need to find transportation corridors now
so that the city doesn’t have to buy out developed properties later. Gibbons explained that many
of Lehi’s existing east-west roads will be sitting at chokepoints or failures by the time full build-
out happens. There will be an estimated load of 183k cars a day, and the roads as they currently
sit are at a 101k cars per day deficit. This is a freeway-level car load. MAG currently has three
freeway alignment scenarios in the works: expanding Pioneer Crossing, expanding Pony Express
Parkway, and creating a new North Lakeshore road. These will help take on the deficit. The
North Lakeshore improves the capacity the most. The council discussed the figures presented for
the three possibilities. Lorin Powell discussed the current traffic the city is seeing, caused by the

eliminate an arterial road. He is in favor of the North Lakeshore Freeway, as it would divide

6) Consideration of Ordinance #57-2025 adopting Title 6 Chapter 5 Section 8, establishing
     Bridge Safety in Lehi City.

Chief Paul presented the item. He explained that the primary concern is for the safety of people
using the Jordan River Trail System. The bridge in question is a north end pedestrian bridge
that’s four feet wide. People were using it to jump into the water, which the bridge is not
designed for, as well as blocking the safe usage of the bridge by other pedestrians.

Councilor Stallings asked about other wider bridges that the city may want to allow fishing on.
She asked if signage would be possible. Chief Paul suggested placing no jumping/no fishing
Lehi City Council Meeting                          4                                 September 9, 2025
signs on bridges where the activities are prohibited. Ryan Wood explained that the way the
ordinance is currently written, everything is prohibited unless signage indicates that it is allowed.
The council liked the idea of reversing the wording so that activities are permitted unless
explicitly forbidden by signage. Chief Paul explained that he sees the ordinance as a no
trespassing sign, meaning that the purpose is to alert someone of something they cannot do, and
the action is otherwise permitted.

Councilor Stallings discussed how to reword the ordinance with Wood. Wood’s concern is that
Lehi City would not be in charge of the signage on the bridge in question. Mayor Johnson
explained that there is another bridge being built by UDOT which may or may not be turned over
to Lehi City. Lorin Powell said it likely will be. Councilor Condie shared that he likes the
direction that Councilor Stallings is going. He recommended moving the phrase “if designated”
to the beginning of the ordinance to clarify. Wood explained that there are flaws in basing the
unlawfulness on signage that can be taken down (unlawfully) by civilians. Chief Paul explained
that an officer would use his discretion to determine if a use of a bridge is limiting or impeding
someone else’s use of the bridge.

Councilor Stalling brought up code from Iowa in regard to discussing changes to section B. She
sees the main concern as obstruction of the bridge. Wood is open to trying out the resolution and
bringing it back to council if needed. Mayor Johnson likes the idea of writing the ordinance in
order to primarily address the obstruction aspect rather than the jumping off the bridge aspect.
Chief Paul explained that the complaint that spurred the creation of the ordinance was due to
bridge use being hampered, but he thinks that the safety aspect is also important and shouldn’t be
ignored.

Motion: Councilor Hancock moved to table Ordinance #57-2025 adopting Title 6 Chapter 5
Section 8, establishing Bridge Safety in Lehi City as presented. Councilor Newall seconded the
motion.

Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Hancock, Yes;
Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

8) Consideration of Ordinance #49-2025, approving a Development Code amendment to
     Table 05.040-A, Residential Bulk and Intensity, reducing the setback for remodels
     and additions in the RA-1 and R-1-15 zones.

Kim Struthers presented the item. The amendment will allow a reduction to setbacks after two
years. The Planning Commission gave it a negative recommendation, as they would prefer not to
have setbacks change after a certain period of time. They were open to the idea of setbacks being
smaller at inception.

Councilor Stallings asked about the reduction allowance. Struthers explained that side setbacks
will be at five feet, and the rear setback has a larger decrease. Councilor Stallings agreed with the
Planning Commission’s assessment that the reduction feels arbitrary. She felt as though the
expansions she sees can fit within the established setbacks.

Councilor Newall brought up that the ordinance was drafted in response to a resident that wanted
to put in a porch but wasn’t allowed to unless it was a detached structure. She also mentioned the
fact that ADUs have much lower setbacks than homes (five feet if there isn’t a window).
Councilor Stallings was still reluctant to endorse the ordinance.

Mayor Johnson shared that he thinks it will be a rarity for people to build as close as possible to
the property line. He worries about infringing on fifth amendment property rights if the
ordinance doesn’t pass. Councilor Hancock mentioned that a porch used for entertaining could
end up being a bigger nuisance than an enclosed building closer to the property line. Mayor
Johnson worries that the city choosing to dictate one way or the other will result in more
micromanagement required in the future.

Councilor Stallings brought up the arbitrariness of the two-year time period. She claimed that
setbacks are protected by the constitution, and a protection for those who valued privacy enough
to buy a bigger lot. Mayor Johnson asked for her reasoning. She clarified that setbacks are
derived from property rights in the fifth amendment, while not a property right themselves.
Mayor Johnson shared that he was not comfortable with that interpretation of the constitution, as
zoning laws didn’t come about until the 1900s, and under Jeffersonian principles setbacks would
be unlawful.

Councilor Newall shared that she feels that the law is already arbitrary in that sheds and detached
building are allowed to be closer to the property line than additions to a home. She argued that
the ordinance would make city code more consistent. Councilor Albrecht agreed that the
setbacks currently feel arbitrary, as the state has required ADUs be allowed in every zone. She
asked what the intent of the two year wait period was. Struthers explained that it was to keep a
smaller footprint during initial building. The setback change is to give a bit of relief to
homeowners that want or need to expand as initial construction usually fits the maximum amount
of setback. The city wanted to avoid developers taking advantage of reduced setbacks. Councilor
Albrecht clarified that it is to distance expansions from the initial construction period.

Councilor Condie agreed that the two year waiting period is arbitrary. He also shared that very
few people are immediately expanding after two years. He thinks its absurd that the initial
complainant couldn’t building a porch after purchasing their house and had to wait potentially
two years to put it in. He suggested that the least arbitrary timing would be the day after the sale.
He feels that the two year wait period is an additional restriction after the restriction of the
setbacks themselves. Councilor Hancock agreed that when ADUs are taken into consideration
the two year setback is very arbitrary. He was in favor of decreasing setbacks overall. Councilor
Newall asked when the two year wait period was implemented. Struthers explained that it was in
place in 2010, and possibly earlier.

Mayor Johnson mentioned that the original reason the setback reductions were introduced was to
allow citizens to expand their homes rather than growing out of them and being forced to move
to other cities.

Councilor Stallings worried that approving the ordinance or changing code would result in
developers asking to be exempt from setback laws.

Motion: Councilor Condie moved to approve Ordinance #49-2025, approving a Development
Code amendment to Table 05.040-A, Residential Bulk and Intensity, reducing the setback for
remodels and additions in the RA-1 and R-1-15 zones and instruct staff to eliminate the two year
waiting period on any zone where the reduction of setbacks is allowed. In order for an addition to
be made the city requires a certificate of occupancy by the owner of the property to prevent
developers or contractors to increase or decrease setbacks. Councilor Hancock seconded the
motion.

Councilor Newall asked that DRC comments be included in the motion.

Amended Motion: Councilor Condie moved to approve Ordinance #49-2025, approving a
Development Code amendment to Table 05.040-A, Residential Bulk and Intensity, reducing the
setback for remodels and additions in the RA-1 and R-1-15 zones and instruct staff to eliminate
the two year waiting period on any zone where the reduction of setbacks is allowed. The R-1-22
zone is included. The table should also be reorganized so it is more intuitive. In order for an
addition to be made the city requires a certificate of occupancy by the owner of the property to
prevent developers or contractors to increase or decrease setbacks. Councilor Hancock seconded
the motion.

Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Hancock, Yes;
Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, No. The motion passed with four in favor and
one against.
