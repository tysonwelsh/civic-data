      RESOLUTION 20-105: AUTHORIZE THE EXECUTION OF AN AMENDMENT
      TO A SERVICE AGREEMENT WITH THE COMMUNITY EDUCATION
      PARTNERSHIP OF WEST VALLEY CITY, INC.
      Paul Isaac, Assistant City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 20-105 that would
      authorize the Execution of an Amendment to a Service Agreement with the Community
      Education Partnership of West Valley City, Inc.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
      follows:

             CEP provides a wide variety of afterschool and out-of-school programming for
             school aged children in West Valley City. These programs have been a positive
             force in the community and have reached numerous families throughout the City.
             The City has supported these efforts financially for several years and CEP has been
             able to leverage funding provided to secure matching grants and additional revenue
             sources. To continue to receive these funds, CEP requires additional assistance

      presentation from CEP that details how the money will be spent and what goals will be

RESOLUTION 20-106: AUTHORIZE WEST VALLEY CITY TO ENTER INTO A
MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING WITH THE UTAH ATTORNEY
GENERALS OFFICE CRIMES AGAINST STATEWIDE ECONOMY (CASE)
TASKFORCE
Robert Hamilton, Deputy Chief of Police, presented proposed Resolution 20-106 that
would authorize West Valley City to enter into a Memorandum of Understanding with the
Utah Attorney General’s Office Crimes Against Statewide Economy (Case) Taskforce.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The Utah Attorney General’s Office has created CASE task force to coordinate
       local law enforcement resources in the investigation of property and economic
       crimes and their associated organizations and networks. The Police Department
       seeks to assign one (1) investigator to this position. The Utah Attorney General’s
       Office agrees to compensate WVC for the cost of that employee’s salary and
       benefits.

       This task force initiates and investigates major property and economic related
       crimes, including organized crime operations, money laundering, and cases that
       requiring personnel or financial resources above what a local agency can supply.

Mayor Bigelow asked if this is a full-time position. Deputy Chief Hamilton replied yes and
noted that the State will reimburse the City 100% (excluding overtime). Councilmember
Buhler clarified that this Task Force would focus more on serious theft issues and not
simple retail theft or shoplifting. Deputy Chief Hamilton replied yes and detailed potential
scenarios of organized crime that this Task Force could investigate. Councilmember Buhler
asked if the Detective assigned from West Valley City would have enough cases within the
City or if they would end up working more in other jurisdictions. Deputy Chief Hamilton
replied that he would anticipate the Detective to be overworked with cases tied to West
Valley City and noted that there are many cases related to this type of crime at this time

Force. Deputy Chief Hamilton replied the City can only have one Detective at this time, as

could expand in the future. Councilmember Huynh asked if there is a significant problem
with crimes detailed in the MOU that occur in West Valley City. Deputy Chief replied that

RESOLUTION 20-107: APPROVE A BETTERMENT AGREEMENT BETWEEN
THE CITY AND THE UTAH DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION FOR THE
BURIAL OF CERTAIN POWER LINES ON AND NEAR 3500 SOUTH AND 5600
WEST
Dan Johnson, Public Works, presented proposed Resolution 20-107 that would approve a
Betterment Agreement between the City and the Utah Department of Transportation for
the burial of certain power lines on and near 3500 South and 5600 West.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       UDOT is reconstructing and widening portions of 3500 South and 5600 West as
       part of the Mountain View Corridor project. The city requested the project
       betterment from UDOT to bury overhead power on 3500 South between 5450 West
       and 6000 West, and on 5600 West between approximately 3600 South and 3400
       South.

       The power burial betterment was originally requested in December 2018, when the
       city and UDOT executed Cooperative Agreement No. 1 for the construction of
       Mountain View Corridor from 4100 South to SR-201. The work has since been
       completed and the execution of this agreement will authorize the city to pay UDOT
       for the city-requested betterment work. Per the agreement, payment is due October
       15, 2020.

The City Council will consider Resolution 20-107 at the Regular Council Meeting
scheduled July 21, 2020 at 6:30 P.M.

RESOLUTION 20-108: APPROVE THE PURCHASE OF STREETLIGHTS FROM
MOUNTAIN STATES LIGHTING FOR LOCATIONS IMPACTED BY THE
MOUNTAIN VIEW CORRIDOR
Dan Johnson, Public Works, presented proposed Resolution 20-108 that would approve the

purchase of streetlights from Mountain States Lighting for locations impacted by the
Mountain View Corridor.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       UDOT’s work on Mountain View Corridor is impacting various streets within West
       Valley City. The city has requested a betterment for UDOT’s contractor to install
       streetlights on these streets. The city has opted to purchase and supply lights
       directly to the contractor for installation to take advantage of a discount offered
       from the city’s supplier, Mountain States Lighting.

       This resolution authorizes the city to spend up to $375,000 to purchase 131
       streetlights for use on the following streets: 3500 South, 5600 West, SR-201 South
       Frontage Road, Cilma Drive, Masters Drive, 3575 South, Darle Avenue, 2400
       South, Marsha Drive and Anna Caroline Drive.

Mayor Bigelow noted that this has not been previously approved by the Council and the
budget has not been reviewed for this project.

The City Council will consider Resolution 20-108 at the Regular Council Meeting
scheduled July 21, 2020 at 6:30 P.M.

     Councilmember Lang asked if there is a reason why the City reduced the number
