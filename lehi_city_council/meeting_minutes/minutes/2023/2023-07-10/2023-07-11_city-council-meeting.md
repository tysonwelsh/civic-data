2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Fiber Network Update
Shaye Ruitenbeek, Fiber Administrative Services Manager, gave an update on the City’s Fiber
Network project. She informed the Council of the division’s growth and customer connections.
Currently there are 54 customers connected with 72 sign-ups in total. Pre-registration has seen
some growth and construction is underway for multiple cabinets. Take rates are being monitored,
and there is an interest in comparing numbers with a similar project in American Fork. The
division will analyze survey data and collaborate with ISPs on strategy development.

2.2 Planning Department’s Report from Lessons Learned in Netherlands Part I
Mike West, Planning Manager, and Kim Struthers, Community Development Director, presented
what they learned about transportation on their self-funded trip to Netherlands. Mr Struthers
recognized that although they don’t expect to be like Netherlands, he believes there are planning
principals that can be adopted. They explored the country using bikes, walking, and public
transit. They discussed the Dutch cycling network, emphasizing the convenience, safety, and
directness of bike paths, which encouraged cycling as a mode of transportation. They highlighted
the integration of cycling with public transit, with bike paths leading directly to train stations and
bike-friendly facilities at transit centers. The Netherlands' approach to traffic circulation, traffic
calming measures, and priority for cyclists were also discussed. They mentioned the use of
separate road networks for different functions, such as limiting access on arterial roads and

9. Consideration of Resolution #2023-40 approving an Amendment to the Lehi Tennis Club
   Development Agreement.
   The petitioner, Jacob Hansen, presented some adjustments he wanted to make to the agreement
   due to changes in the project's economics.

    Mr. Hansen explained that they still aimed to build the approved metal building for the tennis
    club, but due to economic challenges, they wanted the option to change the building style.
    They also requested the ability to phase the development, starting with clay courts and later
    completing the larger indoor courts. Additionally, they wanted to explore the option of
    covering the clay courts, either temporarily or permanently, to accommodate for increased
    demand during winter.

    The Council expressed concerns about potential changes that could alter the original intent of
    the project and its impact on the neighborhood. They emphasized the importance of adhering
    to the initial design and its integration with the surrounding area.

    Mr. Hansen acknowledged the concerns and clarified his intentions to stay within the original
    parameters set forth in the agreement. He also presented the possibility of adding a swimming
    pool to the project, ensuring it would not exceed the parking requirements and usage limits.
    However, the council expressed hesitations about the potential impact on traffic and parking.

    The Council agreed to have Mr. Hansen come back with a revised version of the original
    agreement that addressed some minor changes, such as extending the timeline of the project.
    However, more significant alterations, like the building style and adding a swimming pool,
    would require further discussion and a clearer plan before formal approval.

    The council decided to have a motion to direct Mr. Hansen to work with staff on specific
    changes and bring back a revised developer's agreement for further review and approval.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to table this Resolution and direct staff to modify the
                agreement to allow for an extension and the construction of clay courts at this time.
                Councilor Albrecht seconded the motion.

    There was further discussion and clarification on the motion.

    Amended Motion: Council Condie amended his motion to include that the amendment would
            allow for an additional two years with a one-year extension on the application
            process, and allow for the construction of clay courts. Also adding that a semi-
            permanent surface for the driveway may be used instead of gravel. All other aspects
            of the developer agreement would remain unchanged. Council Albrecht seconded
            the amendment to the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Mayor Pro Tempore
    Hancock, Yes; Councilor Koivisto, Absent; and Councilor Southwick, Yes. The motion passed
    with 4 in favor and 1 absent.
