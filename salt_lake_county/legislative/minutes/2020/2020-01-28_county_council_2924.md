2. PUBLIC HEARING
Mr. Todd Draper , Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, Planning & 
Development, delivered a PowerPoint presentation on what Olympia Hills Development is, 
its proposed location, density, the proposed land use plan, design standards, and the 
steps the development would have to follow for approval of each phase.
Mr. Ivan Hooper , Avenue Consultants, continued the PowerPoint discussing the traffic 
impact study, the study area, data collection absorption rates, and mitigation measures.
Mr. Draper concluded the PowerPoint presentation stating the Council will be 
considering approval of three applications: a general plan amendment, a zone change, and 
a P -C zone Master Development Agreement.  The GSLMSD planning staff is 
recommending approval of all three components.
Mr. Bruce Baird , Legal Counsel, Olympia Hills Development, stated the developers of 
the proposed Olympia Hills Development have addressed all 13 concerns the Salt Lake 
County Council had regarding this development, as well as concerns of nearby residents .  
The project has specific requirements to address regional transportation solutions and the 
impact on water.  This project provides for affordable housing so people can live there .  
The developers have also worked with the Jordan School District on the impact this 
development would have on schools in the area. People who move into this development 
would help pay for the needed schools.  
Mr. Justin Swain , Utah for Responsible Growth, delivered a PowerPoint presentation 
stating the residents in the area support developments if they are done right.  He reviewed 
concerns the residents have with this development, which include the Southwest 
Community Land Use Plan, which calls for no more than five units per acre, not the higher 
density of this proposal; the live, work and play concept; the Master Development

Council Member Burdick opened the public hearing.
The following citizens spoke in opposition to the proposed project 
expressing concern because of increased taxes, the high density of the 
project, infrastructure not in place, increased volume of traffic, utilities, lack 
of public transit, impact fees, air quality, setting of precedent for other 
unincorporated areas, opposition by surrounding cities, emergency 
evacuation issues, and possibility of mining operations.   
Citizens also noted the need for open space and affordable and senior 
housing.  The development is inconsistent with the Southwest General Plan 
and it allows the developer to walk away from the project at any time .  
Council Members should wait for the Southwest Visioning Study and listen 
to residents before voting on this issue.
