     Chair Jacob called the meeting to order 7:45 pm.


 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Fiscal Year 2025 Tentative Budget Discussion
          Council Member Shelton asked that the Council have an opportunity to review a list of
          items requested by departments but not included in the Mayor’s Budget prior to making
          budget decisions. Council Member Green agreed. Chair Jacob noted that individual line
          items in the budget were not binding. Council Member Shelton felt he had been asked to
          make a choice at the previous budget discussion before knowing all requests. Council
          Member Bedore agreed.

          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck had been making a list of requests from
          the Council and reviewed a list of possible changes to the budget previously discussed by
          the Council, including Council transcription software and turnout gear for the Fire
          Department. Responding to a question from Council Member Shelton, Chief Maxfield said
          he did not think storage of additional turnout gear would be an issue.

          Ms. Steck requested a work session with the Council in late fall/early winter to discuss
          events before commitments were made and requested an additional $5,000 in the budget
          to cover costs for interpreters in the courts, which had increased dramatically over the
          last few months.

          The following budgets were reviewed and discussed:

               • Legal Services
                 Council Member Green asked if the Council should be reviewing an accounting of
                 Legal Services time spent by department for budget allocation. Ms. Steck expressed
                 the opinion that an accounting of time by department would probably not be worth

               utilizing software on hand to track use of time by department.

               Council Member Green asked if a metric was in place in each department to indicate
               when additional staff members would be needed to service the growing population.
               Chair Jacob trusted department heads to be aware of the needs and make requests
               accordingly. Council Member Green believed some type of plan needed to be in place.

              • Other: Non-Departmental, debt, transfers out
                Ms. Steck explained the total change of 2% in the Non-Departmental Budget from
                the prior year. She suggested expansion of the Employee Downpayment Assistance
                Program in the budget from a possible 10 participants to 16. Council Member Green
                asked if the Historical Society had submitted a formal budget request. Responding
                to a question from Council Member Shelton, City Administrator Korban Lee spoke of
                benefits of the Employee Downpayment Assistance Program. Council Member
                Bloom expressed support for the program.

               Ms. Steck made the Council aware that FY 2025 was the last year for the existing
               general obligation (GEO) bond.

              • Administrative Services
                Ms. Steck explained the request for a full-time Accountant II position, and a part-time
                Passport Agent. Mr. Lee spoke of a plan to audit software used across all
                departments. Ms. Steck intended to leave the Accountant II position vacant for six
                months and look further into efficiencies that may be possible with software.

              • Internal Service Funds (IT Management Fund and Benefits Management Fund)
                Staff answered questions from the Council about laptop replacement. Council
                Member Green suggested starting a reserve fund for IT replacement similar to the
                Fleet Reserve Fund. The Council and staff discussed employee use of personal cell
                phones versus City cell phones for City business. Mr. Lee suggested adding cell phone
                allowance to the list of line items for Council consideration. A member of staff
                expressed confidence that the cell phone matter could be figured out internally
                without having to add it to the budget.

              • Community Development Services Fund
                Ms. Steck explained the Development Services Fund would need to be subsidized by
                $2.5 million in FY 2025 ($890,983 from Reserves and $1,123,305 from General
                Fund). Community Development Director Scott Langford answered questions from
                the Council. Council Member Green suggested moving responsibility of chicken
                licensing from the business license division to animal control.

               Council Member Shelton noted that no new employees were requested for Code
               Enforcement, and said he felt the City had been unable to respond to code
               enforcement requests in a timely manner. Mr. Langford said the City had three full-
               time code enforcement officers who were extremely busy and were the best he had
               ever seen. Council Member Shelton expressed the opinion that the service level was

                 not where it needed to be for residents. He believed it was easier for criminals to
                 blend in when neighborhoods were trashy and expressed a desire to add a code
                 enforcement officer in FY 2026 if not in FY 2025. Chair Jacob suggested adding
                 another code enforcement officer to the list of line items for Council consideration.
                 The Council and staff discussed deadlines for truth in taxation and adoption of a
                 tentative budget.

               • Public Works Department
                 Public Works Director Brian Clegg and Ms. Steck answered questions from the
                 Council regarding Public Works budgets. Ms. Steck commented that the City did not
                 have an ongoing revenue source for road maintenance. Mr. Lee said the increase in
                 construction costs coupled with a decline in the pavement condition index would
                 result in a perfect storm of awful conditions for maintaining roads. He added that
                 the City may potentially face hundreds of millions of dollars in unfunded
                 maintenance liabilities if a way was not found in the next few years to put more
                 money toward surface maintenance. A member of staff commented that the State
                 was feeling the same pressure. Council Member Green suggested the utility transfer
                 should be dedicated to roads. Mr. Lee reported that staff wanted to come back with
                 a long-term strategy for consideration in the next few months.

                 The Council and staff discussed damage caused by park strip trees and the cost of
                 repair. Staff answered questions about planned street/intersection projects. Ms.
                 Steck proposed scheduling an additional work session for budget discussion prior to
                 adoption of the Tentative Budget.


 3. ADJOURN
     Council Member Whitelock moved to adjourn the meeting. Council Member Green
     seconded the motion, which passed by unanimous vote (7-0).

     The meeting adjourned at 10:13 pm.
