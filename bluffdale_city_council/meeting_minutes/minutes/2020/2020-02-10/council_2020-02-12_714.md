4.     Presentation, Consideration, and Vote on a Resolution of the Bluffdale City Council
       Adopting an Emergency Operations Plan (EOP), Staff Presenter, Natalie Hall.

Emergency Preparedness Manager, Natalie Hall provided the 2020 Emergency Operations Plan
("EOP") and stated that the plan must be approved annually. It also must be reviewed and
approved any time more than 50% of the City Counci l membership changes.

Ms. Hall provided an overview of the plan and highlighted important sections. She noted that
Section I outlines the five phases of emergency management which include prevention,
preparedness, response, recove1y, and mitigation. The section also describes the Incident
Command System, which is a nationwide system used for emergency response by both government
and private organizations to ensure that everyone is working from the same plan to maintain
consistency regardless of their location in the count1y .

Ms. Hall noted that all emergencies in the City begin and end locally, although the City coordinates
with County, State, and Federal governments. She also identified the types of emergencies listed
in Section 3 such as hazards, natural causes, and human causes, and discussed what type of
emergencies would be included in each catego1y. She mentioned that the City is required to write
a Hazard Mitigation Plan every 25 years that must be approved by FEMA and the City Council.

Mayor Timothy repo11ed that he attended a FEMA training and explained that there are specific
steps that must be followed in order for the City to get reimbursed for damages in the event of an
emergency or disaster. Ms. Hall planned to outline those steps and added that documentation is
the main component.

Ms. Hall noted that the City has an Emergency Communications Center ("ECC") as outlined in
Section 4 that is located in the Community Room of City Hall. She noted that the room is a secure
location that only approved staff can access it. She explained that this is where emergencies are
coordinated.

Ms. Hall provided an overview of the section on declaring an emergency and noted that the timing
of making a declaration is very important. Once an emergency is declared, the City becomes
eligible for funding. If the City does not declare an emergency, it will not be eli gible for funding.
Ms. Hall added that the declaration is made in the form of a resolution.

Ms. Hall explained that there are different levels of emergencies in the ECC and the ECC may
initially be opened to monitor the situation after which the levels and number of staff may increase
if the situation escalates. There are 15 Emergency Support Functions (ESF) detailed in the back
of the manual. Each ESF will have a desk in the ECC and a person supporting each function.

Ms. Hall highlighted the organizational chart that shows how the ECC would potentially be
organized as well as the steps for reporting and notifi cation. Eve1ything must be documented
