Public Comment (7:34)

Chair Handley read public comment preamble and opened the public comment period.

Bonnie Schiffler-Olsen, of Provo, shared her believe that Chapter 9.18 of city code threatens the well-
being of the unhoused. She also discussed how rousing the unhoused compromises their safety. She
petitioned for Mayor Kaufusi to proclaim an emergency moratorium to the anti-camping ordinance until
temperatures improve. She also urged council to establish an emergency shelter.

Gary Lindley, of Provo, discussed accident involving his wife that occurred on November 15. He
requested the city consider changing the light at the intersection where the accident occurred.

Ryder Nielson, of Provo, agreed with Mr. Lindley and shared a few instances where he had almost been
hit at the same intersection.

Kendall Clark, of Provo, asked that crosswalks and traffic patterns are looked at to prevent further
accidents. He also discussed the need for equipment to clean sanitation receptacles.

Cameron Evans, of Provo, said that his heart hurts for the unhoused. He shared his concern for when the
unhoused population are arrested for camping, they have no way to keep their possessions.

Heather Holland, of Provo, shared her support for the unhoused people. She discussed how it costs
much less to shelter and care for people instead of criminalizing them.

6.         A resolution of the Provo City Municipal Council outlining their support for adoption of the
           2022 recommendations of Utah's Uniform Building Code Commission regarding building
           energy efficiency. (23-010) (51:48)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-2, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Melia Dayley, Council Analyst, shared this item was discussed in the work session. She explained that
every six years, the building codes are evaluated and that this resolution shows support for the changes.

Don Jarvis, Sustainability Coordinator, answered questions that were raised during the work session.

Councilor Handley explained the importance of building codes are as they relate to air quality. He asked
for support of the council.

Chair MacKay opened item for public comment.

Eileen Miller, of the Utah Valley Home Builders Association, said that there are several amendments that
they have submitted to the legislation. She explained that the proposed changes will increase the cost of
housing making affordable homes even more isolate. She provided council with the amendments.

Chair MacKay closed public comment and brought the discussion back to council.

Councilor Fillmore said he believes the council was only presented one side of this and requested that in
the future, more discussion on items prior to taking a stance.

Chair MacKay invited Bill Peperone, Director of Development Services, to discuss his opinion on the
changes.

Mr. Peperone said he is disappointed that he was not invited to this discussion.

Councilor Fillmore asked Mr. Peperone questions around his concerns for these changes.

Mr. Peperone addressed his concerns about possible increases in impact fees.

Councilor Handley apologized for not including Mr. Peperone in discussions. He expressed if there is not
unanimous support, then he would propose to withdraw the item. He asked for feedback from the other
council members.

Councilor Shipley discussed the importance of reevaluating codes as things change over time. He said he
is in favor of the last paragraph of the resolution. He mentioned he is more comfortable with the
wording that is less specific.

Councilor Handley agreed.

Vice-Chair Whipple said she appreciates sending a signal to legislature while allowing latitude for minor
changing. She explained why she is in support of more energy efficient homes.

Chair MacKay stated that it is hard to not hear both sides of the story. She also discussed the importance
of updating codes as things are constantly changing.

Councilor Ellsworth and Councilor Fillmore discussed their issues with the process.

Councilor Hoban also expressed his discomfort with the process.

     Motion:      Councilor Handley made a motion to withdraw the Resolution. Councilor Shipley
                  seconded.

7.         An ordinance adopting the Provo City General Plan and appendices. Citywide application.
           (PLGPA20210364) (1:24:29)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-1, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Brandon Larsen, City Planner, presented the additions and changes to the General Plan. He asked if Mr.
Jones needed clarification on which appendices were included in the ordinance.

Mr. Jones suggested that all the appendices be included in the ordinance.

Councilor Fillmore asked who the directive came from. After Mr. Larsen answered, he said it seems like
the requestor is asking for something very prescriptive instead of general.

Mayor Kaufusi provided some information regarding the pedestrian bridge.

Chair MacKay opened public comment. With none, she closed public comment.

Councilor Handley expressed his support for passing this ordinance.

Chair MacKay and Councilor Fillmore echoed Councilor Handley’s desire to pass the ordinance.

Mr. Jones shared two courses that the council could take.

Vice-Chair Whipple said she would like to pass the ordinance tonight. She added that more clarification
could be sought.

      Motion:      Chair MacKay made motion to amend ordinance to include all appendices. Councilor
                   Fillmore seconded.

      Vote:     The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Hoban,
                Mackay, Shipley, and Whipple in favor.

Chair MacKay called for a 10-minute recess.

With no objections, the Provo Municipal Council recessed to allow for staff to amend the ordinance for
council to vote on.

Mr. Jones shared the changes made to the ordinance.

Councilor Handley called for a vote.
