        2.2    Discussion Relating to Truck Parking and Trucking Routes. (Council Member
               Wentfv Astou).

Mr. C rowell repmied that staff was asked to lead a discussion on ordinance options regarding iarge
commercial trucks in residential zones. Concerns had been expressed by residents and the Council.
It was noted that larger trucks are of concern. The current home occupation limit without exception
is the Class l to 3 which is generally up to an F-350 size. If a special exception is granted for a
home occupation, an applicant can go to 19,500. A special exception can be requested with a home
occupation and sets an expectation regarding the potential impact. The existing Truck Route
Ordinance begins restricting routes at 26,000 pounds, which is Class 7. Smaller trucks, such as a
Class 4, sti ll allow for cargo to be hauled and for someone to effectively operate their business.
Heavier trucks have a greater impact on City infrastructure, which was why they are limited to
ce1iain routes.

Mr. Crowell referenced recreational vehicles that should be addressed when crafting an ord inance.
Issues with large trucks include noise, vibration, exhaust, air quality, and impeding traffic. Staff
had had issues with the fo llowing:

    •   Businesses operating without a license; and
    •   Inconsistencies with recently adopted secti ons of the Home Occupation Code.

Mr. Crowell stated that there must be evidence to proceed with any cases. There were concerns
that Bluffdale does not support overregulation. Many moved to Bluffdale to do business from their
homes, and they have a history of allowing semi-commercial uses on large residential properties.
Others are not operating a business with a negative impact who are independent contractors. He
noted that this cou ld perhaps have an unintended consequence. Possible solutions were di scussed.
The simpl est would be to address truck routes and adjust the map but not deal with the take-home
policy addressed in the zoning ordinance and business license.

Currently, the ordinance allows heavy truck traffic to use Heritage Crest Way to Porter Rockwell
Boulevard. That was considered a potential technical change. Another option was to create

Wendy Aston did not object to Vernal City ' s Code, which prohibits large trucks altogether. She

Mr. Crowell stated that there have been complaints about the storage of large numbers of RVs on
prope11ies. One pa11icular case was being settled. Four RVs was determined to be a fair number

advertising RVs as rentals online. He was aware of others who have multiple RVs on their
property.

Dave Kallas found it to be troubling since many moved to Bluffdale to be able to bring a work
vehicle home with them. As the City grows, however, it is becoming problematic. He felt it should
be allowed relative to a home occupation. He compared someone who brings a vehicle home from
work at the end of the day to a business that operates out of a residence. He suggested that it match
the Use Table that was created a few years ago. Many people drive semi-trucks and park them at
their homes. For a home-based business, he did not object to allowing Class 4 vehicles. It should
be allowed for someone who is a truck driver for hire if the access is on the street and engineered
for that type of weight. It should be clear that only one vehicle is allowed and not the storage of
multiple vehicles, which has been the case. He did not support a commercial trucking business
being operated from a home. Mayor Hall pointed out that the Use Table does not allow for a
trucking business, but it was acknowledged that it is still happening. Council Member Kallas
stated that the truck must be able to be parked on the property and would need to front a road that
is designed for heavy vehicles.

         5.1    Presentation of the Annual Report of the Courts. (Judge Scott Mickelsen).

Judge Scott Mickelsen updated the Council on the Court and what has taken place over the past
year. He shared his background and reported that he is one of few non-attorney justices in Salt
Lake County. He was hired 10 years ago and at that time there was no requirement for an attorney
judge. The law has since changed and requires justices to be an attorney for a first- or second-
class county. He has a master's degree in Crimina l Justice. Before working for Bluffdale C ity, he
worked as a law enforcement officer for 37 years before retiring as a Captain from the Unified
Police Department ("UPD"). He handled budgets of roughly $11 million for hi s precinct, served
as a Hostage Negotiator, and handled special proj ects.

Judge Mickelsen reported that in 202 1 there were 2,209 cases filed in Cowt. Nearly all of those
cases required more than one hearing. That was a substantial increase from 2020 when there were
1,347 cases filed. Court is held one-half day per week, but they often work two days. Of the 2021
cases, there were 2,040 traffic citations, 172 misdemeanors, 23 DUis, and 13 parking viol ations.
During the year they hired a new pa1t -time C lerk.

The Administrative Office of the Courts ("AOC'') requires Clerks to become certified . To
accomplish that, they are required to attend fo ur major blocks of training. The Supreme Court has
