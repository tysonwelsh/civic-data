       4.1 Consideration of Single-Family Dwelling Evaluations in POD 2 of the Hidden Oaks Subdivision
           located generally at 6973 W 13090 South in the R-1-15 Residential Single Family Zone
           Applicant: Dan Milar and Joshua Petit (authorized agents)
           Acres: ±7.82
           File No: P2023-051
November 15, 2023 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                              Page 3 of 10
Planner II Howa presented the request for item 4.1, which involved the consideration of single-family
dwelling elevations for twenty-four residential lots in POD 2 of the Hidden Oaks MDA. The applicant also
submitted an Architectural Review Committee (ARC) approval letter for all proposed building elevations
as required in the Design Guidelines. The design requirements were assessed based on the city code,
MDA guidelines, and vested design standards from 2018.

The elevations presented met the standards, except for some homes not meeting the 40% brick/stone
requirement. It was noted that this could be addressed during the building plan submission phase by
adding compensating features. It was clarified that these requirements applied only to areas within an
MDA with designated design standards. Questions were raised about the variety of house styles, and
applicant representative Laurel Bevan clarified that these were base models, and customizations
prevented duplicate home styles within close proximity.

Commissioner Rypien moved to approve item 4.1 Consideration of a Single-Family Dwelling Evaluations in
POD 2 of the Hidden Oaks Subdivision located generally at 6973 W 13090 South in the R-1-15 Residential
Single Family Zone with the following staff recommendations:
    1. Receive and agree to all remaining corrections from City departments.
    2. All building elevations meet the required forty percent (40%) brick/stone requirement on all street
        frontages (as vested under the MDA).
    3. The “Emily Base Plan” (by Gordon Milar) must be modified to meet the minimum 5:12 roof pitch
        requirement or be removed from the list of optional building plans for Hidden Oaks POD 2.
Commissioner Garcia seconded the motion.

Planning Manager Spencer presented item 4.2, consideration of a phasing plan for the Mardam ranch
subdivision. He mentioned that the subdivision had received an extension last year to record within a two-
year period ending on August 18, 2024. The current discussion revolved around the approved plat and the
proposed phasing plan. The applicant intended to divide the subdivision into three phases, with the first
phase commencing soon after the property's closure, followed by phases one and two either
simultaneously or within a few months of each other. Phase three will be done when the market gets
better. Staff recommends approval with conditions.

The applicant representative, Austin Richards, confirmed the accuracy of the proposed timelines. Their
goal was to close on the property mid-next year.

The discussion touched on the engineering aspects, such as the retention parcel and stormwater
considerations.

Subdivision comprised of forty-four (44) single-family lots at approximately 12914 S 6100 West in the R-1-

   1. All conditions of approval for the original preliminary subdivision application on August 16, 2018,

       4.3 Consideration of a Conditional Use Permit to develop a commercial site plain in the Herriman
           Office Plaza, consisting of a professional office building and a stand-alone multi-tenant
           building located in the Sommer Sports Subdivision, located at 14114 S Rosecrest Road in the
           C-2 Commercial Zone
           Applicant: Greg Steffensen (authorized agent)
           Acres: ±1.36
           File No: C2023-103

Planner Howa presented the request for item 4.3, which involved the consideration of a conditional use
permit for a commercial site plan in the Herriman Office Plaza. The proposed development was a multi-use
project located off Rosecrest and Mirabella, specifically Lot Two of the Sommer Sports Complex. The plan
included a 14,000 sq ft office pad with a building standing 32 feet tall. The top level was designated for
office and retail space, while the lower level would serve as dedicated parking with 27 underground parking
stalls, and 52 ground level spaces. Additionally, there was a 2,600 sq ft retail pad with a drive-thru for fast
food or retail. There were discussions about parking concerns and the existing demand due to the nearby
sports complex. The proposed development aimed to alleviate some of the parking issues by providing 79
stalls, which exceeded the required 70. Staff recommends approval with conditions.

The applicant presented elevations and renderings of the project, showcasing the use of brick, stucco, and
dark bronze materials, exceeding 60% on all elevations. Landscaping plans met minimum requirements,
but there were suggestions for adjustments in specific areas.

Commission members raised questions about pedestrian safety, sidewalk placement, and the possibility of
a crosswalk. Applicant Greg Steffensen addressed these concerns, explained the planned pedestrian flow
and emphasized the secure entry with a keycard access roll-up gate.

       4.5 Consideration of a Site Plan for Lot 2 of the J & J Commercial Subdivision located at 5652 W
           13100 South in the C-2 Commercial Zone
           Applicant: Alexander Winder (authorized agent)
           Acres: ±0.84
           File No: P2023-115

Planning manager Spencer presented the request for 4.5, which focused on the consideration of the site
plan for lot two of a recently approved subdivision. The proposed project involved a building of just over
3,000 square feet, potentially accommodating three tenants. The structure was planned to be a shell finish,
standing at just over 22 feet tall, with brick and metal siding as the proposed materials.

City staff provided an overview of the project, emphasizing that while the building looked great, the metal
siding proposed did not meet the current zoning standards. They recommended a change to an approved
material, such as cement board or stucco. Additionally, concerns were raised about the percentage of brick
coverage on the rear elevation, falling slightly short of the required 60%. Other points of discussion
included the placement of pergolas for outside dining in relation to windows, the absence of dumpster
elevations, and the dedication of park strips and sidewalks.

The Planning Commission engaged in a detailed discussion, with specific focus on the proposed metal
siding. Questions were raised about the gauge of the metal and the ongoing revisions to the related
ordinance. It was clarified that the metal proposed did not qualify for a variance. The possibility of waiting
for the ordinance to be codified or proceeding with an alternative material was suggested.

Concerns were also raised about the elevation of the building and its potential impact on headlights for
southbound traffic on Rose Crest Road. The applicant Alex Winder was questioned about the reason for
raising the lot by four feet, and the commission expressed a preference for minimizing the elevation
difference to avoid potential issues with the sidewalk's ADA compliance. The commission also addressed
concerns about the building's elevation, its potential impact on traffic visibility, and the need for additional
planting or a hedge to mitigate headlight disruption.
