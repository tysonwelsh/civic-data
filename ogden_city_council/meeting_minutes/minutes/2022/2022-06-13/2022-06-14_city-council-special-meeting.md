Proposed Ordinance 2022-27 amending the budget for Fiscal Year July 1,
2021 to June 30, 2022 in the amount of $2,082,350 for Swift Building
expenditures that were not anticipated

A memo from the City Comptroller regarding a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022 budget amendment came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated Administration is proposing an amendment to the FY2022 budget to recognize and
appropriate funds from Business Depot Ogden Retained Earnings ($2,082,350) for transfer to the Redevelopment Agency
($2,082,350) to cover additional environmental remediation costs at the Swift Building. The discovery ofa leaking underground
storage tank that has the potential to contaminate the river requires unanticipated site work. The cost of the Environmental
Protection Agency (EPA) remediation efforts is not included in this budget amendment.

Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and stated the proposed budget amendment will bring the total amount of
appropriations to the project to $6,398,325.

Chair Nadolski inquired as to the implications for the City if the budget amendment were not approved. Community and
Economic Development Director Cooper stated that failure to fund the environmental remediation would leave the Ogden River in
a precarious situation; the worse of the clean-up efforts and costs have been satisfied, but ongoing work is needed and failure to
complete the project would render previous work meaningless and obsolete.

Minutes of Special Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah, June 14, 2022

The Chair then called for a public hearing on proposed Ordinance 2022-27, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2021 to June 30, 2022 by
increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of $2,082,350 from sources as detailed in the body of
this ordinance; and increasing the appropriations for a gross increase of $2,082,350 as detailed in the body of
this ordinance; and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
passage.”

The City Recorder presented written notice of said hearing and affidavit of posting on the 3rd day of June 2022
specifying the time and place of this meeting as the time and place when and where the proposed ordinance would be given a
public hearing and be considered for final passage.

Angel Castillo stated that while she agrees that the Council is in a position where it must approve this budget amendment
and complete the environmental remediation project, she would like for the Council and Administration to examine the process
that led to this situation and make sure that procedures are put in place to ensure something similar does not happen again in the
future.

Nicholos Otillo stated he served in the military and one of his assignments was to manage projects for the government;
this included the cost, schedule, and performance of certain projects. At the beginning of every project, he knew his budget and
when money would be spent; the difference between a project and ongoing operations is that a project has a clearly defined scope
and end date. His question is when the spending on this project will come to an end and when the residents of the City will know
if costs and schedules have been exceeded. Also, it is important to identify a point at which a project will be shut down if it
exceeds costs and schedules. The Council has the authority to stop a project if it is mismanaged or not clearly defined from the
beginning.

Heath Satow echoed Ms. Castillo’s comments; he spoke to the Fire Department’s inability to respond to fires at the Swift
building. There were two fires in the past and he wondered if the costs the City is dealing with now would be lower if the matter
would have been addressed sooner before the building deteriorated to the condition that created the environmental conditions. He
stated the City’s initial estimate for the cleanup was $500,000, though the EPA estimated $2 million. It turns out that all parties
greatly underestimated the costs.

There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR, ALL VOTING AYE.

Council member Hyer stated he is still hopeful that it will be possible to seek recourse and reimbursement for some of the
costs from the previous owners of the property. He asked if City staff is still exploring those opportunities. City Attorney Williams
stated Administration is still working on that matter, but negotiations are complicated and may involve litigation. Council member
Hyer stated he understands the difficulty of the negotiations and noted that regardless of that issue, he believes the cleanup needed
to happen.

Chair Nadolski asked if there is a timeline for a decision on the reimbursement issue. Mr. Williams stated that he is
hopeful that there will be resolution sometime next year.

Council member Richey stated it would be helpful for the Council to have information on ongoing cleanup needs and
costs for future planning purposes. Mr. Cooper stated that future work will be needed to achieve a full certificate of completion
from the State of Utah for the site. He stated there is another property in the area that needed environmental cleanup and it took
the City 18 months to complete that work; however, he feels that the Swift site is much more contaminated and involved. It is
difficult to project the amount of time and money it will take to achieve compliance because it is subjective and incremental
depending upon the property and the contaminants in the soil. He stated he has tried to predict the total budget for the cleanup
efforts, but the work has been much more involved than anticipated and he was unable to accurately predict the scope.

ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA,

Proposed Ordinance 2022-29 amending the budget for Fiscal Year July 1,
2021, to June 30, 2022, in the amount of $4,000,000 for the Marshall White
Center design

A memo from the City Comptroller regarding a Fiscal Year (FY) 2022 budget amendment came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated Administration is proposing an amendment to the budget to recognize and appropriate funds
from General Fund Fund Balance ($2,000,000) for transfer to the Capital Improvement Fund CIP project RG003 ($2,000,000) to
cover design costs for the new Marshall White Facility. Funding for demolition, site work, and construction will be appropriated
at a later date.

Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and stated the proposed FY 2023-2027 CIP includes an updated project brief for
the Marshall White Facility. A budget of $23 million has been recommended by the Administration with funding sources as
follows:

e ARPA Funds $ 5,000,000
Donations $ 1,500,000
e RAMP Grant $ 1,500,000
e Bond Proceeds $15,000,000
o =©TOTAL $23,000,000.

Administration is recommending the budget amendment to appropriate funds to the CIP fund for project RG003.

The Chair then called for a public hearing on proposed Ordinance 2022-29, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City amending the budget for the Fiscal Year July 1, 2021, to June 30, 2022, by
increasing the anticipated revenues for a gross increase of $4,000,000 from sources as detailed in the body of
this ordinance; and increasing the appropriations for a gross increase of $4,000,000 as detailed in the body of
this ordinance; and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
passage.”

The City Recorder presented written notice of said hearing and affidavit of posting on the 3rd day of June 2022
specifying the time and place of this meeting as the time and place when and where the proposed ordinance would be given a
public hearing and be considered for final passage.

Jason Reyneveld expressed disagreement with spending this kind of money in the current financial climate; it is difficult
to predict project costs given inflation rates and the poor commodities market. He stated other budget demands are very pressing
and this project should be delayed until there is more certainty related to economic conditions that impact this type of endeavor.

Minutes of Special Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah, June 14, 2022

Angel Castillo stated she is very excited to be at this point after a great deal of time discussing this project. She noted that
the person who made comments before her clearly does not understand the manner in which American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA)
funds are managed and she fully supports the use of ARPA funds for this type of project. She also believes the Council should
look into new market tax credits rather than issuing a bond for this project. With new market tax credits, an agency could purchase
the credit and issue a loan at zero percent interest or below market rate interest, which are two tax indicators that influence
projects like this. Additionally, the City’s bond rating will be preserved.

Betty Sawyer expressed support for this budget amendment, and she views it as the first step in securing financing for the
project moving forward. She does not believe that construction costs will be decreasing any time in the future and now is the time
to move forward.

Matt Nicholas stated he does not know a great deal about the project, except that the total project cost could be upwards
of $23 million; it seems that the taxpayers are being asked to sign another blank check for a project without a known scope. This
seems to be an irresponsible decision from a financial perspective.

Heath Satow stated he is happy to see some movement on this project, but he does have some concerns about the
information gathered by the consulting firm that has done work on the project. They were supposed to gather demographic
information to inform the project and their failure to do that means they did not fulfill an important component of the request for
proposal (RFP). He has tried to find out who was accountable for the RFP process and selection of the firm, but he has been
unable to find good information about the selection committee and firms that responded. He does hope VCBO Architects will doa
great job, but it is important for information about the process to be clear and transparent.

Alisha Washington thanked the Marshall White Advisory Committee for their efforts and for communicating with City
Administration and the Council regarding their findings and community input about the project. This is a project desperately
needed by the community and now is the time to move forward.

There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER WHITE, ALL VOTING AYE.

Council member White state that the action is to move $4 million within the budget, but only $1.5 million is needed at
this time. She asked if there is a reason to move the entire $4 million at this point in time. Ms. Stout stated the action is actually to
move $2 million and this is based upon a percentage of the total project. She noted that $2 million will be moved from the General
Fund and there will be a $2 million allocation to the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) fund.

Public Comments

Wilson Tolman stated that he would like to talk about renewable energy; it is important to be visionary in planning and
preparing. He spoke to the geographical issues the community faces regarding developing renewable energy sources, but one
option Weber County, Ogden City, and the State of Utah can embrace is public transit.

Kerry Wayne addressed Mayor Caldwell, noting he has sent several emails asking the City to address the dust that is
being created at the project on the western edge of Business Depot Ogden (BDO), which is a great nuisance for Marriott-
Slaterville residents living near the facility. He stated something needs to be done as the City should not be immune from abiding
by its own ordinances. He provided photographs of the current dust conditions and noted that Ogden City, The Boyer Company,
and Marriott-Slaterville are not responding. There is no reason that Ogden City cannot be a good neighbor to the Marriott-
Slaterville residents. The residents are looking for a schedule of when Ogden City officials will meet with them and address the
problems; he no longer knows who to call about the problems in the area, but Ogden City needs to be responsible for their
property and cannot hide behind The Boyer Company. He stated The Boyer Company is installing landscaping in the area, but
they are not using dust control methods. The residents have been waiting 16 months without any action from Ogden City.

Angel Castillo discussed ongoing drought conditions that are causing dry conditions on the hillsides surrounding the City
and she suggested the City make decisions early regarding fireworks restrictions so that vendors are aware of any restrictions
before they spend money on fireworks that they may not be able to sell. She then stated she was shocked to hear comments about
the Ogden Airport about the time spent mowing grass at the facility, especially during a drought. She asked why the City is
spending money mowing grass when there are other options for using artificial turf or grass that is slow growing and drought
tolerant. She then discussed the average home cost in Ogden City and stated this is an indictor that the City is not making effective
efforts to promote housing affordability in the community.

Jason Reyneveld stated his comments are directed towards activities of the City’s Redevelopment Agency (RDA); he
noted he does not believe the City should be in the business of real estate development. Rather, the City should be focused on
enforcing laws, managing public utilities, and providing public safety. As a small real estate developer who has dedicated his
entire life savings to acquiring land and building quality housing in Central Ogden, he has found it difficult to compete with the
City and has noticed that private investors are not getting involved. He stated that Ogden City gives itself full benefits through
bureaucratic processes that make it difficult or impossible for private developers to compete. The RDA buys land over the term of
several years in order to assemble a large parcel that can be redeveloped. He stated there are many people who love this town and
need to be given the ability to pursue redevelopment or new development that is not influenced by the RDA.

Vice Chair Lopez asked Mr. Reyneveld ifhe has spoken to anyone in City Administration. Mr. Reyneveld answered no
and indicated the only City representative he has spoken with about his concerns is Council member Richey.

Heath Satow stated that last week during the City Council meeting the Mayor called him a liar rather than publicly
answering the questions Mr. Satow asked him. All he is asking the City government to do is to make a public statement as to why
the Brown’s Ice Cream project is ‘such a mess’. He read several statements that he declared to be fact about the project, relative to
the marketing of the property, the developer that was selected for the project, the multiple false statements provided by City
Administration regarding the project, the misstated value of the building, control over the project, the relationship between the
City and the broker for the project, and the information that has been hidden from the Council. He stated that he is not claiming
that anything illegal has happened because he knows that all actions that have been taken are legal under the laws governing
redevelopment agencies. He is simply saying that he feels that greater transparency and accountability to taxpayers is needed. He
asked that the Council and Mayor acknowledge the errors and move forward with developing policies to ensure something similar
does not happen again.
