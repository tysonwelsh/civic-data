West Valley City
An ordinance change to add restrictions on the use of lawn in landscaping


At the request of the City Council, City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to add
restrictions on the use of lawn in new residential development.

Earlier this year the State legislature appropriated $5 million for a statewide grass removal rebate
program as part of HB 121. Applicants may receive up to $1 per square foot for replacing lawn
with drought-resistant landscaping. This program will be launched in the spring of 2023 through
the website UtahWaterSavers.com. According to the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District,
this new program can be combined with existing lawn replacement programs so that applicants
can receive up to $2 per square foot for replacing lawn.

To be eligible for the program, a project must be located within a county or municipality with the
following lawn limitations on new residential development:
      No lawn on parking strips or areas less than eight feet in width in new residential
       development.
      No more than 35% of the front and side yard landscaped area in new residential
       development is lawn.

The City Council wants to adopt these standards so that property owners within the City will be
eligible for the new lawn replacement funds. While the City already has a regulation prohibiting
lawn in parking strips and areas less than eight feet in width, this standard only applies to
developer installed landscaping.
There are three locations within the City where lawn or artificial turf are required in at least 50%
of the 15 foot wide parking strip space. These three locations are along Redwood Road, 3500
South between Redwood Road and the Jordan River and 5600 West between 2100 South and
3100 South. If this ordinance is approved, artificial turf would be required for new residential
projects in these locations.

ZT-7-2022
Cabco 5600 LLC
An amendment to Section 7-14-404 of the zoning ordinance to change the location of the
restrictions on automotive sales, automobile service, and automotive parts sales from
between 2455 South and 3100 South to between the Riter Canal and 3100 South

Bob Furstenau with Cabco 5600 LLC has requested an ordinance amendment to change the
location of the restrictions in the 5600 West Overlay Zone on automotive sales, automobile
service, and automotive parts sales from between 2455 South and 3100 South to between the
Riter Canal and 3100 South. While the 5600 West Overlay Zone extends from SR-201 to 3100
South, the restrictions on automotive sales, automobile service, and automotive parts sales only
apply between 2455 South and 3100 South.

Mr. Furstenau owns the property directly north of the Riter Canal on the west side of 5600 West.
His property is currently impacted by the 5600 West Overlay Zone restriction on automotive
uses and would like the option of pursuing an automotive use.

Mr. Furstenau provided the following justification for the proposed change:

       “The Riter Canal is a natural boundary between the retail section south of the canal
       versus the industrial/manufacturing uses north of the canal. There are approximately 87
       existing retail/restaurant/service businesses south of the Riter Canal that meet the
       requirements of the retail overlay zone. There are 5 existing businesses north of the canal
       none of which are retail.”

In support of his justification, Mr. Furstenau provided a list of businesses located north of the
Riter Canal and businesses located south of the Riter Canal. He also provided a map of the area
that shows the location of many of the businesses on the list. The list and map are included with
this report.
The 5600 West Overlay Zone, which covers 250’ of property on both sides of 5600 West, was
first adopted in 2004. At the time of adoption, much of this portion of 5600 West was
undeveloped. In staff’s view, the Overlay Zone has been important tool in shaping the
development that has occurred along this section of 5600 West.

With that said, staff tends to agree with the applicant that the Riter Canal seems to be a good
transition between retail/commercial uses to the south and industrial/manufacturing uses to the
north. In staff’s view, automotive sales, automobile service, and automotive parts sales would
not be out of place with the industrial/manufacturing uses located north of the Riter Canal.

Z-11-2022
West Valley City
Lake Park Corporate Centre
Zone change from M to BRP
384.8 acres

West Valley City has submitted a zone change application for 384.8 acres of property located
within the Lake Park Corporate Centre. The current zone is M (Manufacturing) and the proposed
zone is BRP (Business/Research Park). The current General Plan designation is Business Park.
Of the 384.8 acres proposed for rezoning, 240.1 acres are part of the Stonebridge golf course and
are owned by the City.
Staff is proposing this zone change for several reasons that are underlined and covered below.
The General Plan designation within Lake Park Corporate Centre for the areas outside of the golf
course is Business Park.


   The General Plan includes the following description for the Business Park designation:
   “Business Park areas on the General Plan Map illustrate the districts where very large office
   uses are grouped together and surrounded by open space, which provides a better transition
   to nearby residential neighborhoods.” In staff’s view, the BRP zone is more closely aligned
   with the Business Park description in the General Plan in that the BRP zone is much more
   restrictive with land use than the M zone and requires a higher percentage (15%) of
   landscaping than the M zone (5%). As examples of these use restrictions, the BRP zone
   prohibits uses like heavy truck sales and service, heavy industrial, outside storage, self-
   storage facilities, towing and impound yards, truck transfer companies, and vehicle recycling
   facilities while the M zone allows these uses.
A portion of the Lake Park Corporate Centre is already zoned BRP.
   Of the 580 acres within Lake Park Corporate Centre, 195 acres (34%) is already zoned BRP.
   Excluding the golf course parcels owned by the City, there are 7 parcels that are partially
   zoned M and partially zoned BRP. This creates confusion for lenders and developers when
   trying to finance and construct a project.
The Lake Park Corporate Centre is largely developed as an office park.
   Most of the uses within Lake Park are large office buildings. Examples of businesses within
   Lake Park include Deseret First Credit Union Corporate Office, Discover Financial Services,
   Intermountain Health Care, Rocky Mountain Power, and Utah Housing Corporation.

Suburban Land Reserve (SLR) is no longer involved in the governing board of Lake Park
Corporate Centre.

   The master declaration of easements, covenants, and restrictions for Lake Park Corporate
   Centre includes the following language:

       “All Parcels shall be used exclusively for commercial purposes primarily as offices, but
       shall include uses which are an integral part of the business of the Owner or Occupany of
       a Parcel and which are located in Buildings constructed as required by the Design
       Standards and used in a manner consistent with Declarant’s intention that the Project be
       developed and used as a corporate business center. Subject to applicable zoning
       ordinances, commercial use shall specifically include governmental, professional or
       business offices, banks or financial institutions, research and development facilities, retail
       sales, hotels and medical facilities. So long as Declarant shall remain a Class “C”
       Member, Declarant shall, in its discretion, determine if an intended use is an appropriate
       use within a corporate business center. At such time as Declarant shall cease to be a Class
       “C” Member, the Board shall, in its discretion, determine if an intended use is an
       appropriate use within a corporate business center.”

   SLR was the successor of the original declarant of Beneficial Development Company.
   However, now that SLR has recently sold its remaining land holdings within Lake Park,
   decisions about the appropriateness of uses within Lake Park as well as potential changes to
   the use restrictions in the master declaration are now up to the board that is comprised of the
   landowners.

The Council recently rezoned a parcel within Lake Park that was partially zoned M to BRP.

   At the request of the property owner, the City Council rezoned the property at 4625 W Lake
   Park Blvd. from M to BRP in June of 2022.

There is significantly more land within the City zoned M than BRP.

  There are over 8,000 acres of property within the City zoned M; however, there are only 295
  acres of property zoned BRP. In staff’s view, there are plenty of opportunities to develop the
  uses that are allowed in the M zone but not the BRP zone in areas outside of Lake Park
  Corporate Centre and retain the atmosphere that exists in Lake Park.

Only one of the 12 landowners that would be affected by this application expressed concerns
about the change.

  During the discussion on the zone change application mentioned above, the Council asked
  staff to explore the possibility of rezoning the M zoned portions of Lake Park to BRP. Staff
  sent letters to all 12 property owners of the 18 parcels whose zoning is all M or partially M to
  solicit input on the possibility of a rezone to BRP. Two of the owners responded and only one
  of those had objections to BRP zoning. The opposing entity, Lake Park Project, LLC owns the
  Home 2 Suites and the two adjoining vacant parcels. Taylor Keys with Lake Park Project,
  LLC provided the following concerns about this application:

       “The primary concerns from M to BRP would be the following:

           1. Brew Restaurant/liquor retailer - currently permitted in M not permitted in BRP.
              We are not sure what type of restaurant we’d attract once second hotel is
              completed, want our options open.
           2. Event center indoor/outdoor - currently permitted in M not permitted in BRP. If
              hotel had an event center component to it we would not want to lose this option.
           3. Fast Food establishment w/ Drive thru - currently permitted in M not permitted in
              BRP. There are many restaurants that want a drive-up capability to serve clients.”

Mr. Keys suggested two options for addressing his concerns. The first option would be to
exclude the three parcels owned by Lake Park Project, LLC from the zone change. The second
would be to amend the BRP zone to allow for the uses he would like to retain.
If the Commission agrees with the concerns raised by Mr. Keys, staff would not recommend the
first option he recommended because many other industrial uses would still be allowed if the M
zone was retained. However, staff is not opposed to the second option with one exception –
outdoor event centers should not be allowed in the BRP zone. For reference, an outdoor event
center is defined in the Zoning Ordinance as “an outdoor stadium, arena, amphitheater, or similar
venue that hosts sporting events, Concerts, theatrical performances, rodeos, or similar outdoor
events”.

An ordinance change to amend Sections 7-6-301 and 7-6-308 of the
Zoning ordinance by adding use restrictions and design standards to the
BRP zone.


City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to Sections 7-6-301 and 7-6-308 of the zoning
ordinance by adding use restrictions and design standards to the BRP (Business/Research Park)
zone. The proposed use restrictions would limit the amount of warehousing in the BRP zone to
60% of all buildings on a lot. The restrictions would also limit the amount of light industrial to
60% of all buildings on a lot. The proposed design standards would prohibit overhead doors from
facing high-image arterial streets and require warehouses in the BRP zone to meet the
commercial design standards.

There are currently four zones that allow both light industrial and warehousing – BRP, C-3
(Transitional Commercial), LI (Light Industrial), and M (Manufacturing). The table below lists
the current acreage of each of these zones. Altogether, these zones make up 43% of property
(excluding roads) in the City. In the zoning ordinance, the use “light industrial” is broadly
defined as: “the indoor manufacturing, predominantly from previously prepared materials, of
finished products or parts, including processing, fabrication, assembly, treatment, and packaging
of such products, and incidental storage, sales and distribution of such products, which does not
produce noise, odors, vibration, hazardous waste materials, or particulate that will disturb or
endanger neighboring properties”.

 Zone                      BRP                 C-3                  LI                   M
 Acreage                   295.1               27.2                76.7               8,082.2

Staff is forwarding the proposed use restrictions for two reasons. First, the two main areas of the
City with BRP zoning – Lake Park Corporate Centre and Metro Business Park – are
predominantly office uses. The proposed use restrictions will help prevent these areas from
becoming predominantly industrial. Second, given the large amount of property zoned to allow
industrial uses, there are plenty of opportunities for exclusively industrial use. Adding more use
restrictions to the BRP zone will further differentiate it from other zones.

For reference, a copy of the commercial and manufacturing zones use table is included with this
report. As seen in this table, the BRP zone is already generally more restrictive on land use than
the C-3, LI, and M zones. In addition, the BRP zone’s required landscaping percentage of 15% is
higher than the 10% in the LI zone and the 5% in the M zone.

The proposed design standards are intended to improve aesthetics within the BRP zone by facing
overhead doors away from main streets and by requiring buildings that are strictly warehouses to
still meet the commercial design standards.

C-26-2022
Full Service Storage, LLC
2222 W 2300 S (Printers
Row) M Zone (4.41 acres)


Full Service Storage, LLC is requesting conditional use approval for outside storage of
recreation vehicles, boats, trailers, and automobiles. They are also requesting a modification to
the 7-10- 103 high image arterial landscaping requirements. The property is zoned M,
Manufacturing, and the West Valley City General Plan designates this area as light
manufacturing. The surrounding zoning is also M. Adjacent land uses are office and light
industrial. North of the property is UDOT-owned right-of-way for the I-215 and SR-201
freeways.

The existing structure on the property was constructed in 1977, ZC 19760178, as an office
building, and has been used that way until recently. Last month a building permit application
for a tenant improvement was submitted to the City, B22-02729, with a valuation of $600,000.
The applicant intends to convert the two-story, 118,180 sf office building into a two-level
storage area for high-end RV’s, boats, trailers, and cars. The permit valuation triggered a
review of the 7-10-103 high image arterial landscaping standards required along the north
property line.

During discussions with the applicant about the landscaping staff also discovered that the
proposal also includes outside storage of small to midsized boats and RV’s on the property.
This is a change of use and requires conditional use approval.

The existing site is mostly landscaped, besides the north edge of the property, and is
completely paved with asphalt, as it formerly served as the parking lot for the building. The
applicant intends to divide the existing parking lot into 3 different areas. The parking lot on the
south side of the building will be used as an after-hours drop-off location. It will be fenced and
gated. The applicant previously installed a fence on the property that does not meet the 20’
minimum setback requirement from the sidewalk for a fence of that height. The fence will be
relocated to the edge of the drop-off area. Vehicles that are dropped-off in this area cannot
remain there for more than 24 hours, or it would be considered an outside storage area and
require screening.

The area on the east side of the building, towards the south end, will continue to be used as
employee and customer parking. About 46 parking stalls will remain and the applicant
anticipates there will only be two to three full-time employees at the building at one time. The
business offers valet service to their customers, so the majority of clientele will not visit the
property. The applicant expects three to four customers coming to the site on any given day.

The remainder of the parking lot will be fenced off for the outside storage area, which also
wraps around to the north side of the building. Per 7-7-119, outside storage areas are to be
screened from public view and appropriately surfaced. The entire area is paved with asphalt.
The applicant is proposing to add slats to the chain link fence, which will help screen the
storage area from the south side of the property. The existing building will also provide
screening from 2300
South. The applicant is proposing to screen the north edge of the property through
landscaping, which also needs to be installed to meet the 7-10-103 requirements. The adjacent
ramp for the freeway is quite a bit higher than the subject property, so installing a fence or
adding slats to the existing fence would not accomplish much. Staff recommends that
evergreen shrubs/trees that grow over 6’ in height be used to provide a solid barrier.

The property does not currently meet the 7-10-103 requirements for landscaping along high
image arterial streets along the north edge of the site. The original landscape plan for the
building showed some trees along that property line, but there is currently no landscaping there
now. The code requires 20’ of formal landscaping, with one tree provided for every 30’ of
frontage, and 50% lawn, with the remaining area to have 50% live plant material. The applicant
is requesting a modification to this requirement, as outlined in 7-10-104. There is currently
nearly 20-feet of area from the edge of the parking lot to the existing fence running along the
right-of-way line. However, there is a drainage ditch that occupies a large portion of this area.
The applicant is requesting to provide formal landscaping in the 4 to 8 foot area between the
edge of asphalt and the ditch. The applicant is willing to install the 12 trees required by the
code, and also install 50% live plant material. The area is too narrow to require lawn. The
applicant’s proposal included lower-growing shrubs and ornamental grasses. Staff recommends
that the applicant install the 12 required trees and that in all other areas evergreen shrubs/trees
that grow over 6’ in height be used. Doing this will meet the 50% live plant material
requirement, as well as provide better screening for the outside storage area.

The other existing landscaped areas are well-maintained. The original landscape plan for the
site showed four trees in the front landscaped area between the sidewalk and the parking lot.
The applicant needs to add two trees back in this area. The original plan also showed trees
along the west side of the building. Those have since been removed. There is no requirement in
the current code for trees adjacent to a building that is next to another commercial property, so
staff recommends that the additional trees be placed in the north-west corner of the site to
further help screen the storage area. There should also be 50% live plant material, not including
tree canopies, in this landscaped area.

The applicant has not provided plans for signage on the property. The base of an existing
monument sign is located near the sidewalk. The applicant can try to get a new permit using the
existing base, or it should be removed. Any wall signage on the building also requires a permit
and should meet the Title 11 requirements. No signage is allowed on fencing.

The valuation of the project also requires a review by our Public Works Department for the 2300 South
right-of-way improvements. The applicant has also been in contact with the Fire Department concerning
fire access on the site. Coordination for these issues, as well as any other issues should be handled with the
appropriate departments/agencies.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as

                 evergreen shrubs/trees that grow taller than 6’ in height.
              2. Vehicles shall not be parked/stored in any areas not specified as outside
                 storage for periods longer than 24 hours.
              3. Surfacing of the outside storage area shall be asphalt or concrete and shall
