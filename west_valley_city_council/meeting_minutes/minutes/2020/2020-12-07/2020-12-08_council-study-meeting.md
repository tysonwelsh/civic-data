       Proposed Resolution 20-179 related to the proposal to be considered by the City

       ACTION: RESOLUTION 20-179, AUTHORIZE WEST VALLEY CITY TO
       ACCEPT THE EDWARD BYRNE MEMORIAL JUSTICE ASSISTANCE
       GRANT
       Colleen Jacobs, Police Chief, discussed proposed Resolution 20-179 that would
       authorize West Valley City to accept the Edward Byrne Memorial Justice
       Assistance Grant.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              The U.S. Department of Justice Program is awarding the West Valley City
              Police Department the 2020 Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance
              Grant (J.A.G.). The allowable expenses of the grant are the purchase of
              equipment, to fund training, to fund programs they approve through the
              application process. The funds are allocated to West Valley City based on
              a formula that considers the population of West Valley City and our part
              one crime statistics. There is separate funding allocated to each state to
              ensure fairness. The J.A.G. Program allows states and local governments
              implement otherwise unfunded programs designed to improve the criminal
              justice system.

              The Police Department’s Public Order Unit (POU) is a group of officers
              and supervisors that have been specifically trained to deal with civil unrest
          MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – DECEMBER 8, 2020
                                 -3-


                    including facing violence and riots. The POU needs specialized training,
                    less-lethal equipment, gas masks, as well as communications and protective
                    equipment. The additional equipment and training this grant would fund is
                    desperately needed and in keeping with the best practices for Public Order
                    teams.

                    The officers, supervisors, and commanders of the POU need to be
                    properly trained. The Public Order Command Course is essential for the
                    supervisors and commanders assigned to manage the Public Order Unit.
                    The training covers the constitution, legal limitations, tactics, teamwork,
                    communications, deployments, risk management, reporting, and other
                    topics directly related to supervising the officers that are protecting life
                    and property during protests and riots.

             Mayor Bigelow asked how many officers are trained in the department and how
             many additional officers this grant would fund for training. Chief Jacobs replied
             there is one supervisor and 6 officers. She noted another supervisor and 6 officers
             would be trained. Training would encompass response to protecting life and
             property during protests and riots.

             The City Council will consider Resolution 20-179 at the Regular Council Meeting
             scheduled December 20-179, 2020 at 6:30 P.M.

       RESOLUTION 20-16: APPROVE A BUSINESS STABILIZATION AND
       REVITALIZATION GRANT AGREEMENT
       Mark Nord, CED, presented proposed Resolution 20-16 that would approve a
       Business Stabilization and Revitalization Grant Agreement.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

             This is the first of multiple Business Stabilization and Improvement Grants.
             This agreement with VFM directs improvements at the Mall. The Parties
             agree that the improvements being required will create a more inviting space
             for customers to gather. Further, in an effort to solidify and perpetuate the
             success of the City’s larger businesses long into the future thereby avoiding
             necessary redevelopment, this agreement also provides for a matching
             grant. Bringing foot traffic to the mall is essential to its long term success.
             Continued upgrades to the mall grounds provide for areas where
             entertainment, gathering and shopping can occur.

             Retail trends demonstrate that “entertainment shopping” is the future of
             retail. In order for the Valley Fair Mall, which is a power retail center for
             the City to continue to take advantage of that type of shopping traffic,
             changes need to be made to the outdoor areas. These changes are
             specifically targeted at encouraging people to come, linger and shop. The
             Economic Development Department uses data and market studies to curate
             various programs which provide the greatest return on any RDA
             investment. This program is an explicit result of that targeted use of funds.
   MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – DECEMBER 8, 2020
                          -11-


              The Business Stabilization and Improvement Grant was set up to assist
              targeted High Visibility West Valley City businesses in upgrading their
              image, presence and sustainability. It is also targeted at ensuring that these
              power producers invest in their locations and remain successful long into
              the future. The Grant is a match dollar per dollar up to $200,000 with
              parameters around improving visual aesthetics i.e. landscaping, business
              façade, signage etc. The goal of this grant is to raise the image and quality
              of some of our local businesses while also requiring private reinvestment.

       Councilmember Buhler asked how much money the RDA has for this and how long
       this project will continue. Mark replied there was a million-dollar initial plan. He
       noted that many of the businesses plan to exceed the match and have been very
       responsive. He indicated that this program can be expanded and extended into the
       future. Councilmember Buhler asked who this is available to. Mark replied
       currently the top 25 businesses. Councilmember Buhler asked how the money is
       provided. Mark replied as a reimbursement. Councilmember Lang asked if the City
       would allow xeriscaping on projects for water conservancy. Mark replied that he is
       unsure. Councilmember Lang stated that funding can be provided for xeriscape
       plans from conservancy districts. Mark replied that this may be a good option for
       the City to look into as additional funding.
