36. APPROVE AND AUTHORIZE THE COMMISSION CHAIR TO EXECUTE AN AGREEMENT
       BETWEEN UTAH COUNTY AND AMERICA’S FREEDOM FESTIVAL, INC.
(Track A, 25:00)
Commissioner Ivie expressed concern for the high and heartbreaking rates of suicides in the community, especially
among teenagers and young adults identifying as lesbian, gay, bisexual, or transgender (LGBT). He summarized
America’s Freedom Festival, which annually takes place in Provo with some financial sponsorship from Utah County,
last year approved the request of a group called Encircle to participate in their parade. Commissioner Ivie explained
Encircle is a group which works to break down cultural stereotypes and offer a safe place for youth who may be high-
risk suicidal. Commissioner Ivie commended the group for their courage and reprimanded America’s Freedom
Festival for making a last-minute decision to disappoint the group by revoking their participation in the parade. The
three commissioners discussed the role of tax funds used by America’s Freedom Festival and their responsibilities to
the community, with Commissioner Graves expressing concerns over government dictating the operations of private
organizations.

(Track A, 31:30)
Freedom Festival Director Paul Warner noted there have been between 40 and 50 people or groups denied participation
in the history of the parade, but this particular entity garnered media attention. He explained the festivities are to
celebrate America and not to debate controversies. Mr. Warner mentioned his corporation has been in
communications with Encircle after the mistake made last year. He expressed support for the group addressing youth
suicide, but equated the situation to current national discussions regarding firearms; pointing out the moral
hypothetical quandary of allowing a group in favor of ‘gun control’ to participate in the parade, would the Freedom
Festival then be required to have a group advocating ‘pro-gun’? The commissioners discussed funding sources and
use of monies with Mr. Warner, and Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore suggested adding a non-
discrimination clause in the contract as related to use of county grounds.

(Track A, 45:55)
Amber Eliason noted she is a volunteer with Encircle but is not speaking in an official capacity on behalf of the group.
She acknowledged their participant application was submitted late for the 2017 festivities, but only late enough to pay
an additional fee and not so late as to be unable to join the parade. Ms. Eliason outlined the group’s plan for the July
4, 2017 event: white shirts on approximately 100 kids dressed for a ‘sports’-themed pre-parade walk joined by former
Brigham Young University and National Football League (NFL) quarterback Steve Young – he and his wife Barbara
Young donated funds to Encircle – while playing music on a Bluetooth speaker. Ms. Eliason noted she spent personal
money to donate the speaker and there would have been giant beach balls and candy to pass through the crowd.
Encircle’s participants were specifically instructed by email that this is a ‘family-friendly’ event and not a ‘pride’
parade. Ms. Eliason emotionally clarified any promotion of the idea that the group would have been controversial or
inappropriate is only because “these kids belong to a certain community that some [people] don’t feel comfortable
with.” She further added that as a registered non-profit 501(c)(3) organization, Encircle cannot legally advocate
political issues.

(Track A, 54:15)
Brianna Cluck has no connection to Encircle but is a member of the LGBT community. Ms. Cluck reiterated Encircle
cannot legally advocate political issues as a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization despite Paul Warner equating the group
to ‘pro- or anti-gun’ earlier in the meeting. Using that same example, however, Ms. Cluck noted two separate
Constitutional Political Party groups were allowed to participate in that same parade where Encircle was denied. She
made suggestions for updated policies at Freedom Festival Inc., including specifying what is and is not allowed and
providing notice 72 hours in advance to allow opportunities for groups to correct potential violations of those rules;
this would reduce favoritism and provide fairness and consistency in the process. Tosh Metzger added comments
regarding the reputations of Provo City and Utah County, talking about his involvement with Provo Pride and agreeing
with previous comments about the unfairness of the short notice and redaction of Encircle’s approval to participate in
the parade. Mr. Metzger also noted the problematic decisions to allow participation and advocacy of certain entities,
such as missionaries for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, while denying LGBT kids and others.

8.     ADOPT A RESOLUTION TO CREATE A RECOMMENDING COMMITTEE CHARGED WITH
       REVIEWING RELEVANT SALARY STUDIES AND RECOMMENDATION TO COMMISSION
       FOR SALARY SCHEDULE FOR UTAH COUNTY EMPLOYEES
(Track A, 1:12:00)
Commissioner Graves summarized the intention of this proposed committee is to review all available salary
information in addition to relevant input from Utah County Department Heads in order to make recommendations for
salary schedules and how to implement $1,000,000 which was designated for employee salaries during the 2018
budget process. Commissioner Ivie expressed concerns regarding public accessibility to employee performance
records, and the Board discussed the risks with Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore. Commissioner Graves
mentioned having optimism for implementation beginning in May/June 2018 with pay retroactive to January 1, 2018,
and Commissioner Lee outlined hurdles since the beginning of the year. Utah County Human Resources Director
Cammie Taylor added comments about administrative oversight.

John O’Hara of the Utah County Sheriff’s Office specified he is present at today’s meeting during time off from the
county. He noted ‘committees are where ideas go to die,’ and bemoaned the significant problems the Sheriff’s Office
is encountering with hiring and retaining employees. He and the commissioners discussed legislative versus
