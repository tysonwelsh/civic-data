Petty Cash Policy

               Mayor Clayton stated last month the Council discussed the need to have a petty
cash fund because they do not have access to funds for the cemetery. The Council created a
petty cash policy and named Council Member Bailey as the fiduciary of the account. This action
was taken because of the cumbersome process the Salt Lake County is putting Copperton
through to get its own money.

He received an accounting of the cemetery and all the revenues have been accounted for. He
asked for details on other expenditures and will bring the information to the next Council meeting.

            Nathan Bracken stated he has forwarded an authorizing resolution to Council
Members. He reviewed a list of recommended minor changes to the policy.

               Mayor Clayton stated this money will be strictly for cemetery use, not Council

Unified Police Department (UPD) & Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area Update

    RFP for contracted police services

               Council Member Pazell stated tomorrow the governance board of the Unified
Police Department (UPD) will meet to consider proposals for a way to handle UPD votes.
Currently, the way the interlocal is set up, each community has one vote. When the UPD was
established, the original cities were Cottonwood Heights, Taylorsville, and a number of other cities
with similar populations.

The board is recommending changes to the interlocal agreement, which takes two-thirds vote of
the members to make a change. The proposals are:

   1. Voting weighted by level of contribution to UPD. That would give Copperton about .0008
      percent of the vote. The metro townships contribute very little. This would give the bulk
      of control to the Salt Lake County.

               Mayor Clayton asked what percentage of the vote would go to the Salt Lake
County.

               Council Member Pazell stated 40 percent.

               Council Member Bailey asked if the Salt Lake County was pushing this proposal.

               Council Member Pazell stated no. Initially Riverton proposed this, but then
withdrew their concern. Now the attorney for UPD is pushing it, which means he is doing it for

US Census Letter

                Nathan Bracken stated there has been discussions and email exchanges
between the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD) and the U.S. Census
Bureau. Although a final determination has not been made, the Census Bureau believes metro
townships are not governing bodies, but a subdivision of the Salt Lake County. That determination
seems to be done in comparison with metro townships in Iowa. However, Utah law indicates
metro townships are subsidiaries of the state. Iowa code indicates metro townships are
subsidiaries of the counties.

Mark Anderson, legal counsel to the GSLMSD, put together a letter to the bureau, and he has
revised it. Among other things it indicates that metro townships contract with the GSLMSD, but
can get their services elsewhere. Indeed, most key municipal services, such as police and fire,
are provided by entities other than the GSLMSD.

              Mayor Clayton suggested that Council Members discuss the issue with their
congressional delegation.

               Mr. Bracken stated the letter would be copied to the Utah congressional
delegation. He asked if he had the Council’s approval to send the letter. He would be happy to
spearhead the outreach on this issue. He will be in Washington D.C. later this month and may
have time to visit the Utah congressional delegation.
