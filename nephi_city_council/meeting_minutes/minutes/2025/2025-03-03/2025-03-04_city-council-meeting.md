CHILDREN’S JUSTICE CENTER AGREEMENT:

City Administrator Seth Atkinson informed the council the Children’s Justice Center (CJC) has occupied a
home at 240 N Main. They provide services to children who have been victims of abuse. Interviews can
be held in a comforting environment in order to bring perpetrators of children’s abuse to justice. Having
a facility like this has been a helpful service to have in the Juab County area. Prior to this, our police
officers used to travel to Utah County to use their facility. Our current CJC serves both Juab and Millard
Counties.

Recently the CJC has received sufficient funds, through generous donations and grants, to construct a
new facility in Nephi City. They have secured land and would like to build the facility (which will look like
a typical single family residential home) on the south end of the city. They have requested any help that
the city may provide to assist with their endeavor. The city council has verbally agreed to provide this
assistance with the caveat that any fees waived at the time of construction would be paid if the facility
were sold or used for another purpose. The agreement presented to the council specified the terms.
The city’s staff and attorney had reviewed the agreement.

Councilor Travis Worwood made the motion for the mayor to sign the to waive construction fees.
Councilor Shari Cowan seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

DWR AGREEMENT AMENDMENT:

Seth Atkinson, City Administrator, explained that for the past several years, Nephi City has been engaged
in a land exchange process with the Utah State Department of Natural Resources, Division of Wildlife
Resources (DNR/DWR). The land exchange is meant to provide additional land to the east of the golf
course for the purposes of course expansion. To date, the city has negotiated with the U.S. Fish and
Wildlife Service to determine how the land exchange could take place, had a yellow book appraisal done
for both properties, had both properties surveyed, and had an environmental assessment completed. All
information for the exchange has been sent on to the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service for their review. They
are short-staffed and the review has taken some time.
Seth Atkinson said that although we anticipate an acceptance of the exchange, the deadlines specified in
the original agreement, amendment #1, and amendment #2 have expired and it is necessary to adopt
another amendment to extend the time frame. The amendment extends the deadline to December 31,
2025 for the first land exchange (City land to DWR) and June 1, 2026 for the second land exchange (DWR
land to City). Mr. Atkinson stated that it is hoped that this will be the final extension amendment.

Councilor Skip Worwood made the motion to authorize the mayor to sign amendment #3 extending the
deadlines with the Utah State Division of Wildlife. Councilor JD Parady seconded the motion. The
motion passed unanimously.

ORDINANCE 03-04-2025—BACKFLOW PREVENTION ADOPTED:

City Administrator Seth Atkinson introduced Cameron Johansen with the Nephi City Water Department
and Kyle Marchant, Nephi Public Works Director. Cameron is certified to administer the city’s backflow
prevention program. He has put together a code change that adds the Cross-Connect Protection

K-9 AGREEMENT:

Seth Atkinson, city administrator, explained that it had come to the attention of the Police Department
that the Fair Labor Standards Act, and recent court cases, have supported compensation for a police
officer who is providing home care for a K-9 dog. Most agencies provide this compensation in the form
of a taxable stipend. In order to meet the intent of the federal law, staff has drafted an agreement for
the officer within the Police Department assigned to the K-9 unit. The proposed agreement established
the stipend amount and an amount for back pay. The back pay portion will be paid from a personnel
contingency line in the Capital Projects Fund specifically for items of this nature. The agreement also
requires adherence to the city’s K-9 policy.

The agreement has been reviewed by the city attorney and city staff as well as Officer Hurst, the city’s
K-9 officer. Staff recommends that the council authorize the mayor to sign the K-9 Officer Agreement.
Skip Worwood made the motion to authorize the mayor to sign the K-9 compensation agreement.
Jaramie Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
