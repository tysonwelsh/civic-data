 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Discussion of the Fiscal Year 2026 Tentative Budget
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck explained there had been a gap of
          $2,059,453 in the FY2025 Budget between on-going revenue and on-going expenses, with
          actual ongoing expenses exceeding revenue by $468,544. To maintain current service
          levels, the FY2026 Tentative Budget included a proposed 2% property tax rate increase;
          however, even with the 2% property tax rate increase, current service level expense still
          exceeded revenue in the Tentative Budget by $306,716. The Council and staff discussed
          sales tax estimates and franchise tax revenue. Ms. Steck reported utility franchise taxes
          for the new annexation area would be transferred to West Jordan City beginning July
          2025.

          Ms. Steck presented estimated revenues and expenses for new service levels in the
          FY2026 Tentative Budget, with new service level expense greater than revenue by
          $22,575. She reported new growth revenue for FY2026 as $729,832, and new growth
          expense of $304,665, with available on-going revenue from new growth equaling
          $402,592. The Council discussed the portion of current service level and new service level
          that could be attributed to new growth. Vice Chair Whitelock suggested increases to Code
          Enforcement were needed because of new growth. Ms. Steck said staff identified that a

          new police officer, new firefighter, new parks position, and new streets position could be
          attributed to new growth.

          Council Member Jacob believed revenue from new growth property tax should pay for
          the same services paid for by existing property tax revenue. Council Member Green
          agreed. Council Member Jacob did not think budget software or HR/finance software
          should be included as a new growth expense. Council Member Green did not think the
          citizen engagement expense should be included under new growth. Council Member
          Jacob suggested the two requested police officer positions not included in the Tentative
          Budget could be included as a new growth expense of $270,000. Council Member Green
          expressed the opinion that new growth expenses needed to be managed methodically so
          the City grew at the right pace.

          Council Member Green was not comfortable adding $10,000 in on-going expenses to the
          budget for non-programmed events. Vice Chair Whitelock agreed and said she was also
          not comfortable including $30,000 for holiday lights in the budget as an on-going expense,
          since events and lights were “wants”, not “needs”. Mayor Burton commented that events
          and holiday lights affected the City’s image. Council Member Jacob did not consider the
          events and holiday lights to be wants. Chair Lamb enjoyed holiday lights in Midvale every
          year, and was excited that West Jordan would also provide holiday lights for the
          community. Vice Chair Whitelock felt it would be nice if the City could afford to offer
          increased holiday lights to the residents, but not for a property tax increase.

          Ms. Steck asked for feedback from the Council regarding what they wanted to include in
          the FY2026 Budget. Chair Lamb expressed support for including Axon DraftOne (AI
          report writing) and license plate readers, not currently included in the Tentative Budget.
          City Administrator Korban Lee and Police Chief Wallentine listed multiple efficiencies
          that would be added to the Police Department with Axon DraftOne. Vice Chair Whitelock
          expressed support for funding Axon Draftone, but said she was not comfortable adding
          the license plate readers. Chair Lamb said he did not believe the Police Department would
          ask for license plate readers unless they were needed.

          The Council discussed requested items included in the Mayor’s Tentative Budget, and
          items not included (additional concrete crew with four positions, two police officers,
          Axon DraftOne, and license plate readers). Council Member Jacob commented that much
          of the work done on the FY2026 budget in the last six months was done with estimated
          numbers that had turned out to be different. Council Member Green asked if there were
          efficiencies that were missing in the FY2026 Tentative Budget. Council Member Jacob said
          the City proved during the pandemic that more could be done with less, but said he
          questioned whether doing so would be doing citizens and employees a disservice. Council
          Member Jacob expressed confidence that department heads looked for efficiencies every
          year with the budget process. Ms. Steck said staff examined budgets scrupulously.

          Chair Lamb suggested using one-time funds for the holiday lights as a compromise. Ms.
          Steck commented that sales tax revenue had remained flat for five years, and said she was
          grateful the City had adopted the practice of sales tax revenue in excess of 5% growth

          being used as one-time revenue. She cautioned that FY2027 would be difficult if sales tax
          revenues remained flat.

          Ms. Steck showed one-time revenue after expenses in FY2026 of $1,029,994, and asked if
          the Council wanted to follow the practice of transferring the amount to the CIP Fund, or
          use some of the one-time revenue for other purposes. Vice Chair Whitelock said the
          Tentative Budget included $30,000 for holiday lights, said the Council did not know what
          the plan was for those funds, and said the City already had lights for City Hall. She said
          she could not support the ongoing expense not knowing what would be done. Mr. Lee said
          the amount budgeted for holiday lights was in response to a discussion during the Council
          Retreat about community building and more events and holiday festivities. He said staff
          did not yet have a tight plan for holiday lights, but the amount was added to the budget
          because staff thought it was what the Council wanted. Council Member Bloom expressed
          support for the holiday lights as a one-time expense, and emphasized the importance of
          it being a community event with an official tree lighting. Council Members Whitelock,
          Green, Bedore, Bloom, and Jacob showed support for leaving the $30,000 for holiday
          lights in the budget as a one-time express.

          Responding to a question from Council Member Green, staff described plans for traffic
          calming measures included in the budget. Responding to a question from Vice Chair
          Whitelock, Ms. Steck said an additional 2% property tax rate increase could be added to
          the budget, for a total increase of 4%, to fund the list of requests not included in the
          FY2026 Mayor’s Tentative Budget. Another option was to use one-time revenue to fund
          the list and reduce the transfer to the CIP Fund. Council Member Jacob pointed out the
          City had many capital improvement projects that needed the funding as well. Council
          Member Green suggested a dedicated annual allotment to the CIP Fund. Ms. Steck said the
          Council had discussed implementing a dedicated CIP Fund allotment amount in the past.

          Public Works Director Brian Clegg pointed out the request had been for a four-man
          concrete crew, and one of the maintenance worker positions was already funded in the
          Tentative Budget. Ms. Steck said removing one maintenance worker position from the on-
          going request list would reduce the needed amount by $70,000.

          Ms. Steck showed average home value in West Jordan over the last fifteen years (more
          than doubled since 2010), and showed a downward slope in property tax rate over the
          same time period. In 2025, the percent of commercial property value increase exceeded
          the percent of residential property value increase. Ms. Steck explained that without the
          proposed property tax rate increase in the FY2026 Tentative Budget, the average home
          would see a decrease in West Jordan property tax of approximately $25. With the
          proposed 2% property tax rate increase, the average home would still see a decrease in
          West Jordan property tax of $17 in FY2026. Ms. Steck showed a comparison of certified
          tax rates in several area cities, with West Jordan near the lower end.

          Council Member Jacob said he believed all items on the unfunded request list could
          justifiably be added to the budget (new concrete crew of three, 2 police officers, Axon
          DraftOne, and license plate readers). Vice Chair Whitelock expressed support for funding
          one police officer, Axon DraftOne, and one maintenance worker. Mr. Clegg commented


Committee of the Whole Minutes               Tuesday, June 10, 2025                           Page 3
          that one additional maintenance worker without the requested crew supervisor and
          heavy equipment operator would help, but not make much of a dent. Addition of the
          entire concrete crew requested would help the City catch up with the two-year backlog
          of concrete work. Class C Road funding would also be stretched further with an additional
          concrete crew. Mr. Clegg answered questions about trees in park strips and damage to
          sidewalk. Council Member Green expressed a desire to establish tree planting guidelines
          that would reduce the need for sidewalk repair in the future. Mr. Clegg added that park
          strip tree roots also caused problems with curb and gutter.

          The Council and staff discussed funding the additional requests with increased property
          tax versus one-time revenue (reducing transfer to CIP Fund). Ms. Steck emphasized the
          need for continued transfer to the CIP Fund, and cautioned that one-time revenue for
          transfer to the CIP Fund in FY2027 was anticipated to be lower. Council Member Green
          commented that the additional concrete crew could be considered part of capital
          improvement. Council Member Jacob said he would not be comfortable adding an ongoing
          expense to capital improvement (one-time revenue). Mr. Lee pointed out that contractors
          for concrete work were paid from the CIP Fund.

          Council Member Green suggested forward-funding the requested police officers and Axon
          DraftOne out of reserves, knowing the City would receive additional property tax revenue
          from the newly annexed areas in a year. Ms. Steck said his suggestion was feasible. Council
          Members Bloom and Bedore expressed support. Council Member Jacob suggested adding
          the requested list of expenses to the budget, funded with both a 3% property tax rate
          increase and one-time revenue as a compromise. Council Member Bedore said he was
          comfortable with a 3% property tax rate increase.

          Ms. Steck said she heard a majority of the Council express support for a 3% property tax
          rate increase, adding two police officers, Axon DraftOne, license plate readers, and three
          personnel for a new concrete crew to the FY2026 Budget under on-going expense, moving
          $30,000 from on-going to one-time expense (holiday lights), and reducing transfer to the
          CIP Fund to approximately $800,000. A majority of the Council were in agreement and
          indicated support for adding $18,000 for Good Landlord Program software.

          The Council and staff reviewed FY2026 Tentative Budgets for Fleet Management. Staff
          emphasized that the City’s existing hydro excavator was always in use, and the new hydro
          excavator included in the budget was needed. Council Member Bloom suggested the
          Council keep the potential need for another hydro excavator in mind in the next couple
          years. Vice Chair Whitelock suggested adding a new ongoing line item for new vehicle
          reserves. Council Member Green asked if Parking Enforcement would be better off with a
          postal-delivery-type vehicle than the full-size vehicle included in the budget. Staff
          responded a full-size vehicle would better suit Parking Enforcement needs.

          Ms. Steck presented the FY2026 Highlands Special District Tentative Budget. Vice Chair
          Whitelock asked why a Special Services District was approved for the Highlands area.
          Council Member Jacob said there had been quite a debate at the time, and said quite a few
          amenities had been proposed for the area under the old WSPA zoning. He said the trails
          and open space in the area were the best in the city. Council Member Green compared the

            Highlands Special District to a massive HOA. Ms. Steck expressed the opinion that the
            Highlands Special District should be annually self-sustaining, and reserves should not be
            necessary. Mr. Lee said he disagreed, and said he wanted to see a bit of reserves in the
            budget for something like a full playground replacement if needed.

            Ms. Steck thanked the Council for taking the time to review the Tentative Budgets. She
            said she would prepare the FY2026 Tentative Budget as discussed by the Council that
            evening.
