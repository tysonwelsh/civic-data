Chair Brown said the Commission has voted to bring forth some conceptual ideas about 
potential changes to the zoning map and to receive input from the public.  The Community 
Development Director, Pat Putt, will lead the discussion on this.   
 
Director Putt said he appreciates everybody coming tonight and thanked them for fitting this 
into their schedule.  This is an opportunity for Staff and the Planning Commission to check in 
with the public about the direction they are taking.   
 
Director Putt explained they have had a Development Code that has been around for quite a 
while without any changes.  The last substantial review of the zoning was approximately 
2003 or 2004.  He will try to bring the public up to date with the work that has been done.  
They need to understand what they have been working on in its entirety.   
 
First, they have been working on the General Plan.  It is extremely important and is the most 
fundamental of the documents.  The reason it is important is because both Federal and State 
Governments say if they are going to affect personal property rights by creating rules, they 
have to have a real legitimate public reason for doing that.  That legitimate reason is 
explained in the General Plan.  The General Plan has the written and illustrated description of 
how they are going to grow into the future.  A little over a year ago, they updated and 
adopted the Eastern Summit County General Plan.   
 
This is the first plan that has acknowledged how they will develop in the next 20 to 25 years.  
Ranching, farming, logging, and mining will continue, along with a tremendous amount of 
residential growth.  Another thing the plan recognized is that it is difficult to balance all of 
the competing needs.   
 
There is the need to balance fundamental property rights with how they are going to grow.  
They realized the old General Plan and the existing Code doesn’t do a very good job of 
creating the tools to deal with the growth; that is to say, how much and where.  If they are 
going to see more residential opportunities, they need to have more residential options in 
their list of zone districts.  There should be an opportunity for commercial businesses in the 
right locations.  The right opportunities in the right location would be a benefit.   
 
The plan recognizes they need a lot of tools.  The tools are the zones they created and what is 
allowed in those zones.  Without regular reviews, the Code can become fragmented and 
contradictory to itself.  The Code needs to be as predictable and as streamlined as possible.  
Nothing they have done has been finalized.  They want to present the package in its entirety 
to the County Council.   
 
Director Putt said they have created a section of definitions.  This will help everyone be on 
the same page when they are discussing zoning and different applications.  They want things 
to be as clear as possible.  One of the past terms that has created confusion is the “lot of 
record.”  They propose to move away from that term to the “grandfathered right.”  He said 
the definition of the lot of record had evolved into something that didn’t work.  They want  
 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
Regular Meeting

the grandfathered right to be something that is based on zoning and property size.  It won’t be 
about when a lot was created, who owns it, or if a fence line was moved.   
 
The new criteria will be if a parcel meets the minimum lot size of the zone that it is in.  If the 
answer is yes, the landowner can move forward.  If it doesn’t, it will be determined if there is 
a grandfathered right attached.    
 
Another option for a landowner would be the land use right.  A land use right would be 
similar to a development right, but it is not quite the same.  Development rights will be 
associated with building something like a house.  A land use right may be something like a 
quarry or logging activity.   
 
A lot of bureaucracy and unneeded process has been removed from the Code, while still 
achieving quality design and public safety.  A hallmark of this is how they have streamlined 
the subdivision process.  Five lots or less will be routed through an administrative, in-house 
process.  Six lots or more will follow a process like what they have seen in the past, which 
would be a review by the Planning Commission and the County Council.     
 
Director Putt said they are trying to make the document more user-friendly by including flow 
charts.  He added that the sketch plan is a concept plan.  They are reviewed by Staff at an 
administrative level.  Once that step is completed, an application is filed, requiring another 
fee.  It has been decided to make this step optional.     
 
The requirements of the Development Code are under review.  They want to discern if these 
are reasonable.  Does the required information serve a value?  Is there a purpose behind it?  
The safety standards have remained, but the process has been streamlined.  Building and 
engineering standards have been worked on as well.   
 
Director Putt said sometimes there are reasons that people need to divide land that don’t 
involve building something.  Agriculture is one reason; probate is another.  Other reasons 
could be as a result of a court order, an inheritance, or for financing a mortgage.  Sometime 
banking institutions will only finance smaller pieces of land.  The Code now allows for these 
things to be taken into consideration and recorded without having to go through a formal 
subdivision process.   
 
When someone applies to develop a subdivision, a review will occur to see if the property is 
in conformance.  Zoning will determine the uses, the density, and the location.  With the 
possible exception of one, they are not talking about eliminating any of the zones that 
currently exist.  They are going to add things that will create more opportunity for residential 
development.  Director Putt outlined the zones that are currently being studied.   
 
1) An Agricultural zone (AG-1).  This zone allows one unit per acre and a fairly broad pallet 
of agricultural uses.  This zone might replace the highway corridor (HC) zone.  They 
don’t want to create a zone that has unintended consequences such as non-conformity.  
Currently, there are a lot of properties in this area with agriculture.

2) The AG-6 zone.  This is a new zone.  This has been designated as a one-acre home site 
with five acres of agriculture.  This will allow the property to stay in greenbelt.  There is 
potential for this zone to be a problem solver.  In Eastern Summit County, 60% to 75% of 
the AP-40 zone is non-conforming in size.  If the AG-6 zone is applied in the right 
locations, the problem of the non-conformity will mostly be solved.   
3) The rural residential zone (RR) is one unit per one acre.  Director Putt said the difference 
between this zone and the AG-1 zone is that it will have a more restrictive, smaller list of 
agricultural uses.  The minimum lot size might be as small as .75 acre.   
4) The overlay zone will be used if someone wanted to proceed with a master plan project.  
This would include placing development in the correct locations, dealing with 
infrastructure, and access.  It might be possible to achieve as many as three units per acre.  
This is not a remaking of the cluster bonus because the overlay zone allows an 
opportunity to put the development locations where it makes sense.  In some situations 
clustering may make sense.  In other areas the developer may want to have more of a 
dispersed development pattern.   
5) The residential subdivision (RS) zone would be a single family development with little to 
no agricultural uses.  This could be three units per acre.  That is a Jeremy Ranch 
subdivision type of density. 
6) The recreation commercial (RC) zone.  Blue Sky Ranch is an example of this type of 
business.  Other possible areas could be around Rockport or Echo.  This zone would 
allow the Commission to identify areas where this could occur.   
 
The Commission has been debating among themselves where these zones would be put.  
They hope to largely cure non-conforming properties by looking at the development pattern 
currently on the ground.  They realized they won’t be able to fix 100% of the situations.    
 
They are also looking at the geography.  Can the ground accommodate certain types of 
development or use?  Considerations being taken into account are excessive steep slopes or 
soil instability, such as flood plains and wetlands.  
 
Director Putt said the State requires municipalities to have annexation areas to accommodate 
future growth.  These areas may or may not come into the cities at some point.  Until they are 
taken into a city’s jurisdiction, they are still part of the unincorporated County.  The County 
doesn’t want to compete with the municipalities, so they are trying to find the middle ground.  
Summit County doesn’t want to propose uses that are radically different than the vision of 
the different municipalities.    
 
They also want to consider the placement of zones from an economic development 
standpoint.  They want to consider if there is a location that could make a certain type of 
zone become a reality.  This could be something like a recreation or a commercial enterprise.     
 
Director Putt said zoning is the tool used to help implement the things that are described in 
the General Plan.  He emphasized these ideas are a work in progress.  The public will be kept
