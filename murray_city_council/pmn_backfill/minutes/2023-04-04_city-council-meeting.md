 1. Consider a Joint Resolution of the Mayor and Municipal Council of Murray City, Utah Declaring
    Friday, May 5, 20223 as Murray Arbor Day., Mayor Hales and Council Chair Hrechkosy presenting

       PRESENTATION

       Mayor Hales invited board members of the Shade Tree and Beautification Committee to the
       podium and then read into the record the Joint Resolution declaring Friday, May 5, 2023, as
       Murray Arbor Day.

       During the meeting, it was discussed that Arbor Day is a national and international observance
       that celebrates the significance of trees, as they provide a source of joy and spiritual renewal.
       The value of a tree cannot be measured, as its worth is subjective and varies depending on the
       individual's emotional and spiritual connection to it.

       It was highlighted that trees offer many benefits, including reducing soil erosion, moderating
       temperature, cleaning the air, producing oxygen, and providing habitats for wildlife. Trees also
       provide enjoyment, shade, cover, and safety for people and other living creatures.

       The council affirmed its commitment to managing the confined spaces along streets and near
       buildings to ensure peaceful coexistence between trees, utilities, buildings, and people. The
       Shade Tree and Beautification Commission, which promotes the planting and care of trees and
       vegetation, plays a significant role in enhancing the city's beauty and value, making it a more
       desirable place to work, play, and live.

       As a Tree City USA for 46 years, Murray City is reminded of the slogan, "Murray is a City
       Without Equal." The Mayor and Municipal Council of Murray City, Utah, jointly resolved to
       declare May 5, 2023, as Murray Arbor Day, acknowledging the importance of trees and the
       benefits they provide to the community.

       MOTION

       Councilmember Cotter moved to adopt the Joint Resolution of the Mayor and Municipal
       Council of Murray City, Utah Declaring Friday, May 5, 20223 as Murray Arbor Day. The motion
       was SECONDED by Councilmember Dominguez.

       ROLL CALL

       Ayes: Councilmember Markham, Councilmember Cotter, Councilmember Dominguez
       Excused: Councilmember Turner, Councilmember Hrechkosy
       Motion passes 3-0

       DISCUSSION

       Mayor Hales finished reading the Joint Resolution into the record.

       Darin Bird and Judith Payne urged citizens to support efforts to protect trees and woodlands
       by planting trees that would benefit future generations while beautifying the city. The Shade
       Tree and Beautification Commission is responsible for organizing an annual event to celebrate
       Arbor Day and thanked the Mayor for his words and invited everyone to attend the event in the
       park on May 5th. They also shared that Murray City was the longest Tree City, USA community
       in Utah.

 The meeting was open for public comment.

 Lawrence Horman

    Lawrence Horman addressed the council and encouraged Murray City to propose a permanent
    inclement weather center for homeless individuals who have nowhere else to go during extreme
    temperatures. He suggested that the center could be used year-round as a homeless community
    center, with facilities like showers and washing machines available. Lawrence believed that such a
    center could ease the burden on existing service providers downtown and provide a safe and
    comfortable space for the homeless. He also noted that homeless individuals often use truck stops
    and community centers with gym and pool memberships to access hygiene facilities and that a
    permanent center like this could offer daytime services as well.

 Drue Kehl

    Drue Kehl introduced himself as a resident and a representative of some volunteer effort being
    planned by community members. He shared that he was tasked to provide a flood mitigation plan for
    the Little Cottonwood waterway that runs through their stake, supported by the state and members of
    the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints. They planned to provide flood mitigation on April 29
    from 8 am to 1 pm by placing 15,000 sandbags along the creek in key locations that are on the city and
    county watch list. Drue Kehl asked Murray City for support, particularly in providing police presence,
    portable restrooms, and guaranteed resources for their efforts.

    The council and Mayor asked follow-up questions about the volunteer efforts and Drue Kehl confirmed
    that the event was being organized and volunteers were being recruited, but they want the city's
    assurance to pull it off with their resources. It was determined that Drue Kehl will continue to
    coordinate with city staff after the meeting.

 Clark Bullen

    Clark Bullen, a District Three resident, expressed gratitude to the Council and Administration for
    delaying the power rate increase pending further review. He also commended Public Works and the
    Power Department for their efforts during the recent severe winter weather. Clark Bullen mentioned
    when the council approved changes to the zoning for this property, they only considered height,
    parking, and green space, and it was mentioned that there would be a continued conversation about
    this property. Clark Bullen requested the council to do a Charrette or Town Hall to gather citizen
    feedback and map solutions to discuss the future of the property, which is currently owned by the
    Murray City RDA and serves as a community space. Clark suggested discussing allowed density,
    historic design/development standards, parking for the renovated theater, the feasibility of preserving
    the building, and the big tree out front. The property is currently zoned for high-density Mixed Use, and
    all proposals are required to include residential, but some council members would like to see
    commercial like a medical office building. If they want to see commercial-only projects, they should
    consider changing the zoning. Clark Bullen strongly encouraged the council to consider a Charrette or
    Town Hall because he has seen projects get to the proposal stage that citizens are not in favor of and
    have to go back to the drawing board, and it would be less painful to gather feedback now.

 No additional citizen comments were given, and the open citizen comment period was closed.

     PRESENTATION

     Ben Ford presented a PowerPoint presentation about the request to approve the 2022
     Municipal Wastewater Planning Program (MWPP) report. This report was required to be
     submitted to the Division of Water Quality and covered the financial health of the wastewater
     utility, the overall condition and assessment of the collection system, and mandatory reporting
     items.

     Ben Ford highlighted only the changes that had occurred since the previous year and invited
     questions.
Ben Ford is the Direct Responsible Charge (DRC) person for the Murray City collection system,
which was a grade three system. He noted that the annual user charge had increased from
$463 to $572.88, a change of $113. He also reported that the utility had enough revenue and
reserves to maintain financial stability, due to the rate increase and impact fees implemented
the previous year. He added that an impact fee study was included in the budget for the
upcoming year. The total replacement cost for the system's equipment had increased slightly.
Ben Ford then provided information on the system's three lift stations, and 135 miles of pipes,
and reported that there had been no major overflows in the past year, only one minor overflow
of about 100 gallons caused by a large grease deposit and a 55-gallon plastic bag. Ben Ford
speculated that a large garbage bag was the cause of an overflow. He reported that this was
the only overflow in the past year, which was required to be reported annually. The system had
28 new connections, mainly from the completion of the Fashion Creek Court subdivision and
the nearing completion of the Wynwood subdivision. The system served approximately 36,800
people and had over 9,400 total connections. Other districts, such as Murray, Cottonwood
Improvement, and Mount Olympus, also served parts of the city. Ben Ford, as the Chief DRC,
listed the operators employed in the system and noted that the system was compliant with
operator certifications. The management plan, a living document that guided the system's
operations, was audited annually, and a full audit was conducted every five years. Ben Ford
reported changes to the management plan due to the retirement of a department employee
and the hiring of a new supervisor. He also mentioned a change to the ownership range due to
fluctuations in Central Valley ownership. His city staff had identified problem areas that were
being maintained and repaired as resources allowed. These were listed on a hotspot list.

DISCUSSION

During the meeting, several speakers discussed various topics related to the wastewater
system. First, Councilmember Markham commended Ben Ford and his team for keeping up
with the state requirements and providing important guardrails for the department to ensure
the system's maintenance. Councilmember Markham also inquired about the city's opportunity
for employees to advance through the different levels of certification. Ben Ford confirmed that
the city provides opportunities for employees to advance up to a wastewater tech three-level
and beyond, depending on a vacancy.

Next, Councilmember Cotter asked about the city's water and sewer customer system
program. Ben Ford explained that the city does not have an official capacity assurance
program as defined by the state but adjusts potential leaks on a case-by-case basis.

Regarding the number of days with sewage bypass overflow or basement flooding in the
system due to rain or snowfall, Ben Ford mentioned that the system had some infiltration due
to an older system but did not anticipate any basement flooding this year.

Ben Ford also confirmed that any contractor that damages the line is responsible for fixing it,
although the city has someone to inspect the repairs. Lastly, Councilmember Cotter inquired
about the city's written policy regarding continuing education and training for wastewater
operators. Ben Ford replied that although there is no written policy, the city pays for all operator
certification tests and training expenses. However, the city reserves the right to not pay for
expenses if an operator is not invested in the process. Finally, the Council and Mayor
commended Ben Ford for his work and praised him as an example of an employee who had an
opportunity to advance and grow with the city.

 1. Consider an ordinance , Brenda Moore presenting
   Consider an ordinance repealing paragraph (B) of sections 7.04.020, 13.48.060 and amending
   section 15.20.260 of the Murray City Municipal Code relating to operational transfers of the Solid
   Waste and Recycling Fund, the Stormwater Fund, and the Power Fund.

      PRESENTATION

      Brenda Moore shared a proposal to amend an ordinance to repeal paragraph (B) of sections
      7.04.020 and 13.48.060 and amend section 15.20.260 of the Murray City Municipal Code
      relating to operational transfers of the Solid Waste and Recycling Fund, the Stormwater Fund,
      and the Power Fund. The purpose of this ordinance is to update the financial standards of
      these funds to maintain fiscal soundness.

      In section 2, the ordinance repeals paragraph (B) of sections 7.04.020 and 13.48.060 of the
      Murray City Municipal Code. These sections relate to the Solid Waste and Recycling
      Management Fund and the Stormwater Utility Enterprise Fund, respectively. The repealed
      paragraphs specify the reserve level for the funds and the annual transfer from these funds to
      the City's General Fund.

      In section 3, the ordinance amends section 15.20.260 of the Murray City Municipal Code
      relating to the Power Fund financial standards. The amended section specifies the operational
      transfers from the Power Enterprise Fund to the City's General Fund, excluding certain
      disbursements and revenues from the calculation. The City Council may modify the operational
      transfer.

      The ordinance aims to maintain the fiscal soundness of these funds by updating their financial
      standards. In addition, the Murray City Municipal Council would continue to monitor and
      manage the funds to ensure their stability and liquidity.

      DISCUSSION

      Councilmember Dominguez expressed confusion regarding the need to change the language if
      the city is no longer performing the required solid waste and stormwater transfers. Brenda
      Moore clarified that the transfers are still required by the ordinance, but the city has stopped
      performing them and is now attempting to align the ordinance with current practice. When
      Councilmember Dominguez inquired about the state's position on the matter, Brenda Moore
      explained that while some individuals within the state may disapprove of the transfers, the city
      can follow all the rules. Brenda Moore also recommended leaving the transfer percentage
      unchanged during budget time, although the language in the ordinance would now allow for
      modifications while maintaining consistency across water, wastewater, and power sections.

      MOTION

      Councilmember Cotter moved to approve the ordinance repealing paragraph (B) of sections
      7.04.020, 13.48.060 and amending section 15.20.260 of the Murray City Municipal Code
      relating to operational transfers of the Solid Waste and Recycling Fund, the Stormwater Fund,
      and the Power Fund. The motion was SECONDED by Councilmember Dominguez.
