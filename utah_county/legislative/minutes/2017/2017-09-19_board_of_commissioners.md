12.     ADOPT (OR DENY) AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND SECTIONS 2-2 AND 3-25 OF THE UTAH
        COUNTY LAND USE ORDINANCE TO ADD REQUIREMENTS FOR EARTH EXTRACTION
        OPERATIONS RELATED TO AIR AND WATER QUALITY, REQUIRED LANDSCAPING, HOURS
        OF OPERATION, NOISE, AND OTHER RELATED IMPACTS (Continued from the September 5, 2017
        Commission meeting)
(Track A, 12:00)
Bryce Armstrong of the Utah County Community Development Office came forward to continue this discussion which
began two weeks ago during the September 5, 2017 Commission meeting. Mr. Armstrong summarized the Utah
County Planning Commission has recommended the Board of Commissioners deny this ordinance of restrictions on
gravel pit operations, such as not being able to operate within one mile of a residence or agricultural production and
specifying hours for earth extraction at night. Mr. Armstrong and Commissioner Lee discussed the proposed
ordinance compared to other jurisdictions – Box Elder County, Brigham City, and Perry City – and potential
exceptions to hours of operation. Mr. Armstrong also confirmed for Commissioner Graves county staff began
reviewing local concerns over gravel pits at the request of Commissioner Ivie. Commissioner Graves questioned why
the county does not more heavily regulate mink farms, or restrict the amount of manure from farm animals, or specify
fruit can only be picked during certain hours of the day if the solution to complaints is to create new ordinances. He
pointed out this proposed ordinance would not impact any of the current mining operations but would create additional
burdens on groups that are already heavily regulated. Commissioner Graves stated it might be in the best interest of
West Mountain residents, but it may not be in the best interest of Utah County.

(Track A, 23:45)
Commissioner Ivie answered the questions posed by Commissioner Graves. Commissioner Ivie explained there are
going to be increasing conflicts with competing interest as the population of the county grows. He spoke of the
damage caused by dust to orchard, fruit, and alfalfa production, pointing out agriculture is also very heavily regulated.
Commissioner Ivie acknowledged some companies are spending millions of dollars to mitigate issues while others
ignore the problem, and the legislative process is needed to create balance. Commissioner Graves pointed out the
Utah Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) already monitors dust pollution, and Commissioner Ivie countered
the DEQ does not respond to site visit requests. They debated this point, with Commissioner Ivie providing an
example of being told directly by a department head at the DEQ that they do not have enough staff to monitor all
issues; even an issue of recycled asphalt being disposed without proper barriers was addressed in a manner of ‘as long
as it is not getting into the culinary system.’ Commissioner Graves replied the argument cannot be one-sided; if a
high water table is the issue, then farmers should not be allowed to add pesticides to their soil which could potentially
impact his drinking water. Commissioner Lee opened the discussion to public comment.

(Track A, 29:40)
David Bennion has lived in West Mountain since 1982. He talked about the gravel pit restrictions being lifted during
the ‘I-15 Core Project,’ an expansion of the Interstate-15 in Utah County, to meet construction needs – Mr. Bennion
and his family lived with day and night operations including bright lights, noise, and dust. While that was a sacrifice
made for “the greater good,” Mr. Bennion expressed residential zoning would now be in the best interest of the public.
He asked the Board to consider banning any future pits as the minimum requirement, and would prefer prior
regulations be reinstated.

(Track A, 32:05)
West Mountain resident, and member of South Utah County Community Voice, Julie Sainsbury, is in favor of the
proposed ordinance restricting earth extraction operations. Ms. Sainsbury talked about deficiencies at the state level
and needing the county to help as the local government. She noted it is the responsibility of the commissioners to
protect the health, safety, and welfare of county residents.

(Track A, 36:10)
Dave Kallas of Clyde Companies in Orem noted he personally lives near a gravel pit in Bluffdale, Utah. He reiterated
the earlier point by Commissioner Graves that this proposed ordinance would not impact existing operations. Mr.
Kallas noted Clyde Companies has operations in Brigham City and Box Elder County, and have co-existed with the
peach farm industries there for years. He explained how the ordinance hours of 6:00A.M. to 7:00P.M. have led to
increased traffic during those times, and regular weekly meetings with the mayor to request project exemptions.

Referring to the proposed hours of operation for 7:00A.M. to 7:00P.M. in Utah County, Mr. Kallas talked about daily
variations in weather and seasonal changes which would impact the quality of their product. Noting even the now-
remote ‘Pelican Point’ gravel pit will eventually be surrounded by residences, he suggested the Board consider
approval of applications on separate case-by-case circumstances rather than impose a far-reaching an overall county
ordinance.

(Track A, 42:15)
Kristy Beck, a West Mountain resident, listed many problems which accompany gravel pits: dust, traffic, ‘Jake’
brakes, potholes, cracked windshields, and spilled loads; but most of all, there are safety issues. Ms. Beck mentioned
truck drivers travelling 30 miles per hour over the speed limit in a 25-mile-per-hour zone. She also pointed out that
while the tractors are registered in Utah, the trailers are registered in Idaho – the company is not contributing through
their tax dollars to fix the very same roads they are ruining.

(Track A, 48:10)
Paul Washburn of Clyde Companies explained the materials extracted from the mines are being used locally for streets,
hospitals, and schools. He emphasized their resources are not shipped overseas, or even cross-country. Mr. Washburn
surmised most of the pollution in Utah Lake is from farmers, reminding the Board the individuals who work for Clyde
Companies are paid living wages and in turn pay their property taxes. He acknowledged there cannot be industry
without regulation but advised against overly burdensome restrictions.

(Track A, 50:30)
Sheryl Fowers thanked the commissioners, Bryce Armstrong of the Utah County Community Development Office,
and their staff for their time today and this proposed ordinance. She talked about the muddy wheels of the gravel pit
trucks, and suggested adding requirements to minimize debris into the ordinance. Ms. Fowers also proposed adding
a dedicated truck lane in the area. She detailed the rigorous inspections performed by many government entities for
her fruit-growing operation. Responding to a comment made earlier regarding peach farms in Brigham City, Ms.
Fowers noted those trees have died and the farmers are needing to plant new trees. She also mentioned ongoing
lawsuits surrounding this issue.

(Track A, 57:05)
Spring Kenton of the Utah County Commission Office mentioned she attended a meeting with the Utah Department
of Environmental Quality in which Commissioner Ivie was told the division does not have enough staff to regularly
conduct site visits and investigate complaints, emphasizing his earlier statements.

(Track A, 58:30)
Commissioner Graves noted this proposed ordinance is being compared to other cities instead of other counties. He
suggested West Mountain should annex into another town, or create their own, if they disagree with county ordinances.
Commissioner Graves stated he is offended his integrity is being questioned by a fellow commissioner and assistant.
He noted Utah County is the lowest taxing entity in the state while maintaining the second highest population; if the
county is going to follow the examples set by other counties and cities, the taxes should also be raised at the same
rate. He reiterated the proposed ordinance will not impact existing mining operations – it would be wiser to widen
the road and add a truck lane to improve safety.

(Track A, 1:02:45)
Commissioner Ivie clarified he is not attacking the integrity of Commissioner Graves, but asking for additional contact
information for personnel at the Utah Department of Environmental Quality because they are both having very
different experiences in receiving answers. Commissioner Ivie stated he does not want the ordinance to be overly
burdensome and encouraged dialogue between all parties. Commissioner Graves pointed out the only contaminants
found in dust are remnants of what is already in the ground, and he personally would prefer breathing mining dust
than farmland dust due to health concerns over pesticides and fertilizers. Commissioner Ivie countered that lime in
the ground does not hurt people, but can when inhaled through the air.
