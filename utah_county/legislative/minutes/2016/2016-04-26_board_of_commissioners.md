18.    ADOPT A RESOLUTION APPROVING AND AUTHORIZING THE EXECUTION OF AN
       INTERLOCAL COOPERATION AGREEMENT WITH THE REDEVELOPMENT AGENCY OF
       PROVO CITY REGARDING 200 CITYVIEW TIF FINANCING - Continued from the April 12 and
       April 19, 2016 Commission Meetings
Commissioner Ellertson said this keeps coming back to us and we need to be sure we understand the
dates and amounts, Soren Halladay said the base date is 2015 instead of 2016. The amount is $66,000.
The original completion estimate was for 10 years, but now they are asking to increase that time to 2029.
They are proposing 75% from the county.

Larry Ballard said the reason that he’s here is because this seems to be a big deal. He has a concern
that this will be county-wide tax incremental financing and he believed TIF’s would become county-wide
What is missing here is that private business has some public costs and the burden is placed on the
taxpayer. He continued the original concept of the TIF would shift to smaller businesses, but has now
been shifted to big business, He said the local officials usually don’t consider what the growth would be
without the TIF. He asked that the Commissioners disapprove this request. If Orem decides to use a
TIF, all of the other cities would do this. Mr. Ballard thought disapproval would be better.

Commissioner Ellertson said the taxes world be given to the City of Provo and he asked if this would start
a county-wide precedent, Mr. Ballard said “yes”. He expressed the understanding the mayors of the
cities should have an understanding of this. Commissioner Ellertson said this is not the first time this was
done in developing cities, and Commissioner Lee agreed, saying this is happening more and more.
Mr. Ballard said this leads to regionalization, and this is his main concern. His purpose in speaking is
to give more information. .

Peter Anderson said his concern is the taxes. The purpose of the state constitution is to stop anything
that is illegal and unconstitutional and he felt there has been a long time of playing favorites and giving
money to big cities.

Commissioner Ellertson said he appreciates the fact that they compete and raise taxes, He said this
consideration is to get cities moving on projects and he agreed it is important to be aware of ramifications
in other areas, but finally it doesn’t increase the taxes. David Shawcroft noted the incremental payment
is just that; a payment.

Commissioner Lee said it is the consensus; that when we approve a TIF, the business is getting
subsidies and Mr. Ballard said the county is playing favorites in these issues.

Commissioner Lee said this is about the free market system, in order to create, you must have some of
these projects. His concern is that it does favor big business, so he would like to get a broader idea of
this development, He said he doesn’t get this and would like to address some concerns with Cameron
and Soren to possibly restructure the request. He added, he would also be more comfortable with an
amount lower than 75%.

Commissioner Graves asked what the basis for continuing this matter would be, and Commissioner
Ellertson said he would like to take a week to be sure everyone understands this proposal.

                                         PUBLIC COMMENTS
Larry Ballard read the following comments into the record:
      At a time when local governments’ efforts to foster development, from direct subsidies to the use of eminent
      domain to seize property for private development, are out of control, TIF’s only add to the problem. There
      are hidden costs that taxpayers don’t know about. Cities generally assume they are not really giving
      anything up because the foregone tax revenue would not have been available in the absence of the
      development generated by the TIF. That assumption is often wrong.

      There is always this expectation with TIF’s that the economic growth is a way to create jobs and grow the
      economy, but then push the costs across the public spectrum. What is missing here is that the cost of
      development private business has some public costs. Road and sewers and schools are public costs that
      come from growth. Unless spending is cut – and if TIF really does generate economic growth, spending
      is likely to rise, as the local population grows, the burden of paying for these services will be shifted to other
      taxpayers. Adding insult to injury, those taxpayers may include small businesses facing competition from
      well-connected chains that enjoy IF-related tax breaks. In effect, a TIF subsidizes big business at the
      expense of less politically influential competitors and ordinary citizens.

      The original concept of TIFs was to help blighted areas come out of the doldrums and get some economic
      development they wouldn’t otherwise have a chance of getting, The end game again is that this tax financing
      construct shifts the taxes being paid from wealthy corporations to small businesses and regular people. The
      rest of us pay taxes for normal services like public safety, building inspections and street maintenance.
      Those services come out of the general fund. As the cost of services goes up, and the money from the
      general fund is given to these businesses through a TIF, the tax burden gets shifted to the regular private
      citizens and small business owners who don’t have the same political clout. It is a crummy way to treat your
      taxpaying, law-abiding citizens

      Local officials usually do not consider how much growth might occur without a TIF. In Chicago the
      neighborhood Capital Budget Group, a coalition of 200 Chicago organizations that study local public
      investment looked at 36 of the city’s TIF districts and found that property values were rising in all of them
      during the five years before they were designated as TIFs. The study showed that the city invested $1.6
      billion in TIFs, even though 1.3 billion in economic development world have occurred anyway. So the
      bottom line is that the city invested $1.6 billion for $300 million in revenue growth. Again, these TIF monies
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS
April 26, 2016
Page 9

       are diverting tax money that otherwise world have been used for government services.

       The NCBG study also found that the 36 TIF districts world cost Chicago public schools $632 million in
       property taxes based on development that would have occurred anyway because the property tax rates are
       frozen for schools as well. This doesn’t merely mean that the schools get more money. If the economic
       growth occurs with the TIFs, that attracts people to the area thereby raising enrollments. In that case, the
       cost of teaching the new students will be borne by property owners outside the TIF district.

       Finally, let us focus on the fact that the TIF incentive is not being used for a non-profit, a library or a school.
       They are for-profit business that gains an advantage over competitors. Where the TIF is normally used to
       lure in big corporate business, the consequences for the small business community can be extremely
       detrimental. Taxes that would otherwise go to schools, libraries, fire departments, and other urban services
       are diverted to entice and subsidize large corporation enterprises. I would urge the Utah County
       Commission to disapprove this proposal.
