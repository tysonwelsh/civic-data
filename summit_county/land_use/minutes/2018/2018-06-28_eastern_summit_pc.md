3.   Public hearing and possible action regarding a Plat Amendment to combine Lots 
16 & 17 of the Summit Subdivision; 3092 Crosstie Ct; SUM-16 & SUM-17; Martina 
Nelson, representative for owner. – Amir Caus, County Planner  
 
Planner Caus shared the background. The applicant is proposing to combine two lots. 
Density would be reduced to mitigate impacts to the infrastructure. Staff is recommending 
approval. Plat amendments where lot lines are being adjusted are usually done 
administratively but an easement is being removed in this case, so the Planning 
Commission is the land use authority.

Commissioner Wheaton asked if the easement needs to go anywhere else. Planner Caus 
answered that the easement will be removed for one lot area where the lot line is being 
removed, but will still go around the perimeter of each lot. The easement is a utility 
easement and the service provider approved the removal. 
Chair Clyde stated some of the Findings refer to plat notes that are CC&Rs and not County 
Ordinance. Planner Caus responded that according to the Promontory Development 
Agreement the County will enforce certain design guidelines. 
 
The public hearing was opened. No public comment was made, and the public hearing was 
closed. 
Findings of Fact 
1. The Summit Subdivision was recorded on April 3, 2007 as part of the overall Promontory 
Specially Planned Area.  
2. Lessie and James Miller are the owners of record of Parcels SUM-16 (1.2 acres) & SUM17 
(1.2 acres), located at 3092 Crosstie Ct., Promontory, Summit County, UT.  
3. The proposed Plat Amendment is legally described as “Summit First Amended Lot 16A.”  
4. As identified in Exhibit A of this Staff Report, the proposed Plat Amendment will result in 
combination of Lots 16 and 17 of the Summit Subdivision and the removal of the 10 foot utility 
and drainage easement from the lot line shared by the two lots.  
5. Note 10 of the Summit Subdivision states; Developer reserves the right to designate any lot or 
combination of lots shown on the plat that is in excess of one acre in size as an “estate lot.” The 
maximum building size limit of a home designated an “estate lot” within this plat is 8,000 square 
feet of Enclosed Livable Space, as defined in the Design Guidelines and any applicable 
Supplemental Design Guidelines and the minimum building size limit is 3,500 square feet of 
Enclosed Livable Space… The above size limits may be further limited by the Design Guidelines 
and Supplemental Design Guidelines. The maximum building size limit of home straddling two 
lots (as described in note 5), may be increased to a square footage not to exceed 12,000 square 
feet of Enclosed Livable Space for an “estate lot”… if approved by the Design Reviewer in the 
exercise of its sole discretion, in connection with the approval of a specific house plan.  
6. Note 5 of the Summit Subdivision states; The combination of two adjacent lots into a single 
lot may be permitted in certain areas as determined by the Declarant, in the exercise of the 
Declarant’s sole discretion. If an owner purchases two adjacent and contiguous lots, and the 
proposed house location straddles a lot line, it will be necessary to have the revised building 
envelope approved by the Design Reviewer in connection with the approval of a specific house 
plan. The approval or disapproval of the Design Reviewer may be made in the exercise of its sole 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission

discretion. Further, the combination of lots may require the approval of Summit County for a lot 
line adjustment or an amendment to the Plat. Request for such approval may not be submitted to 
Summit County without prior written approval of the Design Reviewer.  
7. Lots 16 and 17 of the Summit Subdivision are considered to be an “estate lot.” The applicant 
is requesting to combine the two lots which will result in an increase of the allowed livable space 
from 8,000 sq. ft. to 12,000 sq. ft.  
8. The applicant has provided a letter from Promontory supporting the proposed amendment.  
9. The proposed lot combination would reduce the potential density which could result in less

10. Postcard notices announcing the public hearing were mailed to property owners within 1,000

Conclusions of Law 
1. There is good cause for this Plat Amendment.  
2. The proposed Plat Amendment will not result in additional density.  
 
Conditions of Approval 
1. All service provider requirements shall be met.  
2. Per Summit County Council’s finding on September 16, 2009, a lot combination does not 
permit the re-platting of density that has already been designed, platted, and sold to private 
individuals. 
 
Commissioner Hanson made a motion to approve the Plat Amendment to combine Lots 
16 and 17 of the Summit Subdivision according to the Findings of Fact, Conclusions of 
Law and Conditions of Approval found in the Staff Report. Commissioner Wilde 
seconded the motion. 
 MOTION CARRIED (4-0)  
 
4.  Public hearing and possible action regarding a Plat Amendment on Lot 52 of the 
Deer Crossing Subdivision to relocate the 10-foot-wide Open Drainage Easement; 
8175 N Sunrise Loop; DC-52; Martina Nelson, representative for owner.  -Amir Caus, 
County Planner 
 
Planner Caus shared the background. Lot 71 of the Deer Crossing Subdivision had an 
approval to have a drainage easement moved in a previous Planning Commission meeting 
and this application is for a similar request. The applicant is proposing to move the 
easement to allow building where the current easement is located. Staff is recommending 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission

approval because the new easement better follows the natural contours of the land where 
actual water will flow. The proposed building site is less steep versus the other side of the 
lot.  
Martina Nelson, the applicant representative, stated that the drainage easement is flat like 
a platter, with no v-shaped ditch. The ends of the easement where it enters and exits the lot 
are the same. The original easements were proposed using aerial photography and are not 
as accurate as survey grade. Chair Clyde stated there is an obligation to property owners 
downstream, but the surveyor and Staff say this application is a better easement. 
Commissioner Wilde asked if there is a culvert across the road. Ms. Nelson answered 
there is not. There is no culvert drain or any drainage that goes under the road. 
Engineering did not find a reason to have drainage under the road. 
The public hearing was opened.  
Shawn Potter stated Promontory supports this application. 
The public hearing was closed.

4. The applicant proposes to situate a home on top of what is now identified as a drainage

5.   Public hearing and possible action regarding a Plat Amendment to combine Lots 
81 & 82 of the Deer Crossing Subdivision; 8195 N Garden Ranch Rd; DC-81 & DC-82; 
Martina Nelson, representative for owner. -Amir Caus, County Planner 
 
Planner Caus shared the background. This application is to combine lots 81 and 82 of the 
Deer Crossing Subdivision. Due to the odd shape of the lots, this will improve access to the 
lot and improve services to the lot. The density will be decreased. Staff is recommending 
approval of this application. 
Martina Nelson stated that by combining lots, the owner cannot build a house twice the 
size, but the owner may build a house one-and-a-half times the size. 
 
The public hearing was opened.  
Shawn Potter stated that Promontory has no objection to the lot combination. 
The public hearing was closed.

Commission Items 
Chair Clyde asked for clarification of the signature line of approval on the minutes. 
Director Putt answered he will let the commission know about the signature line. 
Chair Clyde stated that the public should have an opportunity to make comment about the 
changes being made to the Engineering Standards. Director Putt stated there is a law that 
states the Engineering Standards must be moved into the Development Code by a certain 
date. In the process, some changes needed to be made and Staff felt it appropriate that the 
Planning Commissions see what changes were made and why they were made. County 
Council directed Staff to create an Ordinance that would adopt the Engineering Standards 
by reference to keep the existing standards to comply with the law. The Engineering 
Standards will be reviewed by the Planning Commission at a future date for improvement. 
When the Standards were presented to the County Council there was a public hearing. 
Chair Clyde stated there seems to be a disconnect between Engineering and Planning, with
