GSLMSD – Contracted Staff Reports

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

                  Battalion Chief Russell updated the Council on the following:

     Managerial Staffing Policy is in place.

     Upstaffing Policy is in place. This policy allows the UFA to put additional ambulances and
      emergency response vehicles into service with little or no advance warning. The
      resources are now in place, stocked, and ready to go. Upstaffing can be implemented at
      any time of the day, with a short or long period of duration.

     The call volume for March is down significantly, with approximately 20 percent lower rates
      when compared to last March. This includes the earthquake response. However, these
      numbers are starting to increase.

     All staff that can are working from home.

     The station has implemented several control measures to keep the firefighters safe, this
      includes additional cleaning, all personnel on duty must wear mask in public and in the
      station, and no visitors allowed at the station.

     One firefighter has tested positive with COVID-19, tests have been done on 17 others.
      Personnel has responded to 182 incidents where it has been confirmed that there was a
      significant exposure to the virus.

Quiet Title Action on Library Road

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated when the
Kearns Library was originally built, the road was not properly dedicated to the County. Since that
time, Kearns Township has taken ownership of the road and a Quiet Title is needed in order to
close the road for the construction of the new library. Kearns Metro Township will be reimbursed
by the County for all expenses relating to the Quiet Title.

              Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Butterfield, moved to
approve the contract. The motion passed unanimously.

MCI Metro Franchise Agreement

                Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated all previous
utility agreements have been done under Salt Lake County. Work is being done to move the

        WHEREAS, the PROVIDER desires to provide voice, data or video transmission services
within the TOWNSHIP and in connection therewith to establish a telecommunications network in,
under, along, over and across present and future rights-of- way of the TOWNSHIP; and

        WHEREAS, the TOWNSHIP, in exercise of its management of public Rights-of-Way,

                            ARTICLE 1. FRANCHISE AGREEMENT.

        1.1    Agreement. Upon execution by the parties, this Agreement shall be deemed to
constitute a contract by and between TOWNSHIP and PROVIDER.

        1.2    Franchise Description. The Telecommunications Franchise provided hereby shall
confer upon the PROVIDER the nonexclusive right, privilege, and franchise to construct and
maintain a telecommunications network in, under, above and across the present and future public
Rights-of-Way in the TOWNSHIP. The franchise does not grant to the PROVIDER the right,
privilege or authority to engage in community antenna (or cable) television business; although,
nothing contained herein shall preclude the PROVIDER from: (1) permitting those with a cable
franchise who are lawfully engaged in such business to utilize the PROVIDER's System within
the TOWNSHIP for such purposes; or (2) from providing such service in the future if an
appropriate franchise is obtained and all other legal requirements have been satisfied or (3)
leasing all or a part of PROVIDER’s telecommunications network to affiliates or third parties,
provided that PROVIDER maintains ownership of the telecommunications network. Nothing in
this Agreement shall limit or in any way limit or negate the obligations of third parties that use or
lease PROVIDER’s system to comply with all applicable laws, regulations, and TOWNSHIP
Ordinances.

       1.3     Licenses. The PROVIDER acknowledges that it has obtained the necessary
approvals, licenses or permits required by federal and state law to provide telecommunication
services consistent with the provisions of this Agreement.

       1.4     Relationship. Nothing herein shall be deemed to create a joint venture or principal-
agent relationship between the parties and neither party is authorized to, nor shall either party act
toward third persons or the public in any manner that would indicate any such relationship with
each other.

                              ARTICLE 3. TERM AND RENEWAL.

         3.1     Term and Renewal. The franchise granted to PROVIDER shall be for a period of
ten (10) years commencing on the first day of the month following execution of this Agreement by
the TOWNSHIP, unless this Franchise be sooner terminated as herein provided. At the end of
the initial ten (10) year term of this Agreement, the franchise granted herein may be renewed by
the PROVIDER upon the same terms and conditions as contained in this Agreement for an
additional five (5) year term, by providing to the TOWNSHIP's representative designated herein
written notice of the PROVIDER's intent to renew not less than ninety (90) calendar days before
the expiration of the initial franchise term.

        3.2    Rights of PROVIDER upon Expiration or Revocation. Upon expiration of the
franchise granted herein, whether by lapse or time, by agreement between the PROVIDER and
the TOWNSHIP, or by revocation or forfeiture, the PROVIDER shall have the right to remove from
the Rights-of-Way any and all of its System, but in such event, it shall be the duty of the
PROVIDER, immediately upon such removal to restore the Rights-of-Way from which such
System is removed to as good condition as the same was before the removal was effected.

purposes and shall not include the provision of telecommunications service to third parties.

for the coronavirus disease of 2019 (“COVID-19”) on March 14, 2020 (the “Declaration”) pursuant
to Chapter 2.86 of the Kearns Metro Township Code (the “Code”); and

       WHEREAS, consistent with Chapter 2.86 of the Code and consistent with its own terms,
the Declaration is set to expire on April 13, 2020, thirty days after its adoption; and

       WHEREAS, the Declaration may be continued or renewed with consent the Kearns Metro
Township Council (the “Council”) pursuant to Chapter 2.86 of the Code and Utah Code Section
53-2a-208 of Utah’s Disaster Response and Recovery Act; and

      WHEREAS, guidance from the President of the United States received on March 29. 2020
recommended that states continue stay home orders until at least April 30, 2020; and

     WHEREAS, the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation expects the peak of the
COVID-19 pandemic in Utah to be April 24; and

        WHEREAS, the Council finds that the health, wellness, and safety of Kearns and its
citizens are served by continuing the Declaration for at least thirty additional days beyond its
current term; and

        WHEREAS, these conditions continue to constitute a “State of Emergency” within the
intent of the Disaster Response and Recovery Act found in Title 53, Chapter 2a of the Utah Code
Annotated 1953, as amended; and necessitate that Kearns continue the proclamation of a local
public health emergency.

       NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED, effective immediately by the Kearns
Metro Township Council that:

        SECTION 1: The Council consents to and hereby extend the Declaration by thirty days
from its current expiration, and sets the new expiration of the Declaration’s terms as May 13,
2020; and

       SECTION 2: The Mayor of Kearns may amend the Declaration consistent with Utah’s
Disaster Response and Recovery Act and Chapter 2.86 of the Code as needed to address the
coronavirus public health emergency as it evolves; and

      SECTION 3: This resolution shall be filed with the office of the Salt Lake County Clerk
pursuant to Utah Code Section 53-2a-208(c); and

      SECTION 4: This resolution shall take effect immediately upon its publication pursuant to
Utah Code Ann. §10-3-711(1) and Utah Code Ann. § 10-3-712.

       APPROVED and ADOPTED this 6th day of April, 2020.

Legislative Overview 2020

                  Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, reviewed the following
legislative bills that were discussed during the 2020 Legislative Session:

     S.B. 214 – Gambling and Machine Sweepstakes Amendments

              Mr. Bracken stated there were several lobbyist opposing this bill but it past with
over 90 percent of the votes in both houses. Governor Herbert has signed it.

     H.B. 273 – Property Rights Ombudsman Amendments

                Mr. Bracken stated this bill made some changes to the Property Right’s
Ombudsman Office. If a city takes an adverse decision from the Ombudsman Office to court and
losses, the city could be on the hook for $250 a day in damages from the date the ombudsman
decision to the date of the final court order. This could have a chilling effect on the ability of cities
to deny applications it feels does not comply with the code. The bill was changed to include
language that the denial or conditioning of a building permit needed to be knowingly and
intentionally wrong for the damages to apply. This puts the onerous on the cities to tightened up
its procedures as much as possible.

     H.B. 359 – Municipal Annexation Revisions

                Mr. Bracken stated this bill cuts the allocation that townships and the MSD receive
for roads from 14 percent down to 7 percent. Language was included that change the effective
date to 2025, giving time for the metros to get legislation passed to be considered full pledge
municipalities in order to get all the benefits that other cities get. This would give the metros the
authority to make up the difference.

Council Priorities of Land Use Changes

                 Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated a moratorium
has been placed on construction within the Kearns Metro Township so it could update its general
plan. The Steering Committee is currently working on this update. In the meantime, he would
like to work on changes to the housing element plan and the subdivision ordinance. He asked
the Council if it had any other priorities it would like him to work on while it waits for the general
plan to be updated.

               Mayor Bush stated she would like to see changes to the zoning ordinances.

               Mr. Bracken stated any changes to the zoning ordinance or conditional uses
should wait until the general plan is updated.

            Mayor Bush asked if the Council needed to be concerned about H.B. 273 when it
makes changes to the subdivision ordinance.

                 Mr. Bracken stated he wanted to make changes to the subdivision ordinance
because of H.B. 273. The changes he would propose is to make it clear that when the subdivision
is approved that is what is approved. The contractor cannot come back with substantial changes
to the plat. If there are substantial changes then the plat needs to go back before the planning
commission for approval. The housing element focuses on affordable housing and makes
changes to the mother-in-law apartments ordinance. The moratorium gives the Council time to
make the needed changes.

All Council Members agreed with the proposed priorities.
