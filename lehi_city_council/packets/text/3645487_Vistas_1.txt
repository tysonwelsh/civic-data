  REPORT ANALYSIS

The applicant requests approval of revisions to the approved amenities for the Vistas at the Point Transit Oriented
Development Project. This previously came to the Planning Commission on May 8, 2025 and again on September
11, 22025. The applicant has made some updates to their request and provided more details. Staff also has included
more information. This submittal includes the minutes from the City Council meeting and a link for the wind study.

Previously the applicant requested removal of the amphitheater. Their updated request seeks to change the
amphitheater to a dog park. The applicant provided justification for this proposal in their narrative and provided a
conceptual layout of the park.

The applicant also provided a conceptual layout for the overlook park on the trail. The overlook park was part of
the original amenity approval. Both of these will require site plan approval before construction and City Staff will
ensure they meet all Code requirements for the dog park.

The Planning Commission originally approved a substitution of amenities on August 26, 2021. With 404 apartment
and townhome units the applicant needed to provide 14 picnic areas, 2 sports courts, 6 playgrounds/dog parks, a
clubhouse at least 1,500 square feet in size and 2 pools, tennis courts, or splash pads. The applicant proposed the
regional trail through their site with an overlook park at the top, a clubhouse, and an amphitheater.

The Planning Commission makes the decision on approving any substitutions to the required amenities, including
this removal of the amphitheater. In doing so the Planning Commission should consider if the proposed dog park
provides a similar or greater benefit to the Vistas at the Point community.

The included DRC comments were based on the first submittal and do not reflect the updated plans.

Lehi City Planning Commission,

We are pleased to present this proposal on behalf of Geneva Rock Products for an updated
amenity plan in our forthcoming Lehi residential community, which includes 304 apartments and
100 townhomes. After thoughtful consideration and research, we ask that the originally planned
amphitheater be replaced with a dog park and a scenic overlook park. Please see the included
concepts and planned layout.

At Clyde Capital Group part of our Vision statement is to produce sustainable
community assets we can enjoy and share with others for generations. Our vision is grounded in
creating usable, inviting spaces that bring joy and utility in every season. An amphitheater tends
to be highly dependent on nice weather, scheduled events, and is more season-limited, especially
in colder months when attendance drops. In contrast, a dog park and a passive overlook park
offer daily, year-round access, including during winter. Dog parks are often noted as among the
most reliably active community spaces throughout the year. In Utah roughly 36 percent of
households have a dog.

This isn't a hypothetical advantage, statistics bear it out. Approximately 85 percent of dog parks
remain open year-round. Dog owners visit dog parks on average 2 to 3 times per week, and
22 percent include dog park visits in their weekly routines, even during colder months. In
addition, dog parks significantly boost social and mental health and 91 percent of Americans
believe dog parks benefit their communities.

Economically and socially, the merits are equally strong. Residential areas near dog parks see
property values rise by around 2 to 6 percent and up to 12 percent in some cases. Beyond
monetary gains, dog parks build community networks: neighbors meet and forge relationships
while their dogs play, and in some parks, community advocacy groups form to maintain and
enliven the space.

Likewise, the inclusion of an overlook enhances the benefit of open space in the slope north and
east of development. I have always enjoyed watching the paragliders and hang gliders when I
have found myself in that area and I imagine this Overlook Park full of community members
enjoying the elevated views and spectating the neighboring recreators. Additionally, exposure to
natural, elevated viewing areas supports stress reduction, encourages walking and reflection, and
can raise property values. Together, the dog park and overlook create a versatile network of
amenities that elevate daily life for residents of all ages, regardless of season.

In our community of 404 homes, we anticipate the dog park will attract a substantial number of
regular users, both canine and human, throughout the year. Its off-leash design welcomes pet
owners in all seasons. The overlook park complements this by drawing families, individuals, and
neighbors into an accessible, contemplative space that fosters health, social engagement, and
peaceful enjoyment, even when the amphitheater would lie dormant.

We believe these amenities will deliver enduring value beyond the original proposed
amphitheater. These community assets ensure constant activation, social cohesion, and aesthetic
richness, benefiting both our residents and the city at large.

consideration of this improved amenity plan.
