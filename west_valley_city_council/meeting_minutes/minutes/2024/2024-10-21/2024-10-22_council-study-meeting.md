    Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
    information as follows:

           City staff is proposing this change at the direction of the City Council.
           Currently, tattoo establishments are only allowed in the Manufacturing (M)
           zone where there is no overlay zone. Permanent cosmetics are allowed in
           the Mixed Use (MXD), City Center (CC), Neighborhood Commercial (C-
           1), General Commercial (C-2), Transitional Commercial (C-3), and
           Manufacturing (M) zones.

           In December of 1997, the City Council adopted Ordinance 97-65 that made
           tattoo establishments a conditional use in the C-3 zone and a permitted use
           in M zone. In March of 2017, the City Council adopted Ordinance 17-10
           that removed tattoo establishments as a conditional use in the C-3 zone. It
           is unclear how tattoo establishments were handled prior to December of
           1997.

           The following definitions are helpful background information from Section
           7-1-103:
                “Tattoo Establishment” means any location, place, area, Structure,
                  or business used for the practice or instruction of Tattooing or
                  Body Piercing, excluding Permanent Cosmetics Establishments.
                “Tattoo” means a mark or design made on or under the skin by a
                  process of pricking or ingraining an indelible pigment, dye, or ink
                  in the skin, excluding Permanent Cosmetics.
                “Body Piercing” means the act of piercing any part of the body of
                  an individual, excepting the ears, for the purpose of allowing the
                  insertion of earrings, jewelry, or similar objects into the body.
                “Permanent Cosmetics” means a mark or design made on or under
                  the skin by a process of pricking or ingraining an indelible
                  pigment, dye, or ink in the skin for masking discolorations or
                  cosmetically enhancing facial features which shall follow the
                  natural line of the feature. “Permanent Cosmetics” are limited to
                  eyeliner, eyebrows, and lip coloring procedures.

    The City Council will consider Ordinance 24-44 at the Regular Council Meeting
    scheduled November 12, 2024 at 6:30 P.M.

A.     AIRSOFT GUN DISCUSSION
       Colleen Jacobs, Police Chief, stated that a young woman had previously presented
       on airsoft guns, and a communication item had been requested. The police
       department has responded to over 90,000 calls so far this year, 43 of which involved
       airsoft guns. Thirteen of the 43 calls were related to juveniles. Chief Jacobs stated
       that the use of airsoft guns violates city ordinance 21-3-104, classifying them as
       projectile weapons and making it a Class B misdemeanor. She noted that Parks and
       Recreation reported that airsoft guns were commonly used in parks but often went
       unreported. To address this, suggestions included educating employees to report
       incidents, posting signs in parks, and spreading awareness on social media. Chief
       Jacobs emphasized that airsoft guns look very realistic, affecting public safety
       perceptions. While the city ordinance addresses many concerns, it does not cover
       the removal of the orange tip, a safety feature. Chief Jacobs stated that in one
       incident, the orange tip had been removed. She noted the difficulty law enforcement
       faced when responding to calls, as fake guns could look identical to real guns, and
       in some cases, real guns were painted to resemble toys.

       Councilmember Nordfelt asked if Chief Jacobs had any recommendations. She
       replied that she would suggest an ordinance that would forbid removing the orange
       tip. Councilmember Harmon stated that he doesn’t believe there are enough
       incidents to implement an ordinance at this time. Councilmember Whetstone stated
       that he would support signs that forbade airsoft guns in parks. Mayor Lang
       suggested education efforts. She indicated that a single incident of an officer
       injuring or killing someone for possession of an airsoft gun would be detrimental.
       Eric Bunderson, City Attorney, suggested taking the issue up with the legislature.
       Councilmember Fitisemanu stated that he would prefer looking at this on a City
       level first. After discussion, the Council requested an ordinance change drafted by
       Legal that would address the concerns mentioned.

       The Mayor and Council had no further questions or concerns.

B.     COUNCIL REQUESTED FOLLOW UP

       Sunset Hills Traffic Enforcement
       Eric Bunderson, City Attorney, explained that there is a process by which a private
       road can receive a quasi-public designation, allowing the police to enforce traffic
       laws on it. This process involves the Homeowners Association (HOA) holding a
       majority vote—not just from the board, but from the actual residents. Once the
       majority of the HOA agrees, they present the results to the city. Eric recommended
       that the HOA handle the voting process themselves. Once they return with proof of
       majority support, the city could easily pass an ordinance, following a public
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – OCTOBER 22, 2024
                            -6-


       hearing, to allow enforcement on those streets. The main challenge, he noted, would
       be for the HOA to secure the necessary vote.

       Ifo Pili, City Manager, emphasized the importance of managing expectations with
       residents regarding police enforcement on private roads. He clarified that while the
       police would enforce laws if present, they do not have the resources to permanently
       station officers in private areas. He expressed concern that residents might expect
       continuous police presence, which the city cannot provide. Ifo explained that
       enforcement would occur as it does throughout the city—officers would respond if
       needed, but not on a permanent or preferential basis. He highlighted that even on
       public roads, many residents already request more traffic enforcement. The goal
       was to ensure that the process was clear and that expectations were properly
       communicated to avoid misunderstandings. Councilmember Whetstone asked if
       this would apply to snow removal as well. Ifo replied no and stated it would only
       apply to traffic enforcement.

       Councilmember Fitisemanu asked if they could request speed bumps. Mayor Lang
       stated that this should possibly be their starting point within their community.
       Councilmember Nordfelt stated that there may be unforeseen issues.
       Councilmember Fitisemanu suggested that staff reach out and encourage them to
       try other things within their HOA before taking this step.

       The Mayor and Council had no further questions or concerns.
