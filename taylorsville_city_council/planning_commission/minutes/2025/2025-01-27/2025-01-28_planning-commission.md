BRIEFING SESSION - 6:00 P.M.

Chair Don Russell called the meeting to order at 6:03 p.m. and turned the time over to
Senior Planner Jim Spung who mentioned that Commissioner Wilkey was attending via
Zoom. He explained that contrary to the verbiage on the agenda, Item #4 was not a
request for a reasonable accommodation but instead was a request for Conditional Use
Permit.

Long Range Planner Mark McGrath indicated that his presentation during the regular
meeting would be very brief.
Terryne Bergeson gave a brief overview of Item #4. She explained that one of the
applicants, Marcela Ayala, already had a reception center in town but was merely wishing
to relocate it to 4311 South 2700 West at the new Beltway West Center. This was possible
with only staff approval due to the new standards but because the area of the new space
exceeded 2,500 square feet, the application fell under the Planning Commission’s
purview. This particular space was slightly more than 5,000 square feet. The property
was zoned for Professional Office and the applicants had submitted a site plan showing
that their specific space was approximately 900 feet away from residences. The owner of
the property would be working with the Building Department in order to make the
necessary updates to the building so that the space could be used as a reception center.

, That work would be done before a business license was issued. Part of those updates
would include soundproofing.

CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT (Administrative Action
4. Public Hearing and Consideration for a Conditional Use Permit to Operate a
Reception Center at 4311 South 2700 West in Taylorsville, Utah (File No. 1C25
— CUP-000502-2024; Terryne Bergeson, Planner)

Ms. Bergeson explained that Marcela Ayala currently operated the Munequitos
Event Center in the Westwood Shopping Center. She and her business partners were
interested in relocating to a larger space at Beltway West Plaza. Under the newly adopted
standards, a reception center with an active license could relocate with approval of an
administrative conditional use permit and venues with more than 2,500 square feet of
floor area shall be reviewed by the Planning Commission.

The subject property was bounded by Professional-Office Zoning on the North, South and
West sides, with I-215 on the East.
A public notice was mailed to property owners within 300 feet of the subject property on
January 15, 2025 and no comments had been submitted from members of the public.
Reception centers over 2,500 square feet were a permitted use in the professional office
zone with the approval of a non-administrative conditional use permit. The owner of an
actively licensed center could relocate within the city, subject to review for compliance
with newly adopted standards for reception centers, §13.11.250 (Ex I). The new location
was not within 1,000 feet of another licensed center and was over 900 feet from the
nearest residential zone. The proposed location met proximity requirements and qualified
to hold events until 12:00 a.m. as outlined in the standards.
Beltway West had adopted cross-access agreements that allowed shared parking but
encouraged owners to attempt to keep parking on their own lot. The reception center
would be located at the far south end of Building A, on lot 1. The lot had over 136 parking
spaces within its boundaries, well over the required 10 per 1,000 SF now required for
reception centers.
Property owners were making improvements to prepare the space for the use and had
stated that soundproofing in the walls would be installed, as well as additional bathrooms
(based on occupant load). The Building Department reviewed the preliminary floor plan
(Ex G) and estimated that the proposed space could have a maximum occupant load of
506. A final determination would require coordination with an architect to show how the
gathering space will be used and to satisfy bathroom to occupant ratios defined in the
IBC. Occupant load calculations ranged from 15 sq ft per person with permanent tables

Chair Russell invited the applicants to step forward and address the Planning
Commission. Marcela and Michael Lowe introduced themselves.
Commissioner Quigley asked how long they had had a reception center at current
location. Mr. Lowe responded that it had been almost two years. He understood that many
other centers resulted in problems for nearby residents and the police department, but
they had no such issues. Their facility was typically used to host family parties of 50-100
people. The new location would be of a larger scale and would allow for bigger, more
upscale events.
Commissioner Willardson thanked the applicants for running a business without the
problems that so many other similar businesses seemed to have.
Chair Russell opened the public hearing. However, there was no one who expressed a
desire to speak either in person or online, so the Chair closed the public hearing.

unanimously.
