Welcome,

Whether you have just joined our staff or have been at Riverton City for a while, we are
confident you will find Riverton City a dynamic and rewarding place to work, and we look
forward to a productive and successful association. We consider the employees of Riverton
City to be one of its most valuable resources. This manual has been written to serve as the
guide for the employer/employee relationship.

This manual is a unilateral expression of current Riverton City policies and procedures. The
intent of this manual is to provide guidance to management and employees. It is not intended
to be comprehensive or to address all the possible applications of, or exceptions to, the general
policies and procedures described. With the Assistance of Riverton City department managers
and supervisors, employees must become familiar with the Riverton Municipal Code, and all
City policies (such as Occupational Safety), which apply to the department in which an
individual is employed. Employees with questions concerning eligibility for a particular benefit,
or the applicability of a policy or practice to, should address such questions to the Human
Resources Department.

The procedures, practices, policies and benefits described here may be modified or
discontinued from time to time. We will make every reasonable effort to inform you of any
changes as they occur. Some of the subjects described here are covered in detail in other
official documents, ordinances or resolutions. You should refer to those documents for specific
information when necessary.

As an employee of Riverton City, you will be expected to read, understand, and follow the
policies and procedures contained in this manual.

1.1 INTRODUCTION
This document supersedes any ordinance, resolution, policy or action previously approved or
undertaken by the Riverton City Council. While it is the policy of Riverton City to establish reasonable
rules of employment conduct and to ensure compliance with these rules through a program consistent
with the best interests of the City and its employees, this Manual is not and shall not be construed as an
explicit or implied contract and shall not modify the City employee status as an at-will employee. This
Manual shall not vest employees with any property rights in excess of rights established under
federal or state constitutional or statutory law.

                             PART 2               EMPLOYMENT POLICIES
2.1 EQUAL EMPLOYMENT OPPORTUNITY
Equal Employment Opportunity has been and will continue to be a fundamental principle at Riverton
City, where employment is based upon personal capabilities and qualifications without discrimination
because of race, color, religion, sex, age, national origin, disability, or any other protected category as
established by law.

This policy of Equal Employment Opportunity applies to all policies and procedures relating to
recruitment and hiring, advancement/promotion, compensation, benefits, termination, and all other
terms and conditions of employment. The City will continually review its policies and procedures to
identify and eliminate: 1) barriers that unnecessarily exclude protected classes; and 2) practices and
policies which create a discriminatory impact upon employees.

The Human Resource designee, under direction of the City Manager, shall ensure compliance and
implementation of this policy through reporting and monitoring procedures. An employee’s questions
or concerns should be referred to the Human Resources Department.

Appropriate disciplinary action may be taken against any employee willfully violating these policies.
2.2 AMERICANS WITH DISABILITIES ACT (AS AMENDED) (ADA)
Riverton City shall not discriminate against a qualified individual with a disability because of his/her
disability in regard to job application procedures, hiring, advancement/promotion, discharge, employee
compensation, job training, and other terms, conditions, and privileges of employment. In selecting
applicants for employment, promotion, or transfer, Riverton City may utilize qualification standards,
tests, or selection criteria that screen out, tend to screen out, or otherwise deny a job or benefit to an
individual with a disability if such standards, tests, or criteria have been shown to be job related, are
consistent with a business necessity, and if such performance cannot be accomplished by reasonable
accommodation as required by law.

Riverton City shall not require a medical examination or make inquiries of a job applicant to determine
whether he/she has a disability, or about the nature or severity of such disability. The City may require
a medical examination after an offer of employment has been made and prior to the commencement of
the employment duties. The City may also condition an offer of employment on the results of such
examination if:

    1. All entering employees within that job description are subjected to such examination regardless
       of disability;
    2. Information obtained regarding the medical condition or history of the applicant is collected and
       maintained on separate forms and in separate medical files, and is treated as a confidential
       medical record;
    3. The results of the examination are used only in accordance with law.

The City may make inquiries into the ability of an employee to perform job-related functions. At the City
Manager’s request, employees with accommodations due to disabilities may be asked to update the
Human Resources Department annually about whether the accommodation is meeting the employee’s
needs, or if modifications need to be made to the accommodation agreement. Riverton City may
require voluntary medical examinations, including voluntary medical histories, which are part of an
employee health program. A test to determine the illegal use of drugs, or any use of alcohol in the
workplace is not considered a medical examination.

REASONABLE ACCOMMODATIONS
Governing federal law prohibits discrimination against persons with disabilities who, with reasonable
accommodation, can perform the essential functions of the job in question. In order to implement this
“reasonable accommodations” concept as defined by law, Riverton City reserves the right to make
exceptions to any of the provisions contained in this manual.

If a job applicant with a disability discloses the need for an accommodation to allow him/her to perform
the job, the City will carefully evaluate the request and make reasonable accommodations as required
by law. The City has no obligation to evaluate or make any accommodation without prior request from
the applicant. Applicants seeking accommodation for a disability should contact Human Resources. At
any time following hire, an employee may become afflicted with one or more disabilities. Such an
employee should discuss any new temporary or permanent disability with their supervisor/Human
Resources and should candidly explain and request any desired accommodations.

Recent amendments to applicable federal law have created an expanded and extensive list of
“disabilities.” If a supervisor receives notice of an employee’s disability, or receives inquiries or requests
from employees regarding accommodations, he/she must forward the notices/requests to Human
Resources for review.

An employee or applicant requesting an accommodation must complete the City’s ADA Access and
“Accommodation Request” form and have his/her certified medical provider complete the City’s “ADA
Request for Information from Medical Provider” form. Both of these forms are available from Human
Resources.

Under no circumstances may a supervisor or anyone other than the City Manager grant or deny a
reasonable accommodation. All reasonable accommodations must be reevaluated annually and
reviewed by Human Resources and the City Manager.

ADDITIONAL DISABILITY INFORMATION
If the employee is temporarily disabled, the City shall hold the employee’s position open for six (6)
months. The City may fill the position with temporary personnel for the six-month period the employee
is temporarily disabled. The City may elect to extend the length of time the position is held open for up
to one (1) year from the onset of the disability. If the employee does not return to work prior to the six-
month mark after disability, the City may fill the open position. If the employee returns to work


Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                                   Page | 2
between six (6) months and one (1) year from the date of disability, the employee will be offered the
first available, comparable opening for which he/she is qualified. The employee’s employment status
with Riverton City will be terminated if he/she is unable to return to work prior to the completion of one
(1) year of disability. Employees on either an approved disability or workers’ compensation leave of
absence will be responsible for paying only the employee portion of the health, dental, and life
insurance premium until their employment is terminated with Riverton City. Upon termination of
employment, the terminated employee will be eligible for COBRA. When the employee is ready to
return to work, he/she will be required to provide a physician’s statement that he/she is able to perform
the essential functions of the position. (See light duty section in this Manual.)

LIGHT DUTY
An employee who is able to resume work after disability leave of absence, may be brought on staff in a
light duty capacity for a period of up to 30 days unless a longer period of time is approved by the
Department Head and the City Manager. The employee shall be on light duty until the employee is able
to perform all essential functions. Employees may be required, if able, to perform their regular duty for
part of the work shift. An employee shall promptly notify the appropriate Department Head of the
employee’s ability to perform light duty. The corresponding Department Head shall assign light duty
activities, but “make work” projects are not required to be created for the employee. Activities will be
commensurate with the employee’s current skills and abilities. Activities may not be confined to one
department, but may be based on City-wide needs. Employee pay while on light duty may be different
from the hourly wage received prior to entering disability leave or leave of absence status dependent
upon the discretion of the Department Head. Once the employee fills a different or new regular
position, the pay will be adjusted in accordance with the position.
2.3 ANTI-DISCRIMINATION AND ANTI-HARASSMENT
The purpose of this policy is to:

    1. Declare Riverton City’s commitment to provide a work environment free from all forms of
       discrimination and harassment (the terms discrimination and harassment are used
       interchangeably herein);
    2. Prevent City employees from being subjected to any form of harassment;
    3. Ensure full compliance with Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Utah Antidiscrimination
       Act of 1965 (Utah Code Annotated, Title 34A, Chapter 5), and other federal and state laws
       prohibiting discrimination;
    4. Advise employees of their rights and responsibilities in achieving the goal of a workplace free
       from harassment;
    5. Define clearly what constitutes harassment;
    6. Provide an efficient, effective, and timely mechanism for reporting, investigating, and resolving
       internal complaints of harassment without fear of retaliation.

HARASSMENT POLICY
All City employees have the legal right to work in an environment free from harassment and
discrimination. It is critical that all employees treat all other employees with dignity and respect.
Harassment of a job applicant or employee by a supervisor or co-worker on the basis of race, religion,
color, national origin, disability, pregnancy, gender, or age, or any other protected class is explicitly in
violation of state and/or federal law and will not be tolerated by the City. Any harassing or otherwise
inappropriate behavior which impacts the workplace, or has the potential to impact the workplace, will
not be tolerated. An essential component of this policy is that all employees are allowed to work
without fear of retaliation under this policy. Any action taken by an employee in retaliation against


Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                                  Page | 3
another employee who has reported, or may report workplace harassment shall be deemed to be a
separate violation of this policy and may subject the perpetrator to additional discipline. This policy
applies to all terms and conditions of employment, including but not limited to: hiring, firing,
placement, promotion, disciplinary action, layoff, reinstatement, transfer, leave of absence,
compensation, fringe benefits, and training. City management is committed to vigorously enforcing this
policy at all levels of the City organization. This policy is in effect at all times and in all places.
Employees found to be participating in any form of job-based harassment or discrimination, including
retaliation against another employee, shall be subject to disciplinary action up to and including
termination from employment.

DEFINITIONS
Harassment includes but is not limited to the following behaviors:

Harassment, generally: Harassment is unwelcome conduct that is based on race, color, sex, religion,
national origin, disability, and/or age. Harassment becomes unlawful and a violation of the Riverton City
anti-harassment policy where: (1) enduring the offensive conduct becomes a condition of continued
employment, or (2) the conduct is severe or pervasive enough to create a work environment that a
reasonable person would consider intimidating, hostile, or abusive.

Physical Harassment: Assault, impeding or blocking movement, leering, or the physical interference
with normal work privacy or movement when directed at an individual on the basis of race, religion,
color, national origin, disability, pregnancy, gender, or age. This includes pinching, patting, grabbing,
intentional brushing against another’s body, inappropriate behavior in or near bathrooms, sleeping
facilities and eating areas, giving of unsolicited or inappropriate gifts of a personal or private nature, or
making explicit or implied threats or promises in return for submission to physical acts.

Sexual Harassment: According to the federal Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, sexual
harassment consists of unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, and other verbal or
physical conduct of a sexual nature when:

    1. Submission to such conduct is made either explicitly or implicitly a term or condition of an
       individual’s employment;
    2. An employment decision is based on an individual’s acceptance or rejection of such conduct;
    3. Such conduct unreasonably interferes with an individual’s work performance, or creates an
       intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment.

Verbal Harassment: Epithets, derogatory comments, insults, slurs, propositioning, jokes or otherwise
offensive words or comments on the basis of race, religion, color, national origin, disability, pregnancy,
gender or age, whether made in general, directed to an individual, or to a group of people regardless of
whether the behavior was intended to harass. This includes, but is not limited to, inappropriate sexually
oriented comments on appearance, including dress or physical features, sexual rumors, code words,
race oriented stories, subtle or direct pressure for sexual activity, and stating or implying that an
individual’s job performance is attributable to the person’s gender.

Visual Forms of Harassment: Derogatory, prejudicial, stereotypical or otherwise offensive posters,
photographs, cartoons, e-mails, use of computer or Internet, notes, bulletins, drawings or pictures on
the basis of race, religion, color, national origin, disability, pregnancy, gender or age. This applies to
either posted material or material maintained in or on City equipment or personal property in the
workplace. Employees must understand that harassment often involves perception. What may be
intended as innocent or harmless conduct may be perceived as inappropriate harassment-based

Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                                   Page | 4
conduct. Since one person cannot control another’s perception, it is always a better choice to avoid
conduct that could be perceived as being inappropriately harassing in nature.

RETALIATION
No employee, supervisor, or department head may take any action in any form against a person for
filing a charge of harassment, participating in an employment harassment proceeding, or opposing
harassing practices, even if such complaint is found unjustified, so long as the complainant’s action is
based on a reasonable, good-faith belief that the complained of practice violates anti-discrimination law.
No employee, supervisor, or department head may take any action in any form against a person because
that person is suspected of filing a charge of harassment or participating in an employee harassment
proceeding. For purposes of clarification:

Participation: Participating in a proceeding means taking part in an employment discrimination
proceeding, including but not limited to, filing a charge of employment discrimination, cooperating with
an internal investigation of alleged discriminatory practices, or serving as a witness in a harassment
investigation or litigation.

Opposition: Opposing discriminatory practices means informing an employer that the employee
believes the employer is engaging in prohibited discrimination, including but not limited to, complaining
about alleged discrimination against oneself or others, threatening to file a charge of discrimination,
picketing in opposition to discrimination, or refusing to obey an order reasonably believed to be
discriminatory. The manner of opposition must be reasonable.

RESPONSIBILITIES
Management and Supervision: It is the responsibility of management to:

    1. Keep this policy current;
    2. Ensure each employee knows the policy;
    3. Check the workplace and work environment regularly to ensure the policy is being followed;
    4. Enforce the policy;
    5. Ensure that any violation of this policy brought to their attention is dealt with fairly, quickly, and
       impartially;
    6. Make necessary corrections in the behavior of their employees when a deviation from this
       policy is noted or reported;
    7. Set the proper example at all times.

Employees: Every employee has the responsibility to:

    1. Know the policy;
    2. Comply with the policy and avoid any conduct that constitutes harassment;
    3. Report instances of harassment;
    4. Cooperate fully in any investigation, including the obligation to provide truthful and complete
       evidence and testimony in any investigation or proceeding;
    5. Refrain from making any bad faith or known false complaints of harassment;
    6. Refrain from retaliation of any kind.

COMPLAINT PROCEDURE
Confrontation: There may be situations where the affected individual may recognize that the alleged
harasser is not aware that his or her conduct is harassing and the affected individual may feel


Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                                  Page | 5
comfortable speaking directly to the alleged harasser. If the affected individual feels he/she is the victim
of any form of harassment or discriminatory treatment, the affected individual should, but is not
required to, inform the person(s) participating in this behavior that he/she finds it offensive. This one-
on-one dialogue has been demonstrated to be an effective way to end harassing behaviors. If the
inappropriate behaviors do not stop, the offended employee can initiate a complaint as described
below. Because confrontation is difficult for some people and because of the complex nature of
harassment, employees are not required to confront an offending party prior to initiating this complaint
procedure.

FILING PROCEDURE
Reports: The City cannot respond to and remedy harassment unless it is made aware of the conduct.
The failure to promptly file a report may adversely affect an individual’s right of recourse, may result in
harassment of others, and will hinder the City’s ability to respond effectively to a complaint. The filing
of a report will facilitate the handling of the complaint, but the paramount concern is to provide a
means, whereby a report may be filed without fear of retaliation or adverse consequences, while
assuring the rights of all parties are protected. Reports of discriminatory workplace harassment may be
made orally or in writing and shall be made to any one of the following:

             Immediate supervisor
             Any other supervisor or manager
             Any Department Head
             Human Resources
             City Attorney
             City Manager
             Mayor
             City Council Members

By the Subject of Harassment: Any employee who believes he/she has been the subject of harassment
must report the incident within 30 calendar days of the alleged incident.

By an Observer: A report may also be filed in the same manner by a person who observes harassment,
but who is not the subject of the harassment.

By Management: Any supervisor or manager who observes inappropriate behavior or receives a
harassment complaint shall notify immediately the victim/complainant’s Department Head. Any
Department Head who observes inappropriate behavior or receives a harassment complaint shall
immediately notify the City Manager.

By the Perpetrator: An individual who believes that he/she has engaged in harassing behavior may self-
report in the same manner. Self-reporting will not negate the consequences or policies set forth herein,
but will be considered as one factor in resolution of the matter.

INVESTIGATION AND HANDLING

GENERAL GUIDELINES
Investigation: Every complaint of harassment or discriminatory treatment will be investigated
thoroughly, fairly, impartially, and promptly, and under direction of the City Attorney. Where necessary,
the City may retain the services of qualified consultants and experts to investigate complaints.



Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                                 Page | 6
Confidentiality: Information will be disseminated on a strict need-to-know basis and confidentiality will
be maintained to the fullest extent possible; however, there can be no assurance of complete
confidentiality. All employees shall be reminded that the complaint shall not be discussed with others
and that retaliation is prohibited.

Protection: Reasonable steps will be taken to protect the reporting individual and others participating in
the proceeding. A person who files a complaint in good faith with reasonable belief that there has been
harassment will face no adverse consequences.

False Complaints: Any harassment complaint which is conclusively proven to be false shall result in
discipline up to and including termination if the complainant is found to have knowingly made a false
complaint. This section is not intended to discourage employees from making complaints regarding
harassment. However, false complaints adversely impact the workplace and the career of the accused,
and will not be tolerated.

Obstruction: An employee who is involved in a harassment investigation shall not question, coerce,
intimidate, or retaliate in any way against an employee who has filed a complaint of harassment, or
against employees who have participated in the proceeding. Disciplinary action will be taken against
any employee who obstructs any investigation of harassment or retaliation.

Recusal of Management: Any supervisor, manager, or department head accused of harassment shall
not conduct or participate in any part of the Complaint Procedure outlined herein except as an accused
subject of the allegation.

INITIAL INVESTIGATION AND INFORMAL RESOLUTION
Purpose: The purpose of the initial investigation is to determine if there appears to be merit to the
complaint and if informal resolution may be appropriate and possible.

Initial Investigation: The complainant’s Department Head or his/her designee shall conduct the initial
investigation. Initially, the complainant shall be interviewed and any evidence of harassment offered by
the complainant shall be reviewed. If it is determined that there may be merit to the complaint, but
that informal resolution may be appropriate and possible, the Department Head or his/her designee
shall continue the investigation, interviewing the alleged perpetrator and any other persons who
possess relevant information. If no merit is found, the Department Head may still meet with the parties
involved to attempt to resolve the complaint or conflict.

Informal Resolution: After completing the initial investigation, if the complaint is resolved informally to
the satisfaction of the complainant, an entry shall be made in each party’s personnel file, except as
indicated otherwise, noting:

    1. A harassment complaint was filed;
    2. Harassment was found to have occurred (no such entry will be made in the complainant’s
       personnel file);
    3. Harassment was not found to have occurred;
    4. The investigation was inconclusive as to whether harassment occurred;
    5. The parties mutually agreed to a resolution of the complaint;
    6. The specific features of parties’ resolution.

Role of Human Resources: The Department Head shall notify Human Resources of the complaint, and
persons undertaking the initial investigation and informal resolution may consult with Human


Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                                 Page | 7
Resources, as needed. Separate records of the investigation and informal resolution shall be retained as
protected files maintained by Human Resources and in accordance with the Utah State Archives
Retention Schedule.

Referral for Further Investigation: After initially interviewing the complainant, (1) if the Department
Head attempts informal resolution but is unsuccessful, or (2) if the Department Head determines that
there may be merit to the complaint and informal resolution would not be appropriate, he/she shall
immediately direct the complaint to the City Manager for further action.

FORMAL INVESTIGATION AND FORMAL RESOLUTION
Initial Determination of Action: After an initial investigation has been conducted and if there is no
informal resolution and/or conciliation of the complaint, the Department Head, City Attorney, Human
Resource designee, and City Manager shall determine:

    1. If there appears to be merit to the complaint;
    2. If additional investigation should be conducted;
    3. If additional investigation is required, whether action is required to secure the personal safety of
       the complainant or other individuals. Any appropriate precautionary action may be taken,
       including but not limited to separation of the parties, additional supervision, or administrative
       leave of either party, with or without pay;
    4. If additional investigation is required, whether the investigation should be conducted internally
       or by an outside investigator;
    5. If additional investigation is required, and who should conduct it.

Outside Investigator: The City shall:

    1. Solicit a neutral and unbiased investigator;
    2. Solicit an investigator with appropriate investigative experience;
    3. Direct the scope of the investigation, but the City shall not direct the method of investigation.

Notice to Parties: Prior to commencing the formal investigation, the City shall notify:

    1. The alleged perpetrator of the nature of the complaint, but the City shall not be required to
       provide him/her with details underlying the complaint or the initial investigation;
    2. The complainant and the alleged perpetrator of the City’s intent to investigate further;
    3. The complainant, the alleged perpetrator, and all intended interviewees of the outside
       investigator’s qualifications;
    4. All interviewees that the interviews are being audibly or video recorded.

INVESTIGATION, INVESTIGATION REPORT AND WRITTEN DECISION
The investigation may include interviews with the complainant, the alleged perpetrator, and any other
persons determined by the investigator to possibly have relevant knowledge concerning the complaint.
This may include victims of similar conduct. The investigator may accept written comments, but no such
comments may be anonymous.

    1. All interviews shall be audibly or video recorded.
    2. Factual information gathered through the investigation will be reviewed to determine whether
       the alleged conduct constitutes harassment, giving consideration to all factual information, the




Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                                Page | 8
         totality of the circumstances including the nature of the verbal, physical, visual or sexual
         conduct, and the context in which the alleged incident(s) occurred.
    3.   Upon completing the investigation, the investigator shall prepare a written report. The report
         shall identify all witnesses interviewed, all written information and physical evidence received,
         all documents and other evidence reviewed, and all findings of fact made and the basis for such
         findings. Copies of the report shall be provided to the complainant and the alleged perpetrator.
    4.   The complainant and the alleged perpetrator may respond to the investigation report in writing
         within five (5) calendar days.
    5.   Upon receipt of the written responses from complainant and the alleged perpetrator, the City
         shall determine and conduct any further investigation made necessary by the parties’ written
         responses.
    6.   If no further investigation is necessary or upon completion of any further investigation, the City
         shall issue a written decision as soon as practical after receiving the investigation report and any
         written responses from complainant and the alleged perpetrator. The decision shall include a
         summary of relevant findings of fact and conclusions, including a determination that the
         conduct either constituted or did not constitute harassment, or that the investigation was
         inconclusive.
    7.   The results of the investigation and the determination as to whether harassment occurred shall
         be reported to appropriate persons including the complainant, the alleged perpetrator, the
         supervisor, the Department Head, and Human Resources.

IMPLEMENTATION ONCE A DECISION IS FINAL
Harassment: If harassment is found to have occurred, discipline appropriate to the nature of the
harassment shall be imposed, and copies of any disciplinary records shall be placed in the perpetrator’s
personnel file.

No Harassment: If harassment is found not to have occurred, written notice to the parties shall be
provided. An entry (without identification of the other party) shall be made in the complainant’s
personnel file noting that a harassment complaint was filed along with a notation that harassment was
found not to have occurred.

Inconclusive Determination: If the investigation and determination are not conclusive as to whether
harassment occurred, written notice to the parties shall be provided. An entry shall be made in each
party’s personnel file noting that a harassment complaint was filed along with a notation that the
investigation was unable to determine if harassment occurred. Other parties and individuals involved in
the investigation shall not be identified by name in such notations.

Protected Records: Separate records of the investigation, the City’s written decision, and any
disciplinary action shall be retained as protected files maintained by Human Resources. These may be
reviewed and considered by management in the event similar matters pertain to either party in the
future. Such records shall be maintained in accordance with the Utah State Archives retention schedule.

TIME EXTENSION
The time requirements set forth in this procedure may be extended when it is determined to serve the
interest of fairness and justice to the parties involved.

2.6 EMPLOYMENT
PROBATION PERIOD
Every new employee and any current employee who has moved to a new position will go through an
initial period of adjustment in order to learn about Riverton City and about his/her job. At the beginning
of their probation period, every new employee and any current employee who has moved to a new
position will be given a copy of the current evaluation criteria for which they will be evaluated. The
supervisor will go over the criteria to make sure the employee understands the evaluation criteria and
how the employee’s performance may be measured against the criteria.

Every new employee and any current employee who has moved to a new position will be evaluated for
continued employment during the probation period. During this period, the employee shall hold the
status of an at-will employee. The at-will status of employees undergoing a probationary period equally
applies to current employees who have moved to a new position. The probation period is six (6)
months, unless extended as provided herein. During probation, employment may be terminated for any
reason, at any time, with or without cause, and for any reason at the discretion of the Department
Head. If the probationary employee’s performance is unsatisfactory, the Department Head may choose
to notify the employee verbally of the performance deficiencies and give the employee an opportunity
to correct his/her performance. If before the conclusion of the probation period the employee’s job
performance continues to be unsatisfactory, the employee shall be notified in writing of failure to
complete the probationary period and employment will be terminated. Under appropriate
circumstances, the probation period established for the employee may be extended. Any extension
must be done in writing and must not be extended beyond three (3) months following the end of the
probation period. No supervisor or other representative of Riverton City (except for the Mayor, with the
advice and consent of the City Council) has the authority to enter into any agreement for employment
for any specified period of time, or to make any agreement contrary to the above.

At the end of the probation period, the employee and his/her supervisor will have a performance
evaluation using Riverton City’s approved evaluation program and the same criteria given the employee
upon their entry as a new employee or employee that has moved to a different position of Riverton City.
Provided his/her job performance is “satisfactory” at the end of the probation period, he/she may
continue in Riverton City’s employment. This performance evaluation and the results of such evaluation
shall not obligate management to a particular course of action relative to the probationary employee,
nor shall it create any property/due process rights of the probationary employee relative to his/her
job/position. Any merit raise that may be given at this time shall be given as of the effective date of the
end of the probation period. The performance evaluation will become part of the employee’s
permanent personnel file. Employee rights of appeal do not apply during this probation period, whether
a new employee or a current employee who has moved to a new position.

EMPLOYEE CATEGORIES
Based on the conditions of employment, employees of Riverton City fall into the following categories:

FULL-TIME
A full time employee is an employee hired for an indefinite period who works the standard working
hours of Riverton City each week. To be considered full-time, an employee with benefits, the employee
must work a minimum of 32 hours per week. Full-time employees may or may not qualify for specific
Riverton City benefits. Benefits may be pro-rated based on a 40 hour week for those employees
working less than 40 hours.

For those employees scheduled to work less than 40 hours per week, the employee’s schedule may
require more than 32 hours some weeks and less in other weeks. Supervisors must monitor hours
worked so those on 32 hour weekly schedules, on average for a calendar year, do not exceed the 32
hours per week. Adjustments on pro-rated benefits must be made if the yearly total exceeds the
average of the 32 hours.

PART-TIME
Part-time employees, hired for an indefinite period, are classified as exempt or non-exempt and work a
regular or irregular schedule of 20 hours or less per week. Supervisors must monitor hours worked so
weekly schedules, on average for a calendar year, do not exceed the 20 hours per week. Part-time
employees may or may not qualify for specific Riverton City benefits.

TEMPORARY/SEASONAL
A temporary employee is hired for a specified project or time frame, less than nine (9) months. A
temporary employee is a non-exempt position and is paid by the hour. In lieu of social security, the City
will contribute funds into a 457 with URS. Temporary employees do not receive additional
compensation or benefits provided by Riverton City.

2.7 PERFORMANCE EVALUATIONS
GENERAL POLICY
Personnel evaluation is closely related to employee development, promotion, and compensation. All
supervisors shall hold semi-annual performance evaluation/goal-setting interviews with subordinates
and regular follow-up meetings to motivate, train, and develop employees. To determine if the
employee shall be recommended for continued employment with Riverton City, a performance review
will be completed by the immediate supervisor and approved by the Department Head, Human
Resource designee, and the City Manager at the completion of the probationary period.

Performance evaluations will consist of a review between the supervisor and the employee using
Riverton City’s Performance Evaluation Software. It is the policy of Riverton City that employee
evaluations be conducted in a manner which will ensure fair treatment and an objective evaluation of
employee performance. Goal setting is critical for the development of performance plans and
standards. Goals define, in broad terms, the underlying purpose of a given activity or set of activities.
The goals should be in line with the Strategic Plan of the City developed yearly by the Mayor and the City
Council. There are certain fundamental principles that govern the establishment of goals, objectives,
and performance standards.

    1. Participatory Goal Setting. In setting goals and objectives of employees, the supervisors should
       seek to involve employees in the process. Goals set by supervisors should be quantifiable, and
       consistent with the objectives of the Riverton City Council and management team.




Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                              Page | 16
    2. Outline Results to be achieved. There should be room for flexibility. The supervisor should
       discuss with the employee how much will be done, when it needs to be completed, and the
       resources required.
    3. Relate to Organizational Objectives and Goals. In the process of initially formulating
       performance plans, each employee should be provided with the larger picture and how the
       employee’s work contributes to the organization. This is the responsibility of each supervisor.
    4. Define Objectives. Objectives must be clearly defined and understood by both employees and
       their supervisor.
    5. Give Support. Employees should understand that they will be fully supported by their
       supervisor in pursuing the achievement of the mutually agreed upon objectives and standards
       and receive the resources they need in order to accomplish their goal.

PERFORMANCE STANDARDS
Supervisors will be given, by the Human Resource designee, the Performance Evaluation Standards for
each new hire under their jurisdiction. The supervisor will be required within thirty (30) days of hire to
meet with the new employee to discuss and explain the standards since they will be evaluated against
those at the end of their probationary period. The supervisor shall explain how they use the rating
system in the evaluation process. This will make it clear to the employee how the rating numbers will be
used relative to the supervisors standards. Existing employees will have the standards on each
subsequent evaluation from the evaluation software used by Riverton City.

Performance evaluations are an ongoing process and require supervisors and subordinates to meet
periodically to discuss achievements, review performance, and mutually agree on strategies to eliminate
performance deficiencies. This ongoing process culminates in the written evaluation at the end of the
semi-annual performance evaluation period.

    1. Employees shall be made aware of the time frames and actions to be taken to improve
       performance and to increase the value of service.
    2. Employees shall know what role their supervisors shall play in providing them with assistance
       toward improved performance.
    3. Employees will be allowed to complete a performance evaluation using the Riverton City
       evaluation software.
    4. The employee’s supervisor will also complete a performance evaluation. The performance
       evaluation will then allow employee and supervisor to compare/contrast results of the
       evaluation.
    5. Employees shall have the right to add relevant comments to their evaluations.
    6. The evaluation software used will have ratings tied to each of the performance standards.
       Those ratings should reflect the actual performance of the employee during the evaluation
       period.

2.8 DISCIPLINARY ACTION
As a matter of policy, Riverton City believes in and practices individual responsibility and accountability.
The City believes in allowing employees to govern their own conduct within acceptable standards of
behavior. The City relies on individual good judgment and a sense of responsibility. Riverton City
expects from its employees integrity, mutual respect and courtesy, effective and efficient performance,
considerate coworker relations, considerate customer and citizen relations, responsiveness, and loyalty.
To maintain a desired level of performance, and to protect our citizens, other employees, City property,
and City interests, Riverton City has established certain standards of conduct. All employees are
expected to adhere to City performance standards. Failure to meet Riverton City standards of
conduct/policies, or conduct which violates any City ordinance, resolutions, policy procedures, or
administrative directives, may result in corrective action or discipline up to and including termination.

The intent of corrective action is to formally document problems while providing the employee with a
reasonable time within which to improve performance. The process is designed to encourage
development by providing employees with guidance in areas that need improvement such as poor work
performance, attendance problems, personal conduct, general compliance with City policies and
procedures, and/or other disciplinary problems.

CAUSES FOR DISCIPLINARY ACTION
All employees are expected to meet Riverton City’s standards of work performance. Work performance
encompasses many factors including attendance, punctuality, personal conduct, job proficiency,
attitude, and compliance with Riverton City’s policies and procedures. If an employee does not meet
these standards, Riverton City may, under appropriate circumstances, take corrective action. The intent
of corrective action is to formally document problems while providing the employee with a reasonable
time within which to improve performance. Corrective action may include a requirement to successfully
complete training, verbal warnings, written warnings, transfer to another position of less remuneration
(demotion), suspension, or termination. The process of imposing corrective action is designed to
encourage development by providing employees with guidance in areas that need improvement.
Generally, the degree of corrective action taken should take into consideration: 1) the severity of
employee conduct; 2) employee work history; and 3) likelihood employee conduct will improve
following corrective action.

TYPES OF DISCIPLINARY ACTION
The following is an explanation of the disciplinary action utilized by Riverton City. This process is
intended to facilitate principles of due process, progressive discipline and corrective action. Under
appropriate circumstances, Riverton City may deviate from this process and impose discipline up to and
including termination of any misconduct deserving such action. Employees holding “at-will”
employment status are not entitled to undergo progressive discipline as described in the following
paragraphs.

VERBAL WARNING
Whenever grounds for disciplinary action exist and the supervisor determines that more severe action is
not necessary, an employee’s supervisor should verbally communicate to the employee that the
employee has demonstrated deficiency in their conduct. The supervisor should also communicate how
the employee may correct his/her actions. The act of providing a verbal warning should be documented
by setting forth the reasons for the warning and the expected behavior in the future. The
documentation should be signed by the supervisor to record that the employee has received a verbal
warning. Whenever possible, sufficient time for improvement should precede additional disciplinary
action.




Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                               Page | 20
WRITTEN REPRIMAND
Following issuance of a verbal warning for a category of misconduct, or if employee conduct warrants
more serious corrective action than a verbal warning, a written reprimand (also known as a written
warning) should generally be used. When issuing a written reprimand, the supervisor shall discuss the
problem and present a written reprimand to the employee in the presence of the Human Resource
designee. The written reprimand should clearly identify the problem and outline a course of corrective
action. Consequences for continued misconduct of a similar nature should be discussed if the problem
is not corrected. The employee should acknowledge receipt of the warning by signing the written
reprimand. In addition, the employee should be provided an opportunity to include any additional
comments in writing before signing the written reprimand. A record of the discussion and the
employee’s comments will be placed in the employee’s file.

A written reprimand may include a specific time in which an employee must complete corrective action
referred to as a “warning period.” Employees are not eligible for salary increases, awards, promotions
or transfers during the warning period. Once the warning period has been completed, the supervisor
and employee shall discuss the outcome of the disciplinary action and whether the employee has
successfully changed their behavior. If the behavior has been changed as indicated, and the employee
should have received a salary increase during that period, the supervisor may implement the increase
without being retroactive from the time period of the written reprimand.

If the behavior has not been corrected within the specified time frame, the supervisor may impose
additional discipline up to and including termination.

SUSPENSION WITHOUT PAY
For any conduct which is a serious offense, critical offense, or a repeated offense following a written
warning, the supervisor or designee, with the advice and consent of the City Manager, may suspend an
employee without pay for up to, but not exceeding, thirty (30) calendar days. The Mayor, with the
advice and consent of the City Council may suspend appointed individuals similarly, provided
appropriate investigation procedures have been properly followed. A supervisor cannot issue a
suspension for a period longer than two days unless predetermination procedures are first completed,
as required in this Employee Manual. Therefore, a supervisor should arrange for a predetermination
hearing whenever suspension is a possible outcome of an investigation of employee misconduct.
Documentation establishing a pre-determination hearing and the level of suspension imposed shall be
signed by the supervisor or designee and the employee, and shall be permanently placed in the
employee's personnel file. If the employee refuses to sign the documentation, the supervisor or
designee will so state. An employee suspended for disciplinary reasons shall continue to receive
Riverton City contributions to retirement, health, dental, and disability and life insurance programs. The
employee shall pay the employee portion of insurance premiums to continue coverage through the
period of suspension.

DEMOTION
The supervisor or designee, with the advice and consent of the City Manager, may demote or reduce an
employee in grade. Demotion may be appropriate when it appears that a recently promoted employee
is not meeting expectations of a new position. Demotion is not a commonly used method of discipline
and should not be used if an employee’s behavior demonstrates multiple occasions of similar
misconduct. Rather, written warnings followed by suspension and (if necessary) termination should be
used to remedy repeated disciplinary problems. The Mayor, with the advice and consent of the City
Council, may demote or remove any individual in an appointed position. Documentation establishing a
pre-determination hearing and the conclusion that a demotion is necessary shall be signed by the


Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                              Page | 21
supervisor or designee and the employee, and shall be permanently placed in the employee's personnel
file. If the employee refuses to sign the documentation, the supervisor or designee will so state.

TERMINATION
If a decision rendered from a pre-determination meeting is to terminate the employee’s employment, a
representative from Human Resources and the Department Head will confer with the Attorney’s Office
and City Manager prior to formulating the termination document and presenting it to the employee.
The representative from Human Resources will coordinate the preparation of final wage and benefit
payments with the Finance Department and prepare an exit letter explaining termination and COBRA
information. The termination document will include the reasons for termination. It will also inform the
employee of the Termination Appeals Process. The Department Head conducts the termination meeting
with the d supervisor and Human Resource designee and/or City Attorney. The Department Head, in
conjunction with Human Resources, will be responsible for maintaining a record of the meeting. The
employee will be asked to sign the termination document and be given a copy for their personal
records.
2.9 GENERAL STANDARDS FOR IMPOSING DISCIPLINARY ACTION
The following standards are required to be followed during the course of any disciplinary action which
results in written reprimand, suspension, demotion, or termination.

CONDUCTING AN INVESTIGATION, ADMINISTRATIVE LEAVE DURING AN INVESTIGATION
Depending on the severity of an allegation of misconduct, the Department Head, with City Manager
approval, may place the employee on suspension with or without pay pending an investigation.
Suspension without pay is warranted only in extreme allegations of employee misconduct such as
(without limitation), commission of a crime while working for Riverton City. If the suspension is without
pay and the investigation shows no discipline is warranted, the employee will be paid for the time during
suspension.

No employee investigation will be conducted without City Manager approval. The supervisor with the
assistance of the City Attorney shall conduct an appropriate investigation into the allegations which
form the grounds for disciplinary action. Under special circumstances such as allegations of harassment,
discrimination, theft of City property, or illegal conduct of a City employee, the City Manager may hire
experts or investigators who are not regularly employed by Riverton City to investigate the allegation or
provide consultation.

PRE-DETERMINATION MEETING
Prior to the involuntary termination, demotion, or suspension without pay for longer than two days of a
regular full-time employee who is not on probationary status or who is not an at-will employee, the City
shall convene a meeting where information regarding allegations of misconduct is presented or made
available to the employee. Part-time, probationary, seasonal, and temporary employees are at-will
employees and are not entitled to due process in the form of a pre-determination meeting. Any other
employee holding an at-will employment position is not entitled to a pre-determination meeting.
Finally, the City Manager, the Assistant City Manager, Department Heads, and at-will employees are not
entitled to a pre-determination meeting, unless otherwise required under an existing employment
contract.

The purpose of the pre-determination meeting is to allow the employee to present any information or
evidence that he/she believes is relevant to the allegations of misconduct. If it is determined that a pre-
determination meeting is appropriate, the Department Head or the City Attorney shall conduct the


Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                               Page | 22
meeting. Other Department Managers and/or Supervisors may be present to offer input, if needed.
The Human Resource designee should also be present. Written notice of the pre-determination meeting
shall be given to the employee at least three (3) working days before the meeting. The notice shall
describe the facts relating to the employee’s misconduct, and refer to the appropriate sections of the
Employee Manual or other policies, administrative directives, resolutions or ordinances or laws which
the employee has violated. Each employee shall be afforded access to City rules, policies, and
procedures before undergoing a pre-determination hearing. The employee should also be told he/she
may bring evidence or witnesses the employee believes to be relevant to the meeting. A record will be
kept of the meeting. Following the pre-determination meeting, a final decision shall be presented to the
employee within ten (10) working days from the date of the pre-determination meeting. The
Department Head (with City Manager approval) may request an extension of up to a maximum of
twenty (20) working days from the date of the pre-determination meeting.

The Department Head, in conjunction with the Human Resource designee shall conduct disciplinary
action in a consistent manner from employee-to-employee. If no serious misconduct or violation is
found through the investigation, the warnings will be removed from the employee’s personnel file, and
the employee will be paid for his/her regularly scheduled work hours. In determining the type and
severity of the disciplinary action, the supervisor or designee in conjunction with the Human Resource
designee may consider aggravating and mitigating circumstances such as: the repeated nature of
misconduct; prior disciplinary action imposed; the severity of the misconduct; the employee's work
record; and/or the potential of the misconduct to harm person(s) or property.

For disciplinary action other than a verbal warning, the supervisor or designee in conjunction with the
Human Resource designee shall notify the employee, in writing, of the findings of the pre-determination
meeting. The written statement shall include:

    1. The grounds for disciplinary action, including a description of the specific misconduct for which
       the disciplinary action is being imposed;
    2. Any prior disciplinary action imposed;
    3. The disciplinary action to be imposed;
    4. The effective date and duration of the disciplinary action;
    5. The corrective action necessary for the employee to avoid further disciplinary action;
    6. Suspension, demotion, transfer, or termination of an employee shall require the approval of the
       City Manager.
2.10 APPEAL PROCEDURES
Right to appeal. Employees who are disciplined by termination or suspension for more than two (2)
days, or by reassignment to a position of less remuneration, may appeal the outcome of a pre-
determination meeting. However, not all employees hold the right to appeal the outcome of a
disciplinary action taken by an employee’s supervisor. Moreover, not all corrective disciplinary actions
are subject to appeal. The following appeal procedures are not available to the following employees, or
under the following circumstances.

    1. Employees holding “at-will” employment status;
    2. An employee in receipt of a verbal warning, a written warning, or a suspension for up to two
       days;
    3. An officer appointed by the mayor or other person or body exercising executive power in the
       municipality;
    4. A head of a municipal department;
    5. A deputy of a head of a municipal department;


Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                             Page | 23
    6. A probationary employee of the municipality;
    7. A part-time employee of the municipality;
    8. A seasonal employee of the municipality.

APPEALING TO A HEARING OFFICER
Some employees, the exceptions noted above, have the right to appeal their employment to a Hearing
Officer for transfers to a position of less remuneration, demotions, suspensions without pay for a period
in excess of two (2) days, or termination of their employment. An employee must submit his/her
written notice of appeal to the Riverton City Recorder within ten (10) days from the date the employee
is either terminated, suspended without pay (in excess of two days), or demoted. An employee who
fails to timely file a notice of appeal will be deemed to have waived all appeal rights. The City Recorder
shall notify the Assistant City Manager of the filing of a notice of appeal no later than three (3) calendar
days after the City Recorder’ s receipt of notice.

The a hearing officer shall possess sufficient skill to mediate disputes under Utah law and shall be
selected pursuant to ordinary purchasing procedures established by the Riverton City purchasing
ordinance.

The Hearing Officer may obtain the assistance of independent legal counsel to assist in matters affecting
the appeal procedure, during deliberations before the Hearing Officer, and to publish findings and
opinions. The costs and expenses of conducing the hearing and any fees paid for independent legal
counsel appointed to the Hearing Officer shall be the responsibility of the City. Employees shall pay for
their own legal counsel if retained. Within thirty (30) days from the City Recorder’s receipt of a notice of
appeal, the Hearing Officer shall schedule dates to consider any pre-hearing motions of the parties and
additionally schedule a hearing date.

The employee who is subject of the appeal may appear in person and be represented by counsel, have a
public hearing, confront the witnesses whose testimony is to be considered, and examine the evidence
to be considered by the Hearing Officer. The Hearing Officer shall uphold the disciplinary process and
action imposed by the City, unless the employee-appellant presents sufficient evidence to demonstrate:
(1) The employee-appellant did not perform the conduct alleged; (2) sufficient reason exists to justify
the defendant’s conduct; or (3) the level of discipline imposed is excessive, given the severity of the
allegations and other factors related to the disciplinary determination.

The employee-appellant carries the burden of proof during the course of the appeal hearing. Findings of
the Hearing Officer must be supported by a preponderance of the evidence. If the Hearing Officer does
not rule in favor of the employee-appellant, the Hearing Officer may not impose a more severe level of
discipline (such as ordering termination of a suspended employee). If the employee-appellant proves by
a preponderance of the evidence that the employee is not guilty of the alleged misconduct, sufficient
reason exists to justify the misconduct, or the level of discipline imposed is excessive, then the Hearing
Officer may overturn the disciplinary determination, or modify the disciplinary determination by
imposing a less severe level of discipline (such as ordering written reprimand in lieu of suspension). If
the Hearing Officer overturns the Employee Disciplinary Action, the City shall: (1) reinstate any loss of
pay associated with the Employee Disciplinary Action; and (2) the City shall remove the overturned
record of the disciplinary action from the employee’s personnel file, or order modification of the same
to accurately reflect the determination of the Hearing Officer.

All proceedings before the Hearing Officer shall be recorded for the purpose of creating a record for
review. The Hearing Officer shall publish a written record of its findings of fact and conclusions of law
within fourteen (14) calendar days following the conclusion of the appeal hearing. If the Hearing Officer

Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                                Page | 24
upholds the Employee Disciplinary Action, the employee may then appeal to the Court of Appeals
pursuant to Utah Code section 10-03-1106. Utah Law requires the Riverton City Council to adopt by
ordinance the method and manner of choosing the members of the Appeal Board, the number of
members, the designation of their terms of office, the procedure for conducting an appeal, and the
standard of review in an appeal hearing. The Riverton City Council may adopt policies and procedures
reasonably necessary to aid in the hearing of an appeal. Where any ordinance or provision of this
Manual is inconsistent with statutes governing the Appeal Board, the statute(s) shall govern.
2.11 GRIEVANCE PROCEDURES
GENERAL POLICY
A grievance is defined as a complaint made by a City employee of a decision, or action taken by the City
which affects an employee’s employment relationship with the City, except disciplinary actions.
Disciplinary action appeals, if permitted, shall be handled under the Employee Discipline Policy. All
employees have the right to file a grievance. Employees who believe that they have a grievance against
Riverton City shall exhaust the administrative procedure set forth in this policy before addressing their
grievance through any other forum. An employee may file a grievance about any perceived work
related injustice or oppression resulting from an act, occurrence, omission, condition, or unfair labor
practice. Issues addressable throughout the grievance process include, but are not limited to:

    1.   Employee-supervisor relationships, and relationships between two or more employees.
    2.   Duty assignments not affecting job classification
    3.   Shift and job location assignments
    4.   Working conditions such as physical working conditions, safety conditions, etc.
    5.   Practices affecting granting of leave
    6.   Inadequate resolution by a supervisor of workplace harassment or discriminatory conduct

Allegations relating to all forms of workplace harassment or discriminatory conduct may not be handled
through grievance procedures. Grievances should be resolved at the lowest administrative level
possible. Employees and supervisors shall attempt to resolve grievances informally by discussing the
grievance issues before any formal written grievance is filed. Each employee pursuing a formal
grievance must prepare and submit their separate written grievance/appeal. Written grievances shall
contain, at a minimum, the following Information:

    1.   Name of the employee
    2.   Date the action underlying the grievance occurred
    3.   Nature of the grievance
    4.   Historical information related to the grievance
    5.   Requested Resolution
    6.   Signature of the employee filing the grievance and date filed

Employees will be allowed a reasonable amount of time during work to prepare written grievances.
Employee grievances must be filed within ten (10) working days of the action or event giving rise to the
grievance, or within ten (10) days of the employee’s acquiring knowledge of the action or event giving
rise to the grievance.

At each level of the grievance process, after an administrator has received an employee grievance, the
administrator shall have ten (10) working days to respond in writing to the grievance. If an
administrator is unable to answer the grievance within the specified time period due to exigent
circumstances, the administrator may take an additional ten (10) working days to answer the grievance


Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                              Page | 25
if s/he notifies the employee in writing of the exigent circumstances and that the extension is being
exercised. If the grievance remains unresolved or the decision is considered unacceptable, the
employee may appeal the decision to the next higher level of appeal.

At each level of review during the appeal process, the recipient of the appealed grievance shall provide a
written response to the appeal within ten (10) working days, unless urgent circumstances require the
recipient to delay providing a response, in which case the recipient of the grievance may take an
additional ten (10) working days. Absent urgent circumstances, if the supervisor fails to respond within
the allotted time, the employee may proceed to the next level of appeal. Only the issues presented in
the original grievance may be considered throughout the appellate process. A grievance and any
necessary appeals shall be processed through the following chain of command:

    1. Immediate supervisor
    2. Department Head
    3. City Manager

The decision of the City Manager constitutes the final level of appeal and is not appealable.

CONFIDENTIALITY
A written grievance shall be private data under the Government Records Access Management Act
(GRAMA) of the State of Utah. The City Manager may declare the grievance documents to be
confidential and/or order the entire record, or any part of it, sealed.

FILING
No document relating to a grievance shall be placed in the employee’s personnel file, unless a grievance
gives rise to the conclusion that an employee’s conduct fails to meet existing Riverton City standards,
and disciplinary determination may be warranted. In such instances, the grievance shall be included as a
part of the documents and other evidence assembled during the course of an investigation into
employee misconduct.

VOLUNTARY TERMINATION (RESIGNATION)
When an employee chooses to leave employment with Riverton City for any reason other than

Involuntary Terminations occur when a supervisor or designee concludes there is cause for termination.
At-will, temporary, probationary, or part-time employee may be involuntarily terminated for no reason

RECLASSIFICATION
There may be occasions when a department’s needs change, such that a current position(s) within that
department must be reclassified to meet the department’s needs. When a position is reclassified such
that the essential job functions, job title, and/or qualifications change significantly enough to be
considered a new position(s), the old position(s) will be considered eliminated.

Whenever it is necessary to reduce the number of employees in Riverton City because of lack of work or

OUTSOURCING
There may be occasions when a department’s needs change such that it becomes either more cost
effective and/or more efficient to outsource all or part of a department. This must first be approved by
the City Manager and determined that it is in the best interest of the City to outsource all or part of a
department. The incumbents in the position(s) being outsourced will have the opportunity to apply for
any open positions available if the incumbent is otherwise qualified for the position. If there are no
open positions or the incumbents don’t meet the qualifications for any open position, they will be
terminated and given up to one (1) month severance pay upon termination.

MEDICAL
The American with Disabilities Act (ADA) prohibits illegal discrimination by an employer against an
"otherwise qualified individual with a disability.” Consequently, an employee should not be terminated
for medical reasons without prior consultation with legal counsel. An employee may be terminated if
they are unable to perform their job and go on long-term disability.

DEATH
If an employee of Riverton City dies, their estate receives all pay due and any earned and payable
benefits as of the date of death.

COBRA
Any employee that is separated from Riverton City is entitled to a continuation of insurance coverage
according to the mandates of the COBRA or current Federal Law. Federal Public Law 99-272 (which
became effective July 1, 1986, and is known as COBRA) requires that all employers of 20 or more full-
time employees offer a continuation of group insurance coverage to individuals who fall under one of
the following "qualifying events:"

    1. Termination of employment (other than for misconduct) for a maximum continuation period of
       eighteen (18) months.
    2. Reduction of work hours below eligibility requirement for a maximum continuation period of
       eighteen (18) months.
    3. Dependent coverage termination due to death of an employee for a maximum continuation
       period of thirty six (36) months.
    4. Divorce or legal separation from employee for a maximum continuation period of thirty six (36)
       months.
    5. Spouse or dependent of Medicare eligible employee for a maximum continuation period of
       thirty six (36) months.
    6. Dependent child who ceases to be a dependent under the generally applicable requirements of
       the group plan for a maximum continuation period of thirty six (36) months.

Under the Act, a qualifying individual is entitled to continued medical group insurance coverage identical
to that which is provided to similarly situated beneficiaries to whom a qualifying event has not occurred.
Individuals who are entitled to continued benefits under COBRA guidelines are required to pay the
entire premium required under the policy during the entire period of the continued coverage. The
premium a qualifying individual will be required to pay may not exceed one hundred and two percent
(102 %) of the applicable premium for any period of continued coverage. Failure to pay the monthly
premium will result in a cancellation of the insurance. The insurance benefits offered under the COBRA
guidelines will be terminated if and when any of the following occur:

    1. A qualifying individual fails to pay the premium at the time it is required.
    2. A qualifying individual becomes eligible for coverage under any other group insurance plan due
       to employment or remarriage.

At the expiration of a qualifying individual’s maximum continuation period, the offer of continued
insurance coverage under COBRA is made independently of any other offer to continue insurance that
may be required under any applicable state law. A qualifying individual has sixty (60) days from the


Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                              Page | 30
termination date of current coverage to decide whether to continue insurance coverage under this plan.
If the qualifying individual decides to apply for the continued coverage, all due and owing premiums
must be paid before coverage will be granted. Failure to apply for coverage within sixty (60) days of
termination date will constitute a waiver of the qualifying individual’s right to continued coverage under
the COBRA guidelines. Qualifying individuals are not required to apply for or accept coverage under
COBRA.

3.1 TIME SHEETS
NON-EXEMPT EMPLOYEES
All non-exempt employees of Riverton City are required to maintain an accurate record of all their hours
worked or compensable on the City approved time sheet. The time sheet will be available on-line.

Time sheets must be dated and signed by both the employee and the employee’s supervisor or
supervisor designee in the supervisor’s absence. An employee’s signature on his/her the time sheet
verifies the time accounted for on the time sheet is accurate and represents fully the time worked or
compensable. The supervisor’s signature represents that he/she has reviewed the time sheet and
agrees with their employees’ representation of hours worked or compensable time is accurate.
Supervisors should take extra care when approving time sheets since they are affirming the accuracy of
each employee’s timesheet. It is understandable that each supervisor cannot individually track the
number of hours worked by each employee under supervision to determine exact accuracy of each time
sheet. However, items such as vacation, holiday, overtime, compensable time, on-call time, etc. should
be scrutinized before signing each time sheet. Supervisors shall train their employees on filling out the
time sheets, and make sure each employee understands the importance of accurately filling out time
sheets.

It is against City policy to allow non-exempt employees to work “off-the-clock,” and not track the hours
they work. It is also against City policy for any City employee to instruct another City employee to work
off-the-clock. Employees should report such instances immediately to Human Resources. Failure to
follow these guidelines could invoke discipline for the employee and/or the supervisor.

Executed time sheets must be turned into the Payroll Department no later than the next working day,
following the end of the payroll period. Failure to do so may prevent payroll processing and may
prevent the employee’s receipt of payment by the scheduled pay issue date. It is the sole responsibility
of each employee to follow the above procedures in order to receive an accurate and on-time pay
voucher.

EXEMPT EMPLOYEES
All exempt employees of Riverton City are required to maintain an accurate and legible record of days
worked and days not worked, but are compensable as vacation, sick, personal or other leave on the
approved City exempt/salaried time log. These logs need not be signed by the employee since they are
electronically produced, however the submitting the log to payroll with the employee’s name on the log
is an implied signature and carries with it all the same inferences of accuracy.

submit the same on or before the deadline, the employee may not be compensated for hours in

3.3 OVERTIME PAY
As used in this section, or in any section relating to overtime pay, “law enforcement employees” are
employees who are not FLSA exempt; are empowered by State or local ordinance to enforce laws
designed to maintain peace and order, protect life and property, and to prevent and detect crimes; who
have the power to arrest; and who have undergone training in law enforcement.

Overtime is calculated for all non-exempt employees, other than law enforcement employees, at time
and one-half (1 ½) for all hours worked over 40 hours in any given work week. Overtime is calculated for
all non-exempt employees, who are law enforcement employees, at time and one-half (1 ½) for all hours
worked over 80 hours in a 14-day work period. Overtime must be approved by the employee’s
supervisor and is not guaranteed as part of an employee’s regularly scheduled shift. For pay purposes,
jury, military, funeral, vacation, sick, and/or any other time defined by FLSA, with the exception of
holiday pay, will not be considered “time worked.” Any day defined as a Holiday is considered “time
worked” for calculating overtime pay.

An employee who is required to be “on-call” for immediate return to work if needed shall receive $31
per day, each day the employee was assigned to be on-call. The employee shall also receive regular pay
for the actual hours worked when called out to work. All on-call employees shall be allowed to take a
vehicle to and from work while assigned to on-call duty. An employee shall receive a minimum of two
(2) hours pay for each time the employee is called to work. While on-call, the employee shall not
consume alcoholic beverages or do anything to impair the employee’s ability to perform on call
assignment duties. Generally, on-call employees must be able to arrive to the City within thirty (30)
minutes of receiving a call.
3.6 SALARY PLANNING
GENERAL POLICY
Human Resources, in conjunction with the City Manager, Mayor and City Council, shall be responsible
for the development and maintenance of a uniform and equitable compensation plan for Riverton City,
which shall consist of minimum and maximum rates of pay for each position and such intermediate
steps as deemed necessary and equitable. Salaries shall be linked directly to the position classification
plan and shall be determined with due regard to the following considerations:

    1. Equal pay for equal work;
    2. Ranges of pay for other positions;
    3. Prevailing rates of pay for similar employment as determined by a benchmark procedure
       conducted by Human Resources;
    4. Cost of living factors;
    5. Other benefits received by employees;
    6. The City Budget as approved by the Riverton City Council and economic conditions of Riverton
       City.

The City Manager, or designee, shall assign each position a grade that corresponds to the type of work
performed and the skills and training required by the job. Pay will be determined by positional grade
assignment performed. Each grade appears on Riverton City’s pay scale, adopted by the City Council. A
salary survey will be performed a minimum of once every year to ensure that pay grades are consistent
with comparable classifications among other cities, as determined in a benchmarking procedure.

Pay adjustments based on merit, cost of living adjustment (COLA), and/or benchmark outcomes must be
approved by the City Manager, and are subject to availability of funds.

The grades within the pay scale plan shall feature minimum, midpoint, and maximum levels of pay for
the grade. These levels will be adjusted by the percentage of any COLA approved by the City.

APPOINTMENT
Pay for newly hired employees shall normally be set within the pay range assigned to a job class.
However, the City Manager may approve above midpoint, as warranted by job qualifications and

MERIT INCREASE
The City Manager, subject to approval of the City Council by adoption of the Riverton City budget, and
further subject to funding available, shall adopt pay increase guidelines effective the beginning of each
fiscal year. Merit increases shall be at the discretion of the City Manager and recommendation of the
supervisor based on a satisfactory employee evaluation and the parameters established in the approved
annual budget.

Regular full-time and part-time employees are eligible to receive a merit increase. Temporary, seasonal
or probationary employees shall not be eligible to receive a merit increase. Probationary employees
may receive a merit increase upon successful completion of probation.

The supervisor must complete an employee’s performance evaluation within thirty (30) days preceding
the effective date of a merit increase. If the merit increase is given to an employee, it may be calculated
such that the effective date of the merit increase took place on the beginning day of the fiscal year for
which the merit increase was awarded. The increase is calculated from the beginning day of the fiscal
year to the actual time the increase was paid. The increase may be paid in a lump sum, based on regular
hours, on the first pay check of the following month in which the merit increase was warranted.
Probationary employees are not eligible for the lump sum payment.

Merit increases are considered during the performance evaluation scheduled between August 1 and
November 30. A merit increase shall not exceed the maximum pay scale assigned to a position grade. If
an employee reaches the maximum range assigned to his/her position no merit increase shall be given.

SELECTIVE SALARY ADJUSTMENT
The City Manager may approve, in writing with reason supporting the approval, a selective salary
adjustment in order to mitigate an inequity to a specific position. The selective adjustment is subject to
the availability of funds budgeted by the City Council.

COST OF LIVING ADJUSTMENTS (COLA)
When the City Council, in conjunction with the annual budget process, grants a cost-of-living adjustment
(COLA) all Grades within the pay scale will be adjusted by that amount. Regular full-time and part-time
employees are eligible to receive a COLA. Temporary and seasonal employees may not be eligible to
receive a COLA, which would be determined during the budget process. COLA’s will be implemented on
the 1stpayroll in the beginning month of each fiscal year, unless designated differently by the City
Council during the budget process.

Multiple categories of pay increase awarded simultaneously shall be calculated in the following order:

    1.   Cost of Living Adjustment;
    2.   Merit;
    3.   Selective Adjustment;
    4.   Longevity Increase.

REASSIGNMENT
Except when due to a demotion, permanent disability, light duty assignment, or disciplinary action, an
employee who is reassigned shall be paid the same salary received prior to the assignment.
Reassignment may be due to a supervisor action. If an employee requests a reassignment, or applies for
a position that is a lower grade, the employee will be compensated according to experience,
qualifications, etc. but not more than the top of the pay scale for the lower grade.

If an employee requests a reassignment, the employee must apply for an open position and compete

There may be occasions when a position is reclassified due to market and/or benchmarking data. If the
City Manager approves and reclassifies a position to a higher level due to market and/or benchmarking,

give a salary increase based upon the employee’s current relationship with that market and/or
benchmarking data. A reclassification increase is subject to availability of funds.

If the City Manager approves reclassification of a position to a lower level due to market and/or
benchmarking data, the incumbent’s salary shall remain the same. If the incumbent’s salary exceeds the
maximum of the new range, the incumbent is ineligible to receive a salary increase until the salary range
increases to incorporate the incumbent’s pay rate. An employee is ineligible to receive cost-of-living
increases until the salary range increases above the employee’s current wage.

Employees may use accrued personal leave, vacation leave, or sick leave to make up the difference

result in medical treatment not being paid for or the possibility of disciplinary actions.


Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                               Page | 36
MEDICAL ATTENTION
An employee who sustains a bona fide on-the-job injury will seek medical attention from the medical
facility authorized by Riverton City. These facilities are posted in each of the major City facilities such as
Administration, Water, and Public Works buildings. If possible, the injured employee should go to
Human Resources and fill out an authorization form before going to the medical facility. If that is not

nature of the injury and make arrangements to have the paperwork filled out no later than 24 hours

or take the employee to the nearest emergency facility. Human Resources will then work with the
supervisor to get the necessary paperwork filled out and make sure the employee is taken care of
appropriately.

The employee must tell the doctor, HOW, WHEN and WHERE the accident occurred and that it was a
work related injury. The doctor will complete a medical report and give the employee a copy. This copy
should be given to Human Resources, if possible, within 24 hours of treatment. (Do not submit doctor
or hospital bills for on-the-job injuries or illness to the regular medical plan.)

INITIAL REPORTING OF ILLNESS OR INJURY
Reporting the accident or illness is critical to qualification for payment under Workers’ Compensation
eligibility. If an employee is injured while on the job, no matter how minor, the circumstances shall be
reported to Human Resources and the supervisor immediately. Human Resources will be responsible
for filling out the appropriate paperwork according to the current workers compensation carrier and/or
state regulations. (See Incident Reporting procedures in the Risk Management section of this manual).

Employees with CDL licenses must follow DOT regulations if involved in an accident and/or injury while
operating equipment defined under the DOT regulations.

REPORTING WHILE OFF THE JOB
While on leave because of a bona fide on-the-job injury or illness, an employee must contact his/her
supervisor, the Human Resources designee, or the City Manager to report on their condition. Failure to
provide the required medical status reports may result in revocation of the leave and disciplinary action
taken against the employee, up to and including termination.

RETURN TO WORK
All employees must return to work after the approval of the attending physician. A statement from the
attending physician stating the employee is able to resume normal duties will be required before
returning to work. Failure to return to work when directed may result in disciplinary action taken
against the employee, up to and including termination. An employee who is able to return to work in
light duty status may be required to work in a different department and perform duties not contained
within the employee’s current job classification. Workers Compensation leave for periods greater than
six (6) months shall not be granted.

At the time of final release or settlement of a workers’ compensation claim, if no vacancy exists, and if a
reasonable unsuccessful effort has been made to place the employee in another position, they may be
terminated and paid any accrued benefits due.

5.1 FAMILY AND MEDICAL LEAVE ACT (FMLA)
GENERAL POLICY
The Family and Medical Leave Act of 1993 requires many employers, including “public agencies” to
provide up to a total of twelve (12) work weeks of unpaid leave during any (12) month period for
“eligible” employees at the time of the birth or adoption of a child or at the time of a serious health
condition affecting the employee or a family member.

A single “public agency” is further defined under Section 3(x) of the Federal Fair Labor Standards Act to
include a city or a town.




Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                                Page | 44
A “serious health condition” is an illness, injury, impairment, or physical or mental condition that
involves inpatient care in a medical facility or continuing treatment by a health care provider.

“Intermittent leave” or a “reduced leave schedule” for medical reasons can be taken under this policy
when medically necessary. Intermittent leave or a reduced leave schedule can be taken only if the
employee and Riverton City mutually agree to that arrangement. Intermittent leave is leave that is not
taken consecutively. A reduced leave schedule is a leave schedule that reduces the usual number of
hours per work week or hours per work day.

ELIGIBILITY
To be “eligible” for FMLA leave, an employee must:

    1. Have been employed for at least twelve (12) months by the employer;
    2. Have been employed for at least one thousand two hundred fifty (1,250) hours of service;
    3. Be employed by an employer who employs at least fifty (50) people within a seventy five (75)
       mile radius around the worksite.

LEAVE OPTIONS
At either the employee’s or employer’s option, certain kinds of paid leave may be substituted for unpaid
leave.

NOTICE AND MEDICAL CERTIFICATION REQUIREMENTS
The employee may be required to provide advanced leave notice and medical certification. FMLA leave
may be denied if the following requirements are not met:

    1. The employee ordinarily must provide thirty (30) days advance notice when the leave is
       “foreseeable.” When it is not possible to provide thirty days advance notice, the employee
       should provide such notice as soon as possible.
    2. The employee may be required to provide the employer with medical certification to support a
       request for FMLA leave because of a serious health condition. If the employer requires a second
       or third opinion, they will both be at the employer’s expense.
    3. A fitness for duty report is required before an employee returns to work with the employer.

BENEFITS AND EMPLOYMENT STATUS
During the FMLA leave, the employer must maintain the employee’s health benefits coverage under any
“group health plan” that the employee has with the employer. The use of FMLA leave cannot result in
the loss of any employment benefit that accrued prior to the start of an employee’s FMLA leave.
However, no seniority or other benefits will accrue during the FMLA leave once an employee has
exhausted his/her accrued personal, vacation, and sick leave. Upon return from FMLA leave, most
employees must be restored to their original or equivalent positions with equivalent pay, benefits, and
other employment terms.

5.6 SICK LEAVE
Sick leave is to be used by regular employees who are otherwise unable to perform their regular duties

the birth or adoption of a child, and other leave eligible under the Family Medical Leave Act (hereinafter
referred to as “qualified sick leave use”).

Use of sick leave shall be considered abused when an employee follows a pattern of using sick leave on a
specific day of the week, using sick leave other than for purposes previously stated, or using sick leave
beyond current accruals. An occurrence is consecutive sick days that would have been normal
workdays. Any return to work after a sick occurrence will constitute the end of an occurrence. Any
subsequent sick days will begin a new sick occurrence. If a supervisor has reasonable cause to believe
sick time is being abused, they may require an employee to provide a doctor’s note for all sick time
used.

ELIGIBILITY
Sick leave shall be available to all full-time, regular part-time, and probationary employees. Seasonal,
temporary, provisional, and emergency employees are not eligible for sick leave. Sick leave will be
available to employees as soon as it is accrued.

employees accrue sick leave on a prorated scale, based upon the employee’s regularly scheduled work
hours. The employee will begin to accrue sick leave immediately upon being hired by Riverton City. Sick
leave may be used during a probationary period. Sick leave shall not accrue if an employee is in a leave-
without-pay status. Sick leave hours will be rolled over to the next year.

TERMINATION
An employee who is terminated or resigns voluntarily shall not be compensated for unused accrued sick
leave.

supervisor or designee at least one (1) hour before normal starting time on each day of absence unless
the circumstances surrounding the absence make such notification impossible. The supervisor should

notification should be worked out between the supervisor and the employee.

When possible, notification should be made with personal contact. Voicemail will only constitute
notification when all other attempts to make a personal contact have failed.

5.15 CATASTROPHIC LEAVE
Catastrophic Leave is a voluntary program and is not a guaranteed benefit.

Who is Eligible. All employees, regardless of their length of service and employment status are eligible
to receive contributions of leave from other employees. Full-time, three-quarter time, and half-time/job
share employees who have been employed with the City at least six (6) months may donate time.

When an Employee is Eligible. An employee is eligible to receive catastrophic leave donations upon
date-of-hire. An employee can donate time after six (6) months of employment.

Benefit. The purpose of Catastrophic Leave is to permit employees to contribute a portion of their
accrued vacation leave or compensation time to another City employee, when such employee has
suffered a catastrophe injury or illness. This type of leave may also apply to an employee who is caring
for a terminally ill member of his/her family. Under hardship circumstances described below, an
employee may donate, to other employees of equal or lesser grade and step, accrued vacation or
compensation time, if donating employee will have a combined minimum of ten (10) days of accrued
vacation and compensation time left on the books, after the donation. The donation must be approved
by the employee’s Department Head and the City Manager. The donated leave will be allocated on an
as-needed basis to the employee receiving the leave. Those donating will have time deducted from
their accrued vacation or compensation accrual balances only when the receiving employee uses the
time. If the employee receiving the donation returns to work before all “committed” donated time is
used, the donation will be returned to the employee committing the donation without being deducted.
Listed below are the provisions of the plan:

    1. Participation is voluntary.
    2. For the purposes of this leave program, “catastrophe” shall include an illness or event which is
        monumental, unusual, unexpected, immediate in nature, and which is expected to preclude the
        employee’s returning to work for an extended period of time. The City reserves the right to
        request supporting documentation.
    3. The receiving employee must have been absent from work due to injury or illness and have
        exhausted all earned leave credits, including but not limited to sick leave, vacation leave, holiday
        leave, compensation time, executive leave, etc., and is therefore facing financial hardship.
    4. If the employee is approved for long term disability benefits, they are no longer eligible to
        receive catastrophic leave donations.
    5. The hours will be deducted from the donor’s leave, converted to dollars and used to
        compensate the recipient at the recipient’s regular rate of pay.
    6. Donations will be listed in the order in which the Finance Department Head receives them. For
        each pay period, a sufficient number of donor hours will be converted and used to compensate
        the recipient. Unconverted donations shall revert to the donor.
    7. Donations must be in half-hour increments.
    8. Donated hours are irrevocable.
    9. Employees may donate vacation or compensation time to employees at or below their same pay
        grade on the classification schedule.
    10. Requests must be initiated by the employee, recommended by the Department Head, and
        receive final approval from the City Manager.

Riverton City values its reputation for integrity and high ethical standards practiced by its staff. Riverton

City employees who are involved with information of significant public interest may not use this

6.4 ATTENDANCE, PUNCTUALITY, ABANDONMENT, PRODUCTIVITY
Riverton City employees’ value is determined in part by dependability. Each individual employee’s
performance is essential to the successful achievement of Riverton City objectives and strategic plan.
Riverton City depends on employees and expects them to be here on time and fully prepared to work.
Employees should continue to work their schedule until quitting time, every day they are scheduled.
Regular attendance and punctuality helps establish a positive reputation, directly affects performance,
and will add to opportunities for advancement. Employees are expected to strive for perfect attendance
and arrive to work on time. Excessive absenteeism and tardiness will result in disciplinary action up to
and including termination.

Circumstances may cause an employee to be absent from work for part of a day. However, failure to
maintain regular attendance may put employment with Riverton City in jeopardy. If it is necessary to be
late or absent for any reason, the employee is responsible for notifying his/her supervisor before the
employee’s work shift begins.

SCHEDULED ABSENCES
For scheduled absences such as vacation or scheduled medical procedures, etc. an employee must
submit a “Time off Request” form and get all the required approvals. This must be done in a reasonable
time frame for the approval process. Scheduled absences must be approved by the supervisor, at the
supervisor’s discretion

UNSCHEDULED ABSENCES
Time off work without obtaining approval through submitting a “Time off Request” form will be
considered an unscheduled absence. An employee’s accrued personal leave or sick leave shall be
applied toward the employee’s unscheduled time away from work. Unscheduled absences which are
not due to employee illness, or are not approved by an employee’s supervisor as personal time, shall be
considered a failure to maintain regular attendance and will affect the employee’s performance
evaluations and may be grounds for disciplinary action up to and including termination.

For unscheduled absences, an employee must speak directly with the employee’s supervisor or manager
before start time, unless circumstances make that impossible. Voicemail and email are insufficient
unless authorized by the employee’s supervisor in predetermined situations. An absent employee shall

explain the absence and explain when the employee expects to return to work. Employees scheduled to
be absent for more than one (1) day must call in daily unless other arrangements are made with the
employee’s supervisor. Employees scheduled to be absent for more than three (3) consecutive days will
be required to provide an excuse note from a physician.

ABANDONMENT
An employee who fails to call (no call / no show) their supervisor following the above requirements to
report their absence and request the absence be authorized may be deemed to have voluntarily

PRODUCTIVITY
All employees should maximize their productivity and look for ways to reduce and/or eliminate the

6.5 CONFLICTS OF INTEREST
Riverton City Municipal Code prohibits employees from business activity which creates a conflict of
interest and regulates how employees may seek and obtain secondary employment. Guidelines
provided in this section regarding conflicts of interest and outside employment are not
exhaustive. Therefore, employees are required to thoroughly review and understand the Riverton City
Municipal Code provisions applicable to the conflicts identified in this Manual.

Employees are required to complete an “Officer and Employee Disclosure (Secondary Employment and

supervisor, Department Head and the Human Resource designee prior to accepting secondary
employment or when there is a personal investment or business conflict. Riverton City will rely on this
disclosure in order to determine if and how to act upon an actual or perceived conflict of interest. A

termination. The following are considered to be conflicts of interest with the City:

SECONDARY EMPLOYMENT
Employees are required to regard Riverton City as their primary employer with the exception of seasonal
or part-time employees. The City recognizes the right of employees to engage in activities outside of
their employment which are of a private nature and unrelated to City business. However, the employee
must disclose any additional secondary employment prior to accepting that position. Seasonal or part
time employees must disclose existing secondary employment at the time of hire.

PERSONAL INTEREST OR INVESTMENT
All employees must disclose any personal interest or investment which creates or has the appearance of
creating a conflict between the employee’s interests and his or her public duties.

Every employee must disclose any ownership of a substantial interest in any business entity which may
be subject to the regulation of the City or which does or anticipates doing business with the City.
Substantial interest shall include the ownership, either legally or equitably, by the employee and his or
her spouse or minor children, of at least 10% of the outstanding shares of a corporation or 10% interest
in any business entity. Whenever the performance of an employee’s official duty shall require action on

that the decision will have an individualized material effect on such interest, distinguishable from its effect

ACCEPTANCE OF GIFTS
An employee shall not knowingly receive, accept, take, seek or solicit, directly or indirectly, for himself
or herself or another, a gift that the public servant or volunteer public servant knows, or with the

avoid even the impression of a conflict of interest.

minimus, non-pecuniary gift if acceptance of the same conflicts with Riverton City’s purchasing
ordinance or conflicts of interest ordinance.

6.9 ALCOHOL AND DRUG FREE WORKPLACE
POLICY STATEMENT
Riverton City believes alcohol and illegal drugs in the workplace are unhealthy and dangerous, not only
to the employee involved, but to other employees. The unlawful manufacture, distribution, use,
dispensing, possession, or being under the influence of alcohol and illegal drugs is prohibited on City
premises, in City vehicles, in employees’ vehicles, or at any time the employee is representing the City.

The City believes that a healthy and productive workforce, safe working conditions free from the effects
of drugs and alcohol, and maintenance of the quality of the City’s products and services are important,
not only to the City, but also to the employees and the general public. The abuse of drugs and alcohol
creates a variety of workplace problems, including increased injuries on the job, increased absenteeism,
increased financial burdens on health and benefit programs, increased workplace theft, decreased
employee morale, decreased productivity, and a decline in the quality of products and services.
Therefore, the City hereby adopts this policy for testing employees and prospective employees as
related to drugs and alcohol in the workplace.

This policy is designed to provide guidance to City officials on employee management. Violation of this
policy should not give rise to a cause of action against the City or its supervisory personnel, nor should it
create any presumption that a legal duty has been breached. Any employee who voluntarily comes to
management (Department Manager, Department Head, Human Resources, or City Manager) prior to
any reasonable suspicion of a policy violation, or prior to a random test or conviction of a violation of
the law due to drug or alcohol abuse, and admits that he/she has a drug or alcohol addiction problem,
will be allowed to enter a treatment program (at the employee’s own expense) and will not face
termination so long as the employee successfully completes treatment, and is found to remain sober
following treatment. Subsequent substance abuse problems will be handled in accordance with the
disciplinary actions outlined in this policy.

WORKPLACE RULES
    1. No employee may possess, use, distribute, or be under the influence of alcohol on City
       premises, in the City’s vehicles, or in an employee’s vehicle while used on City business.
    2. No employee may manufacture, distribute, dispense, possess, use, or be under the influence of
       drugs or a controlled substance on the City’s premises, in the City’s vehicles, or in a customer’s
       vehicle. However, employees may possess, use, or be under the influence of a prescription or
       over-the-counter drug if taken as prescribed or directed. Note: It is the employee’s
       responsibility to notify the supervisor if he/she is taking prescription medication which may
       inhibit his/her ability to perform essential job duties. At the City’s discretion, employees may be
       asked to take time off or perform light-duty tasks if the prescription medications they are taking
       prevent them from performing essential job functions.

SEARCH AND SEIZURE
It is the intent of Riverton City to enforce its policy banning any illicit drug or alcohol in any detectable
amount on City premises, parking lots, equipment, toolboxes, vehicles used for City business, or job sites
where City work is being performed. The City has the right to search such vehicles or items at the City’s
discretion to expose any concealed drugs or alcohol and to seize any such substances.

be judged as satisfactory to the City in its sole but reasonable discretion. If a urine sample provided by a
prospective employee or an employee is determined after appropriate testing to be an excess fluids
sample, the prospective employee or employee shall, at the City’s discretion, be required to provide
another urine sample (the “second sample”) at a time and under conditions and requirements that are
within the sole discretion of the City. In the event the second sample is determined, after appropriate
testing, to be an excess fluids sample, the prospective employee shall not be offered employment with
the City.

In addition, when any sample is deemed by the collector at the time of collection to have a temperature
out-of-range, or to have been adulterated, the prospective employee shall not be hired and the current
employee shall immediately produce a second specimen for testing. If he/she refuses to provide a
second specimen or if the second specimen is also deemed by the collector to have been adulterated or
temperature out-of-range, he/she shall be subject to City action.

PURPOSE
The intent of this policy is to ensure all property maintained by Riverton City is kept in the best possible
working condition and to ensure proper utilization. Property shall be defined as any piece of
equipment, furnishing, vehicle, building or supply leased, owned, donated or otherwise in the custodial
care of Riverton City or any person acting as its agent.




Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                                 Page | 61
GENERAL CONDITIONS
It is the responsibility of each employee to maintain his/her work environment in an orderly fashion and

While employees are at work they are expected to use discretion while using company phones (whether
land line or cell) or personal cell phones. Personal calls during the workday, regardless of the phone
used, can interfere with employee productivity and be distracting to others. Riverton City will not be
liable for the loss of personal cell phones.

    2. Conduct personal business during lunch time and breaks where possible;
    3. Ensure family and friends are aware of the policy;
    4. Limit personal calls on City-owned cell phones so as not to affect over usage of allowed pooled

    5. Not text on City-owned cell phones;

6.12 EMPLOYEE DRIVING QUALIFICATIONS
All employees or volunteers who are required to drive a city-owned vehicle while conducting business
for or on behalf of Riverton City must meet the minimum qualifications outlined in this policy.

    1. Upon hire, all drivers must possess a valid driver’s license with the classification appropriate to
       the type of vehicle being driven.
    2. Upon hire, all drivers are required to provide a copy of their driver’s license to Human Resources
       to be kept in their HR file.

MOTOR VEHICLE RECORD REVIEW
The Human Resources Department will use driver’s license information to verify each driver may
lawfully operate a vehicle on the roads in the state of Utah. The driver qualification evaluation will be
based on the driver’s Motor Vehicle Record (MVR) as well as other work-related motor vehicle incidents
that may not be reported to the Driver’s License Division (DLD) and recorded on the driver’s MVR. All
violations or incidents recorded on the MVR, whether they occurred on the job or not, are included in
the driver qualification evaluation.

    1. MVR review is initially conducted for potential new hires as part of the background screening
       process.
    2. An evaluation is based on MVR moving violations for the past 3 years, work-related motor
       vehicle incidents for the past 3 years, and serious violations for the past 5 years.
    3. MVR review will be monitored regularly by the Human Resources Department. However,
       employees must notify their supervisor and Human Resource designee within 30 days of a
       conviction for any traffic violation.
    4. MVR review information is considered a personnel record, and is not subject to public records
       inspection or release unless required by subpoena, court order, or law.

BORDERLINE DRIVING STATUS
The following actions are required for current drivers who have a Borderline driving status:

    1. The City Manager or designee must provide written authorization to allow the driver to continue
       driving on City business while classified as borderline driving status. Such authorization will
       include instructions and the expected duration of borderline status.
    2. Borderline drivers must complete defensive driving instruction as provided by the City
       Attorney’s Office.
    3. Failure to maintain an acceptable or borderline driving status may result in action up to and
       including suspension or revocation of City driving privileges and/or termination of employment.

UNACCEPTABLE DRIVING STATUS
The following actions are required for current drivers who have an Unacceptable driving status:

    1. The City Manager or designee must provide written notice to the driver and their Department
       Head that the driver is prohibited from driving any vehicle on City business until the driver
       receives written notification that their driving status has returned to either borderline or
       acceptable status. Drivers who transition from unacceptable to borderline status require written
       authorization to drive as described in Section 3.0 above.
    2. While classified as unacceptable status, the MVR will be reviewed every 30 days to monitor if
       additional moving violations have occurred.
    3. Failure to maintain an acceptable or borderline driving record may result in action up to and
       including suspension or revocation of City driving privileges and/or termination of employment.

7.1 GENERAL SAFETY POLICY
Riverton City’s policy is to provide a work environment free of hazards that may cause physical harm or
illness to employees and citizens. Each City employee is entitled to work in a safe environment and is


Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                               Page | 68
required to work in compliance with all regulations, standards, and special safety policies. Failure to
adhere to safety policies may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of
employment.

Due to each department’s unique operations, each department will establish and maintain its own safe
operating policies and procedures as needed and will continually review and update them as needed.
7.2 EMPLOYEE RESPONSIBILITIES
Employees are required to:

    1. Maintain a clean, orderly, and safe work area that is free from hazards at all times.
    2. Perform work in a safe manner so as not to jeopardize oneself or others in the work area.
    3. Use protective equipment and safe materials.
    4. Identify and become familiar with the emergency standard operating plan and annexes,
       evacuation procedures and other general safety issues in the event of an emergency.
    5. Report all accidents, injuries, or near-miss incidents that the employee is involved in or has
       witnessed in accordance with the Incident Reporting guidelines.
    6. Complete mandatory safety training.
    7. Follow department-specific safe operating policies and procedures that are relevant to
       workplace operations.
7.3 SUPERVISOR/MANAGER RESPONSIBILITIES
Supervisors are required to:

    1. Establish and provide adequate, documented, training to employees on the department’s
       Standard Operating Procedures and other safety policies and practices which meet Utah
       Occupational Safety and Health Administration guidelines in order to ensure the employee
       performs his/her job in the safest manner possible.
    2. Ensure that all employees are provided with current material that addresses the emergency
       standard operating plan and annexes, evacuation procedures and other general safety issues.
    3. Ensure that all protective equipment and safe materials are used.
    4. Enforce all safety and accident prevention policies and procedures.
    5. Regularly inspect worksites, materials, and equipment to ensure work areas are free of hazards,
       and take immediate corrective action to eliminate hazardous conditions.
    6. Investigate and immediately report any accidents, injuries, or near-miss accidents in accordance
       with the Incident Reporting guidelines.
    7. Enforce department-specific safe operating policies and procedures that are relevant to
       workplace operations.
7.4 INCIDENT REPORTING
All accidents or incidents causing employee injury, illness, property damage, or possible liability for the
City must be reported in accordance with the steps outlined below. Failure to report an incident, no
matter how minor, may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination.

         1. The employee should immediately stop what they are doing and notify a supervisor.
         2. The supervisor should immediately notify the Human Resource designee for instructions on
            whether a drug screen is required.
         3. The supervisor should notify the Risk Manager and complete an Incident Report form with
            the employee. The completed form must be submitted to the Risk Manager along with all



Riverton City Personnel Policies & Procedures Manual                                                 Page | 69
              other relevant information pertaining to the incident (photos, witness statements,
              supervisor investigation, etc.)
7.5 SAFETY COMMITTEE
The primary purpose of the Safety Committees (the “Committee”) is to ensure, to the extent possible,
that Riverton City is practicing safe work procedures. The Committee is organized under direction of the
City Manager and shall serve in an advisory capacity on matters concerning safety conditions of Riverton
City. The Committee will participate in the accident review process and also participate in programs
such as safety audits, near miss reporting, accident investigations, and the safety incentive program.
The Committee may determine that some form of discipline is warranted for violations of Riverton City
safety policies and make recommendations that are within their scope of authority.
