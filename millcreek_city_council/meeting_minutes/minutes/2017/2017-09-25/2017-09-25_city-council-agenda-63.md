6.1 John Janson, Planning Presentation Regarding Proposed Amendments to the M-1 and M-2 Zoning
Ordinance

Mayor Silvestrini explained to all those in attendance and those listening via Facebook, first, the City will
have a presentation by their planning consultant, John Janson. Following the presentation, the City will
conduct a public hearing; following the public hearing, the Council will consider an ordinance.

Mr. Janson presented with his PowerPoint presentation.

Mr. Janson discussed the areas and changes being proposed. He noted that a few years ago, a
Redevelopment Area (RDA) looked at the area, and the study found some blight. In addition, there was a
plan to look at transit-oriented uses.

Mr. Janson went on to say they looked at the zoning map of the area. The M-1 zoning is along State
Street; the M-1 and M-2 zoning are along Cottonwood Creek; the M-1 zoning and Apartment zoning are
next to the Big Cottonwood Creek to meet into the Jordan River, the Trax stations, and into Fireclay and
in the Meadow Brook area.

Mr. Janson presented a few pictures he took of the area on Friday, September 22, 2017. He stated they
have seen a lot of changes in Salt Lake County, which is a part of the Meadow Brook Study. He pointed
out that south of the Bud Bailey Apartments, there is much concern regarding how the residents are
interacting with the area. He added there are other apartments south of the Meadowbrook area, and it
is approved as a mixed-use area. They should expect to see more residential in the area.

Mr. Janson addressed the area further south, along Main Street. He noted that area is being proposed as
mixed use but it will initially be residential. He went on to discuss the area south of Fireclay. He said the
Planning Commission is reviewing a proposed development from RMP Development. It is a 7-acre
development, which transitions to a residential building going all the way around the site with parking in
the middle.

Mayor Silvestrini said there is some confusion with the proposed development. The City did not have all
the correct information they needed. It was proposed at Planning Commission with 400 units, and the
developers are coming back with a new zone that is meant for high-density with a mixed use; in addition
to having a development agreement.

Mr. Janson referred to the Bud Bailey Apartments. He noted there are junk vehicles in the street, which
is a common site in the streets. In addition, there is concrete and storage along Big Cottonwood Creek.

Mr. Janson switched to the staff report, and he indicated he would quickly go through the draft proposal
of the code amendments.

Mr. Janson discussed the Community Council and public comments they had. He said they mailed out
notices twice; however, with the first notice they had inaccurate information from the County and a lack
of public comment; therefore, they sent out a second notice. He explained that State law has changed a

5

lot over the recent years, and it is no longer required to mail out noticed, but the City Council wanted to
do that.

Mr. Janson said they had a lot of phone calls; the calls were individuals trying to figure out what is going
on with the code amendments. He pointed out there is a lot of concern with the Brickyard parcel that is
partially zoned M-1. The question has come up, if | have a business that is not licensed, am |
grandfathered. Mr. Janson indicated they discussed that issued with legal counsel at length and the
answer is no. It has to be a legal business.

Mr. Janson stated the term is grandfathered uses; uses that become at odds with the zoning ordinance.
When the County ordinance has allowed a business to step down from one use to another —
transitioning from one use to another. He stated that conversation went on for a few months.

Mr. Janson pointed out the County has some language in the M-1 and M-2 zones, and they proposed
some changes to that language. He explained the reason for that change is to address concerns of the
property owners.

Mr. Janson referred to the proposed code amendments. He stated the idea is to go from a M-1 zone
and M-2 zone to Light manufacturing zone; however, there is a lot of heavy manufacturing in the M-1
zone. He noted the M-2 zone is heavy manufacturing, and the City allows warehousing in the
manufacturing zones.

Mr. Janson explained anything over an acre in the M-1, Light Manufacturing zone, is a conditional use. In
the proposed M zone, there will be a table, which will show permitted or conditional uses. He went on
to say, he spoke with two gentlemen, and they were concerned their trucking businesses would not be
allowed. Mr. Janson indicated it is a conditional use but it is allowed.

Mr. Janson stated, as a concession to the proposed M zone, new outside repair and fabrication would
not be allowed; however, outdoor uses would be allowed in the rear yard and side yard. In addition, 50
percent of the parcel could only be utilized. Legal existing uses can use 100 percent of the parcel for
outdoor use. Mr. Janson addressed concerns with the M zone adjacent to residential uses. He stated
the operations of businesses in an enclosed building could include almost anything. He went on to say,
the height of a building was reduced from 75-feet to 40-feet.

Mr. Janson said they spent time on how many felt their uses would become non-conforming. He said he
is not sure that is true with this draft. They have offered that any of the property owners that want a
zoning determination letter/status of property letter, will not be charge for a letter. He noted the letter
usually costs approximately $150.

Mayor Silvestrini inquired what the purpose of a zoning determination letter is for. Mr. Janson explained
it describes what is there and provides a letter of the status. He mentioned itis a good letter to get.

Mr. Janson explained a nonconforming use runs with the land; it does not go with the property owner. It
is transferable.

Mr. Janson stated this is a unique situation. There are not a lot of improvements along the street. The
Council can change the nonconforming use, and the ordinance is not meant to be 100 percent inclusive.
He said if a property owner will help with improvements to the front of the property, the City could be
amenable to allowing expansion.

Mayor Silvestrini pointed out there are multiple letters that had been sent in by property owners
included in the Council’s packet. In addition, there is one response from him to Mr. Black. He went on to
say, he received one letter from Fred Jones, and he distributed the letter to the City Council that is not in
the packet.

6.3 Discussion and Consideration of Proposed Ordinance No. 17-__, an Ordinance repealing the M-1 and
M-2 Zone of the Millcreek Municipal Code, approving a New Light Manufacturing Zone M-1 of the
Millcreek Municipal Code, and Changing the Zoning Map of Millcreek for the Zones designated M-1 and
M-2 to the New M-Zone. No Changes to Zoning District Boundaries are proposed

Mayor Silvestrini thanked everyone for their comments and for engaging with the Planning Commission
constructively as it makes it better for the property owners and businesses. Mayor Silvestrini stated
Millcreek does appreciate the businesses in the City.

Mayor Silvestrini explained the City is faced with a lot of problems. In addition, they are faced with
pressure from the State of Utah and the Legislature in regards to cities being resistant to high-density
housing along the transportation lines where the population is expected to double in the future.

Mayor Silvestrini stated as a Council, they are seeing this area transition. The area has preceded the City
and what they are seeing before them is more apartments. He pointed out there is a 440 unit project for
the Wind River parcel; it is coming. He said it is like a rainstorm coming and they cannot ignore it. They
can try to accommodate it.

Mayor Silvestrini acknowledged that housing does not always bring revenue to the City; however, they
have to make it more livable for the City. In addition, he knows not every business is blighted, but there
are legitimate junkyards in the area. There are businesses with no curb and gutter, and the City has to
do something to fix it. This is an effort to do something. He pointed out it is not the City that is making it
happen; it is the market. Mayor Silvestrini stated the existing zone is a zone that was implemented by
Salt Lake County 60 years ago. He explained he could go back to 1980 when it was codified, and it is
difficult to figure out past that.

Mayor Silvestrini addressed allowable uses in the current zone: blacksmiths, meatpacking, incinerators,
rock crushing, oil refineries, etc. He stated those uses are no longer compatible in the area. He said the
point is, |am concerned with doing something with the zone; however, those types of uses are clearly
not appropriate.

Mayor Silvestrini said he agreed with the property owners on some of the things in the ordinance and
there are some ambiguities. He thought the Council needed to have a Work Session to address the
issues/ambiguities and clarify those things. Mayor Silvestrini added he personally wanted to work ona
transitional zone and make it more palatable and reach out to the property owners and have further
discussion with the City Council.

Mayor Silvestrini indicated he know this is their property, and he is a firm believer in that; however, they
have to prepare for the transition they are seeing. They have to have some compromise. He did not
think they could not do anything; they have to do something. Mayor Silvestrini stated he’d rather have
a sidewalk so people can walk down the street.

8.3 Penalty Fee Waiver Requests

Mayor Silvestrini indicated he discussed this item with the Council previously. He said if a business did
not renew their business license on time, they were assessed a penalty fee. He added he discussed the
policy, acceptance and waivers with the Council. He explained when a particular business is late, and if
they had a decent excuse, he waived the fee. However, if the business is a repeat late payer, he denied
the request.

Mayor Silvestrini reviewed the businesses that he authorized waivers for with the Council. He noted
there was only one request he denied.

Council Member Jackson inquired about the letter that was sent to the business owners. She asked if
the letters specified why the fee waiver was or was not granted. Mayor Silvestrini indicated it did not;
the letter just said he was waiving or not waiving the fee. Council Member Jackson suggested the letter
say why the fee was or was not waived. Mayor Silvestrini indicated he could do that.

Motion: Council Member Jackson moved to ratify the letters and waivers as presented by Mayor

Silvestrini and suggested the letters state why the fees were or were not waived. Council Member
Catten made the second. The motion passed unanimously.
