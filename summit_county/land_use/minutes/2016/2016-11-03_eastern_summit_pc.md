The	Stewart	Ranch	Park	Subdivision	was	platted	in	1938.		The	lots	range	in	size	from	
0.95	acres	to	2.23	acres.			
	
Planner	Northrup‐Robinson	said	some	of	the	lots	have	been	modified	slightly.		A	
vicinity	map	was	used	to	show	the	modifications.		Although	they	are	different	than	
originally	platted,	they	still	meet	the	lot	of	record	definitions	and	are	considered	legal	
lots	of	record.		These	small	lots	contain	critical	lands.									
	
Planner	Northrup‐Robinson	said	that	some	lots	outside	of	the	plat	will	be	directly	
impacted.		CD‐2248‐A	is	a	legal	lot	of	record.		It	has	a	home	that	was	constructed	in	
1958.		On	that	lot	are	some	outbuildings	that	include	a	tack	shed,	tennis	court,	barn,	and	
garage.		There	is	a	fairly	large	pond	that	will	be	affected	by	this	amendment	and	will	
require	a	lot	line	adjustment.		This	property	is	not	within	the	subdivision	plat.		
	
Planner	Northrup‐Robinson	said	that	Parcel	CD‐2248	is	not	a	legal	lot	of	record.		She	
added	that	the	applicant	is	proposing	to	adjust	lot	lines	which	would	result	in	a	
modification	of	the	boundaries	of	the	subdivision.		She	pointed	out	the	lots	that	are	
unbuildable	because	they	would	not	be	able	to	meet	setback	requirements	from	the	
river.		It	would	be	difficult	to	place	a	septic	system	on	these	lots.					
	
Planner	Northrup‐Robinson	described	the	proposed	amendments.		Lots	A	and	B	and	
portions	of	Lot	C,	D,	and	E	will	be	vacated	outside	of	the	plat.		This	action	will	result	in	
all	of	the	previously	mentioned	improvements	(home,	tack	shed,	pond,	etc.)	being	
outside	of	the	subdivision	or	Parcel	CD‐2248‐A.		The	lot	of	record	status	will	be	
maintained	for	CD‐2248‐A.			
	
The	two	building	lots	of	A	and	B	will	be	relocated	into	parcel	CD‐2248	and	the	building	
rights	will	be	maintained.		If	approved,	this	action	will	create	larger	lot	sizes	that	could	
meet	the	stream	setbacks	and	would	be	more	suitable	for	septic	systems.		A	small	
portion	of	Lots	C	and	D	will	be	combined	into	parcel	CD‐2248‐A.

A	lot	line	adjustment	will	occur	on	Lot	E.		Lot	G‐1	will	be	incorporated	into	CD‐2248.

Planner	Milliner	reminded	the	Commission	this	is	a	continuation	of	a	discussion	they	
have	had	regarding	Chapter	2.		The	point	of	concern	the	Commission	has	had	is	with	
regard	to	ridgeline	development.		Under	the	current	Code,	an	individual	is	prohibited	
from	building	a	structure	on	a	ridgeline	that	is	viewed	from	a	public	driveway.			
	
Staff	was	given	instructions	by	the	County	Council	to	change	the	language	so	an	
individual	can	have	a	reasonable	process	to	allow	building	on	a	platted	lot	or	a	lot	of	
record.			The	Commission	has	had	discussions	about	adopting	view	corridors.		At	the	
last	meeting,	they	came	up	with	an	idea	that	has	been	dubbed	as	the	“ridgeline	donut.”					
	
Originally,	the	inner	circle	was	designated	as	1/3	mile.		A	home	within	the	inner	circle	
would	not	be	required	to	have	any	kind	of	review.		The	outer	circle	would	be	1½	miles	
in	width.		If	there	is	a	public	roadway	that	goes	through	the	outer	circle,	and	if	there	is	a	
proposed	structure	that	may	ridgeline,	the	applicant	will	be	required	to	go	through	a	
low	impact	permit.		Certain	criteria	must	be	met.		Since	the	last	meeting,	the	inner	circle	
has	been	increased	to	½	mile	per	the	instruction	of	the	Planning	Commission.			
	
Planner	Milliner	gave	some	examples	of	how	this	would	work.		They	would	lay	the	
“donut”	with	the	crosshairs	over	the	subject	property.		If	there	were	a	public	road	going	
through	the	outer	circle,	the	property	would	be	reviewed.
