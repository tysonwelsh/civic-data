                             SPECIAL HERRIMAN CITY
                         COUNCIL WORK MEETING MINUTES
                                      Monday, August 16, 2021
                                     Approved October 27, 2021
The following are the minutes of the City Council Meeting of the Herriman City Council. The meeting was
held on Monday, August 16, 2021 at 5:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Fort Herriman Conference Room, 5355
West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted
in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

Presiding: Mayor David Watts

Councilmembers Present: Jared Henderson, Sherrie Ohrn, Steve Shields, Clint Smith.

Staff Present: City Manager Nathan Cherpeski, Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager
Tami Moody, City Attorney Chase Andrizzi, Communications Manager Jonathan Lafollette, Communications
Specialist Destiny Skinner, Chief of Police Troy Carr, and City Recorder Jackie Nostrom

5:00 PM - SPECIAL WORK MEETING:
1. Call to Order
Mayor Watts called the meeting to order at 5:04 p.m.

2. Administrative Reports
   2.1.  Herriman City Council strategic planning and visioning discussion – Nathan Cherpeski,
   City Manager
         • Purpose
         • Create short-term (1 year) and mid-term (1 to 3 years) goals and provide direction of 3 to
            5 priorities
         • Question to consider
         • What does a successful Herriman look like?
         • How do we get to success?
         • What investments move us forward?
         • Are there efforts we are currently undertaking that don’t add value or move us forward?
City Manager Nathan Cherpeski presented a 10-year plan in draft form for a capital improvement plan and
illustrated the impact of financial burdens. He noted the model presented was based off assumptions based on
information received from the Finance Department. Mayor Watts voiced his concern of having considered
conservative assumptions and relayed his position he did not want to have resulting missed opportunities.
Manager Cherpeski responded he would like to propose a standard to maintain a fund balance. Mayor Watts
requested to insert a minimum threshold parameter within the illustration.

City Manager Cherpeski pointed out from previous analysis; most fund balance estimates were based on a three
to five-year average. He acknowledged the lack of history pertaining to some funds; however, could review
property taxes and growth to help, but it was still in progress. This analysis would be part of the budget discussion
next year to help the Council determine how to spend allocated resources.

He highlighted multiple vision statements were found and observed the differences and recommended the vision
and mission statement being revisited. City Manager Cherpeski explained a vision statement is where the Council
would want to end up and a mission statement is the plan put in place to achieve the vision.

                                  Councilmember Jared Henderson arrived at 5:14 p.m.

The five-vision objective highlighted in the budget included:
       • Fiscal responsibility and sustainability
       • Public safety and protection
       • Community engagement
       • Community vibrancy
       • Economic Development

City Manager Cherpeski felt these were more conceptive. He explained his goal was to come up with at least three
priorities for the City to focus on short-term. He asked the Council to think about what a successful Herriman
looked like and how they would know it had been achieved. He also suggested the Council to consider investments
needed to achieve the goal, for example, money, staff time, or political resources. He also questioned if staff is
working on projects currently which do not add value or move the City forward as he did not want to be efficient
in something which would ultimately be ineffective.

Councilmember Ohrn offered an anecdote of low hanging fruit. She suggested there was a lot of time and effort
spent chasing the “low hanging fruit” or the next big thing to bring in revenue and keep the City moving; however,
staff need to focus on the state of the City and how to maintain the current community prior to looking for
additions or enhancements.

Mayor Watts expressed his desire to focus on the Olympia process, updating design standards and completely
outlining the Master Development Agreement process to the standard desired by the Council. Councilmember
Smith stated the City should focus on transportation needs as current roads are either in disrepair or are not
functioning at the appropriate level. He pondered a more proactive approach to meet the future demand of
transportation and noted some would argue the City is not meeting the current demand; however, there were
compounding factors. Currently 12600 South could not be used as an east/westbound option and further
discussion with Utah Department of Transportation needed to be conducted. Transportation is a high priority
for the residents. Mayor Watts agreed. Councilmember Ohrn shared her focus would be essential services
including water, transportation, public safety, power, gas, and any other service which would offer the residents a
higher quality of life. She defined non-essential services as parks and entertainment with the basis they would not
be needed for daily living.

Councilmember Smith focused on the finances as there has been a lot of focus on commercial growth to help
make the City more sustainable. The City has tried to solicit businesses to help support the sales tax base and
observed projects trying to move forward with the same goal in mind.

Councilmember Henderson reiterated critical services provided by the City include: water, roads, and public
service and suggested those should be considered the starting point and listed items he would like to see over the
next 12-months:
    • Financial Position: not having to use one-time monies for operational expenditures. Those funds should
        be allocated for capital projects.
    • Quantify and know contractual obligations.
    • Focus on a true maintenance plan.
Councilmember Henderson opined the financial stability of the City would be the determining factor for success
regardless of potential growth. He suggested the City needed to preserve non-residential uses, develop an
economical plan including daytime population, large recreational areas as well as transportation plans for true
ingress and egress. Councilmember Henderson acknowledged Herriman needs destination locations due to the
unique position and suggested partnering with Salt Lake County and other entities to encourage sporting events
to be hosted in the community. He defined a successful economy are based on those interdependent faucets and
not a single aspect. He stated ultimately, the City needed to balance the land use matrix.

Councilmember Smith interjected a goal should be able to increase incrementally as new growth has been
captured. If this is not done, a deficit would be created, and it would be difficult to catch up. He agreed there was
a financial component needing to be addressed while developing a plan to meet contractual obligations without
leaving out critical components in the process. He reiterated the City was behind on road maintenance; however,
recognized the continual increased costs if the City failed to act.

Councilmember Ohrn asked what funds were ahead. City Manager Cherpeski noted utilities tend to take care of
themselves because of how they are structured. Every time the City takes on maintenance for a public road, the
cost is ongoing. He rhetorically asked if the Council takes thoughts like this into consider when making decisions.
He re-evaluated the capital improvement plan outlining the fund balance to illustrate how cost assumptions move
the numbers around. He indicated once the model has been completed, it will be brought back to the Council so
they are aware of when funds have been committed for a specific purpose, they cannot be used for other things.
He explained the model would not be 100-percent accurate; however, would offer a level of understanding the
trends. City Manager Cherpeski further noted staff was working on realistic growth in different departments for
Council consumption.
Councilmember Smith asked if the potential census numbers were considered and if this consideration would
require an adjustment to the budget. City Manager Cherpeski responded the numbers were estimated and noted
the Census would be available in September.

Councilmember Ohrn reiterated the need to have a balanced community and the Council needed to have accurate
data in order to make an informed decision on what would be truly needed. She questioned if there was a tool to
use to help determine the balance. Councilmember Henderson recalled a study which had been conducted several
years ago. The City had taken several steps; however, never finalized a cohesive plan.

City Manager Cherpeski pointed out the rapidly growing community would result in some areas within the City
to be short staffed. He agreed a balance needed to be determined and staff would start building a tactical plan
once Council had established a strategy. He suggested it would be valuable for Council to complete a strategic
planning discussion.

Councilmember Shields found these exercises were challenging due to the subjective viewpoints. He stated the
Council would need to know the destination in order to know what tools should be utilized along the way to
achieve the goal. He noted he heard individuals advocating for balance and austerity; however, they were not the
same thing. He opined the community needed essential and non-essential services which were complex choices.
He stated assumptions and assertions needed to be based on realistic data and used a metaphor for making
decisions in a vacuum, which was unwise. The path forward was dependent on the destination and
Councilmember Shields notated he could not support an idea without knowing the end goal.

Councilmember Henderson stated the Council needed to start somewhere and either choosing a short-term or
long-term goals would be good starting points. He also discussed having a destination as to what the Council
would like to achieve. He recognized this would be a “rolling” 3–5-year plan because the destination could change.

Councilmember Shields commented how there was not really a method to government finance. He indicated
there was a lot of information lacking to help them understand and make informed decisions. The Council does
not know where the revenue would be going because it seems to be constantly changing. He reiterated he does
not know the contractual obligations for the City and said the Council has been asked to make decisions; however,
additional information would be necessary to offer appropriate direction.

Councilmember Shields observed the current one-year budget being reviewed and thought it would be a good
goal to have one-time monies being allocated to pay for one-time expenditures while recurring revenues would
pay for ongoing maintenance.

Councilmember Henderson asked what anticipated projects would commence within the next 12-months which
needed additional input from the Council. He recommended making the plan workable which could “free up”
short short-term money instead of spending the funds just because they are available. Councilmember Shields
suggested the Council tried to give answers on projects when needed and added there should be flexibility built
into the project cost for unexpected expenses. He requested staff incorporate the information in projects they
would like the Council to consider along with the necessary contractual obligations to be able to come up with a
contingency plan for unanticipated costs.

Councilmember Smith appreciated the explanation from Councilmember Henderson. He referenced his goals for
the City and restated the Council needed to understand the full obligations in order to prioritize funding
allocations for projects. He pointed out everyone kept indicating the need to fully understand the obligations.

City Manager Cherpeski agreed the City had some components for a five-year plan and now would look to
incorporate all of those pieces into the same document in order for the Council and staff to work towards a
common goal. First, a strategic plan would be built while strategizing the tactics to achieve success. Second, goals
would be created where they could be referenced easily while maintaining focus. City Manager Cherpeski opined
in a perfect world; everyone would have a common goal. He noted the Council goals would be set at a higher
level while staff would produce measurable goals to allow for checks and balances to ensure the work being done
would be effective.

Manager Cherpeski summarized the discussion and outlined what would be brought back to the Council. He had
anticipated coming out of the discussion would be a list of higher-level goals to offer direction in coming up with
measurable goals. Councilmember Henderson commented his definition of success would be for the City to be
financially stable regardless of potential growth. The city needed to have a future goal to figure out where to begin.
He asked what could be achieved in the next 12-months to help make some of those long-term decisions. Mayor
Watts requested a financial analysis comparison between optimistic, realistic, and conservative plans to determine
what would be reasonable. He added his long-term goal for the City is public safety and financial stability.

Further discussion ensued regarding the Council and staff having a clear understanding of each other’s point of
views. Councilmember Shields declared this work is about collaboration between the Council and staff. City
Manager Cherpeski informed the Council of the lack of staff the City currently employs based on population size
which would be a contributing factor in the Council’s feeling of not receiving the information requested. The
Council further discussed the need to ensure the City had the resources necessary to accomplish the goal.

Discussion started regarding council and staff being on the same page and understanding each other’s reasonings.
Councilmember Shields commented it was about council and staff together. Manager Cherpeski noted that the
city is short on staff for how large the community is. Which is contributing to the lack of information council is
looking for. More discussion between council commenced over the staff needed to accomplish what is needed.
Councilmember Ohrn recited a comment from someone notating staff operate in fear of the Council and opined
this mindset was due to the differing assumptions between Council and staff. She relayed the importance to
convey the message that work being conducted is a collaborative effort.

Councilmember Henderson commented in every situation there is data to consider, and the information
interpreted was subjective. When something is brought to the Council for consideration, a moderator needs to be
able to interpret the desire of the Council, clarify the information for staff to be able to put the information
together for the Council to reconsider.
    City Manager Cherpeski stated Herriman was well positioned for the future. He encouraged the Council to reach
    out if information outlined in a staff report was unclear so any assumptions could be disseminated in order for
    the Council to make an informed decision.

3. Adjournment
   Councilmember Henderson moved to adjourn the City Council meeting at 7:03 p.m. Councilmember Shields seconded the motion,
   and all voted aye.
