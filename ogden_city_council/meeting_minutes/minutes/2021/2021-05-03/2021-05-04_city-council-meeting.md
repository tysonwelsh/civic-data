                 The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00p.m.;
        participate in Council/Mayor/Police Department discussion; discuss Council business; and hear Council comments.

        Agenda Review
                 Chair Blair and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled
        to begin at 6:00p.m.
                 Council Executive Director Eller-Smith then introduced the Council Office's newest staff member, Ross Watkins, Policy
        Analyst. Mr. Watkins provided the Council with a brief overview of his professional and personal background and stated he is
        excited for this opportunity to work for Ogden. The Council and Mayor Caldwell welcomed Mr. Watkins to the City.

        Council/Mayor/Police Discussion
                  Mayor Caldwell stated he has nothing specific to report but expressed a willingness to answer questions from the Council
        regarding any topic.
                  Council member Stephens inquired as to Administration's ideas regarding the use of American Rescue Plan Act 2021
        (ARPA) funds that the City may receive in the coming fiscal year. He also asked for an update regarding any redevelopment plans
        for the old Fred Meyer site on 12th Street. Mayor Caldwell reported there are some very unique rules and restrictions for use of
        ARPA funds and Administration is working through those matters before determining how to allocate the funds. The great thing
        about the ARPA program, when compared to the 2020 CARES Act, is that the City is not faced with developing a plan and
        spending the funds in such a short period of time. He noted Ogden City will receive about $26 million, with Weber County set to
        receive approximately $50 million. The State of Utah will receive $1.5 billion and there are many concerns about how the funds
        are allocated and spent; specifically, many cities plan to use the funds for infrastructure projects, which may overwhelm capable
        contractors in the State and drive up prices for such projects. He added future generations will be charged with repaying the
        money and it is important to invest the funding in ways that will benefit those future generations and improve their quality oflife.
        He then noted that he does not have information to provide regarding the redevelopment of the old Fred Meyer site at this time,
        but he will ask the City's Community and Economic Development for that infonnation. He noted that the property is privately
        owned, and the City does not have much control over it at this time.
                  Vice Chair White referenced drought conditions and the importance of the City promoting wise water habits, which
        includes not wasting water to maintain grass and other landscape features. Mayor Caldwell agreed this is a very important issue
        and one that Administration is working to address with other community partners; he will provide infonnation on fmalized
)       infonnation campaigns to the City Council once they are available. Council member Hyer mentioned projects being promoted by
        other entities whereby property owners would be incentivized to convert their park strip area from grass to hardscape or other
        waterwise landscaping features. Mayor Caldwell stated that is a great idea and he will take it under advisement as he continues

discussing the issue with City Administration and other partners. Council member Choberka suggested consideration of allowing
residents to plant a vegetable garden in their park strip as well.
          Police Chief Young then addressed the Council and thanked them for the opportunity to engage in this discussion; he
introduced his new Deputy Police Chief, Jake Sube, and provided information about the process he utilized to recruit and
interview for the position. Mr. Sube also provided the group with a brief overview of his professional and personal background,
after which the Council welcomed Mr. Sube and congratulated him on his new position. Chief Young then used the aid of a
PowerPoint presentation to provide the Council with information regarding his direction for the Police Department; this included a
focus on recruitment and retention and the goal of reaching full staffmg levels in order to make a considerable impact on crime
levels in the community. He thanked the Council for the action taken to increase wages for police officers as this has helped him
to improve his recruitment and retention efforts. He added that community engagement is also very important to him and one
thing that he is focusing on at this time; this is especially important on the heels of the COVID-19 pandemic and the social unrest
that occurred in 2020. It is vital for the community to personally know its Police Chief and the officers that are sworn to protect
and serve them. Police Department accountability is very important to him, and he is charging each Division Manager in his
Department to focus on accountability and employee support intended to improve employee morale in the Department. He is also
pursuing technology and equipment improvements that will ensure compliance with changes in State and Federal laws regarding
policing and to improve customer service in the community. Also, he is employing training programs that are also aimed at
improving customer service in the community. Finally, the top priority for the Department is to provide public safety and ensure
citizens have a safe environment to live, work, and play in.
          Several Council members thanked Chief Young for his efforts to engage and reach out to the community to discuss many
important social justice issues. They also commented on the quality of the Department's leadership and the police officers
employed by the Department.


         The meeting adjourned at 5:56p.m.
