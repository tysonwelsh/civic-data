Rob Hunter seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Station Area Planning – LaNiece Davenport, MAG (2:52) HB462 (Housing Affordability Amendments, Rep. Waldrip) requires cities with fixed rail transit stations to adopt station area plans for those stations and update general plan and zoning to implement the station area plan. MAG, in partnership with the Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC), Utah Transit Authority (UTA), and the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity (GOEO), can provide technical assistance to help communities complete or update their SAPs. SAP provisions have an effective date of June 1, 2022. The first application deadline for these technical assistance funds is July 15, 2022. Overall objectives of HB462 include maximizing development potential in appropriate areas, collaborative city-led planning approach, and allowing cities to determine how to best meet shared objective. Shared objectives include the opportunity to increase availability and affordability of housing, promote sustainable environmental conditions, enhance access to opportunities, and increase transportation choices and connections. Requirements, timelines, and technical assistance processes were discussed. Federal performance Measures-Statewide Target Adjustments – Bob Allen, MAG (41:39) Federal Transportation law requires all States and MPOs to set targets for and track specific transportation performance measures. In 2018, UDOT set and MAG ratified, targets for safety, infrastructure, and mobility. Incrementally, the State and MPOs can change those targets. UDOT would like to make minor adjustments to the previously set targets. MAG must adopt those new targets or determine its own targets and how it will achieve them. Jason Bond moved that TAC recommend that the MPO Regional Planning Committee adopt the adjusted statewide targets for the required federal performance measures as presented. Robert Mills seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. FY23 Unified Planning Work Program Approval – Shawn Eliot, MAG (1:22:55) The Unified Planning Work Program (Work Program) outlines the work that MAG, UDOT, and UTA will perform within the Mountainland MPO planning area. The Work Program is updated annually and contains budget for MPO programs and staff. The FY23 Work Program has a total budget of $3.52m, $1.76m for staff and admin costs and $1.76m for studies, programs, and contracts. Revenue for this $3.52m budget is approximately 40% federal funds while 60% is state and local funds form UTA, UDOT, MAG state funds, and local governments. Staff costs are down this year by $442k due to a restructuring in the Regional Planning Department allocating more staff time for rural planning outside the MPO. Studies and contracts are up $1.11m due to larger regional studies, MAG staff managing local studies funded through the TIP selection process, and the creation of a new grant program called the Transportation to Land Use Connection (TLC). Mary De La Mare-Schaeffer moved to recommend that the MPO Regional Planning Committee approve the FY23 Unified Planning and Work Program as presented. Chris Trusty seconded the motion.
