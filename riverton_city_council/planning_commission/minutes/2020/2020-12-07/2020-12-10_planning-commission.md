       A.     HOME OCCUPATION, CLIVE SNELSON IS REQUESTING A CONDITIONAL
              USE PERMIT FOR A HOME OCCUPATION FOR “CK FITNESS” TO BE
              OPERATED FROM A HOME AT 12843 SOUTH GOSHUTE DRIVE.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the application is for
a Conditional Use Permit for a home occupation located at 12843 South Goshute Drive. CK
Fitness is offering personal fitness training to customers in common scheduled times. They
would like to conduct business from 6:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. each day. Each customer will
come for about one hour. Customers will come at different times during the day. They
expect to receive one to six visitors a day. The ordinance allows up to eight. Trainings were
to be held in the garage. Customer parking will be offered in the driveway, which can
accommodate two cars.

Mr. Prestwich asked how the training will be offered in the garage while having two cars
parked in addition to the customers’ cars in the driveway. He illustrated how this would
occur. The applicant tried to comply with the ordinance and is reserving 50% of the garage
for the home occupation. No public comments were received on the application.

report. One of the conditions specifies that the hours of operation shall be from 6:00 a.m. to

Commission could change it. Investigations were still going on about the impact of the home

      B.     HOME OCCUPATION, RONALD HOLLAND IS REQUESTING A
             CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR A HOME OCCUPATION FOR
             “BALLISTIC ALLIANCE TO BE OPERATED FROM A HOME AT 1246
             SIRMINGO WAY.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the request is for a home occupation
application for Conditional Use Permit from Ballistics Alliance. The property is located at
1246 Sirmingo Way which is in an older established neighborhood. The home has a garage
and a long driveway to provide parking for customers. The use is for firearm sales.

Mr. Prestwich explained that there are always concerns about whether the use should be
allowed in neighborhoods. He explained that the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, and Firearms
(“ATF”) checks on these types of uses and ensures that a Firearms License has been
obtained before proceeding. The ATF requires an applicant to attain a business license for
conditional use approval and then follow up to get a business license.

Customers come by appointment only. It was noted that guns can be purchased online,
shipped to a home, and a time arranged to make an exchange. All of the paperwork and
files are completed with the ATF. All ATF requirements and guidelines must be met and
there will be very little, if any, impact on the neighbors.

Mr. Prestwich stated that no public comments were received on the item. Staff
recommended approval subject to conditions set forth in the staff report. The hours of
operation were proposed as 7:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. The Commission can reduce that at their
discretion.

There were no public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner James moved to APPROVE Application PLZ-20-2043, “Ballistics
Alliance”, to be located at 1246 Sirmingo Way, subject to the following conditions:

      1.     The site, structures, and use shall remain in compliance with any and all
             applicable Riverton City standards and ordinances, specifically the City
             Home Occupation Ordinance (18.190) and applicable Building and Fire
             Codes.

      2.     The applicant must obtain and maintain a Federal Firearms License.

      3.     The applicant must obtain and maintain a Riverton City business license.

      4.     The Home Occupation must operate within the Fixed Standards and
             applicable Qualifications and Conditions as outlined in the Home
             Occupation Ordinance and this approval.

       C.     REZONE, GORM KLUNGERCIK IS REQUESTING A REZONE OF
              PROPERTY LOCATED AT 2998 WEST 12600 SOUTH, THE CURRENT
              ZONE IS PROFESSIONAL OFFICE (C-PO) AND THE REQUESTED ZONE
              IS GATEWAY (C-G).

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich reported that this is a rezone of an existing approved office development west
of the High School. The property is located at 2998 West 12600 South. The property location
was identified on a map displayed. Mr. Prestwich reported that what has been built so far is
the parking and one pad. Five pad sites are available. The applicant built the first building
but struggled to fill space. It was planned as a medical office complex.

The applicant approached the City with ideas for different tenants. Mr. Prestwich stated that
the problem was that the Commercial Professional Office Zone is more limited in terms of
uses that are allowed. As the applicant was exploring potential tenants for the site they
determined that it is was in their best interest to apply for a rezone to Commercial Gateway.

Mr. Prestwich identified the Commercial Gateway on the map and stated that it is not out of
character with the area. The General Plan designation is Business Park, which makes both
the Professional Office zone and the Gateway Zone viable uses within it. Commercial
Gateway uses allow for a more of a retail mix than the Professional Office. Mr. Prestwich
stated that while the available uses would change with the zoning, the site would still be
limited by available parking and the configuration of the site. Staff recommended approval.

Commissioner Rushton asked if the changes would require the applicant to apply for another
site plan in case the result would differ from the existing site plan. Mr. Prestwich responded
in the affirmative and explained that there will be no significant changes to the site plan. The
changes would be mainly to the uses. Commissioner Rushton observed that there is
residential to the north of the property and asked about the height restrictions. Mr. Prestwich
stated that the height restriction is the same between the two zones and includes a limitation
of 20 feet. There is an additional one foot for one every foot in building height separation.
The impact on the neighbors will not change because of similar height limitations in both
zones.

Commissioner Rushton asked if there is a mechanism for an Interconnect Agreement.
Mr. Prestwich stated that the original site plan included a floating easement and there is a
cross-connection easement. The applicant, Gorm Klungervik confirmed that there is an
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                  5

easement that connects to the western portion of the property. It was an existing easement
that was required by the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”).

Chair Cannon asked the applicant if he has a good relationship with his neighbor to the east.
Mr. Klungervik reported that he met the neighbor once and had spoken to him. He explained
that they will not be applying for anything extreme on the property, however, there has been
an increase in requests for daycare centers, restaurants, and gym type facilities.

Commissioner Lefgren asked if the parking would change with the zone change.
Mr. Prestwich stated that the parking ordinance is based on use so it will be the same.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the staff report and state that the request is for a rezone of property
located at 12761 South 3600 West. The property has frontage onto 3600 West and is part
of the Stampin Up company. Stampin Up has made the decision not to expand further and
the owner is marketing the property for sale. The parcel is about eight acres in size and has
a mix of zoning consisting of CPO and M-1. There is no parcel boundary that corresponds
with the original campus plan for Stampin Up. The property is split between the two zones.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that the property to the south is Commercial Professional Office in
M-1, which is Light Industrial. To the east is a professional office but with an Elderly Housing
Overlay. To the north is RR-22. The bulk of the site is a church meetinghouse but the
zoning is Residential. Across 3600 West is Commercial Regional.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the General Plan designation for the property and explained that
it includes a mix of Business Park and Light Manufacturing. He reported that there was a
recent update to the land use map and the Commission discussed amendments to the
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                              6

General Plan being required before zoning that differs from the designation in this case. In
the proposed amendment, the City did not include an Elderly Housing Overlay or Elderly
Housing Designation in the General Plan.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the adjacent properties bear the Elderly Housing Overly that
is not designated as such in the General Plan. When the potential for the rezone application
was brought to the City, the City looked at the General Plan to determine if an amendment
was required for the application to proceed. Since there is no corresponding designation,
there is nothing for the General Plan to be amended to, which means that the General Plan
is ‘silent’ on the question of the Elderly Housing Overlay in this case. There was no
compelling reason why the application could not be accepted. The Planning Commission
can decide whether the amendment should be made to the General Plan.

Mr. Lethbridge reiterated that the proposed zoning is Professional Office with the Elderly
Housing Overlay. The reason for this proposal is that the Elderly Housing Overlay cannot
be applied to Light Industrial in its current form. For the portion not already zoned
Professional Office, the proposed rezoning would change that and apply the overlay to the
remainder of the property.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that a couple of questions were raised regarding the Elderly Housing
Overlay. There is language in the ordinance that specifies that each application pursuant to
elderly housing shall be for a site of not less than one and not more than 10 acres. The
acreage for the proposal is less than 10. If the rezone is approved, there would be more
than 10 acres of contiguous Elderly Housing Overlay. This application, however, is specific
to the parcel of less than 10 acres. It was noted that the limitation may impact future site
plan applications.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that the applicant is showing a distinct boundary between existing,
new, and certain types of project areas. The application met was determined to meet the
10-acre threshold requirement in the ordinance and is 8.32 acres in size. As far as
addressing the density limitations in the ordinance, the Elderly Housing has a maximum
density of 20 units per acre for Independent Senior Housing. The maximum density for a
Nursing Home Convalescent Center or Assisted Living Facility is as approved under the site
plan because a Convalescent Center operates in terms of rooms or beds and so discretion
was placed with the site plan.

Mr. Lethbridge presented a Concept Plan submitted by the applicant and explained that
when a rezone is reviewed and approved, there is no particular site plan or layout being
approved. The Concept Plan helps the Commission and the Council understand the
applicant’s intent in looking at how density can be distributed on the property. He made it
clear that if the zoning in the Concept Plan is approved, the zoning runs with the land and
will remain consistent for another future owner.

Mr. Lethbridge identified on a map the portion for the Independent Housing and explained
that the 20-unit per acre maximum density is not a guaranteed density or an open-ended
allowance for the site. The Planning Commission and City Council have the authority to put
