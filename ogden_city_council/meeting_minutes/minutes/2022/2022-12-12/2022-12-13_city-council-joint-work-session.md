Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Mid-Year Fee Adjustments

Golf Division Manager Brenkman summarized proposed amendments to golf fees; the most previous proposed fee
increases were effective on January 1, 2019, for green fees, season passes and certain punch tickets and 2021, with a $1.00
increase in motorized golf cart rental per nine holes. Historically, green fees generally increase in the marketplace on an every-
other-year basis. As a result of the increased amounts of rounds being played, Administration has found that a number of
customers are supportive of periodic fee adjustments, if the quality of the experience continues to improve at both courses. Sixty-
two (62) new golf carts will debut in 2023, and Administration will also move forward with continued irrigation projects and
course enhancements. The business of golf is very competitive, and the consumer has many choices in our local market.
Consequently, Ogden courses must continue to provide a superior product, which comes at a cost. As appropriate, at times, it is
necessary to pass a cost increase on to the consumer. He presented a chart illustrating the proposed rate adjustments of $1 per nine
holes and $2 per 18 holes, between $13 and $20 for various types of punch passes, and between $50 and $200 for season passes,
with a $600 fee increase for the annual high school team fee. There will also be minor adjustments to discount rates, cart fees, and
driving range fees. The proposed golf fee amendments are estimated to increase golf revenue by approximately $235K annually.
Administration will propose a budget increase of $115K for the period of January to June of FY23 in the golf fund to recognize
the additional golf revenue anticipated.

Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brenkman centered on any feedback the Golf Division has heard from customers

Deputy Comptroller Sorensen summarized the recommendation to charge a collection fee; in May of 2021, the Fiscal
Operations team created an internal collections group to serve as a repository for the city’s outstanding debts that could be
managed and worked by a centralized team. The collections group currently manages debts for general billing, utility billing, code
enforcement, parking, and risk. The group is also actively managing the parking citation software in conjunction with the Police
Department. The collections group is intended to be self-funding but does not retain any of the revenues they collect. The
proposed collection fee is estimated to increase revenue in the amount of $125,000 annually. Administration will propose a budget
increase of $60,000 for the period of January to June of FY23 in the general fund for collection fees if Council adopts the fee
amendment.

Council member White stated she previously owned a collection agency, and she is aware ofa legal requirement to notify
individuals that a collection fee may be charged for any collection services. She asked if that language will be included on City
statements for costs that may be subject to collections. Mr. Sorensen answered yes. There was brief discussion among the Council
and staff regarding the time frame for payment of certain fees and civil penalties before the past due fee would be sent to the
City’s collection group.

City Recorder Hansen then explained her office charges fees for Government Records Access and Management Act
(GRAMA) requests, notary services, and cemetery document recording. These fees have not been amended in over 20 years.
Specific to records request, over the last several years there has been an increase in requests for specific reports generated by
software programs used in the City. These reports are not generated for the City’s use but are available in the software program.
Environmental site assessment requests have also increased and tend to involve multiple departments. These requests come from
companies who provide the service at a cost to their customer; however, they obtain most of their records from government. These
requests impact multiple departments such as Fire Department, Code Enforcement, Permitting, Inspections, Engineering,
Planning, and the Recorder’s Office all in one request. She concluded by discussing notarial acts; the Utah Notaries Public Reform
Act (46-1-12 Fees) was amended in 2019 increasing the fee for notarial acts to $10.00 per signature. It is proposed Ogden City
update this fee to bring it into compliance with State law; no fee is charged to notarize City business. The proposed Recorder fee
amendments are estimated to have a minimal effect and no amendments to the budget are needed. She concluded by summarizing
the proposed removal of fees for cemetery recording, faxing, obtaining a copy of the Municipal Code, making tapes or compact
discs responsive toa GRAMA request, and preparation of duplicate lien releases.

Brief discussion centered on the amounts charged for work associated with responding toa GRAMA request; Council
member Blair stated the rates seem low and will not actually cover staff time. Ms. Hansen stated that is correct; the intent is not
for the fees to be revenue generating, but to cover some of the costs associated with submitting a GRAMA request. Chair Nadolski
added that these types of fees can help to deter individuals from submitting frivolous requests. This led to discussion of the
number of requests processed by the Recorder’s Office, the types of records that are most commonly requested, and the ability to
differentiate between a private citizen request and a business request.

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 First Quarter Capital Improvement Plan Report

Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023; the report provides the status and pertinent financial information on all funded CIP projects for the
first quarter of FY2023. The projects are separated into General Fund and Enterprise Fund projects with the General Fund projects
further divided into projects by City department. Projects funded with bond proceeds are highlighted in the Enterprise fund.

Pamona (B Avenue)

be added to A Avenue from 24" Street south to its terminus south of Lake Street. The petitioner also requests that the honorary
street name “Pomona” be added to B Avenue from 24" Street south to its terminus south of Lake Street. He provided historical
information about Miles Goodyear; he was an early trapper and fur trader in the Utah area. He established Fort Buenaventura near
the confluence of the Ogden River and Weber Rivers. He is considered by many to be the original European descent settler of
Utah. He lived at the fort along with his Ute wife Pomona until the fort was purchased by James Brown at the request of Brigham

Young. The petitioner is requesting the honorary name for B Avenue to recognize Miles Goodyear’s wife. Consistent with
previous honorary street name processes, the city would produce and install the honorary street signage, but the petitioner would
be responsible for the financial costs of both. Council staff confirmed this responsibility with the petitioner on Thursday,

Mr. Brierley presented optional signage for the honorary street names and indicated there is some concern about the
intersection of 24" Street and Martin Luther King, Jr. Street because there would be four different street signs in one location,

specifically his implied involvement with slavery, the Diversity Commission claimed that supporting a request to name a street
after him is contrary to the Commission’s strategic plan, which is to “promote equity, cultivate positive cultural awareness,
promote cultural traditions, and enhance the well-being and quality-of-life of those living in or visiting Ogden, Utah.” Despite
their strong opposition to Miles Goodyear Avenue, the Commission did not directly address or provide an opinion on the proposed

Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on the need to update the street naming policy for the City,
though there is a lack of specific recommendations from the Diversity Commission, Planning Commission, or City Administration
for updates to the street naming policy. There was then discussion regarding the Diversity Commission’s position and the fact that
the Planning and Diversity Commission’s recommendations are opposing one another. Deputy Council Director Symes noted that
the Planning Commission considers applications from the perspective of compliance with the General Plan and land use
ordinances while the Diversity Commission considers applications from a cultural perspective and that is why he feels that their
recommendations oppose one another. Council member Hyer indicated this is the reason it is important to update the City’s street
naming policy. Council member Choberka agreed; the current policy does not require a recommendation from the Diversity
Commission, but staff solicited one because they understood that is likely something the Council would ultimately request.
Discussion continued among the Council and staff and the Council concluded to move the application forward to a vote of the
body with the understanding that each Council member reserves the right to vote based upon their own conscience. They indicated
that when policy changes are considered in the future, they would lean towards including language that will discourage naming
streets after individuals because doing so has been divisive in recent history of the City.

Citizen (no name given) inquired as to the plans for snow removal in the project area in the winter time. He then noted
that Mr. Cooper’s presentation indicated that previous high-rise projects fronting major streets were part of Ogden’s history and he
noted those projects were successful because the City had a world class, awesome public transportation system, but that is no
longer the case. He asked if the City has a plan to address the need for public transportation. He noted that Mr. Cooper’s
presentation included information about the market rates for housing, but he asked if the City has researched market rates for retail
units. One thing that makes Ogden what it is are the large number of locally owned shops, but those businesses will not be able to
afford market rate retail leases and he fears the City will get nothing but large chain businesses and restaurants, which is not what
Ogden desires.

Mr. Cooper stated that the City is working with Utah Transit Authority (UTA) on a major public transit line that will run
from downtown Ogden to Weber State University (WSU); the concept of building structure parking and creating environments
where people park once and move around a large space is at the heart of being efficient and sustainable. Connecting the project to
25" Street and providing adequate parking for all people coming to the site will benefit WonderBlock and 25" Street. As the City
has looked at how to manage parking more efficiently, one of the benefits of moving to a paid management system is that it will
disincentivize people to drive cars and, instead, choose mass transit. The project feeds into the concept of creating a walkable and
sustainable community. He stated that the City has heard a great deal about the need to preserve the unique nature of 25" Street;
unfortunately, the entire City of Ogden cannot be the same as 25" Street and other uses are needed and desired. The City has
sought to honor 25" Street and what it currently offers while creating a different type of space for other retailers so that visitors to
the downtown can experience both because they are not mutually exclusive. It is reasonable to believe that new businesses should
complement, rather than harm existing businesses and they can be compatible with one another.

Glen Gersburger, JF Fisher Companies, addressed snow removal; he stated that he oversees facilities management for JF
Fisher and the design of this project includes a ‘snow-melt system’ so that snow doesn’t need to be pushed to other locations and
will evaporate instead. He also addressed retail and stated that JF Fisher has hired consultants from California who work on
lifestyle centers and can help to curate the prefect match of retailers for this particular area and use. There will be summertime
space available to outdoor retailers and market rents will not be charged for that specialized use area and for special events.

Chair Nadolski asked for more information on the snow melt system; rather than evaporate, the snow will melt, and he
asked where it will run to. Mr. Gersberger stated that in all of the spaces considered part of the public realm will have hydronic
systems that will contain pipes that will provide heat under the surface to melt the snow and allow it to drain into the storm water
system. Mr. Cooper stated there are two other locations in downtown Ogden that have this type of system: one is around the
Megaplex and the other is around the Ensign South building and they have been very beneficial in terms of time saving and snow
removal.

Hank Costen asked if there will be opportunities for ownership of units in the project and, if not, is that decision based
upon market research.

Mr. Cooper stated that all 354 residential units in the project will be for rent and not for sale; there is a unique market for
condominium ownership and the JF Capital team has some specific information about lending for condominium projects and their
financability. Mr. Loomis stated that there are not many lenders who will embrace this type of project and if it included a
condominium aspect, it would be nearly impossible to secure financing. A condominium component would create too much risk
for the developer and lender and would also create an environment inviting lawsuits in the future. There will be an association that
will manage the entire project and it would be problematic to also have a homeowners association competing with the association
for the main project.

Vice Chair Lopez asked if there are other projects like this one that do include a condominium component. Mr. Loomis
answered there are very few condominiums in downtown Salt Lake; there are some buildings that are dedicated for sale, but it is
hard to have condominiums in a mixed-use community. Vice Chair Lopez asked if it is hard or impossible. Mr. Loomis stated he
will not say it is impossible, but it would be very hard to find a lender that would agree to this type of a project with a
condominium aspect. Chad Besinger, JF Capital, echoed the comments about the difficulty in securing financing for a project with
a condominium aspect; there may be small condominium projects in downtown Salt Lake, but nothing as large as this with a
condominium component. Vice Chair Lopez asked JF Capital representatives to expound on the risk associated with
condominiums. Mr. Besinger stated that if upwards of 300 units were condominium-ized, there will be more owners to work with
and some who will undoubtedly be upset about something that is eventually litigated. Developers do not want to engage with that
many owners in one project.

Council member Richey asked if there is an option for units to transition to owner-occupied units in the future or if they
will always be rental units. Mr. Besinger stated that as long as JF Capital owns and manages the project, the units will always be
for rent rather than for sale.

Chair Nadolski referenced the mention of a space in the project that can be used for special events or vendor markets, and
he asked how that type of space will be paid for and managed. Mr. Besinger stated that JF Capital will charge all users in the
project area common area maintenance costs and the fees generated by that activity will be kept in a fund that can accommodate
that type of use. Chair Nadolski asked if that will impact current special events in downtown Ogden, such as the farmer’s market.

Mr. Besinger stated that he believes it will complement existing events on 25" Street. Mr. Cooper added that special events
planners in Ogden City have been involved in planning for this space and they are supportive of the plans. Chair Nadolski asked if
fees generated at the project will be sufficient to cover ongoing maintenance and service of the space. Mr. Besinger answered yes
and noted that JF Capital will pay those costs. Chair Nadolski asked if that includes paying for public safety employees needed to
ensure safety of events. Mr. Besinger stated that police officers will be offered a reduced rent rate to live at the project because
their presence and the presence of their vehicles help to ensure the safety of the whole project area. Mr. Cooper added that studies
were conducted regarding the success of similar projects in other cities, including the Dairy Block in downtown Denver; the use of
dead-end spaces was carefully examined, including the maintenance and safety of those spaces and he believes that JF Capital has
the capacity and expertise to manage it in a way that will compliment existing special events in Ogden’s downtown. Chair
Nadolski stated that his concern is calls for public safety response, code enforcement, fire marshal inspections for special events,
and other demands for City resources that are not being accounted for. He stated that the City has utilized tax increment financing
(TIF) for other projects, but the scale of this project is much greater, and he is wondering how to pay for City services associated
with the project in the future. Mr. Cooper stated that with new infrastructure, the demand for utility services is likely to be very
low in the short term. With a concentration of so many people living in a small space, there will likely be a demand for medial
services and police response and those services are funded by the City’s general fund and not TIF or any other project revenue. As
the direct and indirect benefit of the WonderBlock project expands to the City’s general fund overtime due to increased sales and
property tax revenue, the City will be able to hire more public safety employees to increase staffing for response to public safety
calls for service.

Vice Chair Lopez stated he knows there are projects in other communities where condominiums are available for sale and
he would like to explore that option further for this project as the lack of for-sale units may be a ‘deal killer’ for him. Home
ownership opportunities are very important to him. He then asked if anyone has addressed the fact that the vision for this project
was created prior to the COVID-19 pandemic and the fact that the demand for office space has reduced dramatically since that
time. He wondered if there is too much office space included in this project. Mr. Cooper stated he is very comfortable with the
amount of office space included in the project area; it is 100,000 square feet, which is not a lot when compared to other project
areas. He noted efforts are currently underway in Farmington to build infrastructure to support one million square feet of office
space near the Station Park project. He stated that he has worked with the Northern Utah Economic Development Alliance, and
they have performed far more extensive studies than Ogden City has, and they have found that, in general, there is a lack of office
space in northern Utah. The use of office space is changing, but there is still a need for office space. He reiterated he feels the
amount and type of office space included in the project is appropriate and will be utilized. Mr. Besinger added that COVID has
certainly had an impact on the manner in which office space is used, but the market is very deep and spaces that were vacated
during the pandemic have been filled and there is still a demand for increased office space.

Mitch Vance, JF Companies, addressed Vice Chair Lopez’s question regarding the availability of for-sale units in the
project area. He noted that he was previously employed by a municipality and the Planning Commission for that city commonly
asked why apartments were being built instead of condominiums that were available for sale. He stated that the planning staff
researched the issue at length in that municipality and they found many complications associated with creating a for-sale product
at the onset of a project; in most cases, apartments were built and offered for rent for the first several years, but condominiumized
a couple of decades later. He stated it is a very complex process.

George Pappas stated that he owns various properties throughout the City; he asked if the developer will contribute $124
million to the project. Mr. Cooper stated that the portion JF Companies will provide to the project is debt and equity. Mr. Pappas
stated a building must be built before there is equity associated with it. He asked if the City has acquired a certified financial
statement for the developer; if the City is going to be going into business with a developer, it is necessary to understand their
financial position. He noted that he has survived three depressions and he cautioned the City to perform its due diligence to ensure
that all information is known about this development partner. Mr. Cooper stated the City has extensively examined the financial
position of JF Companies and has also spoken with their past partners and people they have worked with; he feels very confident
about their ability to perform. He stated that the City has not acquired certified financial documents or obtained a certified
auditor’s opinion regarding their company. Chair Nadolski asked if the City will request audited financial statements for JF
Capital. Mr. Cooper stated that if the Council requests such, Administration can request audited financial statements.

Stephanie Vaughan asked if there has been any consideration of placing greenhouses on top of the buildings in the
project area to grow food for residents, the grocery store, and local businesses to utilize. She asked if there may be a nature space
or community garden on top of the parking structure. Chair Nadolski stated that the Council has received similar questions about
placing solar panels on top of the structures to make them more energy efficient. Mr. Cooper stated that the parking structure, and
possibly some of the other buildings in the project, will include solar panels to provide energy for as much of the project as
possible. Green roofs are not part of the scope of the project at this point because it will be necessary to locate cooling towers and
other infrastructure in those areas, but there will be an open public realm that will be a combination of hard and soft scape that will
be complimentary to the uses on the site.

Angel Castillo stated she wanted to be clear that she is not opposed to this project, but she is concerned about the fiscal
sustainability of the City’s infrastructure and the manner in which TIF is being used. She stated she knows that the City has
performed studies about the viability of the project, but those studies may be based upon outdated information; if the data is more
than 120 days old, it is no longer considered current given the looming recession and predictions of increased unemployment

rates. She stated there are many experts that could advise on this project, including the use of TIF subsidy. Money to pay off the
initial TIF subsidy will be generated by property taxes that would otherwise never have been collected and it is essential to ensure
that the TIF is truly necessary in order for the project to go forward. She stated the project will put a strain on the City as
mentioned by Chair Nadolski; increased population in a small space will result in an increased demand for public safety support
and the City needs to carefully consider those demands. She reiterated that updated data should be gathered before making
decisions on the TIF subsidy. Mr. Cooper stated that a thorough analysis of the use of TIF funding has been performed; the project
needs $64 million of additional investment beyond what the development and financing community is able to provide, and this is
based upon market yield and net operating income projections. The number of market studies that have been performed are being
refreshed with current data as needed and those that are not being refreshed are based upon enduring economic principles in terms
of value created and using TIF that can be recaptured utilizing future policy decisions. It is inaccurate to suppose that the
recommendation to allocate TIF funding to the project is not based upon an extensive amount of data and analysis on both the part
of the City and the development team. He is happy to share all of that data with the Council at any time. Vice Chair Lopez stated
that Ms. Castillo’s question is whether the studies are based upon old data. Mr. Cooper stated that his answer is no; the data that
needs to be refreshed is constantly being refreshed; has the development analysis and bond underwriting proceeds, all of the data
will be thoroughly investigated by the financing team because their level of risk is as high or higher than the City’s.

Mr. Pate reapproached and asked what the City is in danger of losing and how the project will benefit the City. Mr.
Cooper stated that it is difficult to answer that question; his position is that ‘a rising tide raises all boats’ and he feels this project
will benefit all of downtown Ogden, including businesses that may currently be struggling. The market is a competitive
environment and sometimes businesses are forced to close ifthey cannot adapt to those changes. This proposal is based upon solid
economic principles that can be relied upon as tried and true through the evidenced growth of the City. He will continue to work
in good faith with good partners to try to what is best for the entire community. The City’s support of this project will not
eliminate other business support programs in the community or cause 25" Street to be neglected or ignored.

Heath Satow noted that the City has given more time to the development company to speak than has ever been given to a
citizen in a single public meeting. He added he does not know what to make of Mr. Cooper sitting in the Mayor’s seat tonight. He
is not upset with the developer as they are only doing what developers do, which is to find projects that are optimally profitable
for them. He has no doubt that they are very happy with this proposed arrangement. He referenced Maslow’s hierarchy of needs
and noted that the Council is only being fed the information that they absolutely need. He referenced drought conditions in the
State of Utah in recent years and questioned the inclusion of a water wall in the project that will result in the waste and
evaporation of water. He stated those that actually live in this community are very concerned about the misuse of valuable
resourced, but he acknowledged that the developer does not live in Ogden. There are housing concerns, but the units in this
project will not be offered below market rate; the City is being told that it is necessary to build more expensive units to help
people with lower incomes and that is the most Orwellian direction he has ever heard. He noted that TIF is not free money. Chair
Nadolski stated that he asked Mr. Cooper to sit in the Mayor’s seat because he knew that Mr. Cooper would be responding to the
majority of the questions asked tonight.

Amelia Jones stated she owns a business on 25" Street and she is aware how busy the alley space behind her business is
at the present time. She noted she has a lot of concerns about the hotel and the associated parking for it not being located close
enough to it. She asked if the site layout is final or if it can be adjusted as the project moves forward and determinations are made
that changes are needed. She then referenced the discussion about the housing units being for rent rather than for sale; she has
visited with JF Capital and was under the impression that they have not done extensive property management and she would like
to hear their experience in managing a rental project and their commitment to manage this project for a long time in the future. It
would be very concerning to her if they are planning to sell the project after 10 years because there are no assurances of who
would buy the project and how they would manage it. She does not believe the City should be in this project to make money;
instead, the City should be interested in making the best decisions for the entire community. She noted that JF Capital has
indicated they have done some recruitment in terms of businesses that will be located in the project area, and she asked if there has
been any consideration of finding businesses that will not be competing with existing businesses nearby. She is also curious about
how JF Capital knows whether Ogden can handle the density proposed for this project and if there is any possibility that they
would consider mixed-level housing. Mr. Cooper stated the City has received quite a few comments about the design and site
layout; in terms of the hotel, it was important for it to be nearest to 25" Street to give a view of that area, but that design may be
adjusted due to some concerns expressed. In terms of green space, there may be opportunities to increase the amount of green
space to be used by residents on the site. He appreciates the concerns about the operation of the hotel and associated parking, but
the proposal is based upon typical operations of these types of uses in a dense mixed-use environment; no one on the development
team feels that the distance between the hotel and parking area will be prohibitive in terms of the hotel user. He stated that people
are willing to walk and pull a suitcase from a parking area that is a reasonable distance from the hotel. Valet parking is an option
as well. He noted he agrees with Ms. Jones on the importance of making decisions that are best for the community rather than
rooted in revenue generation. He stated that the principles being applied to this project are worthy of pursuing in and of
themselves, but the potential to recoup any investment is an added bonus that the developer is supportive of. In the event that the
project is sold and the City does not recoup any of its money, it will still be the benefactor of a wonderful project that will improve
the community. He then addressed the question regarding recruitment of businesses that may compete with existing Ogden
businesses; there is a relatively small amount of retail square footage at WonderBlock, and he believes that it will be
complimentary to 25" Street. Retailers will perform their own due diligence and will likely avoid locating in a space where they
will face competition in their trade. There will likely be some competition, but the City is committed to creating an environment

that is conducive to existing businesses and augments a visitor’s experience to downtown Ogden. He concluded he is not
concerned about the capacity of downtown Ogden to absorb the density included in the project area.

Chair Nadolski invited a representative of JF Capital to address their rental property management experience. Mr.
Besinger stated that JF Capital focuses primarily on asset and property management; they have built over 4,500 apartment units
that they self-manage and they employ a team of 75 people specializing in property management, including office, industrial,
retail, and multi-family products. Their resume is fairly deep in terms of property management and they look forward to managing
this project as well.

Chair Nadolski then announced he feels it is appropriate to conclude the meeting at this point given the late hour; next
week, the discussion will continue with a focus on the paid parking system proposal.

The meeting adjourned at 11:04 p.m.
