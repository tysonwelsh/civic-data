Proposal 
The applicant is requesting to upgrade approximately 16 miles of the power distribution system 
throughout Summit Park and Timberline with additional upgrades extending into parts of 
Pinebrook and the western side of Jeremy Ranch. The upgrades will include completely 
removing 65 distribution poles and replacing 117 poles with new, taller poles to accommodate 
new overhead wire. The three-phase power within the area will be buried underground. The 
upgrades are part of Rocky Mountain Power’s wildfire mitigation plans and will bring the 
system into compliance with national electric and avian safety standards.

2 
 
Area Map 
 
 
Background 
Rocky Mountain Power is the primary electricity provider in the Snyderville Basin. Rocky 
Mountain Power has initiated a multi-year effort to upgrade existing infrastructure to reduce 
wildfire risks. Many of these upgrades have already occurred or have been approved. The 
current proposal includes rebuilding approximately 16 miles of existing electrical distribution 
assets on the Summit Park 11 circuit.  
 
The existing three-phase power will be placed underground while the single-phase overhead 
wire will be replaced with a covered (insulated) conductor or tree wire. Several poles will be 
replaced to support the heavier conductor wire.  
 
The Snyderville Basin Development Code (Code) classifies these distribution poles as Utility 
Facilities, Aboveground, which is defined as: “utility towers and transmission and distribution 
towers greater than forty five feet (45’) in height”. The project area will include the Summit 
Park, Timberline, Discovery Ridge, Pinebrook, The Woods of Parley’s Lane, Hidden Cove, and 
South Ridge subdivisions. Nearly the entire project area is located within the Hillside 
Stewardship (HS) Zone while one parcel outside of a subdivision is within the Mountain Remote 
(MR) zone. The Code identifies Utility Facilities, Aboveground as conditional uses within both 
6
3 
 
zones. The underground infrastructure is defined as Utility Facilities, Underground and is a low 
impact use in all zones. 
 
Within the project area there are 186 existing power poles affected by this proposal. The most 
common pole height is 40 ft, but heights range between 35 and 55 ft. The proposal includes 
removing 65 distribution poles completely. 121 poles will be replaced. Four of those will not 
change in height. The remaining 117 poles will be replaced with a taller pole. Approximately ten 
will be increased by 10 ft and approximately 109 will be increased by 5 ft. This proposal would 
raise the most common pole height to 45ft while the maximum pole height will remain at 55 ft. 
Exhibit B details the proposed changes.  
 
The pole replacement process includes two methods of digging holes: equipment can dig the 
hole which will require vehicle access to that location; or the holes can be hand dug which will 
include foot traffic and wheelbarrow access. Either method will require a hold that is 18-24 
inches in diameter. Poles will be set via crane, line truck, or helicopter with foot traffic around 
the new structure location. The typical area of disturbance will be a 10ft-by-10ft area. The 
installation of underground facilities will likely include boring or open trench excavation.  
 
For both aboveground and belowground construction, all disturbed areas will be reclaimed to 
either match or exceed conditions upon arrival. Landscaping and grass will be replaced or 
reseeded as necessary.  
 
All aboveground and belowground work will occur within the Rocky Mountain Power easement 
areas. Specific easement information is available upon request.

The accompanying conditional use permit application is to rebuild 16 miles of existing, electrical 
distribution assets on the Summit Park 11 circuit to mitigate wildfire risks.  The distribution assets being 
rebuilt are on the north and south sides of Interstate-80 in the Summit Park area.  
 
The existing, three-phase power will be placed undergrounded while the single-phase, overhead wire 
will be replaced with covered (insulated) conductor or tree wire. Several poles will also need to be 
replaced to support the heavier covered conductor and to meet national electric safety codes and avian 
safe standards.  
 
Protecting the communities we serve while providing safe, reliable power is one of Rocky Mountain 
Power’s top priorities. This project will improve Rocky Mountain Power’s electrical system, improve 
safety, and reduce wildfire risk in the area. This is one of several projects Rocky Mountain Power is 
working on and will be implementing over the next several years within the fire high consequence area. 
Along with these efforts, we have increased inspections and vegetation management near our facilities 
to mitigate wildfire risk.  
 
We look forward to working with the county on this and future projects to better serve the communities 
in Summit County.
