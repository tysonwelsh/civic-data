       a.     Discussion of Future Options for Improvements to Riverton Culinary Well
              Systems.

Mayor Staggs introduced William Richardson from Pure Stream and Total Water Management
(“TWM”).

Mr. Richardson thanked the City for the opportunity to present at the meeting and stated that
TWM has a myriad of technologies. They analyze water problems in various environments and
assess what problems can be solved. He identified the following four challenges TWM
encountered as they assessed Riverton City’s current water situation:

        1.    The City’s desire to utilize their own water.

        2.    The water system needs to be redundant, scalable and robust to meet the
              expectation of the life usage of the system.

        3.    The cost of the water system.

        4.    The water system needs to be certifiable.

Mayor Staggs stated that he, Interim City Manager/City Attorney Ryan Carter, and the City’s
Water Team met with Mr. Richardson and TWM. The company is familiar with water technologies
and have been proven. This is a different company than the vendor that previously presented
before the Council.

Mr. Richardson reviewed the items that would be discussed during the presentation.

President and CEO of TWM, Neil Richardson, reviewed the technology behind the company’s
water systems and the history of the company. He explained that the company has two separate
portions, one being Pure Streams, which is the industrial type of water management and the other
being TWM where water logistics is more significant. He explained that water logistics are suited
for Riverton City because of the City’s distributed source. The Council viewed a video about the
company.

Neil Richardson recommended that Riverton City use the Reverse Osmosis (“RO”) system, in
conjunction with the company’s Version F treatment. The Analytics Specialist for TWM reported
that the RO system meets the requirements and needs of the City. He reviewed other possible
options and explained why they were not appropriate for Riverton City. A picture of an RO system
was displayed. A flow chart of Riverton City’s potential wells was displayed and explained. He
reviewed the City’s water characteristics and the process of the RO system. 98.8% of total water
pulled from the well is sent for culinary use. The remaining 1.2% is disposed of.

Mayor Staggs reported that in the year 2013, Corolla Water Engineers prepared a report
analyzing water treatment methods and RO was one of them. The estimate to implement a
concentrated plant to service the City’s wells was at least $50,000,000. The most significant issue
at that time was handling the concentrate. This concentrator from TWM is a solution that is key.
The size and mobility of the system placed on a well is unique to the system seven years ago.

The Specialist stated that the package TWM is offering is being evaluated on a four-well site
basis.

Council Member Stewart asked about the lifespan of the RO system. The Specialist responded
that with proper maintenance the system can last up to 20 years. Council Member Stewart
recognized the importance of preparing for the replacement of the RO system in 20 years. Neil
Richardson stated that the TWM team implements improvements during the lifespan on the
system to help prevent the need to replace it after 20 years.

William Richardson stated that TWM’s business model does not require any capital expenditures
from the City. They provide the design, build, maintenance, and operation of the equipment over
the course of the contract. TWM does not require City funding to implement the system. As TWM
went through the evaluation process, they were mindful of the maintenance that would be required
for this system. William Richardson stated that TWM was careful about the water process they
selected for Riverton City.

William Richardson stated that TMW has the ability to assess any water issue and has a unique
advantage as they assess. TMW has the capability to be forward-thinking and prepare for the
future of their water systems. TWM provides four acre-foot pricing through a service contract they
provide. Maintenance on the system will be provided, however, TMW is aware of Riverton City’s
Water Team and desires to have a cohesive relationship with them by providing training and
support. William Richardson thanked the Council for their time.

Mayor Staggs reported that TMW is proposing $600 per acre-foot with no capital expenditure or
additional expense from the City. This would be a 20-year agreement; however, the City currently
has a contract with the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District (“JVWCD”). The City does not
want to be in a situation where they are forced to pay for two contracts. Development Services
Director, Jason Lethbridge, is preparing to meet with the JVWCD to address the issue. The
Trustees of JVWCD are supportive of the potential of increasing the availability of water resources
throughout the valley.

Mayor Staggs approved moving forward with TWM. He commented that the reason he
approached TWM to present was so the Council could get familiar with this type of concept. If
Riverton City desires to move forward with the RO system, TWM can implement their process in
the next fiscal year and begin on the Green Well. The RO system will accomplish preparing City
water to a standard equal to or better than the JVWCD.

Council Member McCay stated that she has family members who have retired from and currently
work for the Space Dynamics Lab in Logan, Utah. She feels this could be a positive solution to
the population density that has been approved of in the area.

William Richardson stated that one of the factors TWM is anticipating is population growth over
the life of the contract and agreement.

Council Member Stewart asked if TWM is currently presenting to other cities in the area. William
Richardson stated they currently have municipal projects in California but not in this area.

Council Member Buroker asked if other wells besides the Green Well have been studied. Mayor
Staggs stated that another company completed the study, however, TWM received the study
analysis of all of Riverton City’s wells. She also asked if this process would assist in solving the
problems at the sewer plant with water softeners. William Richardson stated that it would.

Mayor Staggs reported that Water Pro is currently drilling test wells and has asked Riverton City
to participate in paying 20% of that cost. It will be advantageous for a company like TWM to
speak with the drilling company so that Riverton City can use the water immediately.

Council Member Stewart approved of moving forward with TWM since they assist in
accomplishing Riverton City’s water goals.

Council Member Wells asked if Riverton City considered implementing the system on another
well where the TDS is not as high. Mayor Staggs stated that this has been considered, however,
the Green Well is still twice the hardness of the JVWCD. Officials are not just considering the
hardness and TDS levels of the water, but the amounts of water that can be processed. An RO
system would ultimately need to be put on four wells to be completely self-sufficient from the
JVWCD. Mayor Staggs stated that the cost should be less intensive on a Green Well because of
the slipstream. TWM will be able to use more water coming naturally from the well, so significantly
less water will need to be treated at this well versus others.

William Richardson agreed with Mayor Staggs and stated that treatment will vary depending on
the water characteristics of each well. He praised Riverton City’s ability to be self-reliant and
stated that the City could be completely dependent on their own system.

Council Member Wells asked if the other wells have the same pressure as the Green Well. Mayor
Staggs stated that the evaluation of the pressure of the Green Well was ultimately from an

Public Works Director, Trace Robinson, reported that the City recently obtained a grant in the
amount of $220,000 to build a bridge over the Welby Jacob Canal on Midas Creek Trail. The City

rails and a concrete deck that is 90 feet long and 8 to 10 feet wide. The plan in hand meeting
was previously held and the final drawings were being completed with the expectation that RB
Engineering will go out to bid.

         c.        Ordinance No. 20-08 Amendments to Section 18.175, Wireless
                   Communications Towers, Of The Riverton City Code, Addressing
                   Architectural Standards For Wireless Communications Towers And
                   Approval Processes For Towers On School District Properties, Amendments
                   Proposed by Riverton City.

Mr. Lethbridge stated that the above item includes changes to Section 18.175, Wireless
Communication Towers. There are three main changes proposed to the ordinance. First, the
amendment includes language that specifies that telecommunication towers on School District
property do not require review and approval by the City. With few exceptions, development on
School District property is not subject to local zoning requirements or standards under State law.
Staff will continue to work with the School District where possible to address potential issues, but
no formal review or approval is required.
Page | 10 Minutes – City Council Meeting
March 17, 2020



Second, the amendment establishes criteria for determining the need for architectural screening
of wireless communication towers. Such screening, as defined in the amendment, may include
the use of water tanks, clock towers, artificial trees or other vegetation, and other methods as
determined by the Commission. The amendment states that the Commission may require
architectural screening where one or more of the following circumstances exist:

         1.        The tower is in proximity to residential dwellings and architectural screening is
                   necessary to mitigate significant visual impacts.

         2.        The tower is in an area of historic or cultural significance.


         3.        The tower is located in a park or other public area where screening is necessary
                   to mitigate visual impacts.

         4.        The tower sits within an area determined by the Commission to be an entryway to
                   the City.

         5.        Other circumstances as determined by the Planning Commission

Finally, the amendment clarifies language relating to approval authority for Conditional Use
Permits for wireless communication towers. With this change, the Planning Commission and City
Council can independently require a Conditional Use Permit.

The Planning Commission, in their review of this ordinance amendment, recommended approval
of the changes as described.

Council Member Stewart asked if wireless towers located on private property near entrances to
the City need to have architectural screening. Mr. Lethbridge stated that they do.

our historical zoning maps from at least 2002. The areas designated as “Public Utility” correspond
with the Old City Hall Building and the Old Public Works shops and yard. The Old City Hall
Building and the Old Public Works Building were removed in 2013-2014 as part of the Main City
