WORK SESSION

City Property Encroachment Policy Update:
Ryan Blair, Property and Environmental Services Manager, and Emily Flint, Law Clerk,
presented on the City Property Encroachment Policy. Flint researched 10-15 cities in
the west and how they handled encroachments. Blair stated that while working on the
Treasure Hill easements, the team discovered that there was a lack of policy, internal
and external, for City property-related encroachments. Blair sought direction from
Council regarding approach: proactive versus complaint-based. A proactive approach
required more cost upfront when considering surveys, manpower, extensive policy
development, and the creation of an inventory of encroachments. Currently, staff
reviewed encroachments based on complaints or applicant-driven requests, which could
allow for some encroachments to go unaddressed or unenforced. However, with this
model, resources were dedicated to addressing the current problem areas and it

required an overall lower administrative cost. Blair furthered that the high-level budget
for a proactive approach would include one full-time employee (FTE) at $125,000 and a
$100,000 operating budget. Blair mentioned that program operating costs would be
higher for the first few years but could decrease as areas came into compliance. Blair
stated that the staff recommendation was to not pursue lease or license fees, but they
would be open to discussion if Council thought that it should be included in a policy.

Flint highlighted staff delegation within the model: Option 1, Low: nearly all
encroachments must be approved by Council. There could be a very specific list of
exceptions (safety, access, temporary); Option 2, Medium: Council and staff would work
together to create a tiered framework based on several factors (purpose, place,
permanence); Option 3, High: staff would approve almost all encroachments. There
could be a very specific list of instances where Council approval would be necessary
(specific areas or above a certain size). Blair stated that a tiered oversight approach
would be created for the encroachment type.

Council Member Toly asked staff to help Council understand the scale of the
encroachment problem. Blair stated that staff had an idea of the scale and that most
encroachments are located in areas like the golf course, where large sections of open
land are involved. Council Member Ciraco asked if staff anticipated having more or less
encroachments in Park City 20 years from now if no action was taken at this time. Blair
stated that those numbers would likely stay relatively flat. Council Member Zegarra
asked if there was an entity that told the City that encroachments need to be enforced.
Blair confirmed there were no other outside parties telling the City that they needed to
enforce encroachments. Council Member Miller asked if staff would recommend fees on
future encroachments. Blair stated that there would hopefully be a process set up where
staff could charge an application fee for such instances.

Council Member Toly asked if there was a way to have high-level, proactive
encroachment enforcement, such as on the golf course, which got high use by the
public. Blair stated that staff could return with a list of high impact areas. Council
Members Toly and Parigian asked if there was another alternative to adding an FTE.
Blair stated there might be another way, but staff would need to consider what that
might look like. Council Member Ciraco shared his concerns about growing full-time
staff and stated that other departments offset those costs with fee structures. Council
Member Parigian asked what level of expertise was needed to create a rudimentary list
of encroachments. Blair stated that staff already had a list. Council Member Ciraco
proposed an initial grace period, or an application of $1,000, but if a resident came in
proactively, the cost would be $200, to incentivize compliance while alleviating the
burden to the City.

Mayor Dickey asked if doing nothing now would hinder the City’s ability to take action in
the future when encroachments were found. Flint stated that it would not matter,
because the encroachment was still on City property. Council Member Zegarra asked if
the encroachments were a safety issue. Flint stated that they could be, but typically the

safety issues were in the right-of-way (ROW). Council Member Parigian asked if
adverse possession mattered regarding encroachment. Luke Henry, Assistant City
Attorney, stated that generally, you cannot claim adverse possession against
governmental entities or property. Council Member Parigian asked if golf course

Council Member Miller supported a hybrid approach to a resolution that would involve
data building around high-profile areas to determine next steps, but would remain
complaint-based in other areas. Council Members Zegarra and Ciraco supported a
complaint-based approach. Council Member Toly requested a hybrid approach, but only
if the cost was below $125,000. If the cost was above that, she favored a complaint-
based approach. Mayor Dickey stated that in a tight budget year, hiring an FTE to
resolve a problem that was not an issue did not seem like the right appropriation of
funds. Council Member Parigian indicated he was leaning toward a proactive approach
and did not want to hire an FTE unless it could be paid for with fees and licenses.

Mayor Dickey summarized that the majority of Council favored a hybrid approach, with
the caveat that a budget number lower than an FTE would return to Council for
approval. Blair clarified that Council direction was to start a database of the high-profile
areas, and not to return with a policy on them. Mayor Dickey stated that Council would
like to take action on some subset of encroachments if necessary. Blair sought
clarification on the database usage after it was compiled and how it would relate to the
creation of a policy. Council Member Toly asked City Attorney Margaret Plane if there
was a state code regarding encroachment fees. Plane stated that there was not a state
code that established the rate a governmental entity could charge for its property. Plane
furthered that an analysis on a high-profile area, such as the Municipal Golf Course,
could help direct policy. Council concurred.

1. Consideration to Authorize the Acting City Manager to Sign a Memorandum of
Understanding with 1251 Kearns, LLC and Emporium Properties, LLC to Realign
Munchkin Road and Create a Larger City-Owned Lot at 1825 Woodbine:
Chris Eggleton, Economic Development Director, stated that design work began in
2018, progressed over the years, and was now at a critical planning stage, with target
construction to begin in the fall of 2027. He recapped that Council had already made
significant investments in the Bonanza Area, including: approx. $5M for construction
and redesign of Homestake Road; approx. $7M contract to bury power lines in Bonanza
Park, and the partnership with J. Fisher Development on the construction of
EngineHouse. Eggleton played a short video to outline the different possibilities for
Munchkin Road relocation: Option 1, relocate through City-owned property, the Recycle
Utah center; Option 2, shift the alignment slightly north, through a portion of the events
center, or through the Boneyard property. The video illustrated that Option 2 would
require a partial demolition of the event center and the creation of new easements,
which would benefit the business owner and the City. Option 2 would mean the City
must reimburse the Boneyard landowner for some utility relocation costs, and it must

1. 2026 Legislative Session Update:
Michelle Downard, Strategic Initiatives Advisor, updated Council on bills being
discussed during the Legislative Session that could potentially impact Park City. She
stated that a change regarding political signs and where they could be placed might
require the City to establish a process for removal. Downard added that the bill
associated with primary residential property tax rates had not moved forward and was
still in committee. She stated that the childcare tax credit bill passed and was awaiting
the Governor’s signature. HB215, the bill regarding landscape restriction amendments,
would require a code amendment to restrict any municipality from prohibiting property
owners from removing vegetation from their property, which was located in the wildlife-
urban interface zone. HB381 passed and prohibited modifying and tampering with e-
bikes, and it requires individuals under 21 to wear helmets when operating. HB479,
election code modification, was still in the Senate Rules Committee. HB548, Primary
Election Amendments would allow municipalities to have the option of holding a primary
election if the number of candidates exceeded twice the number of open seats, but a
primary election would be required if there were three times the number of candidates
as seats available. This bill was still in committee. SB262, the bill that would prohibit law
