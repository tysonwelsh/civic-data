                                           REGULAR AGENDA
2.     APPROVE AND AUTHORIZE THE COMMISSION CHAIR TO EXECUTE AN AGREEMENT
       BETWEEN UTAH COUNTY AND AMERICA’S FREEDOM FESTIVAL, INC. (Continued from the
       March 13, 2018 Commission meeting)
(Track A, 04:00)
Commissioner Ivie summarized Utah County’s sponsorship of America’s Freedom Festival is possible through
tourism tax funds for promoting cultural and recreational activities. He had concerns with the first draft of the
agreement related to inclusion, but a non-discrimination clause has been added since the Board meeting last week.
Utah County resident Kevin Wilcox read a prepared statement from ‘Mormons Building Bridges’ in support of the
non-discrimination clause (a copy of which can be found attached to these minutes) and encouraged the Freedom
Festival to use this opportunity to show ‘everyone is welcome home’ in Provo.

Tisha Olsen explained she is a 22-year veteran who has served in seven different wars; she and her brothers and sisters
in the LGBTQ (lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer and questioning) community have “written a check to this
country up to and including our lives.” Ms. Olsen advocated the importance of diversity. Executive Director John
Gustav-Wrathall of Affirmation: LGBT Mormons, Families and Friends also read a prepared statement (a copy of
which can be found attached to these minutes) explaining the primary cause of depression amongst LGBT Mormons
is family and community rejection. It is important to come together in public spaces to celebrate common ground.

Roni Jo Draper is the daughter of an interracial couple whose marriage was not considered legal until 1967. She also
serves as the president of the local PFLAG (Parents and Friends of Lesbians and Gays) chapter. Ms. Draper described
the hardships her third son has endured as a queer individual and commented on how it may have helped him to see
others like himself embraced and included as participants in a parade. She suggested the Freedom Festival reconsider
their choices to place value on some families and not others, and challenged the commissioners to pledge her tax
dollars only to events that are inclusive of all.

(Track A, 18:25)
Lehi resident and business owner Debra Oaks Coe reiterated the suicide rates have increased threefold locally in the
last few years. Ms. Coe referenced published works by American academic psychologist Dr. Thomas E. Joiner and
summarized two main reasons that lead people to want to die: not feeling valued in society or feeling like a burden,
and feeling as though you do not belong. Ms. Coe read a letter from Taryn Hiatt, Utah County resident and the
Utah/Nevada Area Director for the American Foundation of Suicide Prevention, advocating for the inclusion of
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS

LGBTQ groups in the Freedom Festival. Attorney and Utah Eagle Forum representative Dani Palmer countered that
the breakdown of ‘traditional’ families, specifically children being raised in homes without fathers, has a greater effect
on youth being excluded by their communities. Ms. Palmer stated it is inappropriate to force a parade to provide a
platform of sexuality, and an LGBT group should be treated as any other group asking to participate – America’s
Freedom Festival should be allowed to control the messages sent at their event.

(Track A, 25:15)
Provo resident Brianna Cluck shared her experiences as a queer youth with no positive representation of people such
as herself. Ms. Cluck expressed there are a lot of things that can destroy a family but “being gay is a lot lower on the
list than parents rejecting their children for being gay.” She supports the addition of a non-discrimination clause in
the Freedom Festival contract, and noted the festival should reject taxpayer funds if they do not wish to comply. Susan
Krueger-Barber is Vice Chair of the Joaquin Neighborhood in Provo which consists of 15,000 people and has property
one block away from the parade route. She reminded the Board the motto of Provo City is ‘Welcome Home;’ this
includes everyone no matter their race, background, or sexual orientation. Cristobal Villegas grew up in Mapleton,
graduated from Springville High School and Utah Valley University, served as a missionary and now owns a home in
Springville – but lived much of his life carrying a secret. Mr. Villegas asked the Board if they really want to support
an organization using taxpayer funds to continue excluding and chastising members of their own community. Provo
business owner Hunter Phillips explained he and his business partners made the conscious decision to stay in Utah
Valley for the unique beauty of the area and the people. While he appreciates the non-discrimination clause, Mr.
Phillips asked the commissioners to set a higher bar and not only tolerate but actively celebrate the multiple facets and
diversity in our community.

Tosh Metzger is on the Board of Trustees for Provo Pride. He mentioned 31 businesses in downtown Provo requested
rainbow flags to display during their festival, an indication that the community is supportive, and the Freedom Festival
should embrace the positive changes since the 1950’s. Spanish Fork resident Kyle Chitton explained he and his wife
are trying to raise four children to be loving and accepting; he hopes the parade organizers will be more inclusive and
reflect the community. Freedom Festival trustee Steve Shallenberger talked about travelling the world and this
festival’s unique purpose of celebrating America. He noted the many volunteers coming together for this event and
asked those present to trust this process to work itself out. Singer-songwriter and recording artist Mindy Gledhill
touted the wonders of this unique community which was founded by Mormon pioneers who knew what it was to be
marginalized and rejected. She supports the addition of the non-discrimination clause to provide a safe and supportive
space for marginalized groups now, as has always been intended.

(Track A, 40:30)
Commissioner Lee expressed all present would agree the Freedom Festival is one of the jewels in the community and
the nation – none of those commenting today stated the event should be shut down. He spoke of the trials and errors
in growing and going forward. Commissioner Graves thanked Tisha Olsen for her service in the armed forces, and
expanded on the comments made by Hunter Phillips about ‘setting the bar higher’ for inclusion, love, and acceptance
for all. Commissioner Ivie noted it is not moral superiority that made America great, but rather the keen ability to
correct past errors.

5.     INITIAL 20 YEAR REVIEW OF THE DIXON AGRICULTURE PROTECTION AREA
       (APPROXIMATELY 7.72 ACRES) TO DETERMINE IF THE AGRICULTURE PROTECTION
       AREA SHOULD BE CONTINUED AND REAUTHORIZED FOR ANOTHER 20 YEARS; OR TO
       REQUEST THE PLANNING COMMISSION AND AGRICULTURE PROTECTION AREA
       ADVISORY BOARD TO REVIEW AND SUBMIT RECOMMENDATIONS FOR MODIFICATION
       OR TERMINATION
(Track A, 54:30)
Commissioner Ivie outlined the legislative and review process, explaining county ordinance requires the Dixon
Agriculture Protection Area be initiated by the Board of Commissioners and then submitted to the Agricultural
Protection Review Board and the Utah County Planning Commission because the Utah County Community
Development Office has received more than five complaints regarding the area. There will be opportunity for public
comments in favor or opposed to the renewal of the agricultural protection area through those boards and commissions
before a recommendation is returned to the Board of Commissioners. Commissioner Graves mentioned emails which
highlighted complaints which were not directed against agriculture but generally concerned about not taking care of
the property. Commissioner Lee reiterated the vote today is to initiate the review process. Utah County Deputy
Attorney Robert J. Moore echoed Commissioner Ivie’s comments about the process and Peggy Kelsey of the Utah
County Community Development Office mentioned the next Planning Commission meeting will be April 17, 2018 or
May 15, 2018. Ms. Kelsey also detailed the area and the terms of initial protection status approval twenty years ago.

(Track A, 1:09:20)
Jonathan Madsen lives near the Dixon Agriculture Protection Area and asked about notification for the Agriculture
Protection Area Advisory Board meeting. Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore explained the information
will be available on the state public meeting notice website once dates are set. Brian Perrin referenced an aerial map
of the area to note there are now 394 residences within one-quarter of a mile of the area. Bob Richards mentioned the
county requires these areas to adhere to ‘best agricultural practices’ but he was unable to find those definitions for a
mink farm. Mr. Moore replied statute does not define those terms and the Agriculture Protection Area Advisory Board
helps ascertain the standard. Ryan Holt talked about the rigorous certification programs for mink farmers and their
self-regulation. David Johnson asked the timeline of the review be made clear to the public. Katie Parry talked about
a new neighbor recently moved from California who was unable to find information about the mink farm; Ms. Parry
finds it dishonest to not publish and communicate that issue to potential property-buyers. Chris Bowerbank lives one
block south of the farm and has been working closely with the community to understand the agriculture protection
area process. He urged the Board to set aside emotion or personal bias to look at the facts when deciding whether or
not to renew the Dixon area.
