2.      A presentation regarding the FY2023 Budget- Mayor's Office (22-016) (0:34:13)

Wayne Parker, CAO, presented. Mr. Parker gave an overview of the Mayor’s Office budget. He
highlighted the elements of the general plan that the Mayor’s Office supports. He also outlined
key priorities for fiscal year 2023, including operationalizing the city hall and public safety
facility, the wastewater treatment plant, opening a west side grocery store, completing planning
for redevelopment at the mall, and completing the general plan and related planning efforts. He
outlined several un- or underfunded services: a federal lobbyist contract, a permanently funded
state-level lobbyist, a comprehensive citizen survey, and restoring their graduate student intern.
Notable expenditures in the Mayor’s budget include interlocal contributions, contract services,

Parker said they began a contract with a lobbyist when they were trying to secure funding for the
Provo Airport. Paul Rogers & Associates firm helped secure substantial funds for the Provo
Airport. Mr. Parker explained how the Mayor’s Office staff have assisted with various lobbying
efforts. Councilor Katrice MacKay asked if there were any details Mr. Parker could share about
the west side grocery store. There is not anything they can share at this time. Presentation only.

5.      A presentation on budget fund types. (22-016) (1:58:05)

Kelsey Zarbock, Budget Officer, presented. Funds are the foundation of government accounting
and budgeting—understanding the fund types will assist the Council in navigating the budget. A
fund is a distinct accounting entity with a self-balancing set of accounts. Funds may exist for
different reasons, such as certain restrictions and legislation governing the use of certain dollars.
There are governmental funds which relate to government activities and include the general,
special revenue, debt service, and capital project funds. Proprietary funds are the enterprise and
internal service funds. Ms. Zarbock outlined the examples of different city functions and which
funds house them. Enterprise funds relate to business-type activities happening outside the city
organization, such as utility services. In contrast, internal service funds are business-type
activities that take place internally within the city (including fleet, facilities, customer service).
Councilor David Shipley asked how customer service works; Ms. Zarbock noted that customer
service in the internal service fund related to utility billing functions.

Ms. Zarbock explained that the budget is organized by fund type. The budget book also contains
a functional unit matrix which cross-references funds to specific departments. Mr. Shipley asked
about the recreation center; John Borget, Administrative Services Director, explained that the rec
center revenues cover its direct operating expenses, but other elements of Parks and Recreation
are not self-sustaining such as parks maintenance. The rec center also receives property tax
toward debt service. The ice arena is completely self-sustaining and is its own enterprise fund.
Other sections of the budget include the proposed budget by fund and the principles of financial
management section. Presentation only.

7.      A discussion regarding the Council's budget priorities. (22-016) (2:24:49)

supported synchrony between these planning efforts. Councilor David Shipley commented that
he would support following Public Works’ typical process for reviewing and improving
