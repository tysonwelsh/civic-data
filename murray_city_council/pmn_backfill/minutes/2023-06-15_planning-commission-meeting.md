property in a flag lot. It is in the R-1-8 zone, which does allow for flag lots, subject to approval

setbacks required.

Project # 23-052 & # 23-054 and

Susan Nixon presented the application by Applegate LLC for subdivision approval of an
amended plat and conditional use to allow multi-family development of Fuji Townhomes. The
property is currently 23.7 acres. The proposal is to split out a 5.11-acre portion of property from
Planning Commission

the 23.7-acre parcel to develop forty-two (42) townhomes. It is the policy and practice of the
Murray City planning staff that every application that comes in for subdivision or conditional use
is reviewed on its own merit. Staff takes in the application, reviews the code, discusses the
issues with various Murray City departments, and consider the surrounding area, to come up
with the staff report. The property is currently zoned R-M-15, which allows for up to twelve units
per acre. This proposal would equal about eight units per acre, which is under the limit allowed.
The area is surrounded by single-family residential areas. Ms. Nixon showed images of the
Applegate Community indicating how the property is currently laid out. She then presented
photos from inside the community. This request is to split off the 5.11-acre parcel from the 23.7-
acre from Applegate HOA. They would realign what is now Applegate Drive and relocate it to the
north and west. It would then become Fuji Avenue and would remain a private drive. The
utilities would also be relocated. The applicants are asking to connect Fuji Avenue to the west
with a public road, Spring Clover Drive. When this project was built in the 1970’s, Murray City
didn’t have a requirement for so many access points to developments. Since then, Murray City
has adopted the code that mandates any multi-family projects with more than 100 units must
have at least two accesses. This is why the applicant is requesting to connect to Spring Clover
Drive. Both the Applegate and Fuji communities will have vehicular, pedestrian and utilities that
are cross accessed. Both communities would share the existing and newly proposed amenities.
A bit of history was provided by Ms. Nixon. In 2018 the applicant was required to change the
zoning from R-1-8 to R-M-15, which is more in line with what the community is. During that
process, there was an existing cell tower. These are not allowed in the R-M-15 Zone, but were
allowed in the R-1-8. The property became nonconforming after switching zones. For legal
nonconforming structures, you can expand or relocate on the same property. The applicant
applied for approval for expansion to move the cell tower to a different location closer to 5300
South and Murray Boulevard.
In 2020 Applegate Condominiums received preliminary subdivision, PUD and conditional use
approvals. The CUP approval was good for two years. This approval lapsed in June 2022. This
current application has been submitted to amend the condos and withdraw the 5.11 acres, as
well as the conditional use for the townhome rental units. Ms. Nixon presented an image of the
plat for the 5.11 acres, with the boundaries outlined, for comparison and orientation. Applegate
currently has 172 condo units, which are individually owned. The proposed 42 units would be
owned by one entity, and be rental units, making a total of 214, if approved. There is access
currently off Murray Parkway onto Applegate Drive and the proposal would be to reduce the
width of Applegate Drive from 75 feet to 43 feet. It will also be relocated to the north and west.
The 43 feet would consist of six-foot sidewalks on each side, two-and-a-half feet of curb-and-
gutter, as well as 26 feet of hard surface. This is the minimum required by the fire department.
The summarized proposal is for Applegate Drive to be relocated to what will be Fuji Drive and
then connect to Spring Clover Drive. They are also proposing to have an access gate that would
be controlled by a key fob or access code with a transponder to get in and out. The city had
concerns with the connection to Spring Clover and the impact that would have on the
neighborhood to the west. Upon staff review, based on the 2019 Traffic Study (revised in 2022),
staff recommends an additional gate at the connection of Spring Clover Drive and Fuji Drive. It
would be emergency access only, which will be controlled by Murray City, for fire, police, and
maintenance for sewer clean up. The Traffic Impact Study indicated that there would be five to
ten percent traffic going to the west, which is 60 – 100 cars per day, which doesn't sound like a
