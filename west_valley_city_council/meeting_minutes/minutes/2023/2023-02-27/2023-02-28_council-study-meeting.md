RESOLUTION 23-17: AUTHORIZE WEST VALLEY CITY TO ENTER INTO A
MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING WITH THE UNITED STATES
MARSHALS SERVICE FUGITIVE TASK FORCE
Colleen Jacobs, Police Chief, presented proposed Resolution 23-17 that would authorize
West Valley City to Enter into a Memorandum of Understanding with the United States
Marshals Service Fugitive Task Force

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       In an effort to coordinate local law enforcement resources in the apprehension of
       persons who have active state and federal warrants for their arrest, law enforcement
       agencies of the greater Salt Lake Valley, State and Federal agencies have united
       efforts and resources to form the United States Marshals Service Fugitive Task
       Force. Members of the police department are deputized through the Marshals
       Service and generally participate in a part-time role. The police department has
       worked in partnership with the US Marshals Service for many years and this
       partnership continuously aids in the apprehension of some of the most violent
       offenders in our community. This agreement clarifies the responsibilities of
       participating agencies to establish the groundwork for the administration and
       operation of the taskforce.

       The primary mission of the task force is to investigate and arrest, as part of joint
       law enforcement operations, persons who have active state and federal warrants for
       their arrest. The intent of the joint effort is to investigate and apprehend local, state
       and federal fugitives, thereby improving public safety and reducing violent crime.
       Targeted crimes will primarily include violent crimes against persons, weapons
       offenses, felony drug offenses, failure to register as a sex offender, and crimes
       committed by subjects who have a criminal history involving violent crimes, felony
       drug offenses, and/or weapons offense. Task force personnel will be assigned
       federal, state, and local fugitive cases for investigation.

Mayor Lang verified that the officer is assigned as needed. Chief Jacobs replied that it’s a
collateral initiative so the officer will work normal West Valley street crimes in conjunction
with assisting the task force on their efforts. She noted that many of the fugitives that are
   MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – FEBRUARY 28, 2023

investigated have a tie to West Valley City and neighboring jurisdictions. Councilmember
Whetstone asked if there is a cumulative list of these types of task forces the City
participates in and how participation interacts with regular officer duties. Chief Jacobs
replied yes and stated that there may be a minor strain on regular duties but most often the
collaborative effort helps reduce crime in West Valley City by regional fugitives behind
bars.

RESOLUTION 23-18: APPROVE THE INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT BETWEEN
WEST VALLEY CITY AND SALT LAKE COUNTY FOR SECURITY SERVICES
AT THE WEST VALLEY COUNTY LIBRARY
Colleen Jacobs, Police Chief, presented proposed Resolution 23-18 that would approve the
Interlocal Agreement Between West Valley City and Salt Lake County for Security
Services at the West Valley County Library

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Salt Lake County wishes to hire off-duty West Valley City Police Officers to
       provide security and law enforcement services on an as needed basis at the West
       Valley branch of the Salt Lake County library system. The county wishes to enter
       an interlocal agreement to govern the conditions under which they hire off-duty
       officers. The interlocal agreement is consistent with the Police Department’s
       secondary employment hiring procedures.

Mayor Lang asked if this position is voluntary overtime. Chief Jacobs replied yes and stated
that the County is charged the typical overtime rate that other agencies are charged minus
the administrative fee. Councilmember Christensen verified that this would only apply to
the West Valley branch and not the Hunter one. Chief Jacobs replied yes. Councilmember
Whetstone asked if the City is assisting in efforts already since the contract is dated January
1, 2023. Chief Jacobs replied that the County likes to do the contract on an annual calendar
year and has just recently asked for the service.

The City Council will consider Resolution 23-18 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
March 7, 2023 at 6:30 P.M

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as

       West Valley City and the Salt Lake City-Utah Committee for the Games (SLC-
       UTCG) have entered into an Olympic Venue Use Agreement that will allow the
       Committee exclusive use of the Maverik Center to host Olympic Men’s and

       or the Olympic & Paralympic Winter Games of 2034.

       Paralympic Committee (USOPC) to bring the Olympic Winter Games to Utah in
       2030 or 2034. The final bid and subsequent decision from the International
       Olympic Committee (IOC) are set for 2023 or later.

       It is anticipated that the IOC will invite SLC-UTCG into a Targeted Dialogue
       regarding hosting a future Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games sometime in
       2023. As part of that process, SLC-UTCG must obtain initial guarantees that
       confirm a range of information and obligations from public authorities and other
       organizations. In order to stay ahead of this process, SLC-UTCG hopes to complete
       the guarantees by March 31, 2023.

       West Valley City government demonstrated unwavering support that underpinned
       the success of the Salt Lake 2002 Olympic Winter and Paralympic Winter Games.
       SLC-UTCG now looks to build on that foundation.

A.   POTENTIAL FUTURE AGENDA ITEMS
     Wayne Pyle, City Manager, stated that an emergency construction item has arisen.
     He requested that Dan Johnson, Public Works Director, provide additional
     information. Dan stated that there is a storm drain issue on Toulouse Street. He
     indicated that there has been flooding in the area and after further investigation,
     Public Works has discovered that an old corrugated pipe installed in the 1970’s is
     constantly below water level which means the pipe is constantly full of water. Dan
     indicated that as the issue of replacement was looked into and additional problems
     were discovered including the realization that the pipe does not currently tie into
     any drainage system. Dan stated that it’s important to get the pipe capped and to
     enter into an emergency agreement with a company who can re-route the pipe to be
     installed into Bangerter as construction on the highway moves forward. He noted
     that a home in the area is experiencing significant flooding which is likely due to
     the corrugated pipe though he added that the pipe hasn’t collapsed but likely has
     holes. Mayor Lang and the Council agreed to review and potentially approve a
     resolution to enter into a contract during next week’s Regular City Council meeting.
