Public hearing and possible action regarding Silver Creek Village Development 
Agreement – Jennifer Strader, County Planner 
 
County Planner Jennifer Strader presented the staff report and explained that the Silver 
Creek Village Specially Planned Area (SPA) was approved in 2011.  The project consists 
of approximately 1,300 residential units and 50,000 square feet of commercial density on 
approximately 244 acres.  The SPA was valid for 24 months, but the developer requested, 
and the Summit County Council approved, two extensions to the SPA, giving the 
developers until March 31, 2015, for the development agreement to be adopted.  She 
explained that the development agreement will be the Code for this project, and it was 
submitted shortly after the SPA designation was approved.  It has been reviewed by 
various entities and a subcommittee of the Planning Commission.  She reviewed the 
items for discussion from the previous work session.

The first issue is on-street winter parking and concerns about the existing County 
ordinance that restricts on-street parking.  She read the language in the ordinance and 
explained that, after further review by the subcommittee and County Staff, it was found 
that two street segments can accommodate on-street parking, and they will be 
appropriately signed.  She confirmed that the County Engineer is comfortable with the 
on-street winter parking language in the development agreement. 
 
The second issue related to signage, and she explained that this application was submitted 
first in 1997 and is being processed under a previous Development Code.  That Code 
allowed for a project to have a comprehensive sign plan that was specific to the project.  
This project proposes two monument signs at the entrances to the subdivision and ghost 
ad signs on the buildings.  The developer has changed the monument sign request to 45 
square feet and 6 feet in height, and Staff is comfortable with that proposal.  With regard 
to the ghost ad signs, the request has been changed from 50% of the wall area to 40% of 
the wall area.  The current Code allows 1 square foot of sign area for each 2 linear feet of 
building façade frontage up to a maximum of 60 square feet, and a building that is more 
than 250 linear feet is allowed a 75-square-foot sign.  Taking that into consideration, 
Staff is comfortable with the developer’s request for the ghost ads to be 40% of the wall 
area.  All other signage will meet current Code standards. 
 
With regard to duration of the agreement, the applicant has changed the duration from 35 
years to 25 years with an option to extend to a time frame that would be sufficient to 
complete the project, which would have to be approved by the County Council.  The 
developer would have to request an extension before the development agreement expires.  
The developer will also submit an annual review, so Staff will have an indication of 
where they stand with the project as it goes forward. 
 
Planner Strader recalled that there was a question about wood burning devices, and the 
language has been clarified to state that all fireplaces shall be non-wood burning.  That 
note will also be included on all future plats.  There was a concern about not having 
specific due dates for the annual review, and the language has been amended to state that 
on the first business day of March during the term of the development agreement, the 
developer will provide a progress report.  It is anticipated that the developer will find 
more petrified wood during construction, and the agreement has been amended to state 
that the developer will assemble the petrified wood encountered during construction to be 
included in the petrified wood park.  That obligation will also be communicated to any 
construction workers.  The Planning Commission also wanted to be sure the affordable 
housing units are consistent with the finish and quality of the market units, and language 
has been included to assure that. 
 
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission hold a public hearing and, based on 
the information in the staff report and the public input received at this meeting, forward a 
positive recommendation to the Summit County Council for the development agreement 
based on the findings of fact and conclusions of law in the staff report.  Planner Strader 
noted that the development agreement must be adopted and approved prior to March 31.

Commissioner Harte asked about the timing of the trail system.  Wade Budge, 
representing the lead developer, explained that they donated over 80 acres in the 
development to the Snyderville Basin Special Recreation District and will pay for 
construction of a loop trail.  They will have 24 months from the time they record the 
development agreement to complete that trail.  Other trails will be built interior to the 
project as the project develops.  Commissioner Harte asked about access to the loop

tunnel under Highway 40 will be built with the loop trail, and the developer will clean up 
the underpass under Highway 40 as part of the trail construction.  He explained that no 
trailhead parking is planned at this point, but that is up to the Recreation District to 
determine.  Mr. Budge confirmed that the loop trail will exist at all times during 
development of the project.

Commissioner Barnes asked the developer whether they have done traffic studies for 
this development.  Mr. Langvardt explained that the traffic study was completed for the 
proposed density.  One recommendation in the traffic study was to install the roundabout, 
which is documented as part of the SPA approval.  He verified that all their commitments 
are in line with that traffic study.  Commissioner Barnes asked if lighting has been 
addressed.  Mr. Langvardt explained that, although this is a SPA where they can basically 
develop their own Code, they have worked with Staff and adhered to the Code 
requirements wherever possible.  They have specifically committed to follow whatever is 
in the current Code.  He added that lighting will continue to get better and more efficient, 
the Code will reflect that, and they will comply with the updated Code.  They do not want 
a development with 15-year-old technology.  Commissioner Barnes requested an 
explanation of how Mountain Regional is set up in order to address the concerns about 
water.  Deputy County Attorney Jami Brackin explained that all these concerns were 
addressed when the SPA was approved and were thoroughly vetted and approved by both 
the Planning Commission and the County Council.  The developer had to be sure there 
was enough wet water to address each unit.  The water companies have to test to be sure 
they have wet water to meet their obligations every year, and Mountain Regional has 
sufficient wet water to serve this area and has provided a willing-to-serve letter for this 
project.  Commissioner Barnes explained that the reference to re-use of water had to do 
with water that will go through the treatment plant that will be used for irrigation.  He 
understood that there would be no wells or drilling associated with this project, and he 
believed the main source would be from the Weber River.  Commissioner Barnes 
referred to the comment regarding school fees.  Ms. Brackin explained that the State 
Legislature prohibits the County from even considering or discussing school impacts.
