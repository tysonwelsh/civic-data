2.      A presentation regarding annual Council training. (23-002) (00:17:06)

Brian Jones, City Attorney, presented. Mr. Jones shared a video prepared by the State Auditor’s
Office regarding the Open and Public Meeting Act and its provisions. Mr. Jones then presented
the Government Records Access and Management Act. GRAMA governs how records are
classified, managed, and used. The law recognizes two constitutional rights: the public’s right of
access to information and the right of privacy. Mr. Jones reviewed how to analyze whether or not
something is a GRAMA request and how to respond in each instance, as well as best practices
for responding to GRAMA requests. Every record is classified in one of the following
categories: public, private, protected, controlled, or exempt. Every record that is not classified as
private, protected, controlled, or exempt, is automatically classified as public. Mr. Jones gave
examples of each of these types of records and what would qualify as each type under GRAMA.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked about the role of the City Recorder. Mr. Jones elaborated on
the City Recorder’s responsibilities. The OPMA training covered some of this; taking meeting
minutes is not part of the recorder’s job duties by statute, but is the responsibility of the Council.
The current arrangement was made between the branches of Provo’s government that the
responsibilities for minute-keeping are divided between the city recorder and Council executive
assistant. Councilor George Handley asked Mr. Jones to review information about email
exchanges between Councilors and what is appropriate for discussions via email versus a public
meeting. Mr. Jones responded to this question; the OPMA requires that if there is a quorum
gathered in a location in a meeting for the purpose of conducting business, then that meeting
must be open and available to the public. This does not prohibit the members of the public body
from having conversations or electronic communications outside of the meeting; however, any
formal action must take place in an open meeting. OPMA and GRAMA work in concert; for
example, discussions that happen via email are subject to GRAMA. Councilor Rachel Whipple
asked if email discussions were okay as long as the Council did not count votes or decisions. Mr.
Jones indicated that the Provo Council has often been very open about their deliberative process
in open meetings, so he has not had many concerns in the past.

Mr. Handley suggested the use of email may help make the Council’s meetings more efficient
and asked about the permissibility of this. Mr. Jones shared some insight about the difference
between the legal and political considerations of whether that would be appropriate. Mr. Jones
responded to this question with examples of scenarios that could be problematic or others that
would comply with the statute, noting the distinctions and nuances of what was legally allowed
versus the political considerations the Council may wish to take into effect. Mr. Handley asked if
there was a requirement about how much a Councilor has to say about why they were voting a
particular way. Mr. Jones explained that there was not a requirement about a Councilor
specifically, but the issue revolves around how much information there is in the public record.
Mr. Jones explained the legal standard and how this might be evaluated in a legal case. Councilor
Bill Fillmore asked if an issue has been discussed at length in a Work Meeting, whether that
satisfied the requirements; Mr. Jones indicated that it did, since the Work Meetings were open to
the public and part of the public record.

3.      A resolution of the Provo City Municipal Council outlining their support for
        adoption of the 2022 recommendations of Utah's Uniform Building Code
        Commission regarding building energy efficiency. (23-010) (00:55:40)

Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, presented. She shared background information on this item. The
Utah Uniform Building Code Commission makes recommendations to the state legislature’s
business and labor committees every six years. These code items include items related to energy
efficiency (on about 3 pages of the full 290 pages). The proposal received a favorable unanimous
recommendation from the Interim Business and Labor Committee.

Don Jarvis, Sustainability Committee Chair, presented. He shared details on the specific code
recommendations. Mr. Jarvis explained that building codes have an enormous effect on energy
use—Mr. Jarvis displayed a graphic that illustrated how energy use has decreased substantially
as building codes have been updated to be more efficient. Mr. Jarvis outlined the cost-benefit
study results of the new code and explained that this will benefit Utah homebuyers. Councilor
Bill Fillmore asked if the building code would apply to remodeled homes at a certain threshold.
Mr. Jarvis believed it was only for new homes, but he could clarify this with Kevin Emerson.
Councilor Katrice MacKay asked if a cost-benefit analysis had been done. Mr. Jarvis said there
were three different cost-benefit analyses. Ms. MacKay was concerned about the affordability of
homes; she wanted to make sure this was measured and balanced. Mr. Jarvis said he spoke with
Representative Marsha Judkins who shared that concern about home affordability. This will only
add less than 0.05% to the total cost of the house; when added to a 30-year loan, it will be a very
nominal cost. Ms. MacKay has heard different feedback from homebuilders, who had many
concerns about the added costs and she did not feel she had enough information. Mr. Fillmore
asked if homebuilders agreed with the 0.05% figure. Mr. Jarvis indicated that the Kem Gardner
Institute was supportive of the change.

Councilor George Handley shared comments in support of the resolution. He noted that it was
very crucial to have more efficiency built into building standards as it could have a great impact,
but it is difficult and extremely costly to retrofit homes with these changes as opposed to
building them that way initially. Mr. Jarvis responded to a question from Mr. Fillmore to offer
clarification about the building code organizations. Ms. MacKay said Mr. Handley’s comments
were compelling; having poor insulation was an issue for many homeowners. She thought the
official energy reviews are compelling, she just wanted a balance of perspectives. Presentation
only. This item was already scheduled for the Council Meeting on January 17, 2023.

4.      An ordinance adopting the Provo City General Plan and appendices. Citywide
        application. (PLGPA20210364) (01:34:10)

Brandon Larsen, Planning Supervisor, presented. Mr. Larsen presented a list of the requests from
Councilors and he explained how each of these requests has been addressed. These additions
addressed details about Provo’s unhoused population, liquefaction, a city limits map, clean and
renewable energy sources, trails and peaks map, references to the sustainability coordinator, and
several other minor items. Mr. Larsen also reviewed the appendices to the General Plan.
Appendix A addresses the public engagement that took place for the general plan.

Appendix B includes the moderate income housing element. Mr. Larsen elaborated on this and
several changes which were made to this to comply with the state requirements. They have
reviewed the revised plan with the Department of Workforce Services at the state and will hear
back soon regarding this review. Appendix C addresses the City’s annexation maps and policies.

Councilor David Shipley noted that area 4 on the map was federal property and was not likely to
be annexed to the City. Mr. Larsen noted that area 6 was similar. It is referenced as sensitive land
and that is why it is included on the map. Councilor George Handley asked about South Fork
Canyon—he noted that the supporting plans such as the river and lakeshore, canyons and
foothills plans could result in amendments to this map. Mr. Larsen noted these were high-level
policies and could certainly be updated consistent with those future plans. Mr. Larsen noted that
the main updates to this section from the previous General Plan are that areas that have been
annexed since them have been updated.

Appendix D contains the updated General Plan Map. In the previous General Plan, there was a
future land use map and several iterations of the general plan map. Staff reviewed these
thoroughly and updated them to be more consistent with the City’s land use designations and
policies. Areas with specific neighborhood or area plans have also been designated on this map.

Councilor Katrice MacKay asked why some ethnicities were not included in the General Plan,
such as 1% African American in Provo which is not referenced. Mr. Larsen indicated that they
did not reference the smaller percentages. Ms. Ellsworth suggested pasting in a chart from the
Census. Mr. Handley thought something that briefly and accurately addresses this, rather than
excluding the information, would be helpful. Mr. Handley did not want it to seem that certain
groups are excluded from the City. It could be important data to refer back to in the future.

Ms. Ellsworth asked about how single-family housing types are quantified in the General Plan.
She thought it was important to distinguish between attached and detached housing types. Ms.
Ellsworth suggested deleting “in key areas, such as the city’s west side” from point 1.a. on page
36. Several Councilors indicated support for this change.

Shannon Ellsworth suggested adding 1860 South Lakeview Parkway as a design corridor (from
the Provo Airport to State Street) as this is a major entrance to the City. Mr. Handley wondered if
this should wait for the Gateways Plan. Mr. Fillmore also supported including that in the
gateway plan later on. Mr. Larsen said the map will require updates by the GIS staff, but he can
make the text changes easily enough. Several Councilors indicated their preference to defer that
