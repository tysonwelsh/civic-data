The following are the minutes of the Planning Commission Meeting of the Herriman Planning Commission.
The meeting was held on Thursday, October 7, 2021 at 6:00 p.m. in the Fort Herriman Conference Room
and the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice
of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members
of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

Presiding: Chair Chris Berbert

Commissioners Present In Person: Brody Rypien, Lorin Palmer, Heather Garcia, Andrea Bradford, Adam
Jacobson, Jackson Ferguson, Forrest Sickles and Daryl Fenn

Commissioners Excused: Andy Powell

Staff Present: City Planner Michael Maloy, Interim City Manager Wendy Thomas, HPD Cody Stromberg,
Deputy City Recorder Wendy Thorpe, Communications Specialist Destiny Skinner, and City Attorney Chase
Andrizzi, Planner II Sheldon Howa, Engineer II Josh Petersen, City Engineer Jonathan Bowers, Assistant City
Planner Clinton Spencer, and Planner Talia Wright.



Chair Berbert called the meeting to order at 6:03 p.m.


City Planner Maloy explained Council recently approved the Olympia MDA, design standards and annexation.
Lieutenant Governor has the plat for final signature, which will become effective January 1, 2022.


Planning Manager Spencer reviewed the Windrose bonus density request for an additional .2 units per acre.
The Commission was to determine if the proposed landscaping of the offsite detention pond qualified as a
system improvement. City ordinance did not have a definition for system improvement. Commissioner Fenn
asked for more information and Planning Manager Spencer provided an overview of bonus density calculations
and previous improvements which have qualified. The applicant proposed to irrigate, landscape, and finish a
detention pond across the street from the new subdivision for bonus density points to increase the
development from 1.8 to 2.2 units per acre, which allowed eleven lots. The Detention pond was functional city
owned property but had not been irrigated or landscaped.



Planning Manager Spencer reviewed the latest changes proposed by the Commission. Objective architectural
standards were reviewed, and he requested feedback. The areas of regulation for the proposed chapter included:
    • Location
    • Height
    • Architectural Standards
    • Development Standard Table
    • Special Exceptions
    • Definitions
The amendment proposed five feet setbacks from the primary structure for all building for fire safety. Small
buildings, shorter than twelve feet tall and smaller than 200 square feet, may be closer to the home. Those
larger than 200 square feet would require five feet setbacks. Commissioners debated allowable sizes and
setbacks for small structures. They agreed small, plastic structures should be allowed with no setbacks or
regulations. Planning Manager Spencer stated the provision prohibited runoff and drainage onto neighboring
properties. The proposed amendment allowed one-foot setbacks for property line, no setback necessary from
adjacent accessory structures, two-foot setbacks from accessory structures on adjacent lots, and five feet behind
front yard setback/ front of the home. Most residents prefer accessory structure placement near the property
lines, not in the center of the yard. Commissioners debated ideal locations for accessory dwelling units on
corner lots. Commissioners debated maximum allowable heights for small and medium buildings, and agreed
increased height should require higher setbacks. Homes along trails may require special exceptions. Side yards
should be included in the calculation of square footage percentages. Architectural standards prohibited exposed
plywood, unfinished material, and canvas type material on the exterior. The addition of cedar shingle was
requested. Commissioners debated allowable exterior material for structures larger than 200 square feet on
large lots. They agreed to continue the discussion after the regular session meeting.

The work meeting was adjourned by consensus at 6:59 p.m. and Commissioners agreed to reconvene after the regular meeting.


Chair Chris Berbert called the meeting to order at 7:02 p.m.


Russ Wilson led the audience in the Pledge of Allegiance.
Full Quorum Present.


No conflicts were offered.


Commissioner Heather Garcia MOVED to approved Item 2.4 Approval of Minutes for the June 3, 2021, Planning
Commission Meeting and all voted aye.




Planning Manager Spencer reviewed the application for the preliminary plat approval for eleven single family
lots. He reviewed the history and timeline of the item. All lots comply with the zoning and engineering
requirements. He identified the detention lot on the east side, and the temporary turn around. Base density for
the A-.25 zone was 1.8 units per acre. The application requested bonus density per the proposed 2.2 acres as per
the following:

Base Density:                          1.8 units per acre
Add 10’ park strip:                 0.1 units per acre
Add ½ acre lots adjacent to
Agricultural uses/zones:           0.1 units per acre
Add installation of “system
improvement”:                        0.2 units per acre
Total:                                   2.2 units per acre

The addition of the ten-foot landscaped park strip buffer between the sidewalk and fence, was consistent with
the existing streetscape. The applicant proposed completion of the landscaping and irrigation of the Jensen Place
detention basin. Per engineering this did not qualify as a system improvement, however the specific definition
of a system improvement was not defined by City ordinance. Approval would be at the discretion of the
Commission. Planning Manager Spencer reviewed the options for action. Staff recommended approval with
four staff conditions of approval.

Commissioners confirmed the onsite detention pond in the southeast corner of the subdivision would be fully
landscaped and maintained by the City. City Attorney Andrizzi explained the City did not have a definition for
system improvements, but Utah State Code identified them as a public facility that benefited more than just the
immediate project. Based on that definition City Engineer Bowers reviewed previous qualified improvements.
If the detention pond served multiple projects, it might be considered a system improvement.

Applicant Russ Wilson with Symphony Homes offered the definition of system improvements was debatable.
Over the past couple years, they have received mixed recommendations from staff. He added the City had
received bids pricing the improvement at $114,000. They sought bids independently for fencing and irrigation,
which totaled $73,000. It was his understanding the city had earmarked $32,000. With that included they would
pay their share of $41,000 for completion. They thought such an agreement would benefit the City. He
confirmed vinyl fencing would be placed between the homes and the pond, and would be landscaped with trees,
shrubs, flowerbeds, and stone mulch per City standards.

Commissioners discussed previous bonus density improvements, debated potential improvements and regional
system improvements. Commissioner Jacobson offered the opinion this was not a regional system improvement
but may warrant a .1 density bonus. He requested citation of specific sections for future bonus density requests.
Commissioner Fenn speculated the discharge would affect City storm drains and this may be a system
improvement, as completion would prevent silt in the piping. Commissioner Jacobson asked why the pond
improvements had not been completed previously. City Engineer Bowers responded it was initially a retention
basin with no discharge until 7300 West was completed. Commissioner Rypien thought the current state of the
area was an eyesore and agreed with Commissioner Fenn.

Commissioners requested a size comparison with the Shoshone pond in the Valhalla subdivision. City Engineer
Bowers replied the Shoshone pond was larger. Commissioners debated whether the developer should receive
credit for the density bonus and whether it met the definition of a system improvement. Commissioner Palmer
proposed an eleven-lot subdivision worked well with the surrounding neighborhood. Planning Manager Spencer
confirmed the City would contribute the $32,000 already set aside for the improvements. Commissioner
Jacobson expressed the City contribution made him less likely to favor a density bonus. Commissioners
requested to know if the earmarked money could be applied to another improvement if the developer funded
the improvement with no City contribution. City Engineer Bowers confirmed the $32,000 was tied to this
specific pond as it was a fee in lieu. Commissioners further debated potential system improvement definitions
and quarter acre zoning requirement. Commissioner Garcia asked if the bonus density allowance for parks
included a size requirement. Planning Manager Spencer responded the provision required half acre size with
amenities and may not just be a detention pond. Chair Berbert stated as this pond was only a quarter acre it was
too small to warrant the density bonus. Commissioners thought they should base their decision on the State
definition for system improvements. Commissioner Garcia proposed a ten-acre subdivision fit better with the
surrounding community, as the larger lots provided a buffer to the larger lots to the south.

Applicant Wilson expressed they modified from the original cul-de-sac and made expensive changes based on
potential system improvements. He questioned the validity of staff recommendation #1 which stated:
Approval with adjacent landowner to east for permission to discharge stormwater from retention pond for
storms exceeding 100-year interval. He requested that requirement be removed from the conditions of approval,
as the detention basin was designed to handle 100-year storm events. City Engineer stated overflow was directed
to the right of way, that previous staff requirement had been mitigated and he agreed that requirement #1 should
be removed.

Commissioners agreed a specific definition of system improvements was needed and agreed completion would
solve a problem for the City but should not be considered a system improvement.

Planning Commission approve the proposed Windrose Subdivision with a minimum of ten (10) lots, as
requested by the applicant with the following requirements to be made in order to record the final plat:
    1. Approval with adjacent landowner to east for permission to discharge stormwater from retention pond
       for storms exceeding 100-year interval.

   1. Approved a bonus density of 2.0 for a total of ten (10) lots.
          A. 0.1 – 10’ dedication of park strip behind the sidewalk
          B. 0.1 – Providing ½ acre lots adjacent to other larger/ agricultural uses or zones
   2. Provide updated landscaping plans for the subdivision showing street trees at thirty (30) feet on center
      in the park strip along Halls Crossing Dr.
    3. Receive approval from the Engineering department of all proposed landscaping in areas to be
       dedicated to the City including the detention area, and ten (10) foot landscaping buffers on 7300 West.
    4. The applicant receive and comply with all other Engineering requirements for approval.

Commissioner Palmer moved to adjourn the meeting at 7:54 p.m. and reconvene the work meeting. All voted aye.

The work meeting recommenced at 7:55 p.m.




Commissioner Jacobson noticed the five-foot rear setback would affect most large yards and not allow many
existing structures, which were close to the property line. Commissioner Jacobson encouraged other
Commissioners to walk around and make note of placement of existing structures. Commissioners discussed
negative neighbor impacts and emphasized the importance of allowing backyard spaces for hobbies without
disrupting views for neighbors. They debated allowable setbacks for multiple structure and lot sizes. Planning
Manager Spencer asked if the Commission preferred three-foot rear setbacks for large structures, or that
setbacks should increase for larger ADUs. Further debate ensued. Commissioner Palmer reviewed ADU
placement setbacks in Riverton City, and recommended all Commissioners walk around the neighborhoods
and check measurements.

Commissioner Palmer requested to be excused and left the meeting at 8:12 p.m.

Planning Manager Spencer compiled the recommendations and agreed to make the requested updates. Special
exceptions would be allowed when the lot had unique characteristics which may include irregular lot shape,
topography, flexibility in determining side / rear yards, and lot adjacency to commercial land uses sides.



Planning Manager Spencer proposed three options: eliminate the density bonus entirely, provide more clarity
of bonus densities by defining system improvements, or establish new lot sizes for the A-.25. Planning Manager
Spencer said the lack of direction had been confusing for developers. Commissioner Jacobson reviewed the
history of density bonuses. Commissioners debated the pros and cons. Commissioners discussed specific
project and system improvements. Commissioner Fenn proposed quarter acre zones should include quarter
acre lots. Planning Manager Spencer clarified the residential zones had more specific conditions than
agricultural zones. Chair Berbert proposed lots in A-.25 be a minimum of quarter acre in size. City Planner
Maloy proposed 10,000 square feet lots with bonus density provisions may have better site planning and
architecture. Commissioners debated ideal central gathering places and unique parks. Commissioners agreed a
list of specific projects defined by area would be beneficial.

The second work meeting adjourned by consensus at 9:00 p.m.
