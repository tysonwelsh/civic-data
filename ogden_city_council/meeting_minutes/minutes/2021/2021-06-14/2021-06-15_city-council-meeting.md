     Residential Reuse in Nonresidential Zones
               In Ogden there are many homes that have been converted for commercial use. In some cases, these homes were
     originally in residential zones, but the zoning designation has changed, and residential uses are no longer allowed. This issue has
     left some former homes in disrepair because of changes in market trends. The proposed amendment would allow such structures
     to return to residential use despite being located in nonresidential zones. Staff proposed an amendment to the zoning code to allow
     fanner nonconforming residences in nonresidential zones to be reverted from commercial use to residential use, provided certain
     conditions are met.
               Deputy Planning Manager Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the proposed zoning text
     amendment, which would allow a home in nonresidential zone that has been converted to a permitted use to revert to a single-
     family dwelling. This would allow for a home to be purchased and renovated to avoid continued deterioration; it also provides
     additional housing options in a tight housing market. The present situation was created as zoning and desired uses evolved from
     single-family, multi-family, commercial, and industrial uses throughoutthe City; as zoning evolved, ordinances were written in a
     fashion that segregated uses from one another. He provided photographs of properties to serve as examples of accommodating
     nonconforming uses; requirements that would be placed on a property with reuse petition include returning the building to its
     original residential character, no more than 50 percent of the front setback shall be paved, cmmnercial signs will be removed,
     compliance with residential building codes, no outdoor storage, and vehicles must belong to residents or on-site guests. Dual use
     options would also be available to provide owners the ability to live and work in the unit. He provided additional photographs of
     prope~iies in which a dual use presently exists.

Reconsideration - Honorary Street Name Designation of 2nd Street from
Century Drive to Wall Avenue
           Council Deputy Director Symes explained Anna Keogh submitted a petition to assign an honorary street name for a
portion of2nd Street between the Union Pacific railroad tracks and Washington Boulevard. The initially proposed honorary street
name was "Bingham Fort Lane" in honor of Bingham Fort, which was located in that area. The proposal was reviewed by the
Planning Commission and a recommendation of approval for a shorter distance was forwarded to the Council with a vote of7-0.
As recommended by the Planning Commission, the honorary street name would apply to the portion of 2nd Street between
Century Drive and Wall A venue, which the Planning Commission found to align with the fort's historic location more closely.
Upon initial review by the City Council, the Council requested that the proposal be reviewed by the City's Diversity Commission.
This review was completed, and the Diversity Commission forwarded a recommendation of denial to the Council. The Diversity
Commission's recommendation was based on the belief that the proposal did not accurately represent and honor the perspectives
of all the peoples of the area at the time. At the Council's February 2, 2021 work session, Council members discussed the proposal
and the recommendation from the Diversity Commission. Council members expressed opposition to the proposal as presented.
The petitioner requested additional time to develop an alternative proposal that would help address concerns expressed by the
Diversity Commission. The proposal was amended to include the name of Chief Little Soldier. The amended petition proposed
assigning the honorary name as "Bingham Ft-ChiefLittle Soldier Ln." The alternative proposal was reviewed at the April27,
2021 work session meeting. At the Council's May 25, 2021 meeting, a vote was taken on the alternative proposal which
concluded in a three-to-three tie. The tie vote resulted in the denial of the petition. After the May 25 meeting, the petitioner
expressed interested in proposing further amendments to the honorary name eliminating the Bingham Fort name and proposing the
name be "Chief Little Soldier Way." At the June 1, 2021 meeting, in accordance with its Rules and Norms, the Council voted to
reconsider the item. This work session will be the opportunity for the Council to provide direction on how it would like to proceed
in reviewing the proposal and considering the newly proposed honorary name.
           Mr. Symes facilitated discussion among the Council regarding communications between Ms. Keogh, Council members,
and Council staff regarding the adjustments she is willing to make to the proposed honorary street name, after which Chair Blair
invited input from Darren Parry, Chairman of the Shoshone Nation. Mr. Parry stated he presented this matter to the Shoshone
Nation Council, and they voted unanimously to support changing the name of the street to Chief Little Soldier Ln. He provided
some historical information regarding the Chief, after which he noted that he and the Shoshone Council were willing to
compromise and include a reference to Bingham Fort in the street name. However, he was pleased to learn that after the tie vote
on the petition, Ms. Keogh expressed willingness to amend her proposal to simply name the street after Chief Little Soldier.
           Discussion among the Council and Mr. Parry centered on the makeup of the Tribal Council for the Shoshone Nation,
after which the Council engaged in discussion of the appropriate name for the street should they proceed with action on an
honorary street name.
           Chair Blair invited input from Ms. Keogh. Ms. Keogh stated she has nothing to add to the email she sent to the entire
Council expressing her position; she was delighted to hear that the Shoshone Council is supportive of the street name change in an
effort to honor Chief Little Soldier.
           After continued discussion among the Council, they ultimately concluded to proceed with consideration of adjustments to
the City's honorary street name ordinance, but to also consider fmal action on the petition in advance of fmal action on the
ordinance amendments. A majority of the Council also indicated they did not believe it was necessary to send the item back to the
Diversity Commission for an additional recommendation.
