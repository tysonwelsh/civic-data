GP-2-2022
West Valley City
Amend the Major Street Plan to extend Lester Street (5700 West) as a 66’ right-of-way
north of 2400 South, add 7200 West as an 80’ right-of-way between 2100 South and SR-
201, and extend 5900 West as a 66’ right-of-way between Beagley Road (2540 South) and
Parkway Blvd. (2700 South)

At the request of the West Valley City Public Works Department, Planning and Zoning staff is
requesting an amendment to the West Valley City General Plan to update the City’s Major Street
Plan to 1) extend Lester Street (5700 West) as a 66’ right-of-way north of 2400 South, 2) add
7200 West as an 80’ right-of-way between 2100 South and SR-201, and 3) extend 5900 West as
a 66’ right-of-way between Beagley Road (2540 South) and Parkway Blvd. (2700 South).

The Major Street Plan defines street alignments and right-of-way widths for existing and
proposed major streets within the City and is referenced several times in the City’s Zoning
Ordinance. The Plan is used to:
    determine building setbacks along major streets that have yet to be widened;
    identify streets needing right-of-way dedication as part of a subdivision; and
    clarify certain land use regulations relating to:
          o greenhouses and garden centers,
          o RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) zone eligibility criteria,
          o shared parking,
          o tobacco oriented businesses, and
          o walls for double frontage lots.

The justification for these three Major Street Plan changes is described below.

Lester Street (5700 West)

With the relatively new signalized intersection at 2400 South and 5600 West, the proposed
extension of Lester Street will serve as a frontage road to 5600 West, improving access for
properties between the Mountain View Corridor and 5600 West.

7200 West

The Wasatch Front Regional Council’s Regional Transportation Plan (RTP) includes 7200 West
between SR-201 and 1400 North (which is north of I-80) as a 5-lane facility. The RTP also
includes an extension of 7200 West between 1400 North and 700 North as a 3-lane facility. In
West Valley, the section of 7200 West between SR-201 and 2100 South is currently striped as 2
lanes with varying right-of-way widths.

5900 West

With the completion of the Mountain View Corridor and the south bound ramp at Parkway
Blvd., truck traffic from industrial areas north of the Riter Canal could access southbound
Mountain View Corridor by traveling south of 6400 West and then east along Parkway Blvd.,
which would impact a largely residential area. Extending 5900 West to Parkway Blvd. would
create a direct connection for truck traffic to Mountain View Corridor along Beagley Road (2540
South). This connection would discourage truck traffic from using Parkway Blvd.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval of the General Plan change.

   2. Continuance, for reasons determined during the public hearing.

   3. Denial.

CA-4-2022
Valley Collision Outside
Storage 2738 S Constitution
Blvd
M Zone (1.74 acres)

Valley Collision, located at 2738 S Constitution Blvd, is requesting conditional use approval for
outside storage of vehicles on the property. The property is zoned M, Manufacturing, and the
West Valley City General Plan designates this area as light manufacturing. Zoning to the north
is M, zoning to the west and south is R-1-8, and zoning to the east is RMH, residential mobile
home. Adjacent land uses include light industrial businesses to the north, a Rocky Mountain
Power corridor to the west and south, and a mobile home park to the east.

This application was continued from the September 14, 2022 Hearing at the request of the
applicant. The applicant wanted more time to address parking on the site. Valley Collision
recently started to operate from the subject property. They are an autobody repair and painting
business. There was a business previously on the property that also did collision repair, C-46-
1997, but that approval did not allow for any outside storage on the property. There is one area
on the north side of the building that was approved for the storage of vehicles during the day,
but the conditional use approval did not allow outside storage. The applicant would like to use
this area to the north, as well as a large portion of the existing parking lot on the south end for
vehicle storage.

Per 7-7-119, outside storage areas are to be screened from public view. The applicant has
installed a chain link fence with slats along the southern area of the existing parking lot that
they would like to use for storage. The area on the north side of the building was previously
fenced with chain link and slats. All areas of the site where vehicle storage will occur was
previously paved with asphalt.

The property has frontage on Constitution Blvd, one of the City’s high image arterial
streets. The site currently has the 20’ required landscaped setback along the frontage and
street trees. Some additional trees were also recently planted as part of a business license
review of the property. All landscaped areas shall be maintained.

The original conditional use approval for the site required 40 parking stalls for one building,
and an additional building was to be built later. A site plan included with that original approval
showed both buildings and indicated there would be 59 total parking stalls on the site. There are
currently 48 parking stalls. With the two outside storage areas proposed on the property, 22
customer and employee parking stalls will remain. The applicant has indicated that in the
building there is 2,000 sq. ft. of office, 4,000 sq. ft. of storage, and 14,000 sf of production
space, which includes 12 auto body bays and 6 paint bays. He also indicated that the highest
employment shift currently has 10 employees, but 15 or 16 employees would be ideal. There
are 4 employees that work from the office area in the building. The applicant also said that they
typically have up to three customers on the site at one time to drop off or pick-up vehicles.

West Valley City code requires 3 customer and employee parking spaces for each service bay.
The parking code requirement is also 1 stall per 250 sq. ft. of office area and 1 stall per 2,000
sq. ft. of storage. Based on the City code, the building square footage, and number of service
bays in the building, the parking requirement for the site is 64 parking stalls. For reference, staff
has provided parking numbers for similar auto-related uses from ITE’s Parking Generation
Manual. In parentheses staff added what the parking requirement for the auto service use would
look like for each scenario:


        Tire Store: 2.85 stalls/1,000 SF Monday – Friday and 4.17 stalls/1,000 SF on Saturday
         (39 stalls)
        Quick Lubrication Vehicle Shop: 5.07 stalls/1,000 SF Monday – Friday (70 stalls)
        Auto Parts and Service Center: 1.69 stalls/1,000 SF Monday – Friday and
         1.07 stalls/1,000 SF on Saturday (23 stalls)

Going from 64 stalls to 22 would be quite a reduction from what is required in the City Code,
however, business operations are different than a traditional auto service or quick lube shop, so
it does seem reasonable that some relief from the standard WVC code requirements be allowed.
Since their original application submittal, the applicant has revisited their proposal and is
willing to set aside 13 parking stalls within the fenced-in storage area for employee parking.
They were also proposing to stack two rows of cars behind these parking spaces during the day
for a staging area. The Fire Department visited the site, and they will permit one row of cars
stacked behind these stalls, which would still allow for fire truck access. With these 13 stalls,
and the 22 additional stalls outside of the storage areas, there will be 35 stalls available on the
property for customer and employee parking. All access driveways need to remain clear and
open, as well as all drive aisles outside of the storage areas. Staff has listed three different
parking scenarios in the staff alternatives.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as
the following:

             1. All outside storage areas shall be screened from public view using chain
                link fencing with slats and shall be paved with asphalt or concrete.
             2. Parking on the property shall meet one of the following requirements:
                        a. All 7-9 requirements shall be met, which includes providing enough
                            parking for 3 stalls per service bay, 1 stall per 250 sq. ft. of office space,
                            and 1 stall per 2,000 sq. ft. of storage space. In the current proposal
                            there are 18 service bays, but some of these service bays could be
                            removed from the building, which would reduce the total amount of
                            parking required.
                        b. Parking on the property can be reduced from the requirements of 7-9,
                            but there shall be at least 36 customer and employee parking stalls
                            provided on the property, or 2 stalls per service bay. No additional
                            stalls shall be required for the office and storage areas of the building.
                        c. Parking on the property can be reduced from the requirements of 7-9. A
                            minimum of 22 customer and employee parking stalls shall be provided
                            on the property, outside of the vehicle storage areas. 13 additional
                            employee parking stalls shall be provided within the vehicle storage
                            areas provided that only one row of vehicles be allowed to be stored
                            parallel to the curb behind the 13 employee parking stalls.
              3.    There shall be no parking in vehicle maneuvering areas outside of the
                    vehicle storage areas.
              4.    There shall be no parking in access driveways.
              5.    There shall be no automobile repair work done outside.
              6.    Landscaping on the property shall be maintained.
              7.    All requirements of any affected department or agency must be met, including
                    but not limited to Granger Hunter Improvement District, WVC Public Works,
                    WVC Building Inspections and WVC Fire Department.
              8.    Subject to review upon valid complaint.

Continuance, for reasons determined during the public hearing.
