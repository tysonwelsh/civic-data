Active Transportation (Walkability) Program Update:
John Robertson, City Engineer, and Heinrich Deters, Open Space Manager, presented
this item. Robertson reviewed past projects paid for with bond funds, including the
Bonanza Tunnel, the Comstock Tunnel and the Kearns Tunnel, and noted these
projects greatly helped connectivity around town. Other projects included sidewalks and
colored crosswalks, bike lanes on SR 248, the Summit Bike Share project which
provided e-bikes to get around the community, marked sharrows, pathway finding, and
Rail Trail improvements. Funds were also spent on the Summit County Active
Transportation Plan and annual maintenance.

The projects planned for this year included bike lanes and sharrows for Deer Valley
Drive, Old Town Stairs on 6th Street, and bike corrals at various locations around town.
Possible future projects were the Bonanza bridge, which would connect the Arts and
Culture District to Prospector; improved crossings at Iron Horse, Prospector, Munchkin,
and Bonanza; a crossing at Snowcreek/SR 248, the Munchkin and Homestake

Council Member Doilney thought the Snowcreek crossing should be a priority since

Council Member Worel requested signage for the Deer Valley trail and was concerned

about that trail as well as other trails. Council Member Worel requested the signage in

Council Member Gerber favored all the proposed projects. She asked how some of
these paths could be used to help the transit routes. Robertson stated he would study
that to make sure there weren't gaps between paths and transit stops. Council Member
Gerber asked when it was okay to bike on sidewalks, and noted that information should
be distributed to educate bike riders. Deters stated eight feet was the minimum width for
a multiuse pathway. Robertson stated one of the projects was a safety awareness
campaign and the requirements for bike paths would be included.

Council Member Henney stated a lot of work went into getting the active transportation
bond and the City was enjoying the results of those efforts today. He liked seeing the
alternate modes of transportation. He only favored at grade crossings to take away
roads from vehicles. He favored over grade bridges and under grade tunnels. He also
noted he liked the wayfinding signs.

Council Member Joyce stated the most heavily used trail was the Rail Trail, and he
supported taking control of that trail from the State. He indicated he liked the Quinn's
Park and Ride Rail Trail connection and the Bonanza Park Arts and Culture District
connection, but didn't like where the money was coming from. He proposed that the City
not use the Active Transportation Bond funds for the City's other projects like the
Bonanza Bridge and Quinn’s Park and Ride, but use those funds for stand-alone active
transportation projects.

Council Member Gerber asked if the City could apply for active transportation grants.
Robertson stated the City had applied for federal funding to widen and complete the trail
at Three Kings. There were other funding options and the City would be considering all
of them. Deters added that staff was working to coordinate projects concurrently,
especially the SR 248 corridor project, to include transit and active transportation.

Discuss Cluster Mailboxes in Old Town:
Hannah Tyler, Senior Planner, and Jonathan Weidenhamer, Economic Development
Manager, presented this item. Tyler stated the Postmaster deemed that door-to-door
delivery was not possible around Main Street, but cluster mailboxes would be
acceptable. In 2013, staff and Council determined cluster mailboxes were not
recommended for the right-of-way because of snow maintenance. The Postmaster was
now charging the primary residents for post office boxes and referring concerned
citizens to the City. She reviewed the challenges associated with having cluster
mailboxes and asked if the City would allow some of the bigger Condos to request
cluster mailboxes from the Postmaster, if Council was interested in paying for the
residents’ post office boxes in areas deemed deliverable within Old Town, or if Council
wanted to refer this issue to the Planning Commission for review.

Council Member Henney asked for more details for referring this to the Planning
Commission and asked if they had jurisdiction over right-of-way concerns and issues.

2020 Water Conservation Plan Update:
Jason Christensen, Water Manager, presented this item and noted great progress had
been made with water conservation efforts. He indicated residential and irrigation water
usage was reduced by over 50% since the plan went into effect. He discussed actions
taken to reduce water loss caused by leaks in the pipelines. He reviewed some plans
for the next two years in detecting and stopping water loss. He noted the 33% water
loss reduction goal by 2030.

Council Member Worel asked if all residents who experienced leaks received leak
detection notices. Christensen stated detection notices could be sent out if there was an
email on file.

Council Member Joyce asked about planned actions for reducing water loss.
Christensen stated staff would research different actions and proceed with projects that
were known to be successful. He thought with the completion of Three Kings Water
Treatment Plant (3KWTP), there would be discussions on moving more aggressively on
curbing water loss. Council Member Doilney praised Christensen for his efforts and
stated conserving water was very important. Council Member Henney thought
Christensen and his team had done a great job with water conservation.

stated as part of the budget, $250,000 was allocated for COVID relief. He stated this
money was held back while State and Federal relief was distributed. He asked how
Council would like the money spent. He looked at other mountain towns to see what
they had done to help their communities. He reviewed his staff report and associated
data in the exhibits and stated any needs from businesses needed to be documented.

Council Member Joyce stated the personal protective equipment (PPE) program was
running out and some other programs were loans. He asked if it would be feasible to
look for those who hadn't received money from other sources. Weidenhamer stated in
the survey, 11% of 135 participants hadn't received relief money. He felt the biggest
gauge would be to have businesses show their need by providing year-over-year
financial statements.

Council Member Doilney wanted businesses to be able to stay open until December,
and didn’t know if distributing the money now or saving it until later in the fall would be
better. Council Member Worel agreed with Council Member Doilney and explained
Jewish Family Services had a revolving small business loan program that she learned
about, and noted $5,000 was not enough to help sometimes. Businesses were also
reluctant to take on more debt. She also discussed that Summit County had relief
money for businesses and those funds would be in the form of grants. She asked if the
City should wait awhile to disburse funds since the County and Jewish Family Services
were distributing funds now.

Mayor Beerman asked if the Chamber was planning on distributing another survey,
since the last one was done in May. Weidenhamer stated one was being drafted for
distribution. Mayor Beerman asked staff to look into the PPE and the Governor’s Office
of Economic Development (GOED) programs to verify that support from the City would
not preclude businesses from receiving help from the State. He also indicated the City
might consider giving larger grants to fewer people to make a bigger difference. Council
Member Doilney stated targeting City support to impact the most vulnerable population
might be something to consider.

Statement on Old Town Mail Boxes:

The last few years have been an exceedingly frustrating experience with my Old Town mail box.
As I understand it, the Postmaster says primary home residents legally entitled to a free PO Box,
but she still charges me $204/year! Further, due to the volume of mail I receive I’m required to
rent a larger box, and if I ask for a smaller box I will lose my current box number (which would
result in a lot of complications). She explained that I can’t have a free or even a discounted box
because the City had the option of installing satellite boxes and they would not do it.

I’ve talked with my neighbors and they are equally disappointed and frustrated. It is clear that
there is strained relationship between the USPS and our local government, but we are suffering
for it. Anything you could do to rectify the situation, including creating satellite boxes (e.g. at the
bottom of Rossi Hill, at the Library, and other convenient locations) or subsidizing full-time
residents’ boxes, would be GREATLY appreciated.
