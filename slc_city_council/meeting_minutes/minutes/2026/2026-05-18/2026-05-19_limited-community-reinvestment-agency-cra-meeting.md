**1.**

**Overview of the Community Reinvestment Agency Budget for Fiscal Year 2026-27**   ~ 1:00 p.m.
  60 min.
The Board will receive a general overview of the proposed budget for the Community Reinvestment Agency of Salt Lake City for Fiscal Year 2026-27. The Board will continue to discuss the Mayor’s Recommended Budget over the next several weeks and will have public hearings on Tuesday, May 19, 2026 and Tuesday, June 2, 2026 at 7 p.m. The Board expects to adopt the budget in mid-June.

**Summary:**

Kate Werrett provided a brief summary of the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2026-27 budget.

Danny Walz provided an overview of the Agency’s finances, operations, and recent accomplishments, including:

* Funds managed by the Agency, including Project Area Funds, Housing Funds, Multi-use Funds, and the Agency Operations Fund
* Key accomplishments such as the Disaster Relief Loan Program, the 9-Line Project Area, closing more than $5 M in housing loans, allocating an additional $8 million for housing initiatives, and launching the Commercial Development Loan Program
* Operational efficiencies
* Project area tax increment revenue
* Changes in expenses across the Agency’s various funds
* Overview of revenues and other funding sources totaling approximately $72.6 million
* Obligated and discretionary expenditures within project areas and multi-use funds
* Review of housing and multi-use fund programs and the Agency Operations fund
* Mayors Initiatives, including:
  + $1M in funding for Main Street revitalization/beautification
  + $2.2M in funding for the Civic Center improvements
  + $3.3M for 500 West/Granary District improvements

Directors, Jill Love, Blake Thomas, and Danny Walz discussed:

* Scope of the Public Utilities feasibility study in relation to the redevelopment of the Public Utility campus
* Clarification on infrastructure improvements for the Ballpark site, including management and activation
* How the Agency calculated administrative fees across various project areas
* The Agency’s role/purpose of providing enhancements to the City and strategic investments in development rather than basic core/maintenance services
* Preserving the Capital Improvement Project (CIP) fund for basic maintenance
* Funding the FY 2026-27 Mayor’s Initiatives to upgrade city amenities for upcoming large-scale events like the Temple Reopening, Olympics, etc.
* Recent cancellation of the Board’s approved $400k allocation for Main Street light posts due to reprioritization of needs along Main Street; funding remaining in the Strategic Intervention account for other projects
* Timing of funding 500 West/Granary District improvements with scope of work to include overdue road reconstruction and utility and curb realignment

**Director Request:**

Director Petro requested information on what the expected expenditures were regarding the allocations for the Mayor’s Initiatives, including reasoning on the selection of these specific areas for enhancement.

**Tentative Closed Meeting**
The Board will consider a motion to enter into closed meeting. A closed meeting described under Section 52-4-205 may be held for specific purposes including, but not limited to:
  1. discussion of the character, professional competence, or physical or mental health of an individual.
  2. strategy sessions to discuss pending or reasonably imminent litigation.
  3. strategy sessions to discuss the purchase, exchange, or lease of real property:
    (i) disclose the appraisal or estimated value of the property under consideration, or
    (ii) prevent the public body from completing the transaction on the best possible terms.
  4. strategy sessions to discuss the sale of real property, including any form of a water right or water shares, if:
    (i) public discussion of the transaction would:
      (A) disclose the appraisal or estimated value of the property under consideration, or
      (B) prevent the public body from completing the transaction on the best possible terms
    (ii) the public body previously gave public notice that the property would be offered for sale, and
    (iii) the terms of the sale are publicly disclosed before the public body approves the sale.
  5. discussion regarding deployment of security personnel, devices, or systems, and
  6. investigative proceedings regarding allegations of criminal misconduct.
A closed meeting may also be held for attorney-client matters that are privileged pursuant to Utah Code § 78B-1-137, and for other lawful purposes that satisfy the pertinent requirements of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act.
