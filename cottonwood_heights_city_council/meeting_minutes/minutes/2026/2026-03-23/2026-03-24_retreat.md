1.      Welcome

Mayor Gay Lynn Bennion called the meeting to order at approximately 2:00 p.m.

City Manager, Jared Gerber, expressed his appreciation for the Citizen Budget Committee’s efforts
but noted that the budgeting process was behind. It was important to find answers at the meeting
so Staff could move forward with the preliminary budget that must be adopted in May 2026. He
asked the City Council to focus on high-level questions regarding revenues and budget cuts.

Finance Director, Scott Jurges, reported that some of the reports they would be reviewing will be
developed with the Citizen Budget Committee. He then reviewed the Fiscal Year (“FY”) 2019-
2025 budget data, including original, adjusted, and actual numbers. Budgets are adjusted for a
variety of reasons, primarily for one-time reimbursable police overtime expenses and grants.
When creating the budget for a new year, they typically first look at the original adopted budget
and make adjustments based on that number to ensure that these one-time expenses are not carried
over.

Department-level expenses were separated by salary and benefits and operational expenses, with
a grand total and full-time equivalent (“FTE”) employee count for each department. Mr. Jurges
noted that FTEs were hand-counted as the City’s accounting software does not have that capability.

Mr. Jurges reviewed changes to the total General Fund expenses throughout the study period.
FTEs had only increased by 7.9, primarily because some employees were moved to other funds.
Additions include two Public Works Department employees hired to perform engineering services
that were previously contracted out. Three police officers had been hired for traction control,
which has an associated revenue offset. In that same period, salary and benefits expenses increased
by 57%. Budget Committee Member Evan Chan calculated the actual percentage to be 50%,
which is higher than the Consumer Price Index (“CPI”) for the time period of 30%. However,
Cottonwood Heights salaries are still below market, which puts the City at a disadvantage and
increases the potential of losing employees.

Operational expenses had only increased by 25.3% due to department heads’ efforts to be as
efficient as possible. Transfers from the General Fund to the Roads Capital Improvement Fund
had increased slightly from $1.4 million to $2 million. With the exception of an additional
$200,000 transfer to the Storm Water Fund, all other transfer increases were related to debts
incurred. Total expenses had increased by approximately $10 million since FY 2018-2019,
approximately $3 million in debt service and transfers, and $7 million in true increases in budgeted
expenditures.

Mr. Jurges reminded the Council that costs had increased exponentially. An item that cost $100
three years ago now costs $115 or $120, so the $2 million in roads funds does not go as far as it
once did, but roads still need to be maintained. Other fund data was included in the report, but
they were all dependent on the General Fund.

Council Member Hyland asked about transfers from the General Fund. Mr. Jurges reported that
the Debt Service Fund is fully sourced by the General Fund. Approximately $2 million is
transferred into the Capital Projects Fund, as well as occasional grants that are first deposited into
the General Fund. The Storm Water Fund receives $200,000 in transfers, but that transfer could
be replaced with an increased Storm Water Fee. Council Member Hyland stated that the General
Obligation (“GO”) Bond debt service is supported by property taxes. Mr. Jurges clarified that the
monies are first deposited in the General Fund and then transferred to the Debt Service Fund.

In response to a follow-up question from Council Member Hyland, Mr. Jurges reported that the
City will receive approximately $240,000 per year in lease payments from Walgreens. The project
was currently part of the Community Development and Renewal Agency (“CDRA”) Fund, but the
City Council could make the decision to transfer that revenue into the General Fund to offset
expenditures or retain it in the CDRA Fund to offset future costs of the Town Center project.
Council Member Hyland remarked that residents were told the bond would be supported by tax
revenue, and retaining the revenue in the CDRA Fund would go against that promise.

In response to a question from Council Member Newell, Mr. Jurges confirmed that the moneys
could be split between the two funds. Council Member Holton stated that the Council needed to
consider how each decision will affect a potential tax increase.

Council Member Hyland stated that fees are often demonized, but she now understands the need.
There are properties in the City that do not pay property taxes but do place a burden on the
stormwater system, for example. The Storm Water Fee fairly ensures that those properties pay for
City services. If the fee needs to be increased to match expenses so no General Fund transfer is

Council Member Hyland stated that she and Mayor Bennion participated in the Citizen Budget
Committee, and she had emailed the Council about key takeaways from the meetings. The
Committee had a clear message to tighten the City’s belt, balance the budget, and implement long-
term policies to prevent it from happening again. Cottonwood Heights had increased property
taxes once in the 21 years since incorporation, but the City was now in a position that a resident
described as a gut punch. That resident asked why taxes were not increased more gently so citizens
could incorporate it into their own budgets, which was a reasonable question. The takeaways from
the committee included tightening the budget wherever possible, not offering premium services,
and enacting a modest tax increase to carry the City over until the Town Center and Gravel Pit
begin generating revenue.

Council Member Birrell appreciated the historical tax rate comparison provided by Mr. Jurges.
Since 2007, comparable cities have increased taxes considerably. West Valley City has
implemented increases totaling 71%, Murray 68%, Midvale over 100%, Holladay 69%, Draper
82%, and Bluffdale 66%. Cottonwood Heights had an approximately 15% increase. Costs have
increased dramatically post-COVID. In hindsight, she would have enacted a smaller yearly
increase, but the City Council did not choose to do so. The data indicated that it would not be
unique for Cottonwood Heights to enact a tax increase.

Council Member Newell stated that he and Council Member Birrell brought up a tax increase when
they were first elected, but it was not well-received by the rest of the City Council. However, he
did not want to compare Cottonwood Heights to other cities but rather to develop a plan that would
work for the City. Cottonwood Heights is a unique municipality with a shifting population, and
the economy outside of the city is impacting residents. He agreed with Council Member Hyland
that it is important to look at fees that will close the gap for people who are utilizing City services
but not paying property taxes. He asked for an update on cuts that had already been enacted.

study was recently completed, and tens of millions of dollars in repairs and modifications were
required over the next five to 10 years. Every stormwater project that had been completed to date
was due to an emergency, and they had been unable to begin taking a proactive approach.

Council Member Holton stated that increasing the fee with the goal of eliminating the transfer

costs will only increase over time. Mr. Gerber indicated that it should be rolled into the five-year
strategic plan.

Council Member Hyland recommended that the fee be increased to the equivalent of the $200,000
transfer in the upcoming fiscal year. Mr. Jurges reported that the current fee of $8.44 per ERU
was projected to increase to $8.69. An increase of approximately 15% would be required to offset
the transfer, which would increase the total fee by $1.27 per month or $15.19 per year per ERU.

Council Member Holton asked about the origin of the $200,000 amount. Mr. Jurges stated that at
the time the fee was instituted, the City set an initial target of approximately $1.6 million. The 3%
yearly increase had only generated $224,000 in additional revenue since its inception. Mr. Shipp
stated that the Storm Water Fee Study recommended a fee of over $16, but the Council decided to
set it at $7.50 per ERU. The yearly 3% increases were a recent addition.

After further discussion, the Council agreed that the transfer from the General Fund should be
discontinued and the Storm Water Fee increased.
