Public Comment No comments Transportation Funding and Governance Task Force - Andrew Jackson, MAG Staff Representative Kay Christofferson gave an overview of the responsibilities and duties of the Transportation and Governance Task Force (Task Force). He is a member of the Task Force, Chair of the Funding Working Group, a sub-committee of the Task Force, and House Transportation Committee Member. The Task Force is composed of legislators, government agencies and the private sector. It was established to discuss the transportation needs in Utah for the next 40-50 years to prepare for growth in the state. Recommendations will be presented to the legislature with the intent to prepare for the future. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee September 7, 2017 Page 3 The Task Force consists of three work groups: 1. Funding Working Group, Chair Representative Kay Christofferson –Identify users and beneficiaries of the transportation system and who should be responsible to pay for it, use Value Capture tools to assess the value of property when infrastructures are built as a means to assess potential tax revenues, and how taxes are administered to pay for roads. 2. Governance and Transit Working Group, Chair Senator Wayne Harper – Identify how to structure and organize transportation within the state in regards to highways, transit and ports. 3. Economic Development, Land Use, Aviation, Active Transportation, Rural Issues Working Group, Chair Representative Mike Schultz – Identify how best to use land around transportation, adjust zoning to better coordinate with transportation hubs throughout the state. The Task Force encourages suggestions, input and attendance from government agencies and the public sector. TIP Modification - TIGER Bike/Pedestrian Projects - Bob Allen, MAG Staff In 2016 MAG, WFRC, UDOT and UTA coordinated a grant application to the Federal Transit Administration (FTA) for Transportation Investment Generating Economic Recovery (TIGER) funds. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee September 7, 2017 Page 4 The application consisted of multiple bicycle and pedestrian projects across the Wasatch Front. UTA, as the primary applicant, was awarded the grant. Three of the projects are within the MPO and need to be added to the TIP. 1. $4.9M – Pedestrian overpass in Lehi over SR-92 (Murdock Trail) 2. $3.98M – Pedestrian overpass in Provo over rail road tracks at Provo Front Runner Station 3. $48,153 – Bike/Ped improvements at Lehi, AF, Orem and Provo Front Runner Stations (bike racks, message boards, repair stands) Commissioner Lee asked if there are any stipulations in the TIGER grant that hinder control of moving or modifying the projects in the future in the event the roads need to be expanded. Bob Allen stated that MAG has been awarded funds for these specific projects and to change the scope may create a problem, however future expansions are taken into consideration in the design phase. Steve Meyers with UTA stated because TIGER funds are being used on the projects, the FTA expects there to be bike/pedestrian crossings in the specified areas funds were allocated to. If over the predicted lifespan of the project, the facilities need to be moved, modified or removed, there may be a potential obligation to FTA. Mayor Leifson moved that the TIP be modified to add the identified TIGER projects. Sherrie Hall-Everette seconded the motion. Commissioner Lee suggested a stipulation to the motion that planning be done for twenty years out in regards to designing the bridges to avoid conflicts in the future. Both Mayor Leifson and Sherrie Hall-Everette accepted the modification to the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Third Quarter Cent Follow-Up Discussion - Andrew Jackson, MAG Staff MAG is aware that it was unclear, and many of the Mayors did not understand some of the discussion at the August 3, 2017, MPO Regional Planning meeting related to the roles of the county and MPO to the 3rd quarter cent local option sales tax for transportation. An email was sent to the mayors on August 8, 2017 explaining the situation, in addition to providing a link to the July 5, 2017 County Commission meeting where they discussed the approval of funding for the projects previously approved by the MPO. Andrew Jackson provided follow-up information regarding the August 3, 2017 MPO Regional Planning meeting per the committee’s request. 1. MPOs – Checked with other MPOs a) Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC) is not aware of issues. WFRC has different taxes in their area, in additional to a regional board that serves multiple counties making it a COG issue. b) Dixie MPO does not have this tax option Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee September 7, 2017 Page 5 c) Cache MPO has a similar quarter cent tax and have not had any issues. They simply approve the list and the county funds the projects through a working agreement they have. 2. Legislative Research – Check with Office of Legislative Research and General Council a) They are not an interpretive body, nor are they one who sets policy. They do however look at constitutionality of proposed legislation. Their review is that the code is what it is unless it is changed 3. Attorneys – Contact Attorneys a) Robert J. Schumacher, MAG attorney for Aging Waiver contracts sent a letter stating - May means may, not shall, and recommend means recommend not approve. b) Jared Moss, PAMH Law, MAG attorney for CDBG and Revolving Loan contracts, in addition to other MAG requests sent a more detailed memo discussing the following: Controlling Law: • Language of Section 59-12-2218 mandates in several places that the taxes collected under this section “shall be expended” by the county. • “It is the purpose of this part to provide counties, cities, and towns of the state with an added source of revenue and to thereby assist them to meet their growing financial needs….It is the legislative intent that this added revenue be used to the greatest possible extent by the counties.” • The Legislature’s intent is to “grant the [Utah State Tax] commission exclusive authority to administer, operate, and enforce the local option sales and use tax, without interference from counties, cities, and towns.” Jared Moss stated that it makes no sense that the Utah County Commissioners participate and approve projects during the MPO process, and when presented with the ultimate funding decision they can veto or deny such funds from being expended for the transportation projects approved by the MPO. Andrew Jackson stated that Commissioner Graves said in the discussions the County Commission has had with their attorney, it is understood they do have that veto power because it’s a “may” instead of a “shall”. Available Remedies 1. Private Letter Ruling – Utah State Tax Commission a) Not made public, but would be shared with the county, mayors and MPO members. Could be potentially made public. 2. Petition for Rulemaking – Utah State Tax Commission Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee September 7, 2017 Page 6 a) Petition to make a formal public rule making that says this is the purpose and it is known widely to the public. 3. Amend the Statute – State Legislature a) State Legislators would have to sponsor the bill and vote for it. 4. Extraordinary Relief – Court a) Pursuing court case and having a judge decide. 5. Memorandum of Understanding – MPO & Utah County a) Identify how the two entities are going to function and agree among elected officials or members of MPO and commission on policies and procedures. In the memo from PAMH Law, it was suggested that the MPO could request an amendment to the applicable statute by the State Legislature. One potential revision to 59-12-2218(5) could be the following: 59-12-2218(5): A county, city, or town legislative body may not shall expend revenue collected within a county, city or town from a tax under this section only for a purpose described in Subsections (4)(b) through (f) after the purpose is recommended by: (a) For a county that is part of a metropolitan planning organization, the metropolitan planning organization of which the county is a part: or (b) For a county that is not part of a metropolitan planning organization, the council of governments of which the county is a part. Andrew Jackson suggested changing the word recommended to approved. Mayor Brunst asked if county, town or city could pass their own quarter cent sales tax. Andrew Jackson said it is not available to cities and towns in Utah County because it has already been adopted county-wide. Mayor Brunst recommended to first go through the MPO Finance Committee to have a discussion with the County. If those discussions are not successful, go to the Utah State Tax Commission for a ruling. Commissioner Ivie would prefer a legislative fix on this matter because the word in question is recommend as it needs clarification from the legislature. If the actions of the MPO Regional Planning Committee is to be binding, then the legislature needs to change the verbiage to make it binding, or make it a recommending body to the legislative body of the County, then the County would have discretion to approve or not approve particular projects on the list as the acting legislative body. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee September 7, 2017 Page 7 Andrew Jackson clarified that at the August 3, 2017 meeting he said that the County Commission holds an administrative, executive and legislative roll and the city councils do not, when in fact city councils do have an administrative roll. If the legislative verbiage changes from recommended to approved, it puts the county into an administrative roll rather than a legislative roll. Andrew Jackson recommended a hybrid by having a discussion collectively as the County MPO and Commission, and proposed a modification that is mutually agreed on. Commissioner Lee noted that their attorneys did not receive the letter from MAG’s attorney until today and requested time to review it to give recommendations. Mayor Brunst recommended that the Utah State Tax Commission be involved in discussions between the MPO and Commission on the matter as an advisor. Andrew Jackson will inquire about having John Valentine and Rebecca Rockwell with the State Tax Commission act as an advisor to the meetings. Mayor Gygi recommended all avenues be pursued at the same time to better utilize time. Commissioner Lee asked for clarification. Mayor Gygi restated the remedies suggested by the attorney. Mayor Brunst recommended that the MPO do not seek a private letter ruling, and do not seek a petition of rulemaking until there is a general meeting of the MPO Finance Committee that involves a member of the Tax Commission and legislature as those are actions that will have serious implications. In addition, the MPO Finance Committee will report back to the MPO Regional Planning Committee as to the direction they are going via email. Mayor Clyde asked if the County Commission is going to take a different approach by approving recommendations made in the TIP, and then decide they are a legislative body and choose which projects get done. Mayor Clyde said if that is the case, then the MPO needs to move forward on pursuing all avenues to get the issue resolved. Commissioner Lee stated that the Utah County Commission has always been supportive of TIP recommendations that have gone through the Technical Advisory Committee. However, the BRT and free-fare zones puts them on edge. Going forward, he does not see this type of issue coming up again. Mayor Clyde asked if another project comes up that the Utah County Commission doesn’t agree with, will they take the same stance. Lee stated that they may. Mayor Clyde stated again that all avenues be pursued to avoid future conflict. Commissioner Lee agreed. Mayor Brunst moved that the MPO Finance Committee discuss the issue of the third quarter sales tax; that the MPO do not seek a private letter ruling, and do not seek a petition of rulemaking; and that the MPO Finance Committee report back to the MPO Regional Planning Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee September 7, 2017 Page 8 Committee as to the direction they are going. Commissioner Lee seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Commissioner Ivie commented on Mayor Clyde’s statement regarding the Utah County Commission taking a different approach. He recommended addressing the issue by clarifying the law and giving the Utah County Commission the capacity to override projects in the event they are faced with scenarios such as this, that may be controversial and have negative public support to avoid issues again in the future. Mayor Clyde asked Commissioner Ivie if the MPO wanted to change the law, would the Utah County Commission be supportive, whereas now they want to keep their legislative capability, giving the commission the right to veto projects. Mayor Clyde gaged the support by raise of hands which Mayors approve of the County Commission having vetoing power. No Mayors raised their hand. Mayor Clyde said it shows there is not support for the County to have vetoing power, and if taken to a legislative level, the MPO would be in favor of having the MPO be the deciding vote, not the Utah County Commission. Mayor Gary Gygi said there is precedence for this discussion and gave an example of a project that has been held up for years in Cedar Hills because former Commissioner, Larry Ellertson believed he had a right to veto it and it carried through. Mayor Gygi is concerned that other cities will be effected as well. Commissioner Lee objected to that comment stating that he feels it is not an accurate statement, and former Commissioner, Larry Ellertson is not present to defend himself.
