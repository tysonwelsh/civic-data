RESOLUTION 22-126: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO ENTER INTO A
MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING WITH OTHER AGENCIES TO
CREATE THE SALT LAKE VALLEY REGIONAL PUBLIC ORDER UNIT
Wayne Pyle, City Manager, discussed proposed Resolution 22-126 that would authorize
the City to Enter into a Memorandum of Understanding with Other Agencies to Create the Salt
Lake Valley Regional Public Order Unit.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       All Parties to this MOU have a Public Protection Unit or Public Order Unit (POU)
       concerned with preserving the public peace, preventing crime, arresting offenders,
       and protecting the rights of persons and property. The Parties desire that the POUs
       work as a team pursuant to this MOU under appropriate circumstances for the
       purpose of obtaining maximum efficiency in cooperative law enforcement
       operations.

       All parties are authorized to enter into this MOU pursuant to Utah Code § 11-13-
       202, Agreements for Joint or Cooperative Undertaking.

       Local law enforcement agencies encountering recent civil unrest determined that
       resources were a primary concern. Established protocol for collective efforts was
       not in place and requests for assistance came in emergency situations and lacked
       proper planning and coordination. Establishment of the SLVR POU addresses these
       concerns and provides the ability for participating agencies to identify protocols for
       training, personnel, equipment, transportation, communication, and other
       resources.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Pro Tem Huynh there were no further questions from members of
the City Council, and he called for a motion.

       Councilmember Whetstone moved to approve Resolution 22-126.

       Councilmember Christensen seconded the motion.

Mayor Pro Tem Huynh asked how long the City has been part of this unit. Wayne replied
that this is a new unit. Mayor Pro Tem Huynh asked who will operate the unit. Wayne
replied that if there were an event where a City needed backup, the unit would respond in
that jurisdiction at their request.

A.   CONSIDER APPLICATION S-2-2022, FILED BY MIKE CHAMBERS,
     REQUESTING FINAL PLAT APPROVAL FOR THE KENT BOOSTER
     SUBDIVISION LOCATED AT 4408 SOUTH 4800 WEST
     Wayne Pyle, City Manager, discussed Application S-2-2022, Filed by Mike
     Chambers, Requesting Final Plat Approval for the Kent Booster Subdivision
     Located at 4408 South 4800 West.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:

            Mike Chambers with Hansen Allen and Luce Inc., representing Granger Hunter
            Improvement District, is requesting final approval for the Kent Booster
            Subdivision. The purpose for the subdivision is to configure property lines that
            will be consistent with ownership of existing water tanks between Granger
            Hunter Improvement District and Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District.

            Granger-Hunter Improvement District is in the process of acquiring one of
            Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District’s 5MG tanks and is currently
            soliciting bids to replace the existing booster pump station at 4404 South
            4800 West.

            As part of the tank purchase and upcoming pump station construction,
            Granger Hunter is proposing to acquire from Jordan Valley Water
            Conservancy District, the parcel on which the tank is currently located, as
            well as additional property for tank piping. This acquisition requires that
            the existing property be subdivided.

            The proposed subdivision is gained from a single access point from 4800
            West. Because Jordan Valley still has utilities on their two parcels, both
            Granger Hunter and Jordan Valley will enter into arrangements granting
            permanent access and various easements in and through the property to
            operate and maintain said utilities and appurtenances.

      Upon inquiry by Mayor Pro Tem Huynh there were no further questions from
      members of the City Council, and he called for a motion.

            Councilmember Harmon moved to approve Application S-2-2022

B.     CONSIDER APPLICATION SD-3-2022, FILED BY DAI UTAH,
       REQUESTING FINAL PLAT APPROVAL FOR THE DECKER LANE
       PARTIAL STREET DEDICATION LOCATED AT 2860 SOUTH 2050
       WEST
       Wayne Pyle, City Manager, discussed Application SD-3-2022, Filed by DAI Utah,
       Requesting Final Plat Approval for the Decker Lane Partial Street Dedication Located
       at 2860 South 2050 West


       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              Joe Salisbury, representing DAI Utah, is requesting a partial street
              dedication for a portion of Decker Lane. The property to be dedicated is
              part of the H2O Lofts development and is located at approximately 2860
              South 2050 West

              The H2O development received conditional use approval from the Planning
              Commission in March 2022. During the City’s review of the site plan, it
              was determined that additional right-of-way was needed on the east side of
              Decker Lane.

              Decker Lane was dedicated in 2003 as part of the Decker Lane and Decker
              Lake Boulevard Extension plat. When this right-of-way was dedicated,
              public improvements included curb and gutter on both sides of the street
              and sidewalk on one side.

              However, with new residential development going in east of the existing
