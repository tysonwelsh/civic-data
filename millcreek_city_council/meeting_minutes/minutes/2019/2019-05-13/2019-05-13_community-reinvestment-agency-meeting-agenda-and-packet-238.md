1. Public Hearing on FY 2018-19 Community Reinvestment Agency Fund Tentative
Budget
Laurie Johnson said the Community Reinvestment Agency would be processing two
budgets simultaneously because the funds were recently received from Salt Lake County.
The FY 2018-19 Tentative budget is projected to be $982,069 while the FY 2019-20
Tentative Budget is projected to be $505,000.

Chair Silvestrini said that normally budgets are adopted before the fiscal year begins, but
since there was no money, the Board was doing now to be transparent. The revenue comes
to the Community Reinvestment Agency from tax increment, the additional taxes the area
generated since the formation of the community reinvestment area, not taxes from
Millcreek residents other than those in the redevelopment area. He said by virtue of the
other taxing entities letting the Community Reinvestment Agency keep the money for
twenty years to improve that area, it will help make them more money in the long run.

Chair Silvestrini opened the public hearing. There were no comments.

3. Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-06, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement with Central Utah Water Conservancy District for the
Millcreek Center Community Reinvestment Project Area
Chair Silvestrini said items 3-9 are interlocal cooperative agreements with other
governmental entities. He said the Agency requested agreements with all the taxing
entities that collect taxes in the community reinvestment area to allow the Agency to
receive the increased taxes that these properties generate by virtue of the redevelopment,
so any increased tax revenue will come into the Community Reinvestment Agency budget.
The Agency must spend 20% on affordable housing in Millcreek and the remaining 80%
will be spent on improvements and paying back the bond (previously discussed in the
Council meeting). The other entities have already given their consent to the agreements.
Mike Winder said the agreements have all been approved. He said the entities trust
Millcreek to help grow the tax base.

Chair Silvestrini asked for public comment. There were no comments.

10. Discussion of Tax Increment Financing (TIF) for Opus Green Project in West
Millcreek Community Development Area

Chair Silvestrini said the City Council approved a rezone of this area from manufacturing to
mixed use in contemplation of another development, Millcreek Commons, which included
apartments, a park, and a bridge over Big Cottonwood Creek. That project fell through due to
lack of funding and a new project is in the application process. Mike Winder showed the
Board an aerial map of the project area. Chair Silvestrini said the proposed plan has retail
frontage on Main Street and owner-occupied townhomes. He said the advantage of this
project over the other project is home ownership. Winder said there will be a bridge over Big
Cottonwood Creek. He showed a proposed site plan with 144 units. He said Opus Green will
build a Creekside Trail and Park and give it to Millcreek. The 20% open space required by
code would be 1.43 acres of the 7.14 total site area acreage with a total of 2.80 acres of
proposed open space with 1.15 acres for the park. The developers would build the park and
give it back to Millcreek and be reimbursed on the cost of it with the property tax increment
they produce over a 12-year carry period with a 6.5% interest rate. Winder said that interest
rate was based on the developer’s cost.

Winder went over how the tax increment financing (TIF) reimbursement would be
calculated. The property taxes will be paid in a given year minus property taxes in 2019.
Twenty percent will be subtracted from that sum (since this CDA has an 80% pass through to
the agency and 20% must go to the taxing entities, per the original CDA agreement). This
gives Millcreek the TIF pass-through amount. Four percent of the TIF pass-through amount
must go to the agency for administration, per the original CDA agreement. Twenty percent of
the TIF pass-through amount must go to the Salt Lake County Affordable Housing Fund, per
the original CDA agreement. He mentioned that the affordable housing fund goes through the
County because it was created through the County, but other city CRAs affordable housing
components will be spent in Millcreek. Twenty-five percent of the TIF pass-through amount
shall remain with the agency for future public improvements in the CRA. The remaining
51% of the TIF pass-through shall go to Opus Green as reimbursement for their park

Winder said Millcreek would get a new 1.15-acre park along Big Cottonwood Creek paid for
