PUBLIC HEARING: JUNE 12, 2018 — 5:40 P.M.-RESOLUTION NO. 2018-14 — Authorizing
Conveyance of Real Property — Parcel 05-034-0023, Logan (Factory) — Attorney Edwards
explained the county was a sponsor of a canal project and this property did not show an owner
of record so the county filed a notice of quit claim deed. Adjacent property owners (Nelson Bros.
— The Factory) own both sides of the canal and have requested the county transfer whatever
interest it has to them. This relieves the county of any liability on the property. Assessor
Howell could not place a value on this gap property. The conveyance will clear up title
questions and avoid potential liability on property the county cannot control. County Recorder
Gleed is in favor of the action.

Chairman Erickson opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Joe Harding, representing Nelson Bros. (owners of the Factory apartment complex) said the parcel goes
through the complex and easements remain in place. The sky bridge over the covered canal area is high
enough for work access as may be needed.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Zilles to close the Public Hearing — June 12, 2018-
5:40 p.m.-Resolution No. 2018-14-Authorizing Conveyance of Real Property-Parcel
05-034-0023, Logan (Factory). White seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous,
7-0.

INITIAL PROPOSAL FOR CONSIDERATION OF ACTION

e Review/Approval of Restaurant/RAPZ Tax Committee Recommendations —
Chairman Erickson opened the issue for Council discussion. Council member Worthen
said she supports including the zoo in the RAPZ/Restaurant Tax allocation. Many
people voted for the RAPZ/Restaurant Tax because of the 10% that could be carved
out to provide funding for the zoo; however, it might be wise to award $127,000.00, as
received the last two years, rather than the $147,000.00 requested to indicate the
county is serious about deadlines. Council member Merrill noted this would open up
$30,000.00 in additional recreational funding. Vice Chairman Ward and Council
member Zilles support Worthen’s recommendation, but Zilles observed he received
calls from some who claimed they were turned down in past years when a late
application was submitted.

Council member White remarked the money is already set aside for the zoo and won't
be used for anything else, so he supports the recommendation, but strongly urged
Zootah to begin working on next year’s application now.

Chairman Erickson referred to the Awarded Funds Worksheet and asked for comments.
It was noted that based on the Utah Code Section 59-12-702(11) and Cache County
Code 3.82.050(D), the County Attorney's legal opinion is that the Cache Humane
Society does not qualify for RAPZ/Restaurant Tax funding. Council member Zilles
stated the Society provides a needed service and hoped the county can find funds from
another source for the Cache Humane Society. The Council concurred.

Council member Merrill asked what funds are still available

Denise Lindsay, Finance Office, stated funds not awarded are:
Restaurant Tax - $163,281.00
Cultural/Botanical - $43,003.00
Parks/Recreation - $7,052.00
Zoo — up to 10% - $157,651.00 and commented that any funds not used will fall
back under recreation. Restaurant Tax is for entities that promote tourism; and
municipalities also qualify for RAPZ funding.

Vice Chairman Ward suggested that Little Bloomsbury Foundation be awarded
$3,000.00 and the allocation for Utah Festival Opera and Musical Theater (UFOMT) be
increased by $25,000.00.

Chairman Erickson invited public input.

Brenda Sun, Little Bloomsbury Foundation, invited the Council to their multicultural event
September 29, 2018 and asked that the suggested $3,000.00 be doubled, if possible. They have
no overhead, no administration costs and no payroll. They are all volunteers and provide
opportunities for the community to come together.

Martha Bullock supports Little Bloomsbury Foundation’s request.

Jon Rash, Four Seasons Theater thanked the Council for the funding awarded them.

PUBLIC HEARING: JUNE 12, 2018 — 5:50 P.M.-BIRCH HOLLOW SOUTH REZONE —
Request for a rezone of 10.14 acres, also known as the Tom Pitcher Lot Split Subdivision, from
the Agricultural (A10) Zone to the Rural 2 (RU2) Zone located at 5707 North 800 West, near
Smithfield — Chris Harrild said this property is just west of Smithfield and has access from 800
West in Smithfield. Smithfield City offered no comment when contacted. The Planning
Commission recommends approval because this follows the pattern occurring with two previous
RU2 Zones nearby. Harrild’s concern is that this starts to create a buffer around cities as they
grow and needs to be addressed in the master plan. If approval of a subdivision is requested,
access other than 800 West may not be approved.

Chairman Erickson opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Brad Schumann, applicant, is seeking the rezone to create a couple of lots on the west side of the
property.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Zilles to close the Public Hearing — June 12, 2018-
5:50 p.m. Ward seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 7-0.

Vice Chairman Ward, Council member Zilles and other Council members see no compelling
reason not to rezone. Council member Zilles reminded the applicant that approval of the rezone
is not approval of a subdivision. Harrild will prepare documents for approval of the rezone to be
presented and acted on at the June 26, 2018 Council meeting.

Report on the Financial Statements

We have audited the accompanying financial statements of the governmental activities, the aggregate
discretely presented component units, each major fund, and the aggregate remaining fund information of
Cache County, Utah (the County), as of and for the year ended December 31, 2017, and the related notes
to the financial statements, which collectively comprise the County’s basic financial statements as listed
in the table of contents.

Management’s Responsibility for the Financial Statements

Management is responsible for the preparation and fair presentation of these financial statements in
accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America; this includes
the design, implementation, and maintenance of internal control relevant to the preparation and fair
presentation of financial statements that are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or
error.

Auditors’ Responsibility

Our responsibility is to express opinions on these financial statements based on our audit. We did not
audit the financial statements of Bridgerland Community Ice Arena (a component unit of North Park
Interlocal Cooperative), which represent 4 percent, 4 percent, and 5 percent, respectively, of the assets,
net position, and revenues of the aggregate discretely presented component units of the County. We also
did not audit the financial statements of Cache County Emergency Medical Service Authority, which
represent 13 percent, 12 percent, and 23 percent, respectively, of the assets, net position, and revenues of
the aggregate discretely presented component units of the County. Those statements were audited by
other auditors whose report has been furnished to us, and our opinion, insofar as it relates to the amounts
included for Bridgerland Community Ice Arena and Cache County Emergency Medical Service
Authority, is based solely on the reports of the other auditors. We conducted our audit in accordance with
auditing standards generally accepted in the United States of America and the standards applicable to
financial audits contained in Government Auditing Standards, issued by the Comptroller General of the
United States. Those standards require that we plan and perform the audit to obtain reasonable assurance
about whether the financial statements are free from material misstatement.

An audit involves performing procedures to obtain audit evidence about the amounts and disclosures in

the financial statements. The procedures selected depend on the auditor's judgment, including the
assessment of the risks of material misstatement of the financial statements, whether due to fraud or error.

operational, economic, or historical context. We and other auditors have applied certain limited

information because the limited procedures do not provide us with sufficient evidence to express an
opinion or provide any assurance.

Other Information

Our audit was conducted for the purpose of forming opinions on the financial statements that collectively
comprise the County’s basic financial statements. The supplementary information and accompanying
notes as listed in the table of contents is presented for purposes of additional analysis and are not a

RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING CONVEYANCE OF REAL PROPERTY
PARCEL NUMBER 09-059-0007

WHEREAS, the Cache County owns a small piece of real property identified by tax id number
09-059-0007 and Cache County does not have a future interest in this real property; and

WHEREAS, an adjacent property owner has expressed an interest in acquiring this property;
and

WHEREAS, the County Executive intends to execute a quitclaim deed conveyancing all right.
utle and interest in the real property described in this resolution and attached as Exhibit A to an
adjoining property owner; and

WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. Section 17-50-312 provides that the county legislative body shall
provide by ordinance, resolution, rule or regulation for the manner in which property shall be acquired,
managed, and disposed of and requires the County to provide reasonable notice of the proposed
disposition at least 14 days before the opportunity for public comment and allow an opportunity for
public comment on the proposed disposition; and

WHEREAS, prior to the disposition said Property, Cache County Ordinance 3.40.010 requires
the Cache County Council to declare said property as “Surplus Property”; and

WHEREAS Cache County Ordinances 3.40.020 and 3.40.040 establish the procedures and
protocols that must be followed prior to declaring the property as “Surplus Property” before it can be
conveyed: and

WHEREAS the Cache County Council must hold a public hearing before it can be disposed of
by conveyance to the A Fair Deal LLC; now

THEREFORE, the Cache County Council finds as follows:

I. That it is in the public interest that the subject real property be disposed of as surplus property.
In determining whether the property shall be declared surplus, the County Council has taken into
consideration:

a. Whether the county has, or anticipates that it will have, no practical, economical,
efficient or appropriate use for the property currently or in the reasonably foreseeable
future.

b. Whether the purpose served by the property can be better accomplished by other
alternatives or property.

c. Whether the purpose served by the property or its use either no longer exists or has
significantly changed because of the needs and demands of the county or as may be
determined by a change of policy evidenced by an ordinance or resolution of the county
council.

d. Whether the property is so damaged, depreciated or worn that it is inoperable or limited
in operation without repairs and the cost of such repairs is unreasonable, excessive or
impractical.

e. Whether the purposes and interests of the county would be better served by the
declaration of the property as surplus and the disposition of that property.

THEREFORE, the Cache County Council, after holding a public hearing, resolves that the
Property, better described in “Exhibit A™ shall be de
be disposed of by the County Executive as long as unpaid taxes, administrative costs and fees are

ed surplus, and that such surplus property may

recovered by executing an appropriate deed to the A Fair Deal LLC. This resolution takes effect
immediately upon adoption.
