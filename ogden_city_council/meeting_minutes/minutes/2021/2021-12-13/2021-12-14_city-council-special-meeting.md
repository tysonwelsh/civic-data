          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022
Annual Action Plan (AAP) Amendment #2 came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated as a requirement to
receive federal funding from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), the City's Community and
Economic Development Department prepares and submits to the Council what is known as a five-year Consolidated Plan
(ConPlan) and an AAP. The AAP is used to outline how federal Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) funds, HOME
funds and other federal and non-federal program funds will be used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year. When the Annual
Action Plan is submitted to HUD in May of each year, specific dollar amounts for entitlement grant funds, program income funds,
and carry-over funds are estimated for the next fiscal year. Part way through the year, after the specific dollar amounts are known
or when program income from loans and other programs are received, an amendment must be made to the plan to update the
Annual Action Plan budget. Often when the mid-year amendment is proposed, changes in funding levels within certain programs
and line items are proposed as well. The amendments proposed with this mid-year update includes recognizing program income
and carry-over funding as well as shifting CDBG-CV funding to the new Business Recovery Fund program. There is no
adjustment for entitlement funding for this budget amendment.
          An additional memo from the City Comptroller regarding a proposed budget amendment associated with the AAP Plan
amendment came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposal is to adopt the ordinance amending the
City's FY 2022 budget to reflect the changes proposed in the AAP budget to be submitted to HUD. The proposed budget
amendments total $1,722,700.
          A fmal memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to the
Business Recovery Program guidelines came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Administration is proposing
the Business Recovery Fund for use of CDBG-CV funds. The CDBG-CV funds, received as part of the 2020 COVID-19 relief-
related CARES Act, were received by the City in 2020 and included in the Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 Annual Action Plan budget
under CDBG-CV funded Microenterprise Loan and Small Business Loan programs. The initial funding was placed under these
programs, but funding was separated by the source. The proposed Business Recovery Fund would consolidate the funding
received in 2020 into one newly created program funded solely by CDBG-CV funding. Program guidelines, similar to the
Microenterprise Loan and Small Business Loan programs, are proposed to provide standards for use of the funds. The program is
anticipated to operate until the CDBG-CV funding is expended.
          Community Development Manager Ogden summarized the memos and stated Administration is proposing the Council
approve the second amendment to the FY2022 Annual Action Plan budget. The proposed budget amendment reflects changes
resulting in an overall increase of $1,722,695 in funds for FY2022 over the previously adopted FY2022 Annual Action Plan
budget. This total includes an increase in adjusted program income and carry-over funds. The majority of the budget changes with
this amendment include reconciling actual program income and carry-over funding that was estimated for the remainder of the
FY2021 fiscal year. One other significant change proposed is the consolidation of the CDBG-CV funded Microenterprise Loan
Fund and Small Business Loan programs to the newly proposed Business Recovery Fund. This change is specific to the programs
funded from CARES Act funding related to COVID-19 relief; the non-CBDG-CV funded Microenterprise Loan program and
Small Business Loan program remain funded with CDBG funding.
          Business Information Center Manager Stoffers briefly expounded in the intent of the Business Recovery Program; she
reviewed the program guidelines and the requirements for applicants to receive funding. The Program is replacing the CV Small
Business Loan Program and the Microenterprise Loan Program. The program is intended to allow applicants to respond to,
prevent, or prepare for the COVID-19 pandemic; the goal of the program is to retain five full time equivalent jobs, assist five low-
moderate income micro enterprise owners, and provide five urgent loans to businesses in need. She summarized eligible uses for
the funds, concluding the minimum loan amount will be $5,000 and the maximum will be $90,000.

Proposed Resolution 2021-20 authorizing Administration to renew a loan
agreement with Synchrony Bank, to provide funds for Community
Development Programs
          A memo from Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
renewing the Synchrony Bank Line of Credit. The memo stated as a part of the Annual Action Plan and the City's Administration
of Community Development Block Grant funding, program guidelines are developed for specific programs targeting specific
needs. The program guidelines serve as general parameters for how the city will use funding for that particular program. These
programs guidelines are referred to in the Annual Action Plan but are entirely developed and adopted by the City. Amending the
program guidelines is done through a resolution adopted by the City Council. The current proposal is not an amendment to any
program guideline; rather, it is the renewal of a line of credit the City uses to supplement the funding of community development
programs. The terms for the Synchrony Bank line of credit of $1.4 million remain the same as previously adopted.
          Council member Hyer asked if this program is for profit. Community Development Manager Ogden indicated that it is
not intended to be revenue generating, but sometimes the City does realize a profit upon the resale of a home that has been
rehabilitated. The intent of the program is to simply make homes livable again.
          Council member Stephens asked if the sales price of individual homes are dictated by the market. Mr. Ogden answered
yes; homes are sold for appraised value, which is market driven.

                    A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed zone text amendment
         pertaining to the Capitol Square Project came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Redevelopment Agency
         (RDA) is proposing to rezone the block where the old Rite Aid building used to be located. If approved, this rezone would place
         the whole block under the Mixed Use (MU) zone and would allow for multiple types of land uses within the same zone (i.e.,
         residential and commercial). This zone text amendment is specific to the Capitol Square site and will not apply to other MU zones
         in the City. The ordinance outlines specific land uses and design standards that will apply to the site. The purpose of the zone text
         amendment is to provide a mixed-use development that is designed to be complimentary to the historic community in which it is
         located.
                    An additional memo regarding the proposed zone change for property between Monroe Boulevard and Quincy A venue,
         and 24 111 and 25th Streets came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the subject property is owned by the RDA
         and was once the location of the Rite Aid building. The purpose of the rezone is to allow for a variety of uses within the same
         zone, including townhomes, apartments, single-family residences, commercial, entertainment, and office uses. Mixed Use zones
         also allow for multiple use-types to be incorporated within the same building (e.g., retail on ground level and residential on each
         level above). In addition to considering the rezone request, the zoning code requires the City Council to simultaneously review
         and approve a project master plan. Reviewing the master plan ensures that the developer follows design and layout standards
         established in zoning code 15-39-4. The transmittal included two concept plans for the Council to review. Concept 1 includes a
         grocery store, office development, residential development, open space, and pedestrian ways. Concept 2 is similar, but the grocery
         store would be replaced with mansion townhomes on 24 111 Street. Both would provide a similar number of dwelling units, 358 vs.
         3 61, respectively. The Planning Commission made it clear that Concept 1, with the grocery store, should be a higher priority than
         Concept2.
                    Deputy Planning Manager Brierly summarized the memo and stated the City Council considered this zone text
 )       amendment during a meeting on December 7, 2021. There was some confusion surrounding how to approve the concept plans in
         the Capitol Square rezone ordinance, and because this zone text amendment is directly related to the Capitol Square project, it was
         decided to table both items until this meeting so the Council could get some clarification on the concept plans. The issue at hand is
         that the Planning Commission recommended approval of Concept 1 and requested that the Council return Concept 2 to the
         Planning Commission if the Council decided that was the concept they wanted to use. The solution recommended by Planning
         staff is that the Council adopt Concept 1 if they feel that is the better concept. If the grocery store proves to be unviable, then the
         Concept Plan would return to the Planning Commission and City Council-by default-for an amendment. Additionally, Concept
         1 was not originally included as an attachment to the rezone ordinance, but that has since been fixed. Mr. Brierly concluded that
         each phase of the development will need to be completed in accordance with the overall concept plan for the project; no phase can
         be developed that will preclude the next phase of the project.

Petition 2021-8 to allow brick homes to be painted in the East Central
Community
          A memo from Council staff regarding a petition to allow brick homes to be painted in the East Central Community came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Planning Commission forwarded a recommendation that the City
Council deny the petitioner's request to allow painted brick in the East Central Community. However, they felt that it may be
import;ant to allow brick to be painted in exceptional circumstances. According to their recommendation, brick homes could be
painted after City review and analysis that proves that painting the brick or applying a clear seal coat is the only method to
preserve the brick from further erosion. On December 7, 2021 the City Council voted to adopt the proposed ordinance. However,
due to an administrative error, the Council did not vote to deny the petition. The Planning Commission recommended denying the
petition, and they recommended adopting the ordinance. The Council must decide whether to deny the petition and continue with
the newly adopted ordinance, or they may approve the petition and direct that a new ordinance be drafted to allow painted brick.
          COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED TO DENY PETITION 2021-8 TO ALLOW PAINTED BRICK IN
