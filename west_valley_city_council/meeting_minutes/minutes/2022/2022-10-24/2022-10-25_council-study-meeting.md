RESOLUTIONS SCHEDULED FOR OCTOBER 25, 2022

     RESOLUTION 22-152: AUTHORIZE THE PURCHASE OF PROTECTIVE
     EQUIPMENT FROM CURTIS FIRE
     John Evans, Fire Chief, presented proposed Resolution 22-152 that would authorize the
     purchase of protective equipment from Curtis Fire.

     Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
     follows:

            West Valley City Fire Department has been operating out of
            Surviair/Sperian/Honeywell SCBA and air support for the last three decades. In
            February 2020, Honeywell International notified vendors, and fire departments that
            they are discontinuing their fire service line of SCBA equipment. Without support
            and the ability to purchase the necessary parts to maintain our current equipment,
        MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – OCTOBER 25, 2022
                               -5-


           West Valley City Fire Department will need to move to another brand.

           Being surround by all valley fire departments using MSA equipment, we are
           requesting to move to MSA breathing equipment. The interoperability of having
           the same SCBA as surrounding departments will be beneficial from a safety
           standpoint and interoperability for all types of incidents we respond on.

           A competitive RFP was sent out for the equipment. Curtis Fire was the lowest bid
           received at $584,870.07 and they are also on Utah State contract purchasing.
           Weidner Fire submitted the higher bid at $585,972.00.

    Councilmember Christensen asked if this equipment can be obtained soon. Chief Evans
    replied that the City should be able to receive the packs in 8-12 weeks. He indicated that
    MSA is a longstanding company that had its roots in mining but has expanded to
    firefighting. Councilmember Christensen asked if retraining is necessary. Chief Evans
    replied yes but noted that it would not be extensive. Mayor Lang asked if everyone had
    thermal imaging before. Chief Evans replied that every truck does have a camera but this
    equipment will allow firefighters to see dangerous hot spots more easily and quickly.

    The City Council will consider Resolution 22-152 at the Regular Council Meeting
    scheduled October 25, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

             proposed development is bordered on the north by the Westhampton PUD and

             In addition, 24 guest spaces are provided which brings the parking ratio to
             4.75 spaces per unit. Access to the Church and subdivision will be gained
             from a private street. This project is one of 3 pending developments in the
             City who are vested with private streets. All interior streets comply with
             required widths and turnaround capability for the Fire Department. A
             condition of approval for this project requires that all streets will be
             inspected by a third party to ensure they are constructed appropriately.

  Councilmember Christensen stated that he has heard citizens complain about
  receiving citations and having to come into compliance within 5 days. He indicated
  that he received a citation for his home and he was given 2 weeks. Layne replied
  that the amount of time given would depend on the citation. Councilmember
  Christensen stated that the notice he received was not clear and he had a difficult
  time understanding what he needed to fix to come into compliance. He indicated
  that his definition of a violation did not align with that of the officer who issued
  him the courtesy notice. Layne agreed and noted that everyone has a slightly
  different perspective on what a violation is. Councilmember Christensen asked why
  there was in increase in notices being sent the last few months. Layne replied that
  officers were encouraged to review their beats to ensure residents were in
  compliance. He noted that the Administrative Law Judge is in place to ensure that
  difference of opinions between officers and residents are fairly addressed.
  Councilmember Christensen stated that when he experienced receiving a notice, he
  felt there was a lack of communication from the City. He noted that he believes that
  this is where some of the problems are coming from with residents complaining
  and added that officers are unwilling to work with citizens. Layne stated that it is
  easier to work with residents than to not. He indicated it does go two ways. If the
  City does too much enforcement, there are unhappy residents and if the City does
  not do enough enforcement, there are unhappy residents. He indicated that it’s
  important to try and find a good balance.

  Councilmember Huynh stated that he wants residents to comply with rules and
  regulations. He noted that there has been a trailer in the Chesterfield area where

  be more aggressively resolved by the City than other more minor violations.
  Councilmember Huynh stated that he has heard complaints about Code
  Enforcement officers being rude to residents and unwilling to work with them on
  resolving their issues. He indicated that residents are already frustrated about
  receiving tickets.

  that a balance needs to be found between allowing residents to do what they wish
  with their space and also maintaining nice neighborhoods. Councilmember Harmon
  stated that there is a lot that needs to be done to improve the Code Enforcement
  process including transparency, clarity for both citizens and staff, and open
  communication. Councilmember Harmon stated that too often officers are
