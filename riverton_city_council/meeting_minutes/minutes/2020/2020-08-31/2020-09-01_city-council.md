   a. Salt lake County Recorder’s Office “Property Watch” Service

Rashelle Hobbs, Salt Lake County Recorder, via remote, announced the launch of their new
service “Property Watch” and how it would significantly reduce title fraud, further protecting
people’s property ownership, and promoting transparency for constituents. She said that
citizens could register for the service on the Salt Lake Recorder’s Website. Ms. Hobbs then
addressed questions from Council Members.

Mayor Staggs spoke of CARES Act funding and said that Riverton City received approximately
1.3 million dollars of which approximately $400,000 had been used. He said he gave explicit
direction to staff to find any and all ways the City could expend all the money received. He said
it was recommended that the funds be used for law enforcement, which if done, would build a
fund balance that would enable RLESA to change their budget year from a calendar to a fiscal
year.

City Attorney Ryan Carter explained that in accordance with the Treasury’s Coronavirus Relief
Fund Guidance, the Fund was designed to provide ready funding to address unforeseen
financial needs and risks created by the COVID-19 public health emergency. For this reason a
local government may presume that payroll costs for public health and public safety employees
were payments for services substantially dedicated to mitigating or responding to the COVID-19
public health emergency. He also said that costs incurred for a “substantially different use”
include, but were not necessarily limited to, costs of personnel and services that were budgeted
for in the most recently approved budget but which, due entirely to the COVID-19 public health
emergency, have been diverted to substantially different functions. Mr. Carter said that
generally, law enforcement has presumed to have had all of their duties shift as a result of
COVID. He said that it makes for a very clean audit if you just take the payroll for law
enforcement generally and assign the CARES Act dollars to it.

6. Discussion Items

    a. Utilization of Park Space

Sheril Garn, Parks & Public Services Director, explained that a Council Member had asked staff
to research the construction of additional pickle ball courts within the City. Ms. Garn then asked
about the philosophy of the City Council regarding the placement of pickle ball courts, whether
they be added to the Main City Park for citizen recreation and tournament use or if additional
pickle ball courts should be constructed throughout the City. She proposed that two pickle ball
courts be built at four various locations away from the Main Park. She said she had proposed
that four new pickle ball courts, a tennis court and moving the volleyball court in the Main Park
be included in the current year budget in the amount of $500,000. However, she said that as a
result of the COVID-19 Pandemic those improvements were removed from the budget.

Council Member McDougal spoke in favor of scattering pickle ball courts throughout the City in
order to help address traffic issues at one location. He also said that the residents were paying
for the amenities so they should be able to use them in their neighborhoods rather than using
them for tournament play. He also asked about funding since they were removed from the
current year budget.

Council Member Wells spoke of the popular use of pickle ball courts. He then inquired about the
cost of the courts, along with the lights. He then spoke in favor of constructing pickle ball courts
at various locations within the City.

Council Member McCay favored construction of pickle ball courts at various locations within the
City but constructing four at each location rather than two.

Council Member Stewart reminded the Council Members that the initial cost of the pickle ball
project in the Main Park was approximately $500,000 and said he was concerned about
spending that amount of money while broadband infrastructure was being overlooked. He said
the need for infrastructure was a higher priority for him since he had been working from home
as a result of the COVID-19 Pandemic. He encouraged the Council Members to relook at the
strategic priorities prior to making a decision.

Council Member Buroker spoke in favor of constructing pickle ball courts within different
communities within the City, which she said would enhance the “neighborhoodness” within the
City. She said it would also be good for the residents to see tax benefits and value within their
neighborhoods.

Mayor Staggs said he would like to review the strategic priorities in the near future. In regards to
the pickle ball courts, he said he was concerned about the costs and he deemed the broadband

Salt Lake County grant funding options were discussed regarding pickle ball courts such as

15. Public Hearing(s) / Action Items

    a. Ordinance No. 20-15 - Proposed Amendments to the Riverton City Code, Section
       18.145.100, Updating Terminology Regarding Accessible Parking

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained a proposed change in the Zoning
Text proposed by Riverton City Staff. He said the proposed change was to a very specific place
in the Code and was intended to rectify inconsistencies and update the Code to current
standards and terminology. He said that Section 18.145.100 of Riverton City Code contained
requirements for “Disabled Parking”. He said that, not only was the terminology outdated, so
were several of the standards it listed. In some instances the Code calls to ADA requirements
but lists an incorrect or outdated standard. The proposed change updated the wording to reflect
current terminology, by replacing “Disabled” and “Handicap” with the term “Accessible” or
“Accessible Parking”, which are standard terms for referring to ADA Accessible Parking. The
Planning Commission recommended approval of the proposed amendments.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that on March 12, 2020, the Planning Commission held a public
hearing to review the proposed text change. The Planning Commission, following their review,
voted to recommend approval of the proposed change and staff concurred with that
recommendation.

    b. Ordinance No. 20-16 - Proposed Amendments to Riverton City Code, Section
       18.150.110(2), Allowing Additional Monument Signs for Business with Multiple
       Frontages

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that Monument signs were
regulated by Riverton City Code 18.150 “Sign Regulation Ordinance”. He said the table of sign
standards described the maximum area, height, density, spacing and set-back for monument
signs. He said the density for monument signs limited the approval to “one for sites having only
one business” and directed other monument signs to obtain permission from the Planning
Commission. He said the language excluded a monument sign on a property with a multitenant
building, and did not make a provision for a commercial property on a corner or double fronting
lot. He said the proposed changes to the monument sign standards applied standards similar to
those for pole signs, in particular each eligible development is allowed one sign regardless if the
development was a single user or a multi-tenant building. Additionally, a second monument sign

    c. Ordinance No. 20-17 - Proposed Amendments to Riverton City Code, Section
       18.150, Sign Ordinance, Clarifying Language and Updating Zoning Lists

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that signs or signage in Riverton
were governed by Chapter 18.150 “Sign Regulation Ordinance”. The chapter was adopted in
2008 with several revisions and clarifications since. The ordinance sets out standards for each
type of sign, and designates which type of sign is allowed in each zone. He said the proposed
amendment was for correcting contradictions and deficiencies in the ordinance but does not
alter the existing character or design requirements in the code. The most significant modification
is to the “Table of Permitted Signs” (see 18.150.110). The proposed changes adds the RM-6
(Residential Multi-Family 6du/acre) and PCC (Planned Commercial Center) Zones to the table.
He said the RM-6 zone signage was assigned the same permissions as the RM-8 Zone. The
PCC Zone signage was assigned the same permissions as the CR (Commercial Regional)
Zone. Also, in the Table permissions for “Bus Bench” were removed to be consistent with the
text in 18.150.120 (2). The amendment also removed a restriction that limited certain monument
signs to only a business name and address, but did not refer to specific zone. He said that the
Planning Commission recommended approval of the proposed changes, and staff concurred
with that recommendation.

Mr. Lethbridge said that on May 14, 2020, the Planning Commission held a public hearing to
review the text change. The Planning Commission, following their review, voted to recommend
approval of the proposed change.

Mayor Staggs opened a Public Hearing and called for public comment; there being none, he
declared the Public Hearing closed.

Council Member Stewart MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 20-17 -
Proposed Amendments to Riverton City Code, Section 18.150, Sign Ordinance, Clarifying

    d. Ordinance No. 20-18 - Proposed Amendments to Riverton City Code, Section
       18.10.010, Updating the Zoning Districts List

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained a change in the Zoning Text
proposed by Riverton City Staff. He said the proposed change was to a very specific place in
the code, and was intended to rectify inconsistencies in the code. Section 18.10.010 of Riverton
City Code contains a list of the zones found in the ordinance. He said that it appeared that in the
past the list was not consistently updated as zoning ordinances were adopted or repealed. In
some cases the list was updated, for instance, newer zones like the PCC and RM-6 appeared
on the list. In other cases, by oversight or clerical error, the list was not updated, for instance the
RM-14 zone does not appear on the list. He said the proposed change simply removes zoning
designations from the list, if the zoning designation no longer exists in the ordinance, and adds
zoning designations to the list if the zone exists in the code. The Planning Commission
recommended approval of the amendment, and staff concurred with that recommendation.

Mr. Lethbridge said that on March 12, 2020, the Planning Commission held a public hearing to
review the text change. The Planning Commission, following their review, voted to recommend
approval of the proposed change.

Mayor Staggs opened a Public Hearing and called for public comment; there being none, he
declared the Public Hearing closed.
