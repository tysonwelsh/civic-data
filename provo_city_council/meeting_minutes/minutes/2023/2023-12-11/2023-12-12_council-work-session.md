        Travis Ball, Energy Director, presented.

        Travis Ball discussed Fast Charging EV Rates. He said it's a win for everyone. He explained the
        purpose of the rate, the load factor, the fast charging EV TOU rate, and the code change
        requirement.

        The purpose of the new rate will encourage the adoption of people building charging stations. It
        is expensive and the rate does not support the construction. This would also protect the revenues
        of the power department. Overall, they want to see more charging stations built. There are two
        components to power: demand and energy.

        The demand for the state is 300 megawatts. The energy value is how much energy is used in one
        hour. These two factors go into power. Load factor is energy divided by demand. A low load
        factor does not have a lot of energy but a high demand.

        He showed a graph that shows two different load factors. This is important because this is a
        matter of cost.

        He said they are trying to help people who are providing fast chargers to have a charge rate that
        is economical. They have a customer right now who is paying more money to the City than they
        can charge for the electrical vehicle station. There is no incentive to have EV chargers.

        The energy only costs 4 cents. But a load factor of less than 15% is not economical for an EV
        fast charger. During peak times, they are charging 35 cents. The current rate for the current
        customer makes it so they cannot make a profit. Mr. Ball showed the proposed rates and said it
        would bring the cost to around 16 cents. It saves the customer a lot more which can then
        incentivize them to build fast chargers.

        If there are always charging cars, the cost will continue to decrease. This change rate is a bridge
        for people to build charging stations.

        In order to have the change in rate is to change the code. On 12.02.010, no one else can sell
        power except for the city. They have carved out a section for EV chargers so that people can
        charge a rate for people using those chargers.

        The Energy Board voted on this issue and recommended the new rate and change in the
        ordinance. Gary from the Energy Board emphasized the Board’s support for these rate changes.
        He said it will protect the City and others from undercharging.

        The vision of this plan is to accomplish long term water quality and management, improve safety
