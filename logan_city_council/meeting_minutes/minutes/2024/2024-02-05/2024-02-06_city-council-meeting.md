REQUEST
This code amendment makes changes to the Logan City Land Development Code
Chapters 17.11 District and Corridor Zone Uses, and 17.62 Definitions. This amendment
also includes changes to the Logan City Municipal Code (LMC) Section 5.10 Alcoholic
Beverages, but the Planning Commissions purview is to make recommendations on the
LDC portion of the amendment only. There is a considerable amount of connections
between the amendment to the LDC and the LMC, and changes to one may affect the
other. To place the amendment to the LDC within the context of the whole amendment, a
summary of the changes and review of the LMC amendment are included in this report.
The proposed amendments change the administrative procedures for alcohol licensing to
better conform with state and local laws, policies, and procedures; and makes changes to
the local restrictions regarding alcohol uses, including bars, taverns, brew restaurants,
package agencies, off-premise beer, manufacturing, and distribution.

Municipal Code 5.10
The proposed amendment includes extensive revisions to Section 5.10 within the
business licensing chapter of the municipal code specifically dealing with regulations
regarding alcohol licensing. Staff has reviewed the code section and identified extensive
redundancies, areas that do not align with State code, and areas that do not correspond to
current policies and procedures. This has resulted in a code section that is challenging to
administer and is not user friendly for staff or the public. One of the intents of the
amendment is to alleviate the administrative issues with the alcohol licensing code by
consolidating and clarifying the administrative and application process for alcohol
licensing. These changes are contained in the revised Article I. Administration, which
includes the definitions, license requirements, application and renewal process, review
process, enforcement, and appeal process. The reworked article consolidates several
sections of the previous code into a single administrative section.

Article II Local License Requirements articulates the local restrictions that go beyond
State code. This contains limits on total licenses and codes that pertain to unique alcohol
uses, such as the Brew Restaurant and Taproom. Previously, the code had extensive
redundant sections and articles for various license types that had no specific local
restrictions. Those sections have been removed or consolidated into the streamlined
Article II to make locating local restrictions easier and more intuitive.

The amendment includes several policy changes, including permitting additional taverns,
expanding manufacturing opportunities, and revising the permitted locations of bars,
taverns, and brew restaurants. The changes include the addition of a couple of new use
types, including a taproom in the TC-1 zone, and a small brewery in the CS and IP zones.
The proposal to expand the number of tavern licenses is intended to allow for innovative
products to develop in downtown. The opportunity to include manufacturing in
conjunction with a tavern license through the new taproom use will incentivize local
business and entrepreneurship. An item for the Commission to consider is whether to
generally expand tavern licenses or instead specify those licenses for taproom uses only.
This could be done by creating a unique population limit for taprooms (such as 1 license:
25,000 population), instead of increasing the total number of tavern licenses.

Changes to the Brew Restaurant include allowing it in areas where restaurants are
permitted and updating the definition of a Brew Restaurant to permit sales of beer for off-
premise sales. The change to permit off-premise sales would still require the majority of
beer manufactured to be consumed on-site. This change would allow more flexibility for
Brew Restaurants to market and distribute their products.

The proposed Taproom use aligns closely with the Brew Restaurant model and would
permit manufacturing in conjunction with a bar or tavern. As proposed, the Taproom
would be subject to the population limits of either a bar or tavern, depending on the type
of license they acquire from the State. The use would also be limited to no more than
5,000 sf gross area to limit the impacts and scope of alcohol manufacturing in downtown.

Another policy change is to remove several general prohibitions from Section 5.10 and
instead address those uses through zoning regulations. This is particularly applicable to
the current tight restrictions on manufacturing, which only allow for manufacturing as
part of a Brew Restaurant. The proposal is to expand manufacturing uses and refer to
zoning to identify the permitted location and scale of the manufacturing.

There is also a general prohibition in the current code regarding Package Agency. A
Package Agency license is a license granted by the State to allow the sale of heavy beer
and liquors that are typically required to go through the State Liquor Store system. These
licenses are closely regulated by the State and are only available in a few instances, the
most applicable being at a brewery or winery. Acquiring a Package Agency license
would allow a brewery in Logan to bottle and distribute their heavy beer products without
going through a state liquor store. The State has a proximity restriction for a Package
Agency (600’ pedestrian travel, 200’ straight line from community locations). With the
clear enumeration from the State regarding who can acquire a Package Agency license
and where they can be located, Staff supports the removal of the local prohibition.

Staff also proposes the removal of proximity restrictions for off-premise beer sales
(grocery or convenience stores). The current restriction is 600’ pedestrian travel or 200’
straight line from a community location such as a park or school. The State no longer
restricts the proximity of off-premise beer sales and staff’s recommendation is to follow
suit with the State and remove the local restriction. Eliminating the restriction will make

it easier for neighborhood-serving markets and convenience stores to find suitable
locations to develop.

There is a brief reference to homebrew in the current code addressing alcohol content.
Homebrew has an extensive State regulation that is completely adequate to regulate home
brewing. Staff recommends removing the local restriction and yielding to the State
regulations for homebrew. State regulations include the total volume that can be brewed
(100 gallons per individual), sets whom and where the alcohol can be consumed, and
regulates the sale of the alcohol.

Land Development Code 17.11 & 17.62
Proposed changes to the Land Development Code address alcohol use as it pertains to
zoning. 17.62 Definitions have been updated to consolidate all alcohol-related definitions
into one area and add definitions for a Bar, Small Brewery, Brewery, Distillery, and
Winery.

Chapter 17.11 District and Corridor Zone Uses is updated to include both the new uses of
Taproom, Small Brewery, and Brewery, Distillery, or Winery, as well as address
permitted zones for the various alcohol uses. The retail use changes include limiting the
locations of bars and taverns. Bars would no longer be permitted in the TC-2, MU, COM,
CS, and IP. Removal from the TC-2 and MU zones is to reduce conflict between bars and
zones that are primarily residential. Removal from the COM, CS, and IP is to remove
bars from zones that are primarily car-oriented. An exception in the use chart has been
carved out to allow bars in conjunction with a manufacturing location in the CS or IP
zones, or within a hotel. These changes are similar for a Tavern, other than no exception
for a hotel. It is also proposed to change taverns and bars from permitted to conditionally
permitted in the TC-1 zone.

The Brew Restaurant Use is currently limited to only the TC-1 zone. This was a
deliberate decision made at the adoption of the use in 2008 to help attract a Brew
Restaurant to downtown. Staff’s position is that expanding the Brew Restaurant zoning to
include other areas where restaurants are located may incentivize additional development
of innovative restaurant options. The proposal is to allow a Brew Restaurant in the COM
and MU zones.

Regarding new uses, the Taproom would be conditionally permitted in the TC-1 zone,
which aligns with proposed change to bar and tavern uses. The Small Brewery use would
be permitted in the IP zone and conditionally permitted in the CS zone. The small
brewery would be limited to 15,000 barrels per year and no more than 10,000 sf. Staff
found that the 15,000 barrels a year is a standard dividing scale between large and small
breweries within the industry. Staff had included a land use for a Brewery, Distillery, or
Winery, but is proposing that the city start small with manufacturing and not permit
larger scale manufacturing in any zone at this time. Staff recommends keeping the
Brewery, Distillery, or Winery use listed in the use table even if it is not permitted to
clarify that larger breweries would not fall under other manufacturing uses in the land use
table.
