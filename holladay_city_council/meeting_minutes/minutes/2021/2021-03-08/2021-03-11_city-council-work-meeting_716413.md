II.     2021 Legislative Wrap-Up – Dave and Ashley Spatafore.
Mayor Dahle introduced Dave and Ashley Spatafore. Ms. Spatafore described the process that took
place and stated that it had been a fairly good legislative session. There were a couple of bills that
passed that were not ideal, such as HB 82, which related to accessory dwelling units (“ADU”), and
HB 98, the local government building regulation bill. However, compared to where the session
started, Ms. Spatafore felt good about where it ended.

Ms. Spatafore discussed HB 76, which related to firearm preemption amendments. The goal of the
bill was to create a preemption so counties could not create any sort of ordinance or regulation to
require background checks. The way the bill was drafted, it would also restrict a city from
implementing a discharge of firearm ordinance. Ms. Spatafore reported that HB 76 passed the House
but stalled in the Senate.

HB 82 related to single-family housing modifications. The bill required a city to allow ADUs in
single-family residential zones. In the end, there were several things a city could regulate, such as
permits and off-street parking. Ms. Spatafore noted that HB 82 was one of the priorities for
leadership. She added that HB 82 was the best compromise cities could work out. City Attorney,
Todd Godfrey explained that HB 82 would require a city to approve internal ADUs within 75% of
the area of its primary residential zones. Mayor Dahle commented that the bill could be used to craft
an ADU ordinance for the City of Holladay. Discussions were had about regulations and affordable
housing.

HB 98 related to local government building regulations pertaining to single-family and two-family
homes. A permit application and the building review plan process needed to be done within 14 days
and the inspector needed to come out within three days. If that did not happen, a builder could hire
their own independent inspector. The other portion of the bill related to design elements. There were
certain design elements that a city could no longer require for new homes, which could impact the

overall look and feel of the city. Mr. Godfrey commented that a lot of work was done to protect areas
like the Holladay Village Zone. Council Member Fotheringham wondered if the guidelines were
limited to new home construction. Mr. Godfrey explained that it was not limited to new homes.
However, there were certain carve-outs for historic districts.

It was reported that the two billboard-related bills failed. Mr. Spatafore reported that SB 61 had been
a bigger concern due to the electronic billboards. There were discussions with Senator Scott Sandall
about the Holladay ordinance that does not allow electronic billboards. Ms. Spatafore stated that the
bill was a good example of cities working together. The ULCT and various City lobbyists worked
together to described the potential impacts of the bill.

HB 244 related to the First Class County Highway Road Funds Amendments. Ms. Spatafore reported
that this involved quarter of a quarter sales tax funds for Salt Lake County and there was distribution
County-wide. She felt it should be an equitable distribution determined by population or some other
metric but that was not how it was done. The process was very political and weighted heavily to the
southwest corner of the valley. Mr. Spatafore added that the original HB 244 allocated no money to
the City of Holladay. There were ultimately five iterations of the bill. Ms. Spatafore reported that
Holladay ended up with $300,000 for 15 years for a total of approximately $4.5 million.

Ms. Spatafore discussed other legislative item that did not pass but that we may see again in the next
session. HB 401 was a fee amendment bill that would have put additional requirements on impact
fees. SB 221 was a Short-Term Rental Amendment Bill and a contradiction to one of the negotiated
agreements in HB 82. It would have allowed short-term rentals in ADUs. SB 241 related to sales tax
distribution amendments and was pulled last minute. City Manager, Gina Chamness noted that SB
241 was troubling due to the expected population growth in the City of Holladay compared to other
communities. She felt that it put the City at a disadvantage long-term.

Mayor Dahle thanked Mr. and Ms. Spatafore for all of their hard work on behalf of the City of
Holladay during the Legislative Session.

III.   2020 Arts Council Year in Review – Sheryl Gillilan.
Ms. Gillilan noted that it had been a difficult year due to shutdowns caused by COVID-19. She
discussed the concerts that took place during the summer of 2020 and commented that a lot of positive
comments were received.

Ms. Gillilan noted that after the concerts in the summer, one of the Arts Council members came up
with the idea of a sidewalk chalk festival. It was a success and would take place in 2021 as well.
Prior to the COVID-19 shutdowns, the Arts Council was able to install track lighting in the downstairs
hallway. They were also able to host a violin concert, magic show, and a variety show.

The Arts Council plans for 2021 were outlined. The Arts Council was planning to hold eight concerts
in the park outside of City Hall with safety regulations in place. The Arts Council's recommendation
was not to do the Blue Moon Festival this year. Ms. Gillilan discussed the number of volunteers that
would be required to hold the event as well as the safety measures that would need to be implemented.
Additionally, there was still a lot of uncertainty about future restrictions.

IV.    Discussion on Summer Events.
       a.     Arts Council Events.
The Arts Council events were not discussed further.

       b.     City 4th of July Celebration.
Mayor Dahle reported that it seemed that anyone that wants a COVID-19 vaccine in Utah would be
able to have one by the end of May 2021. However, the guidelines for future events were still
uncertain.

Ms. Chamness overviewed the Summer Events Questions document included in the packet. She
reported that it included a number of questions that had been posed to other cities regarding their
summer plans. Most were in a similar place to the City of Holladay they wanted to hold events but
did not expect those events to look like they had in the past. Ms. Chamness reported that most cities
wanted to move forward with a fireworks display. Concerts, festivals, and other events scored at 50%
or higher while community breakfasts scored lower. Ms. Chamness discussed Health Department
restrictions.

The Council discussed moving forward with fireworks but not the parade or community breakfast.
Ms. Chamness commented that the 4th of July concert was still uncertain. She explained 4th of July
concerts had previously seen approximately 4,000 people. It may be difficult for residents to space
out during a holiday concert. Council Member Fotheringham pointed out that fireworks could be
observed from several locations where a concert would demand that visitors came to the park.

Mayor Dahle believed that if things start to open up, the community breakfast and parade could be
pulled together during the first part of May. He noted that a decision needed to be made about the
concert sooner rather than later. Ms. Chamness reported that the band scheduled for last year was not
available. If the City Council decided that the concert would take place, another band would need to
be scheduled. Discussions were had about deposits and scheduling dates.

Council Member Durham asked about the timeline and logistics for the fireworks. Ms. Chamness
reported that they had been using the same fireworks company for the last five or six years. Mayor
Dahle commented that they would not move forward with planning the parade or community
breakfast. Mayor Dahle suggested that the City Council wait an additional 30 days to determine if
the concert was able to take place. The Council Members agreed to revisit the idea of the 4th of July
concert in one month.

Mayor Dahle mentioned in-person City Council Meetings. He stated that the goal was to have the
first in-person meeting take place in May 2021.

V.    Continued Proposed Property Tax Discussion.
Council Member Fotheringham agreed with the recommendation made by the Citizen Advisory
Group. He noted that half measures usually end up being more expensive in the long run. Council
Member Fotheringham discussed possible percentages and provide additional context.

Mayor Dahle expressed concerns about effective communication. He believed that whatever number
is chosen, it was important to explain what that percentage actually meant to overall property tax
increases. The way the property tax increase was communicated to Holladay residents needed to be
simple and straightforward. Council Member Petersen agreed and stated that residents would want
to know how the increase will directly impact them.

Council Member Fotheringham reported that the constituents he had spoken to expected there to be
a significant increase. However, they were supportive of this as they want to see that the roads are
dealt with. Council Member Durham agreed with what had been said, particularly as it related to
communication and messaging. He felt it was important to help residents understand what the actual
impacts would be for them. He expressed concerns that he had not received a lot of engagement
about the property tax increase. Mayor Dahle noted that a notice would be sent out in July 2021 for
Truth in Taxation documentation. He believed that once those notices were received, Council
Members would start to receive additional feedback from residents.

Council Member Quinn wondered if the $300,000 per year from the Legislature would impact
property taxes. Ms. Chamness explained that this is revenue that did not need to be raised another
way. However, in terms of solving the long-term issues, it would not change the basic equation.
Mayor Dahle believed there was approximately $58 million worth of outstanding capital
improvements related to roads. The $4.5 million from legislation would be useful but would not solve
all of the outstanding issues.

VI.   Adjourn.
Council Member Fotheringham moved to adjourn. Council Member Durham seconded the motion.
The motion passed with the unanimous consent of the Council.

The City Council Meeting adjourned at approximately 6:58 p.m.
