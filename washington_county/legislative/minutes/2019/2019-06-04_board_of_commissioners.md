REPORT_AND_ CONSIDERATION OF 2019 WASHINGTON COUNTY TAX
SALE: KIM HAFEN

Washington County Clerk/Auditor Kim Hafen explained that properties with five (5) years
of delinquent property tax are subject to the tax sale. The 2019 tax sale was held on May
23, 2019 with seven (7) parcels available.

Mr. Hafen recommended rescinding the sale of three (3) parcels of property due to their
only value being a nuisance value. Each of these parcels is very small and is landscaped as
part of a larger, adjacent property or are part of an HOA parking lot. Typically, these
parcels are created by an oversight in surveying when land is purchased and/or sold.

Frank McCown, one of the winning bidders on one particular nuisance property requested
for the Commissioners not to rescind the sale.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Iverson to Accept the Recommendations
from the Clerk/Auditor Kim Hafen with Account Numbers, 913275,
615214, and 908574 Rescinded from the Sale. Motion seconded by
Commissioner Almquist and carried by unanimous vote, with
Commissioners Cox, Iverson, and Almquist voting aye.

CONSIDERATION OF THE 2019 CERTIFIED TAX RATES FOR WASHINGTON
COUNTY AND THE ROCKVILLE/SPRINGDALE FIRE PROTECTION

DISTRICT

Mr. Hafen recommended the tax rate remain the same for the Rockville/Springdale Fire
District at 0.000173 for a total budgeted revenue of $59,224.

Mr. Hafen recommended that the tax rates remain the same for the General Operations at
0.000570; Interest and Sinking Fund/Bond at 0.000075; and the library at 0.00197 for a
total budgeted revenue of $15,100,468.

Mr. Hafen explained that it is being recommended for the tax rate to decrease for the
County Assessing and Collecting levy from 0.000390 to 0.000376 for a total budgeted
revenue of $6,855,600. This is due to the County collecting sufficient funds to operate the
assessing and collecting departments.

CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. _2019-1138-O, AN ORDINANCE
ADOPTING THE INTERNATIONAL FIRE CODE AS AMENDED BY THE

STATE OF UTAH

Emergency Services Director Jason Whipple explained that the International Fire Code is
the standard used for inspection and verification of fire safety throughout the communities.

Hurricane Valley Fire Chief Tom Kuhlmann explained that the fire code is specifically
designated for safety components within any given building. There is a greater need for
these safety standards as the county grows and larger buildings are constructed.

Mr. Kuhlmann mentioned that appendices A, B, C, and D as amended by the State of Utah
should be included under Section G.

Deputy County Attorney Eric Clarke clarified that appendices B, C and D were currently
included in the ordinance, however, Appendix A should be added, which specifies the

appeals process.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Iverson to Adopt Ordinance No. 2019-1138-
O, An Ordinance Adopting the International Fire Code as Amended by
the State of Utah as well as Adding Appendix A. Motion seconded by
Commissioner Almquist and carried by unanimous vote, with
Commissioners Cox, Iverson, and Almquist voting aye.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. _R-2019-2474, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING TOURISM ADVISORY BOARD RECOMMENDATIONS

Washington County Deputy Tourism Deputy Director Brittany McMichael explained that
the Tourism Tax Advisory Board (TAB) recommended funding for several marketing and
tourism projects including: $100,000 to Zion Forever Project for a new Zion National Park
Visitor’s Center Film; $250,000 to Zion Regional Collaborative for a Zion National Park
Usage App; $100,000 to the Trust for Public Land to complete a purchase of real estate at
the entrance to the Zion Narrows; $10,000 to the St. George Chamber Business Summit
for offsetting costs of the Dixie Center; and $7,000 for Marketing of Peach Days in

Hurricane City.

Funding for these projects will be allocated from the Transient Room and Tourism Tax
(TRTT) fund.

CONSIDERATION _ OF RESOLUTION NO. _R-2019-2475, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING A CONTRACT BETWEEN WASHINGTON COUNTY AND

PROGRESSIVE CONTRACTING, INC. FOR THE PURPOSE OF COMPLETING
THE NRCS GYPSUM WASH DEBRIS BASIN PROJECT

Washington County Public Works Director Ron Whitehead explained that Progressive
Contracting, Inc. will be the contractor to complete the work for the Natural Resource
Conservation Services (NRCS) Gypsum Wash Debris Basin project. Washington County
has entered into a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with the Washington County
Flood Control Authority wherein the County will act as the administrator of the debris

basin project.
