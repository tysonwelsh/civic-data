2. Administrative Reports
    2.1. Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) Software Discussion – Kyle Maurer, Finance
         Director
Finance Director Kyle Maurer offered a brief background of the functional limitations of the
financial software being utilized by Herriman City. As the City has expanded, increased
functionality requests have been discussed and could be alleviated with new software.

Director Maurer highlighted the Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) software to help
business processed and dataflow. Currently, Herriman City had been utilizing Pelorus and
offered an overview of the functionalities being utilized with the software. Director Maurer
reported Pelorus was designed for smaller communities and highlighted Herriman City was
their largest user. A different ERP system could be better matched based on the City’s size
and would offer additional functionalities as limitations have risen.

Director Maurer reported a new ERP system could Include new modules, project
accounting, contracts, purchasing cards, vendor access, and self-service. Currently,
purchase orders are created for contracts manually as there was not a good way in the
current system to tract if the contract was a multi-year project. Long-range planning would
be a centralized place for departments such as Finance and Administration whereas now,
there was multiple tracking systems being utilized. Vendors would be able to utilize the self-
service module which would connect to the system to submit invoices and track the status
of payments.

Budget forecasting for both personnel and financial could be submitted online rather than
using a excel spreadsheet. Human Resources would be able to do payroll projects for
position control, employee records, applicant tracking, performance evaluation, and payroll.
The upgrade would eliminate user error unlike the current system.

Director Maurer indicated there were many different tools which could be utilized and
noted the software integration would be different for each department. The software
vendor would have representatives to help with setup and testing prior to going live.
Director Maurer informed the Council Accounts Payable currently have boxes and filing
cabinets in the office as there was a general lack of automated workflow. He explained
Pelorus was a paper-based process and observed the lack of intuitive user interface and
relayed the struggle to track reimbursement agreements, capital projects, and master
development agreements as there was a lack of an integrated document management
system.

Director Maurer explained what ERP landscape now looked like with vendors. Vendors are
no longer offering onsite software as a service. Instead of having physical services at the
city, the vendor hosts the software and data storage. The government’s core competency is
not server administration. The City would not have to maintain servers so a decrease in
maintenance cost was expected. Director Maurer reported the use of customizations had
diminished and the software vendors focus on business process change and standard
processes. Reduced focus on technical support was another issue seen more frequently as
moving to a to a different system would involve flow charts for each business process and
department created to streamline those processes.

Councilmember Ohrn suggested an internal audit for processes be conducted as it could
bring a different perspective to processes. Finance Director Alan Rae stated by contrast
the City would need to change processes based on software capabilities. City Manager
Nathan Cherpeski confirmed as each department was reviewed, documents were being
submitted to finance differently. A portion of this change would be to find the best way to
conduct the process. Councilmember Ohrn agreed staff settles into processes and
suggested it would be good to have a fresh perspective. Director Maurer suggested there
was a dichotomy to the programs and noted he recently attended an ERP selection class.
Vendors were breaking into two groups: large vendor groups would send out consultants
which would look at business processes and suggest the best way to run the system or the
mid-size vendors would bring in trainers to train the group on the functionality of the
system and the client would decide the implementation.

The City would define the scope for the software and determine what the necessities would
include. The vendor would submit proposals based on the request for proposals and the
City would evaluate the vendors based on the parameters set forth. Director Maurer
suggested the City could bring in two to three vendors to conduct a demonstration, after
October 26, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                         Page 4 of 7


which, could be selected to enter into a level of service contract. Contract negotiations
could take months and the phasing timeline could take up to four-years. Director Maurer
highlighted the challenges with implementing a new software including staffing and
financing. Currently, the City is paying approximately $3,100 quarterly to Pelorus for
support. The new system was estimated to cost $400,000 with annual support costing an
additional $40,000-$60,000 which was not currently allocated within the budget.

Councilmember Hodges questioned if the City would outgrow the software. Director
Maurer specified the vendor selected would be able to provide support for the community
at buildout. He reiterated implementing the new program would increase efficiencies in
many areas, ultimately saving the City money. He explained managing expectations was
necessary with the implementation of any new program as the systems would not be able
to do everything. If the program software was updated, there would be a downgrade in
support. Benefits to a new ERP software included creating paperless office, easily access
files, improved information sharing, increased transparency, increased collaboration, and
less duplicated date entry. Director Maurer looked for direction from the Council.
Councilmember Ohrn expressed her support of moving forward with implementing a new
software and Mayor Palmer agreed.
