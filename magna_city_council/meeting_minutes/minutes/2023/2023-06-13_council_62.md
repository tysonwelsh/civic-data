Discussion Items

RTK Tailings Presentation

                 Laura Ingersoll, Senior Advisor, Rio Tinto Kennecott, stated the Bingham Canyon
Mine has been in operation for over 120 years and produces about 12 percent of copper in the
United States. The current permitted mine life is through 2032. The next mining expansion is
called Apex and will produce 1.5 million tons of copper. The permitted mine life for the Apex
project will be through 2040 and will require additional tailings storage capacity.

               Paula Dougherty, Manager, Rio Tinto Kennecott, stated the south tailings pond
had some instability issues in the 1980s but has since been upgraded. Kennecott has been
working on all sides of the south impoundment and has installed 101 dewatering walls, wick
drains, and put down a slope. Because of the work that was done to the tailings pond, it withstood
the earthquake in 2020; however, it is still vulnerable if there is an earthquake of 7.2 or greater.

                 Doug Stauffer, Principal Engineer, Rio Tinto Kennecott, stated the clarification
canals that are used to return water from the tailings facility back up to the Copperton concentrator
will need to be relocated. To put tailings in the new area, a new drainage system would need to
be installed. Pipelines would need to be installed to get tailings to an area and additional pipelines
to get water off the surface and delivered to the existing north tailings. It would be converted into
an operating facility where the embankment would be constructed.

               Council Member Hull asked if there is anything toxic in the tailings.

               Mr. Stauffer stated it has been tested numerous times and the tailings are
consistent with western soil with the exception of some copper.

               Council Member Hull asked how the dust will be mitigated at the north tailings
pond.

              Mr. Stauffer stated there will be a dust mitigation plan like the south tailings pond.
Each year the embankment is raised with topsoil and revegetated.

               Council Member Barney stated in the last two years, there was major grading
done at the south tailings pond and it has not revegetated well, and it has created a tremendous
amount of dust. Residents are concerned about the dust and Kennecott’s lack of attentiveness to
control it. What assurances can Kennecott provide that efforts will be made to prevent the same
conditions that are happening at the south tailings pond? Can Kennecott provide assurance that
there are no toxicities and metal blowing over the community?

                 Mr. Stauffer stated as part of the initial construction, a full sprinkler system would
be installed in the area. Anytime the tailings are not being placed in the embankment, the sprinkler
system is in operation to control the dust. As the embankment grows, the slope would be covered
with topsoil.

                 Ms. Dougherty stated it was seeded as part of the project, unfortunately, it was
seeded at the wrong time of the year. The tailings impoundment is being polymerized. A couple
of million dollars has been budgeted to put a capping material on the slope and vegetate it again.
It will be taken care of permanently next spring.

               Council Member Prokopis asked if a topsoil cap would speed up the process.

                Ms. Dougherty stated yes.

             Matt Starley, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD), asked if there
will be any improvements, development buffers, or anything that would prevent future
development off of 8000 West.

               Mr. Stauffer stated the project is several hundred feet back from 8000 West.

              Council Member Barney stated the canal recirculates water to the Copperton
concentrator. What is in the water and is it safe?

                Mr. Stauffer stated Kennecott has a permit to discharge excess water into the
Great Salt Lake. To meet those permit requirements, the water is tested daily. Over the next
several years, the plan is to complete the engineering and obtain various permits. In 2025-2026
the infrastructure, drainage system, and pipelines will be installed, and relocation of the canal will
take place. In 2027, Kennecott will begin placing tailings.

               Council Member Barney stated in February there were notices posted to social
media that a public hearing was going to be held. There was some backlash from residents and
then the meeting was cancelled with information saying it would be rescheduled. The effort to
make the meeting noticed to the public was not done as well. He looked on the Utah Public Notice
website and did not find anything about the meeting. He did find information after the fact, that a
meeting was held and only one resident was in attendance. Why was the meeting rescheduled
and what efforts were made to notify residents?

              Mr. Stauffer stated the public meeting process is run entirely by the Army Corps
of Engineers, Kennecott does not play a role in that process. The initial meetings are called
scoping meetings and it allows the public to ask the Army Corps Engineers to include things in
the study. Kennecott was told the delay was because the Army Corps Engineers needed
signatures from higher-level commanders in the organization before a meeting could be
scheduled. They went through the process to obtain approvals and then rescheduled the meeting.
There were two meetings held, one at the Webster Center and the other at the recreation center
in Kearns. There will be another series of meetings held around November to get public input on
the environmental impact study.
