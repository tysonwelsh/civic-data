City Engineer, Michael Fazio reported that Dave McCune and Larry Reasch from HDR were
present and had been working diligently to help staff understand the best way to cross the 14600
South railroad. Messrs. McCune and Reasch had been critical to the study and did most of the
work. Mr. Fazio reminded the C ity Counci l that the study was the result of funding from the
Wasatch Front Regional Council.

Mr. McCune reviewed the 14600 South Rai lroad Crossing Study preliminary findings. He
explained that HDR primarily focused on the different alternatives and costs associated with an
upgraded crossing in front of 14600 South. Mr. McCune shared the Project Introduction slide and

explained that 14600 South cutTently crosses the railroad right-of-way under Union Pacific and the
Utah Transit Authority ("UTA" ) FrontRunner at 14400 South. The Union Pacific Railroad track
is on the east and the UTA FrontRunner track is on the west. The roadway curved down to
negotiate the topography because there was high elevation on the east side and low elevation on
the west side. Mr. McCune reported that the Jordan and Salt Lake City Canal is wedged in between
the roadway and the railway right-of-way through the proj ect limits. That fm1her complicates the
proj ect and will be discussed in more detail later on.

The existing crossing includes a single lane at the Union Pacific Railroad Bridge, substandard
vertical clearance, and low design speed. Mr. McCune outlined the project purpose and need as
follows:

   •   Queuing in both directions during peak periods;
   •   Previous traffic studies indicate demand will continue to increase in coming decades;
   •   Prison redevelopment will fu11her boost demand; and
   •   Parallel Corridor Study indicates additional capacity will be required.

The Study Parameters slide was shared. Mr. McCune explained that baseline design parameters
were set to guide the process. He shared an image of a four-lane cross-section. There would be
four-foot shoulders, a six-foot sidewalk on one side, and a 10-foot trail on the other side. The
design speed was 45 MPH, which was typical for a facility of that size. Mr. McCune noted that
the ve11ical clearance is 16'6" to 17'6" and over the roadway , it is 23' 10". It varied over the
roadway because Uni on Pacific has different design criteria for the vertical clearance required over
roadways depending on the type of structure installed.

Mr. McCune shared the Alternatives Selection and Screening-Site Constraints slide and explained
that the main challenges the proj ect faced pertained to elevation differences, the railroads, and the
canal. Dave Kallas wondered if the alternatives will assume some kind of tunnel. Mr. McCune
noted that the alternatives have bridges. There will be bridges with retaining walls on the s ides of
the roadway . T unnels were explored but to reach a depth that the railroad approved of several
other issues would be created in the process. Mr. McCune overviewed the main alternatives as
fo llows:

   •   Maintain the Existing Alig1m1ent;
   •   Canal Relocation;
   •   Alternative 1 - Online Alternative (straight line between the east and west portions of
       14600 South);
   •   Alternative 2 - Offline Alternative (offset slightly to the south);
   •   Alternative 3 - Bypass A lternative (swings out further to the south); and
   •   Alternative 4 - Bypass Normal (horizontal curves to cross the railroad at a more
       perpendicular angle to minimize structure width).

Mr. McCune discussed the HDR process and explained that they have a detailed process allowing

allowed them to make sure all impacts were quantified and the cost estimates were reasonable.
Mr. McCune reported that there had been iteration loops, where HDR looked at a possible canal
relocation. The alternatives in the first round assumed that the canal had to be avoided but they
later looked at whether canal relocation cou ld improve the geometry and reduce the cost of the
roadway and bridge proj ect. An iteration loop was done and there were two revised alternatives.

The Exhibits slides were shared and overviewed. Mr. McCune next discussed the slide related to
Third-Party Outreach. He noted that the two main third parties related to the canal and the
railroads. A general rule of thumb in engineering plmming was that whoever is there first wrote
the rules. Mr. McCune explained that according to that rule of thumb, the order of precedent would
be a canal, railroad , roads. HDR had not yet approached Union Pacific Railroad because they did
not believe they would be willing to di scuss much at the current planning stage. HDR reached out
to UTA and had a meeting with their staff on June 15, 202 1. UTA shared input related to the
alternatives and preferred the ones that took less of the property on the southwest.

In terms of dealing with the railroads, there were two issues with moving forward with the project:

   •    Design approval ; and
   •    Construction impacts to railroad operations.

Mr. McCune reporied that there were two options for construction, which were as follows:
