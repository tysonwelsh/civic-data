1. Discussion
1.1 Millcreek General Plan
Francis Lilly thanked John Janson and Krissy Gilmore for their work on the general plan.
John Janson said the community council meetings went well. The Canyon Rim Citizens
Association appreciated how much the consultants listened to them during the general
plan process and they recommended approval. They had concerns about the city center
but have been supportive of the progress. The East Mill Creek Community Council
focused mostly on the future land use map, which is a new tool. Lilly says most people |
are interested in the map. At that community council meeting, Janson said some people
who recently moved to Millcreek said they hated the idea of density anywhere. They
thought that density brought problems. The East Mill Creek Community Council is
waiting until December to offer any recommendations to the Planning Commission. The
Mt. Olympus Community Council had concerns about technical issues and housing,
though they recommended approval of the general plan. The Millcreek Community
Council talked about the city center and there were some recommendations in a few
areas, but they are also waiting to make a decision about the general plan until next
month. Janson also brought up that there is a new single-family development in a cul-de- |

sac around the southwest area of 1100 E. and 3300 S. that had been labeled as mixed use
on the future land use map.

Krissy Gilmore discussed the major changes on the future land use map from the first
draft that came out in June. The major changes happened between Highland Dr. and 700
E. The City center neighborhoods were Neighborhood 2 but were changed to
Neighborhood 1. 3300 S. was Mixed Use 1 but was changed to Mixed Use 2. Mixed Use
2 allows corridors to be 2-4 stories and Mixed Use 1 is 1-2 stories. The neighborhood
north of Brickyard went from Neighborhood 2 to 1 then back to 2. Chair Tom Stephens
said 3900 S. 2300 E. is shown as retail office and that is a small area for 1-4 stories. The
piano factory is also as retail office. Gilmore said the future land use map is more general
and projects will be more site specific. She read from page 16, which says, “Range of
Density and Scale. It is important to note that the General Plan’s upper limits in the
Range of Density Scale and the Key Characteristics for each Category must not be
interpreted as a presumption of right or entitlement by those planning to develop or
redevelopment a property or group of properties. What constitutes an ultimately
acceptable use, density, scale and key characteristics for a specific location is dependent
on a robust public process, the guidance and goals provided by this General Plan and,
most importantly, the current zoning ordinances.” Lilly said they may need to look at
commercial zones differently and look at ways to incorporate mixed use in. Gilmore said
the public was clear that the core area should not be Neighborhood 2, but Neighborhood
1. She said 700 E. changed to Mixed Use 1 instead of Neighborhood 2 based on
responses from the community councils. Chair Stephens said Mixed Use 1 is an
appropriate designation there. Gilmore said the center area boundaries did not change.
She said she was looking for thoughts from the Commissioners about what to work on
before the December 19" meeting.

Commissioner LaMar said he wondered about intersections around 2000 E. and 2700 S.,
near the northern city border, if they should be something different than a Neighborhood
1. Janson said a new discussion about a potential center could be had if a boundary
adjustment took place. Commissioner Mumford asked about a village center definition
versus a city center. Gilmore said the city center is the densest part of the city with
building heights from 2-6 stories and a village center is 1-4 stories. Janson said the
centers have definitions and they are suggested for areas of future study. Commissioner
Mumford assumed most of the commercial in the City would be from 700 E. to the west.
Lilly said the Olympus Hills Center could change as well as 2300 E. He recommended a
master plan in Mt. Olympus area after the general plan is approved because developers
are ready to do something there. Commissioner Healey asked about a city hall space long
term. Lilly said he anticipated city hall being elsewhere in 5-10 years. Lilly said this
document will be changed throughout the next 20 years. Gilmore said each center vision
is provided in the plan.

Commissioner Healey said 2000 E. and 2300 E. are gateways to the City. Commissioner
LaMar said it may make sense to change it to transition to commercial. Gilmore said
2300 E. is identified as a gateway on the vibrant gathering places map. Janson said they
did not want to do too much damage to existing neighborhoods on 2300 E. Commissioner
Mumford said residents have been adamant about not giving up property for an easement

to widen the road on 2300 E. Lilly said Millcreek residents are not excited about
widening 2300 E.

Janson said there has been a separate group of consultants doing a transportation study
and they have been looking at congestion problems, cross sections, and widening roads.
He said the long-term demand on 2300 E. by I-80 is much greater than it is now. Lilly
said if the City emphasizes mobility, then it would make sense to turn 2300 E. and I-80
into a full interchange. Lilly said the transportation consultant team will be bringing to
the Council a transportation master plan, storm drain master plan, and street cross
sections. He said those items do not need the Planning Commission’s review for Council
approval, but all the maps should dovetail. Lilly would like the Commission to look at
them, so he invited the engineers to present the transportation components that are
parallel to this plan to the Commission in December. Janson said the transportation
chapter, under great connections, has a map about arterials, collectors, and neighborhood
streets on page 74. Chair Stephens said visionary language should be in the great
connections section.

Janson said Highland Dr. is being looked at in the city center study; it has a narrow right
of way and needs a lot of thought. He said the four lanes do not function well, so they are
looking at one lane in each direction with a landscaped median in some places, and street
parking and bike lanes on the street. Janson thinks the traffic will move to 1300 E. Lilly
said south of 3330 S., 1300 E. tapers down and south of 3300 S and Highland Dr. it
becomes a 5-lane intersection. He said there will be a connector from the 5 lanes on
Highland and 1300 E. Janson said the cross sections are typical, but it does not mean that
every lineal foot will look that way, especially on 3300 S. Lilly said the City is looking at
a private lane standard for a PUD in the transportation master plan. Janson said the
transportation master plan could be an appendix to the general plan. He thinks there
might need to be some adjustments with the bike plan in the city center and the bike lane
now shows going into the canyon on the map. There is a sidewalk plan included which is
based on the Walk Millcreek plan. Commissioner LaMar asked about priorities for
neighborhoods with sidewalks. Gilmore said priorities are places that lead somewhere,
like schools or grocery stores. She said the sidewalk plan was a heavily scrutinized map
during the public process.

Janson said the State legislature may push housing related studies tied to transportation in
general plans. The City is pushing higher density into centers and corridors. The State
required the City to have a moderate-income housing plan which is included in the
general plan. He suggested in the moving forward chapter, to have information on
accessory dwelling units. He said the legislature may make cities provide an accessory
dwelling ordinance. Lilly said planning staff gets 1-2 inquiries a week about them. Lilly
said he has discussed this with the Mayor who is trying to get ahead of the situation.
Janson said the plan also says that larger projects must have 10% affordable housing.
Janson said affordable is not just multifamily units but is based on rent versus median
income. Commissioner Mumford asked if it is an income mean and what the rent would
be. Janson said it looks at the City’s rents and the median county income. Lilly said the
median number changes on family size. Commissioner Mumford asked about subsidies.
Janson said it looks at demand but is based on 80% of the median county income. Blaine

Gehring said the public mixes the terms “affordable housing” and “moderate income” up,
but they are two different things according to the state. Lilly said it you are at or above
the 80% area median income there is available housing for you somewhere, if you are
below 80% area median income you will struggle to find housing. Chair Stephens said
the theme throughout the general plan is the protection of single-family residential
neighborhoods. Lilly said there is an advantage to invest in the housing stock, especially
as it deteriorates.

Gilmore said the moving forward chapter is the most important and it goes over the
vision themes. Janson said the point of that chapter is to summarize the other chapters.
Gilmore briefly went through each section:

Unique Neighborhoods — The main marching orders are, create neighborhood studies

and ensure neighborhood compatibility.

Vibrant Gathering Places — This includes emphasizing Millcreek Centers and

supporting a variety of housing opportunities.

Thriving Economy — To create an economic development strategic plan and update

and maintain city infrastructure.

Great Connections — Outlines priorities for a more walkable and bikeable community.

Outdoor Lifestyle — High priorities include creating a park and trails impact fee study,

connecting trail systems on the east side, and providing more equity such as the west

side of the City to 700 E. needs a soccer field. Janson said the City has a lot of park

acreage but its concentrated in one area, there are not many walkable parks. Chair

Stephens asked about the park impact fee ordinance. Lilly said the Council had a

hearing on it and is waiting 90 days to start assessing it. The fee will be with the

building permit; about $430.

Enhanced Culture — Includes the promise program, supporting arts, public art, and

community gardens.

Health and Environment — To review code for conservation design practices, green

building designs, and update the development code to protect significant panoramic

views. Lilly said the City needs an objective way to evaluate views systematically.
Lilly went through the annexation map and said the areas at the periphery of the City are
shown for potential annexation. The City is working on obtaining Brickyard, but it will
be a long process. The northern boundary needs to be squared up with Salt Lake City.
Some residences there are partially in Millcreek and Salt Lake City. He said the City is
justified with having an ambitious map and then scaling back. Janson said there are three
mechanisms for annexation; petition for areas that are unincorporated, boundary
adjustments, and de-annex petition process which is very political. The City received a
notice of intent to file an annexation petition from the unincorporated area below Van
Winkle. The City is providing police and fire services there anyway and it will help with
affordable housing. Lilly said he anticipates the legislature changing the sales tax formula
to weigh more heavily on population. Janson said the open space there is Salt Lake City
owned water facilities.
Janson said there might be adjustments on the transportation part. They will get
recommendations from the East Mill Creek Community Council and Canyon Rim
Citizens Association. Chair Stephens said the goal would be to get their
recommendations by the December 19" Planning Commission meeting. Commissioner
