26            Town Days Discussion
27
28   Sheril Garn, Events and Operations Director, presented an overview of the recent Town Days
29   event. She expressed gratitude to her team for successfully executing Town Days despite
30   challenges such as employee absences due to maternity leave and family medical situations.
31
32   Ms. Garn said there are 23 different events associated with Town Days, with 14 of them being
33   free of charge. She further highlighted the high attendance levels across various events,
34   showcasing the significant community engagement. Specific events, such as the rodeo, car
35   show, parade, miniature golf, and various games and activities, were discussed, along with
36   estimated attendance figures. Notably, the parade's logistical challenges were mentioned,
37   emphasizing the need for careful planning and coordination.
38
39   Ms. Garn also mentioned sponsorships, volunteer contributions, and various aspects of the
40   event's management, such as flag ceremonies, workouts, and breakfast. The impressive
41   number of participants in activities like the country mile run and various games illustrated the
42   community's enthusiasm.
43
44   Ms. Garn concluded by acknowledging the City's role in making Town Days an accessible and
45   enjoyable experience for residents regardless of their budget.
46
47   Josh Lee, Communications Director, presented a summary of the survey results related to Town
48   Days. He said the survey was a success, with a positive response rate. He discussed adding a
49   third date for the rodeo, with roughly 50% of respondents expressing support, 20% opposing the
50   idea, and approximately 30% remaining indifferent. He said the overall sentiment conveyed by
51   the survey results was that the community had a positive outlook on Town Days.

25            Riverton Police Services and Recruitment Discussion
26
27   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, introduced a proposal related to enhancing
28   employee benefits and engagement. He noted that Chief Barfuss’s team had taken the lead in
29   formulating the plan. Mr. Hicks explained that the proposal consisted of two main components.
30   The first component focused on employee benefits and retention. Recently, a new trend had
31   emerged among other agencies, where they allowed employees to retain their years of service
32   from various agencies when calculating vacation or leave time. This approach aimed to motivate
33   employees and attract them to the agency. The proposal recommended that the City adopt a
34   similar approach by allowing both police personnel and civilian staff to use their full years of
35   service from all URS governmental entities when calculating leave time. For law enforcement
36   personnel, it was specified that only their law enforcement experience would be considered.
37
38   Mr. Hicks emphasized that this proposal did not entail changes in the leave levels or amounts
39   offered by the City, but rather a modification to the way years of service were calculated. If the
40   Council approved, an amendment to the policy manual would be prepared to include this
41   provision.
42
43   Councilmembers and Mayor Staggs asked clarifying questions about the proposed policy
44   change.
45
46   Ryan Carter, Acting City Manager, expressed his cautious optimism about the proposal and its
47   potential impact on recruitment efforts. He conveyed that while he believed in the intention of
48   the staff to enhance the City's market position, he acknowledged the possibility of external
49   factors, such as economic changes, impacting the situation in the future. He acknowledged the
50   importance of maintaining a competitive edge in terms of attracting talent and pointed out the
51   dynamic nature of the job market.

16   Riverton Police Department Presentation – Chief Blair Barfuss
17
18   Chief Barfuss explained the comparisons of staffing changes over previous years, with a shift in
19   rank titles to deputy chiefs and organizational restructuring to align with responsibilities. The
20   incorporation of animal services and code enforcement into the police department was
21   highlighted. Graphs depicting a five-year trend in calls for service demonstrated a consistent
22   rise followed by a significant reduction in December 2022. It was noted that proactive calls,
23   including traffic stops and citizen encounters, are also included in the calls for service statistics.
24
25   Chief Barfuss said the data indicated a pattern of increased calls for service in the first year,
26   followed by either stabilization or decrease in subsequent years. Similar trends were observed
27   in reported offenses. It was suggested that the initial year of the department's formation saw
28   heightened proactive policing, but concerns arising in 2020 regarding police perception,
29   proactive policing, and government support led to a decline in officers' proactive efforts.
30
31   Chief Barfuss highlighted several achievements, including the establishment of core values
32   ("Riverton PD pride"), departmental restructuring for clarity, the release of law enforcement
33   policy manuals, and the reinstatement of the motors unit to address traffic enforcement needs.
34   A peer support group and a chaplain program were introduced, and efforts to enhance the major
35   accident and negotiations teams were undertaken.
36
37   Chief Barfuss discussed future departmental goals, with a primary focus on achieving DEP
38   accreditation through the Utah Chiefs of Police Association, potentially within the next few
39   months. Community policing efforts were to be intensified through district assignments of
40   officers to councilmembers, addressing specific community concerns. The evaluation of staffing,
41   improved officer recruitment and development, conducting salary surveys, and transparency in
42   communication were emphasized. Looking ahead, areas of high risk management were
43   identified, including the implementation of professional standards review boards for
44   transparency and addressing complaints. The resumption of animal control services and
45   proactive code enforcement with community resolutions were also highlighted.
46   Councilmember Wells asked if the police department tracks reportable accidents within the City
47   each year. In response, Chief Barfuss explained that data tracking and analysis are integral to
48   their operations. He said the police department monitors daily activities, including the specific
49   type and duration of each officer's involvement in calls. Additionally, he said they closely heed
50   citizen concerns and complaints, particularly regarding matters like crosswalks near school
51   zones.
