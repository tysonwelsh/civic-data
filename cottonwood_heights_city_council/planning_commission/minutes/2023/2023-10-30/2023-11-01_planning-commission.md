WORK SESSION

Chair Dan Mills called the Work Meeting to order at 5:01 p.m.

Chair Mills reported that the Town Center Committee held its first meeting. As one of the
committee appointees, he believed the involvement of the members would be extremely beneficial
to the City. Input was invited.

Senior City Planner, Samantha DeSeelhorst reported that the Wasatch Front Regional Council
(“WFRC”) will host a workshop on Monday, November 6, 2023, to discuss long-range planning
efforts. The workshop will take place from 4:00 p.m. to 6:00 p.m. Chair Mills emphasized the
importance of maintaining a well-functioning relationship with the Council.

1.0    Form-Based Code Discussion.

Ms. DeSeelhorst introduced Mark Morris, Principal Landscape Architect/Founder of VODA
Landscape + Design. Mr. Morris presented the Form-Based Code update and stated that it is part
of the General Plan update process. Input and questions from the Commission Members were
encouraged. He shared the prototype discussed at a previous meeting and stated that the intent is
for it to serve as an ongoing resource for new applicants and the Planning Commission. It will act
as a primer to clarify what a Form-Based Code is, its focus, and the factors driving the decisions.
The corner parcel at 2300 East and Fort Union Boulevard was used as an example. Mr. Morris
referenced the Hillside Plaza and stated that the Town Center District is quite large. The corner
property was chosen to illustrate that as development occurs, all of the parcels will contribute to

what they hope the Town Center catalytic project will set in motion. He reported that the current
setbacks differ with the Form-Based Code with most parameters set by a range that presents a
minimum and maximum. Height is also expressed in a similar range. The Town Center Form
District has a maximum height of four stories and a minimum height of two stories. He explained
that with any redevelopment, they want to progressively increase concentration in those areas.
With the addition of other parts of the City to the Form-Based Code, the discussion of appropriate
heights will be key.

Community and Development Director, Michael Johnson stated that what they have proposed as
a starting point for discussion is the Town Center and an area that is not adjacent to a lot of
residential. As more residential areas are considered, heights will change. Chair Mills questioned
the safety of potential earthquakes and seismic issues. Commissioner Chappel stated they do not
tend to see the same kind of hazards that are present at the gravel pit and along Wasatch Boulevard.
Given a normal site, she believed from a seismic safety issue, height is not the biggest factor and
suggested a high-quality plan review. Mr. Morris explained that height is established by story with
each of the floor heights defined in the Tables and ground floor height is established separately
from upper floor heights. The Town Center Form District ground floor height ranges from 12 feet
to 24 feet with upper floors ranging from nine to 12 feet. Smaller parcels have a built-in limitation.
When considering the Town Center District, the first story height is essential for the catalytic
project to work.

Parking was next discussed. Ms. DeSeelhorst stated that the metrics in the Code do an acceptable
job of pushing parking to the back of the site and bringing buildings closer up for a more desirable
product. The ratios for the different uses should be considered and discussed by the Commission
but Staff has done background work to ensure they are in the right area. She believed this resolves
frustration with the current Parking Code limitations and will provide clarification for potential
developers. Mr. Johnson noted that shared-use parking may also be utilized when there is a mix
of uses. Chair Mills stated that there have been instances where developments can do shared
parking garages where there are Interlocal Agreements between business entities and buildings.
Intentionality in mass parking was recommended.

Mr. Morris reported that he has been involved in conversations with the City of Clearfield and the
Millcreek City Planning Director who has been working with a Form-Based Code for the last five
years. Both cities encouraged shared parking, which allows for more use and provides flexibility
on the site. It was noted that there are national standards available for reference detailing how to
calculate shared parking. Millcreek City follows a specific guideline and recommended making it
part of the discussion.

Mr. Morris referred to the Tables in the Code pertaining to transparency and blank wall limitations.
Transparency is defined by the ground and upper floors and is intended to ensure the ground floor
levels are as transparent and open as possible. Use is considered in the Form-Based Code and is
secondary to the other form questions.

Commissioner Anderson asked if a developer were to purchase a corner space, what direction the
City would provide. Ms. DeSeelhorst explained that the developer would be given a worksheet
similar to filling out an application that would guide them through the different requirements.

Mr. Morris stated that every city that adopts a Form-Based Code has a customized process that
works for them. The first step would include a Pre-Application Meeting with a checklist that
mirrors the prototype specific to the parcel in question and provides flexibility while detailing how
it would define the space.

Commissioner Chappel stated that when discussing an increase transparency required at ground
level with a potentially taller building, they may be deliberately pushing a structure toward a
building type. When these issues come back for a Structural Plan Review, it may add additional
cost to the structure. She felt that with all of the requirements, it becomes complex enough and
may not always be reviewed by someone with specific knowledge of the building. The Structural
Engineer Association of Utah conducted a study that determined that only one in four buildings is
getting a real plan review. She believed these issues should not preclude the directives but felt
they may heighten an issue. Mr. Morris stated that transparency, like the others, includes a range
and may provide clarification when considering building types.

Commissioner Smith questioned the role of the Planning Commission. He asked if they are acting
in an advisory role and who is defining what is and is not attractive. Ms. DeSeelhorst explained
the role of the Form-Based Code is not to be an architectural guide. It leaves flexibility for
different styles of opinion and provides non-negotiable items and how the building should be
located on the site. She confirmed that the role of the Planning Commission is to define parameters
and then make a recommendation to the City Council. Mr. Johnson stated that the Town Center
is owned by the City and ultimately has full control over what goes with the ability to formulate
the Form-Based Code around what is decided. He believed some of the principles of the Code are
conducive to successful town centers.

Commissioner Anderson felt there needs to be further discussion and understanding of when the
Planning Commission gets involved and knowledge of the metric. Chair Mills noted they are still
taking part in discussions and figuring out their role.

Commissioner Poulson stated this will be a major mindset change for the City. Fort Union
Boulevard has four lanes with a bike path and asked how long it will take before the Form-Based
Code is implemented. It was noted the City owns a key site in the area that would act as a catalytic
project once the City goes in and redevelops that site. Staff confirmed that would be the catalyst
for the reaction.

Mr. Morris stated this is an incremental process and there will be a catalytic project with the
Hillside Plaza. Over time, the intention is that the City will have changed the rules for those nodes
and will be in keeping them as new development comes along.

In response to a question regarding updates, Mr. Johnson explained that there may be properties
that wish to remain as is and other properties that will inherently come in and wish to redevelop.
They intend to apply the same mixed-use ordinance to all the properties along the corridor while
providing flexibility and options for a successful long-term project while inching closer to the
City’s vision for those major corridors. Mr. Morris believed these types of city-led projects could
bring about positive change.

Commissioner Shelton stated that because the City is going through this process, it changes the
perspective of those who are involved in the planning. He believed the pace of change would be
determined by the incentive or disincentive created in the Code. He stressed the need to find a
balance. Mr. Morris explained they have talked with several cities that have been involved in
Form-Based Code to determine what they found to be disincentives. The intent is to provide more
flexibility and options but the last thing they want to do is put in so many requirements that it
becomes too difficult to make changes.

Chair Mills stated that as this is considered, the Commission was encouraged to think about the
process and what it is they are moving away from and working toward. He also asked that they
consider the threshold of what may be taken care of by Staff and what would go to the Commission.
