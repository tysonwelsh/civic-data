2.6) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City's request for review of the Village
Overlay General Plan amendment, removing the Village Overlay designation at the
southwest corner of 1100 West and Pioneer Crossing.
Brittney Harris presented information on the item. She clarified an overly zone was added
previously and that the City Council recently directed staff to remove the overlay zone due to
density concerns and needed road improvements. She noted that the change would remove an
estimated 390 units that were planned for this area and stated that those units could be allocated
to a different area or just removed all together, but that would be a decision of the City Council.
She clarified that there are no vested rights to the 390 units.
Chairman Jackson opened the public hearing.

Tony Trane stated that there is potential for more density in this area as the park develops and as
road improvements are made. He also noted that the commercial zone needs to be properly
designated.

Cameron Spencer from Flagship Palms expressed relief at the removal of the village overlay
designation. He explained that the overlay was difficult to implement, as other surrounding
property owners were unwilling to zone their land to comply with the overlay.

Larry Hadfield expressed his appreciation for the removal of the overlay zone. He voiced
concerns about the existing traffic challenges on 1100 West and hoped that future development
in the area would include measures to alleviate these issues for the local community.

Kenneth Gade was critical of the original village overlay zone but expressed support for zoning
that’s consistent with the surrounding community.

Brittney Harris acknowledged the online comments that were in support.

Chairman Jackson closed the public hearing.

                Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to recommend approval of the Village
                Overlay General Plan amendment, removing the Village Overlay designation at
                the southwest corner of 1100 West and Pioneer Crossing; including all DRC
                comments; with the findings that the amendment will have a positive affect, that it
                meets the goals, purposes and intent of the General Plan, that it will not have a
                substantial adverse effect on surrounding properties and that it is suitable to
                accommodate any future uses; and noting that the City Council requested the
                removal of the overlay zone. Commissioner Everett seconded the motion.

                Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner
                Eyre, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes; Commissioner Jones, yes. The motion
                passed unanimously.

2.9) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for a Development Code
Amendment adding the Critical Homeownership Overlay Zone.
Brittney Harris presented information on the item. She expressed that a collaborative effort with
the city has been put into this item to address affordable housing and homeownership.

Jason Harris presented on the development code amendment and the overlay. He discussed the
exponential growth in Utah's home prices, noting that the average cost for an existing home was
approximately $600,000, with new construction being much higher. The primary goal was to
prioritize homeownership. He mentioned that Paul Hancock and Heather Newall were essentially
the sponsors behind the overlay, while he was involved as a concerned resident of the City,
particularly regarding home affordability and rising costs.

He raised the question of what could be done to reduce home prices in order to maintain
availability. He explained that the land price constitutes the majority of a home's structure cost,
and by lowering the allocation of land cost, they could significantly reduce home prices. The
zone overlay and required Development Agreement would grant the City Council full discretion
to work with builders on plans to make housing more affordable. Builders would submit two
concept plans: a base concept and an attainable concept, with a target price agreed upon through
concessions. This plan would then be submitted to the City Council for review and approval.

Building equity was highly encouraged by remaining in the home. In Lehi, affordability was still
relatively high compared to surrounding communities. The criteria for homeownership would
require the homes to be owner-occupied with a set target price, and they would be offered to
critical and essential workers, Lehi residents, and first-time homebuyers.

Concerns with government overreach were discussed. Brittney Harris clarified that all
participation would be voluntary and could provide affordability, especially with the State
introducing funding to assist with housing.

Concerns with condensing lot sizes were discussed and how that could impact current residents.
Concerns hindering property owners' ability to grow equity were also discussed and where the
equity would go if the ten-year plan was not met.

Commissioner Eyre asked if the projected numbers would be higher and if it would meet
infrastructure needs. Brittney stated if there were critical infrastructure concerns it would be
denied. Craig Chambers stated setting precedence would be more of a political issue rather than a
legal issue and that all development agreements come through the Planning Commission and
City Council.

Chairman Jackson asked if Council Member Paul Hancock would like to address the item, as his
name had been referenced throughout the presentation and conversation. Paul Hancock
expressed that he wanted to hear the questions and concerns, explaining why he was willing to

sponsor an idea of this magnitude. He noted his concerns with housing affordability, stating that
his children would likely never be able to afford a home.

Mr. Hancock stated that without a development agreement, the target price would not be locked
in, potentially increasing, and defeating the purpose of the concept while failing to hold the
developer accountable. He also stated that the city should not absorb additional costs and
mentioned that the mandated legislation regarding ADUs was concerning, emphasizing the need
for a proactive approach.

Chairman Jackson opened the public hearing.

Marshall Gary stated that existing subsidies needed to be acknowledged. He noted that
investment fraud was at an all-time high, even though first-time homeownership was being
incentivized. He expressed his excitement for the concept and looked forward to finding more
people who shared his enthusiasm. He also emphasized that being "house poor" was a real
concern, and that the people who needed this the most were those unable to attend the meeting.

Alyssa Daly voiced her support for the concept and disclosed that she worked as a realtor. She
shared that buying her first home years ago was a frightening experience, and that was years ago
when the market was different. She echoed the comments made by Lehi staff, stating that they
were at the forefront of examining housing from a new perspective. She remarked that Lehi had
a strong economy due to its healthy housing efforts, but that advantage was slowly disappearing.
She was worried about the children’s future and feared they might be priced out of a community
they loved.

Michelle Stallings spoke as a private resident of Lehi City, rather than in her capacity as a
Council Member. She mentioned that she was unaware this item was coming up and would have
preferred to see it discussed during a Work Session, as it raised concerns. She emphasized that
the General Plan was meant to manage growth and infrastructure, and expressed concern that it
would be disregarded if a development agreement like this was implemented. She also worried
that zoning designations, which were intended to protect residents, were being used to control
land use for builders' profit. Stallings was particularly uneasy about City Council setting target
prices, acknowledging that there were many subsidies but feeling that this approach was an
overreach. She hoped that the entire Council would carefully consider an item of this
significance.

Kenneth Glade strongly opposed the item, stating it was "ridiculous" that it was even being
heard. He acknowledged that Mr. Harris had been transparent from the beginning, but pointed
out that Harris wanted more density, housing, and growth, which the residents of Lehi did not
want. He argued that this was exactly what the village overlay was about and noted that policies
and procedures were in place to avoid limiting builders from buying high and selling low. He
recalled that Lehi was affordable twenty years ago, which had benefited him, but expressed
concerns about government officials intervening in housing costs, calling it poor public policy.
Glade believed such policies would lead to secretive, backdoor developments and was concerned
about public oversight, expressing his complete opposition to the concept.

Chairman Jackson closed the public hearing.

Brittney Harris acknowledged that there were strong feelings and opinions regarding the issue of
affordable housing. She expressed that it should rely less on subsidies. She hoped this concept
could be viewed from multiple angles and serve as a steppingstone toward making a positive
difference.

Chairman Jackson agreed that a Work Session would have been an ideal setting for this
discussion before it was brought to a public meeting and suggested tabling the item.
Commissioner Everett acknowledged that valid points had been raised by both sides of the
argument. Concerns about the proposed twenty percent return were discussed, emphasizing the
need to ensure that the community’s broader needs were being met, including considering the
flow of who would be purchasing the homes. The Commission would like more time to
investigate guarantees and safeguards for the concept, as further refinement may be needed.

Chairman Jackson made it clear for the record that he would recue himself from a vote on the
item that evening unless it was tabled.

                Motion: Commission Chair Jackson moved to table this item until the
                Commission can review it more at a work session meeting. The item would then
                be brought back for consideration and noticed as a public hearing. Commissioner
                Eyre seconded the motion.

                Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner
                Eyre, no; Commissioner Kunze, yes; Commissioner Jones, yes. The motion
                passed 4-1.
