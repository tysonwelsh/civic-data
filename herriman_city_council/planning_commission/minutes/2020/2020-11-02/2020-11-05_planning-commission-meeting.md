            1.3     Discussion of the Draft Accessory Structure Ordinance (File Number Z2018-009)
    City Planner Maloy stated the intention was to determine objectives for residential accessory building
    regulations. When the draft was presented to Council more research and information was requested. In the
    current code accessory structure regulations were contained in multiple chapters. Consolidating all accessory
    dwelling regulations into one chapter has been in development by staff. Commission insight was being sought
    to determine the values, priorities, uses and compatibility of structures for the community. Current guidelines
    do not allow utilities, therefore detached structures may not be used as residences.

    Chair Berbert was in favor of residential usage in zones with larger lots, with strict guidance and City safety
    inspections. The size of the structures and zones should be closely regulated. Accessory apartments should
    have a designated chapter, separate from other uses, such as storage. Commissioner Rypien mentioned open
    air structures for Recreational Vehicle (RV) storage. City Planner Maloy stated new code was needed to define
    limits for Recreational Vehicle storage and minimize the rental of land for RV storage. Accessory apartments
    need to be attached or inside the main dwelling and were limited to one floor. They cannot be standalone
    units. Unusual lot shapes that fall out of the normal pattern may need more flexibility and special consideration
    which could be accomplished with conditional use approval.

    Commissioners agreed to recommence the work meeting after the regular meeting.

         3.1    Request:        Conditional Use for a Dance Studio Known as Lifehouse Performing
                Arts
                Applicant:      Nesha Woodhouse
                Address:        2286 W Porters Point Cove
                Zone:           M-1 (Manufacturing)
                Acres:          2.55
                File Number: S2020-078
City Planner Maloy explained the applicant requested conditional use approval for a dance studio known as
Lifehouse Performing Arts. The property was located in the Rockwell Landing flex space, in the northern most
section of the building. The applicant proposed a clockwise circulation for safe pickup and dropoff., with
permanent directional signage. This location would be rehearsal space only. All performances would take place
at a separate performance hall. They have more parking spaces than required.

Applicant Nesha Woodhouse was not present.

Requirements
   1. Receive and agree to the recommendations from other agencies.
   2. Parking shall be provided at 1 space per 250 square feet of floor area plus 6 employee parking spaces.
   3. Traffic Should circulate the building in a clockwise direction for drop-off and pick up.
   4. Provide permanent signage to direct trafffic circulation around the building for drop-off and pick up.
   5. No signs are approved with this requeste; a separate application will be required. No signs are allowed
         on public property.

Commissioner Lorin Palmer moved to approve item 3.1 file # S2020-078 Conditional Use for a Dance Studio known as
Lifehouse Performing Arts with five staff recommendations. Commissioner Jackson Ferguson seconded the motion.

         1.5    Discussion of the General Plan (Susie Petheram, FFKR)
FFKR Susie Petheram summarized proposed modifications to the Future Land Use (FLU) map, which were
based on feedback from the Planning Commission. Open space was updated on the south side to reflect Army
Compatible Use Buffer (ACUB) locations. Utility changes were made near the Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy District, to the south and east sides of the Real Salt Lake complex. She stated there was potential
for office mixed use along Mountain View Corridor. A few small commercial parcels were added along 6400
West. Commissioner Ferguson recommended a right of way for SR85 be added in the southeastern
employment center. FFKR Susie Petheram clarified the Office Mixed Use category included residential uses,
but the Employment Center category did not. The area along Porter Rockwell was thought to have
employment zone potential and be well suited for commercial use. FFKR Susie Petheram reported the next
step would be to have the information available for public review and then schedule a public hearing.
