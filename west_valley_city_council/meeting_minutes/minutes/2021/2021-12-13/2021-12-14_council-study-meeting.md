REVIEW AGENDAS FOR REGULAR CITY COUNCIL MEETING AND
SPECIAL REDEVELOPMENT AGENCY MEETING OF DECEMBER 14, 2021
GPZ-15-2021
Steve Pastorik indicated that he spoke with the developer regarding adding curb, gutter,
and sidewalk to the project. He stated that their preference would be to not add these
improvements because it would require the removal of mature trees. Mayor Bigelow stated
that he feels a solution could still be found that would allow for both options. Wayne noted
that there are no sidewalks or improvements on this side of 5450 West. Councilmember
Lang stated that this area is changing from agriculture to residential and as families move
into these units, the need for sidewalks will become a higher priority. She indicated that
she would rather have the developer install the improvements now and not have the City
be responsible in the future. Councilmember Buhler noted that drainage will likely become
an issue for neighboring property owners because runoff will have no where to go since
there are no other improvements on this side of the street. Councilmember Lang stated that
there are only about 4 trees that would be removed. Mayor Bigelow stated that he would
be comfortable with a meandering sidewalk. Councilmember Lang noted that there are
drainage solutions.

GPZ-14-2021
Steve Pastorik indicated that the applicant has removed one unit and added an additional
16 parking stalls. Councilmember Lang stated that she likes this improvement but added
that she would like 3200 West signed as no parking. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that
the 3200 West is not wide enough for people to park on. Wayne added that if the issue
arises, the City can sign streets as no parking at any time. Mayor Bigelow stated that
tenants may end up using parking stalls as their parking spaces. Councilmember
Christensen stated that the HOA would be responsible for enforcement.

SMI-6-2021 – SMI-11-2021
Eric Bunderson stated that Legal has reviewed State statues and City ordinances and has
found that the application is in compliance and is legal as detailed in the memo emailed to
the City Council the previous day. Councilmember Lang stated that she is uncomfortable
with the application because it needed an interpretation from Legal. She stated that she
would not be comfortable moving forward with his until the language in the Code could be
cleaned up. Mayor Bigelow stated that Valley Fair mall is like West Valley City’s
downtown. He indicated that this is a walkable community and should be treated differently
than other strip malls in other parts of the City. Councilmember Lang stated that the
ordinance is not clear and she believes strip malls should be allowed on ½ acre lots. Wayne
stated that the Council can discuss and change this policy moving forward if they would
like but this application is separate from that discussion. He indicated that Legal has

reviewed and determined that these lots are legal and this is an administrative application.
Councilmember Lang stated that she struggles with the interpretation. Councilmember
Huynh agreed. Councilmember Buhler stated that the City’s Attorney has reviewed this
application and has determined that it is legal. He indicated that since this is an
administrative application, the applicant can sue the City if it is denied. He noted that the
applicant will win that lawsuit and will use the meeting minutes and recording as evidence
and that will result in a costly battle for taxpayers. Mayor Bigelow agreed and stated that
the strip mall definition can be clarified but that would need to occur at a future date.

RDA Meeting
Wayne indicated that Karen Lang will not be able to serve as Chair for the January 4, 2022
RDA meeting since she will be presiding as Mayor. He indicated that the Council will need
to appoint a temporary Chair for that meeting so the official Chair and Vice-Chair for the
2022 year can be formally chosen.

Bangerter Highway Resolution
Mayor Bigelow noted that that the resolution for Bangerter Highway was updated with
further detail on the Option 6 solution that was preferred by Council.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow, members of the Council had no further questions or
concerns regarding items listed on the Agendas for the Regular City Council and Special
Redevelopment Agency meetings scheduled later this night.

             State Statute Title 10, Chapter 6, Utah Code Annotated 1953, as amended,
             allows the City of West Valley to amend its budget during the year. The
             West Valley City holds public hearings on budget amendments on a
             quarterly basis each fiscal year.

             the Family Fitness Center and UCCC; also, posted on the Public Notice
             Website and West Valley City’s Website. Notice was given that a public
             hearing is to be held January 4, 2022 at 6:30 p.m., West Valley City Hall,
             3600 Constitution Blvd., West Valley City, Utah

      The City Council will consider Ordinance 21-68 at the Regular Council Meeting
      scheduled January 4, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

CONSENT AGENDA SCHEDULED FOR JANUARY 4, 2022
A.  RESOLUTION 21-215: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO ENTER INTO A
    RIGHT OF WAY PURCHASE AGREEMENT AND ACCEPT A
    WARRANTY DEED AND A TEMPORARY CONSTRUCTION
    EASEMENT WITH AND FROM XIUMENG WU AND JIANAN XIAO FOR
    PROPERTY LOCATED AT 2554 SOUTH LAKE CREST DRIVE
    Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 21-215 that would authorize the
    City to enter into a Right Of Way Purchase Agreement and Accept a Warranty Deed
    and a Temporary Construction Easement with and from Xiumeng Wu and Jianan Xiao
    for Property Located at 2554 South Lake Crest Drive

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:

             The Xiumeng Wu and Jianan Xiao property is one of several properties
             affected by the required removal and replacement of pedestrian ramps to
             meet current ADA standards as part of the 2022 Pedestrian Ramp Project.
             As part of this project pedestrian ramps will be constructed at various
             locations throughout the city in locations where they do not currently exist
             and existing pedestrian ramps which do not comply with current ADA
             standards will be removed and replaced.

             facilitate construction of the pedestrian ramps. Compensation in the amount
             of $425.00 was negotiated based upon land values for properties in the area.

      of the City Council.

      The City Council will consider Resolution 21-215 at the Regular Council Meeting
      scheduled January 4, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.
