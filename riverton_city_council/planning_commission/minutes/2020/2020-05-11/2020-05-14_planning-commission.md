1.     MINOR SUBDIVISION, BRANDON WIXOM IS REQUESTING APPROVAL OF
       SUNDAY DRIVE SUBDIVISION, A 3-LOT RESIDENTIAL SUBDIVISION LOCATED
       AT 12939 SOUTH SUNDAY DRIVE.

Development Services Director, Jason Lethbridge, presented the staff report and reviewed the
zoning history of the property. The property was identified on a map displayed. Mr. Lethbridge
explained that the proposed subdivision creates a lot with the other two lots wrapped around it. It
is designed so that each lot can meet the 14,000 square-foot minimum requirement. Although
this is unusual, the proposal was determined to comply within the minimum zone standards.

Lot 103 will be accessed by a private lane and the distance between the property boundary and
the garage meets all minimum requirements. An easement will be in place that will allow access
from Sunday Drive to the garage area and is only for the private lane. There are no restrictions
with respect to how Lots 101 and 102 deal with the property line.

Mr. Lethbridge reviewed the conditions proposed for the approval of the application. A significant
amount of public comment was generated for this application that was forwarded to the
Commission Members.

Commissioner James expressed concern with the nine-foot strip on the side of the property and
feels this will create issues in the future. He asked if alternative layouts have been received from

the applicant or the engineer. Mr. Lethbridge reported that the applicant previously made efforts
to arrange this section of land to comply with the City’s requirements more readily.

City Engineer, Brian Moore reported that there are retention basins on the north and south
of the property.

Commissioner James commented that placing the retention pond on Lot 103 would be
easier than putting it into two lots with two different owners. Mr. Lethbridge stated that the
applicant arranged the lots to meet the 14,000 square-foot minimum lot size.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Lethbridge explained that the ordinance does not
require a private lane to cross property lines.

Chair Cannon asked if the developer currently owns the lots and why Lots 101 and 102 do
not have access to the private lane. He suggested shifting Lot 103 to the south.
Mr. Lethbridge reviewed the requirements for lane access and stated that currently, the lane
has been sized to allow only Lot 103 to have access; however, the lane could be widened
to allow all three lots to have access. In response to a question raised, Mr. Lethbridge
explained that Lots 101 and 102 could request approval for the private lane use in the future.

Commissioner Lefgren asked about the setback requirement for a private lane pertaining to
a resident and a structure. Mr. Lethbridge reviewed the ordinance and stated that there is
not a separation requirement from the edge of the lane. Commissioner Lefgren asked if
utilities can be applied to the easement. Mr. Lethbridge confirmed that they can and that
this is a typical use of the private lane.

The applicant, Brandon Wixom, participated in the meeting via Zoom and reported that his
company owns the lots. The intent was for the homes to be elevated and facing west and
for the property to have the proper drainage. He was willing to conform to any requirements
and stated that this was the purpose of hiring Engineer, Dale Bennett. The retention ponds
were strategically placed to not interfere with construction on Lots 101 and 102. The intent
was to build homes that are consistent with the area. Mr. Wixom reviewed the history of the
property.

Commissioner James asked the applicant if the north retention area could be placed under
one ownership. Mr. Wixom reported that it is possible, but only with City approval.
Mr. Lethbridge reported that all retention ponds can be placed on one lot as long as each lot
meets the minimum requirements. Mr. Bennett noted that it was possible to move the
retention pond fully onto Lot 102.

City Attorney, Craig Bott, asked if the barn will lose structural integrity if the retention pond
is flush against it. Mr. Bennett reported that placement of the pond against the barn is
possible since the edge of the easement is at the high point of the retention pond and will
not endanger the barn.

Riverton City’s Long-Term Storm Water Management Plan was reviewed. A Geotech
analysis was requested because of concern with the infiltration rate of the pond. It was
noted that the retention rate has been closely monitored.

Commissioner James asked if the long-term agreements work. The City Engineer reported
that there are good intentions behind the agreements; however, their effectiveness has yet
to be determined. Mr. Lethbridge stated that the effectiveness of the agreements is to give
the City better enforcement. In response to a question raised, Mr. Moore reported that the
agreement is on all three subdivision lots.

Commissioner Rushton asked if there are current animal rights on the property and if they
will be retained by Lot 103. Mr. Lethbridge reported that there are no animal rights on any
of the lots.

Commissioner Rushton asked when Planning Commission Meetings will be open to the
public. He did not feel that neighbors receive adequate representation with virtual meetings.
Mr. Lethbridge responded that the Governor has relaxed restrictions to a yellow level as a
result of COVID-19. It is anticipated that the Planning Commission Meeting scheduled for
May 28, 2020, will allow for a limited number of attendees. The desire was to open the
meetings to public attendance and comments as soon as possible.

Commissioner Hansen asked if the ordinance prevents the renting of the homes built on the
subject property. Mr. Lethbridge reported that it does not.

Chair Cannon asked if the nine-foot strip of property was designed for economic reasons or
for shared ownership of the retention pond. Mr. Bennett responded that the lots did not meet
the minimum requirements until they were arranged this way. Chair Cannon asked if it is
possible to give this portion of property to Lot 103. Mr. Lethbridge responded that because
of the 14,000 square-foot minimum, it is not possible. The applicant, however, can apply for
a variance, or follow the process for a rezone.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Lethbridge explained that the property owners are not
allowed to place a fence along the property line because of the shared maintenance
agreement and the fact that a fence in front of the retention pond will not excuse the owner
from maintenance. Mr. Bennett reported that the retention ponds are two to three feet deep.

There were no further public comments. Chair Cannon closed the public hearing.

Commissioner James considered the proposal to be a contrived solution to create three lots
and did not support the request.

Commissioner Lefgren did not support the current plan and state that the private lane needs
to be addressed. He commented that the purpose of the Code is to enforce the liability of
the lot and he did not approve of the applicant’s manipulation of the property to fit the zoning.

3.    GENERAL PLAN, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING AMENDMENTS TO THE
      LAND USE ELEMENT OF THE GENERAL PLAN.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the staff report and stated that the above item was discussed
during a City Council Work Session. The City Council discussed areas within their districts
and gave specific direction for Land Use designations within the General Plan. This issue
was noticed as a public hearing, however, given the impact and intent of the General Plan,
approval from the Commission was not required. Mr. Lethbridge desired to provide an
opportunity for the Commission to give feedback and for the public to comment.

Mr. Lethbridge identified the property located north of 12600 South and east of 1300 West.
The current General Plan shows a preservation designation for this property that has current
A-5 zoning. The City Council and the Council Member responsible for the area expressed
the intent to keep the designated zone. For this property specifically, the Council suggested
creating a new designation in the General Plan for agricultural preservation. The three
parcels are designated as low-density residential with a one-third acre minimum lot size,
consistent with the adjacent subdivisions. They have limited and private lane access.

Commissioner James asked if the new designation allows structures to be built on the
parcels. Mr. Lethbridge confirmed that they will and reviewed the A-5 zoning requirements.
Previously, the A-5 zone revision removed agricultural industrial uses. Animals are allowed
but are limited in the area; however, any animal commercial use is prohibited.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that several amendments listed in the new designation are intended
to bring the General Plan in line with the underlying zoning. The properties were displayed
on a map showing potential locations.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Lethbridge reported that there is not a proposed
change to the zoning on the east side of 11800 South, however, the matter was scheduled

to be discussed at the next City Council Meeting. As quickly as the Council approves a zone
change, it is anticipated to be addressed with the General Plan designation.

Mr. Lethbridge next reported and reviewed the potential rezoning of several areas along
Redwood Road.

Commissioner Rushton asked if the purpose of the historical zoning is to retain what
currently exists in the area. Mr. Lethbridge stated that the intent is to maintain the
architectural styles that currently exist. If adopted into the General Plan, the ordinance would
address how the architectural styles would be applied.

Mr. Lethbridge next reported on the City Council’s desire for a revision in the General Plan
concerning elderly housing. He identified areas that are appropriate for elderly housing.
Mr. Lethbridge reported that staff will propose amendments to the City’s Elderly Housing
Overlay to make it more applicable and include limitations and address concerns previously
discussed. The General Plan will not determine densities or lot sizes. Mr. Lethbridge
reviewed the Elderly Housing Overlay Ordinance.

Commissioner Rushton referred to the Calamar developer that asked for the removal of the
senior designation on the back portion of the property. The developer saw a greater need
to develop for non-senior residents. This may go against affordability standards by
restricting it to a specific population. Mr. Lethbridge responded that it is important to be
careful in the zoning created. Commissioner Rushton explained that the aging demographic
will end over the next 15 to 20 years and asked if the City had observed the population
curve. Mr. Rushton responded that the General Plan has a 3 to 5-year life cycle and it is
difficult to plan beyond that. Following the adoption of the General Plan, the City will have
the ability to amend it, if necessary.

Mr. Bott explained that once the areas are zoned for elderly housing, the CC&R’s will prevent
designation changes. Mr. Lethbridge stated that if there are amendments they will apply
only to undeveloped land.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that few properties are being considered for the Elderly Housing
rezone and residents tend to be more comfortable accepting elderly housing. Potential
properties being considered for the Elderly Housing zone were reviewed on a map
displayed.

Chair Cannon asked that if a specific property identified would undergo a downzone if zoned
for elderly housing. Mr. Bott responded that it would not be considered a downzone. Chair
Cannon disagreed and stated that anytime a commercial zone becomes a residential use
the property is considered a downzone. He asked if the property owners have been notified
of the zone change. Mr. Lethbridge responded that because the matter is in the discussion
phase they have not been notified; however, a meeting was scheduled to meet with certain
property owners to discuss the proposed changes. The property displayed is zoned mixed-
use. Mr. Bott explained that an upzone or downzone is based on subjective evaluation.

Chair Cannon explained that complications may occur if a buyer purchases a parcel based
on the current zoning and the City changes the zoning. Property owners need to be involved
in the process. Mr. Lethbridge clarified that the General Plan differs from the underlying
zoning. The property will retain the zoning that is in place and the General Plan is intended
to guide the general land use. Mr. Lethbridge stated that any changes made will not proceed
to the Council until the property owners and residents are notified.

Commissioner James explained that the land use map entitles the land and the zoning map
should be consistent with the land use element. By doing this, residents and property
owners will be notified at a very early stage. Mr. Lethbridge commented this has been a
point of discussion with the Council and they intend to bring the General Plan to a level of
consistency with zoning and their intent with the properties. Mr. Bott stated that the General
Plan should guide zoning decisions.

Mr. Lethbridge next reported on the designation for the southeast side of Redwood Road
and 12800 South. The area is currently designated as Estate density and the Council has
recommended this be maintained. Other designations in the area were reviewed.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that the Stampin’ Up property located at 3600 West and Water
Street is currently zoned for a mix of office and light industrial. The property was identified
on a map displayed. Stampin’ Up is not expanding the business and has marketed unused
property.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Lethbridge responded that opportunities will be
provided for feedback from the residents concerning the property mentioned above to find
the correct land use for the area. There is a discrepancy between the light industrial zoning
in this area and what is designated in the General Plan, so potentially the City may initiate a
zone change.

Commissioner Hartley commented that the property should be low density. Commissioner
Rushton believed that it has possibilities for development. Commissioner James stated that
the site has the potential for townhomes using the same density as proposed.

In response to a question raised, Commissioner Rushton stated that one-quarter acre lots
can be developed in the area and suggested possibilities. It was noted that the subject
parcel was never developed as envisioned.

Commissioner Hartley asked if Stampin’ Up will remain. Mr. Lethbridge reported that they
currently produce stamps and complete work for other companies.

Commissioner Hartley reported that he previously completed a General Plan and shared his
experience. He appreciated staff’s direction and felt it will prevent issues in the future. He
suggested that the Commission be prepared to vote on applications even with resident
disapproval.

Commissioner Hansen commented that if Stampin’ Up were to go out of business, it is
important to consider the impact another light industrial business will have on the area.

Commissioner Rushton stated that because there is no re-designation or identification for
the east side of Redwood Road, the City demonstrates that it is unwilling to determine the
land use subjectively. He suggested that staff complete a table displaying current and
proposed designations including the philosophies explaining each designation.
Recommendations and the areas not being designated should also be identified.

Mr. Lethbridge identified other areas where zone changes were envisioned. He reported on
concerns pertaining to Monarch Meadows and the 13400 South area. The amount of traffic
was not anticipated in this area 20 years ago and has made access difficult for homes facing
13400 South. A community commercial designation for the area was being proposed.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Lethbridge stated that the City is not obligated to
exercise eminent domain and assist the developer in acquiring property. The General Plan
designation identifies areas where commercial development is possible and the assembling
of the ground, fulfilling City requirements, is dependent on developers and property owners.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that a table will be prepared as Commissioner Rushton suggested.
Staff recommended that discussion of the above matter be continued to the May 28, 2020,
Planning Commission Meeting to allow for additional information to be prepared and for
noticing. It was anticipated that the meeting will be more accessible and open for public
comment.
