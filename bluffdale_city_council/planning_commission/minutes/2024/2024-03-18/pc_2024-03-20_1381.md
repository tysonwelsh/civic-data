4. PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on a Proposed
Text Amendment to Bluffdale City Land Use Regulation Section 6.20.020(D)(1)
Regarding Prohibiting Parking of Restricted Vehicles on Public Property, Streets, or
Sidewalks, City of Bluffdale, Applicant. (Application 2024-12) — Staff Presenter, Fred
Donaldson.
City Attorney, Fred Donaldson, reported that there was some discussion regarding whether the
Amendment needed to be presented to the Planning Commission, however, because "Land-Use
Regulation" is a very broadly defined term in the Utah Municipal Code (10-9a-103) the decision was
made to bring it to the Planning Commission. He further stated not all the potential land use

regulations can be found in Title 11 of the Utah Municipal Code. The Amendment being brought
before the Planning Commission is covered under Title 6.
The proposed change is to amend the Parking Code related to truck routes to state, "No restricted
vehicles shall be allowed to park on public property, streets, or sidewalks in the City, except while
loading or unloading merchandise or household goods, and in no event shall any such restricted
vehicle remained parked for longer than eight hours." The Code currently only addresses parking of
restricted vehicles on public streets or sidewalks in areas of the city that are zoned Residential. The
Amendment will expand the definition to disallow parking of restricted vehicles on streets, sidewalks,
or any public property everywhere in the city. They must be parked on private property. |
Mr. Donaldson indicated that the change was necessary because there are some mixed-use areas in
the City, mainly along Porter Rockwell Boulevard where semi-trucks are parking and sometimes
idling for hours at a time in the early morning, which is causing disturbance to the residents in the
adjoining neighborhoods. There is also some concern about semi-trucks parking and staging on
public property in another part of the City.
Chair Cragun asked if, in addition to noise concerns with large trucks or tractor-trailers parking on
public streets like Porter Rockwell, there is a line-of-sight problem for traffic as well. Community
and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, stated that it could be an issue, but it wasn't a
consideration when the Amendment was drafted. The area on Porter Rockwell is not set up for
parking and has a lot of traffic. The Amendment should help the safety issue as well.
Commissioner Swanson stated that he knows there has been a line-of-sight problem at the corner of
14600 South and Rockwell Ridge. When vehicles park along the side of the road going up the hill,
you cannot see well enough to know when it is safe to enter the roadway. He asked if the Amendment
would apply to detached trailers, if it only applies to full tractor-trailers, or if that is covered under
other ordinances. Mr. Donaldson confirmed that a different section of the Code addresses detached
trailers. :
Commissioner Swanson expressed concern that the three-minute window is too narrow since a driver
may be making a valid delivery that could take them a few minutes to complete. He recommended

increasing the time limit to 15 minutes. Mr. Donaldson stated that, per the current Code, the vehicle
can only be unattended for three minutes. It could be longer than three minutes as long as the vehicle
is attended by the driver.
Commissioner Swanson understands this may not be germane to this particular Amendment because
they are only addressing the edits but he can see a situation where a driver needs to go through
reception to get permission to start unloading. As a result, they might have to be away from their
vehicle for longer than three minutes. Mr. Donaldson believed the Amendment was also designed to
discourage idling. If someone is going to be away from their vehicle for more than three minutes
they should turn it off.
Mr. Crowell stated that Title 6 Regulation 6.30.010 outlines general restrictions on parking a detached
trailer. You cannot park a detached trailer overnight on any public street or right-of-way. It does not
cover parking a detached trailer on private property.
Commissioner Swanson asked if a motor home is a restricted vehicle. Mr. Crowell confirmed that
the restrictions are for vehicles 26,000 Gross Vehicle Weight (“GVW”) and greater. He indicated
that some smaller box trucks will not meet the weight restrictions so that might need to be addressed
once there is more data available on enforcement.
Commissioner Swanson had a neighbor who ran an RV rental business out of his home and used
Heritage Crest Way which widens out toward commercial as his parking lot, but he is no longer there.
Mr. Crowell replied that another company was using the County’s Wardle Fields Park at times, but
he was unsure if they were still there. He mentioned that things happen and law enforcement cannot
be everywhere all the time.
Commissioner Luker expressed concern about the negative impact the Amendment could have on
Old West Days and rodeos. For these events, tractor-trailers are used for the animals and parked
overnight on public property. A portable stage is erected on public property for a week as well. He
asked if there is a permitting process to allow for these situations. Mr. Donaldson stated that he
considered adding the phrase "without written permission" to the Amendment. He did not add it, but
it could be.

Mr. Crowell asked if the City would grant licenses in that situation. Mr. Donaldson confirmed that
but did not believe the situation was common. He asked if the vehicles are typically large in that
scenario. Commissioner Luker confirmed that the rodeo's animal trailer is large and typically the
stage is a full-size trailer. Mr. Donaldson agreed that a recommendation should be made to the
Council to add "without written permission" to the Amendment.
Chair Cragun asked if those vehicles park on public streets currently. Commissioner Luker responded
that they are on public property like the arena and the park. Chair Cragun agreed that the language
needs to be added because there are times when contractor or City vehicles need to be parked
somewhere that is disallowed by the regulation. Mr. Donaldson stated that that should be a simple
change and can be added now. Chair Cragun stated that someone with the City would need to be
tasked with providing that written permission.
Commissioner Swanson asked about enforceability. There was a question as to whether the
Amendment was written to be enforceable. Mr. Crowell stated that the legal team has been
strategizing and is working to create a civil enforcement procedure and clarify what is the
responsibility of police officers versus Code Enforcement. When they draft the Code, they believe it
will be enforceable. With regard to human resources in the field, sometimes there is more staff
available and sometimes less so things have to be prioritized. With civil enforcement, there should
be a better capacity to process lower-level violations. Mr. Donaldson stated that the Amendment is
being proposed by the police and by City Prosecutor, Samantha Smith, so he believes they fully intend
to enforce the regulation.
Commissioner Swanson asked if other places in the Code would determine whether the violation is
civil or criminal, which Mr. Donaldson confirmed was the case.
Commission Flynn asked if violators would receive a warning notice prior to ticketing or if they
would receive a ticket without the warning. Mr. Donaldson did not know how such issues are
presently handled, but the administrative enforcement system is not currently in place. Once the
administrative enforcement system is in place, enforcement will change from criminal to civil.

Mr. Crowell stated that whether the police issue a ticket will be at the officer's discretion, but there is
no requirement to issue a warning. Mr. Donaldson confirmed that there is no requirement.
Chair Cragun opened the public hearing.
Courtney Peterson asked if the regulation would apply to school buses. She stated that several
residents park school buses on the road. Mr. Crowell indicated that it could apply because buses are
generally over 26,000 pounds gross weight. Chair Cragun stated that she has seen them park near her
neighborhood when they are early for their routes. Mr. Crowell stated that even without the changes,
it is already prohibited without exception. Mr. Donaldson confirmed that was the case.
There were no further public comments. Chair Cragun closed the public hearing.
Commissioner Flynn moved to forward a POSITIVE recommendation to the City Council on
the Proposed Text Amendment to Bluffdale City Land Use Regulation Section 6.20.020(D)(1),
Application 2024-12), with the following finding:

1, That language be added to allow exceptions when written permission has been

given.
