BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Final Subdivision Plat for approximately 1.32 acres located
at approximately 11875 South 700 West (Exhibit B). The property is currently zoned R4 (Single Family
Residential, 10,000 square foot lot minimum). The applicant is requesting that a Final Subdivision Plat
be approved to allow for three lots within the Jenson Farms development.

The original Jenson Farms subdivision rezoning application and development agreement (“Agreement”)
was approved by the City Council in October 2017. The Agreement outlined a process for dedication of
land to the city for a new park, road stubbing and street connections to the planned subdivision to the
north and a chart showing the minimum number of different sized lots required in the subdivision. The
preliminary plat approved in 2017 had six phases with a total of 85 lots. Since the approval, a majority of
the land east of Willow Creek has been sold to the Canyon School District as a potential school site. That
sale to the school district removed phases 4 and 5 or 28 lots from the development. To date, the
following has been approved:

ANALYSIS
General Plan and Zoning. The Land Use Map of the General Plan calls for the Residential Medium
Density land use designation for the subject property (see Exhibit C). This category is characterized as
follows:




The property has been assigned the R4 zoning classification, supporting approximately four dwelling
units per acre (see Exhibit D). The purpose of the R4 zone can be found in Draper City Municipal Code
(DCMC) Section 9-8-020(B)(4) and states: “the purpose of the R4 and R5 zones is to permit medium to
medium-high density residential developments with single-family homes. These zones are intended to

that require less maintenance and provide a balanced lifestyle.”

The R4 zoning designation is identified by the General Plan as a preferred zoning classification for the
Residential Medium Density land use designation. The subject property is surrounded by R4 zoning on
all four sides.

Subdivision Layout. Phase 6 contains three lots. The three lots will be developed with single family
residences. Two of the properties have access from Ridge Oak Lane and the northern parcel of this phase
is a corner lot with access to both Ridge Oak Lane and Rolling Hills Way. Both of the streets
surrounding these three parcels have been completed at this time. The lots range in size from 20,839
square feet to 16,802 square feet and comply with size, width, and frontage standards for the R4 zone and
the Agreement (Exhibit E).

Criteria for Approval. The criteria for review and potential approval of a Final Subdivision Plat request
is found in Section 17-4-060 of the DCMC. This section depicts the standard of review for such requests
as:

          “Within a reasonable time following the recommended approval of the final plat by the Zoning
          Administrator, the final plat shall be submitted to the Planning Commission for its review and
          consideration. A courtesy notice may be provided for the meeting in accordance with section 17-
          1-085 of this title, but is not required. The Planning Commission shall not be bound by the
          recommendations of the Zoning Administrator and may set its own conditions and requirements
          consistent with this title. If the Planning Commission determines that the final plat is in
          conformity with the requirements of this title, other applicable ordinances, and any reasonable
          conditions as recommended by the City's staff and Zoning Administrator or on the Planning
          Commission's own initiative, and that the Planning Commission is satisfied with the final plat, it
          may approve the final plat. If the Planning Commission determines that the final plat is not in
          conformity with this title or other applicable ordinances or any reasonable conditions imposed, it
          may disapprove the final plat specifying the reasons for such disapproval. No final plat shall
          have any force or effect unless the same has been approved by the Planning Commission and
          signed by the Mayor and City Recorder.”

STAFF RECOMMENDATION

Staff finds that the application complies with the DCMC, and recommends that the Planning Commission
review the request, receive public comment, and approve the application based on the findings listed
below and the criteria for approval, as listed within the staff report.

If the Planning Commission decides to approve the request, staff recommends that they include the
following conditions of approval:

1.        The final subdivision plat must comply with the Agreement, and all amendments to said
          Agreement, and the DCMC.
2.        Provide a title report dated within 60 days clear of any tax liens with the submission of the signed
          mylar per DCMC Section 17-4-030(O).
3.        Change “Lone Ridge Court” to “Lone Ridge Cove” on the mylar submitted to the City for
          signature.
4.        Change address for Lot 603 to 11862 South on the myalr submitted to the City for signature.
5.        Once final plat has been approved, a Land Disturbance Permit is required prior to construction
          activities onsite. Permit is obtained through the Engineering Division.
6.        Any work in a public right-of-way will require an Encroachment Permit obtained through the
          Engineering Division.
7.        Add public utility and drainage easements (PU&DE) per DCMC 17-5-050(G). Lot 603 is
          missing a side lot PU&DE. This must be included on the mylar submitted to the City for
          signature.
8.        Add Clear View Triangles, per DCMC 9-27-080(F), to corner lots. This must be included on the
          mylar submitted to the City for signature.

The findings for approval as are follows:

1.        The proposed development plans comply with the standards found in DCMC Section 17-4-060.
2.        The proposed development plans meet the intent, goals, and objectives of the Draper City General
          Plan.
3.        The proposed development conforms to the general aesthetic and physical development of the
          area.
4.        The public services in the area are adequate to support the subject development.
