2. Mayor's Budget Message
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated the technical changes were different than usual this year, 
due to federal appropriations.  Her office is recommending $ 8.4 million in water 
conservation projects, which will save 11 billion gallons of water a year and set an 
example for other governments, businesses, and households.  It also recommended 
contributing up to $ 7 million to defease the Shelter the Homeless’ bond, so Shelter the 
Homeless can stop fundraising for its capital projects and double down on service to the 
community.  The Mayor ’s Office will continue to roll out great eligible American Rescue 
Plan Act (ARPA) initiatives and government program eligible initiatives.  It is going 
through programs on a weekly basis to see what aligns with the community.  Inflation is a 
realty now, and it is hurting families, so the County needs to prepare as it moves forward 
to next year’s budget to make it easier for people who are having a hard time.  
Mr. Darrin Casper , Deputy Mayor of Finance & Administration, stated fund balances 
are historically healthy.  The budget team has been careful to segregate the major influx of 
federal dollars, but there is a structural surplus, meaning structural revenues are greater 
than structural expenditures.  Fund balances will provide an opportunity to do some 
one-time projects, and there may still be some deferred maintenance projects that qualify 
for ARPA funds.  However, as the County heads into November, that 8.3 percent 
sustained inflation rate will clobber its structural expenditures.  He also expected increases 
for merit employees could outweigh any revenue growth.  The County will have to be 
careful about adding ongoing expenditures next year and focus on just maintaining and 
helping employees cope with inflation.
