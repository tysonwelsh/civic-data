Project Prioritization – 2024 Legislative Strategy LaNiece Davenport, MAG (10:56) Mayor Kaufusi began the discussion by stating that the 2024 Legislative Strategy is intended for Utah County to come together to support a project that has no funding. The objective is to build credibility and come together with a single project that impacts the region clearly and concisely. LaNiece Davenport presented a process to create a prioritized list of transportation projects to give to our legislators at the next session, specifically for one-time legislative appropriations. This process is separate for the Regional Transportation Plan (RTP). She explained that working together will allow Utah County to be successful as having a unified list is more impactful. An outline demonstrating the planning to funding phases of transportation projects as provided. The process is guided by overarching planning goals and the Vetting Criteria tool is transparent, and data driven. Wasatch Choice Vision Goals identify four-key strategies, including transportation choices, housing options, parks and public spaces, and city and town centers Mountainland’s RTP goals include enhancing the roadway grid network, expand freeways, expressways, and arterials, create a robust regional transit system, and connect the active transportation system. MPO Board October 5, 2023 Page 4 Vetting Criteria is applied to all 70 road and transit projects needed in 10 years. Future RTP roads and transit needs can be added when they have completed the technical study. Active transportation will be prioritized later. The Draft Vetting Criteria for road and transit projects, and its prioritized scoring weight was presented and explained. Concern was expressed that the 2019 data is outdated. Tim Hereth, MAG explained that currently, 2019 is the best year available that shows congestion. The model looks primarily at the growth in traffic up to 2030. Ms. Davenport stated that this strategy is not concrete. The goal is to become unified as a county to know what is being advanced though legislation and what is not. A suggestion was made to invite legislators to the November MPO Baard meeting so they can weigh in on the prioritization process. Former Senator Jake Anderegg said it’s important to plan as though there is money so Utah County is prepared in the event funds become available. He also expressed the importance of adhering to the vote that the MPO Board agrees on. It can be detrimental if someone disagrees and starts talking to the legislators behind the scenes. Additional considerations when voting in November include: 1. Priority list should have no more that 5-10 projects. 2. List should include road and transit project. 3. List should have higher and lower cost projects. 4. List should include at least 1 north and 1 south county project. 5. Each voting MPO Board Member to have 3-5 votes in November. Modifications to the next steps list include: 1. Invite legislators to November meeting. 2. Provide a list of projects ranked using the existing criteria prior to the meeting. 3. Take in consideration that a high need in a corridor could possibly be addressed by another corridor, and how that would rank. 4. Include cost/investment per transit rider. MAG will send the priority list electronically and provide instructions. MAG asks that the MPO Board come back ready to vote. Study and Projects Updates - Safe Street for All Charles Allen, Parametrix (55:14) Safe Street for All (SS4A) is a new federal program developed through the newest transportation bill. It is a specific grant directed at improving safety on the streets in America. US DOT SS4A grant program receives $1B annually for 5 years. There are two grant types: 1) planning grants, and 2) implementation grants. MAG was awarded a planning grant in 2022 to complete a safety action plan. Parametrix was hired to do the safety action plan for the MAG area. The primary objective of the grant is to position for future implementation grants and elevate safety in the MPO/RPO project planning and prioritization process. The intent is to develop projects that are data driven. Local government coordination will consist of monthly stakeholder committee meetings and quarterly update to MAG staff. Public outreach will be conducted via pop-up community events, websites, online survey, pedestrian intercept surveys, and in-person and virtual open houses. Next steps and timeline Ongoing: Crash analysis and public engagement Early October: Stakeholder committee meetings begin Late 2023: Early project identification Spring 2024: Plan adoption June 2024: First package of project concepts complete July 2024: Anticipated Implementation Grant deadline Counties and cities can apply individually or collectively. MAG will be in contact with staff to collect information needed to begin the grant process.
