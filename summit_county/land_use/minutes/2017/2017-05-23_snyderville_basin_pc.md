doesn’t allow it.  Commissioner Klingenstein asked Planner Caus if there is any 
flexibility.  Commissioner DeFord added the Commission has defined a retaining wall 
as a structure.  Attorney Brackin said the structure would be in the setback and there is 
not a Development Agreement to adjust the rules.  The setbacks must be met.     
 
Commissioner Franklin said his next item of concern is the gazebo.  There is a pond.  
Even though it is manmade, there is a 100-foot setback.  The next problem is the 
wetlands.  It has a setback of 40 feet that goes to the middle of the pond.  The gazebo 
would be within both the wetland and pond setback.  That is not allowed.   
 
Commissioner DeFord said he would like to go down the path of looking at why the 
rules are created.  Are they violating the spirit of the rule?  Planner Caus said there isn’t 
a setback requirement for detention ponds.  Commissioner Franklin disagreed.  A 
pond is a pond.  Vice-Chair Harte said at an earlier meeting they came to the 
conclusion that is the interpretation of the Code, even though not all of the Commission 
agreed with it.  The idea was to change the Code so that rule doesn’t apply.   
 
Commissioner Franklin said the frontage road has an 80-foot setback.  There are non-
conforming structures in the setback.  These cannot be improved.  Even though this has 
been done in the past, the Commission is supposed to follow the rules.  Mr. Holmes said 
they would not be within the frontage road setback.   
 
Mr. Bridgewater clarified that the Code does not differentiate between a manmade and 
a natural pond.  He said if this is a problem, it can be deleted.  He said if they don’t build 
the retaining wall, the buildings will be much higher.
