      St. Joseph Catholic School. Presentation from Judge Mark DeCaria
      regarding fundraising efforts for building additions to the St. Joseph Catholic
      School
                 Judge DeCaria repmted on the current fundraising campaign for St. Joseph Catholic School; the funds raised will be used
      to facilitate a building addition for the elementary school. The school is located on the comer of 30 th Street and Quincy Avenue
      and the size of the building addition is 18,500 square feet. The current enrollment for the elementary school is 373, 40 percent of
      which are minority students. He noted that 60 percent of the student population receives tuition assistance and he provided
      additional demographical information, emphasizing the importance placed on student academic perfonnance. The elementary
      school is the oldest Catholic school in the State of Utah. He reported the school is attempting to raise $4 million and 62 percent of
      that total has been raised. The kickoff for the fundraising campaign will be held April 1 at the school and he formally invited each
      Council member to attend. He provided documentation to each Council member providing additional information about the
      project, which will include a gymnasium, kitchen, science lab, computer lab, arts studio, extended day program facilities, office
      spaces, two rooms with facilities for special needs programming, and four classrooms to replace the second and third grade
      classrooms.

Public Comments
           Ralph Price thanked the Council and Mayor for their support of the joint proclamation declaring Febmary as Black
History Month in Ogden City. He encouraged everyone to engage with the minority community throughout the month of
February; this would communicate to the residents in the City that actions are more important than words and they truly believe it
is important to recognize and acknowledge the lives of many pioneers who promoted the recognition of Black History in Ogden
City. He stated that the City's Marshall White Center was named for a fallen African American police officer; he recognizes that
improvements have been made at that facility, but more can be done to make it functional and able to provide a benefit to the
community. He would like for the month of February to be meaningful and serve as an actualization of things that are being done
to benefit the African American community.
           Angel Castillo stated she understands that members of the Council have begun discussions with City Administration and
other community partners about the Marshall White Center; however, this issue has been on the community's mind for two years
and they deserve answers about the timeline of the project. She is very uncomfortable with the message that the City does not
know what they will do about the project. She then referenced discussions in tonight's work session about the potential need to
update the City's General Plan and zoning ordinance; the Planning Commission is forced to deny applications on a regular basis
because they do not conform with the zoning ordinance and she finds it unacceptable that something so important has not been
updated because the Planning staff does not have the time or resources to address it. The City needs to find a way to reduce the
burden on essential staff so they can work on vital projects. The City is losing revenue associated with projects that can not move
forward because of City ordinances. She then noted that one of the topics raised by Council member White during the work
session is a concept called 'soft density'; this provides a plethora of options for increasing density in single-family homes and
encompasses land uses such as accessory dwelling units (ADUs), tiny homes, and smaller footprints for a single dwelling. If
property owners and developers can be allowed to build smaller units or build on smaller lots, housing will become more
affordable in the community. There is not just one answer to the housing affordability issues the City is facing, but a combination
of the types of things she has discussed in relation to inclusionary zoning and soft density would be a move in the right direction.
           Alicia Washington stated she is the owner/operator of Good Company Theater and she addressed the topic of discussion
in tonight's work session relating to amendments to the City's arts ordinance. Having served on the Arts Advisory Committee, she
has been amazed by the quality applications for arts funding. Good Company Theater started by receiving arts project grants from
the City and the Theater complied with guidelines for grant awards, which included having non-profit status. She found fiscal
sponsorship from other arts organizations and this was a very difficult process. It was difficult for her to hear that there were other
arts entities that received the same consideration and grant awards without having complied with those same regulations. It made
her re-assess the work being done by the Arts Committee or any sub-committees. Opening the grant program to arts entities that
are for-profit seems to change the quality of the program in her opinion. She offered suggestions such as requiring that for-profit
entities pair with non-profit entities for the purpose of applying for arts funding from the City; this would ensure responsible
spending of arts funding.
