Marshall White Advisory Committee Update

Sean Bishop, Chair of the Marshall White Advisory Committee introduced Whitney Ward, Principal Project Manager
from VCBO Architects, who used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the Marshall White Center (MWC) project.
There are five phases included in the process aimed at determining the scope of the project:

Phase | — community survey to understand current perceptions and needs.
Phase 2 — Advisory Committee explore ideas and prioritize program elements.
Phase 3 - Community feedback; create concepts and identify preferences with the community.
Phase 4 — Advisory Committee to refine preferred solutions and cost impacts.
e Phase 5— Community vision; present final vision to the community.

The project is currently at Phase 4 and the following preferred amenities have been identified: multi-sport court, indoor
turf field, boxing studio, large event space, community kitchen, Head Start Program, flexible classrooms, indoor track, outdoor
recreation pool, indoor pool, open fitness space, and fitness studio(s). Ms. Ward then summarized the feedback provided through
the three open house events, which yielded feedback regarding the top five priorities for the Center. There were also 150
responses to the online survey, in which participants were asked to identify their top three priorities and provide their own
thoughts/comments. She noted she and the Committee have a clear understanding of the desires of the community in terms of
prioritization of amenities to be included in the space. The next step is for the Committee and Administration to carefully review
the community input in preparation for presenting preferred alternative to the Council during the April 19 work session meeting.

Brief discussion among the Council and Ms. Ward centered on public engagement/involvement in the survey process; the
level of input regarding the location of the project versus the amenities to be included in the project; and concerns about including

Visit Ogden Annual Report

Visit Ogden President and CEO Sara Toliver used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the annual report
for Visit Ogden. The presentation touched on topics such as accomplishments over the past year; community advocacy; marketing
efforts; measurable visitor/tourism data; destination development efforts; and team development and retention. Ms. Toliver
indicated Visit Ogden’s goal is to continue with its positive trajectory and she thanked the City Council and City Administration
for their continued support.

There was a brief discussion among the Council and Ms. Toliver regarding the idea of increasing the number of hotel
rooms in downtown Ogden to accommodate convention/visitor traffic and efforts to recruit destination events to Ogden City.

Council Training, Stormwater Pollution

Heather Wadman, Principal Engineer/SWMP Coordinator, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the
Council with training regarding stormwater pollution. A Storm Water Management Program (SWMP) is a series of activities and
local regulations to prevent pollutants from entering the storm system. The presentation centered in the importance of aSWMP;
minimum control measures included in a SWMP; illicit discharge detection and elimination (IDDE) program components;
mechanisms for protecting a stormwater system from non-storm discharges; and collaboration with other agencies to ensure
proper monitoring and enforcement of the SWMP.

Discussion among the Council and Engineering staff centered on past occurrences of stormwater pollution in the
community.

Employee Compensation

Fiscal Year 2022 Budget Amendment — Employee Salaries, COLA; and Fiscal Year 2022 Salary Schedule
Amendments: Human Resources Manager Judkins reported this proposal, along with the separate but associated salary schedule
amendments, would implement Phase 3 of the Classification, Compensation and Benefits Study and would implement full range
penetration for sworn fire, merit employees, non-merit special employees which includes the Mayor and City Council. It should be
noted that non-sworn police employees are also included in the proposal. The total funding needed for the proposed increase
between April and the end of the fiscal year is $765,675. The annual ongoing increase to the budget for these changes is estimated
at $3,062,700. The funding needed to implement the proposed increases will come from recognizing additional revenue in the
General Fund from higher than anticipated sales tax revenue and from existing enterprise and internal service funds. Funding
sources from enterprise and internal service funds will pay for the increases within those funds. As proposed, $481,625 in general
fund revenues will be used with the remaining $284,050 coming from non-General Fund sources.

Additionally, Administration is requesting an amendment to the salary schedules for all sworn fire, merit, and non-merit
employees to provide a two percent cost-of-living (COLA) increase for all steps within the respective ranges. The Administration
is also proposing to implement Phase 3 for sworn fire, merit, and non-merit employees. Employees will be moved to their correct
steps within their respective ranges based on longevity in their current position. Sworn fire employees will also become eligible to
move to the next step on their anniversary date. The effective date of the new salary schedules would be the pay period beginning
April 2, 2022. Sworn police officers are not included in this proposal as they received increases and were moved to the correct
steps in October 2021. The Administration is proposing to increase the salaries of the Mayor and Council members by the two
percent COLA. The effective date of the new salary schedules for the Mayor and Council would be the pay period beginning April
2, 2022. This is proposed as a separate action from the COLA increases for employees.

Brief discussion among the Council and Ms. Judkins centered on the average pay increase for each City employee, with
Ms. Judkins clarifying that all employees, except for sworn police, will receive a two percent COLA in addition to any other
adjustment to their salary. There was also a brief focus on inflation of costs for services and products across the country and the
impact that is having on each city’s budget; the Council expressed their support for fairly compensating City employees as they
are the City’s greatest asset. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith noted that action on both items will be scheduled for a
meeting in April 2022.

Fiscal Year 2022-2026 Capital Improvement Plan Amendment — Airport
Project

Planning Manager Montgomery reported the current proposal is to amend project ARO14 in the Fiscal Year 2022-2026
Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) expanding the scope of the Airport terminal and holding room facility and to increase the budget
for the project. The initial project scope included more modest improvements to the Airport terminal and facility; however, as
additional federal funding has become available through the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security Act of 2020
(CARES), the Coronavirus Response and Relief Supplemental Appropriations Act of 2021 (CRRSAA), and the bipartisan

Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act of 2021, further funding may be appropriated to the Airport for infrastructure
improvements. The initial scope and budget for the project was more modest and included a one-time total budget of $2,070,000.
The modified project scope and budget includes an annual allocation of $1,000,000 per year for the five-year length of the Capital
Improvement Plan. The expansion of the TSA holding room and terminal facility is needed to accommodate multiple
simultaneous flights. The current capacity of the passenger holding room is less than 270 persons. Each of the carriers’ flights seat
up to 180 passengers making it difficult for more than one flight to be processed at the same time.

Chair Nadolski asked if funding for this project has already been considered and approved by the Council. Council
Deputy Director Symes stated the original brief for this project identified $500,000 of Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease
revenue for the project; he asked what will become of that funding given that the project will now be entirely funded by federal
dollars. Comptroller Stout stated that BDO monies will be used to phase construction and will be reimbursed as grants are
received.
