1.1 Statement of Council Chair Max Burdick Concerning the 
Temporary Conduct of Electronic Meetings of the Council 
Consistent with the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act
20-1243
Attachments: Staff Report
OPMA Findings - December 2020
Mr. Mitchell Park , Legal Counsel, Council Office, read the following 
statement for the record:
Consistent with provisions of the Utah Public and Open Meetings Act, Utah 
Code Ann. § 52-4-207(4), and acting in my capacity as Salt Lake County 
Council Chair, I hereby make the following written determinations in support 
of Salt Lake County Council ’s decision to hold and convene electronic 
meetings of the Council without a physical anchor location:
1. Conducting Council meetings with an anchor location that is physically 
accessible for members of the public to attend in person presents a 
substantial risk to the health and safety of those who may be present at the 
anchor location; and
2. This determination is based upon the following facts, among others:
a. Salt Lake County remains under a state of public health emergency 
related to the ongoing COVID -19 pandemic, and significant, continued 
person-to-person transmission of the SARS -CoV-2 virus continues to 
occur in Salt Lake County;
b. State and local health authorities have adopted “State Public Health 
Page 1 of 13
Committee of the Whole Meeting Minutes December 15, 2020
Order 2020-26, ‘Updated Statewide COVID -19 Restrictions, ’” which 
orders institutions in jurisdictions currently experiencing “high 
transmission” of COVID -19, including Salt Lake County, to limit 
public gatherings and ensure strict social distancing, mask wearing, 
and other requirements at public gatherings;   
c. Mayor Jennifer Wilson has ordered that the Salt Lake County 
Government Center be open to the public on an “appointment only ” 
basis during the current period of Salt Lake County Government ’s 
phased re-opening;
d. Public health authorities, including the Salt Lake County Health 
Department, recommend that the health and safety of 
Councilmembers, County officials and employees, staff, and members 
of the public is best served by holding electronic meetings of the 
Council with limited in-person interaction;
e. The Council has prepared for and demonstrated the ability to 
effectively conduct its public meetings electronically, including 
providing members of the public means to observe the Council ’s 
meetings and provide comments electronically.
These written determinations shall be read into the minutes at the beginning of 
the Salt Lake County Council ’s Committee of the Whole and Council 
Meetings, held in Salt Lake City on December 15, 2020, and at future 
Council meetings as appropriate.   Council staff are instructed to continue 
including in the public notice information on how a member of the public 
may view and make comments at the Council’s electronic meetings.
This written determination is effective until midnight on January 13, 2021, and 
may be re-issued by future written determinations of the Chair at that time.
Dated this 15th day of December, 
2020.

3.2 Discussion Regarding Utah Senate Bill Proposal: Property 
Tax Exemption Amendments
20-1251
Attachments: Staff Report
2021 Fl-0465_007 Property Tax Exemption Amendments
PP Impact
Presenters: Kara Trevino, Legislative Director, Council; Tim Bodily, Deputy 
District Attorney; and Kevin Jacobs, SLCo Assessor (Approx. 15 mins). 
Discussion/Direction
Ms. Kara Trevino , Intergovernmental Relations Manager, Council Office, 
reviewed legislation sponsored by Senator Wayne Harper, which the Interim 
Revenue and Taxation Committee voted to endorse.  The legislation expands 
the exemption on personal property from $ 15,000 to $ 50,000, and it does not 
allow for a shift to make up that revenue loss.  The Utah Association of 
Counties opposed the legislation, and school districts are not supportive of 
it.  
Mr. Kevin Jacobs , County Assessor, stated this legislation will shrink the 
County’s tax base, as well as that of other local governments. As a result, tax 
rates will have to go up.  Currently, the County does not send out small tax 
bills with the small business exemption, but if this passes, it will have to send 
out bills for anything reported above the $ 50,000, and that will take time and 
Page 3 of 13
Committee of the Whole Meeting Minutes December 15, 2020
resources.  
Mr. Tim Bodily , Deputy District Attorney, stated this is a property tax 
exemption and violates the premise of the constitution of property tax.  The 
constitution authorizes personal property used by a commercial business to 
be exempt if the revenue would result in an inconsequential amount.  The 
constitutional amendment that was passed 14 years ago, was a property tax 
exemption of $ 3,200 and only applied to those businesses that had taxable 
value under $ 3,200.  This expands that amount to $ 50,000 and changes the 
implementation of it so that all businesses get that $ 50,000 exemption.  It is 
very difficult to justify that as inconsequential revenue.    
Mr. Chris Stavros , Chief Deputy Assessor, stated the impact is over $ 560 
million in taxable value right now, so nearly $ 8.3 million, and that will increase 
overtime as new businesses are added.  The impact to the County will be 
over $1 million a year.  It is a permanent loss in the tax base.  
A motion was made by Council Member DeBry, seconded by Council Member 
Jensen, to take a neutral position on the legislation, but talk to the sponsor to 
see if the County can get him to compromise, and get more information on how 
this will impact the County, and then address it the first of the year or at the 
beginning of the legislative session.
Ms. Trevino asked if Council Member DeBry would amend his motion to include 
working with the Utah Association of Counties on the legislation.   She also suggested not 
taking a position rather than taking a neutral position.
Council Member DeBry amended the motion to include working with the Utah Association 
of Counties on the legislation.
Council Member Bradshaw  stated he was fine with Council Member DeBry ’s direction 
to get additional information, but was not comfortable formalizing that in a motion.   
Mr. Mitchell Park , Legal Counsel, Council Office, stated unless Council Member 
DeBry withdrew the motion, the Council needed to vote on it.
Council Member Bradley amended the motion to not take a position.  Council Member 
DeBry accepted the amendment.
A substitute motion was made by Council Member Newton, seconded by Council Member 
Bradley, to oppose this legislation, and to have Ms. Trevino continue to watch it, and then

COVID-19 
The Council may Take Action, Including Votes, on any

Necessary Legislative Matters Related to the Ongoing State 
of Emergency
Attachments: Staff Report
County Council Briefing -- 12152020
Presenters: Mayor Jenny Wilson; David Schuld, Intelligence Section Chief, Salt 
Lake County COVID -19 Response; Gary Edwards, Executive Director, Health 
Department (Approx. 30 mins). 
Discussion/Direction
Mayor Wilson  stated cases have been stabilizing, but hospitals are now in 
crisis.  She was concerned with the approaching holidays because spread is 
happening within families.  
Mr. Gary Edwards , Director, Salt Lake County Health Department, stated 
the hospitals are still experiencing the peak of the surge that occurred before 
Thanksgiving and have a few rough weeks ahead.  A surge was anticipated 
over Thanksgiving, but that has not happened.  
Mr. David Schuld , Intelligence Section Chief for COVID -19, delivered a 
PowerPoint presentation showing key numbers (current cases, total cases, 
the daily case rate, countywide hospitalizations, and known COVID -19 
related deaths ), an active case curve, an infection snapshot, a zip code 
surveillance, current cases by age distribution, a race and ethnicity analysis, a 
rolling 14-day comparison, a testing analysis, known versus community 
cases, the potential exposure source, and ICU utilization.  
Council Member Ghorbani  asked if additional efforts were being made for 
homeless individuals.  She was concerned about the encampment closures.
Ms. Erin Litvack , Deputy Mayor of County Services, stated the Mayor ’s 
Office has been doing outreach into the unsheltered community since the 
pandemic began.  That work will continue during winter months.  When the 
County closes a camp, it makes sure it has capacity to shelter those living 
there, and they are given an opportunity to collect their belongings before the 
camp abatement is done.
