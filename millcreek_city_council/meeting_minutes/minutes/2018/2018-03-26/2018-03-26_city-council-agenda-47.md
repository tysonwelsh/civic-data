ordinance and get suggestions from the stakeholders on how to revise it. The stakeholders
can give feedback on the ordinance on how it is or is not working. He asked the Council to
provide recommendations for who should be on the stakeholder committee.

2. Discussion on YouthLinc; Justin Powell and Judy Zone

Mayor Silvestrini said YouthLinc is a charitable organization that operates in the City. Justin
Powell, Executive Director, said he lives in Millcreek and explained what YouthLinc is. It is
a nonprofit that was started 19 years ago by Judy Zone, that helps make connections with
students and the community. YouthLinc consists of 5 core programs: Service Year, Real
Life: Teen Refugee Mentoring, Young Humanitarian Award, Local Service Directory, and
Global Community Leadership. Powell briefly went over each program as outlined in the
handout. Powell said their office is located at Brickyard, but they have grown out of it.

Judy Zone, also a Millcreek resident, said the organization began in 1999, but since 2005
they have been housed in donated office space in Brickyard Plaza. She said they would like
to enter into a capital campaign to get their own space. They are currently looking at property
located at approx. 3900 S. and 600 E. across the street from Granite Connection. It is 3 acres
of green space owned by the LDS Church. YouthLinc would like to partner with the City for
a 5000 square foot building next to a city park. She said it would be a dual-purpose building
for YouthLinc and a large meeting space for the community. YouthLinc is the afterschool
provider for every refugee teen in that area and the kids provide over 24,000 hours of
community service a year. Council Member Marchant asked how many employees
YouthLinc has. Powell said 7 full-time, 2 part-time and hundreds of volunteers. The new
building would be about 5-7000 sq. ft. with 9-10 offices, a conference room, a kitchen and
restrooms. YouthLinc does receive funding through the LDS Church. Council Member
Marchant asked if YouthLinc partners with Sunnyvale Neighborhood Center. Powell said it
is overseen by the Asian Association, which is a YouthLinc partner. Council Member
Marchant thanked Powell for his personal volunteer service and asked how much time he
spends outside of the country. Powell said 2-4 weeks a year, but most of the service is local.

Mayor Silvestrini said YouthLinc is involved in the South Salt Lake Promise Program. He
said if there was public facility in that area of the Millcreek, it would greater serve the
refugee community. He said the City has been looking for performance spaces for the arts
and the space would be a nice recreational amenity. The City first needs to find out if the
LDS Church is willing to sell or lease the property to the City. Council Member Uipi thanked
Powell and Zone for the 24,000 hours of service and their leadership. She said the Millcreek
Community Center is great, but it is far enough away from the population that YouthLinc

3. Discussion on Anti-idling and Sustainability in Millcreek; France Barral
Mayor Silvestrini said Holladay and Cottonwood Heights would be interested in partnering
with Millcreek on hiring a fulltime sustainability officer.

France Barral is a resident of Millcreek. She discussed clean initiatives around the country,
such as anti-idling laws and RE100 (renewable energy). She encouraged the City to look into
the 100% renewable initiative because it impacts the community for decades. She said other
cities, including Salt Lake City, have committed to run their own renewable energies by a
target deadline.

Mayor Silvestrini said the City had no energy footprint when it started, but now the City has
the new city hall, a fuel-efficient fleet, and electric car charging stations in front of city hall.
He has talked to the City’s landlord about putting solar panels on the roof of city hall. He
would like to set an example to the residents.

Barral said she does not stand to benefit from this conversation, she does not represent a
company.

Mayor Silvestrini said Rocky Mountain Power is interested in helping cities achieve these
goals. He said the City could look at adding anti-idling signs posted in various places.
Council Member Jackson said Sandy will be considering a similar ordinance tomorrow.
Holladay and Salt Lake City have anti-idling laws with exceptions and do not issue a penalty
until the 3 violation. The Mayor foresees that enforcement will be problematic. Council
Member Uipi would like to look at working with schools to improve education and what it
would take to hire a sustainability officer. Mayor Silvestrini said Salt Lake City sustainability
officers could come talk to the Millcreek Council. Barral suggested to the Council to adopt
an ordinance with the intent that it is unenforceable. Council Member Marchant suggested
adopting a declaration. Barral agrees that a declaration is a good first step.

2.3 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 18-16, Approving a Cooperative
Agreement with Salt Lake County with Respect to the Salt Lake County Storm
Water Coalition
John Miller said the Coalition requested that the City participate in the Coalition, so
the Resolution is for an interlocal agreement with Salt Lake County. The Coalition’s
goal is to keep people from dumping illegally into streams and to keep water clean. It
will also give the City credit towards the UPDES permit. The agreement is $8,190.81
which is a prorated cost weighted on population. The total Coalition budget is
$193,000, which includes public involvement and campaigns. The agreement is set up
for 5 years with an annual cost increase of 3%. Mayor Silvestrini said if the City did
not sign the contract, they would be responsible for educating residents about storm
water. The agreement is saving the City money and ensures the effort is not
duplicated. The City has already determined to do a rain barrel program effective this
spring. He thinks the contract is a good idea because it saves the City money on
something it would have to do anyway.

Council Member Catten moved to approve item 2.3, Resolution 18-16, Approving a
Cooperative Agreement with Salt Lake County with Respect to the Salt Lake County
Storm Water Coalition. Council Member Jackson seconded. Mayor Silvestrini called for
the vote. The motion passed unanimously.

2.4 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-19, Creating the Millcreek
Community Reinvestment Agency

Mayor Silvestrini said a reinvestment agency is the new term for redevelopment
authority. There is one redevelopment area in Millcreek, Meadowbrook. This plan
allows the City to assist in the development of projects by using increased tax
revenue that projects generate and reinvesting the revenue into the infrastructure to
make projects possible. The CRA is a separate entity that is governed by the City
Council and it has the authority to enter into negotiations with taxing entities to
permit part of the increased taxes to be reinvested into the community for these
projects. Millcreek will receive more tax revenue if these projects are developed and
it gives the City more control on projects. The City has had conversations with
prospective developers on a redevelopment area in “the wedge” and other potential
town center areas. He stated that before the City created a reinvestment area, they
would communicate with the public. Residents seem to support the town centers
right now based on feedback the Council had received. Michael Winder reiterated
what the Mayor said.

Council Member Marchant moved to approve Ordinance 18-19, Creating the Millcreek
Community Reinvestment Agency. Council Member Catten seconded. Mayor Silvestrini
called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.
