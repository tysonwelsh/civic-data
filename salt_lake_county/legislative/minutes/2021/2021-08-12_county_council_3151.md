Order of Salt Lake County Council Chair Steve DeBry calling a special meeting of the Salt 
Lake County Council on August 12, 2021, at 2:00 PM.
Dear Colleagues:
Consistent with Utah Code Ann. §17-53-205, Section 2.09 of the Optional Plan for Salt 
Lake County Government, and Section 2.04.090 of Salt Lake County Ordinance, I hereby 
exercise my prerogative as the Chair of the Salt Lake County Council to order a special 
meeting of the Salt Lake County Council to be held on August 12, 2021, at 2:00 PM in 
Room N1-100 of the Salt Lake County Government Center.
The purpose of the proposed meeting will be for the Salt Lake County Council to 
consider exercising its authority under Utah Code Ann. §26-1-114(7)(d) to terminate an 
order of constraint issued by the Salt Lake County Health Department on or about August 
10, 2021.  A copy of the proposed agenda for this special meeting is attached to this 
notice.
Council staff shall provide notice of this special meeting to the Salt Lake County Council 
and Salt Lake County Mayor and shall also provide notice to the public and media as 
required by the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act, Utah Code Ann. §52-4-101 et seq ., 
and Salt Lake County Ordinance.  A copy of this order shall be included in the minutes of 
the special meeting.

Council Member Newton read the following statement into the minutes:
This has been the most difficult decision I have ever made in my seven years as a county 
councilwoman.  It’s difficult because there are passionate parents on both sides who 
deeply love their children and want what ’s best.  It’s difficult because, although we 
have smart, incredible health officials, there are so many unknowns.  Even they admit

they don ’t know everything about the virus and masking effects.  It’s difficult because 
none of us has a crystal ball to see how our decisions today impact the future.
I am grateful for all who have emailed, called, messaged and visited with us at our 
council meetings.  I have listened to all of you.  Half of you will be happy with my vote 
today and the other half will be disappointed.  To those who are disappointed, please 
know I do not make this decision lightly.
My vote is based on two things: the data and what I know about parents.
To my first point - When we are in the business of public health, data -driven decisions 
are key.  We know children under 12 have very low risk with COVID.  Though the delta 
variant is more contagious, and more children will likely get it, the risk of COVID 
complications are still incredibly low for children under 12.
And second - I cannot, in good conscience, tell a parent that government knows better 
than they do for their child, especially when the COVID risk to young children is low .  
Parents have instinct, intuition, and inspiration when it comes to what is best for their 
children.  We know some children are suffering from delayed cognitive development, 
mental health challenges, and other issues because of masks.  And yet we also know 
masks help diminish the spread of the disease.
Each child is unique and has different challenges.  There should not be a 
one-size-fits-all for our kids.  I believe the right solution is to allow parents to decide 
what is best for their child.  I will be voting to overturn the mask mandate, but I still 
highly recommend masks for those who can.
Some public health officials assert we need to mask these little ones in order to keep the 
spread low and reduce demands on our healthcare system.  Our healthcare system is 
near capacity, not with young children, but with adults - many of whom are not 
vaccinated.  I’m not going to put the burden of the community healthcare system on the 
backs of these little children who have very low COVID risk.  I morally cannot do it.
I’m grateful the state is providing KN 95 masks, a much more effective mask, to parents 
who want on for their child.  All parents should consult their pediatrician as they 
determine what is best for their child this school year.
We will most certainly see cases increase once children return to the classroom and will 
continue to watch this situation closely.  I stand by the recommendations the health 
department has provided to our local schools.  I encourage those who can, to please

Council Member Granato stated it is critical to use science to fight the pandemic.  The

Council Member Snelgrove read the following statement into the minutes: 
As a County Council member, my first priority must always be the health and safety of 
the public, especially that of our children.
Sadly, in Utah one child has died from COVID since the beginning of the pandemic 
eighteen months ago . The loss of this or any child is tragic. However, this fact 
reinforces what we have known from the beginning of this pandemic, which is that 
children are less susceptible to the virus.
In considering whether or not to support the mask mandate I must also weigh other 
factors such as the psychological harm to children in part from their reaction to mask 
wearing. This collateral damage from mask wearing and the lockdown has contributed 
to learning deficiencies, increased anxiety, depression, and suicidal thoughts. Sadly, a 
72 percent increase in visits by children to behavioral health emergency rooms has 
been reported. And, worst of all, we have seen a sharp increase in children's suicides 
since the beginning of the pandemic.
I have received thousands of emails and many phone calls from parents seeking relief 
from the mask mandate for their children who they say have experienced psychological 
and emotional trauma due to their use. They earnestly plead with me that the decision 
of mask usage should be left to them as parents. I agree that the decision to wear or 
not wear a mask should be left to caring and loving parents who are best equipped to 
make the proper decision for their children's physical, psychological and emotional 
health. I cannot and should not second guess a parent's judgment. Therefore, in what 
I believe is the best interest of our children's wellbeing, I will vote to overturn the mask 
mandate. For those parents who support the use of masks, please remember that N -95 
masks are available to all children free of charge.

RESOLUTION NO. 5888
A RESOLUTION OF THE SALT LAKE COUNTY COUNCIL TERMINATING A 
PUBLIC HEALTH ORDER OF CONSTRAINT ISSUED BY THE EXECUTIVE 
DIRECTOR OF THE SALT LAKE COUNTY HEALTH DEPARTMENT ON 
AUGUST 11, 2021, IN RESPONSE TO A DECLARED PUBLIC HEALTH 
EMERGENCY
WHEREAS, Utah Code §26A-1-102(9) defines “order of constraint” as meaning “an 
order, rule, or regulation issued by a local health department in response to a declared 
public health emergency .... that applies to all or substantially all ... individuals or a certain 
group of individuals ... and ... requires an individual to perform a certain action or engage 
in certain behavior”; and 
WHEREAS, the Salt Lake County Health Department is the duly established local health 
department for Salt Lake County; and
WHEREAS, Salt Lake County is currently in a declared state of public health emergency 
related to the COVID-19 pandemic; and
WHEREAS, on August 11, 2021, the Salt Lake County Health Department, through its 
Executive Director, issued a public health order requiring all students attending 
kindergarten through grade 6 at a public, charter, or private school in Salt Lake County 
wear a face covering in all indoor settings, a copy of which is attached to this resolution 
as Attachment 1; and
WHEREAS, this public health order is an order of constraint as defined by Utah law; and
WHEREAS, the Salt Lake County Health Department provided notice to the Salt Lake 
County Mayor of the order of constraint no later than 24 hours before issuing the order of 
constraint, as is required by Utah law; and
WHEREAS, the Salt Lake County Mayor approved the issuance of the order of 
constraint, as is required by Utah law; and 
Page 5 of 7

WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code §§26A-1-114(7)(d) and (9)(a)(iii), the Salt Lake 
County Council, acting in its capacity as the legislative body for Salt Lake County, may at 
any time terminate an order of constraint issued by the Salt Lake County Health 
Department by majority vote of the Council in response to a declared public health 
emergency; and
WHEREAS, the County Council now intends to exercise its legislative prerogative to 
terminate the order of constraint issued by the Salt Lake County Health Department on 
August 11, 2021, in response to a declared public health emergency.
NOW, THEREFORE, be it resolved by the Salt Lake County Council, acting as the 
legislative body for Salt Lake County, Utah that:
The Council hereby terminates the public health order of constraint (See Attachment 1) 
issued by the Executive Director of the Salt Lake County Health Department on August 
11, 2021, in response to a declared public health emergency.  The public health order of 
constraint is terminated effective upon approval of this resolution.
