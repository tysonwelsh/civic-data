2. Discussion and Presentation Items
   2.a. Thanksgiving Point Area Plan Amendment Presentation
   Nathan Ricks stated that they’ve looked at many Transit Oriented Developments (TOD) and
   noted that Thanksgiving Point has all the elements to have a successful TOD. The State passed
   a bill requiring cities to have a Station Area Plan for development within ½ mile of the existing
   Frontrunner station. He said it’s critical that this area be developed in a way to maximize the
   use of that station. The Utah County population is estimated to be 1.6 million by 2060, so there
   will be a need to conserve land and water and he noted that sprawl is not sustainable. Mr. Ricks
   stated that the Greer Company, Thanksgiving Point and Stack Development want to develop
   SMART communities. This acronym stands for sustainable, mixed use, attractive, realistic and
   transit oriented. He noted that this is a proposed 40-year plan that would begin on the east side
   and then grow to the west as transportation corridors are developed. They are planning to
   increase the trails in the area including a Riverwalk and noted that they there would be 77 acres
   of dedicated public open space in this plan. Mr. Ricks then presented more details about the

   Councilor Southwick stated that he would like to have an open house for the residents that are

   will help mitigate traffic for this development or will there need to be more road improvements.

   on the east side can be mitigated with just the I-15 completion alone.

   Mike Washburn stated that they are trying to figure out what the long-term best solution is for
   Thanksgiving Point and part of that is commercial development that will provide cash flow to
   accomplish the mission of Thanksgiving Point. He stated that there is no funding right now for
   the road across the golf course, but that could be developed overtime as funding is available.
   Councilor Condie stated that the residents would be very concerned about losing the golf course.
   Mr. Washburn stated that there is a trade off when you’re looking at a golf course that’s used
   by just a few people compared to dedicated open space that can be used by many. He stated that
   they do need to work with the community to understand what the tradeoffs could be, and also
   to understand what the needs and wants are for amenities.

   Councilor Koivisto recommended that they change their map legend when presenting this is the
   public, because low density in this TOD plan is not what most people in the community could
   consider low density.

   Councilor Koivisto inquired if the developer would be willing to build out roads for this
   development. Mr. Ricks stated that they will develop roads that are germane to the project, but
   roads like Clubhouse Drive will need funding from MAG or UDOT.

   Councilor Hancock suggested that they show the residents what this would actually look like,
   and to have a comparison to show is important. He inquired as to when they expect to have a
   grocer in the development. Mr. Ricks replied that he’s confident one will come, but he’s not
   sure which phase that would be in. There was discussion regarding the parking structure and
   how that could be paid for with public and private partnerships.

   Mr. Ricks stated that he would like to do a fieldtrip with the City Council and look at some
   other developments in Salt Lake City and Millcreek.

   There was discussion regarding the long-term viability of the golf course. Mr. Washburn stated
   that they will likely not be able to have a viable golf course if a road is developed through it.

   Councilor Hancock would like to see a traffic study that shows what it would look like without
   the Clubhouse Drive extension and what would the traffic look like without a neighborhood
   grocer.

   2.b. Discussion of Traverse Mountain Trails
   Councilor Hancock expressed concern that motorized vehicles are being used on the Traverse
   Mountain trail system because people could get hurt and they are eroding the trails. There was
   also concern with emergency vehicle access.

   Chief Paul stated that they could issue a ticket for an infraction by State law, but the Council
   could consider adopting its own ordinance too. He said they could have more bike patrols on
   the trails. Another issue with enforcement is that part of the trail in on private property, but they
   can work with Micron on enforcement.

   2.c. Employee Benefits Presentation
   David Kitchen, HR Director, stated that they’ve been doing some research on parental leave
   and what the fiscal impact would be. There are different options and they could start small to
   minimize impact. An example would be 3 weeks parental leave and then 6 weeks for the birth
   mother. Councilor Albrecht stated that employee retention is important and she’d like to look
   at what other cities are doing. Councilor Condie inquired about the impact to productivity. Mr.
   Kitchen replied that he was supportive of the 6 weeks for the birth mother as they are still
   recovering during that time.

   2.d. Budget Work Session with Department Heads
   Dean Lundell, Finance Director, stated that they began working on the budget in January, but
   since then things have changed with COVID-19. He said it’s still too early to know what affect
   this will have on the budget. He noted that the largest budget in the general fund in Police. He
   broke down the general fund by department, expenditures and revenue and explained the impact
   that has on the budget. The total expenditures for the budget is $181,197,237. Mr. Lundell
   outlined the Financial Policies that can be found in the budget document. He highlighted that
   one of the changes to the policies is that the City will look at adjusting property tax rates every
   two years to adjust for inflation. Lehi continues to maintain relatively low utility rates, but there
   will be an increase on July 1st to the storm drain and water rates. This change was approved a
   few months ago and a public hearing was held. Councilor Southwick suggested that the city
   notify residents of the rate increase. There was discussion regarding possible options to save
   money with the economic uncertainty. Mr. Lundell stated that they could do a lesser pay
   increase for employees or hold off on the pay increase for a few months, but overall, he felt like
   the city is still in a good position financially with the reserves and conservative budget.

   Mr. Lundell stated that he would like some direction from the Council about a new program
   offered by GBS called NICE for $117,000. It would allow an option for employees to see a
