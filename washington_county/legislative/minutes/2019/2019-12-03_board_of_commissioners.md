PUBLIC HEARING: COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT BLOCK GRANT (CDBG) /

2020 FISCAL YEAR
Chairman Cox read aloud the following:

I Chairman Cox hereby open the First Public Hearing for the CDBG Program. This hearing
is being called to provide citizens with pertinent information about the Community
Development Block Grant program and allow for discussion of possible applications for
2020 funding cycle. The grant money must be spent on projects benefiting primarily low-
and moderate-income persons. The Five County Association of Governments, in which

Washington County is a member, is expecting to receive approximately $813,000 in this
new program year.

All eligible activities that can be accomplished under this program are identified in the
CDBG Application Policies and Procedures Manual and interested persons can review it
at any time. Eligible activities include examples such as: Construction of public works and
facilities, water and sewer lines, fire stations, acquisition of real property, provision of
public services such as food banks or homeless shelters. In the past Washington County
has received twenty-three (23) grants for projects such as ADA Improvements and Public
Buildings; The Enterprise Senior Center; Water Planning for the Gunlock Special Service
District; The Senior Center/Meals On Wheel Vehicles; and the Gunlock Fire Station for
the Northwestern Special Service District. A complete list of CDBG projects funded for
Washington County can be found on the Five County AOG website. The County has
handed out its capital investment plan as part of the regional “Consolidated Plan.” This
list shows which projects the County has identified as being needed in the community.

Commissioner Cox then invited anyone with questions, comments or suggestions to come
to the podium and the Commissioners would specifically respond to questions and
suggestions during the hearing.

Seeing no one come forward, Chairman Cox closed the CDBG Public Hearing.

PUBLIC HEARING: REZONING ON THE WEST SIDE OF I-15 AT EXIT 40
NEAR NEW HARMONY FROM _THE A-20 AND RA-5 ZONES TO THE PDC

ZONE.

Chairman Cox opened the public hearing explaining that any interested parties should state
their name for the record and would be allowed two (2) minutes to speak for or against the
issue, with the exception of anyone representing a group being allowed five (5) minutes.

Kolob Canyon and New Harmony Community Alliance representative, Darren Adair,
requested for consideration of the original Vision Dixie Plan to be recognized as a majority
of the residents supported that plan when it was introduced in 2015. The plan suggested
that rezoning only occur when the need was warranted. The community does not feel that
the need is warranted as the entrance to Kolob is not currently used at full capacity. The
residents of New Harmony would like to see the area remain rural.

Paul Williams mentioned that the visitation to the area does not exceed 200,000 at the
Kolob entrance. The amount of visitors will likely not be enough to support a hotel should
one be constructed. Mr. Williams also mentioned that light pollution will degrade the
experience for the neighborhood and communities.

Zion National Park representative Daniel Fagergren stated that the Park is opposed to the
development of the exit as the tight entrance to the Park would not be conducive to large
amounts of traffic. A large hotel constructed there would also impede the current views.

Zion National Park has also expressed concerns that security is limited on that side of the
Park and there are concerns with vandalism and crime that may occur with increased traffic.

New Harmony resident David Osborn stated that there would be adverse impacts as the
small fire district that services the area lacks the equipment to assist with fires in any large
facilities that may be constructed in the area.

New Harmony resident Pamela Wittaker spoke against the rezoning.

New Harmony resident Greg Patterson stated that there would be adverse effects in the
area in regards to water usage and quality.

New Harmony resident Robert Lemaster mentioned the close proximity of his property to
the proposed septic system and spoke against the proposed zone change.

New Harmony resident Shirley Solomon mentioned that the area is rural and the people
that live there enjoy the close proximity to two (2) climates, and stated that there are
numerous abandoned gas stations along the highway in proximity to this proposed
commercial development.

New Harmony resident Art Haynes stated that the proposed development has minimal
benefit to the residents of the New Harmony and Washington County.

Bob Hermanson stated that the planned development is not necessarily a project that will
be done quickly or all at once, however, it would be a plan which enhances the exiting plan
of the area.

New Harmony resident Dick Newsberg expressed concern with ATV and dirt bike use
brought with potential camping in the area.

Sue Henson questioned that if the zone change was approved that all the buildings within
the proposal would be constructed at once.

New Harmony resident Stephanie Kelsomparody stated that the current gas station is
sufficient for the area. She also expressed concerns with safety and fire.

Pat Phiffer mentioned the amount of time it takes in emergency situations for responders
to travel to the area.

New Harmony resident Mike Chandler mentioned that an RV Park was completed at Exit
42 is no longer in business.

Allen Wayman stated that he is currently constructing a home in the area and questioned
the property tax impact on his property if a new development were to be constructed.

CONSIDERATION _OF ORDINANCE NO. 2019-1148-O, AN ORDINANCE
REZONING A CERTAIN PORTION OF WASHINGTON COUNTY LOCATED
ON THE WEST SIDE OF I-15 AT EXIT 40 NEAR NEW HARMONY FROM A-20
AGRICULTURAL _20 ACRE MINIMUM LOT SIZE) ZONE AND RA-5
(RESIDENTIAL AGRICULTURAL 5 ACRE MINIMUM LOT SIZE) ZONE TO
THE _PDC___ (PLANNED __ DEVELOPMENT __ COMMERCIAL) _ ZONE.
HEREINAFTER FULLY DESCRIBED IN THIS ORDINANCE

Commissioner Iverson stated that the proposed project does not seem feasible as the New
Harmony community does not have the water infrastructure or adequate public safety to

support it.

Commissioner Almquist expressed appreciation to the Planning Commissioners and
mentioned that counties are unique in regards to development in sparse populations and do
not necessarily have the ability to stand-alone. Growth has benefits but also has challenges,
and it is his opinion that the current area does not support the proposal at this time.

Chairman Cox stated that if commercial facilities are to be allowed in an area, adequate
infrastructure and public safety needs to be in place in order to keep everyone safe in the
event of an emergency.

Commissioner Almquist also mentioned that it was not the intent of the developers to cause
harm to the area and hoped that the residents would carefully consider the fact that
developers often pioneer efforts to provide services in areas that may need them.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Iverson to Deny Ordinance No. 2019-1148-O,
An Ordinance Rezoning a Certain Portion of Washington County
Located on the West Side of I-15 at Exit 40 Near New Harmony from
A-20 (Agricultural 20 Acre Minimum Lot Size) Zone and RA-5
(Residential Agricultural 5 Acre Minimum Lot Size) Zone to the PDC
(Planned Development Commercial) Zone, Hereinafter Fully
Described in this Ordinance. Motion seconded by Commissioner
Almquist and carried by unanimous vote, with Commissioners Cox,
Iverson and Almquist voting aye.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. _R-2019-2535, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING A_ FEASIBILITY STUDY REGARDING A PUBLIC TRANSIT
SYSTEM FROM ST. GEORGE TO ZION NATIONAL PARK

Commissioner Almquist explained that a transit system is being proposed from St. George
to Zion National Park to assist in alleviating parking issues in Zion and Springdale. A
transit system may also provide a cost-efficient means for commuters in the area.
Washington County has approached Zion Public Finance seeking their expertise in
conducting a comprehensive fiscal feasibility study. The Utah Department of
Transportation is also willing to reimburse the County for the costs of the study.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Almquist to Approve Resolution No. R-2019-
2535, A Resolution Approving a Feasibility Study Regarding a Public
Transit System from St. George to Zion National Park. Motion
seconded by Commissioner Iverson and carried by unanimous vote,
with Commissioners Cox, Iverson and Almquist voting aye.
