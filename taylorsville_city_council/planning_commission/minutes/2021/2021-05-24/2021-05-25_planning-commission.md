     4.1   Mr. Jim Spung presented this item.

           4.1.1    He advised it would be one presentation including both items. He began his presentation
                    saying that         the General Plan is the guidance document for all land development. The
                    definition for Low-Density Residential is: “Low Density Residential (LDR) districts are
                    residential areas that contain existing or proposed residential developments less than6
                    dwelling units per acre (excluding ER Districts) Properties that are assigned the LDR
                    classification are generally (but not necessarily limited to) neighborhoods consisting of single-
                    family dwellings.” The definition for “Medium Density Residential (MDR) districts are
                    residential areas that contain existing or proposed residential development with a density
                    between 6 and 8.9 dwelling units per acre. Land assigned the MDR classification typically
                    contains townhouse and condominium type developments as well as higher density single-
                    family dwelling neighborhoods, (i.e., R-1-5 zoning districts).”

                              Mr. Spung’s answers to her follow:
                              The General Plan map amendment establishes the policy/vision for what types of
                              land uses are appropriate in certain areas of Taylorsville. The General Plan map
                              designation is broader and addresses the general character of development. The
                              General Plan defines “low-density residential” and “medium-density residential”
                              based on the density of development. Low-density is defined as anything 6 units per
                              acre or less – this translates to residential lots at least 7,260 square feet in size. The
                              applicant is proposing to create 6,000 square foot single-family lots so that bumps
                              them into the “medium-density residential” category. Similarly, the number of
                              townhouses proposed would require a “medium-density residential” designation.
                              The Zoning Map designation is the law that implements the General Plan vision. The
                              zoning of a property dictates what can be built, how big it can be, and the
                              development quality standards required. The proposed R-1-6 zoning for the single-
                              family detached homes will allow a single home on a lot at least 6,000 square feet in
                              size. Other development standards also apply (setbacks, height, parking, etc.). The
                              proposed RM-10 zoning will allow multi-family development (i.e., townhouses) up to
                              10 units per acre. If the map amendments are approved by the City Council, the
                              applicant will be required to submit a subdivision application for review. At this
                              phase, the City will review the development proposal to ensure it satisfies all of the
                              zoning standards adopted in the City Code.
