Public Health Emergency Declared by the Salt Lake County

Attachments: Staff Report

Discussion/Direction

Ms. Dorothy Adams , Deputy Director, Salt Lake County Health 
Department, stated she was here to ask for an extension to the Emergency 
Declaration set to expire September 27, 2022.  The Health Department would 
like to extend the declaration to October 13, 2022, to correspond with the 
Federal Government ’s public health emergency declaration.  The Health 
Department is seeing stabilization of hospitalizations due to COVID -19, but it 
continues to see disparities in vaccine uptake and severe cases in the west 
side communities.  It is continuing with its outreach efforts in those areas, 
and the County is still spending several hundred thousand dollars monthly in 
FEMA eligible expenditures.  The Mayor ’s Office and Health Department 
feel extending the declaration is financially prudent. 
Mr. Darrin Casper , Deputy Mayor of Finance and Administration, stated 
Mayor Finance continues to receive FEMA -eligible reimbursements.  If the 
County is spending money on FEMA -related projects and the Federal 
government has the emergency declaration open, it seems prudent to 
consider extending the County ’s emergency declaration as well.  He stated 
the resolution goes through January 3rd. 
Council Member Theodore  stated she could not believe the County was 
still using the emergency as an excuse to get these funds.  She did not 
consider it to be a true emergency.  
Council Member Alvord  stated he shared Council Member Theodore ’s 
sentiments and asked if the County would be declaring an emergency today. 
Mr. Mitchell Park , Legal Counsel, Council Office, stated it is the same 
emergency that was declared in March of 2020.  County ordinance and State 
law require that if an emergency extends beyond thirty days, the legislative 
body must vote to continue that emergency.  The County Council has done 
so twenty times so far.  He read from County ordinance: “A disaster or 
emergency means a situation causing or threatening to cause widespread 
damage or loss of life or significant property damage resulting from an 
attack, internal disturbance, natural phenomenon, public health 
emergency, or technological hazard .”  He then stated a public health 
emergency is separately defined as "an occurrence or imminent credible 
threat of an illness or health condition caused by including an epidemic or 
pandemic disease."
