                                            REZONE

GENERAL PLAN CONSIDERATIONS:

The 2016 Midvale City General Plan locates the subject property in the 7200 South Opportunity.
In Opportunity Areas, the General Plan states that

“[M]inor-to-major changes in current land uses are likely to occur due to market forces,
increasing land values and opportunities to optimize land uses to take advantage of transit and
other public investments. Projects in Opportunity Areas are anticipated to be at higher levels of
density than current land uses and should be carefully planned and designed to integrate into the
fabric of the area and minimize impacts on adjacent and nearby existing land uses. Future
proposed uses in the Opportunity Areas may or may not be consistent with current zoning, and
the desirability of the proposed use and the need for re-zoning should be evaluated on a case by
case basis.”

The General Plan identifies these land use goals for the East Gateway Opportunity Area:

   1. Provide for higher density housing options and taller buildings in the area around the
      TRAX station
   2. Develop a small area plan for the development near the TRAX station
   3. Strengthen the retail areas by diversifying the mix of retail and adding more destination
      and experience shops
   4. Enhance 7200 South with urban design standards and safer pedestrian crossings

   5. Provide and implement new bike routes to complete transportation connections to other
      areas in the City
   6. Support redevelopment of underutilized properties to provide attractive commercial/light
      industrial flex spaces
   7. Work with UDOT to provide enhanced pedestrian access and improvements along 7200
      South near the I-15 interchange

The proposed rezone is consistent with the General Plan because it furthers goal #1 by providing
higher density housing by replacing 4 existing units with 17 residential units, and by raising the
height from 1 story ranch homes to 3 story townhomes. The redevelopment meets goal #5 and #6
though dedication to Midvale of 4 additional feet of right-of-way along Fort Union that will
expand the existing sidewalk to 10-foot wide along this entire block.

ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS:

Section 17-3-1 of the Midvale City Municipal Code requires the Planning Commission hold a
public hearing and adopt a written recommendation to the City Council, advising the Council to
approve, disapprove, or modify a request on all zoning map amendments. The Planning
Commission may only recommend approval of a rezone application if it determines, in written
findings, that the proposed rezoning is consistent with the policies and goals of the general plan
and that the applicant has demonstrated the following:

   1. The proposed rezoning is necessary either to comply with the general plan proposed land
      use map or to provide land for a community need that was not anticipated at the time of
      adoption of the general plan;
   2. The existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or that it
      failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the natural
      characteristics of the land including, but not limited to, steep slopes, floodplain, unstable
      soils, and inadequate drainage; or
   3. The land or its surrounding environs has changed or is changing to such a degree that it is
      in the public interest to encourage redevelopment of the area or to recognize the changed
      character of the area.

In addition to meeting goals of the General Plan, the proposed rezone also satisfies requirements
of Section 17-3-1 by meeting #3 above. The area around the subject site has changed rapidly and
extensively. The area has a diverse mix of business and residential opportunities. The subject site
is on a busy Primary Corridor and provides an ideal residential density buffer to the single-
family/duplex zone to the south. A stated purpose of the 7200 S Overlay Zone is to facilitate
transition of the corridor from single family residential neighborhoods to a mix of commercial
and residential uses. As justification for the rezone, the applicant states that a MU zone would
allow the “highest and best use of these properties” along a main thoroughfare. The proposed
rezone to MU would be consistent—and compatible—with the emerging character of the
neighborhood area.

Notice of the proposed rezone was mailed to 61 property owners within 300 feet of the project,
posted on the property, posted to city and state websites, and advertised in the Salt Lake Tribune.
Notices were also sent to 23 “affected entities” as defined by Utah Municipal Code Title 10-9a-
102. As of Mar 16, 2021, no comments have been received.

   12. Within and adjoining the site, impacts on the aquifer, slope retention, and flood potential

Townhome project is further supported by the City’s 2019 Housing Plan. It provides higher
density housing in strategically located areas of opportunity and encourages reinvestment in the
property, benefiting residents and the community.

Based on submitted documents and comments from the Fire Marshall, City Engineer and Public
Works, the use is not detrimental to the public health, safety, and welfare of the community.

The areas highlighted yellow are included in the conditions of approval for the final site plan
application. The proposed project meets all lot and development standards. The development
consists of 4 buildings with 3 stories each. The buildings contain 4-5 townhome units with either
2 or 3 bedrooms. Many have unfinished basements. Each townhome unit has enough area to
have fenced front or rear yards. The design features and materials are consistent with adjacent
residential development patterns.

The two structures along Fort Union Blvd are at the required build-to line which provides the
best pedestrian experience. The street-facing facades have the required percentages of high-
quality construction material. The site plan indicates an improved pedestrian network around the
development and recreational amenity spaces with access from each residential unit to Fort
Union and 525 E. The private drive, guest parking, amenities, and sidewalks allow adequate
circulation.

The building on the corner of Fort Union and 525 E (unit #1) has two front yards that face public
streets. Title 17 requires an entry door on or within 3 feet of both frontages that must be treated
as a primary element of the front of the structure. Unit #1 currently does not have a front door
within 3 feet of 525 E, and staff recommends adding this to the conditions of approval. All other
architectural standards are met. Consequently, the mass, scale, orientation, and design of the
proposed subdivision is compatible with the nature and character of the surrounding single-
family and mixed-use neighborhoods.

Planning and engineering staff determined a traffic study was not required for this project
because of its size and configuration. There are no adverse, unmitigated impacts to traffic or
street design. The General Plan’s future vehicle network identifies that this portion of Fort Union
Blvd will be at 50-75% of capacity by 2040. There are no private streets, just a shared drive
aisle. However, this drive aisle will be assigned a South coordinate and street name for
emergency response and addressing purposes.

Sufficient utility capacity will be verified and guaranteed through provision of the water and
sewer duty to serve letters required by the subdivision process.

For a master planned development, parking standards are determined by the Planning
Commission based on the proposed uses and the potential for shared parking. The Planning
Commission typically allows parking at the ratio of 2.25 spaces per unit. This project has eleven
units with 2-bedrooms and six units with 3-bedrooms, which would require 42 spaces. The
subdivision provides 42 spaces. There are 2 garage spaces per unit and 8 spaces spread
throughout the project as guest spaces. There is no individual driveway space for the residential
units to use as parking. Residents must park inside their garages.

A submitted photometric for the outdoor lighting plan meets the minimum light trespass at the
subdivision perimeter. Total lumens, correlated color temperature, and down directed luminaires
all meet lighting requirements that mitigate impact on surrounding uses. A 6-foot tall fence will
be placed along the entire southern perimeter that will serve as an adequate buffer to the single-
family zone to the south.

The proposed landscape plan meets the type, amount, locations, and species required in the zone.
An irrigation plan will be reviewed as part of the final site plan. Staff recommends the applicant
provide a percentage calculation of the proposed evergreen materials.

The applicants propose 23,609 square feet (50%) of general open space which meets
development requirements for master planned projects in the RM-25 zone. For amenities, there is
a perimeter sidewalk, picnic table, dog run, and BBQ grill along the south property line.
However, the square feet and percentage of improved common space is not defined. Staff
recommends the applicant provide a percentage calculation of improved common areas which
include sidewalks to the common areas.

The City Engineer found that the proposed development is appropriate for the topography, and
there are no development impacts on the aquifer, slope retention, or flood potential.

The applicant has not provided articles of association and by-laws for the proposed association.
Staff recommends these documents be submitted for review by the City prior to approval of a
final subdivision application and recorded with the subdivision plat.

4.     TXT-21-03; Zoning Ordinance Text Amendment; Citywide; Text Amendment
       Modifying Open Space Standards for Master Planned Developments in RM-12
       Zone; Adam Nash/Land Development LLC (Applicant); Alex Murphy, Planning
       Director (Staff).


PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION MARCH 24, 2021
Mr. Alex Murphy stated that in 2001, the City adopted a new zoning ordinance following
annexation of the Fort Union area. This ordinance established revised the City’s ordinance and
zoning map to fold the former County and City zoning ordinances together. As part of that
ordinance, standards for master planned developments were carried forward from the City’s prior
zoning ordinance with minor changes. Two of the standards adopted addressed open space: a
requirement for 15% of the lot as improved common open space 1 and 50% of the lot as general
open space 2. These standards have frequently been discussed as projects have occurred over the
years but have not been changed since they were adopted in 2001.

In response to concerns raised during the planning process for the Midvale Mills project, Mr.
Nash has proposed a modification to the two open space requirements. Pursuant to the text
amendment process described in §17-3-1 of the Midvale City Municipal Code, Staff has worked
with Mr. Nash on the requested changes and a draft of the proposed changes is now available for
Planning Commission consideration. This text amendment proposes changes to Section 17-7-3.9,
adding the following as a new subsection E:

         Notwithstanding the requirements of Section 17-7-3.9(C), if the proposed master planned
         development is in excess of 10 acres, anticipates more than 60 residential units, and is
         adjacent to properties zoned with an agricultural overlay under Section 17-7-1.15 along
         at least 65% of the project boundary, the applicant may reduce the amount of the
         improved common open space by up to six percent (6%) so long as the total minimum
         open space requirement is increased by the same amount as the improved common open
         space reduction.

This change would create an opportunity for larger master planned development projects in
specific locations to reduce their improved common open space requirement, provided the
general open space is increased by the same amount. Projects eligible to take this option will not
happen frequently, as there are few places left in the City where 10+ acres are available for
development adjacent to property with the Agricultural Overlay.

However, even with limited applicability, Staff does not support this proposal for two primary
reasons: it is not consistent with the City’s General Plan and the proposal is requested to address
an issue that is not related to the zoning ordinance.

First, it is not supported by the General Plan. Areas with the Agricultural Overlay generally fall
within Stability Areas as defined by the General Plan. Included in the goals for Stability Areas
are the following:
1
  “Improved, common open space” means open space, held in common ownership, that is improved beyond that of
natural open space to facilitate an activity or series of activities, including such uses as mini parks, picnic areas,
playgrounds, gazebos, basketball courts, tennis courts, recreation areas and structures such as club houses, pavilions,
swimming pools, skate parks, and other similar uses and facilities. Improved common space does not include for
profit recreational facilities, secondary recreational facilities that happen to be located in an area or on a surface that
has a different primary use such as a basketball court located in a roadway, activity areas that are not held either in
common ownership or public ownership, or areas and/or facilities that do not reasonably lend themselves to the
intended activity. Any other uses specifically listed within each particular zoning district shall not be construed as
falling within this generic definition. (17-2-9 “I” Definitions)
2
  “Open space” means the area reserved in fields, pastures, parks, courts, schools, playgrounds, golf courses, yard,
and other similar open areas that are open to the sky or are open to view on at least two sides, are readily accessible
by foot traffic from the building to which they are accessory; and are not part of any parking area, driveway space,
public or private street or road, or building footprint. (17-2-15 “O” Definitions)
PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION MARCH 24, 2021
   2. Buffer uses in Stability Areas from more intensive land uses nearby, including adjacent
      Opportunity Areas.
   3. Provide for better pedestrian/bicycle connections through and between neighborhoods.
   4. Provide for access to parks, trails and recreation facilities. (pg. 46)

The General Plan also states that the City needs more open space, not less, because it already
falls below the open space per capita recommended by the National Recreation & Park
Association. Reducing improved common open space requirements for master planned
developments will move projects away from meeting these goals, not towards them, and weaken
amenity options for large master planned developments in the future.

Second, the amendment is tailored to resolve an issue that is caused by design decisions and not
the zoning ordinance. Despite applying across the entire RM-12 zone, this request comes as part
of the Midvale Mills project on 8000 South. That project can meet the existing requirement of
15% improved common open space with a trail connection to 150 E and a community garden on
the east side of 300 East, as was shown in the Planning Commission meeting on February 24,
2021. Some neighbors have objected to the trail connection and Mr. Nash has expressed
dissatisfaction with the community garden. If this amendment were approved, those
improvements would be removed from the plan, reducing the overall open space for the project.
This text amendment is not the only option to resolve concerns with the project’s design: lot
sizes could be reduced across the project or units removed to provide the required improved
common open space without requiring the trail connection or community garden. Modifying the
zoning ordinance to address a single project is poor planning and establishes a precedent that the
City is willing to create exceptions to the zoning ordinance for developers.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION:

For the reasons indicated in this Report, Staff recommends forwarding a negative
recommendation to the City Council regarding the proposed text amendment modifying open
space standards in the RM-12 zone.

Midvale Planning and Zoning Commission,

Dear Members of the Midvale Planning and Zoning Commission

We, the HOA Board of the Larson Cove Community, are writing to express our support of the
zoning ordinance text amendment submitted by Adam Nash and his proposed Midvale Mills
development project.

The Larson Cove Community consists of twenty homes and is directly adjacent, on the west and
south sides, of the land where the Midvale Mills community will be built. Furthermore, the so-
called “trail connection” directly touches five homes in our community. As was referenced in
the Planning and Zoning Commission Staff Report authored by Alex Murphy, there has been
extreme concern expressed by our community regarding this pathway. This pathway, for reasons
to be outlined, is ill-conceived and if implemented will have a detrimental impact on the safety and
well-being of both the Midvale Mills and the Larson Cove Communities. We applaud the
developer, Mr. Nash, for meeting with members of our community, recognizing our concerns
regarding the negative impact of the path, and submitting a request for the text amendment. We
are quite surprised and perplexed by Mr. Murphy’s opposition to the proposal. We believe
granting this text amendment is clearly in the best interest of the proposed Midvale Mills
development, the existing Larson Cove Community, as well as Midvale City as a whole.

We would like to respond to several of Mr. Murphy’s arguments for not recommending approval
of the text amendment.


PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION MARCH 24, 2021
Argument 1: Not Setting a Precedent for Future Developments

The reason text amendments exist is because unique situations exist. One size does not fit all, and
while exceptions cannot become the norm, text amendments are for the very purpose of protecting
community members when the letter of the zoning ordinances are in conflict with the well-being of
a community. This particular case is a prime example. We understand Mr. Murphy’s concern in
not wanting to open the floodgates for text amendments in the future; however, he himself
acknowledged that this situation was unique, and the existence of land where this type of
amendment would even apply is almost negligible. Furthermore, not protecting the best interest of
a community because there exists a slight possibility that the city may have to tell a developer
“no” in the future, does not seem to be in line with the purpose of government.

Argument 2: Not Endorsed by the General Plan

In Mr. Murphy’s report to the commission, he referenced some specific directives in the General
Plan. The area allocated for the “trail connection” is already developed and has been
incorporated into yards as was agreed upon many years ago. Designating this path, which would
cross a driveway and would create a narrow corridor opening to 150 E, as “open space”, or
“providing for better pedestrian/bicycle connections through and between neighborhoods”, or
“providing for access to parks, trails and recreation facilities” is nothing but a farce. 150 E is a
road that has no sidewalk, no access to tracks, and has, directly across from the proposed opening,
a large industrial complex including a scrap yard. The path does accomplish the goals of the
General Plan, but instead would harm both communities by creating safety concerns and inviting
perfect cover for nefarious activity. Mr. Murphy mentioned complying with the recommendations
of the National Recreation & Park Association. We would love for representatives of the National
Recreation & Park Association to come to our neighborhood and tell us whether their vision of
“open space” would be better served by leaving the land as well manicured yard space, as
currently constituted, or chopping it up into a nonsensical alley leading to nowhere.

Argument 3: The Developer Can Reduce the Number of Lots

The final reason Mr. Murphy explained for not approving the text amendment is the most
confusing. He states the developer can simply reduce the number of lots in the development. Why
this is so troublesome, is that this recommendation conflicts with the previously mentioned General
Plan and previous recommendations from Mr. Murphy himself. When the first proposal was
submitted to change the zoning of this property, it included high-density housing in the form of
townhomes. Mr. Murphy recommended the passing of these plans on the basis of the General Plan
and the directive to create additional housing because of the housing shortage. At each additional
planning & zoning and city council meeting, the reason to change the zoning was to conform to
the General Plan and to help reduce the housing shortage by increasing the number of available
homes. Upon examination, it does not make sense to see Mr. Murphy suggest a reduction in lots,
in conflict with the General Plan, when the text amendment would provide an ideal solution that
doesn’t require a reduction in lots.

Text Amendment - the Right Solution

In the spirit of aligning competing priorities, we believe approving the text amendment is the right
solution for the community, the developer, and the city. By approving the text amendment, you will

city will gain a beautiful new neighborhood that furthers its mission and offers additional
opportunities for people to live and grow. Please recommend to approve this text amendment for
