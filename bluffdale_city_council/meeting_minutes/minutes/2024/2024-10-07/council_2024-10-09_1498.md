       7.1     Resolution 2024-70 - Recommending Award of CM/GC Contractor for 14600
               South Storm Drain Outfall Proiect. (StaffPresenter Addison Mitton).

Assistant City Engineer, Addison Mitton presented the Staff Report and stated that the retention pond
was constructed in 2022 to collect stormwater from the southeastern area of Bluffdale and needs an

outlet pipe to handle expected long-term volume. The retention pond will not collect stormwater from
the Point project as it will have its own retention ponds but they have expressed a desire to partner on
the project and share the same outfall to the river. Once the design is completed, the project will be
shared with the Point proportionate to their share ofthe improvements.


Mr. Mitton identified the following obstacles with the project:

        Union Pacific Railroad and the UTA railroad tracks, bridge, and columns.
        The Salt Lake City Canal.
        A residential neighborhood.
        The high water table.
        Bangerter Highway and the steep slope between it and the neighborhood.
        The steep slope to the river.
        Power lines.


The project will require a Construction Manager General Contractor("CMGC"),who will begin work
during the design phase rather than when construction begins. The contractor assists with permitting.
                                  BLUFFDALE CITY COUNCIL
                                       MEETING MINUTES
                                    Wednesday, October 9,2024

which in this case will require providing Union Pacific Railroad with the specific means and methods
the contractor will use to install the pipeline. They also help identify and plan for risks, give
constructability feedback and, through value engineering, find ways to reduce costs. It is very
valuable to the City to bring the contractor on early in the design process so they can help reduce
project costs.


The Request for Proposals("RFP") was advertised on September 5, 2024, and closed on September
20, 2024. Three proposals were received. The Selection Team, which consisted of City Engineer
Michael Fazio, Mr. Mitton, and two Point project representatives, reviewed the proposals and applied
a ranking system. The highest scoring proposal and the Selection Team's recommendation was the
Ralph L. Wadsworth / Stacy and Witbeck joint venture. Ralph L. Wadsworth has heavy construction
experience and Stacy and Witbeck has extensive railroad experience.

Mayor Hall reminded the Coimcil that the CMGC process was used for the one-way bridge project,
which was also awarded to Ralph L. Wadsworth / Stacy and Witbeck.

Council Member Wilding asked how the split between Bluffdale's financial responsibility and those
of the Point would be identified. Mr. Mitton reported that there are different pipe size requirements.
Bluffdale requires a 36-inch pipe and the Point project will need a 60-inch pipe. The larger pipe will
be placed deeper, construction will be more difficult because the pipe sections will be heavier, and
permitting the railroad will be more complex. All those costs will be factored in. The cost of the
Bluffdale and Point projects will be compared, and each entity will pay its proportional costs.

In response to a question fi*om Council Member Lord, Mr. Mitton indicated that the FrontRunner
station will be located south of the retention pond. The pond currently does not have an outlet, so it
has limited capacity. When southeastern Bluffdale is built out, its capacity will need to be increased.
An outfall pipe will increase the capacity because it will decrease the water level as it comes in. City
Manager, Mark Reid added that it is a regional retention pond that takes water fi*om 14600 South and
the surrounding area to the south. The pipe will run north along the railroad tracks, cross under the
tracks and canal, then follow the southern embankment of Bangerter Highway to the river.

               OakmanX

Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman presented the Staff Report and stated that Staffis very excited
about the number ofbusinesses opening in Bluffdale, however,the new businesses have brought with
them some issues with temporary commercial signs. Code Enforcement Officer, Russell Cramer
recently spoke with the City Council and discussed these non-compliance issues. Staff wanted to

Ms. Oakman displayed examples oftemporary signs, including:

    •   Quill/feather/blade signs;

    •    Vinyl signs affixed on buildings;
    •   Inflatable signs;
    •   T-post banners; and
    • Pennant signs.


Non-compliant signs that are seen in Bluffdale include:

    • Temporary signs used beyond promotional periods.
    •   Temporary signs used as permanent signs.
    • Prohibited signs like inflatable and blade signs.

Current sign regulations are as follows:

    • Banners up to 24 square feet are permitted through a Temporary Use Permit.
    • Blade/feather/quill signs are prohibited.
    • Inflatable signs, both moving and stationary, are prohibited.
    • A-frame signs are allowed without a permit.

Staffresearched sign ordinances in eight different communities and determined that:

    • Most cities allow temporary banners and feather^lade signs during promotional periods.
    •   Most cities prohibit inflatable signs.
    •   Most cities require a permit for temporary signs.
    • Most cities have an extended duration for which temporary signs can be displayed during
        grand openings.
    •   Most cities do not require a permit for A-frame signs.
    • Bluffdale City strikes a balance with other cities in terms ofaesthetic and business promotion
        considerations, as well as what is permitted and prohibited.

              Procurement Policy. (StaffPresenter, Fred Donaldson),

Mr. Donaldson presented the Code Amendment and stated that City Code requires that the City
Manager or designee approve purchases above $2,000. However,it also states that department heads
can approve purchases up to $500. The Amendment addresses that gap. Mr. Reid added that the top
tier used to be $500. When it was increased to $2,000, the language regarding department heads was
not amended accordingly. Mr. Donaldson reported that the change will allow department heads to
approve purchases ofup to $2,000 and the City Manager or their designee to approve purchases above
$2,000. He noted a typographical error in Part 2, Item 5, where the number is typed as $200 instead
of$2,000.
