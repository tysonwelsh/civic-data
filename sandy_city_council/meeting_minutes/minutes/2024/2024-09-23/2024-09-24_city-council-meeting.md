1. 24-330 Central Wasatch Commission presenting a Visitor Use Study

Attachments: C\C Presentation

Lindsay Nielsen with the Central Wasatch Commission (CWC) provided an overview of
the visitor use survey which was commissioned to look at the tri canyon area: Millcreek
Canyon, Little Cottonwood Canyon, and Big Cottonwood Canyon. Sandy City is a
member of the Central Wasatch Commission. The study collected visitor use data during
2021 and 2022. The study measured total recreational visits by canyon, the total visits by
type of site, the total time spent by visitors, and the average number of times visitors
recreate in the canyons. The survey also collected data on the perceptions of crowding in
the canyons, overall satisfaction, and the sociodemographic characteristics of visitors to
each of the canyons. She provided an overview of the survey results. Visitors are very
positive about their experiences in the canyons. The tri-canyon areas receive roughly 3.2
million visits a year. You can also access additional information using the environmental
dashboard found on the CWC website. Council thanked Ms. Nielsen for her presentation.
Council moved to Item 3 on the Agenda.

2. 24-328 Salt Lake County presenting on their proposed public safety bond
Attachments: Public Safety Bond Information

Action Plan
Presentation
01 Public Safety Bond 2024.09.24
Mayor Jenny Wilson presented an overview of the proposed county public safety bond.
The county team was presenting information on the county public safety bond to all of the
city councils in the county and she thanked the Council for allotting time this evening for
the presentation. The purpose of the bond was to utilize the funding to consolidate and
improve our county jail by expanding Adult Detention Center (ADC) to meet population
growth and capacity needs, increase rehabilitative services and mental health resources,
and to close Oxbow and transfer inmates and services to the expanded jail/ADC. The
bond will also fund the building of a Justice and Accountability Center (JAC) which will
provide cost-effective alternatives to jail for low-level offenders, balance accountability with
resources/real solutions, and will be connected to the criminal justice system. Several
locations are currently being accessed by the county staff for the JAC. Mayor Wilson
went on to state the positive impact this investment in the community and public safety
will have by reducing crime, improving accountability, connection of resources, and cost
effectiveness. This bond received bipartisan support by the County Council and was
supported by a majority of the community members surveyed. She reviewed the savings
to the taxpayers resulting from the use of the proposed Justice and Accountability Center
and the breakdown of associated project costs and timeline. Council Member Dea
Theodore spoke about the collaborative effort and bipartisan support for this GO Bond and
she thanked the Council for allowing time for this presentation this evening.
Council questions followed. Questions included the utilization of the JAC, the expected
reduction in costs through the use of the JAC, how the operating costs associated with
the center would be funded, and the possibility of future increases in tax accessed on
residents to fund the operating costs of the JAC. Mayor Wilson said she would send the
Council more information regarding the projected operation costs of the center and also
indicated there could be future tax increases on county residents to fund the operations
of the proposed center. Council Members also wanted more information on the daily
operations of the center, total bed capacity, and if offenders assigned to the JAC would
be required to stay at the facility. The proposed JAC center was modeled after a facility in
Oregon and the Council requested information regarding the impact and results of the
Oregon facility. The Council also mentioned that it was difficult for residents to find
information on the County website regarding the financial impact of the projected
increased tax associated with the GO Bond.
Mayor Wilson and County Council Member Theodore thanked the Council for their time
and told the Council to reach out to them if they had any additional questions.
Following the presentation, Council moved to the Consent Calendar.

3. 24-329 Sandy City Public Utilities presenting an update on service line inventory
Attachments: Memorandum

System Compliance CRS Letter
Service Line Presentation
Tom Ward, Public Utilities Director, introduced his team: Preston Tucker, Jordan Hansen,
Scott Ellis, Richard Benham and Mark Chandler with CRS Engineers, who helped
compile the information for this evening's presentation. Mr. Benham provided an overview
of the EPA regulations and compliance regarding the Lead and Copper Rule (LCR) and
the Lead and Copper Rule Revisions (LCRR). He spoke about the actions taken by city
staff to comply with the LCRR which is required by October 16, 2024. Currently 1,159
service lines have been inventoried. To date, Sandy City staff has not found any lead
pipes in the city. Lead water pipes were banned by the EPA in 1989. He reviewed the
service line inventory location map with the Council, inspection process, resident
outreach and self inspection, and service line identification method. Next steps include
publishing the Public Viewer Map by October 16, 2024 and submitting the certification to
the Division of Drinking Water by the same date. City staff will continue ongoing water
service line inventory, stay up to date on new guidance, and continue to look for
additional grant opportunities to offset associated costs.
Mr. Benham responded to Council questions and mentioned that business water lines
were included in their inventory and inspection.
