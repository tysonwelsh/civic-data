3.  Public hearing to discuss and possibly take action on amendments to Section 1-6 
of the Eastern Summit County Development Code. the purpose of amendments is to 
create language for Solar Arrays in Eastern Summit County. – Ray Milliner, Principal 
Planner

Planner Milliner shared the background for this application. Planner Milliner stated this 
is the third time the Planning Commission has looked at the language for Solar Arrays. The 
amendments proposed are focused around more enforceable language. The additional 
changes suggested by Commissioner Clyde are included. Staff recommends the public 
hearing be closed and that the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to 
the County Council. Emily Quinton, the Sustainability Manager is available as a resource if 
there are any questions. 
Planner Milliner clarified that mounted solar energy systems are major systems. It is 
recommended that there is not a requirement for screening because of shadow cast. The 
property owner should have the leeway to not screen if unnecessary. Commissioner 
Sonntag stated there should be some mitigation for the appearance of solar arrays. 
Commissioner Wheaton suggested that solar farms should not exceed one acre. Planner 
Milliner answered there has not been a size limitation for solar farms before. Large solar 
farms (larger than one acre) are not allowed in the 2.5-acre, 5-acre, and 10-acre zones, and 
are Conditional Use in all other zones. Green Belt requires at least five acres for agricultural 
use. Solar arrays would not interfere with Green Belt status as long as there is still an 
agricultural use. 
Commissioner Clyde stated that there should be an addition to the language to allow the 
use of all four pivot corners on a pivot irrigation line for solar arrays. Language should be 
added that states Green Belt status is not a problem for solar arrays in the County. There 
are still some terms that are difficult to enforce. There should also be an addition that if a 
Conditional Use Permit is granted then neighboring property owners are not denied the

right to put in their own screening. Screening is not well-defined, and screening is needed 
more for adjacent public streets rather than adjacent residential uses. 
Commissioner Benson stated there should be a scale of maximum glare allowed. Planner 
Milliner answered he did not find any other ordinances that have anything about 
reflectivity. Shadows would be cast that would reduce the effectiveness of the panels. 
Integrated systems are built into homes. Commissioner Sonntag stated that most HOAs 
have pretty strict guidelines for reflectivity. 
 
 
The Public Hearing was opened. No comments were made and the Public Hearing was closed. 
 
 
Findings of Fact:  
1. The goal of Chapter 2 of the Eastern Summit County General Plan is to develop land use 
codes which balance the diversity of desires of Eastern Summit County residents, including 
private property rights. Code Amendments  
2. In furtherance of this goal, §11-1-1 of the Eastern Summit County Code provides that “The 
eastern Summit County general plan was developed to ensure that the rural, agricultural, and 
small-town character of the eastern portion of the county shall remain, even in the presence of 
growth and change. The intention of the county is to assure the managed, proper and sensitive 
development of land to protect and enhance these desired qualities and the lifestyle that exists.”  
3. On September 17, 2020, and on January 7, 2021 the Eastern Summit County Planning 
Commission conducted a public hearing and reviewed new regulations for Solar Energy Systems 
in the Eastern Summit County Development Code.  
4. Solar systems have been shown to reduce the need for electricity generated by fossil fuels, 
which in turn reduces air pollution, reduces the carbon footprint of a home, and reduces our 
reliance on fossil fuels.  
5. The proposed Solar Energy System regulations are designed to provide property owners with 
the opportunity to install solar systems on their property and to regulate and mitigate the impacts 
of the systems on adjacent property owners.  
 
Conclusions of Law:  
1. The amendment is consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the General Plan.

Planner Milliner introduced Emily Quinton, the County Sustainability Manager. Emily

any comments and ideas that will help relate the Code language to the County goals. 
Planner Milliner stated that the RC (Recreation Commercial) Zone has been discussed for a 
few years and keeps getting pushed back but should be discussed again. The RC Zone 
would not initially be on any zoning maps and would require a rezone application. Two 
eligibility requirements include at least ten acres of property and the applicant must 
demonstrate they can provide water and wastewater services before consideration. The 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission and the County Council will review any 
application before approval. Setback requirements were shared. Maximum height allowed 
in the RC Zone would be forty-five feet, which is higher than any other zone. The Planning 
Commission asked that the additional height should be at the discretion of the Planning

describing their vision. The ag tourism operation may not exceed twenty percent or ten 
acres of the operation’s overall gross acreage. The agriculture use must be kept. Hours of 
operation are limited, but exceptions may be considered. Equestrian uses may be
