     1.1. Review of City Council Decisions – Michael Maloy, Planning Director

the next city council meeting would be on July 9th, where Heather Garcia, Andrea Bradford, and
Daryl Fenn would be reappointed for another 3-year term on the Planning Commission. He informed
the commission that voting for chair and vice chair would occur at the first August meeting.

Planning Director Maloy informed the commission that the next city council meeting was scheduled
for July 9th. During this meeting, a text amendment concerning grading and retaining walls would be
brought forward. This amendment was being introduced in response to a notice of a pending
ordinance amendment that had been adopted by the city council, and it was slated for presentation
to the commission in the next meeting. Maloy emphasized the importance of addressing this matter
promptly, given the expiration of the current ordinance at the end of the month.

   1.2. Review of Agenda Items – Planning Staff

Planning Manager Spencer reviewed the agenda items beginning with the request for a one-year
extension to the Walker Family Subdivision. This extension marked the final one, giving the applicant
until June 3, 2026, to complete necessary recordings for the subdivision. The commission
deliberated, contemplating whether such extensions could become administrative decisions in
future processes to increase efficiency.

In addition, the commission reviewed a Conditional Use Permit application for the installation of a
new 80-foot-tall wireless cell tower along with utility cabinets in the C-2 Commercial Zone. Planning
Director Maloy noted that it was intricately related to text amendment item. He emphasized the
collaborative efforts between the applicant and the city’s legal counsel to develop a proposal that
aligned with FCC regulations and addressed Herriman City's needs.

The commission engaged in an extensive dialogue, scrutinizing various dimensions of the proposal.
Stealth designs and their implementation criteria were at the forefront of the discussions, with
contributors debating whether painting alone suffices as a stealth technique. The discourse
extended to the distances between the proposed cell tower and residential areas. Commissioners
deliberated on the adequacy of existing and proposed measures to diminish visual impacts on
nearby residents.

Moreover, the commissioners addressed potential incentives and requirements for co-location,
expressing a preference for utilizing existing structures to limit new monopole constructions, thus
preserving the area's visual integrity and adherence to community standards. The discussions
underscored the need for clarity in the regulatory language to facilitate unmistakable guidance for
upcoming applications, ensuring that new wireless infrastructures seamlessly integrate into
Herriman City's landscape without impeding residential comfort.

   1.3. Presentation and Discussion of Potential Amendments in Title 10 Land Development Code
        to Regulate Detached Accessory Dwelling Units in Herriman – Laurin Hoadley, Planner I

Planner Hoadley presented the results of a survey conducted regarding potential amendments to
regulate detached Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) in Herriman. She highlighted the following key
points:

   1.   71% of respondents voted for square footage over lot size as the basis for applying the code.

   2. The minimum square footage suggested was 10,000 square feet, which aligned with the city
      council's interest.

   3. The building department confirmed that a permit would be required for any dwelling space,
      regardless of size.
July 02, 2025 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                                     Page 3 of 8

   4. The maximum structure size was discussed, with the planning commission suggesting it be
      based on a percentage of the main structure, while the city council considered lot coverage.

   5. Height suggestions were tied between current code and primary structure height.

   6. Maximum occupancy suggestions were split between 2 and 4 people.

   7. Parking requirements were discussed, with the city council viewing it as a way to regulate
      ADUs.

   8. The commission debated whether to allow short-term rentals in ADUs, with some members
      suggesting that owner-occupied requirements could help mitigate potential issues.

   9. The commission discussed the need for separate water meters for ADUs, with concerns
      raised about cost and practicality.

   10. Sanitation requirements were mentioned, noting that a 4-inch pipe would be required from
       the ADU to the main line.

The commission engaged in a detailed discussion about various aspects of the proposed
amendments for detached Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs), which included considerations on
square footage, lot size, structure size, height, and occupancy requirements. They deliberated
parking and utility needs for ADUs, discussing whether separate water meters should be required.
The idea of allowing short-term rentals in ADUs was also debated, with the commission considering
regulation through parking requirements and owner-occupancy stipulations to help mitigate
potential issues.

   4.2. Review and consider a Conditional Use Permit for a new 80-foot-tall wireless cell tower
        and utility cabinets at 3492 W Maradona Drive in the C-2 Commercial Zone.
        Applicant: Mark Williams, Taft, Sherman & Howard (authorized agent)
        Acres: ±0.78
        File No: C2025-076
   https://www.herriman.gov/uploads/ﬁles/5576/Item-4.2-Staff-Report.pdf
Planning Director Maloy introduced the item, noting the presentation would include items 4.2 and
item 5.1 together. He presented an overview of how the request for a Conditional Use Permit was
intricately tied to the proposed text amendments to the Land Development Code, emphasizing the
importance of addressing federal FCC regulations while meeting the city's growing demand for
wireless services. He introduced the consideration of a conditional use permit for a new 80-foot-tall
wireless cell tower and utility cabinets in the C-2 Commercial Zone at 3492 W Maradona Dr. Maloy
emphasized the importance of ensuring the proposed amendments align with regulatory
requirements and serve the community effectively. He outlined how the proposed wireless facility
could alleviate numerous complaints about service issues in high-traffic areas, such as Zions Bank
Stadium and Mountain View Parkway.

During the presentation, Maloy highlighted key elements of the text amendment that had been
collaboratively developed between the applicant and the city's legal advisors, including standards
for stealth design and setback requirements intended to diminish the visual impact on residential
zones. Maloy highlighted that the proposed amendments would introduce more comprehensive
standards and robust regulations compared to the existing two-page code. He also underscored how
these standards aim to streamline the application process, facilitate city review, and potentially
accelerate the development of needed facilities without compromising compliance with federal
guidelines. By introducing these amendments, Herriman aims to align its regulatory framework with
evolving technological requirements and community objectives for better connectivity.
Furthermore, he emphasized the need for public input and the commission's role in considering
further recommendations to refine the proposed regulations prior to their consideration by the city
council.

Mark Williams, representing the applicant, provided additional context and rationale for the
proposed amendments to the Land Development Code aimed at expanding wireless
telecommunication services in Herriman. He highlighted the growing need for improved wireless
coverage, citing numerous complaints about inadequate service, particularly in high-traffic areas
such as Zions Bank Stadium and Mountain View Parkway.

Mr. Williams detailed the various community benefits that would result from the proposed
amendments, emphasizing improved connectivity not just for residents and businesses, but also for
first responders. He noted that about 80 percent of 9-1-1 calls are made from wireless devices,
underscoring the critical need for reliable wireless service in emergencies. He also pointed out that
the increase in data usage and modern technological demands, such as 5G and the anticipated
arrival of 6G networks and quantum technology, necessitate additional facilities to ensure robust
and reliable service.

growth, support small businesses, and improve the overall quality of life for residents.

The commission engaged in a detailed discussion about various aspects of the proposed
amendments, addressing multiple key concerns. One significant topic was the definition and
requirements for stealth designs, which the proposed amendment included to ensure wireless
facilities blended into their environment more effectively. The commission debated whether the
paint could be considered a stealth solution or if more stringent requirements were necessary.

Another major point of debate was the distance requirements from residential areas and schools.
The current ordinance provisions and proposed changes were scrutinized, with suggestions to
ensure adequate distance to minimize any potential negative visual impacts. Co-location
opportunities and incentives were also discussed, with considerations on how to encourage
maximizing existing infrastructure rather than building new monopoles, thus reducing the potential
number of structures.

The impact of wireless facilities on surrounding areas extended to discussions about aesthetics and
environmental considerations, with commissioners expressing the need for facilities to remain
unobtrusive. There was also a consensus on ensuring clarity in the proposed language and
standards to avoid ambiguity and provide clear guidelines for future applications.

After extensive deliberation and acknowledgment of the points raised, the commission expressed
the need to refine the proposed amendments further. The decision was made to continue this item
to a later date, allowing time for additional discussions and adjustments to properly address the
concerns.

Commissioner Jacobson moved to continue, without a date, the recommendation of approval to
City Council of item 5.1 Review and consider a recommendation for potential amendments to
Title 10 of the Land Development Code to expand wireless telecommunication services by
modifying the location criteria and related standards for monopole and stealth facilities in
Herriman.
