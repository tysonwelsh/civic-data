2.     ADOPT A RESOLUTION CONCERNING THE BUS RAPID TRANSIT PROJECT AND
       ASSOCIATED AGREEMENTS (Continued from the January 24, 2017 Commission meeting)
(Track A, 12:54)
Provo resident Diane Christensen expressed much of the public has been left out of the Bus Rapid Transit (BRT)
process. She applauds the commissioners for taking action with this resolution but feels they could do more. Ms.
Christensen questioned if this project is a good and fair use of funds, and whether the Utah Transit Authority (UTA)
has the good of the county in mind. She listed complaints against UTA, including exaggeration of their ridership to
receive a federal grant, delaying GRAMA requests, and conducting meetings and altering contracts in secret. Ms.
Christensen stated she does not trust those who claim it is too late to stop BRT.

(Track A, 19:00)
Steve Myers, representing the Utah Transit Authority, came forward to answer questions. Mr. Myers stated UTA
absolutely wants to be transparent for the public. He provided some history on the BRT process, explaining it is a
result of regional planning through the Metropolitan Planning Association, the Utah Department of Transportation
(UDOT), and UTA. It has taken many years of research and consideration to find this solution for the projected
grown in Provo and Orem. The priority is to provide high quality transit services for the public; Mr. Myers believes
this project will have great ridership. He stated the prior public comment about exaggerated ridership is false, as the
numbers were accurate at the time the report was written. Changes at Brigham Young University has impacted
ridership, but UTA is working with other organizations to meet their goals. Mr. Myers also talked about GRAMA
requests and protected documents, the responsibilities of the project’s Executive Committee, communications
between UTA and the Federal Transit Administration intended to clarify the BRT interlocal agreement, and
partnerships with Provo City, Orem City, and UDOT.

(Track A, 29:35)
Commissioner Graves suggested adding a paragraph to the proposed resolution to address some funding. He
questioned the need to buy ridership if the project is expected to be successful in moving people. Steve Myers
explained ‘free fare zones’ are enacted in other cities to give people a chance to try the system – encouraging the
public to embrace the project means the residents will continue support long-term ventures. Mr. Myers added he
does not think the incentive is needed to meet goals, but there is reasoning in providing it. Commissioner Lee
countered that a free fare zone would cost three million dollars for the first two years; he stated free means
subsidized, because someone has to pay for it. Mr. Myers replied that is not the intent, and UTA is working with the
Mountainland Association of Governments to determine the best way to transition the public from a free fare zone to
a paid service.

(Track A, 35:12)
Commissioner Ivie thanked Steve Myers for attending, and asked about the control of change orders. Mr. Myers
explained the Executive Committee must approve anything costing more than $200,000. He also talked about the
differences between TRIP (Transportation Improvement Project) and BRT (Bus Rapid Transit), clarifying BRT is a
component of TRIP. The combined projects are for the widening of University Parkway in Orem, and the BRT
intermodal. The BRT portion is federally funded with a maximum expense of $150,000,000. The road widening is
a UDOT project with an estimated cost of $40,000,000. TRIP is the common project, where the two may overlap.
Mr. Myers and Commissioner Lee discussed how the funds are allocated, especially in relation to interlocal
agreements and federal grants. Mr. Myers expressed confidence UDOT and UTA are being accountable in use of
funds.

(Track A, 43:48)
Commissioner Lee asked about the improvements along 900 East in Provo, and questioned the call that they are “not
significant in impact.” Steve Myers stated the language comes from the National Environmental Policy Act and
their definitions, and talked about re-routing the original plan from going through the Brigham Young University
campus. He mentioned Provo City and the Federal Transit Administration (FTA), noting UTA is committed to
helping find funding sources for those changes. They discussed the budget, risks, petitions, and dedicated lanes.
Mr. Myers and Commissioner Lee also discussed the FTA and the role of the project’s Executive Committee.

(Track A, 59:03)
Commissioner Lee estimated that in general business terms, based on voting rights being divided between funding
partners, Utah County should be considered approximately forty-five percent of this project. He stated that should
be enough to be notified of all changes and other updates to the project. When Commissioner Lee used the term
‘bait and switch’ in reference to gaining federal funding, Steve Myers disagreed with the assessment; he stated UTA
is following the contract terms. Mr. Myers mentioned representation and challenges, asking the commissioners to
proactively seek answers if they have any questions or concerns regarding UTA. Commissioner Lee countered the
Board may not know there are concerns to be had if they are not being included in the first place. They discussed a
letter dated July 2016 to the Federal Transit Administration.

(Track A, 1:06:27)
Commissioner Graves read his suggestion for an additional paragraph in the proposed resolution:

        That the Board of County Commissioners of Utah County, Utah, hereby do not support the use of
        any additional transportation dollars for free fare for any transit projects.

Commissioner Graves explained the addition is not an indictment on UTA, but instead frustration with the
Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG). Since Commissioners Graves, Lee, and Ivie were not involved
in the original 2014 agreement implementing the Bus Rapid Transit project, they have needed to ‘play catch-up’ and
Commissioner Graves feels MAG has been unacceptable in their communication. Commissioner Graves stated he
fully supports transit projects when they are done correctly. Commissioner Ivie noted project sustainability is vital
for Utah County, one of the fastest-growing counties in the nation, and Commissioner Lee added he is not against
mass transit but the projects need to be handled responsibly. Commissioner Lee repeated his conviction that this
issue should have been presented for a vote by the citizens. Commissioner Graves encouraged the public to remain
active and involved in local politics.

                 Commissioner Graves made the motion to adopt the resolution concerning the
