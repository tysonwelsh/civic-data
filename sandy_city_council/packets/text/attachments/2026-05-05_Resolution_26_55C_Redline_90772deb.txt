                                     RESOLUTION # 26-55C


    A RESOLUTION OF THE SANDY CITY COUNCIL STATING ITS INTENTION TO
 INVESTIGATE THE CONSTRUCTION OF A NEW PUBLIC SAFETY HEADQUARTERS
AND AUTHORIZING THE CITY TO CONDUCT A FEASIBILITY ANALYSIS AND FORMAL
                          NEEDS ASSESSMENT

WHEREAS, the infrastructure supporting the Sandy City Police Department must evolve to meet
the complexities of modern law enforcement; and

WHEREAS, a purpose-built facility is essential for improving officer recruitment, retention, and
mental health through modern design and wellness-focused environments; and

WHEREAS, should the City Council move forward with construction it intends to provide the
community a direct voice in this investment through a General Obligation (GO) Bond on the
November 2027 General Election ballot; and

WHERAS, a formal needs assessment and feasibility analysis are required to determine the
operational and space requirements for the Police Department, the facility location, and accurate
cost estimates for the likely project.


NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF SANDY CITY AS
FOLLOWS:

   1. Policy. Sandy City shall investigate the feasibility of constructing a Public Safety
      Headquarters.
   2. Direction. The Mayor is hereby authorized to conduct, or hire a qualified consultant to
      conduct, a comprehensive feasibility analysis and formal needs assessment for a new Police
      Department Public Safety Headquarters.
   3. Site Evaluation Requirements. At minimum, the analysis shall evaluate the following
      locations for suitability:
          a. The “Justice Campus” Site:
                   i. The parking lot located to the west of the Sandy City Justice Court and City
                      Hall; and
                  ii. The existing Justice Court Building; or
                 iii. A combination of these adjacent locations.
          b. Other locations, as recommended by the City, or by the Consultant, should the
              “Justice Campus” site prove insufficient or ill-suited for the intended use.
   4. Scope. The study must move beyond preliminary estimates to provide an accurate data set
      for the Council and the public. At minimum the assessment shall include:
          a. Space and growth verification: An audit of the preliminary 47,000 square foot
              recommendation to ensure it meets departmental needs through 2028 and beyond

                                                  1
          b. Site-specific suitability studies and cost estimates. The analysis must provide
              conceptual designs and construction cost estimates for each potential site, including
              costs for any specialized equipment that may be required in the new facility.
   5. Analysis Timeline. The Council hereby establishes the following timeline for the Feasibility
      Analysis:
          a. By June 9 May 31, 2026: Determine who will conduct the study and submit the
              study funding request to the City Council
          b. By December 31, 2026: Completion of Feasibility Analysis.
          c. By January 31, 2027: Present feasibility analysis and site recommendation to the
              City Council.
   6. Funding. Following the selection of a qualified consultant by City Administration, the
      council shall be prepared to review and consider appropriating the necessary funds to
      complete the analysis.
   7. Effective. This resolution shall become effective immediately.
