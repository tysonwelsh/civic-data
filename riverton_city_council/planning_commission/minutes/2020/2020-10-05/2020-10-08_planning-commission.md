1.    REZONE, NEWMAN CONSTRUCTION IS REQUESTING A REZONE OF ABOUT
      9.92 ACRES LOCATED AT 1300 WEST WITHERS LANE. THE CURRENT ZONING
      IS RR-22, THE REQUESTED ZONING IS RM-8.

Development Services Director, Jason Lethbridge, presented the staff report and explained
that the property is located at the intersection of 1300 West and 12800 South. The property
was identified on a map displayed. The property is zoned RR-22. The property to the east
is zoned RM-8, which is a multi-family designation. The properties to the north are
Commercial Gateway and Parks and Open Space to the west and directly to the south.
Mr. Lethbridge reported that the General Plan designation for the property is medium-high
residential, which has a density of five to eight units per acre. It contains a mixed-use
designation but residential and commercial would not be allowed to exist at the same time.

Mr. Lethbridge pointed out that the proposed zoning is RM-8, which is a multi-family
designation that allows eight units per acre. There is a version of the RM-8 zone that is
designated for the downtown area and has slightly different requirements. He explained that
the actual designation is RM-8D. The applicant is proposing a Specific Development (SD)
designation, which would allow the Commission and the Council to impose additional
restrictions and limitations on the zoning.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that the Planning Commission would not be approving a plan as
discussed previously and they do not require the submittal of a plan although the applicants
typically do. It is a good indication of what they hope to do and how the proposed zone may

individual lots and attached townhome-style uses. Following the initial submittal, the
applicant met with neighborhood groups and took the feedback received and resubmitted a
Concept Plan. The updated Concept Plan was displayed showing each of the unit types.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that at the neighborhood meeting there was positive feedback

great plan. The applicant was also commended for aligning the street to the golf course,

keep the historic look. He was not sure which silo they will keep but stated they would not
trade a lot for a silo unless it was something that they could work with.

gated community. The residents raised concerns about 1300 West. The applicant explained
that a fence will go all the way around to the backside. The intent was to discourage parking
on 1300 West. An HOA will be established in part to maintain the open space and the pocket
park.

The applicant explained that the project will be marketed as a 55+ community but it will not
be restricted. They have found that this type of product is what that age group is looking for.
Mr. Lethbridge clarified that the request is not for elderly housing.

2.     HOME OCCUPATION, NOAH LEE IS REQUESTING A CONDITIONAL USE HOME
       OCCUPATION FOR RED BEARD ROASTING COMPANY, AN IN-HOME SMALL
       BATCH COFFEE ROASTING BUSINESS, TO BE LOCATED AT 1362 QUAIL
       RIDGE ROAD.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the staff report and stated that the application is for a home-based
business. The property is located at 12600 South and 1300 West. Mr. Lethbridge explained
that the business will involve small-batch artisan coffee roasting that is operated out of the
garage. There will be no outside employees and storage will be relatively minor in sealed
plastic bins. Sales will not be directly from the site and there will be no visitors coming to
the home.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that the application includes additional responses to the question of
noise and other potential impacts. The sound was measured from the equipment to be used.
It was noted that the size and scale of the equipment for home-based businesses are always
considered. Photos were displayed from the product website showing that the coffee roaster
will be tabletop. The size and scope were determined to be within the Home Occupation
Ordinance. The proposed equipment could also be used for personal use; however,
because it is being used for sales it requires a Home Occupation Permit and a Business
License. Specific venting will also be required. The applicant had spoken to the Building

Official and a representative from the Fire Marshal’s Office. If approved, additional
requirements will have to be met to comply with the Building and Fire Codes.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that a section of the Home Occupation Ordinance addresses
neighborhood disruptions and specifies that home occupations shall not create or be
associated with, or produce odors, smoke, dust, heat, fumes, light, glare, noise, vibration,
excessive traffic, or other nuisances. The applicant acknowledged that there will be odors
associated with the roasting equipment.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the Home Occupation Ordinance was intended to prevent
unreasonable or uncharacteristic interference or disruption into the neighborhood. He
reviewed the request with the applicant, the Building Department, and the Fire Department
who concluded that the equipment will not be unreasonable within a residential
neighborhood since it is already permissible for personal use. Staff recommended approval
with the conditions outlined in the staff report.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Commissioner James pointed out that the proposed motion does not include conditions that
would limit the business to one machine. He asked if that is something that could be
controlled by the City or included in the motion. Mr. Lethbridge explained that the intent of
condition number five is to clarify that the business can have only one coffee roaster. The
Commission could clarify the phrasing if desired. Commissioner James pointed out that it
should be made clear. He pointed out that one roaster may not create odors but five or six
running at a time may be cause for concern.

Commissioner Russell stated that it has been reported that the roaster is in use for 18 to 22
minutes per roast. He asked for an indication of the number of roasts that are anticipated
per day/week. Mr. Lethbridge stated that the Commission could specify that in the motion.

The applicant, Josh Hall, indicated that he does not live at the property but is involved with
the day-to-day business activities. He reported that the number of roasts will depend on
sales. Each roast produces two pounds of coffee. The roaster will run for one or two hours
a few times per week in the beginning.

Commissioner James asked if Mr. Hall would object to the approval specifying only one
machine. Mr. Hall stated that the intent is to use the current machine until it is outgrown. It
will then be upgraded to one with a larger capacity. Mr. Lethbridge explained that the
approval of one machine would require the applicant to update the Conditional Use Permit
as the business grows or as new equipment is acquired.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Hall stated that their customers are coffee drinkers
who have limited knowledge of the process and qualities that make coffee great. He
explained that many coffee drinkers want the product for convenience. Mr. Hall clarified that
the sales will be private to individuals, and not commercial.

3.    ZONE TEXT CHANGE, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING CHANGES TO TITLE
      18 REGARDING LOCATING AND OPERATING FOOD TRUCKS.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that the City does not currently have an ordinance that deals
specifically with food trucks. It is not a significant issue but there has been an increasing
number of inquiries regarding the City’s policies. Mr. Lethbridge stated that they are
considering the adoption of an ordinance specifically addressing mobile food trucks and
vendors in the City. Research was done on how adjacent cities have dealt with the issue
and the standards applied to the Riverton City ordinance.

Some of the primary standards included:

      1.     Allowing food trucks on private property in commercial zones only.

      2.     The City shall grant permission and a waiver for public streets and the City
             Park.

      3.     The food truck cannot be parked in a manner that prohibits the flow of traffic
             and service has to be away from the roadway.

The ordinance includes limitations in terms of proximity to other food trucks, fire hydrants,
bus stops, and schools. Mr. Lethbridge reported that there was an issue with the uses being
temporary. The operating hours are from 7.00 AM to 10.00 PM and a food truck cannot
remain on site after business hours. There was an exception, however, for special events.
He pointed out that a street vendor and mobile food truck are completely different.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that feedback from some food trucks purported that the standards
will work well in the City. The issue of having commissary kitchen where food trucks can
come together and share space was not addressed in the proposed ordinance. There had
been no inquiries in that regard. He clarified that the City had at one time allowed a food
truck park that provided space for food trucks. Mr. Lethbridge stated that the matter is not
time-sensitive as there is not a pending application. As a result, the process will not be
rushed.

Chair Cannon asked if the guidelines require a business license and what the procedure is
for operating such a use on private property. Mr. Lethbridge reported that the Utah State
Code allows a mobile food truck with a license in one community to operate in all
communities. The intent was to avoid the need for multiple licenses. Mr. Lethbridge clarified
that permission from the property owner is required.

Commissioner Rushton recommended that the ordinance go back to the drawing board
since it is a redundancy of State law. He saw no need to restate potential issues that the
State is already regulating. Mr. Lethbridge reiterated that the redundancy was intended to
make the requirements clear and ensure compliance. With regard to overnight parking, he
explained that food trucks cannot be in an operating location overnight. The intent would
not be for the food trucks to remain in the same location for days at a time and become
permanent. Commissioner Lefgren supported having some redundant points since allowing
them would enable the City to enforce what is on their books and avoid sending residents to
other departments in the event there are complaints.

A question was raised regarding the number of food trucks that should be allowed in one
area on a public street to address the issue of density. Chair Cannon welcomed food trucks
in the City to improve the quality and variety of food options. Commissioner Lefgren agreed
that there was some redundancy but stated that if there are issues, the residents will call the
City. He suggested that the ordinance address issues that are pertinent to Riverton City
residents and include perhaps redundant issues.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public
hearing was closed.

Commissioner Russell moved to table the matter to a future date and have it brought
back to the Commission after the referenced items have been revised. Commissioner
Rushton seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous consent of
the Commission.

4.     ZONE TEXT CHANGE, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING CHANGES TO
       SECTIONS 17.15.030(2) AND 18.215.020(10) ALLOWING PAYMENT IN LIEU OF
       DEDICATION OF WATER SHARES FOR NEW DEVELOPMENT.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that the above matter involves an ordinance amendment regarding
the dedication of water shares. He explained that the City, for residential and commercial
projects, requires water shares to be dedicated as they connect into the pressurized
irrigation system. The intent is to ensure that the City has sufficient supply to meet the
demands of new projects. Information from the Public Works Development was submitted
with the staff report.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that the developer will be required to dedicate water shares equal
to three acre-feet per acre of ground. He further explained that if water shares are not part
of or attached to the property at the time of purchase, the developer will need to acquire the
needed shares and dedicate them to the City. Mr. Lethbridge reported that the proposed
amendment will allow the developer to pay a fee to the City in lieu of the dedicated shares.
Commissioner James asked what would happen to the existing water shares should the City
Engineer accept a fee instead. Mr. Lethbridge explained that in that instance the developer
would retain ownership of the shares.
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                8
October 8, 2020

Commissioner Russell asked if there was a tangible dollar amount attached to the three
shares per acre. Mr. Lethbridge stated that according to the ordinance, the City Council will
determine the fee annually based on a market analysis of the cost to acquire shares.
Possible options were discussed. Commissioner Russell expressed support for the
proposed ordinance amendment. Mr. Lethbridge explained that the City Engineer’s decision
to accept a fee will be based on whether the City has already obtained an adequate supply
of water shares for the pressure zone in which the subdivision is found. It was stressed that
it would not be a blanket discretion and specific criteria would have to be met.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Lefgren moved that the Planning Commission recommend approval
of the zone text change to Section 17.15.030(2) and 18.215.020(10) allowing payment
in lieu of dedication of water shares for new development.

Chair Cannon preferred that the motion remain vague in terms of water shares. He would
not want a developer to be restricted if they have shares in one canal and the shares are
needed in a different one. Mr. Lethbridge reiterated that the discretion lies with the City.

Commissioner James seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous
consent of the Commission.

Mr. Lethbridge clarified that the fee would be established by the City each year and fluctuate
with market changes.
