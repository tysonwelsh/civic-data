D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY: Commissioner Harvey: Back in 1839, a traveling salesman and his wife gave birth to a
   little boy named John. John did very well selling candy, raising turkeys, taking care of people’s yards. 14 years
   later, they moved to Cleveland, Ohio, where John attended high school and did some bookkeeping. He had another
   opportunity to work for an oil company in Titusville, PA. They weren’t doing so well so he gathered some partners
   to see what they could do with their new found product of oil. He later met a gal named Celestia and they married
   and had several kids. Her dad was quite successful and helped with the Underground Railroad that helped free
   slaves. In the late 1800’s, he would be known for being philanthropic and maintained a close connection with his
   Savior. He created a company called Standard Oil and was so successful that the government had to step in and
   break up his monopoly that had 90% of the country’s oil. His name was John D Rockefeller. He said, “Don’t be
   afraid to give up the good to go for the great.” One of my colleagues was determined to do too much of his own
   work on his farm that could have been hired out and nearly lost his finger. And Commissioner Bolos decided to
   be a whitewater rafter, be adventurous. I’m so proud to work with them.

         Liam Keogh (Attorney’s Office): HB351 established the County Recorders Standards Board standardizing

    4. AGREEMENT WITH QNERGY RELATING TO TESTING OF A POWER GENERATION SYSTEM
       ON COUNTY PROPERTY.
         Stephanie Russell (Economic Development Director): This is a really exciting project to mitigate methane
         coming out of our closed landfill. Methane is a huge contributor to greenhouse gases. A lot of states have
         been implementing legislation for closed landfills; Utah does not have such legislation. However, we have a
         local company, Qnergy, that has perfected a Sterling engine to remove methane out of landfills, pig farms, and
         anywhere there is a large amount of methane. Right now, our closed landfill is emitting methane at pretty high
         rates. This is a pilot program where Qnergy provides all the equipment, research and technicians at no cost to
         the county and all the county is providing is the methane and the landfill. This puts us ahead of the game for
         any future legislation. The benefit of this is it generates power that could possibly be used at the archery range.
         Results will be provided at the end of the pilot program.
         Commissioner Harvey moved approve an agreement with Qnergy relating to testing of a power generation
         system on County property; Commissioner Bolos seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Bolos – aye; Chair Froerer – aye

    7. APPROVAL OF CONTRACT WITH GLOBAL MOBILE, LLC TO UTILIZE GLOBAL MOBILE’S
       BALLOT CURING PRODUCT AS WELL AS THEIR PARTY AFFILIATION PRODUCT.
         Ricky Hatch (Clerk/Auditor): We have used this organization in the past, in fact Weber County was the first
         County in Utah to provide this service to voters. Ballot curing is really signature curing; if a voter forgets to
         sign their ballot envelope, or if they accidentally signed someone else’s ballot, or if they have an injury and
         their signature doesn’t match, the ballot goes through a curing process where we contact the voter and they
         have to provide additional information, including an additional signature, before the ballot can be opened and
         counted. Global Mobile provides this service via a secure channel on the voter’s cell phone. This contract
         renews the service for November’s election as well as for the party affiliation product.
         Commissioner Bolos moved to approve a contract with Global Mobile, LLC to utilize Global Mobile’s ballot
         curing product as well as their party affiliation product; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Bolos – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
