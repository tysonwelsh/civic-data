Discussion/Direction
Ms. Kersten Swinyard , Director, Economic Development Division, Office 
of Regional Development, reviewed the resolution to dissolve the Arbor Park 
Neighborhood Redevelopment Project Area, also known as the Packard Bell 
Project Area, before transferring the project to Magna City.  The time period 
where tax increment could be collected for the project area had ended, and 
this resolution was simply part of the cleanup process. The Economic 
Development Division could not find a record of the dissolution prior.  
RESOLUTION NO. 150
A RESOLUTION OF THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS OF THE 
REDEVELOPMENT AGENCY OF SALT LAKE COUNTY 
DISSOLVING THE ARBOR PARK NEIGHBORHOOD 
REDEVELOPMENT PROJECT AREA
RECITALS
WHEREAS, in 1994, the Redevelopment Agency of Salt Lake County (the 
“Agency”) created the Arbor Park Neighborhood Redevelopment Project 
Area (the “Project Area”) and adopted a project area plan (the “Area Plan”);
WHEREAS, the scope and objectives of the Area Plan are completed and 
the time period whereby tax increment from the Project Area may be 
collected, allocated or used has ended; and
WHEREAS, the Agency now desires to dissolve the Project Area; and
WHEREAS, the dissolution of the Project Area shall have no effect on the 
Agency’s two active project areas located in Magna City -namely, the 
Magna/Arbor Park Urban Renewal Area and the Magna Main Street 
Community Development Area.
RESOLUTION

NOW, THEREFORE, the Board of Directors of the Redevelopment Agency 
of Salt Lake County, Utah, resolves as follows:
A. That the Arbor Park Neighborhood Redevelopment Project Area is

5. Other Committee Business
Board Member Bradshaw asked Kersten Swinyard to give an update as it 
related to the other tasks necessary to transfer the Redevelopment Agency 
(RDA) projects to Magna City.
Ms. Kersten Swinyard , Director, Economic Development Division, Office 
of Regional Development, stated the Redevelopment Agency voted to 
confirm the transfer of those project areas in December.  Since then, staff 
had been working on the process and laying out the various steps necessary 
to pull that off.  The four “buckets” were:

· Active contracts entered into by the Redevelopment Agency (Magna Main 
and Magna Park Urban Renewal Area).  Those projects needed to be 
assigned to the Magna Redevelopment Agency, and that would happen in 
cooperation with the contracted parties.  That would start very quickly.  
· The true -up of increment the Redevelopment Agency had been collecting 
for years from the Magna project areas.  Statutorily, the County Auditor 
finalizes tax revenue for project areas by March 31st of every year.   
· Discussion of properties.  The County Redevelopment Agency owns nine 
parcels of property in Magna City.  Salt Lake County owns three parks 
parcels in Magna City.  These were being contemplated for transfer to the 
City.  The RDA had asked Magna City to confirm that it wanted all of the 
parcels and whether the parcels should be transferred to Magna City or the 
Magna Redevelopment Agency.  The attorneys from both parties were 
working on that. 
· The final interlocal agreement transfer.  This would come to the 
Redevelopment Agency Board to ratify all the prior steps.  This should be 
done by the end of May.  
Board Member Bradshaw  stated he was confident that staff would move 
forward as quickly as possible.  Magna City was anxious to get this done .  
He asked Ms. Swinyard to forward any questions from Magna if necessary 
so the Board could be responsive.
