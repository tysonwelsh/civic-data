3. Public hearing and possible action regarding Amendments to the Eastern Summit 
County Development Code Chapter 11-2-14, Development Standards: Natural 
Resources – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Ray Milliner said what is before the Commission are amendments to the 
natural resources chapter (Chapter 2).  At a previous Planning Commission Meeting,

Staff was directed to make some changes to the language and to create some visual 
corridors throughout the County.  
 
Currently, there are two prohibitions in the Chapter 2 language.  The Code prohibits 
construction on slopes greater than 30%.  It also prohibits construction on a ridgeline.  
This is defined as construction on a ridge of a mountain that daylights into blue sky.  
Any structure that violates either of these prohibitions is not allowed to be built.      
 
Planner Milliner said this means that anyone who has a lot of record with either of these 
scenarios has only one avenue of recourse, which is obtain a special exception from the 
County Council.  The special exception has a $1,000 processing fee with some strict 
submittal requirements.  To appeal this decision, the applicant has to go to District 
Court.  Staff is proposing they insert a low impact process into the language.  A low 
impact permit has a processing fee of $250.   There would be four or five criteria to 
evaluate the application.     
 
Planner Milliner said when this was discussed at a previous meeting, the Commission 
was concerned about the arbitrary nature of considering ridgelines at different vantage 
points.  Staff was directed to solidify different vantage points throughout the County; 
however, based on the size of Eastern Summit County it would be very difficult to 
identify vantage points.  This would be a lengthy process.   
 
Planner Milliner said it is Staff’s recommendation is that in lieu of the vantage points, 
they keep the language as proposed.  He added Commissioner Sonntag has presented 
some possible amendments.      
 
Commissioner Sonntag said that in Promontory, they frequently face this issue 
because most of the lots are steep hillsides.  Often, there is a small piece of developable 
ground, but it is not large enough to put a house on.  There are also situations where
