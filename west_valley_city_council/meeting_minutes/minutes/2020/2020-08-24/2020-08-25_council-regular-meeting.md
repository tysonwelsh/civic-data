        ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 20-32, REPEAL AND REENACT TITLE 18
        OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE
        The City Council previously held a public hearing regarding proposed Ordinance
        20-32 that would repeal and reenact Title 18 of the West Valley City Municipal Code.

        Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
        information as follows:

               In February 2020, the State Division of Water Quality issued a new 5-year
               UPDES Permit to the Jordan Valley Municipalities, including West Valley
               City. This permit allows the City to discharge storm water to waters of the
MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING – AUGUST 25, 2020

          State. New provisions of the permit compel the City to modify its Storm
          Water Management Plan, Engineering Standards, and Title 18 Storm Water
          Management, of the City Code. This revision to Title 18 brings the Code
          into compliance with the permit and into agreement with the other
          documents.

          To better imitate pre-development runoff, the new permit requires new
          development projects to manage rainfall on-site to prevent off-site runoff
          from all rainfall events less than or equal to the 80th percentile rainfall event.
          The details of this requirement are included in the Engineering Standards.
          Sections of the code which previously prohibited on-site storm water
          retention and ponding have been revised accordingly.

          The verbiage in the code was reduced significantly by eliminating obsolete
          and outdated sections, consolidating similar sections and reorganizing into
          more logical chapters. The new Title 18 points contractors, developers and
          others to the Engineering Standards for specific requirements relating to
          storm drainage design, construction and use.

          All the prohibitions and penalties for illicit discharge to the storm drainage
          system remain intact.

   Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of

   OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE

   The City Council previously held a public hearing regarding proposed Ordinance
   20-33 that would repeal and reenact Title 19 of the West Valley City Municipal
   Code.

   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
   information as follows:

          This ordinance has not changed significantly since the City incorporated. A
          major rewrite was needed to eliminate obsolete and outdated requirements.
          The impetus to make this change now is the proposed adoption of updated
          Engineering Division Standards. New state storm water requirements
          necessitated changes to the Engineering Standards, which prompted an
          update to Title 19.

          Chapter 1 of Title 19 relating to railroads has been deleted. Railroad
          crossings are under the jurisdiction of the State of Utah through the
          Department of Transportation.

          Street and sidewalk design and construction standards have been deleted
          from the ordinance and are now included in the Engineering Division
          Standards.

          Bond and insurance requirements for contractors working within West
          Valley City Rights-of-Way have been updated. Minimum bond and
          insurance amounts remain at $10,000 and $100,000, but the Engineering
          Division will now have authority to require a higher completion bond
          sufficient to guarantee proper restoration of the Right-of-Way, and
          insurance equal to the maximum aggregate liability in the Governmental
          Immunity Act for larger projects on major streets.

   Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of
   the City Council, and he called for a motion.

   ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 20-35, AMEND SECTION 1-2-110 OF THE
   WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN
   PUBLIC WORKS DEPARTMENT FEES
   The City Council previously held a public hearing regarding proposed Ordinance
   20-35 that would amend Section 1-2-110 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to
   amend certain Public Works Department fees.

   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
   information as follows:

          The Public Works Department is responsible for the administration of right-
          of-way permits for utility companies and property owners doing work in the
          public right of way. The current fee structure has not been updated in many
          years, and fees collected by the city do not cover the cost to administer the
          program.

          This change adjusts the fee collected for inspection and administration of
          right-of-way permits.

          A major change includes the addition of a “Peak Hour Lane Closure” fee
          assessed on major roads, to encourage construction work to be done during
          times that will have a lesser impact to the traveling public.

          The new fee includes an age-based asphalt damage fee, to discourage the
          excavation in newer pavements.

   the City Council, and he called for a motion.

   ACTION: RESOLUTION 20-136: ADOPT THE 2020-2025 STORM WATER
   MANAGEMENT PLAN
   The City Council previously held a public hearing regarding proposed Resolution
   20-136 that would adopt the 2020-2025 Storm Water Management Plan.

   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
   information as follows:

          West Valley City is permitted through the Utah Department of
          Environmental Quality to discharge municipal storm water runoff to waters
          of the state under the Utah Pollutant Discharge Elimination System. The
          permit term is normally five years (previous permit term was 2013-2018 but
          was extended to 2020). The new permit term is 2020-2025.

          The permit requires the city to handle storm water pollution prevention by
          addressing the following six minimum control measures in the Storm Water
          Management Plan:

          1) Public Education and Outreach on Storm Water Impacts
          2) Public Involvement/Participation
          3) Illicit Discharge Detection and Elimination
          4) Construction Site Storm Water Runoff Control
          5) Long-Term Storm Water Management on New and Redevelopment
          Projects
          6) Pollution Prevention and Good Housekeeping on Municipal Operations

          New requirements are normally placed upon the city with each permit
          renewal cycle, and the city is required to address the changing demands in
          a revision of the Storm Water Management Plan to address changing permit
          requirements. The Public Works Department collaborated with the various
          internal divisions along with the Parks Department to develop this new plan.
          This resolution is before the council to formally adopt the city’s Storm
          Water Management Plan.

RESOLUTION 20-137: APPROVE AN INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT WITH
SALT LAKE COUNTY TO PARTICIPATE AS CO-PERMITTEES UNDER THE
JORDAN VALLEY MUNICIPALITIES MS4 PERMIT
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 20-137 that would approve an Interlocal
Agreement with Salt Lake County to participate as Co-Permittees under the Jordan Valley
Municipalities MS4 Permit.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West Valley City is required to obtain a permit from the State of Utah, Department
       of Environmental Quality, for authorization to discharge municipal storm water
       runoff to waters of the state under the Utah Pollutant Discharge Elimination
       System. Due to the interconnected nature of the drainage systems, the city is a co-
       permittee with other cities in Salt Lake County, collectively referred to in the permit
       as “Jordan Valley Municipalities” under UPDES Permit No. UTS000001. West
       Valley City first became a co-permittee with Salt Lake County in 2003, and has
       subsequently renewed that agreement.

       Under this agreement, the city and county agree to implement and enforce the
       requirements of the state permit within their respective jurisdictional boundaries.
       The cities also agree to share information among each other to assist in permit
       compliance. The City agrees to pay for costs relating to its own storm water
       systems. The City and the County jointly agree that they will reimburse each other
       for expenses incurred in providing services to each other. The City will pay the
       County for a public education and involvement campaign as set forth in the existing
       2017-2022 UPDES Media Campaign agreement.

       of the MS4 permit term, at which point it is anticipated to be renewed.
