PRESENTATIONS:

Z-1-2025 Petition by Eric Nguyen requesting a zone change from A (Agriculture, minimum lot size ½
acre) to RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential). The property is located at 3001 West 4100 South on
1.53 acres. Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov. Continued from April
23, 2025 Public Hearing.

Discussion: Steve Pastorik re-addressed a zone change request for a property at 3001 West 4100 South,
to convert from A to RM zone, for a medium-density residential development. The applicant proposed
building 14 units (14 new townhomes plus one existing house), with the latest design featuring two-unit
buildings instead of four-unit buildings to address concerns about building scale. Commissioner
Woodruff and Commissioner Drozdek expressed approval of the updated design, particularly the
improved massing and setbacks from the property lines. The possibility of adding a pavilion instead of a
sports court was considered, with Commissioner Drozdek suggesting it would be more cost-effective.
The Commission discussed rental restrictions, with Brandon Hill explaining that while rental caps are
possible, enforcement would be challenging. Chair Lovato inquired about the distinction between twin
homes and townhomes under RM zoning, which Steve clarified should follow multifamily design
standards.

Opening Ceremony for Public Hearings

The Commission discussed adding an opening ceremony to their public hearings, with a focus on
including the Pledge of Allegiance. Most Commissioners expressed support for this idea, with some
noting of its potential to foster unity and respect. The Commission agreed to implement this change.

RM Zoning Eligibility

Steve Pastorik presented an analysis of how changes to the Major Street Plan might affect RM zoning
eligibility. After reviewing 1300 West, 4000 West, 4800 West, Parkway Blvd., 3500 South, and 6200
South, Steve Pastorik concluded that even though some streets will be downgraded from 5 to 3 lanes,
there are few properties currently eligible for RM zoning in those areas. The analysis showed that
existing properties either had insufficient acreage, were publicly owned, or the proximity to commercial
areas, made them ineligible or unlikely for RM zoning without a code change. Chair Lovato confirmed
that property owners could still pursue individual rezoning requests if their property meets the
requirements. The Commission agreed not to pursue any code changes at this time, deciding to leave the
existing code as is.


There being no further business with the West Valley City Planning Commission, the meeting was
adjourned at 4:24 PM.
