        Agenda Review
                 Acting Chair Blair and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the regular meeting
        scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m. Council member White referenced the agenda item dealing with the receipt of a rebate from

        water utility enterprise fund, but there may be opportunities for programming the money for sustainability efforts. This led to a

        will provide an infonnational update to the Council in the future regarding optional conservation or sustainability programs that
        could be funded using the money.

        United States and in Utah specifically in 2018; 90,558 nationwide charges of discrimination were resolved and the Equal
        Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) secured $505 million for victims in private sector, state and local government, and
        federal workplaces. In Utah, there were 233 total charges; the Utah Antidiscrimination and Labor Division investigated and
        mediated 53 9 cases and awarded $1.2 million to claimants. Common types of harassment cases include sexual harassment, hostile

        identity, and sex. Victims of harassment are advised to tell the perpetrator to stop their behavior, document the situation, and file a
        complaint. Supervisors must do something to stop harassment behavior and anyone who is not a supervisor should tell a
        supervisor. If the City knows about harassment or should have known about it, the City has the responsibility to investigate and
        take remedial action. Ms. Judkins provided examples ofways to combat harassment and discrimination, after which she concluded

          Information Technologies (IT) Manager Lefgren used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss cyber security. He
discussed the increase in cyber attacks on government entities across the United States; in December of2019, a City user received
a fraudulent email prompting them to click a link and enter a username and password. This information was used to compromise
the individual's email address by sending 910 internal and external emails with malicious information. This resulted in 104
internal users opening an attached PDF. Responding to this attack ultimately cost the City $27,560 in staff time and legal analysis
of the situation. It is important for all City employees to be educated on safe internet habits, social networking dangers, and scams,
hoaxes, and malware. The City has several policies addressing hardware and software purchasing, software and electronic data,
security measures, City sponsored social media, and technology governance and Mr. Lefgren briefly summarized these policies.
He noted the City has employed multi-factor authentication measures, device encryption, data management, and user training
programs to prevent and respond to cyber breaches.
          Throughout Mr. Lefgren' s presentation he engaged in discussion with the Council about examples of compromised cyber
security; scammers are using email, social media messaging, and even text message to gain access to sensitive information for
nefarious purposes.
          Assistant Water Utility Manager LaMay used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation created by the Utah Risk Management
Mutual Association (URMMA) to discuss distracted and defensive driving with the City Council. He has personally been in three
vehicle accidents in his life and all three occurred while he was stopped at a red light; he was struck by distracted drivers and he
takes this issue very seriously. Distracted driving is any activity that could divert a person's attention away from the primary task
of driving; all distractions endanger driver, passenger, and bystander safety. He listed various types of distractions, emphasizing
that text messaging is by far the most alarming distraction. He provided key facts and statistics about distracted driving:
               •     In 2014, 3,179 people were killed, and 431,000 were injured in motor vehicle crashes involving distracted
                     drivers.
               • As of December 2014, 169.3 billion text messages were sent in the US every month.
               •     10% of all drivers 15 to 19 years old involved in fatal crashes were reported as distracted at the time of the
                     crash.
               •     Drivers in their 20's are 23% of drivers in all fatal crashes, but are 27% of the distracted drivers and 3 8% of the
                     distracted drivers who were using cell phones in fatal crashes.
               • At any given daylight moment across America, approximately 660,000 drivers are using cell phones or
                     manipulating electronic devices while driving, a number that has held steady since 2010.
          Mr. LaMay then discussed drowsy driving, which can be compared to drunk driving. Being awake for at least 18 hours is
the same as someone having a blood alcohol content of0.05 percent. Being awake for at least 24 hours is equal to having a blood
alcohol content of 0.10 percent. This is higher than the legal limit of 0.08 blood alcohol content percent in all states. He offered
suggestions for preventing drowsy driving, after which he moved to the topic of defensive driving, which is defined as:
               • Driving proactively to ensure the safety of yourself and others.
               •     Practicing awareness and caution when behind the wheel.
               •     The art of anticipating problems and responding ahead of time so that you can protect yourself and others from
                     dangerous and unexpected driving situations.
               • Never taking for granted that nothing will go wrong, and never assuming that other drivers will do the right
                     thing.
               •     Being prepared for potential threats and being prepared to deal with them before they become a problem.
          The basics of defensive driving include watching the road, maintaining safe speeds, yielding the right of way, using
proper safety equipment, using good judgement, not driving impaired, and not driving aggressively (e.g. tailgating and road rage).
Motorists should avoid risky behavior and assume that all other drivers are engaged in risky behavior. Proper seat belt usage is
most important; approximately 50 percent of the 35,000 deaths due to motor vehicle crashes each year could be avoided if all
drivers wore their seatbelts. He thanked the Council for their attention to this important topic.

Proposed rezone of properties at approximately 133 West Binford
Avenue
          A petition has been filed by the City's Community and Economic Development (CED) Department to rezone properties
located generally at 133 West Binford Avenue. The properties include several vacant properties, including the site of the former
St. Anne's building, several single-family homes and several commercial prope1ties. The proposed rezone would reclassify the
prope1ties from M-2 (Manufacturing and Industrial) to CBD (Central Business District). The proposal is meant to further the
development of the downtown area. The Ogden Redevelopment Agency authorized the Agency's Executive Director to purchase
the St. Anne's prope1ty and the adjacent Western Ice House property in September of 2019.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the proposed rezone of property at
133 West Binford Avenue from M-2 to CBD. The proposed zone change will allow for future expansion of plans for downtown
Ogden. The area to be rezoned is comprised of the former St. Anne's Site (26.6 %), railroad property (49.5 %), and six other
private properties (23.8 %). The City has worked with Utah State University (USU) to plan for potential development of the
railyard area and has developed plans for expansion of downtown development concepts. He presented conceptual designs created
by USU students and identified the properties subject to the rezone. The Planning Commission reviewed the rezone petition and
evaluated its compliance with the General Plan and zoning policies. They detennined the rezone creates an edge/gateway to
downtown development; is harmonious with the character of the area, which includes M-2, CBD, C-2, and R-4 zoning; will
correct split parcel zoning; allows for future development as planned; and allows for more density in the downtown area as
proposed by the General Plan. The Planning Commission also considered whether the petition is consistent with the CBD
Community Plan, which calls for nearly 300 residents in the area of the proposed rezone. The proposed rezone will strengthen
downtown Ogden and increase vitality. They ultimately concluded to recommend approval of the rezone on a unanimous vote.
          Council member White inquired as to the height restriction for high density residential buildings. Mr. Montgomery
indicated there is no height restriction.
          Council member Hyer asked why the CED Department favored the CBD zoning designation over the CBD-I designation.
Mr. Montgomery stated the CBD-I, or intensive, zone is reserved for the City's urban core, which is at 24111 Street and Washington
Boulevard. Council member Hyer stated he believed this area is planned to develop in a fashion similar to the area around 24 th
Street and Washington Boulevard. Mr. Montgomery stated it should, but the CBD-I designation has limited uses while the CBD
zone allows for a broader range of uses.
          Council member Stephens asked if a private individual petitioned for this zone change. Mr. Montgomery answered no
and indicated the City's CED Department is the petitioner. As staff evaluated development potential of the future for the vacant
properties in the area, they felt this rezone would help to prepare for viable projects on the horizon.
          Council member White asked if the properties are located within a special assessment area, to which Mr. Montgomery
answered no.
          Council Deputy Director Symes indicated that action on the rezone petition can be scheduled for February 4, 2020 if
there are no objections.
