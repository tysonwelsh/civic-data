A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC COMMENT REGARDING APPLICATION GPZ-1-2021,

     LOCATED AT 5718 WEST 3500 SOUTH
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled March 9, 2021, in order for the City Council to hear
     and consider public comments regarding application GPZ-1-2021, filed by Alex
     Winder, requesting a General Plan Change from Commercial/Office and
     Office/Education to Light Manufacturing and a Zone Change from C-2 (General
     Commercial) to LI (Light Industrial) for property located at 5718 West 3500 South.

       Proposed Ordinances 21-09, 21-10, and Resolution 21-35 related to the proposal to
       be considered by the City Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed
       as follows:

       ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 21-09, AMEND THE GENERAL PLAN TO
       SHOW A CHANGE OF LAND USE FROM COMMERCIAL/OFFICE AND
       OFFICE/EDUCATION TO LIGHT MANUFACTURING FOR PROPERTY
       LOCATED AT 5718 WEST 3500 SOUTH ON 5.35 ACRES
       Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 21-09 that would
       amend the General Plan show a change of land use from Commercial/Office and
       Office/Education to Light Manufacturing for property located at 5718 West 3500 South
       on 5.35 Acres.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

               The applicant is proposing to develop the subject property as flex space with
               80 to 90% warehouse space and 10 to 20% office. The current C-2 zone
               allows a building with up to 50% warehouse with the balance being office.
               The proposed LI zone allows for a building to be 100% warehouse. Access
               to the property is gained through two easements – one from the east going
               through the Alpine Storage property to 5600 West and the other to the south
               connecting to 3500 South.

B.   14 ELEMENTS OF BEING A GREAT PET OWNER
     Layne Morris, CPD Director, presented a new pamphlet that highlights the 14
     Elements of Being a Great Pet Owner.
           - West Valley Animal Services is dedicated to the care of companion
               animals in our community while providing quality public service and
               adoption outcomes. We are committed to promoting responsible pet
               ownership through education to the public while adhering to City
               ordinances, State laws, and enforcement, for the protection of citizens
               and animals. In addition, we are devoted to participating in community
               collaborations and offering outstanding humane education opportunities
               to further our goal of offering Hope, Service and Mercy to our
               community.
           - Pets have a significant impact on our lives - they keep us company,
               reduce our stress, teach responsibility, entertain us, and add purpose to
               our lives. The decision to obtain a pet is not one which should be made
               lightly or hastily. Those impacts, welcome in our own home, may
               extend to the surrounding neighborhood in unwelcome ways if not
               carefully and deliberately contained. Are you a great pet owner? The
               following are 14 common signs to consider.
                   o 1. The Right Pets, The Right Number of Pets
                            Some pets, such as cats, dogs, ferrets, and pot-bellied
                              pigs, have such potential for neighborhood disruption
                              that they must be licensed with the City. Owning or
                              possessing these pets is a privilege that may be revoked
                              if the neighborhood impact becomes severe. No more
                              than two each of these animals, and a total of four
                              animals may be kept without a special permit from the
                              City.
                            The following non-venomous animals may also be kept
                              as part of the four allowed household pets:
                                    • Birds
                                    • Female chickens
                                    • Ducks
                                    • Fish

                             • Rabbits
                             • Rodents
                             • Insects
                             • Spiders
                             • Reptiles including lizards and snakes
                             • Amphibians including turtles and frogs
                             No other animals, including roosters, poisonous
                              or venomous animals, or wild animals may be
                              kept as household pets
           o 2. Dogs Secure
                  Many people are uncomfortable in the presence of dogs
                     and choose to avoid them. A large and unfamiliar dog
                     can be intimidating, especially to a child who may be
                     outweighed by the dog. All dogs must be leashed in
                     public, including on sidewalks, streets, parks, and
                     schools. Home fences, gates, locks, and accesses should
                     be carefully constructed, inspected, and maintained to
                     prevent a dog from running at large. Any dog found
                     running at large is subject to impoundment and/or fines.
           o 3. Quiet Dogs
                  Dogs should not bark regularly, frequently, or for
                     extended periods of time. Regular, frequent, or extended
                     barking is a violation of City Code and must be
                     addressed and resolved. Although all dogs may bark on
                     occasion, a dog who regularly or continually barks for
                     extended periods of time is an indication of a problem
                     that must be resolved. Typically, dogs who bark are
                     bored, lonely, or territorial. Solutions include increased
                     human interaction, introduction of new toys, or simply
                     taking a dog indoors. Although some people may be able
                     to let the sound of barking fade into background noise,
                     this is impossible for most people, especially those very
                     close to a home with a barking dog.
           o 4. Cats Stay Home
                  Although cats are not typically physically threatening to
                     people, their impact on the surrounding neighborhood
                     can be dramatic and undesirable. Flower beds, sand
                     boxes, and other areas may receive unwelcome feline
                     attention. Did you know a citation may be sited for a cat
                     running at large, or defecating in a neighbor’s flower

                       leash law as dogs.
           o   5. Licensing
                    Licensing can help guard against rabies outbreaks.
                       Before your dog, cat, ferret, or potbelly pig can be issued
                       a license, you must produce proof of vaccination. A
                       Rabies vaccination is required for all dogs and cats who
                       have reached four months of age. The most important
                       reason for licensing your animal is to help the animal be
                       returned to you if it has wandered off and been found by
                       neighbors or Animal Services.
           o   6. Socialize
                    with different situations and reduce the occurrence of
                       anxiety or other behavior issues. Introduce your pet to
                       other people and other animals frequently.
           o   7. Spay or Neuter
                    Spaying helps prevent uterine infections and breast
                       tumors, which are malignant or cancerous in about 50%
                       of dogs and 90% of cats. Spaying a pet before her first
                       heat offers the best protection from these diseases.
                       Neutering a male companion prevents testicular cancer
                       and some prostrate problems. In addition, it helps pets to
                       stay home and not wander looking for a mate.
           o   8. Obedience Training
                    Obedience training in dogs is critical to having a well
                       behaved and happy companion. Training teaches a dog
                       basic instructions and skills. When a puppy or dog has
                       training, both human and animal will communicate
                       better, forming the base of a lasting bond. Cats need
                       training too! Teaching a cat to behave in certain ways
                       will help him/her become social and content around
                       humans and other animals.

                       offensive to neighbors, especially with a breeze. Be
                       courteous to others by carrying “poop bags” for easy
                       clean up and disposal in trash receptacles.
