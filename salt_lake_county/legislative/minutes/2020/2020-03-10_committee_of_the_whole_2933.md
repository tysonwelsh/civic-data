3.3 Legislative Update 20-0288
Attachments: Staff Report
Presenter: Kara Trevino, Council Legislative Director (Approx. 45 mins.)
Discussion/Direction
Ms. Kara Trevino , Intergovernmental Relations Manager, Council Office, 
reviewed the following legislative bills:
-  2nd Sub HB 394 Homeless and Transitional Housing Program Amendments
-  HB 440 Homeless Services Funding Amendments
-  1st Sub SB 244 Homeless Shelter and Services Sharing Amendments
-  SB 165 Emergency Response Plans for Homelessness
Ms. Trevino stated HB 394 establishes a “homelessness czar” appointed by the 
governor.  It has passed the House and is in the Senate.  It has a delayed implementation 
date of July 2021.
HB 440 has already passed.  The bill requires that proceeds from the sale of The Road 
Home property be used toward debt or operations for the resource centers.  
SB 244 is to establish a case management system that is state -wide, so all data is more 
uniform and centrally located.  Funds have been appropriated for this.  This passed the 
Senate and is in the House.

SB 165 has passed the Senate. It requires local oversight bodies, such as the Council of 
Governments, to come up with emergency plans.  The plans are non -binding.  It has 
passed the Senate and is on the House reading calendar.
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated HB 394 is a work in progress.  One concern is the 
advisory nature of the state coordinating committee.  She is slightly opposed or neutral 
about the bill.  HB 440 needs to have money dedicated to it over the next several years .  
The information system provided for in SB 244 will benefit the state.  She did not feel it 
was necessary to have an additional entity, the Council of Governments (COG), weigh in 
on the issue as proposed in SB 165.  
Ms. Erin Litvack , Deputy Mayor of County Services, stated HB 394 allows the County 
time to go through the Kem Gardner Policy Institute and do the study that is funded in HB 
440.  She is also supportive of HB 440, but feels that COG is not the right place for 
emergency planning to be located.   
Ms. Katherine Fife , Policy & Program Manager, Mayor ’s Office, stated HB 440 
focuses on a long -term strategy for meeting homeless overflow needs into next winter.  As 
for SB 244, stakeholders support data sharing and feel combining a case management 
system with the current homeless management information system is a good move. 
Mayor Wilson stated the anchor bill that is needed right now is HB 440.
Ms. Trevino stated HB 440 passed this morning.  
The Council agreed to further monitor HB 394.
-  SB 203 Apprenticeship Utilization Act
Ms. Trevino stated this bill was assigned to a committee; however,  yesterday was the 
deadline for bills to leave their own house.  So it did not pass out of the Senate.  The 
issued will be studied over the interim.  
-  SJR 3 Proposal to Amend Utah Constitution - Annual General Sessions of the Legislature
Ms. Trevino stated this will change when the legislative session can start.  The idea is to 
start the Tuesday after Martin Luther King Jr. Day.  The legislators feel the first few weeks 
of January are a waste.

-  HB 305 Impact Fee Amendment
Ms. Trevino stated this bill changed dramatically.  Now, it just requires some notice.  An 
amendment in the Senate committee carved out specific language that only applies to Rio 
Tinto.  The notice provision for unincorporated development is also clarified.  The 
County is monitoring the bill.
Mr. Mitchell Park , Legal Counsel, Council Office, stated the definition of urban 
development is more than 15 units with an average density of more than one unit per acre.
-  HB 245 Dogs in Watershed Areas
Council Member Snelgrove  stated he is concerned that allowing dogs in Big and Little 
Cottonwood Canyons would add to the congestion in the canyons.
Council Member Bradley stated he is concerned about the water pollution issue.
Ms. Trevino stated this bill has failed.
-  SB 134 Property Tax Exemption for Wildfire Prevention
Ms. Trevino stated the County opposed this bill, but worked with the sponsor to hold 
taxing entities harmless.  It was discussed extensively at Utah Association of Counties, 
(UAC.)  The bill has changed since last week and now requires a constitutional 
amendment for it to take place.  There is also a sunset provision in the bill.
Ms. Catherine Kanter , Deputy Mayor of Regional Operations, proposed the following 
legislative intent for the Council’s consideration:
The Salt Lake County Council is committed to considering measures to mitigate the 
risks to county residents throughout the County posed by the implementation of SB 134 
(in the event it passes as a Constitutional amendment ); it being understood that such 
measures may include a reasonable reduction of the exemption cap percentage.
When the Council would consider the exemption, it would take into account the facts and 
circumstances that were in place at that time and use a reasonable standard to consider 
whether to reduce that exemption.

Undersheriff Jake Petersen , Sheriff ’s Office, stated he is also representing the Unified 
Police Department (UPD) and the Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area 
(SLVLESA).  They are concerned about the effect this bill would have on the SLVLESA 
budget.  He concurred with the legislative intent.
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated she did not want to put an additional tax burden on any 
one sector or group of residents.
Ms. Trevino stated if the constitutional amendment passes, the State Division of

-  SCR 10
Council Member Snelgrove  stated the Council discussed incentivizing County 
employees who opted out of the County health insurance program and joined a spouse ’s 
plan.  The Senate and House unanimously passed SCR 10, which offers such incentives 
to state employees.  It is estimated the net benefit to the state could be as high as $ 20 
million.  The Council should monitor the state ’s program to see if it would work for Salt 
Lake County.

Mr. Tosh Kano , Utah Campaign to Abolish Nuclear Weapons, spoke to the 
Council regarding the United Nations Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear 
Weapons.  He is a survivor of the first nuclear weapon dropped on 
Hiroshima, Japan.  His mother was pregnant with him and within 2400 feet of 
the blast center.  He described the devastating effect radiation has had on his 
health and the after affects of the explosion on people and property.  The 
U.S. needs to set an example for future generations and honestly negotiate 
for the abolition of nuclear weapons. 
Council Member Bradley  asked how the U .S. would deal with other 
countries that did not sign the treaty.
Ms. Deb Sawyer , Utah Campaign to Abolish Nuclear Weapons, stated the 
treaty has only been signed by countries that currently do not have nuclear 
weapons.
Mr. Kano stated between the U .S. and Russia, nuclear bombs have been 
reduced from 24,000 to 15,000.
Council Member Ghorbani  noted the number of Utah families who are 
“downwinders” and have lost loved ones.
Council Member DeBry  stated he would not be signing a letter in support 
of the treaty because it is a matter for Congress, not the Council.  Nuclear 
proliferation is terrible thing and could be the demise of the entire world .  
However, he felt the world was a safer place if the U .S. remained strong 
militarily.
Council Member Granato  stated she knew a member of the Enola Gay 
crew (the plan that dropped the bomb on Hiroshima) and he was haunted by 
the experience for the rest of his life.  
Council Member Snelgrove  thanked Mr. Kano and Ms. Sawyer for 
seeking peace.  However, he felt a U .S. that is strong economically and 
militarily makes for a safer world.

Presenters: Mayor Jenny Wilson, Jill Miller, Associate Deputy Mayor; Clint

Health Department (Approx. 20 mins.)
Informational
Mr. Gary Edwards , Director, Salt Lake County Health Department, stated 
the Health Department has been working on the Covid -19 issue since 
mid-January.  It is working on quarantine measures, tracking those who may 
have been exposed, messaging to high risk individuals, and protecting the 
health care system.  Testing is also increasing. 
Chief Clint Mecham , Salt Lake County Emergency Manager, Unified Fire 
Authority (UFA), stated the UFA is working to support the Health 
Department prepare for Covid -19.  The Incident Command System was 
instituted on Friday.  An Incident Action Plan has been created and will go 
into effect tomorrow.
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated last Friday she declared a state of 
emergency in Salt Lake County, which allows the County to be eligible for 
federal resources.  It is a complex operation and she appreciates the 
Council’s support.  She encouraged all Council Members or their Senior 
Policy Advisors to attend the weekly taskforce meetings.
Ms. Jill Miller , Associate Deputy Mayor, stated she is the liaison between 
the County and Unified Command, and she runs the taskforce meetings .  
They are working with Information Technology to see if employees can work 
remotely, and with Human Resource policies as to what leave employees will 
get if sent home and how they will be allowed back.
Sheriff Rosie Rivera  stated she believed the jail was prepared for this virus .  
Symptomatic inmates will have to be quarantined and one unit can be closed 
down for that purpose.  The jail is being cleaned, temperatures of all inmates 
are being checked, supplies are being ordered, and a procedure is being 
developed to transfer inmates to a hospital if they become ill.
Mayor Wilson  stated she has an exigent item that needs to be added to the 
agenda.
