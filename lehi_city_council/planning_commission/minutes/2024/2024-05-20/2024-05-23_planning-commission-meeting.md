3.2) Public hearing and consideration of Boyd Brown’s request for approval of the
Exchange Business Park Concept Plan, an 8-lot commercial development located at
approximately 1500 North 3600 West, with an exception to have parking between the street
and building and an exception to the building footprint.

After presenting on the item City Planner Brittney Harris went into the background on what
neighborhood commercial is because there have been a lot of questions.

Commissioners asked clarifying questions for staff. They inquired about the height limitations of
the footprints, specifically whether buildings could be three stories tall, noting the need for
compatibility with neighboring residential areas, which are primarily two-story condos. The staff
responded by confirming the height restriction to two stories to ensure compatibility with the
surrounding residential buildings.

Applicant Boyd Brown presented the project to the Commissioners, addressing their questions
about site challenges, including limited access and power easements. He explained the need for a
footprint size exception to accommodate users like a daycare and confirmed that the buildings,
though designed separately, might appear larger due to their layout. Mr. Brown assured the
Commissioners that the design complied with zoning requirements and highlighted the inclusion
of pedestrian-friendly amenities, such as a bike path and gathering spaces, to create a
community-focused area.

Chairman Jackson opened public hearing.

Scott Larsen: expressed concerns about parking and traffic near his home at 1500 North 3661
West. He explained that parking had become a significant issue after a bike lane was added, and
residents were now asked to park behind their garages. He was also surprised to learn that a
small alley behind his house would be used for commercial access. Additionally, Mr. Larsen
highlighted the severe traffic congestion at the 1500 and 3600 intersection and wondered if a
traffic light would be installed. While not opposed to the commercial development, he was
concerned about the impact on parking and traffic.

Cheryl Levinson: stated concerns about the already overloaded road systems in her area and the
potential for further congestion with new commercial and residential developments. She also
questioned the clarity of explanations regarding the Commission's decisions on footprint sizes
and was worried about additional noise and parking issues from small-scale businesses.

Britt Nyborg: proposed converting Building C into a community plaza with seating and greenery
to address traffic issues and improve neighborhood safety. She also suggested repurposing
parking areas for a UTA bus stop and advocated for more green space, appreciating the board's
efforts.

Michael Brown: understands the importance of development but was worried about the loss of
green space due to new development. He questioned why a two-story building was being placed
on the corner, noting that it obstructed the mountain view he initially enjoyed when he bought
his condo, and wondered why this particular location was chosen for the new development.

Cheryl Levinson again: she's wondering about negotiating with the development putting in the
infrastructure. She's curious if that has ever been on the radar.

Scott Larsen again: he is concerned about the garbage cans, and it would be hard to even get one
car through.

Ms. Harris summarized the online comments on Engage Lehi and email sent. Concerns included
the use of native plants and open space, access for emergency vehicles, traffic impacts and
signals, separation of residential and business traffic, and commercial parking use outside
business hours. There was also a question about water table considerations, though clarification
was needed on whether it related to soil or water availability.

Chairman Jackson closed public hearing.

Mr. Brown addressing concerns about the project, emphasizing its design benefits like reducing
neighborhood traffic and aligning with the original master plan. Staff discussed planned traffic
improvements, potential changes to parking and bike lanes, and water table management.
Commissioners debated exceptions to code requirements, particularly regarding building
placement and parking, suggesting adjustments to better fit the neighborhood while maintaining
necessary access. The discussion aimed to balance code adherence with community needs and
project benefits.


Mr. Chambers outlined three key aspects the commissioners need to focus on: 1) Whether the
project meets the existing code requirements, 2) Evaluating the access options and exceptions
presented by the applicant, and 3) If additional changes or exceptions are desired, those should
be addressed through tabling the application for further review rather than altering the current
submission. He emphasized that feedback can be provided, but the final approval should be
based on the applicant’s submitted plan and code compliance.

        Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to approve the exception from where the parking
        between the street and the building, but does not approve the building size footprint
        stating that they did not meet their burden of proof for Boyd Brown’s request for
        approval of the Exchange Business Park Concept Plan, an 8-lot commercial development
        located at approximately 1500 North 3600 West, with an exception to have parking
        between the street and building and an exception to the building footprint; including all
        findings stated in the report and all DRC comments. Commissioner Eyre seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, nay; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
        Commissioner Lockhart, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes. The motion passed.
