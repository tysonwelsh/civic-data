36          Process for Selection of Food Trucks at Riverton Town Days and Friday Fun
37          Nights
38
39   Craig Calvert, Contract Administrator and Purchasing Manager, said that Councilmembers had
40   requested a summary of the vendor selection process, particularly in relation to interactions with
41   local business owners. The City sent out a Request for Proposal (RFP) for vendor participation,
42   which had a submission deadline of February 8. The City limits participation in events like the
43   rodeo and Riverton Town Days due to space constraints and to avoid having duplicate vendors.
44   Multiple vendors selling the same items can result in revenue loss and participant
45   dissatisfaction.
46
47   He explained that the RFP was distributed to a vendor list maintained by the recreation
48   department throughout the year. Approximately 95 email notices were sent out, and the RFP
49   was made available for about 10 days. Once the responses were received, they were evaluated
50   based on specific criteria outlined in the RFP. The criteria for evaluation included favorable
51   participation and performance in Riverton City events or similar government-type events, booth
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   appearance, space requirements, compliance with health department regulations (for food
 2   vendors), and selling prices of items. Vendors were scored based on their responses to these
 3   criteria.

26            Budget and Roles Discussion
27
28   Mayor Staggs explained his proposal regarding the appointment of a city manager and the
29   potential name change to "city administrator." He clarified that the proposal was not aimed at
30   changing the form of government but rather establishing a better process for the Mayor, as the
31   Chief Executive Officer, to appoint a city manager who would be confirmed by the Council. He
32   thought this process would enhance efficiency and avoid potential issues and inefficiencies that
33   could arise when multiple elected officials appoint the same individual. He emphasized that the
34   Council would still have ultimate control over budget and policy decisions, while the Mayor
35   would focus on executive and administrative responsibilities.
36
37   Councilmember Wells stated that he did not want to see the Mayor position reduced to a
38   figurehead and highlighted the accountability that comes with being the face of the City.
39
40   Councilmember McCay discussed a budget item related to the request from Jordan School
41   District to contribute to the busing expenses for students from Western Springs to South Hills
42   Middle School. She acknowledged her reluctance to spend additional funds but recognized the
43   importance of providing safe walking routes for students. She supports the proposal to allocate
44   up to $40,000 per year, covering half of the cost, to support the busing until a safe walking route
45   is established.

 7   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, referred to page 27 of the budget and highlighted
 8   line item 10-51-630, which currently has $5,000 allocated for abatement expenditures. He said
 9   staff's recommendation is to increase the abatement line item by $5,000 to a total of $10,000,
10   and increase the litigation line item, specifically 10-52-315, by $20,000. This would allocate
11   more resources to code enforcement, weed control, and property cleanup. However, he noted
12   that if the Council believes a higher amount is necessary, they can recommend a larger
13   increase.
14
15   Mr. Carter said the objective is not to hire a crew of full-time employees for property cleanup.
16   Instead, the goal is to have funding available to hire a landscaping company as needed for
17   abatement tasks. This approach is more efficient as it allows the City to contract out the work to
18   experienced professionals who can handle landscaping needs, such as mowing and weed
19   control, for both public and private properties.

21   Councilmember McDougal expressed frustration regarding certain incidents where the City had
22   taken legal action against property owners but observed that the properties in question
23   remained in a state of disrepair. He emphasized the need for a stronger approach, specifically
24   financial resources, to address situations where property owners repeatedly ignore their
25   responsibilities.
26
27   In response, Mr. Carter said a tactical decision was made to settle with a property owner rather
28   than pursue the matter in court. He clarified that he was not involved in directing that decision.
29   He said that negotiating settlement deals with property owners who have a history of neglecting
30   their properties may not be an effective solution.
31
32   Councilmember Buroker asked if the suggested budget amounts would allow for litigation and
33   clean-up of properties. Mr. Carter responded that in previous years, the City has never had to
34   spend more than $5,000 out of pocket for property cleanups. He emphasized that while some
35   seed money is needed in the budget, it is likely that most of it will be recovered from the
36   property owners, and he was confident that a budget amendment requesting additional funds
37   would not be necessary.

44   Chief Blair Barfuss, Riverton Police Department, addressed the Council and emphasized that a
45   significant portion, approximately 60 to 70%, of the police department's dedicated time is spent
46   on traffic accidents and related issues. The Chief further explained that approximately 80% of
47   community complaints received by the department are also traffic-related, including concerns
48   about safe streets and walkways for students. The Chief noted that motorcycles were
49   historically discouraged for deployment, but the department currently possesses three
50   motorcycles that were inherited in poor condition from Unified Police after the split. With officers
51   recently completing motor certification courses, the Chief proposed utilizing motorcycles for
     Page | 4 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     June 6, 2023




 1   traffic enforcement at key intersections, construction sites, and school crossings, citing data that
 2   indicates 70% of accidents occur at two specific intersections. He explained that by investing in
 3   two motorcycles per year for a two-year lease or similar arrangement, the upfront cost would
 4   amount to $60,000 per year. The Chief highlighted the lack of proactive enforcement at present,
 5   as the department primarily responds to traffic accidents rather than actively working to prevent
 6   them. Considering the community's repeated concerns about traffic issues and the ongoing road
 7   construction, the Chief asked the Council to consider funding for the motorcycles.

48   a more southern location.
