Developer representative Leif Smith elaborated that the land acquisition is contingent on the MDA
approvals. They can’t officially close the deal until they are approved. Commissioner Oberg doubted
the legality of linking the approvals of the different MDAs.

MDAs, and what happens to the other one. For example, If the military says it is not compatible in

military had sent any similar letters before. Director Maloy responded the previous DAI agreement
was sent to the military for review. The military returned a letter of approval. They did not flag any

opined legally you have a review process through the military, but it doesn’t have any teeth.
Director Maloy disputed, stating he can’t say that completely, they made a recommendation, and we

Chair Powell opened the public hearing.

Army Compatible Use Buffer (ACUB) Coordinator for Camp Williams Ryan Becker- He appreciated
working with the city and the changes the developer has made. Their concern is the proximity of
the residential development to the boundary of Camp Williams. Their largest concern is the
artillery live fire training. The hillside above pods 12 and 13 is what they call the 500 series firing
point where artillery fires from. The map that was shown with the favourable conditions shows the
sound waves and vibrations from these trainings. In unfavourable weather conditions the cloud
cover and higher humidity allows the sound to carry and amplify. They realize the developer has
entitlement to develop this area. They do not recommend changing the agricultural single-family
zone to residential zoning and increase the density and impact of the area.

Commissioner Jacobson moved to close the public hearing, Seconded by Commissioner Garcia
and all voted Aye.

Applicant Representative Leif Smith expounded he believes pods 12 and 13 are the concern. He
clarified Camp Williams doesn’t want the zone changed from A-1-43. They are willing to notify the
property deed owner of the sound issues in the area. They are open to any suggestions from the
Commission.

Commissioner Jacobson requested Coordinator Becker to return to the podium. The map of the
unfavorable weather conditions was shown. He requested clarification on what homeowners in
the noticeable and distinct noise area will experience. Coordinator Becker stated it is usually
described as very noticeable but distant, when you hit about 120 amplitude it starts to have
