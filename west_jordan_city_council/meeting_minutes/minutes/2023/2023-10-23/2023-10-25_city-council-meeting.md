 4. PUBLIC COMMENT
Chair McConnehey opened the public comment period at 6:20 pm
Comments:
Cindy Leach, West Jordan resident, speaking on behalf of neighbors and residents of 8230 South,
voiced concern with a noticeable increase in traffic due to the closure of 1300 West. She said drivers
were ignoring signs placed blocking the road to through traffic. She thanked the Mayor for his help
and believed that full-size diesel and flatbed trucks did not belong on their neighborhood road. She
asked that something more be done.

Ellen Hills, West Jordan resident, thanked the Mayor and City Engineer for their help so far with 8230
South. It was her understanding that the 1300 West project would not be completed until March of
2024 at the earliest and spoke of the impact of the trucks using the road. Ms. Hill asked the City to
make a plan now for what would be done to address the issue.

Rob Pope, West Jordan resident, received a citation from West Jordan City regarding trees out of
compliance. He expressed the opinion that cutting trees to 14 feet above the curb and 8 feet above
the sidewalk made them look ridiculous, and requested that the Council put the ordinance back the
way it was. Mr. Pope noted of trees at City Hall that were out of compliance and believed a $900 fine
was ridiculous. He felt the City had bigger and better things to worry about.

Scott Bell, West Jordan resident, believed the long-term viability of the City would largely come down
to water management. He wanted to xeriscape his front yard next year, spoke of a free chip drop
program available, and asked the City to consider providing dumpsters on demand for disposal of
sod.

Ann Marie Barrett, Manager of Bingham Creek Library, spoke of recent and upcoming library
activities and events.

Scott Howell with Peterson Development reported on the Home Depot grand opening in West Jordan
that evening. He expressed appreciation for the service and leadership of the Council.

Rob Bennett, West Jordan resident, said he was grateful to live in West Jordan City, where people
were willing to work together to find solutions to problems. He thanked Council Member Worthen
for her service on the Council.

Jake McNinch, West Jordan resident, expressed opposition to the Peterson Homes high density
development. Mr. McNinch expressed the opinion that the Peterson Development was not financially
feasible, and was not an optimal use of the land. He said the Planning Commission unanimously
recommended against the plan, and reminded the Council that 2023 was an election year.

Cassie Jones said she seconded Mr. McNinch’s comments on behalf of her neighborhood, and was
opposed to the Peterson Development project.

 5. REPORTS TO COUNCIL
       a. City Council Reports
          Council Member Whitelock gave a shout-out to West Jordan Fire Department for a recent
          Chili Cook-off in support of burn patients. She informed Council of a new vernacular for
          ‘attainable housing for first time home buyers.’ She noted that there were currently
          165,000 entitled units across the state, which seemed to exceed the perceived demand.
          She highlighted that 67.2% of cities in the State had recently updated their general plans
          or were currently in the process. She mentioned a recent state audit of the Intermountain
          Power Authority and suggested the potential of similar audit’s for city interlocal
          agreements.

           Council Member Green enjoyed the energy and enthusiasm at the Home Depot grand
           opening, and thanked City staff for their part in bringing a second Home Depot to West
           Jordan.

           Council Member Jacob agreed that the Home Depot opening was a great event and was
           impressed with the success of the event as well.

           Council Member Pack left the meeting at 6:54 pm

           Council Member Worthen said there were cupcakes at the back of the room, and
           encouraged those in attendance to take one.

       b. Council Office Report

       e. Ordinance No. 23-37 amending City Code to add Section 9-4-4 Sanitary Sewer
          Pumping and Lift Stations
          Utilities Manager Greg Davenport reported that City Council directed staff to prepare an
          amendment to add City lift station standards to City Code. He presented the drafted
          amendment, and noted the Planning Commission reviewed the language and forwarded
          a positive recommendation. Mr. Davenport answered questions from the Council, and
          clarified the City did not currently have any public lift stations. He believed every property
          in the City could be serviced by a gravity system.

           Responding to a question from Council Member Jacob, Mr. Davenport said the City did
           have one private lift station, owned by an HOA. The proposed language would not prohibit
           a private lift station, but a developer would have to demonstrate to the City there was no
           other possible way to install a sewer system.

           Chair McConnehey opened a public hearing at 7:37 pm
