      1. WEBER COUNTY TAX REVIEW COMMITTEE (COMMITTEE) RECOMMENDATION REGARDING THE 2017
         TAXABLE STATUS FOR PARCEL NUMBER 22-171-0002.
            Joe Olsen, of the County Assessor’s Office representing this Committee, stated that Chris Karczewski built his
            home and resided there since 2017 and through the valuation and tax notices identified in 2018 that it was not
            classified as primary. He appealed the 2018 taxable status timely through the Board of Equalization (BOE) and
            it was granted and then requested through this Committee to address 2017, however, this is a BOE function.
            The taxable status was not appealed timely (in September) through the 2017 BOE. He referred to a couple of
            deeds before the Commission identifying the mailing address in Missouri. The county appraiser worked to
            identify taxable status for 2017 but had to default to the mailing address in Missouri. The 2017 valuation and
            tax notices clearly classified it as non-primary, plus it also defined it. He referred to a case in our county where
            the request for primary exemption was denied, it was heard by the Second District Court which found in the
            county’s favor, and the Utah Supreme Court agreed with the Second District Court’s decision. The Committee’s
            recommendation is to deny the request to change the 2017 taxable status. Mr. Karczewski said that it was his
            mistake, but that he overpaid the taxes, and he asked for reimbursement. He addressed Commissioner Ebert’s
            questions stating that it is his primary residence and owns no other property. Commissioner Jenkins stated that
            the Commission has no legal basis to approve this and Mr. Olsen said that it is due to the untimely filing and
            the application could have been accepted even until the following March 31st. There is precedent where the
            Commission has followed this and the courts have also held to it.
            Christopher Crockett, Deputy County Attorney, stated that the law is designed to ensure finality. Courtlan
            Erickson, Deputy County Attorney, stated that the Committee makes recommendations based upon the law,
            that there is a process set up by the legislature for people to appeal by a certain deadline, and there were notices
            sent out listing the status and every property owner in the county received a similar notice and had the same
            opportunities. The law is fair in that Mr. Karczewski had the same opportunity to review and act timely, as set
            by the legislature. He asked if it was fair to give one person a pass and not apply the deadline. There is value
            on adhering to the law. He believes that the Committee’s recommendation is the correct/fair one. Mr. Erickson
            read Utah Code 59-2-1347 which states if a person applies to the county legislative body for an adjustment or
            deferral of taxes levied against property assessed by the Assessor, a sum less than the full amount due may be
            accepted, or the full amount may be deferred, where, in the judgment of the county legislative body, the best
            human interests and the interests of the state and the county are served. Ricky Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor,
            noted that the county has made many clarifying efforts to the tax notices to better inform citizens. Mr. Olsen
            noted that taxable status is extended to the following March 31 because it is identified as a factual error.
            Chair Harvey moved to accept the Weber County Tax Review Committee’s recommendation to deny the
            request regarding the 2017 taxable status for Parcel Number 22-171-0002; Commissioner Jenkins seconded.
            Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Harvey – aye

    6. RESOLUTION APPROVING THE 2019 WEBER COUNTY BUDGET – RESOLUTION 62-2018.
         Scott Parke, County Comptroller, noted that the county has been open to receive public input since the tentative
         budget public hearing on 11/27/2018. The highlights on the changes include moving the gun range from the
         Sheriff’s Office in the General Fund to the Cultural Parks and Recreation Department, moving the Garage from
         the General Fund into its own separate business-type fund, there was a $50,000 appropriation to the YCC from
         the Restricted Housing Fund, $50,000 for the Western Weber General Plan, $27,000 was budgeted for a Roads
         grader, and an additional $10,000 for public defenders for increased contract costs. He said that the last tax
         increase was unpopular but it aided the county to be forward-thinking and now the county has a capital projects
         plan that allows savings in the long run. Changes were made to post-employment benefits and payroll practices.
         Commissioner Jenkins thanked Mr. Parke and concurred that it had been a good budget process. Commissioner
         Ebert said that it had been more of a problem-solving process. Chair Harvey noted that the plan had been to
         lower taxes but because there had been several unsustainable funds, that was not possible.
         Commissioner Jenkins moved to adopt Resolution 62-2018 approving the 2019 Weber County budget;
         Commissioner Ebert seconded.
         Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Harvey – aye

    13. INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT WITH OGDEN CITY REDEVELOPMENT AGENCY TO PROVIDE TAX INCREMENT
        FOR CONTINENTAL COMMUNITY REINVESTMENT PROJECT AREA (CRA) – RESOLUTION 63-2018.
         Holin Wilbanks, County Economic Development Director, said that this relates to a 52-acre area comprising of
         commercial, residential re-investment and additional parking that breeds life back into city’s the urban core.
         Brandon Cooper, Deputy Director of the City’s Economic and Community Development, said that this CRA is
         a 22-year project area or until full buildout from 27th St.-backside of 25th St. from Wall to Washington being
         proposed without eminent domain or finding of blight, it includes public outreach, and if all the projects come
         to fruition there would be a $236 million investment in that area, adding a taxable value of about $162 million.
         This is at 90% participation rate for the city ($7,778,895 tax increment contribution), the Ogden School District
         ($20,171,113 tax increment contribution) and the county ($7,098,802 tax increment contribution).
         Commissioner Jenkins supports this and Commissioner Ebert spoke of past concerns when the county gave
         100% contribution because it did not allow the county to recoup for the additional services.
         Commissioner Jenkins moved to adopt Resolution 63-2018 approving the Interlocal Agreement with the Ogden
         City Redevelopment Agency to provide tax increment for the Continental Community Reinvestment Project
         Area; Commissioner Ebert seconded.
         Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Jenkins – aye; Chair Harvey – aye
