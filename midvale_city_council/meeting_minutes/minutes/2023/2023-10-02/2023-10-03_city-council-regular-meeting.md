	•	EPA LEAD AND COPPER RULE UPDATE
Bryton Mecham reviewed and discussed the EPA Lead and Copper Rule Update.

Midvale EPA LCRR
What is LCRR?
	•	Lead and copper rule revisions (LCRR) was announced by the EPA January 15, 2021.
	•	The LCRR strengthens the current lead and copper rule enforced by the EPA. 
	•	The EPA LCRR is requiring public water systems to evaluate all service lines throughout the PWS and create a material inventory for each service line both city owned and privately owned. 

The History of Lead
	•	Prior to 1986 the united states had no rules specifying what piping materials plumbers were allowed to use. 
	•	1986, Congress amended the Safe Drinking Water Act (SDWA), prohibiting the use of pipes, solder, or flux that were not “lead free” in public water systems or plumbing in facilities providing water for human consumption. "lead free” was defined as solder and flux with no more than 0.2 percent lead and pipes with no more than 8 percent.
 
	•	2011, Congress passed the Reduction of Lead in Drinking Water Act (RLDWA) that amended Section 1417 of SDWA and updated the definition for “lead free” as a weighted average of not more than 0.25 percent lead calculated across the wetted surfaces of a pipe, pipe fitting, plumbing fitting, and fixture and not containing more than 0.2 percent lead for solder and flux.

Why?
	•	In January of 2021, the EPA began the review process of the current lead and copper rule and published the outcome of its review on December 17, 2021.
	•	The review stated that EPA actions to protect the public from lead in drinking water should consider the following priority: Replacing 100 percent of lead service lines (LSLs) is an urgently needed action to protect all Americans from the most significant source of lead in drinking water systems.
	•	EPA announced its decision to proceed with a proposed rule that would revise the LCRR while allowing the January 2021 rule to take effect.
	•	The LCRR effective date is December 16, 2021, and the compliance date is October 16, 2024.

General Requirements to complete EPA LCRR
	•	Inventory all service lines installed before 1988.
	•	Sample at least 20% all Pre-K, Kindergarten, elementary, and childcare facilities once a year for the first 5 years.
	•	Create public communication and education dashboard
	•	Create new lead and copper sampling plan to meet new LCRR guidelines.
	•	Create replacement plan once inventory is completed to eliminate any hazardous materials.

Inventory all Service Lines Regardless of Ownership Status and Intended Use
	•	All community water systems must prepare an inventory of all service lines connected to the public water distribution system, regardless of ownership status. 
	•	In those instances where ownership is split, the inventory must include both the system-owned and customer-owned portions of the service line.

Required Service Line Inventory Material Classifications
	•	Lead
	•	Galvanized requiring replacement (GRR)
	•	Non-Lead (or the actual material, such as copper or plastic.)
	•	Lead status unknown service line unknown (or unknown)

Goosenecks, Pigtails, and Connectors
	•	EPA encourages (not required) water systems to identify the location and material of goosenecks and pigtails (connectors) and to include this information in their inventories. 
	•	Lead from an upstream source can adsorb into the galvanized pipe over time. The LCRR requires that when lead connectors are encountered, they be removed or disconnected.

Notification and Requirements
	•	Water systems must make the inventory publicly accessible, including a location identifier for each LSL unless they serve less than 50,000 people. 
	•	At present, Midvale City serves a population of approximately 36.03K residents. We are fully committed to surpassing the minimum standards set by the EPA, and as part of our efforts, we will make our inventory readily accessible to our residents through an online platform.
	•	Once inventory is completed in full public water systems have 30 days to notify those who have lead service line connections.
	•	Public water systems (PWS) must indicate in their Consumer Confidence Report (CCR) how to access service line inventory information. 
	•	Nonlead PWS must also include a statement that they have no LSLs in their CCR.
	•	Under the LCRR systems subject to LSLR must replace annually at least 7% of the initial number of LSLs in their distribution system. The initial number of LSLs is the number of lead lines in place at the time the replacement program begins.

Sampling Inspections
	•	Targeted service line sampling Flush all the water from the service line by figuring the total volume of water in the service line. Once flushed out, let the water sit for 15 minutes then take a sample if more than 3 micrograms per liter the water is unsafe to drink.
	•	Flushed sampling Collecting a sample from the customer’s tap after a set flushing time. For example, flushing for five minutes could result in a sufficient difference in lead levels to distinguish LSL sites from non-LSL 
	•	Denver Water (Denver, CO), which uses pH adjustment for corrosion control, uses a subset of three samples to assess if a location has an LSL: a first draw, a second draw after a 30-second flush, and a third draw after another 30-second flush. If the average lead concentration is 5 µg/L or greater, they consider it an LSL (Denver Water, 2019, cited in Hensley et al., 2021).

Visua1. Visual Inspection:
1.1. Purpose: The visual inspection aims to identify visible characteristics of service lines that indicate the presence of lead components.
1.2. Procedure:
   a. Examine the exterior of the service line from the water main to the property.
   b. Look for signs of corrosion, such as a dull gray color or white-scale deposits.
   c. Check for the presence of a soldered joint that indicates the use of lead-based solder.
   d. Inspect any visible sections of the service line within the property that is visible.
   e. Document all findings, including photographs, and record the location of identified lead service lines.
l Inspection SOP

Mechanical Excavation SOP
2. Mechanical Inspection (Excavation):
2.1. Purpose: Mechanical inspection involves excavating sections of the service line to visually confirm the presence of lead materials.
2.2. Procedure:
   a. Select areas with the highest probability of containing lead service lines based on structures built prior to 1889 that are not able to be visually inspected. 
	a. Excavate carefully using appropriate tools to expose the service line.
	b. Examine the exposed sections for visible signs of lead, such as the characteristic dull gray color.
   c. Take photographs and document the location and condition of any lead service lines found.

Sampling SOP
3. Sampling if Needed:
3.1. Purpose: Sampling helps to confirm the presence of lead in service lines, particularly when visual and mechanical inspections are inconclusive.
3.2. Procedure:
   a. Collect representative samples from the suspected lead service lines following established protocols.
   b. Use appropriate sampling techniques to minimize the risk of contamination.
   c. Label and handle samples carefully to maintain sample integrity.
   d. Submit the samples to a certified laboratory for analysis.
   e. Obtain analytical results from the laboratory and interpret the data.
   f. Document the sampling process, including sample collection dates, locations, and laboratory analysis reports.
   g. Include the sampling results in the LCRR, indicating whether lead service lines were confirmed or ruled out.
Conclusion:
This SOP provides a systematic approach for identifying lead service lines for the LCRR. By following the outlined procedures for visual inspection, mechanical inspection through excavation, and sampling, when necessary, accurate reporting and compliance with regulatory requirements can be achieved. The documentation of findings, including photographs and laboratory reports, will serve as crucial evidence in the LCRR. Regular review and updates to this SOP are recommended to incorporate any changes in regulations or best practices.

	B.	CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2023-O-13 ADOPTING MIDVALE CITY 	PARKS CURFEW
Glen Kennedy said there has been a significant increase in vandalism, graffiti, and camping, along with other crimes in Midvale City parks over the last year. The negative impacts of these activities include increased costs and time spent repairing, replacing, and cleaning, and in some cases having to take park assets and facilities out of service for periods of time. As a result, the parks and amenities are less inviting to patrons pertaining to safety. 

To assist in reducing these negative activities, and to help the Unified Police Department with enforcement and policing activities in the parks, City staff proposed a seasonal curfew for City parks. The City Council requested the curfew be modified to be consistent year-round and not seasonal. In addition, Midvale Municipal Code currently does not include a specific chapter to govern Parks. This ordinance will create that chapter and the updated curfew section within it.

The updated proposed curfew is as follows: 

PARKS; CURFEWS:
Except for an individual in attendance or participating in a City-permitted event, it is unlawful for any person to be at any City Park between the hours of 10:00 P.M. and 6:00 A.M. the following morning. Any person who is in violation of the provisions of this Section is guilty of a class C misdemeanor.
 
Midvale City Parks Curfew Discussion
Background
Over the past few years there has been an increase in vandalism, graffiti, and other various criminal activities within City parks.

Currently here is no enforceable curfew for City parks
Staff proposed a seasonal curfew to help curb some of the nefarious activities and to assist Unified Police in patrol and enforcement efforts.

Consistent Annual Curfew
Rather than a seasonal curfew, Council indicated the preference for a consistent annual curfew of 10:00 pm to 6:00 am the following day.

Proposed Annual Curfew Ordinance-
Except for an individual in attendance or participating in a City-permitted event, it is unlawful for any person to be at any City Park between the hours of 10:00 P.M. and 6:00 A.M. the following morning. Any person who is in violation of the provisions of this Section is guilty of a class C misdemeanor.

Creation of Chapter 12.18
Currently there is no specific Chapter for governing parks within City Code
The proposed ordinance creates this Chapter along with creating Section 12.18.010 establishing a curfew.
