A.	CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2020-R-06 AMENDING SECTION 17-7-16.2 OF THE MIDVALE CITY MUNICIAPL CODE; ALSO PROVIDING A SAVING CLAUSE AND AN EFFECTIVE DATE FOR THE ORDINANCE
Jana Ward said Midvale City Staff is proposing a text amendment to add “Special Service District Facility for Water” and “Special Service District” defined by Utah Code.  The purpose of the Public Facilities Overlay Zone is to:

	“Provide for locations and appropriate standards for a range of public uses conducted by governmental and specified agencies.  These uses are operated for the benefit of the community and provide basic services and essential support services for the community.  Because of the nature of these uses, they are generally not identified with any specific zone district, having the need to locate in areas that best serve the community.  The overlay zone allows the city council, through its legislative discretion, to provide for areas in which to locate public facilities to best support the community in a cost effective and efficient manner.  It is the intent of this overlay to protect neighboring private uses from incompatible public uses and ensure the impacts associated with the public uses are appropriately mitigated.”

Utah Code defines a special service district as “a limited purpose local government entity” and provides the parameters under which it must operate.  Special Service Districts for water and sewer that serve Midvale include:  Midvalley Improvement District, Sandy Suburban Improvement District, and South Valley Water Reclamation.  The proposed text amendment will allow these entities the ability to use the ability to use the Public Facilities Overlay Zone for new development within existing or new operating sites within Midvale under the legislative discretion of the City Council.

Staff has prepared a text amendment for the Council to consider the adds “Special Service District Facility for Water “and “Special Service District Facility for Sewer” as allowable uses in Section 17-7-16.2, the use list for the Public Facilities Overlay.  No other changes are proposed.

The Planning Commission held a public hearing on the proposed changes on March 11, 2020 and forwarded a positive recommendation to the Council regarding the proposed language.

B.	CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2020-O-07 REZONE APPROXIMATELY 1.5 ACRES LOCATED AT 150-160 EAST 7800 SOUTH TO ADD PUBLIC FACILITIES OVERLAY TO THE EXISTING TRANSIT ORIENTED DEVELOPMENT (TOD) ZONE
Jana Ward said Midvalley Improvement District (MID) is a special service district that was organized for the purpose of constructing and maintaining the area’s sewer system. The purpose of the district is to provide public sanitary sewer collection and conveyance services to the residents within boundaries of the district which covers the area east of the Trax line to approximately 1100 East and approximately South of I-215 to 8400 S.

Its main operations facility is located at 150-160 E 7800 S. MID is proposing to make some improvements to this facility. These improvements are intended to create safer access from 7800 S and a better circulation pattern for the District’s large vehicles and the public’s RV’s and trailers when utilizing the RV dump station on site. This proposal does not expand the current 1.5-acre land area of the MID’s existing facility but relocates and expands a carport structure so it is within 5 feet of the east property line, redesigns the access and circulation through the site by widening the west driveway access from 26 to 40 feet, and makes the 3 access points off of 7800 S one-way. 

The MID property is currently zoned Transit Oriented Development (TOD). The existing use and development of the property predates the current zoning ordinance and is considered non-conforming under the TOD zone. It is not similar to other TOD type developments and is more characteristic of the specific public facility that it is. Because of this and the specific community function this facility serves, the request is being made to rezone this property to include the Public Facilities Overlay and to approve the proposed site plan allowing the following changes to the existing site. These changes will allow the facility to operate more effectively and in a safer fashion:

	•	Construction of a relocated carport with a 5-foot setback from the east property line. The TOD Zone requires a 15-foot landscaped buffer because the east property line is adjacent to a single-family residential zone.
	•	Allowing the existing two-foot retaining wall with 6-foot chain link fence along the east property line to remain. The TOD Zone requires a 7-foot high screening fence adjacent to a single-family residential zone.
	•	Widening the functioning west driveway on 7800 S from 26 feet to 40 feet and making the driveways one-way. The TOD Zone limits one-way drive widths to 20 feet.

In order to consider a rezone of the property to include the Public Facilities Overlay, a site plan is required to be reviewed in conjunction with the proposed rezone application. The intent of the Public Facilities Overlay is to allow specific public facilities to be located and developed to best support the community in a cost effective and efficient manner, while ensuring the impacts associated with the public facility are appropriately mitigated.  The required findings for this type of proposal include the following:
	•	There is a demonstrated need for the public facility within the community at large and it is not contrary to the public interest.
	•	The public facility is consistent with the goals and policies of the general plan and applicable ordinances of the city where feasible.
	•	The public facility is located, planned and developed in such a manner that it is not inconsistent with the health, safety or general welfare of persons residing or working in the city. This includes, but is not limited to the following:

	•	The generation of noise, noxious or offensive emissions, or other nuisances which may be injurious or detrimental to the surrounding area.
	•	The availability of public services to support the public facility, including utilities; vehicular, pedestrian and public transit systems; police; fire; education; and social and health services.
	•	The adequacy of landscaping, screening and buffering, building setbacks, parking, open space, or other development characteristics necessary to mitigate the impact of the public facility on neighboring properties.
	•	Proposed public facilities which exceed the bulk, height, density, or other use standards in the underlying zone must demonstrate such variances are essential to the function or operation of such facility. The development standards of the underlying zone and adjacent development shall be utilized where feasible.

This property has functioned as MID’s operation facility for many years. The use of the property is not proposed to change from what is existing today. The existing access and vehicular circulation on the site, however, are inadequate for the large service trucks entering the property as well as the public bringing in RVs and trailers to utilize the RV dump station. Trucks and RV’s/trailers are forced into oncoming traffic to make the right turn into the facility because the existing accesses are too narrow. In addition, the existing carport structures are continually being damaged from RVs and trailers running into the structures because of the tight space and no clear direction when entering the site. By moving the carport structures and creating clear one-way accesses as proposed, more area is provided for the public to maneuver RV’s and trailers within the property. This proposal provides MID with an opportunity to create a safer environment for MID’s employees and the public. 

The City Engineer has reviewed the proposed widened access and is supportive of the change provided the westernmost curb cut is eliminated and replaced with high back curb as part of the site improvements. Staff believes any potential impact to existing single-family residential properties to the east with the proposed reduced landscape buffer and allowing the existing chain link fence to remain is eliminated by the 65-foot wide canal located between the MID east property line and the existing single-family residential lots.


The items the City Council must specifically discuss and determine if they are warranted and do not negatively affect the surrounding area include the following:

	•	The reduced landscaped buffer, including the retention of the existing 6-foot high chain link fence, adjacent to the single-family residential zone along the east property line.
	•	The widened access drive along 7800 South to accommodate large vehicles.

GENERAL PLAN CONSIDERATIONS:	

The 2016 Midvale City General Plan locates the subject properties in a Stability Area, just east of the border of the Middle State Street Opportunity Area. The proposal to rezone the subject properties to add the Public Facilities Overlay to the existing Transit Oriented Development (TOD) zone is generally supported by the General Plan. The proposal does not change the intensity of the land use.

The General Plan states that:

“In Stability Areas, the current overall land use mix is desirable and preservation of these areas’ character and function is the desired future condition. These areas are nearly fully developed and have little foreseeable change in the types or intensity of land uses in the future.

A.	CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2020-R-17 ADOPTING THE 2019 SALT LAKE COUNTY HAZARD MITIGATION PLAN
Julie Harvey said the purpose adoption of the 2019 Salt Lake County Hazard Mitigation Plan to ensure that Midvale City is in compliance with Disaster Mitigation Act of 2000.

To develop and produce the 2019 Salt Lake County Hazard Mitigation Plan 24 jurisdictions located within Salt Lake County (23 cities/towns/townships and Salt Lake County itself) agreed to participate in the plan.  Planning teams were created, with public works departments, universities, GIS specialists, city administrators, emergency managers, and the public involved with the process and development of the plan.  Salt Lake County Emergency Management (SLCo EM) completed the updated plan with a contractor’s (Integrated Solutions Consulting, Inc.) assistance.

Salt Lake County and all participating jurisdictions, coupled with their respective citizens, stakeholders, and partner agencies, prepared this local hazard mitigation plan with the goal of guiding hazard mitigation planning in reducing the casualties and costs of natural disasters by providing comprehensive hazard identification, risk assessment, capability and vulnerability analysis, mitigation strategies, and an implementation schedule.  This plan demonstrates the community’s commitment to reducing risks from hazards and serves as a tool to help decision makers direct mitigation activities and resources.  This plan was also developed to make Salt Lake County and participating jurisdictions eligible for certain federal disaster assistance, specifically, the Federal Emergency Management Agency’s (FEMA) Hazard Mitigation Grant Program and Pre-Disaster Mitigation Program, and to earn points for the National Flood Insurance Program’s Community Rating System and (CRS), which could lower flood insurance premiums in CRS communities.

Local mitigation plans are required to be updated every five years.  This plan will be an update to the 2015 Salt Lake County Multi-Jurisdictional Multi-Hazard Mitigation Plan.  The Mitigation Plan is a collaborative effort which will serve all of Salt Lake County, including each of the participating jurisdictions, as well as special service districts within the county.  The revision of this plan supports the State Hazard Mitigation Plan mission, which is “to permanently reduce the region’s vulnerability to natural hazards.”

The Plan is intended to promote sound public policy and protect or reduce the vulnerability of the citizens, critical facilities, infrastructure, private property and the natural environment within the region.  The framework of this plan will now serve as a tool to guide, plan, and allocate resources across multi-jurisdictional boundaries.  It will assist jurisdictions in making good assessments of their resilience to disasters and disruptions.  It will serve as a guide to prioritize mitigation and preparedness efforts, allocate funding and guide development in innovative ways and to effectively utilize and share scarce resources.  It is a representation of the county’s commitment to reduce risks from natural hazards.

PLAN COMPLIANCE:  Disaster Mitigation Act of 2000

FISCAL IMPACT:  If the plan is not adopted by Midvale City then the jurisdiction will not be eligible for the Federal Emergency Management Agency’s (FEMA) Hazard Mitigation Grant or Pre-Disaster programs.
