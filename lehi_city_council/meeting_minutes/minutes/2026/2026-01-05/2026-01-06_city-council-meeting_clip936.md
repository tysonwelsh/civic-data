3) Discussion of the 2026 City Council Meeting Schedule and Agendas

    The council discussed how to structure the 2026 meeting schedule, focusing on adding more
    work sessions for a new council while being sensitive to work schedules. Concerns were
    raised about Thursday work sessions conflicting with Planning Commission duties.
    Councilor Stallings opposed combining work sessions and regular sessions on the same
    Tuesday due to decision fatigue, and emphasized that work sessions should be deeper,
    proactive discussions (e.g., budget, master plans), not just pre-council prep. Another idea was
    to hold meetings only the 2 nd and 4 th weeks of the month, or to hold meetings every Tuesday.

4) Presentation and Review of Rules of Order and Procedure, Lehi Municipal Code
1-6-3-1

    Teisha Wilson, City Recorder reviewed the rules of order and procedure from city code and
    Robert’s Rules, stressing that they exist to keep meetings orderly and efficient and that minor
    deviations don’t invalidate actions. She outlined the standard process for items (motion,
    second, discussion/amendments, then roll‑call vote on every item), and advised using
    “postpone” instead of “table” when the council intends to see an item again. The code of
    conduct section emphasized the mayor’s role as chair (voting o nly in a tie), that no single
    member speaks for the body without its consent, and that all remarks must stay on topic and
    avoid personal attacks. Public decorum expectations (no shouting, clapping, or signs;
    disruptive behavior can lead to removal and possibly a Class B misdemeanor) were reviewed,
    along with how agendas are set/changed and how electronic participation is allowed if
    members can fully hear, speak, and vote. Council questions focused on managing public
    comment time, distinguish postpone vs. table, and adding items to the agenda.

5) Presentation and Review of Annual Requirements Related to Ethics and Conflict of
      Interest

    Teisha presented the Municipal Officers’ and Employees’ Ethics Act and related city
    requirements, explaining that officials must not use their positions for personal or financial
    gain and must avoid actions that create conflicts between private interests and public duties.
    She reviewed the new annual financial disclosure requirement (forms due Jan 1 –31 each
    year, filed with her and posted on the website). Ryan Wood, City Attorney, informed the
    Council that larger or questionable gifts can lead to criminal charges. They covered when
    officials must disclose conflicts in meetings, when they must recuse from discussion and
    voting, and that in some cases even leaving the room may be appropriate.

    Ryan emphasized that voting or influencing decisions that clearly benefit you or your family
    can trigger serious penalties, up to a second‑degree felony under the Ethics Act. They also
    clarified that meeting with developers or residents is allowed but may be disclosed on the
    record, and that the focus of decision‑making must be facts, law, and policy —not personal
    relationships or pressure. The session ended with a reminder to consult Ryan in advance
    whenever there is doubt, and that further Open and Public Meetings Act training will follow.
