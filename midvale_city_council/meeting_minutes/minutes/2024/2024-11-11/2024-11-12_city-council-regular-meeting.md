       A.      CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2024-R-52 WAIVING NOTICE UNDER
               UTAH CODE ANN. § 17B-1- 406 AND DECLINING TO PROVIDE
               WASTEWATER SERVICE UNDER UTAH CODE ANN. § 17B-1-407 TO
               THE HOLMES JORDAN BLUFFS, LC AND GARDNER JORDAN
               BLUFFS, LC PROPERTIES PETITIONING TO BE ANNEXED INTO THE
               SANDY SUBURBAN IMPROVEMENT DISTRICT.
Garrett Wilcox said Holmes Jordan Bluffs, LC and Gardner Jordan Bluffs, LC own
properties that are being developed into the Jordan Bluffs Condo Project. Approximately
two-thirds of the project area is located within the Sandy Suburban Improvement District
(SSID). The remaining third of the project area is within Midvale’s wastewater service
area.

In order for the City to provide wastewater service to the northern third of the project,
Holmes and Gardner would be required to install an expensive lift station, and the City
would be required to assume ownership and provide continuing maintenance. This would
be expensive for all parties. However, if the entire project was served by SSID, the
existing grade would allow Holmes and Gardner to develop the project without
constructing expensive wastewater infrastructure. The City would also be able to avoid
the significant costs of providing ongoing maintenance for a lift station. This would
represent a significant cost savings for all the parties involved.

State law does not allow Midvale and SSID to simply adjust the service boundary lines.
Instead SSID is required to annex the service area within Midvale. This annexation can
be initiated by petition of the property owners. If the property owners petition SSID to
initiate the annexation, Midvale can assist in streamlining the process by waiving statute-
required notice and declining to provide wastewater service to the affected properties.
This would eliminate up to 40 days from the annexation process.

This request has previously been approved by the City Council. On September 1, 2020,
this matter was brought before the City Council by Lisa Garner, the City Attorney. The
Midvale City Council passed Resolution 2020-R-39. Holmes Homes received SSID’s
approval to be annexed into their service district area. Unfortunately, the resolutions and
the final local entity plat were not timely filed with the Lieutenant Governor’s Office. It was
communicated to the City that the employee at Holmes Homes who was leading this
project passed away during this process, and this project slipped between the cracks. As
a result, the City and SSID need to pass new resolutions or re-approve their previous

Because the annexation and service of the Jordan Bluffs Condo Project by SSID is
mutually beneficial for the City, Holmes, and Gardner, Staff is requesting the Council
suspend its rules and pass the attached resolution to streamline the annexation process.
This will allow Holmes and Gardner to proceed again with the annexation process as
quickly as SSID will allow.

        B. DISCUSS BUSINESS AND PROPERTY SAFETY AND SECURITY
           IMPROVEMENTS REIMBURSEMENT PROGRAM FUNDED THROUGH THE
           HOMELESS SHELTER CITIES MITIGATION FUND.
Matt Dahl said the State Homeless Shelter Cities Mitigation (HSCM) Fund was
established in 2018 through legislation (HB 499) with the objective to alleviate the impact
of year-round and emergency shelters on the host cities throughout the State. The HSCM
Restricted Account is funded through sales tax revenue from cities that do not host eligible
shelters and direct funding from the state. Host cities must reapply for HSCM funds each
year, and funds awarded must be used within the fiscal year in which they are awarded.
The Office of Homeless Services (OHS) is responsible for reviewing the applications from
eligible municipalities to ensure funding requests are directed to eligible services (public
safety, direct services, or emergency services which mitigate the impacts of the eligible
shelter on the municipality) and disbursing funds to eligible entities (municipalities and
designated service providers, like shelter operators) from the Homeless Shelter Cities
Mitigation Fund, following the guidelines and formula outlined in Utah State Code 35A-
16-4.

This fiscal year, Midvale City (City) received $3,597,026.71, which was approved to fund
the FTE for 9 Shelter Resource Officers, 0.5 victim advocate, insurance and supplies
needed for these FTEs, a weekly clean-up of the 7200 S Corridor, underpass
improvements on Center Street, and $200,000 to pilot a business and property
improvements reimbursement program (Reimbursement Program). The Reimbursement
Program is intended to support businesses, within a ½ mile radius of the homeless
shelter, mitigate the impacts of homelessness or transient activity on their businesses
through the use of reimbursement for the costs associated with improvements to the
property.

The City has been working to connect businesses along the 7200 S Corridor with
resources which help address issues they are facing, especially around crime and
transient activity. Through discussions with various businesses along the corridor, staff
identified a gap in support needed for businesses: mitigation improvements and damage
repair are costly and many of these businesses are looking for financial support to pursue
these improvements. There are no existing grant programs available in Utah to support
homeless and transient mitigation efforts for businesses. The Office of Homeless
Services and the Utah Homeless Services Board have approved this grant program for
Midvale City with the FY 2025 Mitigation Funds.

The grants will be awarded to eligible projects on a first-come, first-serve basis, until June
1, 2025. Each grant awarded will cover improvements up to $20,000, while funding lasts.
Midvale City expects to authorize up to 10 grant awards. Business will submit an
application with proposed project(s) – demonstrating how the project(s) will deter
unwanted behavior specifically related to homelessness or transient activity, or
demonstrating how the project(s) will repair property damage that is transient related.
Once a complete application is submitted, City staff will review the application within five
business days.

The following improvements will be eligible under the Midvale City Mitigation Grant for
Businesses:
       - Security cameras (expansions, updates, etc.)
       - Fencing (type of fencing should be able to withstand damage)
       - Landscaping (no rocks, no areas of overgrowth, etc.)
       - Add Lighting
       - Adapting or removal exterior spigots and electrical outlets
       - Security guards
       - Bathroom alarms or updated locks (such as Halo, Brave Sensors, which detect
       drug use and alert the business)
       - No Loitering or Trespassing signs
       - Damages to the property caused by transient individuals (businesses must be
       able to reasonably demonstrate that the damage is homeless or transient related,
       through police reports, camera footage, or other means).

Applicants are able to request reimbursement for other improvements but will be expected
to show how the improvement will mitigate the impact of homelessness or transient
activity on their business.

Grantees must prove that they are legally able to make the improvements (they are the
property owners, or they have permission from the property owner to do so). Businesses
will have to follow all permitting and zoning requirements for their property. Grants will
only be awarded to applicants who are able to use licensed contractors and professionals
for the project(s), when applicable.

Staff will review the application for completeness and eligibility. They will make a written

PROOF OF PURCHASE, PAYMENT, AND INSTALLATION

1. Proof of Purchase: Proof of purchase should clearly indicate the itemized details of
the goods or services acquired. Examples of acceptable proof of purchase include:
      - Invoice: A detailed document outlining the specific goods or services purchased,
      including item descriptions, quantities, prices, and total amount due.
      - Contract: A formal agreement specifying the terms of the purchase, including
      itemized details and agreed-upon pricing.
      - Bid: A tender, proposal or quotation submitted in response to a solicitation from
      a contracting authority.
      - Quote: A document that a seller provides to a buyer to offer goods or services at
      a stated price, under specified conditions.

2. Proof of Payment: Proof of payment validates that the agreed-upon amount for the
goods or services has been paid. Acceptable forms of proof of payment include:
      - Receipt: A document issued by the vendor or contractor confirming payment for
      the goods or services rendered.
      - Credit Card Statement: A statement from the credit card provider showing the
      transaction details, including the vendor’s name, transaction date, and amount
      paid (personal information and other transactions can be blacked out).
      - Cleared Check: A copy of the cleared check confirming payment to the vendor or
      contractor.

Examples of Acceptable Documentation: The following combinations of
documentation meet the reimbursement documentation standards:
     - Itemized Receipt: An itemized receipt serves as both proof of purchase and proof
     of payment. It should include detailed information about the transaction, such as
     item descriptions, quantities, prices, and the total amount paid.
     - Invoice + Receipt (with matching totals)
     - Invoice + Image of Cleared Check (with matching totals)
     - Contract + Receipt (with matching totals)
     - Contract/Bid/Quote + Credit Card Statement (with matching totals)

Proof of Installation: Proof of Installation should clearly demonstrate the installation of
the items or work purchased is in place and operates as intended. Examples can include
photographs or videos of work done, service reports, installation certificates, onsite
inspection etc.

-ZONING CODE AMENDMENT CRITERIA

Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) outlines the criteria necessary for amendments to the zoning
code. A proposal may only be approved if it demonstrates one or more of the following:
      1. The proposed amendment promotes the objectives of the general plan and
      purposes of this title;

       2. The proposed amendment promotes the purposes outlined in Utah State Code
       10-9a-102;

       3. The proposed amendment more clearly explains the intent of the original
       language or has been amended to make interpretation more straightforward; or

       4. Existing zoning code was the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact.

Staff finds that this proposal meets the third criteria listed above by identifying Home
Occupation Standards as a Supplementary Regulation that can be applied to all zones.
This amendment also adds Home Occupation as an allowed use in the Regional
Commercial Residential Overlay (RCRO) Zone that previously did not allow the use.

-ZONING CODE AMENDMENT CRITERIA

Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) outlines the criteria necessary for amendments to the zoning
code. A proposal may only be approved if it demonstrates one or more of the following:
      1. The proposed amendment promotes the objectives of the general plan and
      purposes of this title;

       2. The proposed amendment promotes the purposes outlined in Utah State Code
       10-9a-102;

       3. The proposed amendment more clearly explains the intent of the original
       language or has been amended to make interpretation more straightforward; or

       4. Existing zoning code was the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact.

Staff finds that this proposal meets the third criteria listed above as the amendment is
related to recent rezones to the Main Street Form-Based Code area, and the Figures
need updated to remain consistent with the current boundary.
