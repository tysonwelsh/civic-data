         The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agenda for the City Council and Redevelopment Agency (RDA)
meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; receive an update on the Zone Ogden project; discuss proposed Ogden Municipal Code
Title 8 Amendments; discuss proposed Ogden Municipal Code Title 10 Amendments – Enforcement of Motor Vehicle Statutes on
Ogden School District Properties; discuss Council/Board business; and hear Council/Board comments.

Agenda Review
        Vice Chair Hyer and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Update – Zone Ogden
          Senior Planner Rypien explained the purpose of the Zone Ogden project is to update the City’s development codes; the
project began in summer of 2023 and is currently in the drafting stage and Planning staff are aided by consultants from the firm
Logan Simpson. The Council previously received an update on Zone Ogden during the work session on March 4, July 8, and July
15, 2025. This is another opportunity for the Council to review the draft chapters of the new development code and to ask
questions and provide input as the code is drafted. Mr. Rypien used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide a review of the
following draft chapters:
              • General Provisions, including property boundary changes and land divisions.
          For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Throughout his presentation, Mr. Rypien engaged in discussion of the proposed updates with the Council regarding street
and block design standards; regulations to require private entities, such as homeowner’s associations (HOAs), to build private
streets and improvements to City standards; and flag lot/subordinate dwelling regulations. Mr. Rypien concluded Planning staff
will be holding a developer focused meeting on September 10; the purpose of the meeting is to receive input and feedback from
developers about the Zone Ogden project. A public open house is tentatively scheduled for October 22 to provide for public
review of the draft of the development code.

Proposed Ogden Municipal Code Title 10 Amendments – Enforcement of
Motor Vehicle Statutes on Ogden School District Properties
          Police Chief Sube summarized a proposal to amend the City’s motor vehicle statutes to authorize enforcement of the
statutes on Ogden School District property and to limit penalties for all traffic violations to those imposed by City ordinance. The
proposed amendments:
              •   Clarify that any penalties imposed for violations of traffic-related state codes adopted by reference may not
                  exceed those imposed under City ordinance;
              • Authorize enforcement of City vehicular traffic and the City’s other traffic-related ordinances on school district
                  property;
              • Remove language relating to motor vehicle insurance;
              • Indicate that school district property shall be treated as a public street, road, or highway for purposes of
                  enforcement of City traffic rules, regulations, and penalties; and
              • Make technical changes to revise the index and align the ordinance language with the Legal Department’s style
                  guide.
         Council member Graf asked if the School District or individual school administrations have the ability to take
enforcement action. Chief Sube stated that will be explored further with the District, but the City needs the ability to take action in
egregious situations.
         Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated that action on this item will be scheduled for next week if there is no
opposition from the Council. No concerns or opposition were expressed.

Proposed Ogden Municipal Code Title 8 Amendments
           Community and Economic Development Director Johnson noted the focus of tonight’s agenda item is to continue the
discussion that has been happening between the City Council and City Administration regarding proposed changes to Chapters 1,
4, and 8 of Title 8 of the City’s code, and more specifically the minimum standards at the Airport and Administration’s vision for
the Airport. He noted that if there is still some hesitation regarding the proposed amendments, Administration is happy to continue
discussion of the proposed amendments in future work sessions; Administration has heard from stakeholders at the Airport and
has worked to incorporate their suggestions and feedback into the proposal, but if the Council wants to hear more from those
individuals, that can be accommodated in a future meeting.
           Ryan Leick of Ascension Group Partners and Airport Manager Condie used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
provide an update on proposed minimum standards for the airport. Adaptive policy benefits include the following:
               • Allow flexibility to attract and respond to investment opportunities (SLCDA GA);
               • Adapt standards to fit all airport businesses (Avionics, Paint, Light Sport);
               • Adjust insurance policies with industry trends;
               • Respond to regulatory changes (GA 30 Civil Rights: statutory/EO updates);
               • Facility and service requirements constantly changing (Avgas trucks / self-serve); and
               • BEST PRACTICE: annual report by Airport to Council on previous and/or anticipated changes to
                     administrative policy.
           Mr. Leick then noted that all airports in Utah, except for Ogden and Provo, have moved from an ordinance in their
municipal code to an administrative policy.
           Council members asked Mr. Leick questions about the differences between the Ogden Hinckley Airport (OGD) and the
airports that are listed next to each of the bulleted items on the list as an example of an airport that has implemented the proposed
minimum standards; there was a focus on security measures and requirements, authority of executive orders for the purposes of
managing the airport, the frequency with which the administrative policy and minimum standards will be reviewed and updated,
tracking of metrics to determine the effectiveness of the administrative policy, the competitive nature of fuel sales at local airports
and the equipment needed to deliver fuel to pilots at OGD, the ability of the Council to regain control of the Airport in the future if
the determination is made that the administrative policy is not effective, and the information that will be included in the annual
report from the Airport to the City Council.
           Council member Myers commented on input the City Council has received from Roy City Mayor Dandoy, which was
largely complimentary, and a resident who provided input on how to make the Airport better. He expressed his support and
confidence in Administration for proceeding forward in this direction. He acknowledged the discomfort with change, but feels the
Mr. Condie and Mr. Johnson are doing good things after having received a very thorough study from Ascension Group Partners.
           Mr. Condie recognized the different groups of stakeholders, City representatives, and tenants at the Airport who were
involved in the process of developing minimum standards for the facility; it is a very exciting time to be part of the OGD, and he
looks forward to realigning and adopting best practices. Chair White noted that the reason the Council has so many questions is
that they have not been part of that lengthy process; she thanked Administration for taking the time to answer the questions the
City Council has.
           Vice Chair Hyer stated he can only speak about airports from the standpoint of being an airport user; the lack of
minimum standards at municipal airports can be very noticeable. OGD has the potential to make significant improvements, even if
that only leads to being considered a place for pilots to stop and get fuel when they are going somewhere. Word of mouth travels
fast in the aviation community and if significant improvements are made, that will be noticed, and people will come to Ogden for
many different reasons. He is supportive of the best practices and minimum standards and acknowledged that this is not the final
destination, but the first step in improving the airport. He believes the Council will have the ability to regain control of the airport
if the determination is made that the proposed amendments are not effective. Chair White agreed and stated she feels the changes
will allow for flexibility in the management of the Airport. He suggested that a member of the Council be allowed to participate in
the Airport Advisory Committee as an ex-officio member. Council member Myers stated he feels there is enough City oversight
on the Committee, and he is unsure it is necessary for the Council to assign a member to the body.
          Council member Blair also expressed his support for Mr. Condie and his vision for the airport.
          Discussion then shifted to development of a strategic plan for OGD; Mr. Condie stated the best way to develop a strategic
plan would be to hire a consultant who could oversee the project. Until a strategic plan is created, he will work to implement the
three pillars that have been identified by City Administration and supported by the City Council: promote local general aviation,
promote commercial air service, and promote economic growth. This led to discussion of the process to develop a strategic plan.
The group then discussed some of the questions the City has received from airport users about how the proposed changes will
impact them.

Council Business
           Update Regarding Recent Fires: Fire Chief Slater reported on recent fire events in the community; he first discussed the
fire at the Lotus Apartment site on 18th Street. The City learned the Tuesday before the fire of some security issues at the site and
Lotus was contacted that night, after which they began securing the building. Unfortunately, the fire still happened. Since then, he
and many other fire officials have gathered with local contractors to discuss the International Fire Code (IFC) and options for
better securing construction sites. There are 14 sites currently under construction and the buildings are at the same stage as those
that caught fire. The City has required fencing on the property, installation of cameras to secure the first floor of the building, and
fire watch in some instances. He noted that the developers of 11 of the 14 sites have complied with these requirements and the
City is still working with the other three; there are some unique circumstances for the sites, but he and Administration will keep
working through those issues. He assured the Council that the Fire Department is working to help similar situations from
occurring in the City in the future.
           Council member Graf asked if the 18th Street fire was a result of arson. Chief Slater stated it is too early in the
investigation to declare whether the fire was a result of arson, but it was definitely human caused.
