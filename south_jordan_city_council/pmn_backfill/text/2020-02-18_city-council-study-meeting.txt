COUNCIL WORKROOM

       A. Invocation: By Mayor Dawn Ramsey

Mayor Ramsey welcomed everyone present. She offered the invocation.

       B. Mayor and Council Coordination

Council Member Shelton said he attended the Legislative roundup meeting last Saturday.

Mayor Ramsey said her appointment to the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Board of
Directors was confirmed.

They discussed HB 273. City Attorney Loose said the purpose of HB 273 is for local
government officials to have accountability when they vote against an item or do a protest vote.
He said there have been bad decisions made by other municipalities. The development
community wants to correct behavior they see as not following the law. Because it adds fees as a
penalty and strips governmental immunity, there is concern that it could be a disincentive to
those who want to run for office.

Council Member Harris expressed concern that they would have to craft their responses with the
city attorney to protect their liability. He said they often don’t know their decisions before they
go into a meeting. They would have to table everything to consult the attorney.

City Attorney Loose said every land use decision would come with an accompanying legal
opinion. He said their South Jordan representation is sensitive to land use issues this year.

City Attorney Loose said there is already accountability for those that break the law. Its been in
the law for years.

Council Member Harris asked if only the applicant has grounds to sue, or could it be any
resident? City Attorney Loose said the bill indicates the applicant, but under current law, any

         C. Discussion/Review of Regular Council Meeting

The City Council reviewed the agenda. Mayor Ramsey said item I is a good project; it has been a
long time coming.

Public Works Director Rasmussen said the $2.3 million for item H will be coming out of the
culinary water fund. He said this is to satisfy the irrigation demand for the Salt Lake County
Regional Park, when it is built. This was scheduled to be a capital project in 2021. Because of
the timing of other projects, they needed this funding authorized beforehand. He said there are
adequate funds in the culinary water fund to handle this project.

Council Member Shelton asked what is the annual cost of this irrigation project? Mr. Rasmussen
said tens of thousands. It would be less than the cost of paying for culinary water for the park.

Mr. Rasmussen said any park property maintenance is paid from the general fund. CM Whatcott
said the city will be paying 50 percent of the maintenance for the Bingham Creek Regional Park
so this will save on water costs.

Council Member Shelton asked what would be required to connect it to homes in Glenmoor?
CM Whatcott said they can do that assessment. He said the people in the area have to pay for that
cost. The city fronts the cost but the residents pay it back.

Engineering Director Klavano said it would be difficult to get secondary water to the Glenmoor
area. CM Whatcott said it is typically so expensive that residents turn it down.

Council Member Shelton asked if they could do a quick study and see if they made the pipe
bigger, could they expand the secondary water project.

Mr. Klavano said most of the lots in Glenmoor are too small for an irrigation project and they
don’t have existing secondary water piping. The Wood Ranch subdivision might be different.
Staff can analyze that.

Council Member Marlor asked how are maintenance costs proportioned between the entities?
Mr. Rasmussen said it is based off water consumption.

Mr. Rasmussen said he feels the cost to put irrigation for most of the Glenmoor area will be
prohibitive. Council Member Shelton said there is dry pipe in Wood Ranch.

CM Whatcott said it may be more logical to tie into the pipe that Kennecott uses for the
Daybreak Lake. Mr. Tingey noted that is a privately owned pipe.

Council Member Marlor said he would like some comparison or understanding of what other
cities do regarding flag lots. Planning Director Schaefermeyer said the policies and how flag lots
are regulated are all over the map. The issue for him is if the city wants to allow flag lots.

         D. Discussion Item: Update on Construction Projects. (By Engineering Director, Brad
            Klavano)

Engineering Director Klavano reviewed a prepared presentation (Attachment A).

Council Member Harris asked if the overlay road project was scheduled maintenance or did it
not have proper treatment over time? Engineering Director Klavano said both. He said they
didn’t start the pavement preservation until 9 years ago. The projects they are doing today will
lead to less mill and overlay projects in the future.

Mr. Klavano reviewed the timeline and the 10400 South Bangerter Highway interchange project.

CM Whatcott noted that the Wheadon property project is a curb, gutter, and sidewalk project.
They are not purchasing the Wheadon property, except maybe some right of way.

Mr. Klavano showed a GIS map with a link giving more information on each project. It can be
accessed for residents on the website.

Mayor Ramsey asked about the library project. Strategic Services Director Tingey said they had
to cancel the contract with the contractor and rebid the project. They have a new contract and
should be starting that project in the Spring.

Mr. Klavano passed out a packet outlining the CIP projects for FY 20-21 that will be discussed at
the budget meeting next week (Attachment B).

         E. Discussion Item: Wireless Communications Facilities Text Amendment to 17.122 of
            the Municipal Code. (By Planning Director, Steven Schaefermeyer)

Planner Brad Sanderson reviewed a prepared presentation (Attachment C).

Meadow Wedekind, Long Range Planning Intern, highlighted the main questions they need
answered as outlined in (Attachment D).

Council Member Marlor said if they have a wall around the equipment, there may not be a
reason to require a building. Mr. Sanderson said that it true in most areas such as
commercial/industrial.

They discussed landscaping around the poles. It was noted there was no water put in with the
landscaping at the Glenmoor tower. Mr. Schaefermeyer said if they want to expand the tower,
they will have to fix the landscaping, but there is still no water. He said it could be challenging to
get water to the site.

Council Member Marlor asked if they do not allow standoff mounts, would they have more cell
towers? Mr. Sanderson said that is a possibility. He said there are also other options such as roof
mounts and wall mounts. Mr. Schaefermeyer said if they stay with the 1 mile radius, there aren’t
as many places to put poles.

City Attorney Loose said the FCC regulations allow non substantial changes, by right. He said it
can change the height of the tower to 120 ft. rather than 100 ft., for example. There is a lot of
litigation for this. There are new rules coming out for it. Attorney Sheeran said there has been a
request by the cell companies to clarify what substantial changes means, and they are asking for
another order from the FCC. He said it is highly litigated and controversial.

It was noted that the average cell phone tower height is 100 ft.

Council Member Zander made a motion to move this as a discussion item and put it at the
end of the regular City Council meeting, under staff reports. Council Member Marlor
seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous in favor.

ADJOURNMENT

Council Member McGuire made a motion to adjourn. Council Member Shelton seconded
the motion. The vote was unanimous in favor.

The February 18, 2020 City Council study meeting adjourned at 6:28 p.m.
