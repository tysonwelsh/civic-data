194   Senior Planner Jim Spung described the development process         as   it stood— what had happened thus
195   far and what would happen next. He explained that the items he would now present were legislative
196   actions necessary for the development. If they were approved, they would then be followed by
197   preliminary subdivision approval before the Planning Commission (an administrative action) and
198   a site plan review at the staff level, also administrative action.
199
200   The applicant was Paxton Guymon of PKEG Investments LLC, and he sought to develop the
201   property using the Site- Specific Development( SSD) zoning classification. The General Plan was
202   intended to guide land use and development decisions that addressed the present and future needs
203   of the City and to accomplish coordinated, efficient, and harmonious growth and development
204   within the City. The City Council, at its discretion, could revise the General Plan that it considered
205   appropriate     and that were in the best interest of the City and its residents   in accordance with the
      Taylorsville City Council Minutes
      January 22, 2025
      Page 6




206   land development  code( LDC),
                                      specifically § 13. 06. 030: General Plan Adoption and Amendments.
207   Overall, the project would include 50 new residential units- 41 rear-loading townhomes and 9
208   single-family homes. There would be four off-street parking spaces per housing unit plus 21 visitor
209   parking stalls. The internal infrastructure would all be privately owned so the city would not need
210   to maintain it. At build- out, the city stood to collect approximately $ 11, 000 more annually in
211   property tax.
212
213   Mr. Guymon had committed to ensuring that at least eight of the townhouse units would be eligible
214   to participate in the Utah First Time Homeowners Program which required an initial sale price of
215   no more than $ 450, 000, and it would need to be owner- occupied for at least the first five years.
216   That restriction ( the five-year deed restriction against renting) applied to all 50 of the units.
217   Following that initial five-year period, no more than ten units could be rented out at any given
218   time.
219
220   A traffic study was conducted and then revisited once neighbors expressed their concerns during
221   a public hearing. The firm concluded both times that the project was unlikely to have a significant
222   impact on nearby intersections.
223
224   Page 3- 6 of the Taylorsville General Plan stated the following: " All amendments                    to the Proposed
225   Land Use Map should be required to consider and not be in conflict with the following principles:
226
227             Promote economic sustainability;
228             Promote efficient use of land and public infrastructure;
229             Provide community amenities and benefits;
230             Protect environmentally sensitive lands;
231             Support alternate modes of transportation including pedestrian;
232             Create safe attractive neighborhoods and protect residential quality of life; and
233             Minimize non- compatible adjacent land uses."
234
235   The proposed development plan included a residential density of approximately 9. 4 dwelling units
236   per acre, which was consistent with the density thresholds identified in the " High Residential"
237   category of 9- 12 dwelling units per acre. After review, staff found the applicant' s request was
238   consistent with the above criteria.
