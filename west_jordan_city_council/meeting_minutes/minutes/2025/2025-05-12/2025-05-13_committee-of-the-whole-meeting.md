 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Discussion of the Fiscal Year 2026 Tentative Budget
          Mayor Dirk Burton said the FY2026 Tentative Budget included a 2% property tax increase
          but most property owners would see a 2% net tax decrease in FY2026 because the City’s
          general obligation (GO) bond would be paid off, resulting in an overall 4% property tax
          decrease. The FY2026 Tentative Budget included the following new positions:
               •   Passport Agent (part-time)
               •   Good Landlord Administrator (full-time)
               •   Engineer (full-time)
               •   Firefighter (full-time)
               •   Water Conservation Manager (full-time)
               •   Custodial Shift: 2 full-time + 5 part-time now in-house
               •   Parking Enforcement (2 part-time)

          City Administrator Korban Lee reported that the City would apply for a grant to fund the
          Water Conservation Manager position, but the position needed to be included in the
          budget in order to apply for that grant. Vice Chair Whitelock asked if filling the position

          would be contingent on receiving the grant. Mr. Lee said the City really wanted the
          position to be subsidized by Jordan Valley Water Conservation District. Administrative
          Services Director Danyce Steck said storm water conservation would be a responsibility
          of the Water Conservation Manager position.

          Mayor Burton showed expenses not included in the Tentative Budget which he would
          have liked to include (concrete crew expansion, one-two police officers, AXON DraftOne,
          license plate reader system), and expressed hope that some of the items could be included
          as the Council worked through the budget process. The Council and staff discussed
          expenses for which impact fees could be used. Deputy Chief Jeremy Robertson answered
          questions about a license plate reader system for the Police Department. Council Member
          Green expressed concerns about privacy. Deputy Chief Robertson said policies would be
          in place to regulate use of the information, which would only be used for criminal
          investigation purposes. Council Member Green would prefer an ordinance be put in place
          to regulate use of the information. Mayor Burton commented that more than the
          proposed 2% property tax rate increase would be necessary to add the items on the list.

          Council Member Bloom arrived at 4:20 pm.

          Deputy Chief Robertson reported of an offer for AXON DraftOne AI reporting software for
          $180,000 per year for the remaining eight years of a ten-year contract that was available
          until July 1, 2025. After that date, the cost would increase to $300,000. Council Member
          Shelton spoke of participating in a ride-along with a police officer during which a suspect
          was apprehended. The subsequent incident report took the officer at least four hours to
          prepare. Council Member Shelton believed the AXON reporting software would increase
          efficiency of report preparation and save the City money. Deputy Chief Robertson
          believed that AXON DraftOne was the only system that would fully integrate with what
          the Department already had, and the only system that would integrate with bodycam
          data. Mayor Burton noted his support for increasing the budget to include the additional
          items.

          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck said the Budget and Audit Committee
          requested that staff prepare growth metrics or triggers to assist the Council with decision
          making. Ms. Steck presented recommended growth metrics/ratios for Streets
          Maintenance, Fleet Management, Police Department, Fire Department, Events, and Utility
          Billing.

          Council Member Green asked if there was a better metric than average time between calls
          for the Police Department. Deputy Chief Robertson believed the average time between
          calls was a good metric for gauging ability to proactively interact with the community.
          City Administrator Korban Lee noted that Police Department efficiency was a focus for
          administration, and ways to save travel time were being explored. Responding to a
          question from Council Member Bloom, Deputy Chief Robertson said parking enforcement
          CSOs would be cross-trained to perform other services if time allowed.

          The Council and staff discussed the future need and funding for an additional fire station,
          and the needs of existing stations. Referring to all departments, Mr. Lee said
          administration acknowledged the need to increase staff over time, but would also look
          for ways to make existing staff as efficient and effective as possible.

          Responding to a question from Vice Chair Whitelock, Ms. Steck said the 3% property tax
          revenue increase shown in the FY2026 Tentative Budget included the proposed 2%
          property tax rate increase plus estimated new growth. Ms. Steck explained that budgeted
          property tax revenue was restricted by the Assessor’s Office. Council Office Director Alan
          Anderson said the County would provide actual property tax figures in June.

          Ms. Steck felt confident in the conservative sales tax revenue included in the Tentative
          Budget and answered questions from the Council regarding General Fund revenues.
          Council Member Jacob commented that less money transferred to the Capital
          Improvement Project (CIP) Fund would mean less money for projects in the future. Mr.
          Lee believed the budget was modest, with limited expansion. Ms. Steck said the Council
          would review five-year plans for the CIP Fund as part of the budget process.

          Ms. Steck explained that because approved annexation would be effective on July 1, 2025,
          the County would retain 2025 property tax revenue for the annexed area. The City would
          not receive property tax revenue for the annexed area until November 2026. Council
          Member Green pointed out the City would take over maintenance responsibilities and
          costs as of July 1, 2025. Ms. Steck said the representative of the State Population
          Committee she spoke to was not responsive regarding sales tax revenue adjustment for
          increased population. Mr. Lee explained that property taxes were paid in November to
          the entity or city in which the property was located on the previous January 1st. Council
          Members suggested sending a bill to the County for the months of services that would be
          provided to the annexed area by West Jordan in 2025.

          Responding to a question from Council Member Green, Mr. Lee explained that bailiff
          services were transferred from the Police Budget to the Court Budget for FY2026 because
          of the change to partnering with Taylorsville for the Court. All Court-related operating
          costs should be reflected in the Court Budget for the partnership.

          Referring to the Council Office Budget, Council Member Bloom asked if shifting from a
          twice-monthly to a quarterly digital newsletter would result in cost savings. Mayor
          Burton commented that the connection to the community would be affected. Staff said a
          quarterly digital newsletter would not result in cost savings because the City paid for the
          platform regardless of newsletter frequency.

          Vice Chair Whitelock was surprised to see in results of the resident survey that
          satisfaction with the professionalism and courtesy of City employees had dropped from
          72 to 65. The Council and staff discussed small efforts that could make a big difference.
          Council Member Green pointed out the Council had not been receiving reports of any

          billing accounts that had been written off. Ms. Steck responded that staff would work on
          providing such reports.

          Council Member Bloom asked about administrative overtime. Ms. Steck said her
          department used comp time instead of overtime. Council Member Bloom believed it
          would be beneficial for the Council and residents to see reports of the savings from staff
          cross-training. Referring to credit card fees, Ms. Steck said staff would bring a proposal to
          the Council regarding credit card fee recovery. Council Member Green suggested adding
          a credit card fee to any City transaction as a convenience fee to cover the City’s cost.

          Referring to Non-Departmental Budgets, Vice Chair Whitelock asked if the name
          “ChamberWest” could be added to the Chamber of Commerce line item. Referring to the
          allocation for the Historical Society, Council Member Bloom did not agree with the $6,000
          budget because she did not feel the City was getting value for the amount. Vice Chair
          Whitelock agreed but was glad the City had a museum.

          The Council and staff reviewed the IT Fund Budget, and discussed implementation of
          billing software. Council Member Green spoke of the need to implement a smart water
          app. Ms. Steck said the software would be presented to the Council when identified by
          staff. Mr. Lee said staff had tried to pace out changes in software. Council Member Green
          said the City needed to be more flexible and adaptable in getting things done, particularly
          when the Legislature called for specific changes.

          Council Member Green referred to the IT Integrator position listed, and asked how
          involved the position would be in extracting and using data to generate reports. Ms. Steck
          said staff had been using and trying to get accustomed to the Informer software program
          for the last year. Council Member Green suggested using Microsoft Power BI if it had
          become the industry standard.

          City Attorney Josh Chandler answered questions about the City Attorney Department
          Budget. Responding to a question from Council Member Bloom, Mr. Chandler said it was
          not necessary to contract outside legal counsel during his tenure with the City but would
          be done on a case-by-case basis if the need were to arise. Staff said they would follow up
          on questions about property acquisition performance and workload measures. Council
          Member Green would rather remove the information until things were figured out.

          Council Member Bloom asked if the growth in the number of victims served by Victim
          Advocates was sustainable with current staffing. Mr. Chandler said the office was doing
          more work than they had done before, but were also tracking the work differently and
          more accurately than before. He knew the office would welcome more help, and spoke of
          expected impacts of federal budget cuts, as well as a volunteer program. Council Member
          Bloom asked if cross-training with Fire Department individuals already trained in dealing
          with mental health issues would be possible, and wanted to make sure the Victim
          Advocates program was sustainable.

            Staff answered questions from the Council regarding the Justice Court Budget. Council
            Member Green asked why the Legal Defender line item was not included in the Justice
            Court Budget to consolidate the costs. Mr. Lee responded the Legal Defender contract
            could not be held by the court because of conflict of interest. Judge Ryan Holtan explained
            notification methods for the court, and answered questions about overtime. Council
            Member Green encouraged Judge Holtan to continue considering the pro tem judge
            program.

            Judge Holtan explained that court transcripts were typed live by the clerk, and minutes
            were prepared using live recordings, followed by a review from the Judge. He said virtual
            hearings for traffic cases had been expanded. Judge Holtan spoke in favor of the Victim
            Advocates program.

            Chair Lamb said the Council would continue with review of the Community Development
            Budget the following week.
