         The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the Special City Council meeting scheduled to begin at
6:00 p.m.; discuss the Fiscal Year 2024-2025 proposed budget regarding compensation and benefits and salary schedules; discuss
the Pay Parking Implementation Plan; discuss the parking and Mobility Enterprise Fund; and Council business.

Agenda Review

meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 Budget
          Compensation and Benefits Proposal – Consultant’s Presentation and Proposed Salary Schedules: A memo from
Council staff explained this agenda item is an opportunity for the Council to hear from representatives from McGrath Consulting,
Inc. regarding the compensation study. The Council has heard from City Administration about proposals from the study, but this
will be the Council’s opportunity to hear from and ask questions of the consultants themselves. Within the FY 2024-2025 budget,
Administration has set aside a total of $4,657,950 for compensation and benefits adjustments. This includes $3,131,275 in the
General Fund and $1,526,675 in all other funds. The McGrath study is intended to provide recommendations on all salary ranges
and position and/or title changes needed to provide competitive wages for City employees. When the budget was proposed in May
of this year, Administration did not know how much funding would be needed to bring employees to the new minimums
recommended by McGrath, bring Public Safety employees to the appropriate step, and provide any merit increases. With the study
completed, Administration will be able to present the final information to the Council.
          Human Resources Manager Briskey introduced Malayna Halverson-Mayes, McGrath Consulting, Inc., who used the aid
of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the compensation study performed by her firm; she discussed employment recruitment and
retention trends and challenges in the public and private sector across the country, the evolution of compensation factors over the
past four years, and compensation philosophies that were employed during the study. She then summarized the methodology
employed to conduct the study specific to Ogden City general employees and public safety employees, identified the cities that
were chosen as comparable organizations for Ogden City, and noted McGrath has made the following recommendations:

          Discussion among the Council and Ms. Halverson-Mayes centered on matters such as performing benchmark
comparisons with private and public entities for which the City is competing with employees; unique benefits that can be offered
in addition to compensation in order to improve retention rates for City employees; the factors, in addition to compensation, that
contribute to employee satisfaction, and City-wide morale.
          Chair Richey also invited department heads to provide input regarding their experience with employee turnover related to
compensation issues; the Council heard from Police Chief Young, Fire Chief Mathieu, and Public Services Director Anderson, all
of which expressed concern about lower-than-average compensation leading to difficulties in retaining quality employees. The
City is essentially training employees to move to another entity for higher pay.

Pay Parking Implementation Plan
          A paid parking program has been a critical element in the overall redevelopment of the downtown area. A paid parking
system was included in the MAKE Ogden Downtown Master Plan adopted in September 2020. An overall parking plan is needed
to help guide the development of larger projects in the downtown area like WonderBlock. An important element of that project is
a parking system that can be used by the public. To help with the parking planning, consulting firm Kimley-Horn was hired to
develop a pay parking program for Ogden. The initial parking study was completed in November 2021 and has had several minor
updates since the initial completion. In November 2022, the Council held work sessions and open houses to present the
information on the downtown parking plan as well as the WonderBlock project to the public. In early 2023, the Council reviewed
and approved bonds for the development of the WonderBlock project, and construction is currently underway on the first parking
structure included in the project. The proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 budget includes several significant elements related to the
paid parking plan. This includes a new enterprise fund and a new full-time staff position. The enterprise fund, to be known as the
Parking and Mobility Enterprise Fund, would operate in a similar fashion to the other enterprise funds the city operates in that
revenue from the paid parking program would be received into that fund and operating expenses would be paid from that fund.
Under the Management Services department, but within the Parking and Mobility division, a new Parking and Mobility
Operations Supervisor is being proposed. Up to this point, Community and Economic Development staff have been performing
the work related to the implementation of the paid parking plan, which has included public outreach to residents and businesses
most affected by the implementation of the plan.
          Deputy Business Development Manager Sawyer used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the Council with an
update regarding the downtown paid parking program. The presentation provided background information related to the MAKE
Ogden Plan, downtown paid parking studies, implementation plans, and a timeline for hiring personnel, publishing a request for
proposals (RFP) for the parking management system, and the rollout of paid parking in the downtown area. The anticipated
timeline for the full implementation of the paid parking plan is as follows:
              • July 2024 (or as soon as the position is approved by the Council) – Hire Parking and Mobility Operations
                   Supervisor.
              • Late Summer 2024 – Complete and publish the paid parking system request for proposals.
              • Late Summer 2024 – Present the new city parking ordinance for approval.
              • Fall 2024 – Award contracts for paid parking system services.
              • Late 2024 – Initial rollout of the first phase of parking equipment and systems for free (25th Street/Kiesel
                   Avenue).
              • Early 2025 – Go online with paid parking in the first phase areas.
         For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

Proposed Parking and Mobility Enterprise Fund
          Comptroller Sorensen explained City Administration is proposing the creation of a parking and mobility enterprise fund
to account for funds associated with development and management of a pay parking system in the downtown area. The
management of the new enterprise fund and staff will be placed in Management Services. The proposal also includes adding
existing enterprise funds to the ordinance that are not currently included. Management of the medical fund will be in the Fire
Department and management of the Airport and Business Depot Ogden (BDO) funds will be in the Community and Economic
Development Department. If the Council chooses to proceed with creation of the fund, it will be necessary to amend the City Code
to identify the fund as well as to adopt a resolution to formally create the fund.
