PUBLIC HEARING ON PARKS AND RECRATION IMPACT FEES:

    Mayor Nielson called the public hearing to order and stated that the purpose of the public hearing was to receive public comment on adopting Parks and Recreation impact fees.  Fed Philpot from Lewis Young Robertson, and Burningham (LYRB) reviewed the Impact Fee Facilities Plan and Impact Fee Analysis prepared by LYRB.  He stated that the proposed impact fee per household for single family was $2,802 and for multi-family was $2016.  
   
     Recreation Director Craig Oswald stated that the impact fees would help supply funding for future projects outlined in the Parks and Recreation Master Plan. 
     Donald Ball asked if the council had discussed the expansion of new green space areas in new housing developments.  He observed that we do not want children that are living east of Central Valley Medical Center to have to walk clear down to Pioneer Park.   He said that there should be a provision that a certain percentage of land in new developments should be reserved for parks and recreational uses.
   Mr. Atkinson pointed out that one of the goals in the Parks and Recreation Masterplan was for parks to be built in each quadrant of the city. 
    Councilor Jones said he had originally been opposed to the impact fees until he better understood that it doesn’t impact current residents but only new people coming in and new developments. 
   Rhett Peterson asked for clarification that the fee would only be assessed to new housing. 
    Christine Cookson asked why the fee was only hitting residential homes and not on non-residential.
Mr. Philpot stated that non-residential developments didn’t impact the need for more parks and recreational facilities as did residential growth. 
    Councilor Memmott asked about how these impact fees could be used as a tool for developers to add green space.  Mr. Atkinson explained that the city could work out agreements with developers that impact fees could be waived or decreased if they added green space to their development.    

Mayor Nielson closed the hearing at 7:20 p.m. and called to order the regular council meeting.

BEAU HOOPER HONORED FOR WINNING ESSAY CONTEST:

     Beau Hooper, a fourth-grade student at Nebo View Elementary, was honored by the council for his essay entitled, “Why I Like My Community,” that won first place at the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) convention held in late September.   A short video showing Beau explaining the many reasons he likes his community was viewed.   Mayor Nielson presented Beau with a Nephi City pin.  Beau received a $500 prize and the school received $1,000 from the League.  The contest is sponsored by the Utah League of Cities and Towns to encourage fourth graders and seventh graders to learn and write about the communities in which they live.

CR CIRCLE 2 SUBDIVSION FINAL APPROVAL:

    Mr. Atkinson reported that the Planning Commission had recommended the CR Circle 2 subdivision for final approval.  This subdivision is an extension of the first 7-lot subdivision on the north end of 800 East.  CR Circle 2 has two lots and would be splitting lot 7 from the first subdivision.  Both lots needed enough public road frontage to meet the code.  To meet the frontage requirements, some frontage on the road proposed by the Winn Ridge subdivision was necessary.  Since the property is not owned by the developers of CR Circle 2, an agreement was created between the two developers that would allow the section of road to be developed by CR Circle 2 if the Winn Ridge development is not completed.  The subdivision now meets all necessary requirements of the code. 
    Councilor Jones expressed his concern about disrupting the tranquility of the cemetery by putting a new road near the Vine Bluff Cemetery.
 Councilor Worwood moved to approve the final plat for CR Circle 2 subdivision.  Councilor Seely seconded the motion.  The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

UDOT SCHOOL ZONE AGREEMENT APPROVED:

    Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that UDOT had asked to sign two agreements with Nephi City governing the equipment involved in signaling school zones along Highways 28 and 132.   The agreements state that the equipment belongs and will be maintained by UDOT and the City agrees to pay the electrical costs of the signals.  If the intersections where these signals are located were ever signalized with a traffic light, the flasher assemblies would be removed at no cost to the City.  Nephi City would also agree to continue to operate the flashers in accordance with the Utah Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices “Traffic Control for School Zones.”   Mr. Atkinson stated that he and City Attorney Kasey Wright had read over the agreements and did not recommend any changes.
     Councilor Ostler moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the Cooperative Agreements with UDOT for the Installation, Maintenance, and Operation of Reduced Speed School Zone Flasher Assemblies on Highways 28 and 132.   Councilor Jones seconded the motion.  The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
