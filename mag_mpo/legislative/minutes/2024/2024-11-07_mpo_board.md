Local Options Sales Taxes for Transportation (00:05:31) Leif Elder is the Director of Policy and Legislative Affairs with UDOT. He talked about the transportation budget related to state roads. With the Local Options Sales Tax (LOST) for transportation there are eligible uses that include the following: ● Development, construction, maintenance or operation of Class A, B, C, D roads ● Pedestrian / active transportation ● Public transit (required for certain options) ● Corridor preservation ● All other forms of transportation ● Airports Page 2 of 5 ● Public safety (only for certain options) The first of the Utah Code LOST started in 1975. Leif Elder stated that in some cases the County is able to choose how much of the tax goes to transportation, and they may not elect for the full quarter of the tax to go to transportation. The distribution of funds for Utah County in 2024 Fiscal Year is as follows. County ~$68 M Cities ~$28 M UTA ~$134 M Total ~$230 M There was an indepth discussion on the distribution of the Fifth fifth LOST rate related to management and distribution of the funds currently and into the future. 2023 RTP Amendment 2 (00:51:45) Kendall Willardson stated these are amendments for extensions, additions, and larger scale changes. These are driven by decisions made by the Utah Transportation Commission in May of 2024 and additional legislative funding. The amendments are broken into three categories. and they are based on Air Quality. There is one Level 1 Amendment ● Amendment 1: A88, Highline Canal changes the phase. The next amendments are level 2 Amendments ● Amendment 2: H31, Cory Wride Freeway changes the phase and cost of the project. ● Amendment 3: H32, Cory Wride Freeway changes cost and frontage roads to freeways. ● Amendment 4 ○ H66, Cedar Valley FWY changes route alignment and cost. ○ H67, Cory Wride FWY removes this project and elements are moved to H32 and H89. ○ H89, Cedar Valley FWY changes route alignment and cost. ○ H90, Other Arterials removes the project from the RTP. ○ H91, Other Arterials changes route alignment and cost. ● Amendment 5: H114, Geneva Rd proposes a new project to the RTP. ● Amendment 6: H115, I-15 Passing Lanes proposed a new project to the RTP. There was discussion related to the proposed Amendment 4 alignment and funding. These proposals will be presented to the ICT, have air quality models run, and a 30-day public comment period. The MPO Board will review the proposed Amendments in January for approval. Page 3 of 5 Public Engagement Plan Adoption (01:10:14) Kendall Willardson stated this is the Public Engagement Plan (PEP). There was a 45-day public comment period that ended with no public comments received. This document goes over how the MPO will inform the public of the various plans and projects managed by the MPO and then different methods of public outreach. Mayor Matt Packard moved to approve the Public Engagement Plan as presented, finding that it adheres to federal regulations and addresses the issues raised in the 2023 Federal Planning Certification Review of the MAG MPO. Mayor Ostler seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Corridor Preservation Provo 800 North (01:12:50) Calvin Clark introduced the corridor preservation request from Provo City. It is for corridor H42 and the property is a single family home. The city was approached to purchase the property by the seller. Mayor Brad Frost moved to approve the city of Provo's request for $460,875 from the Corridor Preservation Fund, finding that it adheres to the fund's requirements. Mayor Carolyn Lundberg seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Congestion Management Process (CMP) Overview (01:14:58) Calvin Clark stated the CMP, broadly speaking, it's an approach for managing congestion, and particularly assessing the alternative strategies for congestion management. FHWA has 8 recommended actions within the policy and process. 1. Develop regional objectives for congestion management 2. Define CMP network 3. Develop multimodal performance measures 4. Collect data/ monitor system performance 5. Analyze congestion problems and needs 6. Identify and assess strategies 7. Evaluate strategy effectiveness This item will be ready for action by the MPO Board in January.
