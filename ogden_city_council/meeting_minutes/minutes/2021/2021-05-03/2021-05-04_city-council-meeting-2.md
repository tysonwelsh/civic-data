Mayor's Proposed Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Budget Presentation
          The Mayor's Proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2021-2022 Budget came before the Council for consideration. A memo from
Council staff explained each year during the first Council Meeting in May, the Mayor is required to present a proposed budget for
the upcoming fiscal year. The Council accepts the proposed budget for review and, through a series of work sessions, reviews the
proposal over the next two months. A tentative budget is adopted after holding an initial public hearing. A second public hearing
is held when the fmal budget is adopted. During the budget hearings, the Council outlines their proposed changes to the budget
and accepts public input. Once the fmal public hearing is closed, the Council makes changes and adopts the fmal City budget. By
State law, a budget must be adopted by June 3Oth. An exception to the timing is allowed if the Council is considering a proposed
property tax increase.
          Mayor Caldwell used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide his proposed FY 2022 budget to the Council; he
discussed the economic outlook for 2021 as a community, State, and Nation recovering from the economic harm caused by the
COVID-19 pandemic. The major economic downturn projected for 2020 due to the pandemic did not come to fruition; Ogden
City experienced a muted economic impact with the federal government providing a buffer via CARES Act funding. The
pandemic on the Utah economy has been much milder than initially expected and a strong recovery is forecast for 2021 according
to the University ofUtah' s Policy Institute economic report to the Governor. City Administration has undertaken several efforts to
boost recovery from the pandemic, including an emergency loan fund for local businesses and implementation of the Ogden
CARES grant program for businesses and non-profits. He then presented a chart offering a budget comparison for the proposed
FY2022 budget with the FY2021 adopted budget; the FY2022 General Fund budget will increase by $7 million, or 11.98 percent.
The total municipal budget will increase by nearly $3 0 million, or 15.62 percent. He identified major changes in the General Fund,
after which he discussed media spotlight pieces of Ogden City. Ogden has been recognized as the following:
              •     Number 9 Best Performing City in the Nation (Milken Institute, 2021).
              •     5 Cities Ready to Build with Remote Workers in Mind (Bloomberg, 2021 ).
              •     Top 50 Best U.S. Cities for Starting a Business (Inc., 2020).
              •     Top 20 U.S. Cities for Manufacturers (Global Trade Magazine, 2020).
              •     12 Small Towns with Beautiful Architecture (Readers Digest, 2020)
          Mayor Caldwell noted one highlight for the FY2022 budget year is the Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) project, which will
build the City's transportation future. The project will include 13 upgraded stations, 1.5 miles of dedicated bus lanes, 11 clean air
electric buses, 19 lights with signal priority, 10-15 minute weekday service, and free fare for three years. The project's expected to
be completed in 2023. There are also several redevelopment projects currently underway, including:
              •     MAKE Ogden, Updated Downtown Master Plan
              •     Wonder Block (former Hostess building)
              •     Electric Alley Remote Work Hub
              •     Union Station Campus
              •     RiverBend Ogden
              •     Ogden Business Exchange and Swift Building Site
              •     Nine Rails Creative District and Dumke Arts Plaza
              •     Capitol Square (former Rite Aid)
              •     Gibson Grove
          Business recruitment and expansion efforts continue at Business Depot Ogden and other project areas throughout the
community.
          The Ogden City Police Department has received notable recognitions for their traffic bureau and safe neighborhoods
project. The Department has also achieved crime reduction of28.8 percent from 2017 to 2020, with continued crime reduction
efforts underway.

        In the Community and Economic Development Department, there has been a focus on housing and neighborhood
improvements with key projects including the Stone Hill project, 550 24th Street, home rehabilitation projects, and the Own-in-
Ogden program. There has also been growth in multi-family housing projects throughout the City.

          The Arts, Culture, and Events Division has been working hard on special events in the community, including the
Farmer's Market for the 2021 season, Monday moving nights, Arts Festival, Juneteenth, Enable Concert, Fann in the City,
Pioneer Days Radel, and the Twilight Concert Series.
          Mayor Caldwell then highlighted projects and expenditures that will be funded using Business Depot Ogden (BDO)
Lease Revenue as well as the list of Capital Improvement Plan projects included in the budget. There have been significant efforts
to invest in sidewalk improvements. Additionally, Administration believes it is important to make significant inveshnents in City
staffmg; the budget includes an increase in the number of full-time employees by a net of five, one position reclassification, and
one position retitled. No positions will be eliminated and there will be no hiring freeze as there are currently 46 vacant positions.
No full-time positions will be laid off; high turnover is a concern City-wide, not just in public safety and recruihnentto fill vacant
positions continues to be a challenge.
          Mayor Caldwell concluded by thanking the Ogden citizens, City Council members, City Council staff, Department
Directors, and Finance staff for their attention to detail when reviewing and contributing to the preparation of the proposed
FY2022 budget.
          Council member Stephens thanked Administration for pursuing electric charging stations in the City in an effort to
conserve energy and promote green energy. He also referenced the consumer confidence index chart included in Mayor
Caldwell's budget presentation and stated that there seems to be some optimism going forward as the 2020 dip in consumer
confidence was not as significant as that in 2009 at the onset of the latest recession. The challenge the City is facing now is the
potential for inflation rates to spike dramatically. Mayor Caldwell agreed and stated it has been very difficult to predict how the
community's economy will react to many difficult situations created by COVID-19; however, he believes Ogden City is in a
better position than many other communities and that is largely due to the hard work of City staff, the City Council, local
businesses, and residents.
          Vice Chair White commended the City's Finance Department for taking extra time to keep the Council informed of real
time conditions in terms of sales tax revenues and how they are being impacted by the pandemic. She stated she hopes that will
continue in the upcoming Fiscal Year.
          Chair Blair thanked Mayor Caldwell for his thorough presentation of his proposed budget.

Public Comments
         Heath Satow stated that Council member Nadolski, a Council member that he respects, made it clear to him his trust and
respect of Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper and how his comments to the Council have come
across as personal attacks on Mr. Cooper. He stated that was not his goal, but he recognizes how it could have appeared that way
and he apologized. He regrets that his delivery of the questions he has asked have taken away from the focus on the questions
themselves. He was and is still frustrated about the lack of transparency in processes the City follows for certain redevelopment
projects and he hopes that his feedback will help those processes to evolve and become more transparent. He still has two
questions and he has emailed them to the City Council for their review. He then stated that as it becomes possible to hold in-
person Council meetings in the Council Chambers, he hopes the City will continue to allow residents to participate via Zoom,
particularly for work sessions that begin at4:00 p.m. as it is difficult for some to attend a meeting at that time of day. Zoom also
makes it easier for many to attend meetings and provide input. He concluded by discussing the claim of the job market in Ogden
being number one in America for 2021; that data is for the Ogden-Clearfield area, which is largely due to the presence of the
military operations at Hill Air Force Base.
         Angel Castillo thanked the Council for the questions they asked during the work session meeting as then engaged in
discussion with Police Department Administration regarding operations of their department. She echoed Mr. Satow's comments
regarding references to the Ogden-Clearfield metro area for various data points; this includes housing statistics that are not
representative of Ogden proper; the median income in the Clearfield area is much higher than the Ogden area and the two should
not be grouped together for the purposes of basing very important decisions on the data for the metro area.
