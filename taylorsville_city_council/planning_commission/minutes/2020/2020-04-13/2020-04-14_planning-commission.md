ZONING TEXT AMENDMENT
3. Recommendation to the City Council for a Zoning Text Amendment to the Taylorsville Municipal Code,
Chapter 131.33.110. Mark McGrath/AICP/Community Development
3.1 Mr. McGrath presented this item. The intent of these proposed amendments to the Taylorsville City

Code is to address an existing conflict within the Code concerning the appeal process for non-administrative

conditional use permits and brings consistency to certain terms used in the Code.

3.1.1. It was recently discovered that there is a conflict in the Taylorsville City Code regarding the process
for appeals of non-administrative conditional use permits, in that chapter, 13.03, states that appeals
of non-administrative conditional use permits are to be heard by the “appeal authority” (currently
identified as a “hearing officer’ appointed by the Mayor with the advice and consent of the City
Council.

3.1.2 Chapter 13.33, Administrative and Non-Administrative Conditional Use Permit, however, states that
non-administrative conditional use appeals will be heard by the City Council. In order to bring
consistency within the Code, Staff is proposing to amend Section 13.33.110 Non-administrative
Conditional Use Appeals, to state that non-administrative CUPs will be heard by the “appeal
authority”.

3.1.3 Section 13.33.100 identifies the appeal authority for administrative conditional uses (i.e., conditional
use permits delegated by Code to Staff), as the “administrative law judge” rather than “appeal
authority” or “hearing officer”, although they are all one in the same. In order to bring consistency
to the verbiage utilized in the Code, Staff proposes to change references to the “administrative law
judge” in 13.33.100 to “appeal authority” .

3.2 FINDINGS:

3.2.1 Current Taylorsville Code contains contradiction in the required process for appeals of non-
administrative conditional use permits.

3.2.2. Amending Section 13.33.110 to identify the appeal authority as the appellant for non-administrative
conditional use permits will bring consistency within the Code concerning appeals of non-
administrative conditional use permits.

3.2.3. Altering Section 13.33.100 to amend the term administrative law judge to appeal authority will bring
consistency to the verbiage utilized in the Code regarding conditional use appeals.

3.3. PUBLIC COMMENTS: Commissioner McElreath opened the public hearing and asked if there had been any
comments received by Staff prior to this meeting with regard to this application, to which he was informed
there had not. Because there was no one wishing to speak either in favor or opposition to this change,
Commissioner McElreath closed the public hearing and opened it for discussion or a motion by the Planning
Commission.

5. 2520 — Wade and Heather Francom - Request for Consideration of a Two-Lot Residential Subdivision on
property Located at 4495-4497 South Heatherglen Circle. Jim Spung/AICP/Senior Planner
5.1 Mr. Spung presented this item. The property owners at 4495 and 4497 South Heatherglen Circle are
requesting to amend the subdivision plat for their properties. These properties are located within the

Heatherglen neighborhood and carry a unique set of conditions which the applicants wish to remove from

their properties by recording a new plat.

5.1.1 In 1978, a standard subdivision plat was recorded; however, in 1979 the plat was re-recorded as a
condominium plat. When the plat was re-recorded as a condominium plat, provisions were put on
the properties including complying with the Utah Condominium Ownership Act and identifying the
property as private, limited common, and common areas. The original condominium plat that was
recorded identifies the area outside of the dwelling units as common space, making the property
legally accessible to both homeowners. Each lot is recorded as a phase of the condominium project
and each lot contains two units that carries its own homeowner's association. Many homeowners in
the Heatherglen neighborhood are not aware of the circumstances tied to their property until they
try to sell or refinance the property.

5.1.2 Dueto these circumstances, Taylorsville City rezoned the Heatherglen neighborhood from A-1 to R-2-
10 (residential two units per 10,000 square feet), to restructure the land use so that potential buyers
or sellers could get financing. Additionally, the zone change allows homeowners to record a new
subdivision plat that replaces the original 1979 condominium plat.

5:2 FINDINGS:

5.2.1 The two properties under question are located at 4475 S. Heatherglen Circle (Unit A, Lot 7) and 4497
S. Heatherglen Circle (Unit B, Lot 7).

5.2.2. Thecurrent zoning is R-2-10 (Residential two units per 10,000 square feet).

5.2.3. This application is to record a simple subdivision plat for 4475 S. Heatherglen circle and 4497 S.
Heatherglen Circle.

5.2.4 All required documentation has been submitted by the applicants.

5.2.5. The proposed subdivision is in compliance with the Taylorsville Municipal Code, Chapter 13.20 and
13.30.

5.2.6 No physical changes to either property are proposed at this time.

5.2.7. No adverse impact is expected on any adjacent properties.

5.3 CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL:

5.3.2. ATitle Report that is less than 90 days old at time of recording is submitted.

5.3.3. The Title Report matches the property owner and the signature on the amended plat is consistent
with the Title Report.

5.3.4 The final plat is compliant with all applicable codes and ordinances.

5.3.5. The applicant will comply with the requirements of all reviewing agencies.

5.3.6 All applicable fees are paid prior to recording of the plat.
