PC 22-056 Mountainside Estates Amendment (Public Comment Closed) -Continued from the
January 26" meeting. [Subdivision Permit] Brent Lawyer/Capstone Consulting LLC & Shree Giriraj
Ji Inc, authorized agent/owner are requesting a Subdivision Permit to modify their existing
subdivision plat by adding 4 building lots. The subdivision is located at approximately 1200 East
1400 North in the Suburban Neighborhood Residential (NR-4) zone; TIN 05-011-0006 (Hillcrest
Neighborhood).


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the proposed Mountainside Estates Amendment to split the eight
southern lots into twelve (six upper lots and six lower lots), gaining 4 new building lots. This will be
the third review of this proposal, and three main issues to be discussed.

The first concern is the gap property that sits between the south property line and 1300 north where
the driveway access to the upper lots is being proposed. It is critical to determine who owns the gap
property to determine whether it can be part of the subdivision.

The second concern is the slope on the south end of the subdivision that has been cut into causing
the slope to be compromised, exceeding the allowed 2 to 1 slope and violating the original
subdivision plat. R. Holley reviewed the history of the gravel pit and how the slopes and cuts have
changed over the years.

The third concern is with the geotechnical report and how the construction of the upper homes and
retaining walls should be sequenced to ensure the stability of the upper lots and the safety of the
lower lots. The commission will need to determine if the upper homes and retaining walls need to
be built before the lower homes, and develop the project in order from east to west or west to east,
or if the lower three retaining walls should be built by the developer before any of the upper lots are
sold allowing the upper homes to be built in any order.

R. Holley also reviewed the condition added by the Chief Building Official requiring a third-party peer
review of each house plan for the upper lots before a building permit can be obtained.

Commissioner Guth asked about the HOA requirement. R. Holley said that when there is common
infrastructure in a subdivision an HOA is required. All of the retaining walls are considered common
infrastructure. The debate at the last meeting is if the HOA should be centered around the amended
12-lot subdivision or the entire 64-lot subdivision.

Darren Farar, Logan City Engineer spoke about an engineering conference he attended recently
where he spoke to an Engineer from Sandy Utah, and asked him how they handle development on
steep slope conditions. He responded that they no longer allow development on slopes greater than
30 percent. OD. Farar said that the applicant has agreed to all of the engineering conditions presented.
He spoke about going above and beyond by adding additional conditions to this project that would
not usually be added, such as requiring a geotechnical report for each building lot, requiring
engineering to be involved in the review of the retaining wall system and the grading of the iots to
make sure that the stormwater is managed appropriately. All of the additional conditions have been
added so that engineering can have more oversite and catch any issues that arise right when they
start. Having the HOA responsible for the retaining walls and requiring an inspection every two years
at first then moving to five years will provide a reasonable level of assurance that the retaining walls
will be maintained appropriately. The applicant has provided enough reasonable data and studies
to satisfy all of the engineering requirements.

D. Farar said he has spoke to the developer and contractor regarding the existing slope being cut
and currently exceeding the allowable 2 to 1 slope. He was told that they have been grading the lot
back to the south following the original subdivision plan, they just have not yet gone to the top of the
lot to bring the cliff face down. D. Farar said he has requested that they do that immediately as it is
an unsafe condition as it sits.

Commissioner Doutre asked R. Holley to cover the specific conditions related to the concerns. The
survey condition has not been added yet.


Commissioner Guth asked if the city has any oversite or any way to regulate the HOA. Mohammed
Abdullahi, Assistant City Attorney said that the HOA will be formed and registered but there is no
way to ensure that the HOA will create a board and function.

Commissioner Lewis asked about bringing in the fill for the upper lots and creating the retaining walls
that connect between the homes and if any erosion would happen before all of the homes and
retaining walls were built. D. Farar said that there would have to be temporary measures taken after
each wall until the connecting wall gets installed and so on until the entire row is complete. D. Farar
said that he is more concerned with the lower walls because they are the walls supporting the upper
lots and protecting the lower lots. He believes that the walls should be built from east to west or west
to east to keep consistency with the wall.

Commissioner Doutre asked if an upper lot was not developed would the slope be safe with just the
lower retaining walls in place. D. Farar said that a 2 to 1 slope that is properly vegetated is safe from
erosion and that is what will be required on the piat.

Commissioner Lucero asked for clarification on which retaining walls are to be built in consecutive
order and how the upper lots will be accessed if it is required that the three lower walls are built
before the upper lots can be developed.

R. Holley spoke about condition number three requiring the three lower walls to be installed in order
from east to west or west to east before any of the upper lots are developed. D. Farar said that this
would create a more uniform structure of the lower retaining walls but it would create a more difficult
construction experience for those upper lots. He believes that a progressive construction of the walls
as each lot gets developed would work as well. R. Holley said that the reasoning behind condition
number three was to protect any of the homes built on the lower lots. Doutre said that makes sense
because it is not the responsibility of the lower lots to allow access to the upper lots if they can get
access from the top of the upper lots. D. Farar brought up not allowing the development of the lower
lots until the upper lots are developed. M. DeSimone said that the developer should decide how
they access their property for construction as it is not the City’s role to dictate or manage their
construction project. The lower retaining walls are required for long-term protection of the lower lots
in case any of the upper lots do not get developed.

PROPONENT: Brent Lawyer spoke about the existing retention pond in the neighboring subdivision
to the southeast corner that drains right onto the slope of this property where it is mainly gravel. Mr.
Lawyer also spoke about several existing homes in Bountiful that are built on similar slopes that have
been there for decades. He said they have done everything they can to address staffs concerns
with this project. He also stated that they just sent an email to D. Farar stating that they will address
the issue with the cliff.

Commissioner Guth asked about the longevity of an HOA. B. Lawyer said that a larger HOA that
has liabilities, insurance, and common areas to maintain is more likely to stay in place. He spoke
about turning the HOA over to a professional HOA management company once most of the lots are
sold.

PUBLIC: The public hearing section for public comments was closed at the previous meeting so no
public testimony was taken.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Guth asked about the gap property and why the commission may
want to require that to be resolved before approval. Commissioner Doutre said that engineering
condition C1 requires the gap to be addressed before the subdivision can be signed off on.


Commissioner Heare said that steps have been put in place to address all of the concerns. There
is not much more that can be done by the developer or the city engineer and at some point, the plan
should be trusted.

Commissioner Lucero said she recognizes the great lengths that have been gone to make this
development work. Looking at the critical land approval code, there is no guarantee that inspections
and maintenance will take place to mitigate any future risk.

M. DeSimone quoted Land Development Code 17.29.250.

Common Area Development Requirements.

C. Prior to the issuance of any permits, the developer shall file a lien in favor of the homeowner
association against each residential lot equal to the pro rata share of ten percent of the total cost of
the common area and facility improvements. Upon payment of the liens, the homeowner association
shall place the proceeds in a restricted fund to be used solely for the maintenance, repair and
replacement of the common area and facility improvements.

The planning commission can require this before any building permits are obtained. The city would
require a bond or a letter of credit.

Commissioner Guth asked if the requirement for regular inspections of the retaining walls by the
HOA is legally binding. M. Abdullahi said that HOA is required to be set up and record the CC&Rs.
M. DeSimone said that a note will have to be put on the final plat so the homeowners are aware of
their responsibility to maintain the retaining walls and this is covered in engineering condition number
seven. M. Abdullahi said that having the twelve lots on their own HOA, those twelve homeowners
have a vested interest in the retaining walls not failing and the HOA is more likely to function,
especially if they have to set aside money.

Commissioner Guth said that it is the commission's role to determine if this is safe and viable long
term. If all conditions are met is the level of risk acceptable for the homeowners on these lots
compared to homes that are not on sloped lots. D. Farar said that there is always more of a risk with
building on a slope. All of the required conditions mitigate the risk to a reasonable level.

Commissioner Croshaw asked about the gap area. D. Farar said that he doesn’t see that being a
roadblock. The only usable need for the gap property would be additional right of way to put in a
sidewalk, parkstrip, and landscaping.

M. DeSimone said that condition number 7k needs to be removed if condition number 3 remains.
Commissioner Guth asked D. Farar if it is important to build the upper homes before the lower homes,
or if he is ok with striking condition 7k. D. Farar said that there are two options here, you can build
the entire lower retaining wall system first before any development or you can dictate how the lower
retaining walls are built and limit the construction of the homes below until after each lower retaining
wall is installed above. D. Farar said he is ok with deleting condition 7k.

Commissioner Peterson said that the commission needs to trust that the engineering has provided
enough reasonable requirements.

MOTION: Commissioner Doutre moved to Conditionally Approve a Subdivision Permit for PC 22-
053 with the amended conditions of approval. Commissioner Peterson seconded the motion. The
motion for approval did not gain a majority of PC members therefore the motion was denied,
defaulting to the denial of PC 22-056 for a Subdivision Permit.

The commission reviewed the findings for denial under the following Land Development Codes:


17.24.020.D, appropriate mitigation is not available.
17.24.070.C, Long-term assurances of stability and safety are not available.
17.40.060.A, Each lot is physically unsuitable for development.

17.49.010, The Homeowners Association's long-term infrastructure maintenance and inspection for
the overall durability of the walls are unattainable and without guarantee.

FINDINGS FOR DENIAL

1. As per LDC 17.24.020.D, the Planning Commission determines this proposal lacks HOA viability,
functionality, and longevity, will have a detrimental effect on the land, and is inappropriate.

2. The Planning Commission determines the proposal is unreasonably safe for human habitation
and a violation of LDC 17.24.070.C. The construction of these proposed lots creates an unsafe
condition for the adjacent lots to the north without any reasonable long-term assurances.

3. As per LDC 17.40.060.A, the Planning Commission determines the proposed building lots are
physically unsuitable for development due to access constraints, the unknown ownership of the
20-foot gap along the south frontage, construction sequencing, and geotechnical challenges.

4. As per LDC 17.49.010, the Planning Commission determines compliance with conditions, and in
particular, Homeowner's Association long-term infrastructure maintenance and inspection for the
overall durability of the walls, is unattainable and without guarantee.

5. The Planning Commission determines that the health, safety, and welfare of neighboring
properties, residents and nearby City infrastructure would be jeopardized if this application was
approved as proposed.

Moved: Doutre Seconded: Peterson Motion for Approval failed: 3-4
Yea: Peterson, Heare, Doutre Nay: Jordy, Croshaw, Lucero, Lewis Abstain:

PC 22-063 Old Ephraim Apartments 2 -Continued from the January 26‘ meeting. [Design
Review Permit] Nathan Whittaker/GBN Enterprises LLC, authorized agent/owner are requesting a
Design Review Permit to construct two new 5,456 square foot, 4-level, 11-unit townhomes on three
parcels totaling 1.29 acres, each unit will include 6 private bedrooms with bathrooms for a total of
132 bedrooms. There will also be a three-level parking structure built behind the townhomes. This
project is located at approximately 655 East 400 North in the Campus Residential (CR) zone; TIN
06-058-0007; -0006; -0005 (Adams Neighborhood).

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a design review permit that includes the demolition of
one existing residential structure and the construction of a new 10-unit student housing structure
along 400 North on a combined 1.29-acre site. The site will also contain the Campus Academy
Youth Treatment Center in the northeast corner, two existing student housing buildings in the
northwest corner, and a 72 stall surface parking area in the center of the site. The new 4-level, 11-
unit townhome will have six bedrooms in each unit. The Campus Residential zone allows for six
students per apartment so the maximum occupancy for this proposal is one student per bedroom.
The front setback is required to be 25 feet due to the single-family zone across 400 north and the
applicant is proposing a 23.5-foot setback in the front that will require approval of a 10 percent
variance from the planning commission. Frontage, lot coverage, design, height, layout, and
pedestrian circulation are all in compliance with the Land Development Code requirements. The
Land Development Code requires one parking stall for every student in this zone. Parking reductions
are allowed through alternate parking plans but cannot go below a 25 percent reduction. The
applicant did not submit an alternate parking plan. The existing northwest buildings contain a total


of 24 occupants. The Campus Academy project was approved with 25 parking stalls. Considering
all buildings and conditional uses on the 1.29-acre site, the total parking requirement would be 109
parking stalls. The proposal shows 86 total parking stalls, 79 percent of the required parking, or 21
percent below the parking requirement. A new alternate parking plan for the entire site could be
submitted to supersede all parking. The proposed site plan shows approximately 16,864 square feet
of outdoor and open space that meets the requirement of the Land Development Code but is being
shown overlapping the required parking areas and dumpster approach areas for Campus Academy.

PROPONENT: Nathan Whittaker said that there are areas of the site where they can add more
parking stalls to meet the requirement if the commission will allow a parking reduction due to the site
being so close to the USU campus and the Campus Academy patients being too young to legally
drive.

PUBLIC: Jeannie Simmonds asked why the already approved 25 parking stalls for Campus
Academy would be allowed to be shared with the neighboring development. She also said that
students may not drive cars every day, many of them have cars that are parked at their apartments
so they can drive home on the weekends.

COMMISSION: Lucero said the project needs to be continued for the committee to review an
alternate parking plan and the open space deviations.

MOTION: Commissioner Lucero moved to continue PC 23-063 to the February 23rd meeting.
Commissioner Lewis seconded the motion.

PC_ 23-008 Land Development Code Amendment 17.09, 17.29, 17.30, 17.31 (Residential
Driveway & Parking Standards) -Continued from the January 12" meeting. [Code Amendment]
Mike DeSimone/Logan City are requesting a Code Amendment to the Land Development Code,
Chapter 17.07-General Development Standards; Residential Zones to remove residential driveway
requirements; amend chapter 17.29-Standards, Specifications, and Improvements to include
residential driveway requirements; amend Chapter 17.30-Supplemental Development Standards to
modify street connection requirements; and amend Chapter 17.31-Parking to update RV & Utility
Trailer Parking in Residential Neighborhoods.

PROPONENTI/STAEF: Mike Desimone, Community Development Director reviewed the proposed
amendments to the following chapters in the Land Development Code:

17.09 General Development Standards: Neighborhood Residential Zones. Updated the graphic for
double frontage lots in section 020. Cleaned up the language in section 030-Residential Accessory
Structures and moved all residential driveway and parking regulations to chapter 17.29.

17.29.060 Driveway Specifications. Updated the residential driveway standards for single
driveways, second driveways, and circular driveways requiring a right-of-way permit through the
Public Works Department and a driveway permit through Community Development. New driveways
must access an existing street and lead to a legal parking area. The driveway width is 12 feet
minimum to 22 feet maximum and 24 feet at the curb cut. Added driveway setbacks from side and
rear property lines and a 30-foot setback for corner driveways. Driveways are required to be hard


surfaces, total hard surfaces in the front yard cannot exceed 50 percent. For a Secondary Driveway,
the total combined width of all driveways cannot exceed 36 feet or 40 percent of the property
frontage. Circular Driveways have a maximum width of 12 feet, the circular portion has to be setback
10 feet from the front property line, and the circular area has to be landscaped.

17.30.170 Connectivity Standards. Updated the minimum number of street connections by the size
of residential subdivisions to 3 connections for greater than 51 residential lots, 2 connections for 21-
50 residential Jots, and 1 connection for up to 20 residential lots.

Commissioner Doutre suggested that this section (17.30.170) be updated to clarify this standard is
for single-family residential subdivisions and not multi-family residential projects.

17.30.180 Infill Development Standards. Increased range from 1 lot to 2 lots or more having to meet
minimum standards in section 180.B and from 2-8 lots to just 3 or more lots having to meet minimum
standards in section 180.C.

17.30.190 Connectivity Standards. Updated Language.
17.30.200 Outdoor Storage. Added E: Prohibited Storage and Accessory Structures.

17.31.080 Residential Parking. Added D: Parking of recreational and utility vehicles in Neighborhood
Residential Districts.

Commissioner Lucero asked if there should be a 10-day limit for the temporary occupancy of a
recreational or utility vehicle while parked or stored on a residential lot in the Neighborhood
Residential. Mr. DeSimone said he would include that in the language being sent to the Council.

PUBLIC: Jeannie Simmonds asked about parking in a circular driveway when the Land Development
Code states you cannot park between the structure and the street. She has no problem with people
parking in the circular drive but what will stop an Accessory Dwelling Unit from being established and
counting the circle drive as parking.

M. DeSimone said that driveways have to lead to a legal parking area when a new home is built, or
a new use is established, and there has to be two legal parking stalls established that are outside of
the front setback. Residents can park in the driveway, Land Development Code 17.29.060.A.9 states
that parking on a residential property is not permitted in the front setback unless in a permitted
driveway.

Hayden Rottler voiced his support for the second driveway amendment because as the code is now,
he can build a shop in his side yard, but cannot legally access it with a second driveway.

MOTION: Commissioner Lucero made a motion to forward a recommendation of approval to the
Municipal Council on a Code Amendment for PC 23-008, with the changes as discussed.
Commissioner Guth seconded the motion.

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL

1. Utah State Law authorizes local Planning Commission to recommend ordinance changes to the
legislative body (Municipal Council).

2. The Code Amendments are done in conformance with the requirements of Title 17.51 of the
Logan Municipal Code.

3. The proposed Code Amendments are consistent with the Logan City General Plan.


4. The proposed Code Amendments to Chapters 17.09, 17.29, 17.30, and 17.31 clarify driveway,
