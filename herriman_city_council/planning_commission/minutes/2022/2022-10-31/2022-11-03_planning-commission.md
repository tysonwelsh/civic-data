PLANNING COMMISSION MINUTES
Thursday, November 3, 2022
Approved December 15, 2022
The following are the minutes of the Herriman Planning Commission meeting held on Thursday,
November 3, 2022, at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main
Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the City
Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Commission, media, and interested
citizens.
Presiding: Chair Chris Berbert
Commissioners Present: Andy Powell, Heather Garcia, Alternate Forest Sickles, Andrea Bradford,
Adam Jacobson, and Alternate Darryl Fenn
Commissioners Excused: Jackson Ferguson, and Brody Rypien,
Staff Present: Communications Specialist Mitch Davis, City Planner Michael Maloy, Planning Manager
Clint Spencer, City Attorney Todd Sheeran, Deputy Recorder Wendy Thorpe, Planner Il Sheldon Howa,
Engineer Josh Petersen and HPD Deputy Chief Cody Stromberg.
1. 6:00 PM - Work Meeting (Fort Herriman Conference Room)
Chair Chris Berbert called the meeting to order at 6:06 p.m.
1.1. Review of City Council Decisions
City Planner Michael Maloy informed the Commission there were no land use items on the latest City
Council meeting agenda; however, the Mayor directed staff to solicit applications for additional Planning
Commission members. Council also expressed a desire to consider making Alternate Commissioners
Sickles and Fenn regular Commissioners when Chair Berbert steps down at the end of the year.
1.2. Review of Agenda Items — Michael Maloy, City planner
Planner ll Sheldon Howa reported neighbors expressed concerns regarding road access and
construction traffic, with regards to item 4.3, the Ramirez Subdivision. The applicant for item 4.2 would
be present online.
1.5. Training
City Planner Maloy made Commissioners aware of a free Land Use workshop they may attend on
Monday, November 7, 2022 in Taylorsville. He asked them to register if they planned to attend.

4.1 Consideration of a request to approve a Special Exception regarding setback
requirements for the side yard at 5953 W Suffolk View Lane in the A-1-10
(Agricultural) zone

Applicant: Nicholas Liebhardt

Acres: +0.32

File Number: P2022-143
Planning Manager Spencer identified the location of the irregular lot shaped property and proposed
location of the structure. The applicant requested a special exception to locate an accessory structure
in what was considered the side yard. The proposed structure had a maximum height of 12’-4” and
was 288 square feet, both of which met City Code requirements. The applicant requested a reduction
in the side yard setback to place the structure three feet away from the property line, which was
adjacent to the trail. The uniquely shaped lot backed up to trails on two sides of the property.
Applicant Liebhardt said if the request were denied he would change the location of the structure.
Commissioners discussed the setbacks, irregular lot shape, trail locations, and applicable exceptions.

Applicant: Wolf Co. (owner)

File Number: S2022-127
Planner Howa presented the request for preliminary plat approval for five half acre lots in a
residential subdivision. Access to the proposed subdivision would be provided from J} Farms Lane,
which was to be constructed by the developers of the J] & J subdivision. Stormwater detention would be
provided via an off-site basin. All lots within the proposed preliminary subdivision plat complied with
City requirements.
Applicant Esther Rowland (online) stated the height of the six-foot-high fence on the south side of lot
5 would be reduced to 3 feet at the 25-foot setback line.

access road J & J] Farms Lane.

Commissioner Jacobson asked if a fee in lieu would be requested for anticipated road development,

Commissioner Jacobson expressed the roadway should extend to the neighboring property and include

a public right of way was the only County road requirement. Commissioner Fenn advised the proposed
road cut through the Fisher property, was in a very steep area and may not be buildable. Also, the
proposed pressurized sewer line might be a problem and access and utility issues needed to be
addressed prior to approval.
Applicant Jose Ramirez explained the road was not new and easement to the Fisher property has been
in place for decades.
Commissioners emphasized in addition to the easement an improved road should be required.
Chair Berbert opened the public hearing.
Sue Scallion remarked she lived in the last house on Majestic Oaks Lane. A proposed road was along
her fence line. Majestic View Lane was a dirt/gravel road, and she thought it was dedicated to
Herriman. She stated the gravel road ended at her house and maintenance was provided by the
neighbors, not by the County or City. She voiced concern about construction equipment as the trucks
tore up her property when The Cove was built. The primitive road has flooded. She advised Majestic
Oaks lane should be a dead-end road until it has been improved.
Brent Wilson remarked he lived at the end of Majestic Oaks Lane. He affirmed the proposed hill was
too steep to be a road. On snowy days emergency vehicles and garbage trucks could not access the
properties. The guard rail was previously broken due to speeding cars. He thought fixing the grade
would cut his access. He advised access to the new lots should come from the Herriman side not from
Majestic Oaks. He expressed a gate should be added to Majestic View Lane or people would take the
shortcut and emphasized this was a dangerous spot in winter.
Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.
Applicant Ramirez’s brother stated Salt Lake County reported no improvements were planned for the
road.
Commissioner Jacobson recounted this project needed more evaluation. He questioned if the roads
met County standards on slope and grade, and access and utility adjustments may be necessary. He
concluded more evaluation was needed due to the many issues with utilities and access. Chair Berbert
affirmed fire, police, and medical emergency vehicles needed access to this area.
