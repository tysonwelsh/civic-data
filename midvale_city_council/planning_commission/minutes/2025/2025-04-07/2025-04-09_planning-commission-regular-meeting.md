REGULAR MEETING

Chair Anderson called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. She
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

1. Brad Reynolds requests Conditional Use Permit approval for a medium/high density
   residential development located at 350 W California Dr in the Transit Oriented
   Development (TOD) zone.

   This property was formerly a large Century Link/Qwest office building. Medium and high-
   density residential developments are an allowed use through a conditional use permit in the TOD
   zone. Midvale Municipal Code 17-7-8.11(A) outlines the criteria necessary for a conditional use
   permit as follows (Staff responses in bold):

   1. The application complies with all applicable provisions of this title, state and federal law;
   Response: Yes.

   2. The structures associated with the use are compatible with surrounding structures in terms of
   use, scale, mass and circulation;
   Response: Yes, see attached plans for details. The project consists of 3-story townhomes
   with masonry/fiber cement siding exterior. The townhomes are compatible with nearby
   commercial, apartment, and residential structures.

   3. The use is not detrimental to the public health, safety and welfare;

Response: Correct.

4. The use is consistent with the general plan, as amended;
Response: Yes.

5. Traffic conditions are not adversely affected by the proposed use including the existence of or
need for dedicated turn lanes, pedestrian access, and capacity of the existing streets;
Response: A traffic impact study was conducted by Hales Engineering in August 2024 to
evaluate the effect of the proposed development on local traffic conditions. The study found
that there is ample capacity with existing streets. No additional auxiliary or turn lanes were
recommended. All local streets are projected to maintain their current Level of Service
after the development is complete. The proposal and traffic study were reviewed by
Midvale City’s Engineering Department and complies with the above-listed criteria.

6. Sufficient utility capacity;
Response: Yes.

7. Sufficient emergency vehicle access;
Response: Yes.

8. Location and design of off-street parking as well as compliance with off-street parking
standards provided for in Section 17-7-8.8;
Response: Yes, see attached plans for detailed information. The project consists of 117
townhome units. All units are 3 bedrooms. The required resident parking is 1.75 stalls per
unit, for a total of 205 stalls. 234 parking spaces have been provided. The requirement for
guest parking is 1 stall for every 4 units, totaling 30 stalls. 30 guest parking stalls have been
provided.

9. Fencing, screening, and landscaping to separate the use from adjoining uses and mitigate the
potential for conflict in uses;
Response: A 6 ft privacy fence has been provided on the north, west, and east property
lines of the project. There is a large open space buffer along the east property line to help
create separation from the existing single-family homes. There are also tree plantings on
the project perimeter.

10. Compatibility of the proposed mass, bulk, design, orientation, and location of the structures
on the site, including compatibility with buildings on adjoining lots and to the street;
Response: The application complies with the above-listed criteria and mirrors the mass and
bulk of the development bordering its western property line.

11. Exterior lighting that complies with the lighting standards of the zone and is designed to
minimize conflict and light trespass with surrounding uses; and
Response: The application complies with the above-listed criteria.

12. Within and adjoining the site, impacts on the aquifer, slope retention, flood potential and
appropriateness of the proposed structure to the topography of the site.
