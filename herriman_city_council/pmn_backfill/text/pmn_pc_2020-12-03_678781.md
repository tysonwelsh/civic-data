delivered to members of the Commission and media.

City Planner Maloy had nothing to report as the City Council had not met recently. They will discuss the
Valhalla rezone and the Joe Darger rezone at their next meeting.


City Planner Maloy explained the Creek Ridge MDA for the transfer of lots had not been finalized yet. The
Commissioners expressed concern with the MDA outstanding, while they were still legally included on the
other plat. City Attorney Andrizzi informed the Commission the Mountain Ridge MDA accounted for the
units when it was approved, and the amendment with Creek Ridge was still under review. They do have vested
rights, but Edge has withdrawn the units from Area B in Creek Ridge, in good faith. The units to come from
other areas were still being determined. Commissioners asked what could be done to hold them to their
commitment. City Attorney informed the Commission he would be more comfortable discussing legal
opinions in closed session. Community Development Director Blake Thomas stated Edge Homes committed
to remove the units and honor the agreement, but they are not owners of all the property yet. They have
committed to honor the agreement and remove 120 units to from Area B, and remove 150 units from Area A.


City Planner Michael Maloy presented creative street designs solutions training. He requested feedback and
ideas and from the Commissioners. They stressed the importance of separation between private and public
spaces and walkable, landscaped streetscapes. Backload alley load homes were discussed. Commissioners
requested more guidance for what types of creative improvements they could request from developers. It was
mentioned that multifamily home areas should have higher design standards. City Planner Maloy asked the
Commissioners to seek out ideas for desirable improvements.

Planning Commission work meeting adjourned by consensus at 6:56 p.m.

City Planner Maloy reviewed the location, site plan, and plat. The applicant requested conditional use approval
for commercial development in the Mountain Point Retail Center. The developer anticipated the need for
cross access and shared parking, as more was needed for Redemption Bar and Grill. Topography and grade
changes have been reviewed and approved by the engineering department. The building masonry and
elevations meet City standards. The condition of approval required a final review by the City Landscape
Architect prior to issuance of the permit. The landscaping plan utilized a water wise, local space approach.

Applicant Cade Cunningham approached the podium. He acknowledge concerns with shared parking spaces
and added the HOA was empowered to assign parking areas. He was comfortable sharing parking spaces with
102 and 103 and a cross access agreement with 101, 104 and 105. He did not know if there were enough
spaces to accommodate Redemption overflow parking. Each building contained three units, and one unit per
building had drive-thru access. Cross access may be included near lot 104. Talks were underway with Beans
and Brews to occupy one of the drive-thru units.

City Planner Maloy explained parking met the City standards. He was not aware the HOA allowed parking
restrictions and will confirm the shared parking allowances. Applicant Cunningham confirmed he had sent
CC&Rs to staff via email and identified the parking section.

Commissioner Jackson Ferguson just realized he had conflicts of interest with the inclusion of Beans and
Brews, and would abstain from voting for item 3.1.

Recommendations:
   1. Comply with all department and agency requirements for final approval as provided by the
      Development Review Committee (see Attachment F – Development Review Committee Comments).

City Planner Maloy stated the applicant requested preliminary plat and site plan approval for 416 units,
including 325 townhomes and 62 single-family lots, for a project known as Mountain Ridge Phases 3-4 and 9-
16. The applicant had new renderings to present.

Applicant Brandon Watson presented the updated trails plan with improved connectivity. They added a ten
foot wide asphalt trail along Watchmen Way, which connected to the nearby park and trail system along the
canal. He expressed appreciation to the Commissioners for making the recommendation. The connection of
the Sentinel concrete trail was displayed with a photo of a similar slip lane from Saratoga Springs.
Commissioner Powell asked if pedestrian ramp access was provided and the applicant answered in the
affirmative. A painted crosswalk connected both sides of the community. Commissioner Palmer offered
concern with traffic short cuts at the intersection, and asked if different material in the crosswalk would reduce
it. Commissioner Ferguson offered the raised sidewalk and the gradual slope worked well, as long as drainage
was addressed. Applicant Watson offered not many homes were accessed at the intersection. If materials were
changed stamped concrete would be preferred. Commissioners debated the potential for shortcut traffic and
ideal crosswalk materials.

Applicant Brandon Watson displayed the ten foot asphalt trail from Sentinel Ridge to the Park. The homes
were alley load attached houses with 21 feet driveways and two car garages. His study showed minimal street
parking. Commissioners discussed ideal sidewalk size. City Planner Maloy stated the MDA required six foot
sidewalks, but the plans in the packet stated five feet wide. The sidewalk width and landscape tree plan would
be refined to agree with the MDA between preliminary and final plan approval.

The vehicular plan combined public and private roadways. The Master HOA would maintain the slip lanes,
islands, and park strips in front of the elementary school. A sub-association HOA covered the townhomes.
