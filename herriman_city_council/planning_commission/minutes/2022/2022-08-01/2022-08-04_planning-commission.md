1.4. Discussion of potential amendments to the Home Occupation Ordinance, which include

allowing some home occupations in accessory structures — Planner Talia Wright

Planner ll Wright asked the Commission for feedback regarding allowing home occupation businesses
to operate in accessory structures. They were allowed in nearby cities Lehi, Orem, Saratoga Springs,
and Riverton, but not allowed in West Valley City or Murray. Commissioners debated possible uses and
utility requirements for habitability. They discussed reasons for prohibiting specific uses and
differences between Accessory use structures and Accessory Dwelling uses. City Attorney Sheeran
reported the requirements used by Bluffdale were based on impact related to number of clients and/or
employees. Complicated uses required more detailed analysis by the city, and uses allowed in the home
were allowed in an accessory structure. Commissioner Garcia was hesitant due to additional employees
and allowed children at home daycares. She worried about setback and proximity to neighbors. Some
may be acceptable, others not. She asked if multiple businesses were allowed at a single-family home.
Per City Planner Maloy the City had no restriction, but standards had to be met. Staff agreed to prepare
amore conservative proposal for presentation in another work meeting. Planner Il Wright would provide
more detail with types, and comparisons to other cities.

1.5. Discussion of potential amendments to the City Ordinance including changes to the fencing

requirements around pools — Clint Spencer, Planning Manager
Planner | Wright reported staff have received complaints and safety concerns regarding fencing around
private pools. Current City Code states: Swimming pools of permanent construction not enclosed within
a building shall be set back at least five feet (S') from all property lines and shall be completely
surrounded by a fence or wall having a lockable self-closing gate and a height of at least six feet (6')
which conforms to Building Code requirements. lf the applicable Building Code has a different
requirement, such requirement shall prevail over this section.
The 2015 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code (ISPSC) states: The provisions of this section shall
apply to the design of barriers for pools and spas. These design controls are intended to provide
protection against the potential drowning and near drowning by restricting access to such pools or spas.
These requirements provide an integrated level of protection against potential drowning through the
use of physical barriers and warning devices.
Because the Building Code only required one form of barrier to the pool, the City cannot require
an applicant to have a six-foot fence if they have a pool cover. Planner | Wright requested Commission
feedback and asked if they were interested in an ordinance requiring both a fence and a pool cover.
Commissioners debated requirements and enforcement options but provided no specific direction. They
asked what other cities required. City Planner Maloy stated he would conduct further research and
discuss the topic with staff and bring it back for further discussion.
2. Adjournment

4.5 Conditional use consideration of roof signs and illuminated wall signs adjacent to
residential dwellings for Lone Peak Hospital Emergency Center located at 15306 S
Forth Herriman Parkway in the MU-2 (Mixed Use) zone
Applicant: Kevin Anderson/R&O Construction (agent)
Acres: +3.37
File Number: C2022-100
Planner ll Howa reviewed the sign request for the 24-hour medical service facility. The sign plan
consisted of two wall signs, two monument signs, two roof signs, and four directional signs. The site
location is within the Herriman Towne Center Master Development Agreement (MDA), and all signage
was regulated under the Design Guidelines of the MDA. The sign plan was reviewed and approved by
the development’s Design Review Committee (DRC). Under the approval of the DRC, an exception was
granted for the roof signs, which were otherwise prohibited under the MDA. The exception was granted
through an entitlement of the MDA, which allowed the DRC to grant variances of the town center’s
design guidelines.
The applicant requested conditional use approval for two internally lit roof signs and one internally lit
wall sign. The proposed roof signs were composed of channel letters and run horizontally across the
east and west building canopies (facing to the north). Each roof sign is approximately twenty-four feet
long and two feet tall, and seventeen feet above grade. The wall sign was located on the building’s north
elevation, constructed of channel letters, and approximately thirteen feet above grade. The proposed
wall sign is 11.5 feet wide and 4.6 feet tall. All signage is adjacent to north-lying residential dwellings.
Sign distances from the rear elevation of the adjacent residential dwellings ranged from 195 to 240 feet.
The applicant proposed the illumination would be no greater than 1.7 candle foot at the property line
and be diffused.
