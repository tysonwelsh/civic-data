1. Discussion of proposed amendments to Canyon Corners Development Agreement
 – 
Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Commissioner Klingenstein disclosed that he does not believe he has a conflict of interest, but 
he was previously an employee of Psomas Engineering, which has done much of the engineering 
on this project, he and Ryan Hales have had a professional relationship for a long time, and Mr. 
Hales worked for him on a few projects.  Ms. Brackin confirmed with Commissioner 
Klingenstein that he believes he can be fair and impartial in making a decision on this 
application. 
 
Principal Planner Ray Milliner presented the staff report and recalled that the Canyon Corners 
development agreement was approved in 2004.  Since then, the development agreement has been 
extended twice, and it is scheduled to expire in December 2017.  The development agreement 
approval was for 61,000 square feet of retail commercial development on an 8.5-acre parcel 
located on Landmark Drive.  The applicant is requesting to modify that approval to reduce the 
number of approved buildings from seven to three, with the largest building being a 45,000-
square-foot grocery/retail building.  In addition to the changes in the buildings, the number of 
parking spaces would be increased, and the amount of open space would be reduced from 2.94 
acres to 2.71 acres.  A proposed access would come from the existing car wash and cut across the 
back of the property to the grocery store.  The applicant would also like to change the sign, 
landscape, and lighting plans, but they have not been thoroughly reviewed yet, because Staff first 
wanted to get input from the Planning Commission on the change to the buildings and size of the

overpass with the outlet mall, crosswalks on Landmark Drive, a bus shelter and turnaround, a 
deceleration lane for the bus shelter, maintenance and construction of the sidewalk along 
Landmark Drive, and a $150,000 contribution to the County for transportation improvements.  
Planner Milliner requested input from the Planning Commission regarding the questions raised 
by Staff.  The primary question is whether the proposed changes to the development agreement 
are appropriate and, if not, whether there are changes the applicant could make that would be 
considered appropriate.  If the changes to the development agreement are appropriate, Planner 
Milliner asked if there are changes the Commission would like to make to the benefit package 
approved 11 years ago to update it. 
 
Planner Milliner explained that most of the issues raised by Staff are related to the transportation 
component of the site.  Issues addressed by Staff are on-site circulation, the left turn onto 
Landmark Drive with the hill, traffic impacts and potential increase in congestion on Landmark 
Drive, secondary access from the car wash, safety concerns about deceleration of cars turning 
right off of Highway 224 into the car wash access, and siting and placement of the transit stop. 
 
Commissioner Lawson referred to UDOT wanting assurances that the driveway access from 
Highway 224 would do no harm to prior approvals and asked what prior approvals and what 
types of assurances they are looking for.  Planner Milliner stated that he believes UDOT wants to 
be sure the use of that driveway would not constitute a danger to the public or people coming off 
the off-ramp.  They would like the applicant to demonstrate that use of that driveway would not 
have a negative impact on traffic flow and safety.  He stated that there were not any previous 
approvals to talk about. 
 
Jean Paul Wardy, President of CenterCal Properties, representing the applicant, stated that some 
things they heard from Staff had to do with the Kimball Junction area evolving into a vibrant 
mixed-use corridor with alternative modes of transportation and being less dependent on cars, 
and one thing they tried to do was look at how to address transportation.  He noted that 
modifications to the development agreement should enhance public benefits that exist in the

Ryan Hales with Hales Engineering described the traffic study and the additional study done 
during skiing outload conditions.  He identified the intersections on a map.  He stated that he 
compared the impacts of what was proposed in the development agreement with the land uses 
currently proposed.  The land uses in the existing development agreement would generate about 
588 peak hour trips, and the proposed land use would generate 466 peak hour trips.  He indicated 
the two options for accessing the site, with the primary access being from Landmark Drive.  
There are two alternatives for the secondary access, one from the car wash site, past the hotel, 
and into the south side of the project, and the other coming straight off of Landmark Drive and 
going to the back side of the stores.  He explained that, more than just doing traffic counts at the

site from SR 224 to Landmark Drive.  He reviewed the number of vehicles that used the SR 224 
car wash access during the peak hour time period and the number that exited using the SR 224 
car wash exit, indicating where the traffic using that access was going and coming from.  He 
provided a diagram of how traffic arrives at the site and the improvements proposed with this 
development.  He stated that they want both a right- and left-turn egress from the site rather than 
a single lane to service both.  They also recognize that they need dual southbound left-turn lanes

plan was approved with the development agreement, and this proposal would request that the 
signs be slightly large to be in scale with the size of the buildings.  He reviewed the lighting plan

Commissioner Peck recalled that the previous plan called for morning traffic for a breakfast 
place and evening traffic for a restaurant, and she did not see how a grocery store would reduce 
traffic during the peak hour.  Mr. Hales replied that their analysis, which showed a 122-trip 
reduction for the proposed project, was a p.m. peak hour comparison between the previous 
development agreement land uses and the proposed uses.  Mr. Wardy explained that the grocery 
store trips would be more evenly distributed throughout the day.  Commissioner Peck asked if 
that would be true of a Whole Foods market with outdoor seating and dinner and all that the store 
offers.  Mr. Hales offered to look at a trip comparison and return with an answer.  
Commissioner Peck asked why they need 7,000 square feet more than the density in the 
development agreement.  Mr. Wardy explained that 5,000 square feet is for the mechanical room 
in the grocery store, and the square footage evolved a little bit because of the site layout.  
Because of the 5,000 square feet in the service mezzanine, there is really only 3,000 square feet 
difference.  Commissioner Peck explained that the County discourages additional density and is 
trying to use what they have now, so she was reluctant to increase the square footage.  She did 
not understand why they cannot fit the proposed project into the square footage allowed in the 
development agreement.  Mr. Wardy explained that, when they ran the trip analysis, the number 
of trips during the p.m. peak was less, so they felt that as long as the number of trips generated 
was less, they would not cause more impact.  Commissioner Peck explained that this is a 
philosophy the Planning Commission has expressed, and she did not want to start chipping away 
at it without some good reasons for doing so.  Planner Milliner agreed that it would be best to 
work with the applicant to get the development to the allowed square footage. 
 
Commissioner Harte was interested in knowing how many more people would come to the site 
in a 12-hour period as opposed to the peak hour traffic to get an idea of the traffic volume this 
would generate throughout the day.  Mr. Hales stated that they would generate about 5,074 trips 
on a daily basis, but from a traffic engineering basis, they always look at the worst time of day, 
because they are trying to solve the issues during the most congested time periods.  He explained 
that the morning peak hour for this site will generate about 160 trips, which he believed was 
similar to the land use approved in the development agreement.  Commissioner Harte asked if 
lunchtime might be the peak hour and if that might shift with this use.  Mr. Hales explained that,

Commissioner Harte asked which would be the preferred second access.  Mr. Wardy replied 
that the preferred access would be the one that comes in from SR 224.  UDOT has confirmed 
that will require a variance, and in order to grant a variance, UDOT would like to see that safety 
and operational issues will be better if the variance is granted than they were before.  Their 
studies show that by adding a deceleration lane, they could make the situation better than it is 
today.  In their last meeting with UDOT, UDOT has asked them to find some more definitive 
data showing where their customers will come from and how many potential customers would 
live north of I-80.  Commissioner Harte asked how much the other alternative would affect this 
area if UDOT says no to the SR 224 access.  Mr. Wardy replied that they think most of the traffic 
will come from the main entrance off of Landmark Drive, and less traffic will come in from the 
secondary access.  Commissioner Harte asked what that assumption is based on.  He believed 
that option would generate a completely different set of numbers than they are currently looking 
at, because the options of cutting through will be different.  He asked why there is a reduction in 
open space.  Mr. Wardy explained that there is currently a lot of grade on the site, and they 
would have to regrade the site to make it flatter.  Commissioner Harte asked what the benefits 
will be to the community in general.  Mr. Wardy stated that this will be much more pedestrian in 
scaled with landscaping, sidewalks, colored concrete, and fire pits.  This site will have more 
amenities for people walking the trail system, and there are more areas for people to enjoy.  
Commissioner Harte asked about the potential uses for the $150,000 transit contribution.  
Deputy County Attorney Jami Brackin explained that traffic was a big concern when this 
development agreement was approved, and it was before the County had a traffic impact fee, 
before they did improvements to Landmark Drive, and before the roundabout at the outlet mall.  
The County knew it would make improvements to Landmark Drive, and this was a way to get a 
contribution for those improvements.  She explained that this contribution would go toward 
anything that would improve the circulation on Landmark Drive.
