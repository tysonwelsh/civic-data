Operations and Capital Improvement Projects (CIP) Budget Presentation Presenter: Brandon

Nelson, CPA, Taggart Bowen, City Engineer, Carlo Okolowitz, IT Manager, and Thayne Carter,
Fleet Manager

Mr. Nelson introduced Mr. Bowen from the engineering division who will speak about
Capital Investment Projects (CIP). Mr. Nelson explained the expansion request process. It begins
with a request from departments, the request goes to a review by the budget subcommittee and
department staff, followed by a department meeting and coordination, and finally it is added to
the tentative budget.

Mr. Nelson presented the personnel expansions for the FY25-26 year. For Fire there is a
request for 3 firefighters/paramedic (funded by Vineyard), a training facility division chief and a
deputy fire marshall (funded by Vineyard and Lindon). For Parks there is a request for a GIS
technician and a public works technician for Heritage Park. The general fund expansions include
a Al report drafting software and patrol rifle replacement for a total of $112,000 for the police
department and $90,000 for a library admin area remodel.

Mr. Nelson explained the proposed expansion funded in FY25. The Public Works
department is requesting a John Deere Gator, a woodchipper, 5 stand-on leaf blowers and a

carpet replacement for the cemetery office. The Facilities Maintenance division is requesting
security equipment and software and lighting improvements for the fire stations. The other fund
expansions include a password manager and automated data classification policy enforcement
software for the IT department. The water fund expansions include a maintenance vehicle and a
John Deere gator.

Mr. Bowen presented on the 2025-2026 Capital Improvement Projects (CIP). He began
with a highlight of 2024-2025 projects. The CIP summary is $2,650,500 for the CIP fund,
$4,529,747 for the water fund, $13,189,060 for the water reclamation fund, $2,705,974 for the
storm water fund and $5,013,540 for streets. This is a total of $28,088,821 for the Capital/CIP
summary.

The CIP park improvements include $710,000 for Windsor Park. The improvement is a
new basketball court, 4 pickleball courts, new bathrooms and lighting for the courts. The
community park improvements will cost $350,000 for upgraded bathrooms and storage. The
Center Street and 800 North Parks landscaping projects will cost $125,000. The park's fiber and
security project will cost $50,000. This project is in year 3 of a 3 year project. The SCERA park
benches and trash cans will cost $35,500. The park's monument signs project will cost $55,000.
The lower cemetery irrigation project will cost $160,000. The Cherryhill and Foothill park
playgrounds will cost $565,000. The City Center Park Ballfield screening project will cost
$35,000. This will screen the ballfield infield dirt for rocks. The Memorial Trailhead project will
cost $500,000. This is located at the cemetery. The Lakeside Park Soccer Fencing will cost
$15,000. The Parks sidewalk leveling project will cost $50,000. The total park improvements
will be $2,650,500.

The Building Maintenance CIP projects include $310,000 for fitness center pools
improvements, $145,000 for Public Safety Building Roof, $230,000 for the Public Safety
building boiler and chiller, and $200,000 for Public Safety parking lot enclosure (year | of 3).
For a total of $885,000.

The Water CIP projects include the Master Plan Projects for $4,479,747 and Center Street
and 800 North Landscaping for $50,000. This is a total of $4,529,747. The Water Reclamation
CIP projects include the maintenance projects for $500,000, Waste Water Treatment Plant
(WWTP) maintenance building bathroom remodel for $26,783, paint for the WWTP doors for
$14,535 and $12,647,742 for Master Plan Projects. This is a total of $13,189,060. The Storm
Water CIP projects include the Master Plan Stormwater Projects for $2,705,974.

The Streets CIP projects include Asphalt Overlay and Reconstruction, Slurry Seal, Crack
Seal, Microsurface, Trip Hazard Mitigation, Misc Street Construction, and Concrete and ADA
ramp upgrades for a total of $5,013,540. These are annual projects to keep roads in good
condition. The total cost for all Capital/CIP projects is $28,088,821.

1600 North Noise Wall Presentation Presenter: Diego Carroll, UDOT Consultant and Eric
Rasband, UDOT

City Council Minutes — April 22, 2025, (pg.2) A complete video of the meeting can be found at www.orem.org/meeting

Mr. Carroll is the consultant hired by UDOT to work on the 1600 North expansion
project. He introduced Mr. Rasband with UDOT. Mr. Carroll is giving an update on the project.
He explained that the project is ready to be advertised. They are finalizing the sale of homes and
then the demolition will begin. Today they will be discussing the noise wall installation. Mr.
Rasband explained that each project needs an environmental study which determines if a sound
wall is needed. The houses that remain are contacted concerning the noise wall. The response to
the wall was positive. Orem City has decided not to put a noise wall in front of Bonneville Park.
Mr. Carroll explained that a sound wall is typically made of concrete and would be taller than a
normal fence. This project would be 9 feet walls. This project is going to bid in the next couple
of months. The expansion goes to the east of State Street but will not require any businesses to
close.

Authorizing the Sale of Said Properties to UDOT for Use in the Widening of 1600 North

and Repaying the Corridor Preservation Funds Received from Such Sale to Utah County
Presenter: Steve Earl, City Attorney

Mr. Earl reported that this item is to seek the City Council approval to surplus nine
properties along 1600 North and authorize the sale of these properties to UDOT. Eight of the
properties are residential properties that the City acquired using corridor preservation funds
provided to by Utah County. One property is Bonneville Park. The City will be selling a portion
of the park to UDOT at fair market value. UDOT is ready to purchase these properties, but
before they can do that they need the City Council approval. Mr. Earl added that the rent money
that was accumulated will be returned to the County after the City deducts their expenses.

PUBLIC HEARING BEGINS 5:15 PM

No Comments
PUBLIC HEARING CLOSES 5:15 PM
Mr. Spencer moved to approve, by resolution, declaring certain properties as surplus and
authorizing the sale of said properties to UDOT for use in the widening of 1600 North and
repaying the corridor preservation funds received from such sale to Utah County. Seconded by

Mr. Killpack. Those voting yes: David Young, LaNae Millett, David Spencer, Tom Macdonald,
Jeff Lambson, Jenn Gale, and Chris Killpack. The motion passed.
