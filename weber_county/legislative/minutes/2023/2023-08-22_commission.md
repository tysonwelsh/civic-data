F. PRESENTATIONS:
   1. Presentation of the 2023 Weber County Women in Business Awards:
            Commissioner Bolos: July was National Women in Business Month. We thought it would be a great
            opportunity to recognize women within Weber County.

            Learning is Power Award: Janie Avery is Weber County's first dedicated County Records Officer. This is a
            brand new position, which means there was minimal in-house training available, and a lot of information to
            learn is a short time. Janie dove right in and achieved certification in both areas offered by the State Archives
            (they usually recommend alternating years between the two). She has also engaged with records officers in
            other counties to learn their best practices and adopt anything that will help Weber County improve. She has
            also delved into state and county code to truly understand the core legal foundation for her role and
            responsibilities. All of this demonstrates what an avid learner she is, and it has already paid huge dividends
            for Weber County.

            Rising Star Award: Kristi Clark: Kristi recently was promoted to the lead support person in our corrections
            visitation group. This group interacts with inmates, their families, their attorneys and keeps the financial
            records for inmates while in custody. This is a challenging job and she has stepped in and we didn't miss a
            beat. She is detail oriented, hardworking and is making great improvements in a very short time.

            Service Star Award: Shirley Robinson - Treasurer's Office - Shirley is a friend to all who enter the
            Treasurer's office. She listens patiently, and responds with kindness and understanding - always - even when
            the conversations are hard and the customers are unhappy or discouraged. She has regular customers (tax
            payers) who come in the office just to see her. She brightens the day for all who interact with her.

            Above & Beyond Award: Jacque Cole: She has completed the new condo/townhome permits in the last few
            years with numbers reaching over 1,000. She works with efficiency and has outstanding quality. She is
            driven and hard working. She completes her tasks with a smile and is always willing to help where she can.
            Even though her workload is one of the highest. She has multiple tasks and always completes them on time.
            She is the positive in the office that is always trying to find activities in the office to promote team building
            and good atmosphere. She is deserving of this award.
            Chair Froerer: Congratulations to these four great employees. Going through the applications, this could have
            gone to a number of people. It just goes to show what we have here in Weber County.
            Commissioner Harvey: Congratulations to you. You have earned this because how great you are. Thank you!

         Commissioner Bolos: I wanted to bring the attention to recent accreditation from the American Corrections
         Association, received on August 9th. The association was founded in 1870 and is the oldest association
         created for practioners in the correctional profession. I wanted to give accommodation to our Corrections
         team. This is not an easy accreditation to achieve.

         Sheriff Arbon: That was a heavy lift and Chief Reese did a fantastic job and so did Captain Ryan and their
         team.

         We started a journey on continuing improvements with the Correctional facility. We have been learning
         about having the proper medical and mental health care at our facilities for our inmates. Recently our team
         took a trip to Bergen County in New Jersey and learned about their reentry teams and what they do for their
         inmates. We asked what we can do more for our inmates when they are released. We created a presentation
         that we want to share with you and the public. A November 2021 Expansion Feasibility Study estimated cost
         of $62.5M for two new pods, a new work release building, and a medical/mental health wing. The revised

         costs in 2023 jumped substantially to $140M. Chief Reese led a team to look at ways to reduce this cost,
         starting with looking at data from the state and Weber County courts, we looked at our own data, we looked
         at what the state prison has done. How can we centralize and not spend so much money? One idea is to
         renovate a portion of the sheriff’s office to create a medical and mental health and then add some wings to
         that to accommodate a male and female section of medical/mental health. This would add 48 beds and relief
         the pressure of housing in the main facility where they are currently mixed in, requiring additional
         supervision.

         The previous medical/mental health cells will be converted to a video court. Some of the struggles we have
         now is transportation. Every time the inmates need to go to court or another facility we have to transport
         them. We are seeing a trend moving towards more and more video court substantially dropping costs of
         transportation. Currently, inmates may have to appear in other courts; they will be transported only to have
         the hearing delayed; there are times when deputies have to travel to other counties to pick up inmates and are
         required to spend the night. Video court allows the inmates to be seen in front of a judge without having to
         travel.

         Multiple solutions have been explored over several years for an administrative building. Parking availability
         and costs drove a decision to go vertical on a four story administrative building to be called the Weber
         County Justice Center. The lower two levels will be the work release center and the administrative offices
         will be on the fourth floor. We want to include allied agencies as a hub and support and to collaborate on
         offering something better for inmates being released to reduce recidivism. We want to reduce building costs
         through renovating in lieu of building new. This building will be more energy efficient, provide for future
         expansion, and will cost up to $150k less in maintenance. Also, no additional land will need to be purchased.
         No additional staff or property required with the proposed project. This will be a 225 bed facility.

         We feel if we do not do this that we increase possible liability and lawsuits and this could worsen over time.
         We feel these new facilities will reduce recidivism and liability while increasing public safety. The bond
         cost is under $5 per month per household and about $60 per year based on a home value of $468k.

         Commissioner Bolos: We have a thorough understanding of the process you’ve gone through to get to this
         point. Thanks for your work and your concern for moving forward and what we need to do in our
         community and reducing recidivism which is our main goal.

         Commissioner Harvey: The data certainly supports the need. Thank you for being so well prepared.

         Chair Froerer: Can you give us an idea of how many of our inmates are from Weber County versus outside?
         And how many deal with mental health and/or substance abuse issues?

         Chief Reese: For a long period of time we housed out of county inmates on contract. Since the pandemic, a
         vast majority come from Weber County. By in large, every inmate has touched some component of mental
         illness or substance abuse.
