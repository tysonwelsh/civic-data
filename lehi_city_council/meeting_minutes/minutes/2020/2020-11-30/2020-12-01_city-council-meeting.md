1. Welcome
   Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present.

2. Discussion Items
 2.1 Alpine School District Update Presentation
 Rob Smith, with Alpine School District, presented on the District’s goals and accomplishments.
 He stated that their number one goal is to attract and retain the best teachers. He said that the
 District believes that the best learning takes place face to face, so they are trying to balance that
 with safety during the pandemic. Mr. Smith stated that Lehi should not expect to see the new bond
 proposal being spent in Lehi. He said that the immediate needs are now west of the Jordan River.

Scott Carlson, Alpine School District Board member, was also present and explained the
accomplishments of the District over the last several years.

2.2 Discussion of Protest Ordinance
Chief of Police, Darren Paul, stated that due to some of the recent protests happening in the State
in residential areas, they felt that this was needed to be proactive in preventing an escalated
situation.

Ryan Wood, City Attorney, stated that this Ordinance is an attempt to strike a balance between the
right to protest while still allowing for appointed and elected officials to feel safe at home. People
can still protest, but this creates a buffer zone so that people feel safe.

Councilor Koivisto expressed concerns that this would limit people’s constitutional rights.

2.3 Discussion of Fiber Network Financing
Jason Walker, City Administrator stated that he would like to discuss the parameters of the
Resolution before the Council votes on it next week.

John from Magellan and Laura Lewis, the City’s Financial Advisor, were participating virtually.
Ms. Lewis explained that the parameters resolution will contemplate using franchise and sales tax,
but the expectation is that the telecommunications revenue will pay off the bond. She said that it’s
like a parent co-singing on a child’s car. A parent does that not expecting to pay for it, but for the
benefit of their child getting a lower interest rate. Pledging the franchise and sales tax will allow
them to get a better bond rating and lower interest rate, even though they fully intend to pay for it
with user fees. This also allows for more flexibility when they need to bond for other projects using
sales tax.

Brad Benson, Street Superintendent, wanted to make the Council aware of issues with road
construction on Triumph Blvd. There are issues with a sewer line, and there’s an easement that
Geneva is still working to obtain. He suggested that they wait and pave the road this spring.
