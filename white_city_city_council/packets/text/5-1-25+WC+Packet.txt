3. Discuss Creation of Community Reinvestment Agency and/or Municipal Building

The White City Council discussed the creation of a Community Reinvestment Agency (CRA)

entities. Mr. Torres reviewed the following information:

                Understanding Utah’s Local Building Authority (LBA) Law


                      White City Council Meeting Minutes – Page 1
Introduction
Utah’s Local Building Authority Act (Utah Code Title 17D, Chapter 2) provides local governments with a
valuable mechanism to finance, construct, and manage public buildings and related facilities. By
establishing an independent, yet closely governed nonprofit entity, local governments can streamline
public project delivery, achieve financial efficiencies, and meet community needs effectively.
What is a Local Building Authority (LBA)?
Under Utah law, a Local Building Authority is a public, nonprofit corporation established by a city,
county, school district, or special district specifically for the purpose of:
   •   Constructing new public facilities.

   •   Acquiring, improving, or expanding existing public facilities.

   •   Financing the costs associated with these projects.

A Local Building Authority is governed directly by the legislative body of the local government (city
council, county commission, or school board), acting as the authority's board of directors (Utah Code
§17D-2-203).
How Local Building Authorities Operate
The process for establishing and operating an LBA includes:
   1. Creation and Governance:
      The local government's legislative body creates the LBA by incorporating a nonprofit
      corporation, approving its articles of incorporation and bylaws, and acting as its governing
      board (Utah Code §17D-2-201).

   2. Project Approval and Management:
      The local building authority proposes specific projects, submits plans and specifications to the
      local government for approval, and manages all aspects of the project, including construction,
      procurement, and financing (Utah Code §17D-2-301, §17D-2-302).

   3. Lease Agreements and Funding Mechanisms:
      LBAs typically finance projects through the issuance of bonds or notes. The local government
      leases the facility from the LBA, making regular lease payments sufficient to cover debt service
      on the bonds, maintenance, and other costs (Utah Code §17D-2-401, §17D-2-402).

Advantages of Utilizing LBAs
Local Building Authorities offer several benefits to local governments, including:
   •   Tax-Exempt Status:
       Property held by an LBA is exempt from all state and local taxes, significantly reducing ongoing
       operational costs (Utah Code §17D-2-104).




                        White City Council Meeting Minutes – Page 2
   •   Simplified Procurement and Project Delivery:
       Projects undertaken by an LBA may bypass certain statutory procurement requirements,
       providing flexibility and efficiency in project management (Utah Code §17D-2-108).

   •   Financial Flexibility and Stability:
       Through issuing bonds, LBAs can provide stable, long-term financing solutions that do not
       directly burden the municipality's credit rating or statutory debt limits.

Typical Uses of Local Building Authorities
LBAs are commonly utilized for community-oriented facilities, including:
   •   City or Town Halls
   •   Police Stations and Fire Stations
   •   Public Works Facilities
   •   Community Centers and Libraries
   •   Educational Facilities
   •   Any other structure that serves a clear public purpose

Regulatory Oversight and Limitations
Utah law provides clear guidance and limitations on the operations of LBAs, including:
   •   Lease Term Limitations:
       Lease agreements between an LBA and the creating local government cannot exceed 40 years or
       the estimated useful life of the facility, whichever is shorter (Utah Code §17D-2-402).

   •   Transparency and Public Involvement:
       Although exempt from standard procurement laws, LBAs must comply with transparency
       requirements such as project approvals by the creating local government body and public
       accountability for the issuance of bonds.

   •   Dissolution and Property Transfer:
       Upon the completion of lease payments or satisfaction of bond obligations, ownership of the
       property automatically transfers back to the creating local government (Utah Code §17D-2-402).


               Understanding Utah’s Community Reinvestment Agency (CRA) Law

Introduction

Utah’s Community Reinvestment Agency (CRA) Law, codiﬁed in Utah Code Title 17C, provides
cities and counties with a tool for promoting economic development, revitalizing underutilized
properties, and enhancing community infrastructure. By leveraging incremental property and




                        White City Council Meeting Minutes – Page 3
sales tax revenues, CRAs serve as catalysts for private investment and sustainable community
growth.

What is a Community Reinvestment Agency (CRA)?

Under Utah law, a Community Reinvestment Agency is a public entity created by municipal or
county governments to:

   •   Foster economic development and redevelopment.

   •   Address community challenges such as economic stagnation, infrastructure deterioration,
       or underutilized commercial areas.

   •   Collaborate with private developers, businesses, and other stakeholders to revitalize
       communities.

A CRA is typically governed by the local legislative body (city or county council), which acts as the
agency’s board of directors (Utah Code §17C-1-203).

How CRAs Raise Funds: Tax Increment Financing (TIF)

The primary funding mechanism for CRAs is Tax Increment Financing (TIF). TIF allows CRAs to
capture and reinvest increases in property tax revenues resulting from redevelopment activities.
This works as follows:

   1. Establishment of a Project Area:
      The CRA identiﬁes a geographic project area needing redevelopment or revitalization.

   2. Baseline Property Valuation:
      The taxable value of the properties within the project area is assessed to establish a
      baseline ("base value").

   3. Capturing the Increment:
      As redevelopment occurs and property values increase, the additional property taxes (the
      "increment") above the baseline are allocated to the CRA for reinvestment in further
      development activities (Utah Code §17C-1-401.5).

Additionally, CRAs can raise funds by entering interlocal agreements with taxing entities (such as
school districts or counties) to allocate portions of sales and use tax revenues toward
redevelopment efforts (Utah Code §17C-5-204).



                       White City Council Meeting Minutes – Page 4
Typical Uses of CRA Funds

CRA funds are used strategically to encourage community revitalization and economic growth,
including:

   •   Infrastructure Improvements: Streets, sidewalks, utilities, public transit facilities.

   •   Redevelopment of Commercial and Industrial Areas: Stimulating private sector
       investment through ﬁnancial incentives or grants.

   •   Environmental Remediation: Cleaning up contaminated or blighted properties to
       facilitate new development.

   •   Affordable Housing Initiatives: Assisting development projects that include affordable
       housing elements.

   •   Community Enhancements: Landscaping, public amenities, and improvements that
       directly beneﬁt the community (Utah Code §17C-1-102).

CRAs typically partner with private developers, community organizations, and other stakeholders
to maximize impact and leverage private investment.

Governance and Oversight

   •   The CRA board (usually the city or county council) provides oversight, policy direction, and
       approves the budget and project area plans.

   •   Transparency and public involvement are mandated by law, with requirements for public
       hearings and disclosures.

   •   A Taxing Entity Committee (TEC)—comprising representatives from affected taxing
       entities—often oversees or provides consent to the CRA budget and tax increment
       allocations (Utah Code §17C-1-402).

Limitations and Compliance

The CRA law establishes clear statutory limitations to ensure funds are appropriately and
transparently utilized:

   •   Funds must be spent within or clearly beneﬁt the deﬁned project area.




                       White City Council Meeting Minutes – Page 5
•   CRAs must comply with state regulations regarding budget approvals, transparency, public
    hearings, and annual reporting.

•   The increment collection period is typically limited (often 15 to 25 years) as deﬁned in the
    project area plan or agreement with taxing entities.

3.      UNIFIED FIRE AUTHORITY REPORT

        3.1   SANDY CITY FIRE QUARTERLY REPORT AND DEFENSIBLE SPACE
              (WILDFIRE PREPAREDNESS PLAN)

       Battalion Chief Matt Stuebner discussed the Community Wildfire Protection Plan (CWPP)
that helps them deal with threats along with their Emergency Management and Deployment
overview. The CWPP provides direction in identifying the best management practices in
reducing wildfire risk through preparedness, prevention and mitigation. They identify the best
practices they can to keep the citizens safe. They focus on structure protection and address
items specific to wildland operations. They also focus on apparatus replacement, wildland



                      White City Council Meeting Minutes – Page 13
specialty approach with advanced training, and wildland personal protective equipment. In
regard to prevention, they focus on public education and awareness with specified measures
you can take to safeguard your home from wildfire. They have a program called Ready, Set, Go
which is a door to door outreach program. In this program, they focus on being Ready – prepared
long before the threat of fire; Set – situational awareness; and Go – act early and follow through
with your plan. On May 17th from 9am to 12pm there will be a Community Wildland Outreach
Day offered at the Bell Canyon Preservation Trailhead. They also offer home inspections for
residents to discuss what can be done in case of a fire. They will be focusing on the homes along
the canyon areas.

       Battalion Chief Matt Stuebner discussed the mitigation Sandy Fire has been doing with
Dimple Dell Park Fuel Reduction with Back Country Horsemen Volunteers as well as county
parks. He discussed the emergency management incident stages including mitigation,
preparedness, response and recovery. He recommended evacuation kits for families in
preparation for evacuation along with keeping your vehicle fueled and backed into your driveway
facing the direction of escape. Some of the community evacuation tips include:
    • Plan and practice two ways out of your neighborhood
    • Know your workplace, school and commuter route evacuation plans
    • Know where and how you are going to leave.
He also recommended preparing and using the Ready, Set, Go plan.

       Mayor Flint requested additional public education for residents so they know not to throw
grass or hay into the Dimple Dell park area due to fire risk. She also recommended looking at
the bridge on the north side where there are issues that need to be addressed.

       Unified Fire Authority (UFA) Battalion Chief Brian Anderton filling in for Battalion Chief
Kenneth Aldridge said UFA just brought on the Camp Williams Seasonal Wildland Fire Team.
He reported the recruitment camp is down to 33 candidates, and they are anticipating another
camp in the fall. They are in the process of opening the new fire station in Eagle Mountain and
are reopening one in the Oquirrh Shadows, Kearns area. He said they are working on a
Community Risk Reduction Program that analyzes analytics and identifies pockets where they
see different kinds of calls and helps them go in and target those audiences.

          ENGINEERING STANDARDS & SPECIFICATIONS

Chad Anderson presented the White City Engineering Standard Drawing and Specifications for
Council discussion. He said there was a bill that passed this year that requires engineering
standards and specifications to be formally adopted. He discussed the APWA standards
regarding installing utilities beneath the roadway.

Paul Ashton presented a letter from Sunrise Engineering representing the White City Water
Improvement District regarding the standards for Asphalt Restoration. The letter indicated the
MSD standards were notably stringent and would elevate the costs associated with all waterline
replacement projects.

Mayor Flint, Council Member Cardenaz, and Paul Ashton declared conflict of interest regarding
this subject since they also serve on the White City Water Improvement District Board or work
for the Water District.

Chad Anderson said the letter was written with a clear bias to the White City Water Improvement
District. He discussed the recommendations indicated in the Sunrise letter. He said the
standards proposed by the MSD are widely adopted so they are not extraordinary. He discussed
patches versus overlays on the roadways.

Mayor Flint said White City Water has always worked with County Operations prior to roads
being built and rebuilt so they can put in a new waterline before the work is done.




                      White City Council Meeting Minutes – Page 16
Chad Anderson said when they know there is a capital improvement project or a new mill and
overlay project moving forward, they do notify the utilities and meet with them at least once a
year to discuss upcoming projects.

Paul Ashton said he was speaking as the General Manager for the White City Water
Improvement District. He said this is an economic issue for the Water District. If these standards
are adopted, the Water District will perform to the standards; however, it will dramatically
increase the cost of pipeline replacement and repair. They normally do the four inches if it’s a
residential street. As for the two inch mill and overlay, water districts do not have mill and overlay
equipment so the contractors would have to go buy the equipment. All of the water districts in
the MSD cities will have the same issue and that has been communicated to the MSD.

Chad Anderson said the two-inch mill and overlay has been a standard for several years but has
not been enforced. If it stays a standard, it should be enforced.

Paul Ashton said that’s why he is asking that it be removed.

Chad Anderson said if the two-inch mill and overlay standard is removed, he would recommend
staying with the six-inch t-patch thickness, which will hold together better.

Mayor Flint said the city does not want any roads to be in disrepair, but if there is a leak that is
repaired and the road is scheduled for overlay, there should be an exception.

Paul Ashton said if the road is less than three years old, is a commercial street, or carries a lot
of weight like Sego Lily, six inches makes a lot of sense. He recommended the six inches be
restricted to the non-residential streets. Those costs will be passed onto the consumers. He said
he asked Sunrise to look at the proposed standards and see how they compare to other cities,
especially Sandy City which is half of his service area. If the roadway is more than three years
old, he would be willing to repave the road.

The Council discussed the need for exceptions for White City and asked the Engineer to include
the exceptions in the standards. The question may be the standards for residential versus
commercial.

Council Member Huish discussed the height and color of lighting for the lights in White City. He
recommended a warmer color light that doesn’t shine into residents’ yards.

The Council agreed with the color of the lighting and making sure the lights shine straight down
so they don’t reflect into yards.

Daniel Torres said they are trying to get engineering standards for all MSD entities. The



                       White City Council Meeting Minutes – Page 17

trying to come up with standards for every entity. If the standards are adopted, they will be
revisited again during that process. He said the engineer has the power to make those
exceptions as needed.

Paul Ashton said the Council needs to see the exception language in the document. He
suggested a small committee be formed to discuss the standards before being brought back to
the Council for adoption.

Council Member Huish said he would like to be on that committee. He said he would also like

AN ORDINANCE OF THE WHITE CITY COUNCIL AMENDING SUBSECTION
19.46.100 INFRASTRUCTURE AND PUBLIC IMPROVEMENTS, CLARIFYING
 WHEN INFRASTRUCTURE AND PUBLIC IMPROVEMENTS MUST BE IN-
     STALLED BY A DEVELOPER SUBJECT TO THE LIMITATIONS ON
                    EXACTIONS IN UTAH CODE


       WHEREAS, White City is a municipality and has authority to adopt land use reg-
ulations, pursuant to Utah Code § 10-9a-501 in accordance with the Municipal Land Use,
Development, and Management Act, Title 10, Section 9a, Utah Code; and
        WHEREAS, an applicant for a building or land use permit shall provide curb, gut-
ter, sidewalk, street, and urban hydrology improvements along the entire property line
which abuts any public road or street in cases where it does not exist at municipal standards;
        WHEREAS, a code section requiring infrastructure and public improvements to be
installed by a developer was inadvertently left out of the adopted Title 19 in 2023;
       WHEREAS, these improvement installation requirements are subject to the limita-
tions on exactions in Utah Code,
       WHEREAS, the Council deems it necessary to amend its land use ordinances for
the protection and preservation of the public health, safety and general welfare;
       WHEREAS, the White City Planning Commission held a public hearing on April
22nd, 2025 to consider amending Title 19 as set forth in Exhibit A (the “Proposed Action”)
in accordance with Utah Code §§ 10-9a-205and 10-9a-502; and
        WHEREAS, the Planning Commission has recommended that the Council amend
its land use ordinances set forth in Exhibit A for the protection and preservation of the
public health, safety and general welfare.
BE IT ORDAINED BY THE WHITE CITY COUNCIL as follows:
1.     Section 19.46.100 Infrastructure and Public Improvements, is hereby revised to
       read as set forth in Exhibit A.

       Ordinance is unconstitutional or invalid, then such portion of this Ordinance, or
       specific application of this Ordinance, shall be severed from the remainder, which
       shall continue in full force and effect.

       needed: (a) for this ordinance to become effective under Utah law, including but not
        limited to compliance with the requirements of Utah Code § 10-3-711; and (b) to
        finalize and post the ordinance to Municode, including but not limited to making
        non-substantive edits to correct any scrivener’s, formatting, and numbering errors.
4.      Effective Date. This Ordinance will take effect immediately upon posting pursuant
        to Utah Code § 10-3-712.

19.46.100 Infrastructure and Public Improvements
The minimum requirements for public improvements shall be a combination of
standards set forth in Title 14 Highways, Sidewalks, and Public Places and applicable
standards set forth in this Title or adopted in White City’s the municipality’s Master
Transportation Plan.
A. Off-Site Improvements Required.
   1. Off-Site Improvements. Subject to the essential link and rough proportionality
      tests for exactions in Utah Code Section 10-9a-508, an applicant for a building or
      land use permit for a dwelling, or a commercial or industrial use shall provide
      curb, gutter, sidewalk, street, and urban hydrology improvements along the entire
      property line which abuts any public road or street in cases where it does not
      exist at municipal standards.
   2. Access to public right of ways. New and reconstructed vehicular entrances to the
      property shall be provided as required in Section 14.12.110. Height, location,
      structural specifications, maximum and minimum cut radii and minimum roadway
      approach angles to the centerline of the street are subject to the approval of the
      Municipal Engineering Division.
   3. Exceptions.
       a. The planning commission may grant exception to the installation of the
          sidewalk in industrial areas where the planning commission determines that
          the sidewalk is not necessary to serve the public need, and the elimination of
          the sidewalk does not jeopardize the public health, safety or welfare.
       b. The planning commission may grant exception to the installation of curb,
          gutter and sidewalk in rural or estate areas where topographic or other
          exceptional conditions exist, provided that the public health, safety and
          welfare is preserved.
       c. The planning commission may require the applicant to sign a delay
          agreement binding the current and future property owner to pay for their
          share of any required improvements that are installed by Magna City within
          10 years of the waiver.
A.B.   Acceptance of Public Streets.
   1. Street lighting shall either be chosen from the municipality’s approved streetlight
      list or installed to match a theme set by developments within the zone or
      neighborhood.
   2. Street lighting shall be installed in conformance with Title 18.
   3. Street Signs and Markers. Standard street name signs shall be installed at one
      corner of all street intersections. The size, design, materials, location, fabrication,
      installation, and maintenance of the signs and poles within the public right of way
      and elsewhere shall be in accordance with the Utah Department of
      Transportation, the Manual of Uniform Traffic Control Devices for Streets and
      Highways (MUTCD), or the municipality’s adopted policies, as applicable.
B.C.   Private Streets.
   1. Private streets or roads shall be designed and constructed to meet or exceed the
      public street standards set forth in Title 14 Highways, Sidewalks, and Public
      Places, as applicable.
       a. Private streets or roads are allowed in the following circumstances:
          (1) If shown on an approved development plan as private streets;
          (2) For multi-family developments; or
          (3) For residential subdivisions where the street is equally shared between
              properties. Such streets shall have a maintenance and operations plan
              included in the declaration of covenants, conditions, and restrictions for
              the subdivision.
C.D. Ingress and Egress Requirements. No building with human occupiable space
   may be erected or enlarged on a parcel in any zone unless such parcel abuts upon
   or has access to a publicly accepted and maintained street, a private driveway
   leading to an approved public street, a private road, or a public or private alley.
D.E. Intersecting Streets and Clear Visibility. In all zones which require a front yard, no
   obstruction to view in excess of three feet (3’) in height may be placed on any corner
   lot within a triangular area formed by the street property lines or right-of-way lines
   and a line connecting them at points forty feet (40’) from the intersection of the street
   lines, except mature trees which are located in the clear sight triangle shall be
   pruned to a height of at least seven feet (7') above the established sidewalk or street
   elevation.
              Figure 19.46.100 A: Intersecting Streets and Clear Visibility.
E.F. Intersecting Streets and Driveways. In all zones, no view obstruction, including a
   sight-obscuring fence, wall, sign, other similar structures, and landscaping which
   exceeds three feet (3’) in height shall be placed within a triangular area formed by a
   diagonal line connecting lines located at the curb line or sidewalk line and driveway
   line ten feet (10’) from the projected intersection of such lines.
              Figure 19.46.100 B: Intersecting Streets and Driveways.




F.G. Acceptance of Private Streets. Prior to acceptance by the municipality, any
   private street, or any driveway allowed for access with a development that is not
   constructed and maintained to the municipality’s adopted street standards shall be
   improved to the municipality’s adopted street standards.
G.H. Driveways. A driveway shall be allowed for vehicular access according to the
   standards set forth in Title 14 Highways, Sidewalks, and Public Places, and Chapter
   19.48, Parking and Mobility.
H.I.   Pedestrian and Bicycle Mobility.
   1. Each lot shall have pedestrian walkways and sidewalks that provide connections
      between the building entrances, neighboring building entrances, parking areas,
      open space, and public trail. Such systems shall be designed to connect with all
      elements within the development, adjacent areas, and transit stops and can
      include sidewalks along public or private streets, wide outside travel lanes, bike
      lanes on roadways, and walkways and trails in alternative locations as
      appropriate. Design, location, dimensions, dedications, easements, and
      reservations shall conform to applicable municipal policies and standards for
      sidewalks, bicycle routes, and trails.
   2. Walkways and trails shall be designed to maximize the safety of users and the
      security of adjoining properties with respect to location, visibility, and
      landscaping.
   3. Bicycle Facilities. Bicycle facilities shall be provided in accordance with the
      standards set forth in Chapter 19.48, Parking and Mobility.

developer subject to the limitations on exactions in Utah code.
