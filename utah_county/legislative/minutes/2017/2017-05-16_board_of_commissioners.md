For the Prayer/Reading/Thought offered at the beginning of the meeting, Utah County Sheriff James O. Tracy talked
about May being National Law Enforcement Memorial Month, with this week dedicated to police. Sheriff Tracy
noted creating a civilized society and maintaining safe communities can cost officers their lives. Duty-related deaths
have increased thirty percent since last year, where here in Utah three law enforcement officers were killed in the
line of duty. Sheriff Tracy read the following poem by Deputy Harry Koch of the Maricopa County Sheriff’s Office
in Arizona:

         “A Part of America Died”
         Somebody killed a Policeman today and part of America died.
         A piece of our Country he swore to protect will be buried with him at his side.
         The suspect who shot him will stand up in court, with counsel demanding his rights,
         While a young widowed mother must work for her children and spend many long lonely nights.
         The beat that he walked was a battlefield too, just as if he’d gone off to war,
         Though the flag of our Nation won’t fly at half-staff, to his name they will add a gold star.
         Yes, somebody killed a Policeman today, in your town or mine,
         While we slept in comfort behind our locked doors, a COP put his life on the line.
         Now his spirit walks a beat on a dark city street, and he stands at each new rookie’s feet,
         He answered the call, of himself, gave his all,
         And a part of America died.

5.     APPROVE THE AUDITOR’S LIST OF PROPERTIES DEEMED IN THE BEST INTEREST OF
       THE PUBLIC TO WITHDRAW FROM THE 2017 ANNUAL DELINQUENT TAX SALE
(Track A, 06:15)
Commissioner Lee asked about the process for withdrawing properties from the annual delinquent tax sale. Utah
County Clerk/Auditor Bryan E. Thompson explained a committee consisting of the Utah County Recorder’s Office,
the Civil Division of the Utah County Attorney’s Office, and his own office meets continuously leading up to the
sale. Tax Administration Supervisor Burt Harvey of the Utah County Clerk/Auditor’s Office added it is an ever-
evolving process, and there have been discussions about whether or not it is necessary to have the commissioners
approve the final list. They discussed statute and how it relates to airport hangars and reclaiming taxes,
summarizing the list is an explanation of which properties are being withdrawn and the Clerk/Auditor’s reasoning
for that action. Commissioner Graves and Mr. Thompson also addressed requests from the federal Internal Revenue
Service and legal notices.

(Track A, 21:50)
Commissioner Graves, Burt Harvey, Bryan Thompson, and Utah County Civil Division Chief Deputy Attorney
David Shawcroft discussed interest, owner redemption, and how a tax sale does not pay off all debts but only what is
owed to the county. Any additional profit is transferred to the Utah State Treasurer as unclaimed property; the
owners or other parties can then go through the state office’s process to claim those funds. Utah County Deputy
Attorney Paul Jones was also included in the discussion.

(Track A, 45:05)
Utah County Health Department Director Ralph Clegg introduced the Health Department Public Information
Officer, Aislynn Tolman-Hill. Commissioner Lee asked about the benefit or need for a media monitoring agreement
when there are applications such as Google Alerts available for free online. Ms. Tolman-Hill explained it is very
time-consuming to manage the multiple platforms for news and information, and repeated attempts to customize free
services have not been comprehensive enough for her needs. On that note, Ms. Tolman-Hill mentioned Meltwater

information from social media, missing traditional platforms such as newspapers, radio, and television interviews.

Tolman-Hill used that point to reiterate her office needs to know about any mention of the County Health
Department, or times the department is sourced or quoted – not only the ‘hot button’ issues or ‘viral’ posts. They
debated their views, and Commissioner Graves ultimately suggested continuing this agenda item to allow time for
additional discussions about media monitoring needs and a potential Request for Proposals (RFP).

1.      DISCUSSION WITH CEDAR HILLS OFFICIALS REGARDING CANYON ROAD
(Track A, 1:00:00)
Cedar Hills City Manager Chandler Goodwin explained the city has concerns regarding State Road 26 (SR26),
commonly referred to as Canyon Road. Cedar Hills is nearing ‘build-out,’ and does not have the trucks, manpower,
or projected revenue to take ownership of Canyon Road. The estimated cost of maintenance for the next thirty years
is $2,800,000 annually, which is the entire road budget. Utah County Public Works Director Richard Nielson came
forward to talk about drainage from the road, and grading driveways to prevent problems. The commissioners and
Mr. Goodwin discussed potential development, with Commissioner Graves noting the Mountainland Association of
Governments (MAG) plans had presented this as a city road during budget planning. They talked about comparable
situations in Pleasant Grove, Woodland Hills, Elk Ridge, and Salem cities. Commissioner Graves expressed
reluctance in a road partnership with Cedar Hills City as the city is making requests far above required standards.
The commissioners and Mr. Chandler discussed specific sections of road designated as county land.
