Legal Updates/Information

Legislative Update

	Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, presented a brief overview on some of the bills that passed during the 2022 Legislative session that could have an impact on the Kearns Metro Township.

	•	Water Efficiency Ordinance

Legislation passed requiring municipalities with more than 5,000 residents to amend its General Plans to account for water.  Since Kearns is almost entirely dependent on the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, it will need to amend its General Plan to include how Kearns will deal with its water in the future.  This will need to be done by October of this year.

	•	Abatement Bill relating to Meth Homes

This bill will help Kearns abate some of the meth homes in Kearns.  The issue is that there was uncertainty in authority of who cleans up a meth home once the Salt Lake County Health Department closes it due to contamination.  This bill makes it clear that once the home is closed, then a due process format will be followed, which will give the property owner a fair amount of time to clean up the property.  If the property is not cleaned up, then Kearns Metro Township can come in and clean up the contamination. 

	•	Railroad Crossing

This bill prohibits railroad companies from charging a $10,000 fee to municipalities whenever questions come up that need to be answered.  It also requires the railroad companies to respond to requests for plan approval within a reasonable time.  The Utah Department of Transportation will clarify what that time will be.

	•	Uniform Code Language

This bill would allow a property owner to relocate an easement.  The property owner could move a road or adjust any easement.  This is something municipalities would not want to happen, so some exclusions for unilateral relocation of easements were included.

	•	Affordable Housing 

This bills states that a municipality cannot require a developer to include affordable housing in its development.  However, a developer can chose to do so due to a development agreement or some type of incentive.  This bill is very problematic for Kearns because it takes a significant tool off the table.  Kearns does not have a lot of tools to incentivize affordable housing.  

	•	Annexation

Another bill restricts the ability of citizens that live within a municipality to challenge an annexation that seeks to add land to a municipality.  This should not matter to Kearns

	•	Moderate Income Housing

This bill would require Kearns to amend its General Plan to deal with moderate income housing.  A municipality would have to select three moderate income tools from eleven or more affordable housing options.  Almost every option puts the cost of providing affordable housing on the municipality with virtually no contribution from the developer.

	•	Licensing for Food Trucks

This bill states that when a food truck operator gets a license to operate a food truck, that license would apply in any other city.  Fire Code and Health Inspections would still need to be done.  

	•	ATV Ordinance/Noise Ordinance

This bill allows for reasonable noise ordinance in respect to street legal ATV vehicles but prohibits the use of business license to regulate ATV rental companies. 

	•	Redevelopment Agency

This bill ties municipalities hands in the types of incentive that could be offer for retail.  His office will continue working on the creation of a redevelopment agency in Kearns. 

	Mayor Bush stated she had Senator Mayne take to the Appropriations Committee, the $2,000,000 request for the bridge on 4015 West. Kearns received the appropriation, so the bridge is now completely paid for. 

	Clare Collard, Utah State Representative, stated she was really excited with the passage of the bill relating to meth houses.  It will help Countywide.  Other bills worked on were a child support bill, which will go to interim and an emergency preparedness bill.
