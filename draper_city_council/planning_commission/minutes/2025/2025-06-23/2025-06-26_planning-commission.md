       C.      Action Item: Mallory White I-ADU Deviation Request
               (Administrative Action)
               On behalf of Mallory White, a Deviation Request from Strict Compliance with the
               Geographical Restrictions for Internal Accessory Dwelling Units (I-ADUs) for
               approximately 0.16 acres, located at 15262 South Steep Mountain Drive. Known
               as Application 2025-0087-VAR, Staff Contact: Nick Whittaker, (801) 576-6522,
               nick.whittaker@draperutah.gov.

Planner Nick Whittaker presented the Staff Report and explained that this is the Mallory White
Internal Accessory Dwelling Unit (I-ADU) Deviation Request. He displayed the Vicinity, Aerial,
Land Use, and Zoning maps. The land use is Residential Medium Density, and the zone is R3.
The property is located in an area that prohibits I-ADUs, which is the reason for the deviation
request. Mr. Whittaker shared the Site Plan provided by the applicant to illustrate the proposed I-
ADU. There will be two parking stalls, and a basement walkout will be installed so the tenant can
access the unit. The unit is proposed to be in the basement of the home. The staircase going into
the basement from the main floor will be sealed off, so the I-ADU will be completely independent.
Exterior photographs of the property were shared. He pointed out the side where there will be a
stairwell installed.

The criteria for approval from Section 9-31-055 of the Draper City Municipal Code were reviewed:

       A.      Deviation Authorized: The Planning Commission may approve a deviation from
               strict compliance with the geographical restrictions of the I-ADU map in Exhibit A
               of this chapter upon finding that:

               1.     The lot or parcel on which the primary dwelling is located is at least six
                      thousand square feet (6,000 ft²);
               2.     No D-ADU exists on the property;
               3.     The proposed I-ADU meets all of the requirements of sections 9-31-030 and
                      9- 31-045, including other applicable requirements of this chapter; and
               4.     At least two (2) parking stalls meeting the requirements of 9-31-045(B)(2)
                      are provided for use by occupants of the I-ADU.

Mr. Whittaker reported that the parking stalls meet the requirements and are at least 9’x18’ each.
The stalls are proposed to be concrete. A question was asked about how to ensure the applicant
installs the required parking spaces. Mr. Whittaker explained that there is a separate application
for the I-ADU. In order for that application to be fully processed, all of the requirements listed in
the deviation will be inspected and reviewed by Staff to make sure there is full compliance before
the I-ADU certificate is issued. As for why this area was excluded from allowing I-ADUs, it was
clarified that prohibited areas were determined based on street widths. Neighborhoods with streets
that were less than the standard were the ones chosen to be prohibited. The thought was it might
be more difficult for those areas to accommodate additional on-street parking. Commissioner Shah
felt comfortable that the application met the outlined requirements and expressed a desire to make
a motion.

The applicant, Mallory White, explained that she lives at 15262 South Steep Mountain Drive and
is interested in putting a basement apartment into the house. Chair Adams reported that the
Planning Commission is supportive of these kinds of units because it is an effective way to add
affordable housing. It looks like all of the requirements will be met once the proposed items on
the deviation are installed. Generally, the parking is the main issue with an accessory dwelling
unit (ADU). A question was asked about communication with the neighbors. Ms. White
confirmed that she has spoken to the neighbors, and there is an awareness of the plans to have an
I-ADU within the home.

Motion: Commissioner Shah moved to APPROVE the Deviation Request from Strict
Compliance with the Geographical Restrictions for I-ADUs, as requested by Mallory White,
Application 2025-0087-VAR, based on the following findings and criteria for approval, as
listed in the Staff Report dated June 13, 2025:

       1.      The lot or parcel on which the primary dwelling is located is at least six
               thousand square feet (6,000 ft²).

       2.      No D-ADU exists on the property.

       3.      The proposed I-ADU can meet all of the requirements of sections 9-31-030 and
               9-31- 045, including other applicable requirements of this chapter, before
               issuance of an ADU is permitted.

       4.      At least two (2) parking stalls meeting the requirements of 9-31-045(B)(2) shall
               be constructed and provided for use by occupants of the I-ADU.

Second: Commissioner Shirey seconded the motion.

Vote on Motion: 5-to-0 in favor.

       D.      Public Hearing: Urbana 2nd Development Agreement Amendment Request
               (Legislative Action)
               On the request of Draper City, a Development Agreement Amendment for the
               Urbana Development for approximately 3.9 acres, located at 285 West River
               Chapel Road. Known as application 2025-0130-DA. Staff Contact: Maryann
               Pickering, (801) 576-6391, maryann.pickering@draperutah.gov.

Planner, Maryann Pickering, presented the Staff Report and displayed the Vicinity, Aerial, Land
Use, and Zoning maps. The land use category is Residential High Density, and the zoning is RM2.
When the Preliminary Plat was reviewed at the last Planning Commission Meeting, she indicated
that this item would be coming to the Commission at a future meeting. It was found that there is
a conflict between the required public utility easements and Title 17. The zoning requires a 5-foot
setback and the public utility easement is a minimum of 7-feet. There will be work done on a
Code Amendment in the future for Title 17 of the Code, but in order to allow the developer to
move forward, the Development Agreement will need to be amended. In the Development
Agreement, the amendment will reduce the side yard public utility easement (PUE) up to 5-feet to
match the approved setbacks. There was no public comment as a result of the notices.

The applicant representative, Damian Mora, explained that he is filling in for Jacob Ballstaedt and
works for Garbett Homes. He noted that a 5-foot PUE is considered an acceptable standard.
Commissioner Shah pointed out that the proposed amendment will shift it from seven feet to five
feet. He asked about the extra two feet. It was clarified that the extra two feet would be for the
homeowner.

Chair Adams opened the public hearing. There were no comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Nixon asked if this would be for all residential subdivisions in the future or only
certain ones. It was noted that the future code change will eventually make it available for all
developments. However, what is currently before the Planning Commission relates to this specific
development.

Motion: Commissioner Fowler moved to forward a POSITIVE RECOMMENDATION to
the City Council for the 2nd Development Agreement Amendment, as requested by Draper
City for the Urbana Development, Application 2025-0130-DA, based on the findings for
approval listed in the Staff Report dated June 17, 2025:

       E.      Public Hearing: Leone Smith Subdivision Plat Amendment
               (Administrative Action)
               On the request of Justin Hansen, representing Leone Smith, a Subdivision Plat
               Amendment on approximately 0.92 acres, located at approximately 13530 South
               300 East. Known as application 2025-0068-SUB. Staff Contact: Maryann
               Pickering, (801) 576-6391, maryann.pickering@draperutah.gov.

Ms. Pickering presented the Staff Report and displayed the Vicinity, Aerial, Land Use, and Zoning
maps. The land use is Residential Medium Density, and the zone is R3. This was approved as a
Minor Subdivision by the Zoning Administrator last year. Since it is a recorded subdivision, any
changes to the plat must be approved by the Planning Commission. Ms. Pickering shared the
original plat that was approved. The future buyer of the lot on the west side would like a slightly
larger lot, so the middle lot line is proposed to be adjusted 10 feet to the east. The lots are more
evenly sized. As for the lot on the right or east side, there were some structures that needed to be
removed in order to comply with setbacks, which has been done. There is a buyer for the property,
so there is a desire to move forward quickly with this application. Ms. Pickering reported that
there were no public comments received on the application.

Commissioner Shah asked about the process and whether a public hearing is necessary.
Community Development Director, Jennifer Jastremsky, explained that it is possible to do a lot
line adjustment, which is an administrative process. However, that does not change public utility
easements or any other encumbrance that may be recorded on the property with the plat. The only
way to move those utility easements over with the property line is to pursue the Plat Amendment
process. The Plat Amendment process involves a public hearing, which is why there is one
scheduled for this item.

2.      Other Business
        Planning Commission Coordination and Updates (as Needed).

Ms. Jastremsky reminded the Planning Commission that there is a Joint Session with the City
Council scheduled for July 15, 2025, at 5:30 p.m. She asked Commissioners to let her know
whether they can attend and to accept the calendar invite. The meeting is for the Station Area
Plan. The results of the survey and the open house will be reviewed. There will also be a draft
preferred concept shared with the Commission and Council to gather feedback on before the
consultants finalize a concept.

Ms. Jastremsky shared an update from the last City Council Meeting. It went until 11:05 p.m. and
was a long meeting. The Alpine Hollow Development Agreement included the recommended
changes from the Planning Commission. There were five conditions placed on the agreement:
