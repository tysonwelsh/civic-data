Excellence in Reliability Award – Mark Montgomery, Light & Power Department

Logan Light & Power Director Mark Montgomery addressed the Council regarding the
Excellence in Reliability Award that was recently presented to the City by the American
Public Power Association (APPA). In providing similar services throughout the nation
and comparatively cities similar in size, the City is one of the best public service
providers in the nation. The rating is based on how many minutes of power outage on
average the public provider has per customer. Logan City’s outage number is 4 or 5
minutes per year per customer. In comparison, a nearby private provider that serves much
of Utah has over 200 minutes per year per customer.

Councilmember López presented the award to Mr. Montgomery for exceeding the five-
year average and goal of providing reliable electric utilities and services.

PUBLIC HEARING – Consideration for adoption of a proposed resolution
declaring the City’s intention to reimburse itself, for expenditures incurred in
connection with purchasing electric turbines, with proceeds of bonds that it intends
to issue; and related matters – Resolution 24-13 – Richard Anderson (36:45)

Finance Director Richard Anderson addressed the Council regarding the proposed
resolution. As prices continue to go up, the City may need to look at other financing
options such as going through UAMPS or for the City to bond and reimburse the City. In
order for the option of a bond to remain viable, a resolution needs to occur simply stating
that in the event the City does bond, the City would reimburse itself for the costs
associated with the purchase of electric turbines. It does not obligate the City to bond;
however, it does keep the potential finance option viable in the event it is needed.

Councilmember Simmonds requested confirmation that a legal requirement to bond is for
the resolution in question to occur.

Mr. Anderson confirmed that is the case. In order for the potential finance option of a
bond to remain viable, a legal requirement of a resolution must occur. This does not
authorize the authorization for the bonding but rather only for the reimbursement to
occur. The actual authorization of bonding would require coming before the Council
again.

Councilmember López inquired regarding the current economic interest rates, if the City
were to bond right now, would the City pay more.

Mr. Anderson answered with interest rates persisting at the current rate, the City would
pay more in interest than is the norm. Interest rates are currently 4% which is what the
City would bond at if implemented. If interest rates dropped later in the future, the City
would then repay interest at 3.5%. The bond itself would be an electric fund bond if
issued. It would not affect the credit of the other funds other than increase the debt load
of the City which is extremely low with the exception of the sewer treatment plant.

Councilmember Simmonds clarified that the reason interest rates are so low is because
the bond is a government bond and is a non-taxable bond. It is a tax-exempt bond for
municipal purposes.

There were no comments and Chair A. Anderson closed the public hearing.

PROJECT
The proponent (Logan City) is requesting to rezone these four sites to match the
appropriate use. Three are new public facilities and are currently zoned TC-1. Those are
proposed to be rezoned to Public. The fourth is the existing (old) fire station in the City
that will be sold and redeveloped. This site is proposed to be rezoned from Public to TC-
1.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), adopted in 2008, identifies these four sites as Town
Center (TC). The General Plan, a nonregulatory visioning plan, describes TC areas as
being a hub of the City and Valley and reflects a vibrant downtown, with a variety of
commercial, mixed-use, and institutional uses and activities. The application of the Public
Zone onto public uses in the Town Center is consistent with patterns of historical uses
and compatible with the range of current uses in the adjacent blocks.

Land Development Code (LDC) - Zoning
Both zones encourage walkable development in the downtown area. TC-1 encourages
dense, walkable commercial and residential development in the downtown area with
unique and interesting building designs. The Public zone is being applied to new public
facilities while the TC-1 zone is being applied to the existing fire station site which is
expected to be sold and redeveloped into a commercial center.

SUMMARY
This zoning action is considered "clean-up" zoning to reflect new construction and
changed land uses.

rezone of the four individual sites are compatible with the zoning pattern on each
individual block.

adopted FLUP and fit into the existing land use patterns of downtown.
