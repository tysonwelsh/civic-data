WORK SESSION
Chair Marianne Ricks called the meeting to order at approximately 6:10 p.m. The agenda items
were reviewed and discussed.

City Planner, Jonathan Teerlink reviewed the proposed zone map amendment requested by a
property owner and reported that the existing zoning is R-1-21. For the purpose of subdividing,
the applicant is requesting a rezone to R-1-10, which would allow for just over 10,000 square-foot
lots. In this situation, the General Plan allows the use of three zones consisting of R-1-8, R-1-10,
and R-1-15. Commissioner Mackin commented that the lot seems narrow if the intent is to
accommodate a private road. Mr. Teerlink was not certain of the width but referred to the concept
subdivision.

Chair Ricks noticed that on the petition that was distributed around the neighborhood there are a
number of co-owners who have signed, which makes it appear that many more people signed the
petition than actually did.

Chris Layton observed that there were several non-conforming lots of similar size and asked if an
evaluation was conducted. Mr. Teerlink stated that he had not performed an evaluation. Jan
Bradshaw recalled that comments were received from residents of the Country Pine Subdivision,
which has smaller lot sizes.

Howard Layton was concerned that an island type of zone would set a precedent for similar
requests in the future. It seemed to him that it tends to be problematic. Commissioner Bradshaw
agreed. Chair Ricks stated that as a general rule they do not want to have a spot zone that is out
of character with the neighborhood.

While Chris Layton agreed that spot zoning in a situation like this one seems irrational and
establishes a bad precedent, to allow for a larger population in the City, rezoning an area that would
allow for this type of development might be the best option.

7.      Accessory Dwelling Units – (“ADUs”) – Discussion of Proposed Allowances and
        Controls of Rough Draft ADU Guidelines.
Mr. Teerlink reported that last year staff polled the City Council with the results presented based
on a work session held previously. Chair Ricks stated that 16,000 square feet would be the smallest
area and the guidelines specify that an ADU requires a property owner to have at least twice the
amount of property required by the zone’s minimum standard. It was clarified that currently guest
houses? ?are allowed in R-1-21 zones or greater. Mr. Teerlink explained that they limit the
definition and use of a ‘guest house’ as a structure that cannot be leased or rented separate from
the main home.

Chris Layton considered it to be too large and precludes opportunities for those who own one-third
acre, which would easily accommodate a home and a “casita” or ADU. A one-third acre lot would
allow for an ADU if enough property is available. Chair Ricks considered a casita more desirable
than a structure that is attached to the home. Chris Layton commented that the suggested lot size
implies that there must be a large enough lot and two dwelling units. Troy Holbrook thought the
verbiage was whether a detached dwelling can be separately owned, which he did not think was
the intent. He stated that in an R-1-8 zone the minimum lot size for an ADU along with an existing
home is 16,000 square feet or twice the lot area of any property zoned R-1-8 or larger. Clarification
was requested. Chair Ricks was of the opinion that a property zoned R-1-8 seemed like a very
small lot for an ADU.

The Commission agreed with the requirement that the property owner live on-site in order to allow
an ADU. The potential of modal homes was discussed as well as attached versus detached units.
It was noted that there are different criteria for each. Mr. Teerlink stated that the Code defines
areas within a home that can be determined to be accessory to the main dwelling. Chris Layton
questioned how small units can be and still be considered an ADU? Chair Ricks commented that

if an ADU is attached to the main dwelling it should relate architecturally to it. If it is detached,
other design elements are acceptable. It was noted that there needs to be qualititative design
elements.

The issue of whether an ADU should have a separate street address and if it could be problematic
for the U.S. Postal Service was discussed. Mr. Teerlink stated that it could be an issue since “half”
addressing is no longer permitted. In addition, emergency personnel would respond to the main
dwelling. A determination would need to be made as to whether ADUs can be serviced with a
separate address. If not, the two units would have to share an address.

Shipping containers and pre-manufactured kits were discussed. Troy Holbrook stated that there
are good ones available that continue to get better. He expressed his support. Jan Bradshaw agreed.
Chair Ricks concurred as long as reasonable standards are met. Chris Layton reminded the
Commission that aesthetics are not the only consideration and there are also life, health, and safety
standards associated with a dwelling unit that are important.

It was noted that off-street parking will need to be provided for ADUs and utilities must be metered
off of the main dwelling. Mr. Teerlink stated that the utility companies will always supply an
accessory building with a second meter. Water, however, may be a different issue. He explained
that the City Council was trying to distance ADUs from the perception of being a separate structure
that could be sold separately. Having a separate address and utilities could confuse real estate
agents and give the appearance that the ADU can be sold separately. Chair Ricks did not want to
create an issue for the City in the future. Chris Layton thought the utilities should be metered but
would not want to require it. He suggested there be flexibility.

The issue of impact fees was addressed. Mr. Teerlink stated that when a new home is constructed,
one-time impact fees would be applied.

With regard to short-term rentals, they would be less than 30 days. Troy Holbrook’s position was
that ADU’s not be allowed for short-term rentals and instead address housing needs in the
community. Chair Ricks and Commissioner Bradshaw agreed. It was determined that no short-
term rentals should be allowed in ADUs.

In terms of using ADUs for a home business, Chair Ricks did not object as long as the business is
owned by the homeowner and there are no customers or employees. It was suggested that ADUs
be allowed as a permitted use in eligible single-family developments with an appropriate lot size.
Chair Ricks supported staff being able to approve ADUs if there are specific parameters in place.

The staff recommendations included limiting the building height to less than the main dwelling
unless it is within the building corridor. Outside of the building corridor, an ADU may not exceed
20 feet. Graduated height requirements must also be met. ADUs may be allowed within the main
dwelling or attached to it. A guesthouse may not be placed in front of the main dwelling. The
floor area allowed for the ADU may not exceed that allowed for guest houses within the R-1 zones
or exceed the floor area of the main dwelling. It was suggested the size be tied to the percentage
of the size of the main home up to a maximum. No more than one ADU should be allowed on a
property. A home-based business would be allowed in an ADU and no motor home or RV would
