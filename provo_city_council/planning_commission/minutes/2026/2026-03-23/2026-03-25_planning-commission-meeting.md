OVERVIEW
Provo City adopted the Data Center Overlay Zone on September 23, 2025. As staff processed
the first application to apply the new zone, they noted a few amendments to streamline the code
and bring it into better compliance with the City’s environmental goals.

First, the proposed amendments would remove power generation as a permitted use. This
would limit data centers to 50 MW since UMPA policies require data centers over 50 MW to
build an on-site power generating facility. This also removes the need for data centers that
require emissions credits to be located at least 500 feet from sensitive properties (homes,
schools, etc.), since no data center would be able to exceed state and federal limits without
energy generation.

Second, the amendments would require data centers to use non-evaporative cooling methods.
This nearly eliminates water concerns around data centers, since the high water use comes
from using evaporation to cool server systems. The water, which is often treated with biocides
that could disrupt municipal stormwater systems, would be required to be removed by a third
party. This is already an available service in the industry.

Third, the amendments propose removing the requirement that data centers over 5 MW must
pay for a third-party environmental analysis. Without power generation and with non-evaporative
cooling, the environmental impact of a data center would be roughly the same as a comparable
office space. Local environmental analysis companies have expressed that they would have
very little to analyze for a data center with those specifications.

The proposed amendments significantly reduce the air pollution and water use from data
centers while preserving the City’s discretion to reject proposed data centers that do not align
with the City’s environmental goals.

14.34A.010 Purpose and Objectives
The Data Center Overlay Zone (DC Overlay) is established to provide prudent development
standards for data centers and similar digital infrastructure facilities as well as any associated
power generation facilities. The provisions of this zone are intended to minimize the negative
environmental effects of data centers and power generation, especially to air quality, water use,
and energy demands.

For the purposes of this Title, a “data center” is defined as a dedicated building or facility
designed primarily for housing networked servers and computing infrastructure for continuous
operation. It excludes computational facilities primarily engaged in cryptocurrency mining,
blockchain validation, or other distributed ledger technology operations.

14.34A.020 Permitted Uses
The following principal uses and no others are permitted in the DC Overlay:
    (1) Data centers.;
    (2) Energy generation facilities and transmission infrastructure, subject to Utah Municipal
        Power Authority (UMPA) policies.

14.34A.030 Lot Standards
Each lot or parcel in the DC Overlay must comply with PCC Section 15.03.100 (Adequate
Public Facilities).

14.34A.040 Project Plan Approval
See PCC Sections 15.03.300 and 15.03.310.

BACKGROUND
The applicant is requesting approval of a project plan for an eight-unit apartment
building, on a parcel of approximately 0.20 acres, in the Campus Residential (CR) zone.
A rezone was approved in 2025 along with a concept plan for a five-unit apartment
building. Subsequently, an ordinance text amendment was submitted and approved that
creates some exceptions for developments under ten units. These exceptions were to
the design standards that pertain to driveway location and minimum habitable floor
area. With these changes to the code, the applicant was able to add three units,
bringing the total to eight.

The property currently has a single-family home that is being demolished, and it is
proposed that it is replaced with an eight-unit apartment building. The property to south
is in the same Campus Residential zone and the other surrounding property is in the
Residential Conservation (RC) zone. The properties in the RC zone have existing
apartment buildings on them, so this development will align with the built environment
around the property.

FINDINGS OF FACT
•   The approved concept plan for this site was approved for five units, and this project
    plan is requesting eight units.
•   The ordinance was amended after the concept plan was approved, giving way for
    additional units to fit on this site.
•   Apartments are permitted in the proposed CR zone (14.14E.020).
•   The lot meets the minimum lot standards (14.14E.020).
•   The project plan meets the setback requirements (14.14E.050).
•   Maximum building height permitted is 75’ and the proposed building is 37’ in height
    (14.14E.070).
•   14 parking stalls are required and 15 were provided (14.37.060).
•   The project plan meets the applicable requirements of city code.

STAFF ANALYSIS
The proposed use allows this underutilized parcel to be used more consistently with the
surrounding properties that have higher densities and is a good infill development within
such a proximity to BYU.

Parking will occupy the ground floor, and the building will be located above. The number
of units has increased from what was approved in the concept plan review and so have
the number of parking spaces. The proposed project will comply with parking
requirements, proving more than the minimum required. There will be vehicle and
pedestrian access from 880 North. Landscaping will be provided in setbacks except for
areas devoted to parking and access.

    PUBLIC COMMENT & REBUTTAL
    Provo City Planning Commission | Hearing Date: March 25, 2026 | PLOTA2025045



RE: Proposed Amendments to Title 14, Chapter 14.34A — DC Data Center Overlay Zone
Specifically: Proposed removal of §14.34A.020(2) permitting on-site power generation and proposed
prohibition of independent islanded power plants under §14.34A.050(7)(a)



The Proposed Amendment Would Eliminate Provo's Most Valuable
Category of Data Center Investment
Volition Industries — a Provo-based company whose CEO, Jeff Chavez, calls Provo home —
respectfully submits this comment in opposition to the proposed amendment that would (1) remove
on-site power generation as a permitted use in the DC Overlay Zone and (2) prohibit independent,
grid-disconnected power plants from operating data centers in Provo City.


We do not oppose improving environmental standards for data centers. We share those goals. But
this amendment, as written, goes far beyond environmental improvement — it categorically
eliminates an entire class of data center operators: the off-grid, fuel-cell-powered sovereign
compute facilities. And that class of operator represents the single most financially beneficial
category of data center that Provo City could attract.


  The proposed amendment does not just regulate data centers. It bans the most
  valuable kind.




1. What the Amendment Actually Eliminates
The staff report frames this amendment as a modest procedural cleanup — removing
environmental concerns and streamlining the code. The Commission should understand what it
actually does: it prohibits any data center of any size from generating its own power on-site and
operating independently of the city grid. This is not a size restriction. It is a total ban on the off-grid
data center model, applied uniformly across every facility that could ever apply for the DC Overlay.

Two separate provisions in the proposed amendment work together to produce this result, and it is
important to understand both:



Provision 1 — §14.34A.020: Power Generation Removed as a Permitted Use (All
Sizes)
The current code lists 'Energy generation facilities and transmission infrastructure' as a permitted
use in the DC Overlay. The proposed amendment deletes this provision entirely. The effect is
immediate and universal: no data center of any size — 5 MW, 30 MW, or 95 MW — may permit,
install, or operate on-site power generation equipment under the DC Overlay. A developer cannot


---

apply for a fuel cell system. A building permit cannot be issued for generation infrastructure. The
use simply does not exist in the amended code.

  • Applies to every data center in the DC Overlay, regardless of size.
  • Eliminates the ability to permit fuel cells, turbines, or any on-site generation equipment.
  • A 5 MW facility is affected identically to a 95 MW facility — there is no size threshold below
    which this exemption applies.
  • A data center that cannot permit power generation equipment cannot operate off-grid. Full
    stop.


Provision 2 — §14.34A.050(7)(a): Islanded Power Plants Prohibited (All Sizes)
Even if on-site generation were somehow permitted, the amendment adds an explicit prohibition
under §14.34A.050(7)(a) — the section governing development standards for data centers of all
sizes. This provision states that 'independent islanded power plants are prohibited' and requires
that any new power generation produced by a data center 'must be interconnected with Provo City
and follow UMPA policies.'

  • §14.34A.050 is titled 'Development Standards for Data Centers of All Sizes' — it applies
    without exception to every facility in the overlay.
  • 'Islanded' means operating independently of the grid — exactly what an off-grid, fuel-cell-
    powered data center does by design.
  • The mandate to interconnect with Provo City applies at 1 MW, 10 MW, 30 MW, and 100 MW
    alike. There is no minimum threshold.
  • A facility cannot comply with this provision and simultaneously operate as a grid-independent
    data center. The two are mutually exclusive by definition.


  These two provisions do not regulate off-grid data centers. Together, they make off-
  grid data centers — of any size — categorically impermissible in Provo City.


The Commission should reject any characterization of this amendment as targeting only large or
hyperscale facilities. The 50 MW threshold that appears elsewhere in the code governs additional
requirements for large data centers — mandatory plant construction, UMPA site plan review — but
the prohibition on islanded power and the removal of generation as a permitted use apply at every
scale. There is no path to a smaller-scale version of this project that complies with the amended
code. The off-grid model is banned, not scaled.


  Off-grid AI data centers are not a regulatory edge case. They are the frontier of the

2. The Technology This Amendment Would Lock Out Is the
Cleanest Generation Available
A central assumption embedded in this amendment is that on-site natural gas power generation is,
by definition, a pollution and noise problem that warrants prohibition. That assumption reflects an
outdated understanding of how natural gas power is generated today. The generation technologies

The gap between a traditional natural gas generator and a modern solid oxide fuel cell or linear

entirely different emissions and noise profiles. Any code that treats them identically — and this

between technologies that are decades apart in their impact.

From Gas Generators to Electrochemical Generation: A Generational Leap
Traditional natural gas generators operate through internal combustion — fuel ignites, pistons fire,
turbines spin. Combustion at high temperatures produces nitrogen oxides (NOx), particulate matter,

problem.


The technologies now powering next-generation AI data centers — including the Bloom Energy

Requiring Grid Interconnection Produces No Benefit to the Utility — Only Cost
There is a deeper problem with the amendment's mandate that all on-site generation must
interconnect with Provo City's utility system. The staff report appears to assume that requiring grid
tie-in is either neutral or beneficial to Provo Power. It is neither. It is a cost with no corresponding
benefit — and the Commission should understand exactly why.


Provo Power's existing grid infrastructure is sized for its current load. It is not sized for a 30–40 MW
AI data center. Serving that load — whether the power comes from the facility's own fuel cells or
from UMPA's generation assets — requires the same grid upgrades: new substation capacity,
transformer infrastructure, distribution reinforcement, and transmission studies. The amendment
does not eliminate those costs. It simply shifts the question of who bears them onto Provo's
ratepayers and capital budget.


  The proposed amendment requires grid interconnection. But interconnection does
  not improve the grid. It just adds load to a system that isn't built for it.


An islanded fuel cell facility that never draws from Provo Power and never exports to it contributes
exactly zero additional demand to the grid. The grid doesn't need to grow. No substation needs to


---

be upgraded. No transmission capacity needs to be reserved. No UMPA load study needs to be
commissioned. The city's entire electrical infrastructure remains exactly as it was — and the city
collects millions in tax revenue from a facility it never had to serve.


Now consider what the amendment's grid-tie requirement actually produces. The fuel cell system
that previously powered the facility in isolation is now required to interconnect. Interconnection does
not increase the amount of power those fuel cells generate — they produce the same output
regardless. It does not add generation capacity to UMPA's system. It does not help Provo Power
serve other customers. A fully utilized islanded power source running at capacity simply cannot
contribute meaningful incremental power to a grid that is already stressed. The physics do not
change because the wires are connected.


What interconnection does produce is cost: engineering studies, protection equipment,
synchronization hardware, metering infrastructure, and ongoing interconnection maintenance — all
of which must be paid for by the developer, ratepayers, or both. It adds regulatory complexity,
UMPA coordination obligations, and potential bond covenant exposure. And it eliminates the
facility's ability to operate as a grid-independent system — which is the entire basis of its business
model and customer contracts.

7. A Better Path Forward
Volition Industries supports meaningful environmental standards for data centers. We propose that
the Planning Commission consider the following alternative framework — one that achieves the
city's environmental goals without eliminating Provo's most valuable industrial development
opportunity:

Require emissions performance, not fuel source prohibition. Rather than banning islanded
power generation, require that all on-site generation meet specific NOx, PM2.5, and VOC


---

performance standards. Modern solid oxide fuel cells already exceed these thresholds. The city
captures the environmental benefit without barring the business model.


Set a 50 MW threshold for environmental review, not an absolute ban. Facilities above 50 MW
should face rigorous environmental review — air quality analysis, water use plans, emissions
modeling. This preserves city discretion without preemptively eliminating an entire category of
investor.


Preserve §14.34A.020(2) for facilities that demonstrate grid-independence for environmental
reasons. A blanket prohibition on islanded power plants ignores the environmental case for off-grid
operation: no grid transmission losses, no coal or natural gas peaker dependency, no water-
intensive cooling towers at distant generation sites.


Quantify the infrastructure cost of grid connectivity before mandating it. The staff report does
not include a capital analysis of what it would cost Provo Power to serve a 65–95 MW AI load. That
analysis should be part of the record before the Commission recommends policy that effectively
mandates grid dependency.

A Note on UMPA: Their Covenants Do Not Reach This Project
One concern that has surfaced in discussions about off-grid data centers in UMPA member cities is
whether UMPA's 2016 Bond Resolution and Power Sale Agreements impose restrictions on private,
grid-independent generation facilities. The answer, based on legal analysis and opinion of the bond
documents, is no — and this Commission should understand why.


UMPA's bond covenants govern one thing: the wholesale power purchase obligations of UMPA
member municipalities. The 2016 Bond Resolution defines 'Revenues' exclusively as payments
received from Power Sale Agreements with member cities. The rate covenants — requiring
sufficient rates to cover 110% of annual debt service — apply to power delivered through UMPA's
system to municipal utility customers.


Volition's facility does not purchase power from UMPA. It does not connect to any member city's
utility distribution system for its compute load. It does not trigger any capacity reservation obligation.
It generates its own electricity on-site via natural gas fuel cells — the same legal status as a
hospital running backup generators, a manufacturer operating cogeneration equipment, or a
telecommunications facility with fuel cell primary power. None of those facilities is subject to UMPA
bond covenants.

14.34A.010        Purpose and Objectives
The Data Center Overlay Zone (DC Overlay) is established to provide prudent development standards for data centers and
similar digital infrastructure facilities as well as any associated power generation facilities. The provisions of this zone are
intended to minimize the negative environmental effects of data centers and power generation, especially to air quality,
water use, and energy demands.

For the purposes of this Title, a “data center” is defined as a dedicated building or facility designed primarily for housing
networked servers and computing infrastructure for continuous operation. It excludes computational facilities primarily
engaged in cryptocurrency mining, blockchain validation, or other distributed ledger technology operations.

14.34A.020     Permitted Uses
The following principal uses and no others are permitted in the DC Overlay:
    (1) Data centers.;
    (2) Energy generation facilities and transmission infrastructure, subject to Utah Municipal Power Authority
        (UMPA) policies.

14.34A.030      Lot Standards
Each lot or parcel in the DC Overlay must comply with PCC Section 15.03.100 (Adequate Public Facilities).

14.34A.040    Project Plan Approval
See PCC Sections 15.03.300 and 15.03.310.

14.34A.050 Development Standards for Data Centers of All Sizes.
    (1) The DC Overlay may only be combined with Light Manufacturing (M1) and Planned Industrial
        Commercial (PIC) Zones. Development within the DC Overlay must comply with the development
        standards of the underlying zone unless otherwise specified in this Chapter.

    (2) Data centers must also comply with all requirements listed in Provo City’s Data Center Policy and
        Application Procedures as well as UMPA Data Center Policies.

(3) No data center or energy generation may be located closer than two hundred (200) feet to any school,
    park, or residential property, measured in a straight line between the closest property lines of lots on

       (a) Equipment that produces emissions in excess of state and federal base limits (or that requires

14.34A.060 Development Standards for Large Data Centers.
   (1) This section applies to all data centers with aggregate power loads exceeding fifty megawatts (50 MW),
       per UMPA Data Center Policies.

       projected load.

   (3) As part of the Zone Map Amendment application, the developers must submit a site plan that includes
       the details of the new power generation facility. The site plan must be reviewed and approved by
       UMPA.

   (4) The construction of the plant may be scaled but must be completed and operational before the data
       center’s load requirement exceeds fifty megawatts (50 MW).

   (5) The developer is responsible for all costs associated with the development, design, construction, and
       operation of the new plant. The developer is also responsible for any costs to update the local power grid
       infrastructure to accommodate the increased load and for any associated system load studies. Once the
       construction and commissioning of the power plant are complete, operation of the plant will be
       transferred to UMPA, with ownership of the plant to be negotiated.
