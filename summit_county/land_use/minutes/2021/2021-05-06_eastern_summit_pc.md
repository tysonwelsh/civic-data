3.  Public Hearing and possible action regarding the Marathon Pipeline Company, 
LLC, Above Ground Crude Oil Storage Tank Low Impact Permit, Expansion of a Non-
conforming Industrial Use on parcel SS-2276-A, located off I-80, near the 
Utah/Wyoming Border; Marathon Pipeline Company, Julius Blanco, Applicant. – 
Tiffanie Northrup-Robinson, County Planner 
 
Planner Northrup-Robinson shared the background for this application. The application 
is for an above-ground crude oil storage tank close to the Utah/Wyoming border. The tank 
will be constructed of steel with a one hundred ten (110) foot diameter and fifty (50) feet 
in height. The Eastern Summit County Development Code Section 11-6-2 allows for 
an Enlargement or Conversion Of A Nonconforming Commercial Or Industrial Use through 
a Low Impact Permit. There are two existing tanks on the site that are the same size and 
capacity as the one being applied for. The existing tanks were built in 1981 and 2004. All 
tanks help with efficient transporting of crude oil to the Salt Lake Valley. There are two 
incoming pipelines to the site. As of now, the existing infrastructure is sufficient, and no 
additional pipelines will be needed. An eighteen-foot height variance was requested and 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission

approved by the Board of Adjustment to meet the maximum height requirement. Access is 
directly off I-80. All permit requirements for Engineering and Building Department must be 
met. Plans will be updated to comply. North Summit Fire’s primary concern is that the gate 
is passable at all times. The applicant has indicated that they will modify the existing access 
to meet the Fire Districts concerns. Special standards for a Low Impact Permit for 
conversions or expansions of non-conforming commercial or industrial uses were shared 
and discussed. 
Commissioner Peterson asked why the height of the tank needs to be increased and 
asked if the increased height creates an unsightly condition. Planner Northrup-Robinson 
answered that a lower height would require a wider tank and would increase emissions. 
Given the existing site conditions, landscaping would not be appropriate. The new tank ties 
into an existing use. The proposed tank would be the same height as the existing tanks. 
Julius Blanco, the applicant stated that the tank generates vapors, with a wider tank 
generating more vapors. The smaller diameter reduces evaporation. The internal floating 
roof does help to control emissions. 
Planner Northrup-Robinson shared this application was noticed as a Public Hearing. No 
Public Comment has been received. Staff recommends the Planning Commission conduct a 
Public Hearing and forward a positive recommendation to the Community Development 
Director. 
Commissioner Benson asked if there will be any flame arrestors or other safety 
equipment in addition to the floating roof. Julius Blanco answered that the applicant will

Findings of Fact:  
1. According to the Summit County tax assessment roles, parcel SS-2276-A is owned by Beaver 
Creek Investments, LC.  
2. The tanks at this facility are owned by Marathon Pipeline LLC but the land is leased by 
Marathon from Beaver Creek Investments, LC, C Bar AMR Holdings and Murphy Ranch 
Holdings.  
3. Parcel SS-2276-A is located in the Agriculture 80 (AG-80) zone district.  
4. The lot is approximately 7.0 acres in size.  
5. The property has direct access from Interstate 80.  
6. The new proposed tank will be constructed of steel with a one hundred ten (110) foot diameter 
and fifty (50) feet in height. The tank will have a permanent cone roof as well as an internal 
floating roof and it will be used to store crude oil with a capacity of 84,000 barrels, 
approximately 3,528,000 gallons.  
7. There are two (2) existing crude oil tanks on site that have the same size, both in dimensions 
and capacity. The first tank was built in 1981 and the second tank was built in 2004.  
8. The Eastern Summit County Development Code Section 11-6-2 allows for an Enlargement Or 
Conversion Of A Nonconforming Commercial Or Industrial Use through a Low Impact Permit 
process that meets the special standards outlined in found in this section.  
9. Eastern Summit County Development Code Section 11-3-7.B states that the maximum height 
for all structures shall be thirty two feet (32') above natural grade.  
10. The proposed tank is fifty feet (50’) above natural grade.

characteristics and capabilities of the NSFD and the mutual aid agencies that may also respond in 
case of emergency.

Environmental Protection Agency's Spill Prevention, Control and Countermeasures (SPCC)

requirements for storage of materials such as crude oil.
