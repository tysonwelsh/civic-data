BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a 3rd Amendment to an existing Development
Agreement (3rd Amendment), attached as Exhibit H, for approximately 41.33 acres located
on the south side of 11400 South, at approximately 491 West 11400 South (Exhibit B).

This property is part of the overall Big Willow Creek development. This development
originally obtained approval in January 2017 for 115 dwelling units on 49.75 acres. The
approval included a Development Agreement limiting the density to a maximum of 2.3
du/ac (dwelling units per acre), and requires a variety of lot sizes from 20,000 square feet to
10,000 square feet in size. The Development Agreement can be found as Exhibit E of this
report. Preliminary plat approval for all 115 lots was approved by the Planning Commission

on July 18, 2017. Phases 1A and 1B obtained final plat approval on September 18, 2018,
with an amended plat approved for these phases on November 21, 2019.

In April 2019 the applicant amended the development agreement (1st Amendment
Development Agreement) in order to add 18.1 acres of property into the Big Willow Creek
Development. The 1st Amendment of the Development Agreement rezoned some of the
18.1 acres to the RM2 zone, but limited any development to a maximum of 9 du/ac. The
remainder of the property was rezoned to R4. The 1st Amendment to the Development
Agreement attached as Exhibit F.

On December 10, 2019 the applicant obtained a rezone and land use amendment for an
additional 4.12 acres of land located adjacent to the development. This area was not
included in the development agreement at the time of rezone, but was later added as part
of the 2nd Amendment to the Development Agreement. The 2nd Amendment to the
Development Agreement also authorized the Phase 2 plat approval and public
improvement construction to commence prior to the construction of the secondary access.
The Planning Commission approved the Phase 2 Final Subdivision plat on August 27, 2020.
The 2nd Amendment to the Development Agreement can be found as Exhibit G of this
report.

Development Agreement. The Agreement seeks to do two things, as outlined below. The 3rd
Amendment can be found in its entirety in Exhibit H of this report.

    1. The 2nd Amendment to the Development Agreement allowed Phase 2 of the
       development to obtain approval and authorized construction of public
       improvements, such as utilities and roadway construction, prior to the completion
       of the secondary access serving the development. Under the provisions of the 2nd
       Amendment to the Development Agreement, the applicant cannot record the
       subdivision plat, or obtain any building permits until the secondary access is
       constructed and accepted by the City. The applicant is asking for the same
       consideration for Phase 3. DCMC Section 17-5-030(G) limits developments to 30 lots,
       unless a second access is provided. Big Willow Creek currently has a single access
       point from 11400 South. The secondary access point will be a future connection
       through the Jensen Farms Subdivision. Phase 1A and 1B contains the 30 lots allowed
       off this one access point. Jensen Farms has been approved, and the secondary
       access improvements are under construction, but not completed. The plan is once
       the connection into Jensen Farms is completed and accepted, the Phase 2 and 3
       subdivision plats will be ready to be recorded and the applicant can begin selling
       lots and obtaining building permits right away.
    2. The City Council approved some text amendments on May 19, 2020 and November
       10, 2020 which modified the private roadway widths, alley way widths and driveway
       depth standards. The intent of the text amendments was to ensure future
       development provides adequate access, snow removal area, and parking spaces.
       The applicant is asking to develop under the previous code provisions with this
       Development Agreement. The applicant began the design process for their Phase 6
       section of Big Willow Creek prior to the City changing the code standards. If held to
       the current DCMC standards, the applicant would have to significantly alter their
       Phase 6 design. The specific provisions of code affected are listed below.
           a. DCMC 9-32-030(C)(5)(a)- alleyways were changed from requiring a 12-feet
               width from midline of right-of-way to garage door, and now requires a
               minimum of 26-foot wide alley way.
           b. DCMC 9-32-030(C)(2)- the code was changed to require a 20-foot deep
               driveway for developments with garages. Driveways are required from the
               alley ways and rights-of-way.

        C.     Procedure: All development agreements, upon proper execution, shall be

               and shall be binding on all successors in the ownership of the affected

9-32-030: DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS:

C. Building Placement And Orientation: Multiple-family projects shall have a strong relationship
   to the public realm that will enhance the safety and efficiency of the project while improving
   the character of the neighborhood.

   2. Setbacks for multiple-family structures shall be within the range of five (5’) to twenty feet
   (20') from the edge of the public or private right of way (ROW). The edge of the public or
   private right of way can be defined as the back of the sidewalk and the setback is measured
   from the building facade. Horizontal variation in setback between units shall be required.
      a. Setbacks should be established relative to existing setbacks in the vicinity.
      b. For projects with front loading garages and driveways, the garage door shall be set back
      at twenty feet (20') from the ROW to allow for tandem driveway.
      c. The setback range shall be fifteen feet (15') to thirty feet (30') from the ROW when:
          (1) The proposed structure is three (3) or more stories.
          (2) The project is adjacent to an existing or proposed collector street as shown on the
          master transportation plan.
      d. Development on corner lots shall consider the provisions of subsection 9-27-080C of
      this title in which the sightline at an intersection shall not be obstructed.

   3. Multiple-family projects with frontage on a public street shall orient units toward the
   public street.
      a. Multiple-family projects shall promote an active street frontage on all public streets.
      The creation of a second frontage road or courtyard area on the street frontage, in which
      units may be oriented toward, can also be an acceptable option for projects located on
      collector roads.
      b. Carports shall be prohibited adjacent to the public right of way.

   5. Resident parking accommodations are encouraged to be located behind multiple-family
   structures. This includes rear loaded garages with alleyways, auto courtyards and parking
   lots.
      a. Alley accessways should accommodate a width of at least twelve feet (12') from the
      midline of the ROW to the garage door in order to permit a feasible vehicle turning radius
      for garage access.

   6. Required guest parking shall be evenly distributed throughout multiple-family projects and
   easily accessible.

   7. On street parking, parallel or diagonal, is encouraged to be provided on private streets as a
   substantial portion of the project's parking requirement. This is meant to create flexible
   parking spaces for units in which residents may have more or less vehicles than what is
   required for each unit.
   8. Landscaping strips in the middle of driveways (between tire lines) should be used to break
   up expanses of impervious surface.
    9. Parking areas shall be broken into smaller parking clusters of twelve (12) spaces or less,
   using landscaping and pathways.

9-27-150: PRIVATE RIGHTS OF WAY:

  A. Maximum Number Of Accessing Lots: In any residential subdivision, a private right of
way shall provide access for no more than twenty (20) single-family lots. Private rights of way
may also be approved for multiple-family dwelling developments, consistent with chapter 32 of
this title.

  B. Width And Cross Section: The minimum right of way width and cross section design of a
private right of way shall be:
    1. Twenty feet (20') wide for two (2) to five (5) single-family residential lots;
2. Twenty six feet (26') wide for six (6) to ten (10) single- family residential lots; and




   3. Thirty-six feet (36') wide for eleven (11) to twenty (20) single-family residential lots.




    F. On Street Parking: On street parking shall be prohibited on a private right of way cross
section less than twenty six feet (26') wide. Parking may be allowed on one side of a private right
of way if the cross section is at least twenty-six feet (26') wide. Parking may be allowed on both
sides of a private right of way if the cross section is at least thirty feet (30') wide. Enforcement of
on street parking restrictions shall be the responsibility of the owners of the private right of way.

17-5-030: STREETS:

G. Dead End Streets: Dead end streets shall serve no more than thirty (30) single-family lots or
units, or one hundred (100) multi-family units, even though it may be intended that they
eventually be extended to connect with other streets in a manner which would eliminate the dead
end. Additional lots and dwellings may be served by such a street at such time as its dead end
nature is eliminated. On such temporary dead end streets, a satisfactory temporary turnaround
area and recordable easement shall be provided at the end thereof to remain and be available for
public use so long as the dead end exists. The City may require improvements to be installed in
temporary turnaround areas. The Planning Commission may approve the platting and
construction of collector and arterial streets which are dead ends.
