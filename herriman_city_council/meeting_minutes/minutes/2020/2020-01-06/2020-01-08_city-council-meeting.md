       Chief
       City Manager Brett Wood noted that this item was reviewed at the previous meeting, and nothing
       substantial had changed since that discussion.

       Councilmember Smith requested that Fire Chief Dan Petersen address the changes that were being made
       from the original interlocal agreement and the reasoning behind those changes.

       Chief Petersen said that the original interlocal agreement was adopted in 2004, and its primary purpose
       was to facilitate their separation from Salt Lake County and the creation of the new entity. At the time,
       they didn’t have many assets or participating communities. The new interlocal agreement would include
       language regarding weighted voting, new members to the board, withdrawals, and capital assets.

       In response to a question from the Council, Chief Petersen explained the process of surplusing an engine
       or other fire equipment. Funds received from the surplus would go back into the capital fund. This was
       done to avoid a spike in membership fees when new equipment was necessary. The term of the
       agreement was 25 years, with a requirement to review the agreement every five years.

       There was a discussion regarding ISO, a private organization that provides information that affects fire
       protection insurance rates for residences and businesses. Chief Petersen clarified there was nothing in
       ISO that addressed response times.

       2.2. East Herriman Water Infrastructure Discussion - Justun Edwards, Director of Public
       Works
       Public Works Director Justun Edwards said that as development continued within the East Herriman
       area, the existing water infrastructure no longer had the capacity to serve future developments within
       water pressure zones 2, 3 and above Mountain View Corridor, as outlined in the Eastern Water System
January 8, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                                 Page 3 of 16
       Source and Storage Analysis by Hansen Allen & Luce Engineering. When this analysis was received and
       reviewed by the City, it was determined not to allow further development until additional infrastructure
       was in place to supply water to the area. Herriman City contracted with Bowen, Collins and Associates
       to perform a study to determine alternative pump station and tank options that would most effectively
       serve this area. The City solicited proposals from engineering firms to perform engineering designs of
       the preferred tank alternatives, and the contract was awarded to Bowen Collins & Associates. They
       offered a full design package consisting of five components and phases, including Pre-Design phase,
       Design phase, Bidding Phase, Construction phase and Post Construction phase. The City was currently
       in the Pre-Design phase. Staff anticipated the construction to begin as early as March 2020, depending
       on the site and alignment. The project could be completed as early as April 2021.

       2.12. Discussion of the Water Impact Fee Adjustment – Justun Edwards, Public Works Director
       Public Works Director Justun Edwards stated that the City had been approached several times regarding
       a water impact fee reduction. Water impact fees were based on lot size; therefore, larger lots would
       require higher impact fees. Following the last adjustment, the City Council requested that a procedure
       be put in place that allows the fee to be reduced by 50-percent only on the portion of the property that
       was deemed unbuildable and/or where slopes are greater than 30-percent.

       The City had recently been approached by an owner, to request a reduction to the water impact fee for
       property that was recorded on the plat as unbuildable. The lot was 1.604 acres in size and located at the
       end of a cul-de-sac. The property owner intended to keep the majority of the property as native
       vegetation, which would not require the use of water for irrigation. There were slopes on the property
       with a grade of 30-percent or greater, with a natural drainage course that ran through the property. The
       full impact fee for the 1.604 acres would be $13,190. The updated impact fee calculation, accounting
       for the reduction, was $8,921.

       8.2. (Continued from December 11, 2019 meeting) Public Hearing and consideration of an
       Ordinance to approve the Parks Master Plan - Wendy Thomas, Director of Parks, Recreation
       and Events
       Director of Parks, Recreation and Events Wendy Thomas explained this item was a continuation from
       the previous meeting. Two updates were recommended to the proposed ordinance, including the
       increase of open space and a property closure.

       Mayor Watts opened the public hearing.

       Dwayne Boring, said that he was speaking as a resident of Herriman and the Vice President of UDV
       Utah. He requested that any decision be delayed until such time as any OHV trail issues in these hills
       were decided upon in a final manner. The Parks Master Plan was not consistent with the Herriman Hills
       Open Space Master Plan. The proposed master plan should address OHV trails, shooting ranges, snow
       tubing areas, recreation on the flat top area of the south mountain, and other uses as outlined in the
       Herriman Hills Open Space Master Plan.

       Councilmember Smith moved to close the public hearing. Councilmember Ohrn seconded the motion. The motion passed
       with unanimous consent of the Council.

       Councilmember Ohrn asked staff to address Mr. Boring’s concerns. Director Thomas said that OHV
       trails were identified in the proposed plan. There would also be a discussion regarding the OHV
       feasibility study when the work session reconvened later that night. She confirmed that the proposed
       plan also included plans for a disc golf course, shooting range, and tubing hills. The purpose of the
       ordinance was to update the Master Plan that was adopted in 2014. The City had experienced a lot of
       growth since that time, and the new plan needed to reflect that. She reminded the Council that the ideas
       in the Master Plan were conceptual.

       Councilmember Smith moved to approve Ordinance No. 02-2020 adopting the parks Master Plan with the following
       amendments: replace “Nicole Martin” with “Steven Shields” for District 4 on the acknowledgements page and increase
       the acres of open space on page 56 to 2,417. Councilmember Henderson seconded the motion.

       The vote was recorded as follows:
       Councilmember Jared Henderson            Aye
       Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn               Aye
       Councilmember Steven Shields             Aye
       Councilmember Clint Smith                Aye
       Mayor David Watts                        Aye

       8.3. Public Hearing to discuss the petition to vacate right-of-way parcels located along
       McCuiston Ave - Blake Thomas, City Engineer
       City Engineer Blake Thomas offered a background of the vacations along McCuiston Ave.

       Mayor Watts opened the public hearing.

       Robert Newton, asked for clarification on how the properties were acquired. He asked if any of the
       property had been taken from the property owners.

       Councilmember Ohrn moved to close the public hearing. Councilmember Henderson seconded the motion. The motion
       passed with unanimous consent of the Council.

       Engineer Thomas explained that the majority of the property was acquired by Salt Lake County via plat.
       He was not aware of any compensation from the County, but some of them were probably given to the
       County as part of the development process. When the property was annexed into Herriman City, it was
       a jurisdictional transfer. Herriman City did not receive any compensation for the road.
