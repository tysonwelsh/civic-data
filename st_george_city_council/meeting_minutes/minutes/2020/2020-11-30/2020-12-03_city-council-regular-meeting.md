PUBLIC HEARING/ADOPTION OF THE IMPACT FEES FACILITIES PLAN, IMPACT FEE
ANALYSES, AND IMPACT FEE ORDINANCE:
     Public hearing to receive input and consider approval and adoption of the
     proposed Impact Fee Facilities Plans (IFFP's), the Impact Fee Analyses
     (IFA's), and the Impact Fee Ordinance.

      Financial Planning and Budget Manager Deanna Brklacich explained the City worked
      with various consultants to update the City’s Impact Fee Analyses (IFA’s) and Impact
      Fee Facilities Plans (IFFP’s). Draft copies of the IFA’s, IFFP’s, and Ordinance have
      been available for public review as of November 24, 2020. This item is to receive
      input and consider adopting the amended ordinance; the updated IFFP’s and IFA’s
      for Culinary & Secondary Water, Local & Regional Wastewater, Storm Drain,
      Transportation, Parks & Recreation, Power, Fire/EMS, and Police; and the updated
      impact fees. There is a 90-day wait period before any impact fee increases may be
      assessed.

      Jason Burningham with Lewis Young Robertson and Burningham provided a
      PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Purposes and guiding

      Finalization. He explained fees that are adopted tonight will be effective in 90 days.

      Mayor Pike opened the public hearing.

      Mari Krashowetz with Southern Utah Home Builders Association (SUHBA) thanked
      the Council for allowing SUBHA to comment. She noted SUBHA has not had much
      time to review the information. They are not against impact fees; however, they are
      concerned with the significant proposed increase. When combined with Water
      District fees, the total impact fees will be approximately $30,000. She noted impact
      fees are not prorated by the size of the home being built. As of 2020, the median
      home price for a new home in Utah is $390,054 in St. George it is $452,726. SUHBA
      recognizes some increases are necessary especially those for Fire, Police, Energy,
      Sewer and Stormwater; SUHBA recognizes the need and supports these increases.
      She asked the Council to delay a decision on Parks, Transportation, and Water
      impact fees as their representatives would like additional time to meet with City staff
      to discuss the proposed increases. She asked if a feasibility study was done to show
      eligible funding resources to cover operation and maintenance costs for new parks.
      She also asked the following: if the general fund can sustain this - their opinion is
      that it cannot sustain the operation of all proposed parks projects; for a detailed
      report outlining the proposed parks and facilities included in the Park & Recreation
      plan; and if the community would be willing to pay for more parks by voting for a
      bond. Walking and biking trails are more popular than participation in a community
      center and are cheaper to maintain. She commented developers donate land and
      some pay for the build cost of community parks. SUHBA does support necessary
      improvements and impact fees; however, they are concerned with ever increasing
      impact fees.

      Drake Howell with Cole West Homes agreed with Ms. Krashowetz’s comments. He
      commented that is seems like the parks of the future are more trailheads that
      provide access to trails rather than the typical expansive grassy, traditional parks of

      parking available; however, when driving by a trailhead it is overrun with cars. The
      cost of a trailhead is far much less than the cost of a traditional park. He
      recommended the Council pause increasing Parks, Transportation, and Water impact

      than the full maximum allowable increase. He also asked the Council to consider a
      graduated scale of impact fees so that attainable housing could be more realistic. He

      the past six (6) years; however, material costs and land values have increased
      dramatically. There is no question that impact fees do not affect the developer’s
      ability to build and selling large homes making money; smaller lots are more difficult
      when impact fees are the same.

      there can be a similarly sized home with eighteen (18) people living in it because of
      the number of bedrooms. The best way to determine impact fees is to use an
      average. He is not sure there is any city in the state who has successfully worked
      out the differentiation on a larger home versus a smaller home.

      Mr. Burningham stated there have been many conversations regarding this issue, but

CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT:
     Consider approval of a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) to operate a bar
     establishment in the arts district overlay zone located at 2 West St. George
     Blvd #5.

      Planner Genna Goodwin presented the request for a conditional use permit for a
      proposed bar establishment in Unit 5 (the barn) of Ancestor Square. She provided a
      PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Arts District Overlay zone; Bar
      establishments in Arts District; Site plan; View from St. George Boulevard, Doorway;
      and Front of building. Staff recommends approval with a finding that the application
      requirements per 10-17 and the review criteria per 10-17(B) have been met.

      Community Development Director John Willis noted state law provides guidance
      regarding use permits. He read a statement from state law as follows: “A
      conditional use shall be approved if reasonable conditions are proposed or can be
      imposed to mitigate the reasonable anticipated detrimental effects of the proposed
      use within the applicant standards.”

      Applicants Jasher and Lisa Feellove explained they would like to create a classy,
      upscale bar in this area. They may not be open every night; however, they want to
      create an upscale experience. There are not many options for 40+ year olds to go
      for a nice date or business meeting. Mr. Feellove’s aspiration is to bring cultural
      aspects to St. George. This bar will be more of a social bar, not to go there to get
      inebriated.

      After being asked by Councilmember Randall, Ms. Goodwin explained parking
      requirements for this area. She noted a restaurant can go in this location currently.
      It is important for this area to stay tied together so it is enjoyable to walk the area.

      Councilmember Larkin commented that parking is a tricky thing and noted that
      Ancestor Square is always busy. This is one area that she would like to see people
      walk to.
