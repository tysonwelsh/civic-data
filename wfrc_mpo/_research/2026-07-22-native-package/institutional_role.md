# WFRC's Institutional Role in Utah Development & Planning

**Scope note:** Everything below is sourced from wfrc.utah.gov (the canonical host; wfrc.org 301-redirects there), le.utah.gov, and adopted WFRC documents fetched directly. Claims I could not verify against a primary source are explicitly flagged.

## 0. What WFRC is, legally

WFRC is **two overlapping things in one organization**:

1. **An Association of Governments (AOG)** — a voluntary interlocal entity organized in **March 1969** by elected officials of Davis, Salt Lake, and Weber counties (Tooele joined June 1969, Morgan 1972; Box Elder's southern urbanized portion is in the MPO area) ([detailed history](https://wfrc.utah.gov/about/detailed-history)). WFRC describes itself as one of Utah's seven AOGs established through interlocal agreements under Utah Code Title 11, Chapter 13 (Interlocal Cooperation Act) ([About Us](https://wfrc.utah.gov/about/about-us/)). *Caveat: the Title 11-13 basis is stated on WFRC's own pages; I did not locate the founding interlocal agreement itself.*
2. **The federally designated Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO)** for the **Salt Lake and Ogden–Layton urbanized areas**, designated **December 26, 1973** by Gov. Calvin Rampton, taking over urban transportation planning from UDOT ([detailed history](https://wfrc.utah.gov/about/detailed-history)). MPOs exist under **23 U.S.C. §134**; Utah Code **§72-1-208.5** defines an MPO by reference to that federal statute (full text verified via the Legislature's XML: [72-1-208.5](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title72/Chapter1/C72-1-S208.5_1800010118000101.xml)).

**Governing board:** 27 members — **21 voting** (19 local elected officials appointed by the **county councils of governments**, seats apportioned by county population, plus one each from **UDOT and UTA** — transportation agencies vote on the WFRC board) and 6 non-voting (state legislators, Governor's Office of Planning and Budget, Envision Utah, added 2008). Chair (2026): South Jordan Mayor Dawn Ramsey. The Council meets roughly five times a year (Jan/Mar/May/Aug/Oct) ([WFRC Council](https://wfrc.utah.gov/committees/wfrc-council/)). The appointment path through county COGs is a load-bearing structural fact: **cities reach WFRC through their county COG**, and WFRC's funding processes route project lists back through those COGs (see §1).

## 1. Federal MPO role: the mandated products and the money

### The product set
- **Regional Transportation Plan (RTP)** — the federally required long-range (20–30 yr) fiscally constrained plan. Current: **2023–2050 RTP, adopted May 2023**, amended several times; the **2027–2055 RTP is in development**; updated every four years ([RTP page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/regional-plans/regional-transportation-plan/), [Regional Plans](https://wfrc.utah.gov/regional-plans/)). The RTP is phased; being in **Phase 1** is a gate for programming (WFRC's own STP rules: "Major highway and transit capacity improvements must be identified in the first phase of the 2023-2050 RTP" — [FY25 Funding Programs Packet](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/FY25%20WFRC%20Funding%20Programs%20Packet.pdf), STP section).
- **Transportation Improvement Program (TIP)** — six-year program of road/transit/active-transportation projects for the two urbanized areas, updated annually. Current adopted: **2026–2031**; draft **2027–2032** out for comment through Aug 2, 2026. Amendments go through Trans Com or the Council ([TIP page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/regional-plans/transportation-improvement-program/)).
- **Unified Planning Work Program (UPWP)** — the annual federally required work program (staff activities, model development, corridor studies, socioeconomic forecasting, air-quality interagency process). Current: [FY26 UPWP](https://wfrc.utah.gov/About/UnifiedPlanningWorkProgram/FY26UPWP_final.pdf).
- **Air-quality conformity determinations** — WFRC determines conformity of the RTP and TIP with the State Implementation Plan through an interagency consultation process ([FY22 UPWP](https://wfrc.utah.gov/About/UnifiedPlanningWorkProgram/FY22%20UPWP%20Final.pdf)); each TIP cycle carries conformity memoranda (currently Memoranda 42A/42B/43a) and FHWA **"TIP/STIP Conformity Concurrence Letters"** ([TIP page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/regional-plans/transportation-improvement-program/)). *The precise current nonattainment/maintenance-area designations (PM2.5, ozone, PM10) were not verified in this pass.*
- **Public participation & Title VI** — a Public Involvement Policy (≥30-day TIP comment periods, barrier-elimination commitments) ([Public Involvement Policy](https://wfrc.utah.gov/PublicInvolvement/PublicInvolvementPolicy/WFRC%20Public%20Involvement%20Policy%20(2).pdf)) and an adopted **Title VI Plan (Oct 2023)** ([Title VI Plan](https://wfrc.utah.gov/PublicInvolvement/TitleVI/2023_WFRC_TitleVIPlan_62025.pdf)).

### The money WFRC itself programs (~$40–50M/yr)
Verified against the [FY25 Funding Programs Packet](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/FY25%20WFRC%20Funding%20Programs%20Packet.pdf) and the [funding page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/funding/transportation-project-funding/) — WFRC sub-allocates the urbanized-area shares of four federal formula programs, by population formula, split between the two urban areas:

| Program | Annual (region, approx.) | Split (Ogden-Layton / Salt Lake UZA) | Funds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **STP/STBG (urban)** | $31–35M | $10–12M / $21–23M | Federal-aid-eligible roads, transit capital, active transportation |
| **CMAQ** | $7–8M | ~$3M / ~$5.5M | Air-quality projects; scored on **emissions reduction per dollar**; "may not be used for major road widening" |
| **TAP** | $2–3M | ~$1M / ~$2M | Bike/ped, Safe Routes to School, ADA compliance |
| **Carbon Reduction Program (CRP)** | $3–4M | ~$1.4M / ~$2.6M | On-road CO₂ reduction |

**Selection pipeline (identical across the four):** Letter of Intent (late September, signed by mayor/commissioner/executive) → the LOI list is **distributed to the county Councils of Governments** → WFRC screens eligibility → full application ("Project Evaluation Concept Report" + Cost Estimation Form; CMAQ/CRP add an Emissions Analysis Form) by mid-December → staff field review + approved technical criteria → **Technical Advisory Committees** → **Trans Com** recommendation → **WFRC Council** approval (spring), programming out to the sixth year (currently FFY 2031 for STP/CMAQ) ([packet](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/FY25%20WFRC%20Funding%20Programs%20Packet.pdf)).

**What WFRC does NOT control:** state Transportation Investment Fund (TIF/TTIF) capacity money (prioritized by the **Utah Transportation Commission** under a written prioritization process developed "in consultation with … the metropolitan planning organizations" — [SB195 (2025)](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2025/bills/introduced/SB0195S03.pdf) referencing that framework), UDOT-administered statewide programs (NHPP, HSIP, freight), and UTA's FTA formula funds. **Safe Streets and Roads for All (SS4A)** is a USDOT discretionary program WFRC does not program; instead WFRC adopted a regional **Comprehensive Safety Action Plan (April 25, 2024)** expressly to make itself and its locals eligible for SS4A implementation grants ([CSAP executive summary](https://wfrc.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/WFRC-CSAP-Executive-Summary-_04-25-2024.pdf), [wfrcsafetyplan.org](https://www.wfrcsafetyplan.org/about)).

### TIP → STIP, and the UDOT PIN system
The WFRC TIP is folded into UDOT's **Statewide TIP (STIP)** — evidenced on WFRC's side by the joint FHWA "TIP/STIP conformity concurrence letters" ([TIP page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/regional-plans/transportation-improvement-program/)); the inclusion-without-change mechanics are the standard 23 U.S.C. §134(j)/23 CFR 450 framework (*standard federal law; I did not verify a UDOT page restating it*). On the UDOT side, programmed projects live in **ePM (electronic Program Management)**: a **PIN ("Project Identification Number") is a unique number defining one or more projects advertised for bidding as one package**; **Master PINs are created by UDOT's Program Development Group during STIP development**; PIN status walks **'Proposed' → 'STIP' → 'Funding'** as a project advances ([UDOT ePM help: PIN reference](https://share.udot.utah.gov/EPMhelp/ePM%20Help/desktop/Project_Management/ePM_710_PIN_Reference_Numbers/ePM_710_PIN_Tab.aspx), [ePM overview](https://www.udot.utah.gov/connect/business/business-applications/epm/), [ePM STIP projects map layer](https://maps.udot.utah.gov/arcgis/rest/services/EPM_STIPProjects/MapServer/0)). This is why WFRC TIP tables carry PIN columns — the PIN is the join key between MPO programming and UDOT delivery.

## 2. State-law roles

- **Utah Code §72-1-208.5** — defines MPOs (by reference to 23 U.S.C. 134), obligates UDOT to cooperate with MPOs in the "continuing, cooperative, and comprehensive" (3C) process, requires **contiguous MPOs to coordinate priorities and submit their plans jointly**, lets the governor + local governments redesignate MPOs that fail to coordinate, and gives MPOs access to Unemployment Insurance employer records (name, worksite, industry, employee counts) for modeling ([verified full text](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title72/Chapter1/C72-1-S208.5_1800010118000101.xml)). The joint-submission clause is the statutory root of the WFRC–MAG coordination machinery (JPAC, joint SAP policy).

- **HB462 (2022) — Station Area Plans, WFRC as certifier.** Codified at **Utah Code §10-9a-403.1** (note: the 2025 LUDMA recodification moved Title 10-9a content into Title 10-20; older citations are the pre-recodification numbering). Municipalities with **fixed-guideway transit stations (rail or BRT)** must adopt a station area plan promoting four statutory objectives (housing incl. moderate-income; sustainable environment; access to opportunities; transportation choices), containing a vision, map, **5-year implementation plan** (specific actions like zoning changes), and objectives statement; the **"applicable metropolitan planning organization" reviews the documentation and provides written certification of compliance**; deadline for existing stations was **Dec 31, 2025** (phased for cities with 4+ stations), and new stations must comply **before transit service begins**; one 12-month MPO-certified extension is allowed ([FindLaw text of 10-9a-403.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ut/title-10-utah-municipal-code/ut-code-sect-10-9a-403-1/), [enrolled HB462](https://le.utah.gov/~2022/bills/hbillenr/HB0462.htm)). In practice: cities submit adopted plans ≥2 weeks before an RGC meeting; staff review → **Regional Growth Committee** → **WFRC Council formally certifies or denies** at its Jan/Mar/May/Aug/Oct meetings ([SAP certification program page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/programs/station-area-planning-hb462/)). Scale (March 2025 joint WFRC/MAG progress report): **127 station areas / 95 eligible stations Wasatch-Front-wide; 38 certified, 49 in preparation, 20 pre-dating HB462, 20 not begun; ~34,000 housing units planned in the first 33 certified SAPs; 79/127 station areas received technical assistance** ([SAP Progress Update, March 2025](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/Station%20Area%20Planning%20Certification%20and%20Technical%20Assistance/SAP%20Progress%20Update%202025%20V2%20MARCH%202025.pdf)). By the later WFRC announcement, the cumulative count reached **72 certified plans enabling 75,000+ homes near transit**, and homes in Wasatch Choice centers had doubled from 75,000 (2010) to 150,000 ([news release](https://wfrc.utah.gov/mayors-and-planners-certify-14-more-station-area-plans-contributing-to-75000-new-homes-near-transit/)). **Important negative finding:** neither §10-9a-403.1 nor enrolled HB462 attaches a direct funding penalty to SAP non-certification — the statute's teeth are the deadlines, the MPO gatekeeping, and adjacent incentives (HTRZ value capture, TA money). Whether later HTRZ amendments (SB84 2023 / SB208 2024 / SB26 2025) made a certified SAP a formal HTRZ prerequisite **I could not verify** — HB462 as enrolled only lists creating an HTRZ as one MIH strategy, and WFRC's [HTRZ page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/programs/htrz/) (which shows WFRC's role: eligibility mapping + bill analysis) doesn't state it.

- **SB34 (2019) — moderate-income housing ↔ transportation funding.** Municipalities must adopt MIH elements in their general plans and report implementation progress; **non-compliant/non-reporting cities are ineligible for programming from the state Transportation Investment Fund, including the Transit TIF** ("the executive director of the Department of Transportation may not program funds from the Transportation Investment Fund of 2005, including the Transit Transportation Investment Fund" — [enrolled HB462](https://le.utah.gov/~2022/bills/hbillenr/HB0462.htm), which tightened the SB34 regime). Reports go to the state housing division **and to the association of governments in which the city is located** (10-9a-408 lineage: [Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/utah/2010/title10/10_08028.html); [current 10-9a-408 summary](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/utah/ut-code/utah_code_10-9a-408)) — i.e., WFRC receives them in its AOG capacity and plays a technical-assistance/coordination role, not a certification role ([WFRC SB34 page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/public-involvement/governmental-affairs-2/sb34-mih-planning/)). Note **HB436 (2026)** paused MIH progress reporting for a year ([DWS reporting page](https://jobs.utah.gov/housing/affordable/moderate/reporting/)).

- **Corridor preservation.** WFRC's role is planner/gatekeeper, not fund-holder: it "identifies priority transportation corridors for which corridor preservation activities are needed, participates in UDOT's Revolving Loan Fund Corridor Preservation Advisory Council and Local Corridor Preservation Fund processes, and assists local governments with corridor preservation tools" ([FY26 UPWP summary](https://wfrc.org/Committees/Budget/2025/02_May8/7%202025Apr30_Final%20Summary%20FY26%20UPWP.pdf)); **inclusion in the RTP is a qualifying condition for local corridor preservation funds** (e.g., Salt Lake County's — same UPWP source; the funds themselves are the state Marda Dillree revolving fund [§72-2-117](https://law.justia.com/codes/utah/2006/title72/72_02018.html) and the county-level Local Corridor Preservation Fund [§72-2-117.5], with county COGs prioritizing — *the COG-prioritization mechanics of 117.5 were not directly verified from statute text*).

- **Wasatch Front Economic Development District (WFEDD) / CEDS.** WFRC operates the federally designated Economic Development District (EDA designation **August 2014** per the [FY25 packet](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/FY25%20WFRC%20Funding%20Programs%20Packet.pdf); WFRC's history page says 2013 — **discrepancy flagged**, the WFEDD program sheet's "August 2014" is the more specific claim). It maintains the 5-year **Comprehensive Economic Development Strategy** with annual updates; inclusion of a project in the CEDS (reviewed quarterly) is the doorway to EDA grant funding ([Regional Plans](https://wfrc.utah.gov/regional-plans/), packet WFEDD sheet).

- **CDBG Small Cities.** Since 1983 WFRC administers the state-delegated CDBG small-cities program for **Morgan, Tooele, and Weber counties (excluding entitlement Ogden; Davis withdrew to self-administer)** — ~$1M/yr; project rating/ranking is done by a **Regional Review Committee of two elected officials per county, nominated by the county COGs**, recommending to the state Housing and Community Development Division ([FY25 packet CDBG sheet](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/FY25%20WFRC%20Funding%20Programs%20Packet.pdf), [detailed history](https://wfrc.utah.gov/about/detailed-history)). WFRC also assists with **Community Impact Board (CIB)** mineral-lease loan/grant applications for Tooele and Morgan communities (packet CIB sheet).

## 3. Wasatch Choice Vision

**Wasatch Choice** is the region's shared land-use + transportation + economic-development vision — "our communities' shared vision for coordinated transportation investments, development patterns, and economic opportunities," expressed as a **Vision Map of anticipated 2050 land uses plus the transportation system** ([wasatchchoice.org](https://wasatchchoice.org/)). Lineage: Wasatch Choice 2040 vision adopted 2005; **Wasatch Choice 2050 adopted 2016** (per [detailed history](https://wfrc.utah.gov/about/detailed-history)) with the refined Vision Map **endorsed by the Council May 2018** after sub-region workshops with city planners ([endorse-vision page](https://wfrc.org/vision-plans/regional-transportation-plan/2019-2050-regional-transportation-plan/endorse-vision/)).

Its organizing device is the **centers framework** — a hierarchy of urban/city/neighborhood centers where growth, mixed use, and transit investment concentrate; both the RTP and the Vision key their project evaluation to these centers ([future-vision page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/regional-plans/regional-transportation-plan/in-progress-rtp/future-vision/)). **The vision is voluntary for land use** — WFRC has no zoning authority — so implementation runs through levers:

- **The RTP is the Vision's transportation element** ([2023-2050 RTP](https://old.wfrc.org/vision-plans/regional-transportation-plan/2023-2050-regional-transportation-plan/)) — transportation money follows the centers.
- **TLC grants** (below) fund the city plans/codes that operationalize centers locally.
- **Toolkits**: "**Implementing Centers**" (assessing barriers to higher-density mixed-use centers and strategies to overcome them) and "**Envisioning Centers**" (a guided small-area scenario-planning process), plus map/data resources including a **summary of designated land uses in general plans across the MPO boundary** — i.e., WFRC actively tracks how city general plans align with the Vision ([tools & resources](https://wasatchchoice.org/resources/), [Implementing Centers PDF](https://wasatchchoice.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/implementing-centers-5-2023.pdf)).
- **SAP objectives are expressly "consistent with the Wasatch Choice Vision"** ([SAP Progress Update](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/Station%20Area%20Planning%20Certification%20and%20Technical%20Assistance/SAP%20Progress%20Update%202025%20V2%20MARCH%202025.pdf)) — HB462 effectively gave one slice of the voluntary vision statutory force.

## 4. Grant/TA programs that shape city planning

- **Transportation and Land Use Connection (TLC)** — the flagship planning-money program: a partnership of **WFRC, Salt Lake County, UDOT, and UTA** (the current program webpage lists **GOPB** as a partner — the partner set has evolved; both sourced: [FY25 packet](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/FY25%20WFRC%20Funding%20Programs%20Packet.pdf) vs [TLC page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/programs/transportation-land-use-connection/)). Program size **~$1.9–2.1M/yr**; lifetime **$15.5M, 180 projects, 62 communities**; **local match minimum 6.77%**, scaled by jurisdiction size. Funds **plans, studies, ordinance/code updates, small-area plans, TOD/first-last-mile feasibility, public engagement — explicitly NOT land acquisition, engineering, or capital**. Cycle: LOI September → application December → awards ~March; LOI lists circulate through the county COGs and a partner selection committee picks projects ([TLC page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/programs/transportation-land-use-connection/)). Typical individual awards run in the tens of thousands to low hundreds of thousands (2018 examples: SLC $100k street typology, WVC $75k ATP, South/West Jordan $90k — [Building Salt Lake](https://buildingsaltlake.com/wfrc-awards-over-1-million-in-grants-to-area-communities/); 2025 cycle: **$1.5M across 13 projects** + $200k local match — [2025 awards coverage](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/6620323/WFRC-awards-15-million-through-TLC-program-to-13-community-planning-projects)).
- **Station Area Plan Technical Assistance** — a **$5M pot funded by the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity**, programmed by WFRC + MAG with UTA, **rolling, first-come-first-served**; as of March 2025, **25 awards / $3.722M allocated** ([FY25 packet](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/FY25%20WFRC%20Funding%20Programs%20Packet.pdf), [SAP Progress Update](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/Station%20Area%20Planning%20Certification%20and%20Technical%20Assistance/SAP%20Progress%20Update%202025%20V2%20MARCH%202025.pdf)). WFRC also runs **ordinance audits** as complementary TA ([TLC page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/programs/transportation-land-use-connection/)).
- Plus the CDBG/CIB/WFEDD programs described in §2 — together WFRC brands itself as administering **nine funding programs** ([FY25 packet, p.1](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/FY25%20WFRC%20Funding%20Programs%20Packet.pdf)).

The institutional significance: for most Wasatch Front suburbs, **WFRC money is how general-plan updates, station area plans, and zoning-code rewrites get paid for** — which gives the regional vision a soft but pervasive editorial presence in local planning documents.

## 5. Governance + ecosystem

- **Trans Com (Transportation Coordinating Committee)** — the short-range policy body; recommends the TIP and amendments to the Council; fed by area **Technical Advisory Committees** ([Trans Com](https://wfrc.utah.gov/committees/transportation-coordinating-committee/), [TACs](https://wfrc.utah.gov/committees/transportation-coordinating-committee-tac/)).
- **Regional Growth Committee (RGC)** — long-range transportation + land use; hears SAP certifications and Wasatch Choice work; two TACs (Salt Lake area, Ogden-Layton area) composed mainly of **city and county planners** ([RGC](https://wfrc.utah.gov/committees/regional-growth-committee/), [RGC TAC](https://wfrc.utah.gov/committees/regional-growth-committee-tac/)).
- **Active Transportation Committee** — established May 2012 by RGC jointly with MAG; advises Trans Com on bike/ped programming in the TIP ([ATC](https://wfrc.org/committees/active-transportation-committee/)).
- **JPAC (Joint Policy Advisory Committee)** — created 2002 by WFRC + MAG with UDOT and UTA to keep plans consistent across the Salt Lake/Utah County line; **expanded 2007 to include Dixie and Cache MPOs**, making it the statewide MPO coordination forum; its coordination "leads to the development of Utah's Unified Transportation Plan" ([JPAC](https://wfrc.org/committees/joint-policy-advisory-committee/), [JPAC MOA](https://wfrc.org/Committees/JointPolicyAdvisory/HelpfulLinksDownloads/JPAC%20MOA.pdf)).
- **Utah's Unified Transportation Plan** — the nationally cited joint product of UDOT, UTA, and the four MPOs (WFRC, MAG, Cache, Dixie): a single fiscally-framed statewide 30-year needs picture stitching the four RTPs plus rural UDOT plans ([UTP summary](https://wfrc.utah.gov/VisionPlans/UnifiedPlan/UTPSummary.pdf), [unifiedplan.org](https://unifiedplan.org/what-is-the-unified-transportation-plan/)). This is the shared analytic basis on which the Legislature has repeatedly acted (transportation funding bills, TTIF).
- **Legislative advocacy** — WFRC runs a formal Government Affairs function: a public **bill tracker** where WFRC takes support/neutral/oppose positions "established by Council members who choose to participate in informal discussions held during the legislative session," annual legislative wrap-ups, and policy work spanning transportation, land use, housing, economic development, parks, and air quality ([Government Affairs](https://wfrc.utah.gov/engage/government-affairs/), [Bill Tracker](https://wfrc.utah.gov/bill-tracker/), [2026 session wrap-up](https://wfrc.utah.gov/2026-legislative-session-wrap-up-explore-wfrcs-resources/)). WFRC (with MAG/ULCT/UAC) was materially involved in shaping SB34/HB462-era housing-transportation legislation — that influence is visible in its own bill summaries, though "who drafted what" is not verifiable from these sources.

## 6. How things flow, concretely

**(a) A road/transit project: RTP → TIP → construction**
1. **Into the RTP**: need identified via travel-demand modeling on WFRC's socioeconomic forecasts + local/UDOT/UTA input; project placed in a phase of the fiscally constrained RTP through the RGC/Council adoption process ([RTP page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/regional-plans/regional-transportation-plan/)). Being in **RTP Phase 1** is required before WFRC will program a major capacity project with STP funds ([FY25 packet](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/FY25%20WFRC%20Funding%20Programs%20Packet.pdf)); RTP inclusion also unlocks county corridor-preservation money for right-of-way ([FY26 UPWP summary](https://wfrc.org/Committees/Budget/2025/02_May8/7%202025Apr30_Final%20Summary%20FY26%20UPWP.pdf)).
2. **Into the TIP**: sponsor city/county/UDOT/UTA files a September LOI → COG circulation → December concept report + cost estimate (+ emissions form for CMAQ/CRP) → staff field review & technical scoring → TAC → Trans Com → **Council approves the new six-year TIP** (with a ≥30-day public comment window and an air-quality conformity determination) ([funding page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/funding/transportation-project-funding/), [TIP page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/regional-plans/transportation-improvement-program/)).
3. **Delivery**: the TIP rolls into UDOT's STIP (FHWA/FTA concurrence letters); the project gets a **PIN in UDOT's ePM** (status Proposed → STIP → Funding), through which design, funding, scheduling, and — for local sponsors — UDOT's administration of federal-aid requirements are managed to obligation and construction ([UDOT ePM](https://www.udot.utah.gov/connect/business/business-applications/epm/), [PIN reference](https://share.udot.utah.gov/EPMhelp/ePM%20Help/desktop/Project_Management/ePM_710_PIN_Reference_Numbers/ePM_710_PIN_Tab.aspx)). *(That local federal-aid projects are administered through UDOT is standard practice I did not separately verify on a UDOT page.)*

**(b) A city land-use plan: TLC grant → adoption → SAP certification → rezone**
1. City wins **TLC or SAP-TA funding** for a small-area plan/general-plan update/code rewrite (Sept LOI → Dec application → March award, or rolling for SAP TA) ([TLC page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/programs/transportation-land-use-connection/)).
2. Consultant/city produce the plan with WFRC/UTA/UDOT at the table (Sandy's five SAPs folded directly into its general-plan RFP — [SAP Progress Update p.3](https://wfrc.utah.gov/Programs/Station%20Area%20Planning%20Certification%20and%20Technical%20Assistance/SAP%20Progress%20Update%202025%20V2%20MARCH%202025.pdf)).
3. City council **adopts** the plan (WFRC certifies only adopted plans, submitted ≥2 weeks before an RGC meeting).
4. **RGC review → WFRC Council certifies** (or denies) at a Jan/Mar/May/Aug/Oct meeting ([SAP program page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/programs/station-area-planning-hb462/)).
5. City then executes the SAP's statutory **5-year implementation plan** — the rezones/code amendments the statute expects ("specific measures like zoning modifications" — [10-9a-403.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ut/title-10-utah-municipal-code/ut-code-sect-10-9a-403-1/)); the certified plan area is also where value-capture tools (HTRZ within ⅓-mile of FrontRunner / ¼-mile of TRAX-BRT stations, approved by the state HTRZ Committee under 63N-3-6xx) and further TA concentrate ([WFRC HTRZ page](https://wfrc.utah.gov/programs/htrz/)).
