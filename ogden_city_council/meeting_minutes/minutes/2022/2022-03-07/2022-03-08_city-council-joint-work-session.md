The purpose of the joint work session was to review the Dinosaur Park Foundation Annual Report; receive training
regarding Cyber Security; review Fiscal Year 2022 Second Quarter Capital Improvement Plan Report; review the Fiscal Year
2021 Annual Grants Report; discuss the Proposed Rezone — 25th Street and Porter Avenue; discuss Council/Board Business
pertaining to the City Flag Project; and hear Council/Board Comments.

Dinosaur Park Foundation Annual Report

Dinosaur Park Foundation Board Chairman Neville used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the annual
report for the Dinosaur Park Foundation. The presentation touched on topics such as accomplishments over the past year;
attendance reports; memberships; total income and expenses at the Park; new exhibits at the Museum; events and outreach efforts
in 2021; 2022 plans; volunteer support; and private donors. He thanked the Mayor and Council for their support and several
Council members congratulated him, the Foundation Board, and Park staff for their efforts at the Park, especially throughout the
COVID-19 pandemic.

Council Training

Cyber Security: Information Technologies (IT) Manager Lefgren provided the Council with training regarding cyber
security.

At the conclusion of the training there was brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Lefgren regarding various
security measures employed in the City and broadband speed in the community.

Fiscal Year 2022 Second Quarter Capital Improvement Plan Report

Comptroller Stout presented the quarterly Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) report for the second quarter of Fiscal Year
(FY) 2022; highlights of the report included projects funded by Department; the status of active CIP projects; total funding
amounts remaining as of December 31, 2021; areport on completed projects; and an update on the CIP and Enterprise Fund bond
draws to date.

Fiscal Year 2021 Annual Grants Report

Senior Accountant Jensen reviewed the Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 Annual Grants Report. The report outlined the
expenditures relating to federal, state, and local grant funds as of June 30, 2021. The City managed more than $36 Million in
various grant funds during the fiscal year and expended more than $26 Million during FY2021. Federal Covid-19 funding
accounted for $8.7 Million of those expenditures.

Discussion among the Council and Accounting staff centered on the general grant writing efforts of various Departments
of the City.

Proposed Rezone — 25th Street and Porter Avenue Update

Council Policy Analyst Watkins reported that on February 15, 2022, the Council considered a rezone request and a zone
text amendment for a project located on the southeast corner of 25" Street and Porter Avenue. The items were ultimately tabled
until March 15, 2022, so council staff could resolve a few issues. The issues included the following: A member of the public
stated that notifications of the meeting were not sent according to state law. Council staff looked into notification records and
found that there were not any issues in the notification process. Second was that Planning Manager Montgomery noted that the
maximum height language in the rezone ordinance was incorrect; this error has been corrected. Additionally, Council staffnoted
that there were issues with the notation and organization of the rezone ordinance, and this has also been corrected.

The Council discussed noticing policies and the various reasons that notices may have been returned to the City as
undeliverable, after which Mr. Montgomery summarized additional adjustments to the architecture of the project since the
Council’s last review. The Council ultimately concluded they were comfortable considering action on project during their March
15, 2022, meeting.

Council Business

City Flag Project: Council Policy Analyst Watkins reported on the Ogden City Youth Council’s efforts to design a City
flag; the Youth Council participated in Local Officials Day, where there was a State Flag Design competition. Out of the 10
largest cities in Utah, Ogden is the only City without a flag and the Youth Council became interested in working on a City flag
project. Initial planning efforts on the project began in February 2022 and the Youth Council will host school competitions
throughout the month of March, with community wide competitions to follow throughout April and May 2022. A design review
committee will review recommendations in July 2022 and propose a final flag design to the Council in August 2022. He then
presented sample flags from other communities throughout Ogden and facilitated discussion among the Council regarding their
desires for a City flag.
