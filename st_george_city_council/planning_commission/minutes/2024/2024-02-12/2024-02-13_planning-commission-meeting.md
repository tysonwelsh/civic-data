   1. River Crossing Master Sign Planned Development Amendment – PUBLIC HEARING: Ryan Cain,
      representing YESCO, is requesting approval to modify the Master Signage Plan for the River Crossing
      commercial development. This property is located east of River Road and south of George Washington
      Blvd. The applicant is River Crossing/YESCO. Case No. 2024-PDA-003 (Staff – Mike Hadley)

       Mike Hadley presented the following:

       Mike Hadley – Mike showed examples of the signs they want and where they would like to place the
       signs. The difference between the B-2 and the B-3 signs is that the ground is lower, so the sign is 10 ft
       higher to compensate for that. The biggest ask of these different pylon signs is, our codes allows a
       maximum of 15 ft if the building is 18 ft or taller, if the building is shorter then it would only allow a 10
       ft max.

       Commissioner Chapman – Does the sign code have a maximum of the amount of signs you could have
       on a property?

       Mike Hadley – Yes, but they have enough frontage that they would actually be able to put a pylon sign on
       each parcel. They are not proposing as many signs as they could.

       Commissioner Draper – I am wondering why they would be asking for 18 ft instead of the 15 ft? I would
       think less is more with signs.

       Mike Hadley – That would be a great question for the applicant.

      Commissioner Rogers – Can you point out which sign is 28 ft?

      Carol Winner – I want to clarify that they are trying to get a sign package. If they were not trying to get a
      sign package they would only get a monument sign, not a pylon sign.

      Ryan Cain – These signs are by definition a monument sign in nature. The body of the sign is monolithic
      in nature. Each parcel would allow a monument sign. We are only allowing for 191 sq ft of signage. We
      are asking for 18 ft of signage because we have reduced the amount. Only 4 of our signs are taller than

      Commissioner Chapman – He is saying that today they could go 15 ft, is that true?

      Carol Winner – Yes, that is correct.

      Commissioner Chapman – If the building is 2 stories?

      Carol Winner – Yes, if the building is 18 ft or taller.

      Trevor Einerson – Building H which already has a building permit is 27 ft high. We don’t have an issue
      with the 15 ft sign, we would concede on that. I’m sure the digital will be a concern with the property
      owners here today and we would concede on that.

      Ryan Cain – The lighting is indirect and halo lighting.

      Trevor Einerson – Indirect lighting here, each of the letters are going to be the same color. A taillight is
      brighter than what you will see on this lighting.

      Chair Anderson opened the public hearing.

      Ken Pucklik – I spent 10 years on the Hillside Review Board. We maintained the quality of life and that
      is kind of the issue here. We got the packet on Friday afternoon. The sign plan does not align with the
      City purpose. The ordinance states that it seeks to limit driver distraction with excessive signage. The
      pedestrians and bike riders in the area that are drawn to there because of beautiful nature trails, it just
      distracts from the area. There are beautiful views there. The excessive height and number of pylon signs
      detract from the beauty the City has tried to preserve. This is not located next to a freeway off ramp.

   2. Title 10 Chapter 23 Landscape Standards, and Title 10 Chapter 17A – 16 Golf Course, Specific
      Standards Zoning Regulation Amendment – PUBLIC HEARING: Carol Winner, representing the
      City of St. George is requesting approval to amend portions of the city code to add additional provisions
      for the purpose of improving the water conservation efforts in the City of St. George. The applicant is the
      City of St. George. Case No. 2024-ZRA-001 (Staff – Carol Winner)

      Carol Winner presented the following:

      Carol Winner – Back in 2022 we did the overhaul of all our regulations to make it more waterwise. These
      are some that we wanted to update. No lawn will be allowed within 10 ft of a roadway. All non-single-
      family development shall have separate water meters.

      Scott Taylor – This is in rare occasions when we don’t have secondary irrigation available, then they
      would have a second culinary meter.

      Carol Winner – The one about the golf courses are they are a Permitted with Standards use. They will
      need to provide an annual water budget to the water department.

      Commissioner Fisher – What happens if they exceed their budget?

      Scott Taylor – Most of them have water shares. They are allowed to use it, but we have really good
      relationships with them, and they are really proud of their water conservation.

      Chair Anderson – Are there any golf courses using culinary water now?
