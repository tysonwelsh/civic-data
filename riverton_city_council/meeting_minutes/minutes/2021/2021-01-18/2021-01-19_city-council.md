Discussion Items

         Discussion of Agreement with Waste Management, Blake Leonelli, Manager, and
         Public Section Solutions and Frank Santiago - David Brickey, City Manager

City Manager David Brickey explained that Waste Management would be presenting their slides
and there was not a need for comments and asked the Council should consult with Ryan Carter,
City Attorney if they had any questions.

Blake Leonelli thanked the Council for being willing to listen to the presentation and expressed
his desire for an open dialogue. He explained that Waste Management has seen that recycling
has a diversion rate of 11% the previous year and it is roughly 8% this year. Mr. Leonelli said
that recycling efforts had made a difference in the community. He explained that the diversion
rate could be a result of the pandemic because people were staying home more and creating
more trash.

Mayor Staggs asked Mr. Leonelli to clarify the diversion rate from the first slide he presented.

Mr. Leonelli clarified that the diversion rate was 10.8% and is now down to 8.3%, diversion,
meaning the amount of trash that is being diverted from the landfill because people are
recycling. Mr. Leonelli talked about the increased costs they have had due to COVID-19. He
explained that Waste Management puts safety first and has provided additional PPE to their
drivers and established other programs for their employees to provide wage guarantees and
assistance with child and elderly care.

Mr. Leonelli explained that they were interested in having the difficult conversation with Riverton
City regarding the contract that was entered and the future of that contract. He explained that
Waste Management has had a substantial increase in actual costs, which is related to the local
hauling site and the growing wages. He asked that the City consider two different options. The
first option being that the City work with Waste Management to adjust pricing and pass this on
to the citizens. The second option would be for Riverton City to do an RFP and look for other
options outside of Waste Management.

Council Member Wells asked when the contract with Waste Management began.

Mr. Leonelli explained that the initial contract was five years with an additional five year option to
renew and that the City is seven and one-half years into the contract.

Mayor Staggs asked if Waste Management was having these conversations with other cities
and if the contract included both garbage and recycling.

Ms. Garn explained that Riverton City has decided that the risk is not something they are willing

David Brickey reported that UDOT is planning to shut down 12600 South east to west at
Bangerter for the next 6-7 months, which will put a lot of pressure on the side roads in Riverton

issues. Mr. Brickey explained that Trace Robinson had good news to report about the sidewalk

Mayor Staggs talked about the Southwest Vision priorities for building a freeway to freeway
connection from Bangerter to Mountain View Corridor. He explained that the current plan shows
13400 South as the street for this connection but knows that the recommendation from staff is
12600 South and that 12600 South makes more sense from a logistics perspective because of
the Mountain View Village.

Councilmember McDougal asked why the Southwest Vision is considering 13400 South over
12600 South and whether they have traffic studies to support their plans.

Trace Robinson explained that 13400 South option originally came from an engineer about six
years ago and at that time we expressed that this would not be a good option because of
Mountain View Village. Riverton City has been telling them for four years that 13400 South is
not a good option but it continues to show up on their plans. Herriman City has provided support
to Riverton for 12600 South to be the connection road because that is the road coming straight
out of Olympia Hills.

Councilmember McDougal asked Mr. Robinson to clarify that Herriman wanted it on 12600
South or 13400 South. Mr. Robinson clarified that Herriman City supports the freeway to
freeway connection being built on 12600 South.

In response to Councilmember McDougal regarding traffic studies, Mr. Robinson said that there
are traffic studies that have been done but they vary. He said that there have been two studies
done. One of the studies done by Ryan Hales shows that with the Olympia Hills traffic, we
could have 80,000 vehicles a day on 12600 South where 13400 South was in the 70,000
vehicles per day range. Mr. Robinson stated that 12600 South actually showed a higher vehicle
count than 13400 South in the future.

Councilmember Wells said that he wanted to address the 12600 South detour for the next 6-7
months and mentioned that he had been posting on social media to let people know. He
wanted to know if 13400 South dead ends at 7000 West and wanted to know if anyone has
acquired that property for future use. Mr. Robinson said he did not know if it had been acquired
by anyone but could find out. He said that there are discussions of U11 going to 13400 South
but that the preference would be to tie it into 12600 South because 13400 South is not a road
that goes all the way to the I-15 freeway.

Mr. Robinson explained that the traffic concern in Herriman is the reason why Riverton City has
built 13200 South and is providing a connection to Old Liberty Way. This will be completed by
the end of summer and should help with traffic.

Councilmember Buroker expressed her support for the connection at 12600 South but said that
maybe UDOT is considering 13400 South as a better option because of the expansion of the
prison in Bluffdale.

Councilmember McDougal said that looking forward, UDOT is going to have to purchase right of
way to build the freeway to freeway connection and wanted to know who would bear that cost
and when those decisions would be made.

Mr. Robinson explained that all of these plans are in the future 2050-2060 plan. He said that
Riverton City has bought enough right of way to expand 12600 South.

Mayor Staggs said that Phase 1 projects are through 2030. He is hoping that with the
transportation bond, if it happens, then some of the large projects can be put on the radar and
that we can move some of these projects up because it would be better planning. Herriman City
said that they would be supportive of whatever direction that Riverton wants to go and 12600
seems more likely for this to happen.

Presentations/Recognitions

    Riverton Police Department Report - Chief Hutson

Chief Hutson said that they have continued to battle COVID in their ranks through the month of
December, which has challenged manpower because they have been required to quarantine
some members to prevent mass spread.

He explained that they are continuing to see higher than normal numbers of vehicle burglaries in
the city and an associated dramatic increase in fraud as perpetrators are stealing credit cards
from vehicles and immediately utilizing the cards to commit fraud before the victim even realizes
the card is missing. It seems the suspects have realized victims are immediately canceling
cards and they are making a concerted effort to quickly “cash in” before they are rendered
useless. This new strategy accounts for a majority of the fraud cases reported during the
month.

He said that Riverton has had several citizens fall victim to a variety of phone scams which
encourage them to purchase gift cards and provide the number and pin of the card over the
phone. He said that one victim sold a trailer and the buyer sent her a check for much more than
the asking price, then asked the victim to refund the excess amount. Of course, the original
check ended up having no money associated with the account.

Chief expressed that he was extremely thankful that Officer Snell quickly returned to work with
no significant injuries after his patrol vehicle was struck by an impaired driver on New Year’s
Eve.

Councilmember Buroker asked Chief Hutson to provide more detail on the COVID vaccination
process. Chief Hutson said that the process was great. The police department went to the Salt
Lake County buildings and they were asked for a copy of their insurance card and then received
either the Moderna or Pfizer vaccine. He said that five officers did not receive the vaccine
because they already had COVID-19 and that of the remaining officers, 85% received the
vaccine.
