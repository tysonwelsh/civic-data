   10.1. Discussion regarding encroachments on City property - Nathan Cherpeski, City Manager & Blake
          Thomas, Community Development Director
City Manager Cherpeski indicated the City owned or had interest in numerous properties throughout the
community. The interests ranged from fee title ownership to easements granted for specific purposes. Staff
identified 84 instances of encroachment by private citizens upon public property and easements. These
encroachments ranged between a few hundred square feet to a quarter-acre. Some instances involved fencing
and others had structures built on the public property. He presented a policy which would provide staff direction
on how to process a request for an encroachment. He suggested in rare instances, it might be in the best interest
of the City to allow the encroachment; however, the majority of situations would require property owners to
remove their private encroachments on the public property. The following were the guidelines set forth in the
policy:
    1. Determine the nature of the City’s legal interest in the property: fee title, dedication, or easement. In
         some cases, the City may not have the right to allow an encroachment.
    2. Determine if granting an encroachment will harm the City or, conversely, be of benefit to the City. In
         making this determination, City Staff will consider the impact of an encroachment to other residents’
         access/use of the property.
    3. In the event City Staff believe granting a revocable encroachment permit would be in the best interest
         of the City and its residents, City Staff shall create written findings articulating what that benefit is and
         why they believe an encroachment is in the best interest of the City and its residents.
    4. City Staff will present the findings to Council for concurrence at a City Council Regular Meeting
    5. If Council concurs, the property owner will execute, and the City record said encroachment (standard
         form prepared by the City) against the property with the County Recorder. The permit shall run with the
         property.
August 25, 2021 – City Council Meeting Minutes                                    Page 10 of 15
   6. If, in the sole discretion of the City Council, it would be beneficial for the City and the property owner,
       the City may elect not to require that improvements be removed.

In the event a property owner encroached on City property without consent, staff would be authorized to
exercise all legal means to have such encroachment removed and property restored to the public. Any deviations
from this policy would be at the discretion of the City Council.

Councilmember Smith moved to approve Resolution No. R30-2020 adopting Council Policy LP-082-001 governing the issuance

    10.4. Discussion and consideration of a resolution approving a Master Development Agreement
         policy - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
Attorney Andrizzi indicated he modified the policy document based on comments received from Council during
their previous conversations. Attorney Andrizzi highlighted the following changes:
    • Section 2.2. Scope. This policy shall apply to all requests for development agreements in the city
        regardless of size or potential application (commercial, residential, etc.). The City Council may waive the
        requirements of this policy for developments smaller than 5 acres in size.
    • Section 2.3. Required Process. Each request for a development agreement shall be initiated by the
        submission of a completed development agreement application (the “Application”). Each Application
        shall be submitted to the Community Development Director. The Community Development
        Director will then forward the Application to the City Attorney who will draft the Development
        Agreement in collaboration with the Working Groups and based upon the approved Council
        requirements for development agreements identified in Section 2.3. Once the City Attorney has drafted
        the development agreement based upon the parameters of this policy, the Council shall convene a
        meeting to discuss and negotiate additional specific terms and conditions of the draft development
        agreement. The proposed development agreement shall then be delivered to the Planning Commission
        for a public hearing and recommendation to the City Council pursuant to State Code and City
        ordinances.
    • Section 2.4. Working Groups. Upon an application being made to the City, the Community
        Development Director shall suggest one or more Working Groups to assist the City Attorney in the
        drafting of a development agreement. The Working Group(s) shall include relevant City Staff
        (engineering, planning, legal, public safety, etc.) and may include no more than two members of Council.
        The Community Development Director shall recommend the number and membership of the Working
        Group(s) to Council at the next available meeting after an application for a development agreement is
        made. The Council may amend or waive this requirement if it determines that such Working Group(s)
        would not be necessary for the proposed development agreement.
    • Section 2.5.4. [Agreement Requirements] Loss of units for land that is developed as a church or school
        or for any use that is related thereto that replaces any form of residential units. The loss of units for that
        area shall be equal to the maximum density multiplied by the acreage of the church/school. For example,
        if a school is constructed on 10 acres and the maximum density is 5 units/acre, the project as a whole
        would lose 50 units.

    10.6. Discussion to Review Modifications to Title 12 of the Floodplain Ordinance - Jonathan Bowers,
         City Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers indicated The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) notified
Herriman City of the updated Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) and Flood Insurance Study (FIS) which
affected Midas Creek, Wood Hollow and Hood Hollow 2. Herriman was sent an update that the modified flood
hazard areas would become effective November 19, 2021. As a condition of continued eligibility in the National
Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) Herriman City is required to adopt or show evidence of adoption of the
floodplain management regulations to include adoption of the latest FIRM and FIS revisions. The revised
language would ensure compliance with FEMA ordinance requirements for floodplain management. It is
expected Herriman City adopt the proposed ordinance modifications prior to the effective date. He looked for
Council direction to bring the amendment for their consideration at the next meeting. The Council agreed.
