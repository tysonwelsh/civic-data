ZONE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATIONS
ZT-2-2022 Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting a zone text change to add artificial
          turf standards and amend several landscaping standards. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at
          801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)
Discussion:   Steve Pastorik explained that this proposal is for an ordinance change for specific
              landscaping standards. He stated the first text change would add criteria to the
              ordinance regarding artificial turf. Steve pointed out that the City ordinance does
              not prohibit the use of artificial turf, but it does not include turf standards. He
              stated that the lower-quality artificial turf is not aesthetically pleasing. Staff
              proposes allowing the synthetic turf to count as up to fifty percent of the live plant
              material within the commercial and residential zones. He noted that the tree
              requirement would remain the same. This proposal would allow artificial turf to
              be used in place of grass along major arterial streets. Steve mentioned that an
              automatic irrigation controller is the current irrigation standard for commercial
              and multifamily. Staff is proposing a smart controller that would use the weather
              to adjust the amount of water needed. Steve said that the current standard for
              landscaping for the front yards of single-unit dwellings has the size of the
              driveway as part of the calculation. He stated that staff is proposing a 30% live
              plant material be the requirement for all front yards of single-unit dwellings
              regardless of driveway size.
              Commissioner Winters mentioned that dirt would shift from rainwater and
              irrigation. He wonders if the turf placed directly on the ground would cause
              wrinkling or uneven ground. He also wonders if the movement of dirt under the
              turf will affect its longevity. Commissioner Winters asked Steve if the ordinance
              would require the substrate to be added between the ground and the artificial turf.
              Steve said that the synthetic turf used as a portion of West Valley City Hall's
              landscaping does not include sublayers. He said that he would look into this
              further and be prepared to discuss this next week.
              Commissioner McEwen pointed out the requirement of a separation between the
              synthetic turf and the base of trees and shrubs. He said he did not see that space
              in the earlier pictures and wondered if that would always be required. Steve said
              that the thought was this space would allow room for dirt and mulch. He
              explained that an immature tree would need room to grow, whereas the trees in
              the photos are mature and would not require space.
              Commissioner Lovato wanted to mention that he has noticed graffiti on artificial
              turf so that the City would be aware of this potential issue.

Z-2-2022      Petition by MOORE HOMES LC requesting a zone change from A (Agriculture,
              minimum lot size ½ acre) to RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) and R-1-10
              (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 10,000 square feet). The
              property is located at 4590 S 5600 W on 10.97 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-
              963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)
Discussion:   Steve Pastorik explained that the subject property was brought to the Commission
              requesting a zone change by Dr. Horton in 2020 and the Planning Commission
              approved the proposal, but City Council denied it.
              Steve said the concept plan submitted for this proposal shows two-story
              townhomes on nine acres of the eleven-acre property, along with a city park the
              residents of the proposed project would be able to walk to.
              Steve discussed the differences between what was proposed in 2020 and the
              current proposal to provide a comparison to what is currently being proposed. Dr.
              Horton had requested an acre on the southeast corner of the property be zoned
              commercial. The density proposed in 2020 was higher than the current proposal,
              and with the commercial zoning, less land for the townhome development. Mr.
              Horton was offering four single-family homes around the cul-de-sac where this
              proposal has placed the City Park.
              Steve said the developer added the park to this concept plan because of the
              comments from the residents with the proposal submitted in 2020. He explained
              that the developer would add the improvements along the cul-de-sac and then
              deed the property to the City to develop in the future. Steve noted that if the City
              decides they do not want to add the park, that land could be used as open space
              for the proposed townhomes.
              Steve displayed the renderings provided by the applicant that includes four
              different building types. He noted that more brick or stone would need to be
              added to meet the 20% requirement. The applicant will also need to increase the
              number of windows on the front of the townhomes.
              Commissioner McEwen asked if there would be parking around the cul-de-sac for
              the residents wanting to use the park. Steve said that usually, parking is not
              provided for a park this small. However, the City could choose to add parking.
              Commissioner Winters asked Steve why the Council denied the proposal in 2020
              and if this proposal addresses their concerns. Steve said, in his opinion, one
              reason is the newer townhomes have a higher quality product than those proposed
              by Dr. Horton. The Council felt the was higher than they would like to see in that
              location. He feels the applicant has addressed the Council's concerns; however,
              he cannot guarantee they will approve this application.
              Steve mentioned that the County records show the older homes to the north of the
              subject property ranging between 1350 square feet to 2200 square feet and the
              proposed development buildings range from 2300 square feet to 2500 square feet.
              Both size ranges include basements.
              Commissioner Lovato remembers the residents having concerns about the
              setbacks when proposed in 2020. He asked Steve if they had increased the
              setbacks. Steve said they had increased the setbacks from approximately 10-15
              feet to 20 feet.


Z-3-2022      Petition by CARTER MAUDSLEY requesting a zone change from A (Agriculture,
              minimum lot size ½ acre) to RE (Residential Estates). The property is located at
              5631 Cilma Drive, 3878 S 5600 W, and 3870 S 5600 W on 0.74 acres. (Staff –
              Brock Anderson at 801-963-3361 or brock.anderson@wvc-ut.gov)
Discussion:   Brock Anderson explained that the applicant would like to create two front lots,
              one would be .38 acres and the other .36 acres, and rezone that property to RE.
              Brock noted that the rear lot would remain A and be .84 acres in size, and the
              applicant is proposing an agricultural building on this lot.
              Brock said the site needs to be cleaned up, and all outside storage not permitted in
              the A zone needs to be removed for the site to meet code requirements. He noted
              Zoning Enforcement would assist with this process.
              Brock pointed out that if this proposal is approved, the rear lot will front Cilma
              Drive, and there is currently a 6’ masonry wall located on that frontage, which
              would need to be addressed. He explained that a twenty-foot setback would be
              required along the frontage, and they would need to adjust the fence to meet code.
              Therefore, the staff recommends this be stated as part of a development
              agreement. Brock asked the Commission for their feedback regarding the wall.
              Commissioner McEwen asked if the applicant is allowed to keep a fence that tall
              in their front yard. Brock said that if they meet the setback requirement, the wall
              can be six feet tall. Commissioner McEwen stated that they would need to
              remove the concrete fence. Brock said that is correct, and they would need to
              remove the portion of the wall running along the frontage of the rear lot.
              Commissioner Winters asked Brock what the purpose of the setback was in this
              case. Brock explained that the setback allows for landscaping to be in front of the
              fence and to keep the front yard open to the street.
              Commissioner Winters asked if the applicant was aware of the retaining wall
              being an issue with their site plan. Brock said that he provided the applicant with
              the information.
              Commissioner Winters asked if the applicant requested this zone change so that
              an agricultural building could be added to the rear lot. Brock said yes, and to
              create the two front lots.


              Commissioner Winters stated that he understands the reason for the setback
              requirement. He isn’t sure if it should be required in this location.
              Commissioner Lovato asked why the applicant initially chose to add a retaining
              wall instead of a fence. He said that the concrete wall was a recent addition and
              removing it might not be the best option. He stated that maybe a permit should
              have been obtained before the fence was installed. Brock said the wall meets
              code currently, and a permit is not required for a 6’ wall. However, if the
              applicant knew that he would be submitting this proposal in the future, the
              addition of the concrete fence would have been premature.

SUBDIVISION APPLICATIONS
PUD-1-2022 Petition by BRAD REYNOLDS requesting preliminary approval for the Meadows on
           32nd and amending lot 1A AFFCU 3500 South Subdivision Amended. This is a
           planned community consisting of 33 units on 3.48 acres. The property is located at
           3554 South 3200 West and is zoned RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential). (Staff
           – Steve Lehman at 801-963-3311 or steve.lehman@wvc-ut.gov)
Discussion:   Steve Lehman stated that this application is requesting preliminary approval for
              the subdivision and an amendment to lot 1A of the AFFCU. He explained that
              AFFCU originally purchased this property, thinking another commercial use
              would be added to the vacant property just south of the Credit Union. Therefore,
              they put in a shared access off of 3200 W for both lots. However, it remained
              vacant, and the applicant decided to request his property be rezoned for residential
              use, which as approved.
              Steve explained wanting to keep the commercial use and residential use separate
              the lot line to the north of Mr. Reynold’s property would be adjusted to match the
              boundary of the townhome project and a separate access would be added. Steve
              pointed out that there is a dip in this property line that is intended to provide room
              for Dutch Brothers to modify their drive-thru.

              Steve said that City Council approved Mr. Reynold's townhome project with a 4.5
              stalls/unit parking ratio. He stated that the applicant is proposing two different
              building types, and per the development agreement, he will provide three color
              schemes. Steve explained that the homes on 3200 W would be fronting the street,
              and there would be sidewalks on both sides of the road within the development.

              Commissioner Winters asked if the applicant was proposing visitor parking. Steve
              said that each unit would have a two-car garage and a driveway that would also
              provide enough space for two cars. He said that at the end of each dead-end street
              within the development will be room for two vehicles to park, and the applicant is
              proposing to have guest parking in front of the amenity area.

              Commissioner Lovato noted that there had been a conversation in the media
              recently regarding noise transference between units. He asked if there was any
              standard in place addressing this issue. Steve said there is a two-hour firewall
              between each unit; however, he isn't sure about the sound attenuation. Steve said
              he would reach out to Jerry Thompson, Chief Building Official, to see if there is a
              standard in the building code and share that information with the Commission.
