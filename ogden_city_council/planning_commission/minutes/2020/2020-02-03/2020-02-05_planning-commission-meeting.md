3. Public Hearing, Petition #2019-24 to amend Ordinance Amendment, to amend definition and use
   sections of Title 15 pertaining to taverns, private clubs and drinking establishments (Attachment E)

  Mr. Spencer stated this is a follow-up from the previous petition to allow certain uses in the BEH
  zone and to allow a liquor only bar which was considered in the January meeting. The Commission


                                                    3
  has also held a work session to consider changes in regulations relating to alcoholic beverage sales.
  He stated as Staff has reviewed the petition, it has been found that City ordinances define a bar and
  tavern, but these definitions refer only to the sale of beer, while some may desire to serve other
  alcoholic beverages. He also stated the State laws have changed relating to alcohol sales and they
  no longer defines private clubs. This action is an effort to both include all types of alcohol sales and
  update the City definitions to comply with State Code. He stated while the State classifies a “bar”
  as any establishment that has a gross sales of alcohol being over 30% the City continues to define a
  restaurant as allowing up to 50% of food sales and limits taverns to two per lineal block. Staff is
  suggesting the definitions of both tavern and private club be eliminated and is proposing a new
  definition for drinking establishments. The limit of two per lineal block will remain effective, and the
  City will continue to allow restaurants with up to 50% of food sales to continue without being
  classified as a drinking establishment and not be counted for the two per lineal block standard.
  Definitions affected include that of restaurant and drinking establishments while eliminating the
  definitions of taverns or bars. He noted other areas of the Ordinance where language for tavern
  and/or private club will be replaced by drinking establishment. He stated Staff recommends
  approval of the ordinance amendment as proposed, and asked the Commissions if there were
  questions or comments. Commissioner Schade noted the MU zone is not identified where tavern
  should be changed to drinking establishment. Mr. Stratford suggested the motion also include any
  other sections of the code where changes to tavern or private club which may have been missed
  also be included.

As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Herman to close the public
  hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Wright and passed unanimously.

  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend approval of the
                ordinance amendments based on the findings the proposed amendments will be
                consistent with the General Plan and meet all zoning requirements and policies to also
                include the MU zone as well as any other section of the Code where reference to tavern
                or private club may have been missed. Motion was seconded by Commissioner
                Sandau and passed unanimously, with Commissioners Blaisdell, Castillo, Graf, Herman,
                Sandau, Schade, Wright and Southwick voting aye.

4. Conditional Use Permit, to allow proposed tavern at 2518 Lincoln Avenue/211 25th Street .(Attachment F)

  Ms. Olson stated the property is in the CBDI zone and the request is for a high-end tavern to operate
  from 5pm until1am Thursdays thru Saturdays. The property consists of three parcels now under
  the same ownership, and Staff recommends they be combined so the frontage of the tavern is on
  Lincoln Avenue rather than 25th Street in order to comply with the two per lineal block requirement.
  The applicant is requesting approval for the use of curbside parking on both 25th and Lincoln as well
  as the Utah District Court parking which is available after 5pm. She stated the Helena is a mixed
  use building with vacation rentals on the upper floors, the Alleged Tavern and restaurant space ono
  the main floor. In considering a conditional use permit factors such as the General Plan policies are
  considered as well as compliance with the Zoning Ordinance. She stated the General Plan speaks to
  preserving historic character as well as improved walkability of the downtown and Staff is
  suggesting a better pedestrian entrance to the building be designed. In doing so, the location of the

                                                    4
dumpster and HVAC equipment may need to be relocated. Nine existing parking spaces are
designated for the vacation rental units. The applicant is proposing additional lighting and security
cameras. As the hours of operation are night-time, the use will not impact existing retail uses on the
street, and due to the nature of the bar being to host lectures rather than music and dancing, effects
to exiting residents is reduced. She stated the existing parking meets the requirements of the
Ordinance by accommodating the residential uses within the building. The applicant is proposing to
install a fence and gate to define the entrance, to be of architectural metal to match existing railing.
Staff has created a recommended site plan which would require the combination of all parcels to be
one parcel, create a 5’ pedestrian sidewalk leading from the parking to the entrance as well as
relocating the dumpster and HVAC equipment and showing six on-site parking space for the
vacation rental units, to include one ADA stall. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to the
following conditions:
1. Complete a lot combination process to obtain the required lot frontage along Lincoln Ave. for
     the proposed bar entrance on the south side of the building
2. Install an additional 24-hour surveillance security camera in the parking lot to increase coverage
     for the proposed public parking area
3. Tavern hours of operation are limited to Thursday, Friday, and Saturday from 5:pm to 1am
4. Install 5’ sidewalk to provide designated pedestrian access to proposed bar entrance
5. Relocate dumpster and modify HVAC enclosure to allow for the installation of a 5’ Sidewalk
6. Provide 6 on-site parking stalls for the owner-occupied vacation units (including 1 ADA stall)
7. Install additional lighting as needed to increase the safety of the entrance
8. Satisfy all City Staff and department requirements

5. Conditional Use Permit/Preliminary Group Dwelling Approval, for 166-unit townhomes at 130-240 W
   17th Street. (Attachment G)

  Mr. Travis Taylor stated while there is currently some remediation work going on for this property,
  this request is for preliminary approval of a project consisting of three buildings housing 30 units as
  well as 13 townhomes on the west side of the property.

  Mr. Simpson stated the request includes both a preliminary group dwelling approval and a
  conditional use permit for the project proposed. There are currently four parcels consisting of 9.52
  acres with the intent for a future subdivision to include both this property and an adjacent parcel.


                                                   6
He stated the Group Dwelling Ordinance allows properties to be sold in the future, care needs to be
taken to assure the project continues to function as a neighborhood and the central theme of the
project is retained over time.

The request is for 166 dwelling units in 16 buildings. The townhomes will have a two-car attached
garage while the apartments will be serviced by surface parking. He stated due to its proximity to
public transit, the parking requirement is 1.5 stalls per unit, and the plan exceeds that amount by 91
spaces but does provide guest parking. He reviewed the Group Dwelling Ordinance standards and
indicated some setbacks are not satisfied, and defined entryways with covered porches for the
apartments and clubhouse. The Ordinance also requires two design elements in an effort to create
a variety in design. Staff recommends some detail such as lintels or quoins be added to the
townhomes and clubhouse buildings in order to satisfy this standard. The Ordinance also requires
the same material on all four sides of each building and requires the project to be fenced but
prohibits the use of chain link fencing in the front yards. Staff recommends the front fencing be
changed to wrought iron. The Ordinance also requires dumpster enclosures to match building
materials so the proposed chain link with slats should be modified to be masonry. Other details
need to be added including two trees per unit, five 5-gallon shrubs per unit, with at least one to be a
flowering shrub, and all landscaping areas are to have 80% coverage within five years. No
streetscape design is shown, and minimum streetscape standards must be met.

He then reviewed the proposed density, stating the base line is identified, and bonus density points
are allowed by ordinance which allows increased density. He stated the applicant is seeking an
increased density using bonus points and reviewed the factors for these bonus points, stating some
modifications are required in order to qualify for all bonus points requested.

Mr. Simpson stated there is an existing irrigation ditch which runs through the property. He stated a
condition of approval is that easements be provided in order to maintain water rights and service,
and no construction would be allowed on easements. Staff recommendation is for approval subject
to the following conditions, stating these will be required to be shown prior to final group dwelling
approval is granted.
 1. All parking lots, drives, and back-up areas meet the required 8’ landscaped setback.
 2. The required minimum of five, five-gallon sized shrubs be installed on the site for each unit,
     with at least one of every five being a flowering shrub.
 3. The required 30% of open space area of the development meet the requirement of 80%
     coverage of trees and shrubs.
 4. The courtyard open spaces meet the minimum requirement of 40% coverage of shrubs and
     trees.
 5. All planter areas meet the requirement of being completely covered with shrubs and ground
     covers within a five-year period.
 6. Xeriscaped areas be expanded to all small planter areas as shown on staff’s recommended site
     plan to meet bonus density requirements.
 7. The proposed excessive parking stalls be reduced along the projects major walkway to provide
     a unified landscaping design and to connect open spaces in the development as shown on
     staff’s recommended site plan, which will meet bonus density requirements.
 8. The proposed excessive parking stalls be reduced to provide well defined crosswalks in order

                                                 7
     to maintain pedestrian connections within the development as shown on staff’s recommended
     site plan.
 9. A street scape be developed along 17th Street frontages that meet zoning requirements and
     street tree types, quantities, and locations be approved by the City Urban Forester.
 10. The fencing between the front buildings and 17th Street be a 4’ wrought iron fence.
 11. The dumpster screening enclosures on the site be 6’ masonry walls with solid metal gates that
     match the materials, architecture, and colors of the buildings on the site.
 12. All the buildings on the site meet requirements to have the same proportionate use of exterior
     finish materials on all four sides of the buildings excluding windows and doors.
 13. The breezeways on the apartment buildings have the required covered porch entry by
