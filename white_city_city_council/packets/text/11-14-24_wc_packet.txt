3. Unified Fire Authority Report

      Chief Aldridge reported that the Unified Fire Authority (UFA) budget was approved in
   June and is available for public review on the UFA website. He also informed the Council
   that two open houses were recently held for prospective firefighters, with new hire testing
   scheduled throughout July. Additionally, Chief Aldridge stressed the importance of public
   safety during the current heatwave, urging residents to immediately contact UFA if they
   observe a person or pet locked in a vehicle.

3.1 Sandy Fire Department Report

       Chief McConaghie presented the June fire and medical statistics for White City,
   reporting a year-to-date total of 146 incidents. He informed the Council that new equipment,
   including two ambulances, a Type 3 engine, and a Type 1 engine, is expected to arrive at
   the Sandy City Fire Department between August and November this year and will be
   available to serve the White City community. Chief McConaghie also announced the hiring

4. Unified Police Department Report

       Detective Josh Smith reported that there was a drive-by shooting in June, resulting in
   property damage but no personal injuries. He noted that crime statistics for June remained
   consistent with typical levels. Detective Smith highlighted an increase in loud party
   incidents, which have led to citations being issued and landlords being notified of their
   responsibility to manage their tenants' behavior. He further stated that officers assigned to
   White City maintain good communication across shifts to stay informed of ongoing
   situations.

      Detective Smith also reported that Sego Lilly Drive and Galena Drive are currently
   under construction and are receiving additional patrols. The speed trailer placed on Galena
   recorded an average speed of 29 mph, with the highest recorded speeds being 60 and 64
   mph. Despite the construction, speed limit signs remain visible. He also noted that the new
   White City patrol officer, with a background in traffic enforcement, will assist in addressing
   speeding concerns. Finally, Detective Smith confirmed that preparations are in place for
   Southeast Township Days.

     Chief Thomas reported that asset negotiations are ongoing between Unified Police
   Department (UPD) and the Sheriff's Office following the recent separation. He informed the

  6.2 Presentation Regarding the Implementation of Moderate-Income Housing -
  Strategy E

      Morgan Julian, Long Range Planner for the Municipal Services District, presented
  findings from a 2024 survey of twenty-six White City residents regarding Accessory
  Dwelling Units (ADUs). She reported that nineteen residents supported the construction of
  ADUs within their neighborhoods, and fifteen expressed interest in constructing ADUs on
  their own properties. However, eight respondents cited financial and regulatory barriers,
  such as zoning restrictions, procedural challenges within the permitting process, and high
  costs related to design, construction, and permitting fees, as significant obstacles. Seven
  respondents expressed a preference for detached ADUs, while those opposed to ADUs
  raised concerns regarding increased traffic, parking congestion, renter issues, and a lack of
  landlord involvement. Proponents of ADUs highlighted benefits such as providing
  affordable housing, generating passive income for homeowners, offering additional housing
  options for family members, and increasing property values. Ms. Julian noted that only two
  ADUs have been constructed in White City to date. She also emphasized residents' interest



                                 White City, Utah • 801-834-2577
in a program to provide design and planning assistance, along with an informational guide
on the ADU application process and related regulations.

       Ms. Julian further noted that continued discussion on ADUs aligns with the General
Plan's objective of enabling residents to age in place. She added that it supports Strategy
E, which promotes the creation, allowance, and reduction of regulations for internal and
detached accessory dwelling units in residential zones.

       Ms. Julian presented staff recommendations aimed at reducing barriers to ADU
construction. Proposed short- and long-term goals include integrating land use and building
permit steps into a single ADU application, developing a comprehensive guide on White
City ADU regulations, grants, and funding sources to assist applicants, and partnering with
local universities or nonprofit organizations to offer ADU design assistance to residents.

      Mayor Flint expressed interest in knowing the property square footage of survey
respondents.

      Paul Ashton, Legal Counsel, inquired about the average lot size in White City, to
which Ms. Julian responded that she would research and report back with this information.
Mr. Ashton suggested reviewing how other communities have approached ADU regulations
and requested that Ms. Julian look into Salt Lake City's approach.

       Mayor Flint also requested information on the lot coverage for properties expressing
interest in ADUs. Ms. Julian confirmed she would follow up.

      Council Member Huish inquired if residents were aware of the specific requirements
for ADUs. Ms. Julian noted that awareness among residents might be based on perception.

        Mayor Flint also highlighted the requirement for ADUs to have their own address,
which could raise considerations for police and fire services. Mayor Flint asked about the
potential implications for emergency services, and Mr. Ashton recommended obtaining
input from both police and fire departments on this matter.

6.4 Discussion and Possible Action Regarding Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling
bid for Additional Cleanup in White City

    Council Member Shelton reported that the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District
(WFWRD) is implementing cost-cutting measures that would reduce the level of service
provided by the Seasonal Container Reservation (SCRP) Program, specifically for the
delivery of dumpsters to homes over a three-day period for community clean-up days. He
noted that community members are required to request dumpsters through the SCRP
website by midnight; however, there aren’t usually enough dumpsters available for White
City residents, and many residents are unable to stay up until midnight to make the
request.

    Rori Andreason, Administrator suggested exploring private options for dumpster
services. Council Member Shelton reiterated that WFWRD is open to working with the city
to find a solution. Mayor Flint expressed a preference for securing additional dumpster
services through WFWRD if feasible but also requested that Ms. Andreason obtain bids for
private dumpster services.

   A RESOLUTION OF THE WHITE CITY COUNCIL APPROVING A 2025 TAX RATE IN
      EXCESS OF THE CERTIFIED TAX RATE BY THE SALT LAKE VALLEY LAW
                       ENFORCEMENT SERVICE AREA
       WHEREAS, the Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (“SLVLESA”) was created by
Resolution of the Salt Lake County Council dated August 18, 2009; and
       WHEREAS, the area of White City is included in the boundaries of SLVLESA for the funding
and provision of law enforcement services to the areas within the boundaries of SLVLESA; and
        WHEREAS, SLVLESA has proposed to budget an approximate 12.87% increase in the tax rate
in excess of the certified rate as defined in Section 59-2-024 of the Utah Code in 2025 to fund costs of
law enforcement through its payments to the Unified Police Department and the Salt Lake County
Sheriff, respond to inflationary pressures, and maintain required minimum fund balances; and
        WHEREAS, pursuant to Section 17B-1-1003 of the Utah Code, SLVLESA submitted a report
on the proposed tax increase to the White City Council (“Council”) at a duly noticed meeting on October
3, 2024; and
       WHEREAS, the Council allowed time during the duly noticed meeting on November 14, 2024
for comment on the proposed tax increase from members of the Council and the public; and
        WHEREAS, Section 17B-2a-903 of the Utah Code requires prior approval of an increase in the
certified rate by SLVLESA from the legislative bodies of any municipality and county whose territory is
located within SLVLESA, or approval of a majority of the municipalities and two-thirds (2/3) of the
County legislative body; and
        WHEREAS, before the SLVLESA Board of Trustees considers to levy a ta rate that exceeds the
certified tax rate, the SLVLESA Board of Trustees shall hold a public hearing on December 19, 2024 at
6:00 p.m. at the Millcreek City Hall to provide to all interested parties the opportunity to be heard
regarding the proposed tax rate increase; and
        WHEREAS, SLVLESA shall provide notices of the public hearing as required by Section 59-2-
919 of the Utah Code; and
        WHEREAS, after considering all public input from the public hearing and all other information
available to them, the SLVLESA Board of Trustees will vote on whether to levy a tax rate that exceeds
the certified tax rate.
        NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE WHITE CITY COUNCIL as follows:
       1.      The White City Council hereby approves for 2025 the budgeting of an approximate
               12.87% increase in property tax revenues in excess of revenues budgeted in the prior year
               and the imposition of property taxes in excess of the certified tax rate to generate the
               increase in budgeted property tax revenues as defined in Section 59-2-924 of the Utah
               Code.
