17          1.1     (Project CUP-22-002) Request from John Elkins, on Behalf of Robert Byrne
18                  for an Eight-Foot Wall Along the Northwest Property Line at 7275 South
19                  Caladora Cove.
20
21   Assistant City Planner, Maverick Yeh introduced the application and identified John Elkins as the
22   contractor. Robert Byrne is the property owner. The proposal is to construct a new eight-foot wall
23   along the northwest property line. The proposed wall will be constructed of cinderblock and stucco
24   to match the existing residence. It is intended to enclose a dog run, help reduce noise and increase
25   security. Currently, there is no privacy between the residences. The maximum height allowed for
26   a fence in any zone is eight feet as a conditional use and up to 12 feet with a conditional use and
27   the consent of the neighbors. In this case, the proposed height of the wall is eight feet so no consent
28   from the neighbors is required.
29
30   Public comments were submitted by Jeff Chatelain and Dan McDonald. Both comments were
31   forwarded to Community and Economic Development Director/Hearing Officer, Michael
32   Johnson, for review.
33
34   Staff found the proposed wall to be reasonable to preserve safety and privacy and recommended
35   approval with the conditions set forth in the staff report.
36
37   In response to a question raised, Mr. Byrne confirmed that the concrete footing is new. The
38   property line slopes down at the perimeter. The concrete foundation is the same height as the
39   foundation of the home. Mr. Johnson stated that there appears to be about an eight-inch difference.
40   His concern was that the foundation, in addition to the eight-foot wall, will exceed the eight-foot
41   maximum height at which point consent from the neighbor would be required. Mr. Byrne stated
42   that he would ensure that the total height does not exceed eight feet. He regretted not being aware
43   of the process and the stipulations from the start. He agreed to do what is necessary to keep the
44   maximum height at or below eight feet.
45
46   Mr. Johnson opened the public hearing.

 1   Dan McDonald was present as Mr. Byrne’s neighbors’ legal counsel. He submitted written
 2   objections and stated that his clients do not feel that a fence should be allowed in the proposed
 3   location. One concern was that the footings for the fence tie into the footings for the home and the
 4   garage structure, which violates the setback requirements. In addition, the fence as designed will
 5   exceed eight feet in height. The plans measure the fence from the footings up, however, City Code
 6   requires the fence height to be measured from the bottom of the natural slope. Further, there is no
 7   demonstration or evidence from the applicant that special circumstances exist. There has also been
 8   no indication that the problem with barking dogs will be remedied by adding the fence.
 9
10   Mr. McDonald reported that Mr. Byrne moving his dogs closer to the neighbors will not only not
11   resolve the problem but will exacerbate it. A fence of any height will not mitigate the barking of
12   the dogs. He cited additional problems with the application regarding conditional use requirements
13   in Section 19.84.050B5-Scaled Vicinity Map. A survey of the backyard was not submitted and if
14   it had been, there would be sufficient evidence to show that there is adequate room to locate a dog
15   run elsewhere on the property to better resolve the noise issue. Scaled plans, drawings, or a
16   variance statement are not required because of the setback issues.
17
18   There were no further public comments. Mr. Johnson closed the public hearing.
19
20   Mr. Johnson commented that the application complies with the City ordinance but he would like
21   more detail regarding the natural grade to the top of the fence. Mr. Byrne asked for clarification
22   regarding where he should measure from and agreed to include the height of the foundation.
23   Mr. Johnson suggested that the matter be continued to allow the applicant time to produce the
24   requested information.

26          1.2     (Project CUP-22-003) Request from Lanny and Cammie Derby for an Eight-
27                  Foot Fence Along the North Property Line at 6997 South Canyon Creek
28                  Circle.
29
30   Mr. Yeh presented the staff report and stated that the applicant is proposing to replace the existing
31   six-foot fence with an eight-foot vinyl fence along the north property line. The proposed fence
32   will provide increased privacy for the applicant and the neighbors. The zoning allows for fencing
33   of up to eight feet in height as a conditional use and up to 12 feet as a conditional use with consent
34   from the neighbors.
35
36   Public comments were received from Jeff Chatelain and Rick Stevenson and were provided to
37   Mr. Johnson. Staff recommended approval with the condition that Building Permits and
38   Inspections be obtained.
39
40   Mr. Johnson asked the applicant, Lanny Derby if he was set on the wood/vinyl composite fencing.
41   Mr. Derby stated that he was not set on it and was working with the neighbor to come up with
42   fencing that they both can agree on and that is reasonably priced. Mr. Johnson confirmed that the
43   fence is on the property line and proposed to be eight feet along the entire length.
44
45   Mr. Johnson opened the public hearing.

 1   Rick Stevenson hoped that the fence contractors would show respect for the property and clean up
 2   after the work is completed. There is a transition line where a six-foot fence will meet the eight-
 3   foot fence.
 4
 5   There were no further public comments. Mr. Johnson closed the public hearing.
 6
 7   Mr. Johnson reported that one condition of approval should be that the neighbors collaborate on
 8   the fencing type and style as it is on a shared property line. Mr. Derby stated that the original fence
 9   has been in place longer than the neighbor’s home, so he felt that the decisions on the fence were
10   his. Mr. Johnson explained that when a fence is on a shared property line it should be the decision
11   of both neighbors. Mr. Derby objected to any conditional suggestion that he would have to get
12   approval from the neighbor on the type and style of fencing. Mr. Johnson tabled the item to allow
13   the applicant to present a final decision on design and materials at a future meeting.
