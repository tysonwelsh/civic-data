WATER REUSE AGREEMENT BETWEEN DRAPER CITY AND
          DRAPER IRRIGATION COMPANY


       This Water Reuse Agreement Between Draper City and Draper Irrigation

Company, Including Reuse Water (“Agreement”) is entered by and between DRAPER

CITY (“City”), a Utah Municipal Corporation, and DRAPER IRRIGATION COMPANY

(“DIC”), a Utah non-profit company and public water supplier supplying municipal and

secondary irrigation water, (collectively, the “Parties”).

                                      R E C I T A L S:

       A.     The purpose of this Agreement is to set forth how the Parties may work

together to facilitate the Parties’ participation in a South Valley Water Reuse Project (also

referred to as “Reuse Project” or “Project”) as defined by the Wastewater Reuse Act,

U.C.A. §73-3c-101 et seq. (the “Act”), to allow DIC’s use of the City’s reuse water related

to the use of the treated effluent from the wastewater generated by the City or the City’s

residents or customers from the reuse of water under Water Right No. 55-4494 “City’s

Reuse Water.”

       B.     Specific projects by which DIC could utilize the City’s reuse water may either

be defined in this Agreement, or in any future agreement.

       C.     The purpose of the Reuse Project is to allow the Parties and other Project

participants to conserve water in the Salt Lake Valley by making a permanent supply of

reuse water, as defined in the Act, available for distribution from the Jordan Basin Water

Reclamation Facility wastewater treatment plant (“JBWRF Plant”), a “POTW” as defined
by the Act, owned by South Valley Sewer District. It is anticipated that Project Reuse

Water may be made available to the areas served by Bluffdale City, Draper City, Herriman

City, Riverton City, Sandy City, and South Jordan City (collectively referred to as the

“Reuse Cities”) and to DIC, which serves areas within Draper City, Sandy City, Bluffdale

City and adjacent unincorporated Salt Lake County (the Reuse Cities and DIC collectively

constitute the “Reuse Entities”). The intended place of reuse includes the service areas

of the Reuse Entities (“Reuse Area”).          A master reuse contract entitled Reuse

Authorization Contract for South Valley Reuse Project will need to be executed by all of

the parties participating in the Reuse Project (“Master Reuse Contract”).

       D.     The Reuse Project is a proposed future project which is intended to meet

the conservation and reuse requirements of Section 207 of the Central Utah Project

Completion Act (“CUPCA”) through which Reuse Water will be provided for use within the

area of the Reuse Entities. The Reuse Water originates from water sources of the United

State Bureau of Reclamation (“BOR”), delivered by Central Utah Water Conservancy

District (“CUWCD”), the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District (“JVWCD”), and the

Metropolitan Water District of Salt Lake and Sandy (“Metro”).

       E.     The underlying water right for the Project is Water Right No. 55-4494,

(“Reuse Water Right”), held in the name of BOR (“Water Right Entity”), as defined by

the Act. Water under the Reuse Water Right is made available on a wholesale basis to

the Reuse Cities (“Reuse Water”).

       F.     The City does not currently intend to construct and install any infrastructure,

including distribution lines, that would allow it to use the City’s Reuse Water directly from

the JBWRF Plant for irrigation of City owned property or for distribution to residents. The

City as a shareholder in DIC will continue to receive DIC water deliveries to City property

within the City. This Agreement does not alter that relationship. In addition, the City

desires to enter into this Agreement to authorize DIC to use City’s Reuse Water from the

JBWRF Plant in consideration for DIC installing certain infrastructure to irrigate the

Galena Hills Park with a portion of the City’s Reuse Water and other consideration

described herein that will reduce the City’s costs associated with irrigating the park.

Enabling DIC to irrigate the park with the City’s Reuse Water instead of culinary water will

result in significant cost savings to the City that are estimated to total between $70,000

and $80,000 per year, representing the difference between what it currently pays JVWCD

for culinary water and what it would pay under DIC’s water rates.

       G.     DIC anticipates using all the City’s Reuse Water annually under the Project.

       H.     The Parties anticipate that the Reuse Water associated with the JBWRF

Plant will be allocated to Riverton City, Herriman City, South Jordan City, Sandy City,

Draper City, Bluffdale City, and DIC based on current and future levels of wastewater

being generated in the Reuse Area, current and future demands for secondary water or

Reuse Water, and available funding.

       I.     When completed the Reuse Project will directly and immediately benefit DIC

customers and City residents by providing them with higher quality irrigation water than

is currently available from DIC. In addition, because the amount of Reuse Water will

increase as growth occurs, the Project will also provide water to meet the future needs of

DIC and the City’s residents.

       J.     The Parties desire to cooperate with one another in supporting the Reuse

Project to create a reliable supply of reuse water in sufficient quantity and of acceptable

quality for reasonable use by the Reuse Entities.

       K.     The Parties acknowledge that, at a minimum, the following approvals will

be required by the Utah State Engineer and Utah Water Quality Board under the

Wastewater Reuse Act, Utah Code Ann. § 73-3c-101 et seq., and Utah Administrative

Code Rule § R317-13-1, et seq.: a) approval of an Application for Sewage Effluent

Reuse; b) approval of a Reuse Authorization Contract; and c) construction permit(s) and

operating permit(s) (collectively, the “Necessary Approvals”).

                                      AGREEMENT:

       NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the covenants set forth herein and for

other good and valuable consideration, the sufficiency and receipt of which are hereby

acknowledged, the Parties agree as follows:

1.     CITY’S CONSENT AND AUTHORIZATION FOR DIC TO USE CITY’S REUSE
       WATER

       1.1    DIC Use of Reuse Water. The City hereby consents and authorizes DIC to

use all of the City’s Reuse Water in perpetuity in exchange for the following express

conditions: (1) DIC will utilize the Reuse Water for the benefit of the City and its citizens;

and (2) DIC will seek to enter into an agreement with the City within five (5) years that will

govern how the Parties will jointly or individually construct the infrastructure necessary to

supply a portion or all of the Reuse Water to the Galena Hills Park. If the Parties are

unable to reach an agreement to supply Reuse Water to the Galena Hills Park, DIC will

continue to supply the Reuse Water to the City and its residents. DIC’s provision of Reuse



                                                                      Reuse Agreement – Page 4 of 13
Water to the City and its residents or to the Galena Hills Park is subject to the City’s

compliance with applicable DIC policies, water rates, and other requirements.

       1.2    Conditions. This Agreement is conditioned upon the execution of the Master

Reuse Contract and DIC’s construction and installation of all water infrastructure needed

for DIC to use the City’s Reuse Water for the benefit of Draper and its citizens or to supply

the City’s Reuse Water to the Galena Hills Park if the Parties reach an agreement under

subparagraph 1.1.

2.     REUSE WATER

       2.1    Reuse Water Right.

       The Reuse Water Right (Water Right No. 55-4494) is the water right that may be

used for all or part of the future Reuse Project. The Reuse Water Right is held in the

name of the BOR and sold under contract by CUWCD, JVWCD, and Metro.                          This

Agreement may be amended in the future to add additional water rights held by the

Parties which are appropriate for use in this Reuse Project.

       2.2    Source of Reuse Water.

       The source for the Reuse Water will be the JBWRF Plant sewage treatment plant

located at 13543 South Lovers Lane, Riverton, Utah.

       2.3    Quantity of Reuse Water.

       The Parties understand that the Reuse Project Water cannot be allocated among

the Reuse Entities unless and until the quantity of water is approved by the Utah Division

of Water Rights (“Division”) for reuse by each Reuse Entity (“Approved Reuse

Amount”) under one or more Applications for Sewage Effluent Reuse filed with the

Division in accordance with Utah Code Ann. §73-3c-302 (“Reuse Applications”).


                                                                     Reuse Agreement – Page 5 of 13
Accordingly, the quantity of the City’s Reuse Water awaits final quantification.

       The approvals of Reuse Applications by the Division may contain depletion as well

as diversion limitations. Each Reuse Entity is anticipated to be solely responsible for

compliance with those limitations and for providing appropriate documentation of that

compliance to the Division, as required by the Division. DIC will be responsible for

compliance with regards to the City’s Reuse Water.

       2.4    Quality of Reuse Water

       The Reuse Water being provided under the Reuse Project will be Type I effluent

under the standards established by the Utah Water Quality Board.

3.     OTHER REUSE EFFORTS

       It is acknowledged by the Parties that DIC, and possibly other parties to the Master

Reuse Contract, have other water rights which could be added to this Project (“Additional

Water Rights”), subject to the capacity of the JBWRF Plant and sewage inflows and

subject to Division approval. If there is reuse water available at the JBWRF Plant under

such Additional Water Rights, it is anticipated that the party or parties owning,

transporting, and/or distributing water under those Additional Water Rights may make a

written proposal to the other parties to the Master Reuse Contract for the use of such

Additional Water Rights within the Project, including which water rights are proposed for

inclusion, whether existing Pump Station facilities or new facilities are proposed, and how

this Agreement and the Master Reuse Contract are proposed to be amended to

incorporate the proposal. For the Additional Water Rights, each Reuse Application, and

accordingly, each Approved Reuse Amount, is expected to be based upon: a) the amount

of wastewater a particular Reuse Entity sends to the JBWRF Plant as an Initial Use Entity

under its own Water Rights; and/or b) the amount of Wastewater sent to the JBWRF Plant

under a specific combination of Wastewater attributable to Water Rights owned by that

Reuse Entity and/or other Water Right Entities pursuant to separate agreements between

those parties, such as this Agreement between the City and DIC.

4.    INTEGRATION

      This document contains the entire agreement between the Parties as to each of

the issues resolved herein, provided, that nothing herein prevents nor obligates the

Parties from entering into a separate surplus water agreement.

5.    COMPLIANCE WITH LAW

      The Parties will comply with all applicable federal, state and local laws, ordinances,

rules and regulations.

6.    ASSIGNABILITY

      No assignment of this Agreement will be allowed absent the written consent of the

Parties, which consent may not be unreasonably withheld.

7.    DEFAULT

      An “Event of Default” will occur under this Agreement if any party fails to perform

its obligations hereunder when those obligations are due and the defaulting party has not

performed the delinquent obligations within twenty (20) days following delivery to the

delinquent party of written notice of such delinquency, or where performance of the

delinquent obligations is not reasonably possible within the 20-day period, has not.

8.    REMEDIES

      8.1    Dispute Resolution

      If the Parties are unable to resolve a dispute after notice of a default has been


                                                                    Reuse Agreement – Page 7 of 13
given under Section 7 or if the Parties are unable to resolve an objection filed under

Subsection 1.3.3, either Party may submit the matter to formal mediation. The Parties will

endeavor to reasonably agree upon a mediator and will mediate the dispute in good faith.

Each Party will be responsible for their own costs and will split the cost of the mediator

between them by dividing the total costs of the mediator by the number of Parties involved.

If the Parties cannot agree upon a mediator, each Party will name a mediator and those

two mediators will name a third mediator. The Parties will participate in good faith in one

session of mediation with the third mediator and will be responsible for splitting the costs

of the third mediator on an equal basis. For disputes involving an objection the City has

filed under Subsection 1.3.3, either Party may pursue judicial action to resolve issues left

unresolved from mediation.

       8.2     Default

       For Events of Default, after the Parties have complied with the provisions of

Subsection 8.1, the non-defaulting party will have the right to exercise all rights and

remedies available at law and in equity, including injunctive relief and specific

performance.     In support of the latter remedy, the Parties acknowledge that their

obligations under this Agreement are unique and defaults may not necessarily be

compensated by purely monetary damages. All rights and remedies will be cumulative.

Furthermore, the non-defaulting party will have the right to cure the default and seek

reimbursement from the defaulting party for the costs incurred in effecting such cure.

Notwithstanding any provision herein to the contrary, the defaulting party will reimburse

the non-defaulting party for all costs and fees related to the cure of such default with

fifteen (15) days following delivery to the defaulting party of a written notice of such costs

and fees together with reasonable supporting documentation. If an enforcement action

of any type is pursued, the prevailing party will be entitled to the recovery of reasonable

attorney’s fees and costs arising from or related to such breach.

9.     TERM OF AGREEMENT

       In view of the large investments in infrastructure associated with this Reuse Project

by several parties and the need for stable, long tern water supplies, the Parties agree that

this Agreement may not be terminated once the Master Reuse Contact is fully executed

and the Necessary Approvals have been secured, except by court order or written

agreement of the Parties, consistent with the intention of the Parties that the integrity of

the Project and the Master Reuse Contract be maintained to the extent possible even if

that Contract is terminated as to one or more parties thereto. Consistent therewith, this

Agreement may be amended in writing by the Parties as the need arises. The Parties

agree that consent to such amendments will not be unreasonably withheld.

10.    NO WAIVER

       Any party’s failure to enforce any provision of the Agreement will not constitute a

waiver of the right to enforce such provision. The provisions may be waived only in writing

by the party intended to be benefited by the provisions and a waiver by a party of a breach

hereunder by the other Party will not be construed as a waiver of any succeeding breach

of the same or other provisions.

11.    SEVERABILITY

provisions hereof will continue in full force and effect.
