    4. FINAL APPROVAL OF OSPREY RANCH SUBDIVISION PHASE 1, CONSISTING OF 31 LOTS
       AND TWO OPEN-SPACE PARCELS. THIS PROPOSAL ALSO INCLUDES DEDICATION OF A
       NEW COUNTY ROADWAY.
         Tammy Aydelotte (Planning): 31 lot subdivision in a geohazard area with only a couple of lots exceeding
         25% slope. Second phase of this subdivision will connect to Highway 158. Final approval recommended by
         both staff and Engineering with conditions as noted in the Staff report. This property in question is in a geo-
         hazard area however only a couple of lots are required by our Land Use Code to show a buildable area due to
         slopes that exceed 25% and will be reflected on the final plat. Wolf Creek Water and Sewer has approved of
         the infrastructure installed. Separate escrows for infrastructure within subdivision boundaries and
         conveyance infrastructure specific to sewer outside of the subdivision boundaries. Escrow has been received
         for all improvements within the subdivision; along with this request for final approval is a development
         agreement in the amount of $3,437,023. Part of the agreement stipulates there will not be any building
         permits issued until all infrastructure has been installed and accepted by the apporpriate improvement
         districts. Developer has agreed to all terms. All proposed roadways are to be public roadways. Nordic
         provided a will-serve letter earlier this year and confirmed recently they do have water available for this
         subdivision.

         John Lewis (Osprey Ranch LLC) from Eden, Utah: Thank you to staff involved. This is a huge project that
         lays groundwork for sewage access not just in this subdivision but also in the valley. We appreciate the
         separate escrows as we eat this elephant one bite at a time. Wanted to share density decision levied in
         November 2021 and make sure it was counted up front. We chose to have 61 units with larger lots and send
         remaining density to the village already earmarked for high density. If we are going to have any say in the
         future of our valley, this is the way we need to do it.

         Chair Jenkins: We received millions of ARPA funds and are dispersing to different agencies in the county to
         start sewage effort in the valley. This is a major step in the upper valley.

         Chair Jenkins: I want to thank Rick Grover and his staff. This is a large project that has been in work for
         some time.

         Commissioner Harvey moved for final approval of Osprey Ranch Subdivision Phase 1, consisting of 31 lots
         and two open-space parcels. This proposal also includes dedication of a new County roadway; Commissioner
         Froerer seconded.
