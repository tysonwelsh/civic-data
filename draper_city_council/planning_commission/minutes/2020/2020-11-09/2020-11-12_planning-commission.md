at least two of the three following criteria: 7-day average positivity rate, 14-day case rate
per 100,000 people, and statewide ICU utilization.

and to manage issues regarding social distancing in order to comply with COVID-19
Transmission Index guidelines and Salt Lake and Utah County Public Health Orders.
• COVID-19 poses a continuing and immediate threat to the health, safety, and
welfare of Draper City residents and the public in general.

6.     Public Hearing: Rasmussen Family Commercial Zoning Map Amendment
       (Legislative Action)

A request by One West Construction, representing the Rasmussen Family Limited Partnership, to
amend the zoning map designation from the A5 (Agricultural, 5 acres minimum) Zone to the CN
(Neighborhood Commercial) Zone for the property is located at approximately 12197 S. 300 East.
Application TEXT MAP-1066-2020. Staff contact is Maryann Pickering, 801-576-6391,
maryann.pickering@draper.ut.us.

Maryann Pickering, City Planner, introduced the property and clarified the location of the site.
She noted the current zoning was A5 and the current land use was Neighborhood Commercial.
She noted it was the only agricultural piece on their map surrounded mostly by commercial with
some residential to the North. The request was to rezone the property to Neighborhood
Commercial and subdivide the property into two lots. She added that the applicant believed the
current land use designation was compatible with the proposed zoning. She provided site photos
of the property and surrounding area. She shared public comments had been emailed to the
commissioners and that she received one more this afternoon from a neighbor living in the vicinity
of the site expressing support for the zone change.

Scott Sabey, Applicant’s Representative, clarified that the Rasmussen property had been farmed
since 1885. He shared that they were not wanting to do anything different from what the site is
designated for under the City’s master plan and general plan. He noted it was a transition area
since it bordered both commercial and residential and believed the neighborhood commercial
zoning would be consistent with a transition area. He added that they believed they met all the
standards to qualify for a rezoning to match the general plan.

Commissioner Fowler asked if they would see the site plan for the proposed charter school and
what potential uses there were for the potential second lot.

Mr. Sabey replied there was nothing planned for the second lot at this point.

Chairman Adams reviewed how public comments would work and opened up the public hearing
for the Item.

Lisa Hansen, 300 East, asked what the City would do concerning the road system surrounding the
lot to be sure it can support whatever is put on the property. She indicated that she believed the
roads were insufficient even with the existing site use.

Rebecca Dura, 446 East 120 South, commented that they have lived in Draper for about five years
and have witnessed a heavy uptick in traffic and road congestion in the area. She recognized that
the intended use was not part of tonight’s discussion but feels the intended use after the rezone
went hand in hand. She expressed concern that the roads could not support the proposed charter
school and noted there was not continuous sidewalks in the area, and she did not believe adding
another K-12 school would be a good idea.

Jeff Hansen, 12057 South 300 East, remarked that he was shocked they were proposing two large
schools so close to each other. He noted the traffic is backed up in the mornings due to the existing
school. He commented that he did not understand why the 71 single-family homes that were
discussed a year ago were denied due to parking. He read Draper City Code section 9-25-030
regarding parking and expressed concern there would not be enough parking for the proposed
school.

Suzanne Nelson, 12073 South 300 East, indicated she was very opposed to the zone change even
though it was a lower impact commercial zone.

Chairman Adams closed the public hearing portion of the Agenda Item.

Mr. Sabey commented regarding what the City would do about the roads in the area that UDOT
has been examining. He noted a charter school could go in whether the land was rezoned or not.
He added they would be able to go through the site plan and traffic studies in more detail at the
next meeting and pointed out this meeting was only to determine if they could rezone the site.

Commissioner Squire inquired what could be allowed in the proposed zone.

Ms. Pickering provided a list of permitted, not permitted, and conditional uses for the proposed
zone. Permitted uses included assisted living facility, residential facility for persons with a
disability, charter schools, churches, government, public school, bank, bar, bed and breakfast,
convenience store, gas station, personal care, preschool, restaurant, secondhand store, among
others. She added that the minimum lot size to create the new proposed zone was one acre. She
shared that the whole site is currently 9.8 acres and could be divided under the new zoning into
smaller parcels.

Chairman Adams pointed out the rezone could allow for higher density than the school by itself
since the property could be subdivided.

Commissioner Squire commented that the master plan is not a forward-looking process and
clarified that she did not feel comfortable moving the zone change forward simply because it was

7.     Public Hearing: Rockwell Point Text Amendment (Legislative Action)

On the request of Todd Amberry representing the Rockwell Point, LLC to amend the permitted
land uses to the existing Major Freeway Arterial Frontage Road (MARF) zoning district. The
Changes will amend Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC) Section 9-11-110. The proposed
changes would apply citywide. Application TEXTMAP-1074-2020. Staff contact is Maryann
Pickering, 801-576-6391, maryann.pickering@draper.ut.us.

Ms. Pickering, City Planner, introduced the Item and explained the requested amendment would
be Citywide and would only amend the use table in 9-11-110 to permit carwashes, convenience
stores, gasoline service stations, and restaurants. She added that there were no proposed changes
to the development standards within the zone.

Commissioner Player inquired the areas the text amendment would affect.

Ms. Pickering pulled up a map and indicated the only parcel with the effected zoning was along
Minuteman Drive. She clarified the area was south of the Maverick near the Water Treatment
Plant.

Chairman Adams noted this location would be a good location for high-end properties such as a
hotel.

Commissioner Squire stated she felt the previous uses written into the code were too much and
was not comfortable with adding even more uses.

Commissioner Fowler asked for clarification on what the current permitted uses were in the area.

Ms. Pickering stated retail, hotels, offices, and other smaller commercial uses. She clarified that
only a portion of the site could be developed as residential and the remainder could be any of the
other uses in the zone.

Todd Amberry, Applicant, explained that they previously planned to have a hotel on the site when
the land was rezoned, but since COVID they anticipated putting in more of an office use and the
developers they have talked to were requesting other uses that were not included in the zone such
as a restaurant. He added that he believed allowing these extra uses would enhance the area.

Chairman Adams opened the meeting up for public comment on the Item. No comments were
offered, and the public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Fowler remarked that she did not like tall buildings next to the highway. She
