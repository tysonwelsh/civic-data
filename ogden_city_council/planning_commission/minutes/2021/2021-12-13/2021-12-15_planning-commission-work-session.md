1. Discussion, CBD Ordinance Amendment Kick-off Discussion with Consultants to incorporate Make
   Ogden Plan.

    Mr. Montgomery explained Ogden City has hired a consultant to assist in the update of the CBD
    Ordinance, and in particular, to incorporate the Make Ogden Plan and other plans into the Ordinance.
    He stated they had been in Ogden on Monday at which time they rode with Staff around the
    downtown area to make them better aware of the existing fabric of the downtown. As part of their
    factfinding, they desire some input from the Planning Commission relating to review of buildings in the
    Central Business District.

    Ms. Jessica Garrow introduced the team of consultants representing Design Workshop from Aspen,
    Colorado, stating Hillary Seminlek has joined the meeting and will conduct part of the presentation.
    She and Hillary were involved with the establishment of the Make Ogden Plan and are anxious to see
    it being implemented. She then reviewed some of the goals of the project, indicating while it is
    important the key factors of the Plan be part of the land use code, it is also important that the code be
    clear and understandable. The review will include land use, setbacks, site coverage, parking access,
    façade standards, pedestrian access and amenities.
    Ms. Hillary Seminlek reviewed the scope of the project stating they had met with each of the planners
    during their visit on Monday and then toured around the CBD area. She presented a potential
    timeline for completion of the project, stating it is hoped the initial draft will be complete by May 2022
    with final adoption anticipated in October. She stated the intent is to create a simple, clear document
    which will identify land uses and design standards for the CBD. While this review is centered on the
    CBD, it also may impact other chapters of the Code, and could also provide some adaptive reuse for
    existing buildings in the area. She then identified questions for the Commission’s response.

    Question 1: Identify what works well with the existing code, and what seems to be lacking.
    Commissioner Garner stated standards need to be solid and felt it is important to have the insight that
    new uses follow the existing community plans. He felt items lacking are lack of input from newer
    citizens, particularly the Hispanic population, stating it is difficult to reach out to a diverse community.
    He stated focus groups are helpful but visuals need to be understandable and interesting, and not the
    typical graphs and tables. Commissioner Sandau stated she is concerned with the lack of information
    available to the public and felt the postcards could contain more detail. Chair Graf stated the
    Commission often depends on the expertise of the City Staff. Commissioner Sandau stated it is
    helpful when diagrams show the relationship of buildings with people and cars in order to show the
    scale of the project. Commissioner Blaisdell agreed scale is important as often the Commission
    approves something and then is surprised the scale is much different from what they thought they
    were reviewing. It is important for the Commission to know what exactly they are approving. Chair

   the process to assure when it is complete that it serves the Commission as intended.

   Question 2: Are there particular concepts or topics in the Make Ogden Plan which should be
   addressed in detail as part of the Code? And a follow-up: Are there things in the Make Ogden Plan
   which should not be part of the Code?
   Commissioner Garner noted technology is quickly changing and it is difficult to anticipate what might
   need to be included for the future. He cited the examples of car charging stations and energy efficient
   buildings as two specific items which may not have been considered in the past.
   Mr. Montgomery stated he has noticed most of the concepts of the Make Ogden Plan center on
   commercial uses while there is much of the CBD which is residential. There also is concern that often
   items are anticipated to be large in scale and done by professionals, while there are still many smaller
   shops and services which may not need the same standards. Commissioner Southwick expressed
   there needs to be balance between private property rights and development standards. He felt there
   should be some flexibility to preserve buildings and design that might be unique and eclectic but might
   not fit with the existing architecture of the downtown. He also felt it important that adaptive reuse be
   allowed rather than scraping and redeveloping. Commissioner Blaisdell stated she often relies on the
   expertise of Staff as they are better versed with the Ordinances and Community Plans.

   Question 3: Are there items that are now reviewed by the Planning Commission which could be
   reviewed administratively such as minor additions? Are there items that are not now reviewed by the
   Planning Commission which should be reviewed?

   Commissioner Sandau noted items on the common consent portion of the agenda substantially meet
   the Code requirements and are typically not discussed She felt this is helpful. Chair Graf asked if the
   Ordinance could contain enough standards that administrative review is allowed, whether there is
   experience of only administrative reviews. Ms. Hillary stated there are a variety of ways these can be
   considered, and each community needs to find its own comfort level in how items are reviewed. Is it
   more important to simplify the process for the applicants, or to involve the public in a commission
   meeting? Commissioner Garner stated the Commission depends on Staff to assure the Code is
   satisfied, but there are often individual concerns which are identified by Staff which the Commission
   may not have been aware of. There also are items which might not have been addressed by Staff but
   are identified by Commission members and the discussion among the Commission is helpful. He felt
   it is helpful when the Commission can discuss an item in detail in a work session format. He also
   stated the State Legislature has changed some laws and care needs to be taken to comply with the
   State code. Commissioner Blaisdell commented each of the Commission members wants what is
   best for the overall community and they appreciate public input, and given enough information, the
   Commission can discuss items and make the best decision for the overall community.

   Commissioner Southwick expressed concern there are other areas within the City besides the CBD
   where better standards need to be developed. Mr. Montgomery stated the existing grant has a
   second phase, which is anticipated to include a re-write of the entire development code.


As there was no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at 7:10pm.
