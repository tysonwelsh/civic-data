STAFF ANALYSIS

Staff support the proposal to add data processing business services in the Airport
Industrial zone (AI). Data processing businesses have been a growing industry in our
state in the last 15 years. Many new centers are being built in other cities in the state of
Utah.

The Airport Industrial (AI) Zone, being newly written, does not use four-digit Standard
Land Use Code designations. It uses broader land-use terms. One current land use
designation in the AI zone is “Professional office related to airport activities.” Staff
suggests amending this language to “Professional Services (except behavior, drug, and
alcohol treatment centers). This is the land-use designation in other zones that permit
data centers currently so the amendment would be consistent with other zones.

One problem we have with a data center in the AI zone is that power substations are
not permitted in the zone currently. A request to include substations was not included in
this application.

Provo City Code Section 14.02.020(2) sets forth the following guidelines for
consideration of ordinance text amendments.

Before recommending an amendment to this Title, the Planning Commission shall
determine whether such amendment is in the interest of the public and is
consistent with the goals and policies of the Provo City General Plan. The following
guidelines shall be used to determine consistency with the General Plan:


       (a) Public purpose for the amendment in question.
       Staff response: The amendment would add a zone to those where data
       centers are permitted. The amount of acreage that could be zoned AI is
       considerable.
       (b) Confirmation that the public purpose is best served by the amendment
       in question.
       Staff response: Staff believes that the proposed amendment does not
       enhance nor hinder the public purpose.
       (c) Compatibility of the proposed amendment with General Plan policies,
       goals, and objectives.
       The applicant felt that this proposed text amendment would not
       modify or impact the General Plan. Because data centers consume
       large amounts of power and potentially water, other business
       opportunities may not be possible. This could potentially impact the
       General Plan.

OVERVIEW

The Finch family is requesting a zone map amendment to apply the Agritourism Overlay
(AT) Zone to their property at 2601 West 280 South in order to construct a new event
center. The property is currently being used for a variety of agricultural-related activities.
The proposed event center would be built on the western third of the lot.

Details of the proposal include a new paved access at the corner of 280 South and
2530 West that leads to a landscaped parking lot with seventy-one (71) spaces. The
event center area includes the 2,965 square foot event center surrounded by
landscaped yards, plants, trees, and walkways.

The applicant has worked with staff to address all utility and infrastructure needs for the
site and has created a plan that will satisfy those requirements. There will be bonds and
fees required for this project to ensure that it is constructed as indicated in the approved
plans.

The Finch family has been working to find a way to open an event center at this location
for some time. In 2023, at a hearing to try to amend the A1 Zone, the City Council
denied that change but encouraged the Finch family to work with planning staff to create
an overlay zone that could be used to establish an event center on the property to
support the continuation of agricultural land uses. Planning staff created the ordinance,
and it was adopted in January 2024. Since that time there has been no formal request
to adopt the AT Overlay Zone in the city. If the zone change is given, the project could
move forward with all necessary permits, and the obtaining of a business license at the
conclusion of construction. However, if the city verifies that any section of the AT
Overlay code is not being met in the future, the use of an event center could be
revoked.

The property is mostly surrounded by agricultural uses on adjacent properties to the
west and south in the A1 Zone, there is a school to the north in the PF (Public Facilities)
Zone, and several single-family homes to the east in R1 (One Family Residential) and
RA (Residential Agricultural) Zones. Though some of the residential neighbors have
shared concerns about traffic and noise, the regulations of the AT Overlay Zone should
help to mitigate any issues related to the event center.

Staff have determined that the related project plan application tied to this rezone
request has met all relevant requirements of the proposed overlay zone, and other city
standards. If built as shown in the approved plans and used in compliance with city
regulations, staff hope that this will serve as a benefit to the community. Further
analysis is found below by responding to criteria in Section 14.02.020, Provo City Code:

OVERVIEW

The Finch family is requesting approval of their project plans for a new event center to
be built on their property at 2601 West 280 South, along with associated parking and
landscaping improvements. The property is currently being used for a variety of
agricultural-related activities. The proposed event center would be built on the western
third of the lot.

Details of the proposal include a new paved access at the corner of 280 South and
2530 West that leads to a landscaped parking lot with seventy-one (71) spaces. The
event center area includes the 2,965 square foot event center surrounded by
landscaped yards, plants, trees, and walkways.

The applicant has worked with staff to address all utility and infrastructure needs for the
site and has created a plan that will satisfy those requirements. There will be bonds and
fees required for this project to ensure that it is constructed as indicated in the approved
plans.

Because this project is dependent on the City Council approving the AT Overlay Zone
on the property, it should be noted that the 2023 City Council encouraged the Finch
family to move this direction to establish an event center on the property to support the
continuation of agricultural land uses. If the zone change is given, the project could
move forward with all necessary permits, and the obtaining of a business license at the
conclusion of construction. However, if the city verifies that any section of the AT
Overlay code is not being met in the future, the structure would stay but the use of an
event center could be revoked.

FINDINGS OF FACT

   1. The property is currently in the A1 Zone (Chapter 14.08, Provo City Code).
   2. The proposed zone is the A1.5(AT) Zone (Chapters 14.08 & 14.08A).
   3. The total property is 8.86 acres.
   4. The maximum area for agritourism use is 2.95 acres (Section 14.08A.050).
   5. The proposed agritourism use is approximately 2.78 acres (~31% of lot).
   6. The maximum occupancy for the building is 240 occupants, which requires a minimum
      of 60 parking spaces (Section 14.37.060 – dance halls).
   7. The proposed parking provides 71 parking spaces.

APPLICABLE ZONING CODES

Chapter 14.08, Provo City Code (https://provo.municipal.codes/Code/14.08)

Chapter 14.08A, Provo City Code (https://provo.municipal.codes/Code/14.08A)

Andrew Terry | February 2, 2025
District 3 feedback on Planning Commission Items

To Provo Planning Commission,

My name is Andrew Terry, and I'm the Neighborhood Chair for District 3. Unfortunately
neither myself or any of my fellow board members could attend the planning commission
meeting tonight (February 26th), but we wanted to share the feedback that we
received from residents of our district in regards to items on the Planning Commission's
agenda.
Items #2 and #3 both pertain to our district as the location for both of those items resides in
the Provo Bay Neighborhood.
This development has been discussed a few times at our neighborhood meetings (April
2023, July 2023, August 2024)
We received feedback from a handful of residents.

In general, most residents value the agricultural heritage of our communities, and felt
preserving that heritage to be valuable. The concept of agritourism, as well as a wedding
event area, were seen as valuable assets for the community. There was positive feedback
from multiple residents on the initial renderings for the buildings and the layout for the
event space (parking, etc)

The original proposal involved a general amendment to the A1 zone to allow for these
designs. There were serious concerns about the ramifications that would have across all of
Provo. With the change to do an Agritourism Overlay, I believe those concerns have been
addressed.

There were concerns raised by a handful of residents about parking and noise. Ensuring
that the space has adequate parking for the events they plan to hold was important to the
residents that lived nearby. Additionally ensuring that noise from the events doesn't spill
over into the neighborhoods, especially later at night, was important. The community
would like to see appropriate measures put in place to address both of those concerns.

Overall the community was positive towards these two proposals, and hopeful that proper
designing and enforcement could address the parking and noise concerns.

Sincerely,
Andrew Terry

       wouldn’t be any runoff or steam generated from the process.
