       b. Discussion regarding potential revisions to City Code Title 16 Administrative Code
          Enforcement Hearing Program
          City Attorney Josh Chandler introduced the proposal to revise Title 16 regarding the
          Administrative Code Enforcement Hearing Program, and said his office prepared two policy
          questions for the Council to discuss and provide direction. Council Member Green commented
          that additional policy questions would be brought to the Council at future meetings.

          Assistant City Attorney/Civil Litigator Paul Dodd said the first question for discussion was
          service of process, meaning how residents were notified of violations when code enforcement
          cases were brought to the City. Under the current Title 16, a notice could be served by any of
          the following methods: regular mail; posting of notice on the property; personal service; or
          publication in a newspaper. Mr. Dodd said concern had been raised about service via regular
          mail, and a recommendation made that every initial notice should be sent via Certified Mail
          instead of regular mail. He explained the current code enforcement process for notices:
          courtesy notice (not part of written ordinance), followed by notice of violation, and notice of
          hearing.

          Mr. Dodd commented that if every initial notice were sent via Certified Mail, the City would see
          an increase in expenses for mailing and staff time. He said West Jordan averaged a little over
          20,000 code enforcement cases a year. Of those cases, approximately 300 a year were found to
          not be in violation after the first inspection (no courtesy notice sent). Approximately 900-1000
          cases a year were resolved after the courtesy notice and were in compliance at the second
          inspection. Approximately 400-500 cases a year were resolved after the notice of violation, and
          only approximately 200 cases a year were scheduled for a default hearing with a notice of
          hearing sent. Mr. Dodd answered questions from the Council.

          The Council discussed options presented, all of which complied with State law. Vice Chair
          Bloom asked if the City would be allowed to incorporate the cost of mailing into the code
          enforcement fee. Mr. Dodd responded it was an option, but many people let the fees sit on the
          account as a default against them. Mr. Chandler added that probably 60%-65% of the
          individuals who received a notice did not comply, and the City ended up with a claim against
          them. Collecting on the claims was labor intensive, and the City would not recoup costs in the
          short term.

          Council Member Pack suggested posting a notice on a front door and taking a photo to have on
          file as proof, which may have more credibility than Certified Mail. Mr. Dodd said Code
          Enforcement often used that approach.

          Council Member Green commented that using Certified Mail would be closer in compliance
          with State statute. He pointed out that notices needed to reach property owners in rental
          situations. Mr. Dodd responded that Code Enforcement always mailed a copy of a notice to a
          property owner if the property owner address was different from the address of occurrence.
          Council Member Green said he wanted to do everything possible to ensure a property owner

          opinion that the City was looking for compliance, not punishment.

          Chair McConnehey said he would support sending Certified Mail when a case reached a Notice

          McConnehey, Vice Chair Bloom, and Council Members Green and Whitelock expressed support
          for a trial period for using Certified Mail for all addresses involved. Council Member Pack spoke
          of the wording of notices and expressed interest in taking the conversation further when
          possible.

          Mr. Hudson asked if the Council wanted the Courtesy Notice codified. Council Members Pack,
          Bloom, Green, and Whitelock indicated they were in favor. Chair McConnehey was not sure
          codifying the Courtesy Notice was necessary if it was being done administratively and wanted
          to retain some flexibility. Council Member Green believed the concept could be codified in a
          generic way.

          Presenting the second question for discussion, staff asked the Council to consider if the
          destruction of vicious and dangerous animals should be adjudicated before an Administrative
          Law Judge (ALJ), or in Justice Court. The City Attorney’s Office was confident in the legality of
          the ALJ hearing and deciding the cases; however, there could be some benefits to having those
          cases heard by the Justice Court:



Committee of the Whole Minutes               Wednesday, November 08, 2023                              Page 4
                      •   The same Court and Judge could hear the criminal case against the owner and
                          the destruction of the dangerous animal case.
                      •   The Justice Court could issue a warrant if the animal needed to be seized. The
                          ALJ did not have power to issue warrants.
                      •   The Justice Court heard the cases prior to 2017 and the cases ran smoothly.
                          Animal Control and the Prosecutor’s Office had no concerns with having the
                          Justice Court handle such cases.

          Possible negative consequences to having the cases heard by the Justice Court if an animal
          destruction case became part of the case against the owner:

                      •   In most criminal cases, the defendant had the right to an attorney appointed to
                          defend them. This could create a significant additional expense if the
                          destruction of the animal was part of the case.
                      •   Most criminal cases had a right to a jury trial, which could take six months to a
                          year before finalization. This would create additional expense and concerns
                          with keeping the dog in a City kennel for such a lengthy amount of time.

          Staff answered questions from the Council, and the Council discussed options presented. Mr.
          Chandler said review by the ALJ was usually timelier and tended to cost less. Council Member
          Green commented that the Justice Court was already there and said he would rather have the
          cases heard by a judicial officer than a quasi-judicial officer to avoid any Fourth Amendment
          issues. Council Member Green spoke in favor of amending the Code to allow for dangerous
          animal cases to be handled by either the ALJ or the Justice Court, at the option of Animal Control
          and the Prosecution.

          Council Member Whitelock said she felt the cases would be better heard in Justice Court. Vice
          Chair Bloom said she did not want animals to be kenneled any longer than necessary, and
          expressed support for codifying the ability to choose between the ALJ and the Justice Court,
          whichever was best for the animal.

          Council Member Pack said he believed individuals would take the situation more seriously in a
          Justice Court. He said his main concerns were for time and the health and wellbeing of the
          animal, which he believed would coincide with the health and wellbeing of the owner. Council
          Member Pack expressed support for whichever process would be quicker and taken most
          seriously.

          Council Member Green suggested allowing discretion for whether dangerous animal cases and
          any criminal proceedings against the owner would be heard in Justic Court separately or as one
          case. Mr. Chandler said he believed the Prosecutor’s Office would choose for the cases to be
          handled separately every time. Council Member Green expressed support for including both
          options. Council Member McConnehey expressed support for including the option for cases to
          be heard separately.

          Staff said a draft would be prepared for further Council review.

       c. Discussion to consider amending City Code Section 6-3E-1 Ownership and Maintenance of
          Fowl to include other groups of fowl and consolidate animal code to one Title.
          Council Member Green introduced a proposal to amend City Code regarding ownership and
          maintenance of fowl, initially brought forward by Council Member Pack. He asked if the Council
          would be willing to expand the definition of fowl permitted (currently only pigeons and
          chickens) by only specifying fowl that would be prohibited. Council Member Green said Salt
          Lake County currently prohibited ownership of geese, turkeys, peafowl, and roosters. Council
          Member Green asked if the Council wanted to consolidate all animal references in City Code in
          Title 6.

          Vice Chair Bloom said she owned chickens, and would love to have ducks and turkeys as well.
          She said she would like Code to be consistent with what was already happening in the City.
          Council Member Whitelock said she wanted the Code to be easier to read and understand and
          expressed support for consolidation. Council Member Whitelock was in favor of listing
          prohibited fowl, and asked why it was recommended that turkeys not be allowed.

          Mr. Etchell expressed support for all animal references in the Code being consolidated in one
          title. He recommended prohibiting turkeys and peafowl and would support prohibiting geese.
          The City already prohibited roosters. He said turkeys tended to escape easily and were difficult
          to capture, and peafowl were very noisy and difficult to capture. Mr. Etchell suggested allowing
          ownership of a maximum of five birds total on a non-nuisance basis.

          Chair McConnehey was supportive of changing the definition to include ducks but was hesitant
          to open the Code up further because of possible unintended consequences. He said he would
          prefer to allow a specific maximum number of fowl, as suggested by Mr. Etchell. Chair
          McConnehey expressed support for consolidating Code. He suggested updating the definition
          in a timely manner for Council review and approval and acknowledged that consolidation may
          take longer.

          Council reached a consensus to promptly revise the definition in Section 6 regarding ownership
          and maintenance of fowl to include ducks in non-nuisance spaces. They agreed to revisit an
          amendment to consolidate all codes related to permitted animals at a later date.

       d. Discussion on possible amendments to Title 2 – Executive Branch Committees
          Council Office Director Alan Anderson explained the proposal to amend Title 2 regarding
          Executive Branch Committees, specifically Chapter 1 (General Committee Provisions) and
          Chapter 5 (Title 2 Standing Committees). He presented current General Committee provisions,
          and asked the following questions:
                      •   Should the Council delete codification of certain committees and allow
                          alternatives to committee creation?

          Council Member Green questioned why the Council was involved in the creation of committees
          and suggested eliminating the section on standing committees. Mayor Burton liked the idea of
          having flexibility to create or dissolve committees as needed and liked the idea of the Council
          having the same flexibility.

          Chair McConnehey agreed with giving the Mayor creation/dissolution/appointment flexibility.
          He liked having the opportunity to recognize those who were serving and thank them for their
          service and believed it would be possible to do that without having the committees codified.
          Chair McConnehey said defined terms of service made sense for the Planning Commission, but

          a maximum number of members if standing committees were eliminated. Vice Chair Bloom was

          Council Member Pack expressed the desire for Council to receive an annual report from the

          committees and transitioning to an annual volunteer appreciation event. Council Member
          Whitelock wanted to eliminate Council advice and consent for volunteer committees as well.
          Council Member Pack said he could see the benefit of having a check and balance and would
          want the Council to receive reports regarding who was serving in what capacities.

          Council Member Green felt the ultimate goal was for individuals to volunteer in the city and he
          did not believe the Council needed to be gatekeepers. Chair McConnehey did not think a check
          and balance was needed for those types of committees and he would like to see a regular report
          to the Council, and current lists of individuals serving included on the City website. He noted
          that the consensus was to eliminate council advice and consent for all standing committees.
