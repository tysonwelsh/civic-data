       1. SCHEDULING. The City Recorder informed those at the meeting of
          upcoming events, meetings, activities, etc. Next Mtg—October 26th @ 7pm.

       2. CITIZEN COMMENTS/QUESTIONS.
          Edward Lexes, South Salt Lake resident, wanted to vocalize his issue with
          some of the comments made at the last meeting about how renters increase
          crime and said that he found them to be problematic. He said that he’s read
          studies that show that renters don’t bring in a lot of crime. Regarding the
          concerns over traffic, he believes that it’s not much of a problem for a city that
          increases its population extensively during the day. If there is concern, it
          should be a U-DOT problem. He shared that his main concern is more
          expensive condos that are unaffordable for current residents. With South Salt
          Lake being one of the poorest cities around, he would rather see apartments be
          built that would allow for low-income housing.

          Mellanie Allgood, South Salt Lake resident, still believes there are some
          issues with the pending development. She shared that as a resident for 36
          years she has seen lots of changes, some good and some bad. She wants to see
          developments be positive for their surrounding areas. Her main issues are with
          proximity of the garbage dumpster’s locations and their being close to her
          community’s fence. She is concerned that they will serve as a steppingstone
          for people to jump over into her closed gate community. She invited the
          Council to come to her community to see the proximity between the fences
          and garbage dumpsters.

       6. INFORMATION.
          Victim Advocate, Jodi Fait, presented an update on the VOCA & VAWA
          grant. October is also Domestic Violence Awareness month and insightful
          tools such as the LAP (Lethality Assessment Program) screening helps police
          officers determine the potential risk that victims are in and be able to better
          connect them to appropriate or time sensitive resources when responding to
          domestic violence calls. A handout was also provided, of which a copy is
          attached and incorporated by this reference.
