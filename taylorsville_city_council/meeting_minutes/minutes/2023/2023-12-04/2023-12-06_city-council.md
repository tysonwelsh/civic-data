         1. 2    Mayor' s Report


     M Mayor Kristie Overson congratulated Councilmembers Barbieri, Burgess and Cochran who
had won reelection to an additional four-year term.

The Mayor pointed out a tree in the council chambers which had been purchased at the Festival of
Trees and donated to the City by the Boynton family. It had a military theme and would lend itself
beautifully to the Wreaths Across America ceremony later in the month.

Jade' s Corner Deli had been recognized the previous week with a ribbon- cutting. Enjoyed
celebrating new businesses in the city. The food was delicious and as always, she thoroughly
enjoyed welcoming new businesses to Taylorsville.

Mayor Overson explained that she attended a Council of Mayors meeting each month as well as a
Council of Governments meeting on a quarterly basis.         Regular topics of discussion with both
groups were homelessness and the affordable housing crisis. She hoped that together they would
find good solutions to both very serious problems. She told those gathered that they could expect
to hear more about each in the future.


The Mayor had recently attended a Wasatch Choice Vision Workshop hosted by the Wasatch Front
Regional Council with representatives of other cities as well. She described it as a great experience
to network with neighboring cities and discuss how to connect our own cities with trails and also
with our neighboring municipalities. She had appreciated the opportunity to collaborate.

The Taylorsville Food Pantry had recently received a remarkable donation of 14, 000 lbs( 7 pallets)
of food valued at almost $ 50,000. The donation had been made by Daily' s Premium Meats, and

Mayor Overson wished to thank them for their extreme generosity. She also wished to thank the
dedicated volunteers who kept the Pantry functioning on a weekly basis.

At the invitation of Congressman Burgess Owens, she had recently attended the Hispanic Heritage
Congressional Recognition ceremony at the State Capitol. The purpose of the ceremony was to
honor those who had made a real difference serving their communities. She was particularly
grateful to be able to celebrate the efforts of Taylorsville resident Jair Benitez.


Taylorsville' s 2nd Annual tree- lighting ceremony had taken place the previous week. The madrigal
choir from Taylorsville High entertained the crowd of about 250 people. Hot cocoa was enjoyed
by all before the countdown to the tree- lighting took place. In the Spring, the tree would be planted
in a city park.

The Chairs of all the city committees recently met with Jay Ziolkowski and City Attorney Tracy
Cowdell to learn about city policies/procedures that they needed to keep in mind during their
volunteer work. She greatly appreciated their efforts in providing this training to the chairs.

Summit Vista had recently hosted their" Iron Person" race. Approximately 40 participants swam
a   total of 4, 000 meters, biked 112 miles and ran a 26. 2- mile marathon over the course of two

He looked forward to warmer weather when he expected his clientele to make use of the new

5.       PLANNING MATTERS


         5. 1     Discussion Regarding the Taylorsville General Plan Update, Chapter 4:
                  Mobility– Mark McGrath

 El    Long-Range Planner Mark McGrath presented information to the City Council regarding
changes made to Chapter 4: Mobility. He reminded the council members of the five guiding
principles that would be threaded throughout the general plan. They were:
        Balance, Diversity, and Social Equity
         Stewardship
         Resilience
Taylorsville City Council Minutes
December 6, 2023
Page 7




         Civil Beauty and Character
         Health and Wellness


There were also the four fundamental specific strategies that had previously been identified.
They were:
       Strength in community identity and character
         Create vibrant economic centers and mixed-use neighborhoods;
         Maintain and create distinguishable, stable, and desirable neighborhoods;
         Create a multi- modal community;

One aspect of the chapter that he still intended to develop was to identify a way to quantify the
above strategies so that success could be measured.


Mr. McGrath displayed information regarding the existing bus routes that travelled through
Taylorsville as well as statistical data pertaining to the increase of annual average daily traffic in
the city from 2011 to 2019. He also pointed out that for a city the size of Taylorsville, the number
of existing trails and bicycle lanes was inadequate. He had heard the same from members of the
public. He pointed out that Taylorsville was the largest city in Salt Lake County that was not
currently tied into the regional transit system, despite being the second most densely populated
city in Utah and right in the middle of the Salt Lake Valley.

Mr. McGrath explained that the document being drafted sought to find a better balance of
transportation modes in the city and to advocate for a new transit line in the city. Active
transportation improvements needed to be prioritized and the streets needed beautification. It
would increase civic pride and economic development. He pointed out that there was a great deal
of grant funding available for projects like these and he believed that if the city made it a priority,
a great deal could be accomplished in active transportation improvements in Taylorsville.


Councilmember Harker was excited about the plan and hoped she would be able to enjoy more
trails while she was still young enough to ride her bike.

Councilmember Cochran inquired if there had been any traction on the idea of having an elevated
trail system above the canal. Mr. McGrath indicated he had heard no more discussion on the idea.


Councilmember Cochran also asked if there was any talk of a subway system in Utah. Mr. McGrath
responded that there had been discussions at one time, but the cost was prohibitive.
