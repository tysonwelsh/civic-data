PC 23-040 Logan Station [Design Review Permit] Michael Jewell/Triiio QOZB Il LLC, authorized
agent/owner are requesting a design review permit for a mixed-use development that includes 543
residential units and 58,000 square feet of commercial space located on 31.39 acres at
approximately 1000 West 200 South in the Mixed Use (MU) zone; TIN 05-064-0028, 05-064-0013,
06-002-0003 (Ellis Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Senior Planner reviewed the Design Review request for a 31-acre Mixed-Use
project located at the Northeast corner of 1000 West and 200 South. The development includes
54,000 square feet of commercial space and 543 residential units. The project consists of
townhomes, multi-story apartments, vertical mixed-use buildings, office space, and freestanding
retail/restaurant buildings.

R. Holley reviewed the density and commercial requirements. The Land Development Code sets a
base density in the Mixed Use zone at 5-20 units (min-max) per acre of land with up to an additional
30 units/acre based on specific density bonuses. As proposed, the overall density for this project is
17.2 units per acre. Phase one is shown at 13.4 units per acre. Progressively and collectively
throughout the subsequential phases, density is up to 21 units per acre (phases 2-5) and then lowers
to below 20 units per acre for the last phases of the project.

This project site is required to have a total of 51,390 Square Feet of commercial space. As proposed
the project is at 54,000 SF of commercial space. However, phases 8-9 do not provide proportionate
commercial space and only with the development of the final 10" phase, the overall total commercial
square footage requirements are met.

The townhomes in phases 1, 8, and 9 with side facades adjacent to 200 South and 100 South do
not meet the required 20-foot setback. As conditioned to meet residential setbacks the project meets
the Land Development Requirement.

The Land Development Code requires a minimum of 60 percent building frontage in the Mixed Use
zone. If the proposed project is looked at per individual block front, building frontage percentages
range from 33-94 percent with eight block frontages being below the required 60 percent. If the
frontage is calculated by block/street frontages cumulatively, and then averaged based on the
number of street frontages, three of the four blocks fall under the required 60 percent at 56, 52, 58,
and 65 percent. Calculating by cumulative block frontages results in closer compliance to the 60
percent, with only 8 percent out of compliance. The Planning Commission will need to concur with
this approach. As conditioned with frontage averages at 60 percent or more, the project complies
with the Land Development Code.

The Land Development Code requires arterial and collector streets to be fronted with commercial
spaces on the main floor. As proposed, phase 1 shows townhomes fronting 200 South, which is
planned to be a collector street. The commission could require commercial or mixed-use buildings
to be added along 200 South in phase 1, or additional green space could be added in lieu of
commercial, but the code does not specify the exact size of green space that would be needed.


The Land Development Code requires multi-family buildings to vary from one another, have four-
sided architecture, contain a mix of materials that wrap all four sides of buildings, and prohibits blank
walls exceeding 40 linear feet. All the proposed townhomes have the same facade design and some
of the buildings are oriented sideways to the street. The Planning Commission will need to consider
and determine the best way to achieve building variations without compromising the modern design.
Townhomes should be orientated facing either a street or a green courtyard while minimizing side
face street orientation. In cases when side fagade street orientation is unavoidable, four-side
architecture should be utilized for better curb appeal.

The Land Development Code requires a total of 273,469 Square Feet of landscaping and useable
outdoor space for this site. The site plan shows several landscaped and useable outdoor gathering
areas that appear to go above and beyond the required. This will be calculated at the building permit
stage to verify full compliance.

Russ reviewed the public comments received and the following updated conditions: Remove
Condition #19 requiring fencing along the west side; Remove Engineering-General, bullet point 11
requiring wetlands mitigation; and reference Engineering-Streets, bullet points 5 and 6 have been

PC 23-042 LDC Amendment 17.27 - Planned Development Overlay Zone [Code Amendment]
Mike DeSimone/Logan City are requesting a code amendment to the Land Development Code,
Chapter 17.27 to include a Planned Development (PD) Overlay zone.

STAFF/ PROPONENT: Russ Holley reviewed the proposed Land Development Code amendment
to add a Planned Development (PD) Overlay Zone to the Land Development Code that will allow
developments to propose code for their specific project within set standards and specifications. This
will only be an option for unique well-planned developments that would not be possible under one of
the existing zones. The PD Overlay zone may be allowed at the discretion of the Planning
Commission and the City Council and may only be applied to sites larger than three acres.

A concept plan review will be submitted for staff, Planning Commission, and City Council in a
workshop setting, with no formal action, to allow the applicant to receive feedback, comments, ideas,
and direction which the applicant can use to prepare their formal submittal. for review, followed by
the formal application for a Zone Amendment, Code Amendment, and Project Review.

When the PD Overlay Zone was workshopped at City Council, the possibility of allowing public
comments during the Concept Plan phase was brought up. Commissioner Doutre stated that she
liked the idea of public comments on the concept plan. Commissioner Lewis said he agreed and
bringing in the public would prevent the perception that the planning was done “under the table’.
Commissioner Heare said his only concern with that is there is not an actual project to comment on
at the concept phase. R. Holley said that the public may bring up ideas that the Planning Commission
or City Council had not thought of.

Jeannie Simmonds said that the public should be able to weigh in on the project while it is still in the
development stage. Commissioner Heare brought up the Sugar Creek development and how they
met with the neighborhood to address concerns with the project. Commissioner Doutre said that if
there are strong concerns with the neighbors it should be discussed during the concept plan so their
concerns can be addressed before the plan is developed.

PUBLIC: Joel Nelson thanked the commission for considering public input and mentioned doing a
public survey. .

Sue Sorensen said she would like to make sure that developers can’t choose this option just to avoid
applying for a zone change and that she loves the idea of allowing public comments.

Crystal Burmingham spoke about a roundabout that was proposed in her neighborhood that didn’t
end up happening because the neighbors didn’t want it. She also thanked the commission for their
consideration to add public comments during the Concept Plan.

Katie Lee-Koven said she agrees with allowing public comments up front, before project
development and asked if a minimum of 3 acres for a PD was too small.

COMMISSION: The commission discussed the size minimum for a PD. R. Holley said that
regardless of the size, a PD is not intended for uses where the proposed development is reasonably
feasible under the city’s existing zoning classifications.
