    b. Redevelopment Agency (RDA) and Community Reinvestment Area (CDR) Update

Randy Sant, via video conferencing, presented information regarding the Riverton City
Redevelopment Agency: Downtown East Community Reinvestment Project Area. He said that
on October 15, 2019, a resolution was adopted creating the Riverton Downtown East
Community Reinvestment Project Area, which included expansion of the remnant parcels from
12600 south to Bangerter Highway to align to the east with properties in a north south formation
to Nicholas Lane from Redwood Road.

Mr. Sant reported that, in working with Advisors Consulting Services for the development of the
City’s Downtown Economic Development Plan, their scope of work was to determine where they
were, where they wanted to go, and how they were going to get there, what would get in the
way of getting there and how do we determine success in regards to a Community
Reinvestment Project Area (CRA). He said they had reviewed the demographics and conditions,
as well as existing studies that had been done. He said they had reviewed the real estate
market in significant detail to determine development opportunities in the CRA. He said they had
also worked to determine strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and challenges, which would
be part of putting together an Economic Development Tool Box. He said a significant part would
be looking at the availability of creating Community Reinvestment Areas within the RDA Study
Area. He then asked how many areas should be created; one large project area, or several
smaller project areas. He said that a budget needed to be prepared, which included the affected
taxing entities that would seem right for their participation. He said that they had tried to identify
the areas that they thought would develop first and recommended project areas, i.e. parcels
along 12600 South, parcels along Redwood Road, the rodeo grounds, as well as other areas.
He said there were 28 steps to creating a CRA, which could take three to seven months. He
said that real estate developers have indicated that there were development opportunities within
the corridor.

Mr. Sant then addressed questions from the Mayor and Council Members and explained that
the ability exists, under the RDA law, to transfer increment from one project area to another. He
then said that to begin with, it would be better to create one CRA; however, everything could be

City Attorney Ryan Carter referred to RCC 18.225.080 Accessory Dwelling Units, and explained
that they were limited to all single-family zones, provided the lot under consideration for an ADU
could meet all technical requirements and applicable building/fire codes. He further explained
that an ADU may be created through converting an existing living area within a single-family
dwelling, as an addition to an existing single-family dwelling, or within a single-family dwelling
created as new construction; converting an existing detached accessory building, as an
addition to an existing accessory building; or as a newly constructed accessory building.

Mr. Carter further explained that the City could only permit an ADU when an owner occupant
lived on the property within either the principal or ADU, and the total number of residents
residing in an ADU may not exceed the number allowed for a “family” for the lot on which the
ADU was located. He then explained that a “family” means one person living alone or a group of
individuals who share ties of blood, marriage, or adoption; a group residing together and
consisting of parents, children, and other relatives by blood or marriage; a group of individuals
residing together who have consented to an arrangement similar to ties of blood or marriage; or
a group not to exceed four unrelated persons living together as a single household unit.

13. Recognitions/ Presentations

    a. Riverton Police Department Report

Chief Hutson reported that Officer Kevin Salmon attended a White House event on July 4th
honoring police officers injured by protestors across the country. Chief Hutson then reported
that experienced Officer Zach Hutto was a newly hired police officer.

Chief Hutson reported that in the month of June there had been a slight increase in the number
of vehicle burglaries in the city during the previous month, which prompted the initiation of a
program of reminding citizens about the dangers of leaving valuable items in their vehicle,
specifically where potential vehicle burglars could see them. He said they were targeting parking
lots in the major retail areas and placing pamphlets on windshields of all the cars in the lot with
a message reminding people to secure their belongings in a safe place. Also, he said they were
partnering with some of the businesses to place a pamphlet in their business to remind
customers to lock their cars and hide their valuables.

Chief Hutson said that they were continuing their efforts to keep the streets safe by focusing on
DUI enforcement and that resulted in 17 DUI arrests for drugs and alcohol, thus keeping the
roadways more safe. Additionally, he said their call volume seemed to be increasing as they
emerge from the pandemic and slowly work toward more normalcy.

Chief Hutson then expressed appreciation to the citizens of Riverton who have gone out of their
way to show their support for the RPD by writing letters, personally stopping by and bringing
gifts, and offering words of encouragement. He said that it had really helped their morale and
boosted their spirits.

Chief Hutson then reviewed the June 2020 Patrol Activity Report Riverton Police Department.

Chief Hutson explained their “Smash & Grab” cards that the police officers use to inform citizens
of their vulnerability of someone breaking into their parked car to grab their personal items that
were left in sight. He then expressed his appreciation for the generosity and support that the
Police Department had received over the past month from the citizens of Riverton.

16. Discussion/Action Items

    a. General Plan Amendment, Land Use Map, a Proposed City-wide Amendment to
       the Land Use Map of the Riverton City General Plan

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that Riverton City had for some
time been moving forward with a scheduled amendment to the Land Use Map of the Riverton
City General Plan. He said the Land Use Map indicated projected future land uses for property
within the City, and served as a guide for development, and re-development of property. He said
the City Council and the Planning Commission had met to discuss land uses, and several public
open-house style meetings had been held.

Mr. Lethbridge said that the Planning Commission, following public hearings, forwarded a
recommended draft Land Use Plan to the City Council. He explained that the Planning
Commission and the City Council differ in two areas of the General Plan map, which he
presented and explained as follows:

         1. The Autumn Hills area along 13400 South and 5600 East. The
            recommendation to the Planning       Commission     was      Community
            Commercial; however, the Planning Commission did not include that in their
            recommendation forwarded to the Council; they recommended 13400 South
            frontage only be designated Community Commercial.

Council Member Stewart said that he had met with two remaining residents that front 13400
south and he said there was possibly one resident that did not want this in the General Plan. He
said that since three lanes had been added to 13400 south, those residents would like to see
options. He said he would like to this see this back in the draft and spoke in favor of the
designating the entire block Community Commercial, which would provide the option of
