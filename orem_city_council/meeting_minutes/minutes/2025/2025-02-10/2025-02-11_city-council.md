Planning Commission Process Discussion Presenter: Gary McGinn, Community Development
Director and Madeline Komen, Planning Commission Chairman

Mr. McGinn introduced Madeline Komen and asked her to speak about the current
planning commission processes. Ms. Komen, reappointed chair of the Orem Planning
Commission, presented an overview of her background and the commission's role, emphasizing

significant improvements made over the past year and a half. With a law degree and a passion for
community planning, Ms. Komen highlighted her experience in municipal law and her
dedication to serving the Orem community. She explained that the commission acts as a
recommending body to the city council on legislative matters and handles certain administrative
decisions, stressing the importance of their role as citizen representatives. Ms. Komen detailed
numerous initiatives aimed at enhancing the commission's effectiveness, including
comprehensive training on zoning, legislative procedures, and ethical conduct. Meeting
processes were formalized with revised motion procedures, consent agendas, and a shift towards
developer-led presentations. Staff reports were improved, and efforts were made to enhance
public interaction through improved etiquette and education. The commission adopted bylaws,
established the option for subcommittees, and developed resource binders to aid commissioners.
Ms. Komen also emphasized the importance of ongoing discussions regarding noticing
procedures and neighborhood meetings. Looking forward, she advocated for more formal
dialogue between the commission and the city council to address high-level planning issues and
proactively shape Orem's future.

The City Council expressed gratitude for Ms. Komen and the planning commission. The
City Council was interested in the binders and cheat sheets that the planning commission had
built. City Staff agreed to get the same binders and cheat sheets for the City Council. Mayor
Young commented that he was impressed that this planning commission took the time to do a
deep dig into the transportation master plan. They were instrumental in improving that plan.

Mr. Macdonald began a discussion about low and high density, he wondered if the City
Council and Planning Commission had the same definitions. After a discussion among the City
Council members, Mr. Clark clarified that in the Orem general plan it defines low, medium and
high density. Low density is R8, R12 and R20, medium density is PRD zones, R6, R6.5 and
R7.5, and high density is anything above. In the general plan, anything adove 7 units per acre is
high density and medium density is 4-7 units per acre. Mr. McGinn explained that information is
something that could be put into a cheat sheet for the City Council. Mayor Young expressed his
concern with any level of density that parking is sufficient for the area.

The City Council discusses with Ms. Komen diversity on the planning commission. Ms.
Komen gave her approach as the chair with three words: Civility, Collaboration and
Professionalism. She explained that many of the planning commission members come to the
same conclusion from different perspectives. She assured the City Council that when the
planning commission gives a 7-0 vote on a recommendation, it is not without discussion and
consideration. She agrees that diversity is a benefit to any commission and she believes the
current Planning Commission has this type of diversity. Mr. Spencer presented to the council the
planning commission votes compared to the City Council votes for the previous two years. This
comparison showed that the Planning Commission and City Council had voted very similarly. He
spoke about the January 14th City Council meeting and was concerned about comments made in
the meeting. He explained that the voting records of Planning Commission vs. the City Council
demonstrated that the Planning Commission is inline with the City Council. Many of the City

Council members expressed gratitude for this discussion and that this has been informative. Ms.
Komen invited the City Council to feel free to reach out to the Planning Commission with further
questions.

Mr. McGinn provided a brief overview of the Orem Planning Commission's appointment
process, highlighting that members apply online and are appointed by the mayor with council
approval, serving two consecutive terms and without political affiliation considerations. He also
presented research showing that this mayoral appointment model is common, with exceptions
like West Valley City and the unique district-based system in South Jordan. McGinn then
suggested potential improvements, including proactive council notification of upcoming
appointments and revisions to the application form to manage the volume of submissions. The
City Council and staff discussed other improvements they would like to see in the new boards
and commissions program.

Ms. Millett expresses disagreement with the idea of requiring two council liaisons for the
Planning Commission. She believes the current seven-member size doesn't necessitate it, citing
other committees with more complex needs that utilize two liaisons. While acknowledging the
time constraints of council members, she questioned the need to increase their responsibilities.
She expressed satisfaction with the proposed improvements to the application process and the
existing checks and balances provided by staff and the current liaison. She concluded by seeking
feedback from fellow council members on whether they strongly support the two-liaison
requirement, or if understanding the process better changes their perspective. Ms. Millett
encouraged their colleagues to identify any specific issues that a second liaison would address.
The City Council discussed this change and agreed to keep one council member on the planning
commission with the invitation for any council members to attend any planning meetings as their
schedule allows.

Mr. Bybee assured the council that the staff has taken good notes on the council’s
suggestions to improve the processes and he will work on a joint City Council/Planning
Commission work sessions before May 2025.

National Association of School R r fficers (NASRO) Award R nition Presenter:
Josh Adams, Police Chief

Chief Adams explained that at the annual conference for NASRO, the Orem SRO
program was recognized as one of only six model agencies across the United States and the first
ever from the State of Utah. The SRO program has been the go-to and beta tester for law
enforcement and school collaboration for Alpine School District. Each SRO has completed the
NAZRO program and two of our SRO are Advance SRO level. Chief Adams highlighted some
of the services provided by our SRO program, which include prepared and updated individual
response plans for every public charter and private school in Orem every year, annual safety
assessments, assisted schools in applying and receiving grant funding to improve school safety,
and at the beginning of each school year our SRO’s and the Chief of Police meet individually
with the administration of everyone of the 21 schools in Orem. The SRO program added a dog to
the team that has been beneficial in the schools and with the students.

Legislative Priorities Presentation Presenter: Nelson Abbott, Utah State Representative

Representative Abbott reported that legislation is about halfway done and that the base
budget has passed and balanced. The legislature has set aside about $150 million for some
possible tax cuts. They are particularly concerned about older Utahns with limited incomes. The
legislature just passed a bill that cracks down on those who traffic in Fentanyl. Representative
Abbott has been working on the Utah Debate Commission which is trying to move down to
UVU.

Ms. Millett asked that Representative Abbott keep an eye on Senator Grover’s bill with
the school district reconfiguration and that the needs are met for our incoming school district.
Mr. Bybee expressed gratitude for Representative Abbott and for his willingness to be accessible
for the City of Orem. Mr. Bybee highlighted some of the concerns for Orem, which are land use

control on a local level, the jurisdictional transfer of the Lakeview Corridor to the State, defining
and regulating short term rentals, utility transportation fees, and looking at the canals in the City
and the opportunity to turn those into trails.
