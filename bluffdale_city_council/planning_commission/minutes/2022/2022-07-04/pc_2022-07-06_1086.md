       Zone located at 14223 South 3450 West, Darrell and Kenra Keddington, Applicants
       (Application 2021-15). Staff Presenter, Jennifer Robison.

Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison, presented the staff report and stated that the subject property
was originally just over two acres and was created in 1987 as part of the Boulden Acres project.
The applicants are proposing to split it into two one-acre lots, which meets the lot size requirement

project moves forward, they will put in the required improvements of curb, gutter, sidewalk, and
park strip along the frontage of both parcels. The existing home will be on Lot 2 and Lot 1 will be a
new lot for a potential dwelling unit.

Mrs. Robison next reviewed what the proposed subdivision would look like. It will meet all
requirements associated with the R-1-43 zone in terms of lot widths, lot frontages, and lot setbacks.
The applicants currently have the water shares needed to move toward the final plat. The
Engineering Department has conducted its reviews and has given its stamp of approval as well.

reported that the lot currently has flood irrigation but will change to pressurized secondary water.

7.     Planning Commissioners HB-409 Training on Conditional Use Permits, Zoning, and
       Land Use. Staff Presenter, Grant Crowell, Zoning Administrator.

Mr. Crowell reported the above topic is very broad in scope. There was discussion on how cities
are planned out. Bluffdale was built by developers who have a significant say in how properties are
designed and built. Zoning is used to ensure health, safety, and general welfare. Cities and counties
have the legal authority to adopt their land use plans and ordinances. Every year, the Utah
Legislature enacts modifications to the Land Use Code. Every five or 10 years, the modifications
are significant. This past year, the Utah Legislature updated the housing rules and what cities can
do in tenns of retail incentives. Cities regulate growth and administer their respective Municipal
Code. Recently, Bluffdale went through the visioning process to update the General Plan, which
sought to be forward-looking. The State was concerned about addressing land use, housing,
transportation, and water conservation. The Bluffdale City Code is available online. Title 11
addresses Zoning and Title 12 addresses Subdivision.

Mr. Crowell next addressed the dichotomy between legislative and administrative land use
decisions. Zoning, the General Plan Map/Text, and Development Agreements are legislative land
use decisions that must be made by the City Council. Decisions that are challenged go to the
District Court. Administrative land use decisions include subdivisions, conditional uses, and site
plans. They should be based on adopted local and state codes as written in the specific language
and development agreement contracts, and substantial evidence on the record. There is a difference
between "public clamor" and "public information." The former cannot b e the basis of decisions.
The latter might provide information that had been overlooked but has significant relevance to the
decision being made. With administrative decisions, if a request meets the City Code, State Code,
or International Building Code, it must be approved, regardless of whether or not the Land Use
Authority likes it.

Evidence on the record is vital to defending or overturning decisions. When Staff Reports are
created for review by the Planning Commission, they have gone through multiple iterations after

being reviewed by the different City Departments. Some land use decisions require a public
hearing, and some do not. Sometimes the Land Use Authority might be the Planning Commission,
or it might be the City Council. In some instances, the Zoning Administrator can be the land use
authority. In rare instances, the City Engineer or City Manager could also be the land use authority.
Each land use authority has its own appeal process when a decision is being challenged. The City
Council is rarely the appeal authority because the City has an Appeals and Variance Hearing
Officer, who acts in a quasi-judicial capacity.

Mr. Crowell stated that the City Code includes a Land Use Table that was created by the City
Council in its legislative capacity. Consequently, land uses have already been determined for each
zone. The land uses are designated as Permitted, Conditional, or Prohibited. Conditional use
requests are very rare because they must be approved if they meet all of the land use and building
standards. If there are potentially detrimental effects that need to be mitigated, it is critical to
remember that "mitigated" does not mean "eliminated." The burden of proof to assert detrimental
effects rests heavily on the city. In addition, the State Code does not require public hearings for
conditional uses because public clamor is not a legal reason for denying a conditional use.

Mr. Crowell displayed the Zoning Map and asked what a reasonable condition might be to mitigate
the potentially harmful impacts of, for example, a plant nursery. Hours of operation was suggested
as a potential condition to implement. Dust was also identified as a potential harmful impact. It
could be mitigated by requiring more paving or compacted asphalt. This is an interesting situation
because Bluffdale does not regulate air quality. That issue is regulated by a different agency.
Parking and circulation would be addressed by City Code. A visual barrier might also be required,
as per the City's landscaping requirements in the Site Plan Ordinance. Mr. Crowell enumerated a
variety of considerations that might be discussed to enhance safety.

Denial of a conditional use application is possible, but the City would have to have solid
documentation to substantiate its claims that hazards of the use could not possibly be adequately
mitigated to allow the proposed land use. He noted an example of a conditional use that the
Planning Commission denied, but the appeal to the City Council reversed the decision.

A conditional use application was expected to come before the Planning Commission for an assisted
living facility. It will also include a site plan review.
