The City’s Budget is based on a July through June fiscal year. By law, it must be balanced,
meaning budgeted revenues must equal expenditures. The projected General Fund balance must
be positive, with an unappropriated balance between 5% and 35%.
The Final Operating Budget includes a General Fund Budget of approximately $17.3 million based
on the following:

e Revenues are conservative estimates to reduce risk and ensure financial resilience.

e Continued investment in public safety.

e Major infrastructure projects underway.

e Strategic staffing increases in critical service areas.

e Maintaining services while preparing for future growth.
Budget priorities include:

e Public safety ;

e Infrastructure and transportation

e Water system reliability

e Parks, recreation, and community events

e Fiscal sustainability
Bluffdale practices a conservative budgeting approach by compiling projected revenues and
adjusted expenditures (appropriations) to ensure that they balance. The budgeted appropriations
are the maximum amount the City is allowed to spend. If requests are higher than those estimates,
Staff must fund ways to prioritize and adjust. In the FY2026-2027 budget, discretionary spending
was reduced in some areas to ensure limited use of Fund balance, essential services were
prioritized, and staffing growth was carefully managed. Budgeted revenues were estimates that
could be affected by the economy or a change in consumer spending habits.

In the budgeting process, revenue and expenditure types are matched. Primary services and
discretionary expenditures are categorized as recurring and non-recurring. Examples of primary
services are street cleaning and park maintenance. Discretionary expenditures that were reduced
in the current Budget included education, training, travel, shop charges, and office supplies.
Recurring expenses include items like park restroom maintenance, and nonrecurring expenses
include items like cleanup of vandalism or flooding at a City park. Ms. Thayer reported that
recurring expenses should always be funded with recurring revenues, and the most critical
recurring expenses should be funded with the most stable recurring revenues.
Municipal budgets and their guiding principles differ from business budgets. The Bluffdale
Budget is based on fund accounting, which has separate funds for specific purposes. Fund
accounting focuses on accountability and compliance rather than profitability. All funds except
the General Fund are restricted to specific uses and cannot be used for other purposes.
e General Fund
© Most City services and operating expenses. As the City is a service organization,
most expenditures are personnel costs.
e Water Operating Fund
o Arestricted fund that includes depreciation like a typical business budget.
e Restricted Funds are set up to collect and expend money for specific purposes.
o Police and Fire Protection Fund
o Capital Projects Fund
o Roads Fund
o Impact Fees
o Redevelopment Agency Funds
o Local Building Authority Fund
* Facilitates construction and financing of City buildings like City Hall and
the Public Works complex.
