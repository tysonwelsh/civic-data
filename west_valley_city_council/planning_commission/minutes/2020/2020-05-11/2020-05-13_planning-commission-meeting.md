Z-3-2020
Richard Defa
3793 South 5725 West
Zone change from A to R-1-8
1.5 acres

Richard Defa has submitted a zone change application for a 1.5 acre parcel located at 3793 South
5725 West. The property is currently zoned A (agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre) with a
General Plan designation of low density residential (3 to 4 units/acre). The proposed zone is R-1-
8 (single unit dwelling residential, minimum lot size 8,000 sq. ft.).

Surrounding zones include R-1-7 (single unit dwelling residential, minimum lot size 7,000 sq. ft.)
to the north and west and A to the south and east. The Planning Commission recommended
approval on a zone change from A to RE (residential estates) on the property to the south. As a
reminder, City ordinance only allows zone changes to R-1-8 on properties less than 2 acres. The
property to the south is about 8 acres.

Surrounding uses include single unit dwellings to the north and west and vacant property to the
south and east. The subject property includes a home that was built in 1909 that will be
demolished.

The applicant has submitted a concept plan that shows the subject property being subdivided into
5 lots. The average lot size is 10,487 sq. ft.

The Zoning Ordinance requires that “All new Subdivisions involving a rezone of property, or a
PUD, shall participate in a Development Agreement that addresses housing size, quality, exterior
finish materials, Streetscapes, Landscaping, etc.” The applicant has committed to build homes
that meet the City’s design standards.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. The application should be approved as it complies with the General Plan. The
      approval is made subject to a development agreement that requires that the new homes
      meet the City’s design standards.

   2. Continuance.

   3. Denial.

GPZ-3-2020
Joe Salisbury
2892 S Decker Lake Dr.
General plan change from commercial/light manufacturing to high density residential and
zone change from A and M to RM
16.05 acres

Joe Salisbury with DAI has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for 16.05 acres of
property located at 2892 S Decker Lake Dr. The property is currently zoned A (agriculture,
minimum lot size ½ acre) and M (manufacturing) with a General Plan designation of
commercial/light manufacturing. The proposed zone is RM (multiple unit dwelling residential)
and the proposed General Plan designation is high density residential (12 to 20 units/acre).
Surrounding zones include A and RM to the west, A and M to the north, M to the east and M and
RM to the south. Surrounding land uses include the E Gate Apartments, Decker Lake Youth
Detention Facility and Decker Lake Park to the west and north and office uses like Petzl and the
Department of Homeland Security to the east and south. The subject property is currently vacant.

The applicant is proposing a total of 320 housing units including 247 apartments and 73
townhomes with 55 of the townhomes being rear load and 18 being front load. The townhomes
would be two-story buildings, range in size from 2 to 4 bedrooms and include two-car garages
for each unit. Each apartment unit would have one garage space in either a tuck-under garage or a
detached garage. While the applicant did provide a couple of potential apartment renderings,
details as to the unit mix and unit sizes were not provided. The attached concept plan and
application materials from the applicant provide details on and support for the proposed project.

By way of background, a General Plan/zone change request was made on this same property
back in 2013 by DAI for a 368-unit apartment development with a density of 23 units/acre. The
Planning Commission recommended denial to the City Council and the City Council denied the
application.

Just last year, DAI submitted another General Plan/zone change request on this same property for
4.8 acres of office/warehouse and 136 townhomes. Shortly after their submittal, the City initiated
a moratorium on all zone changes to the RM zone. Although DAI’s application was submitted
before the moratorium took effect, they chose to postpone their application while the Council
considered potential code changes. Once the Council adopted several code changes relating to
multi-unit residential design standards and RM zone eligibility criteria, DAI chose to withdraw
their previous application and then submitted this application.

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for an apartment development?

   2. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further City goals?

These two questions are explored below.

Is this the right location for an apartment development?

When considering this question, there are many aspects to consider including recent code
changes, transportation infrastructure, neighboring land uses and balancing City objectives across
the City. Each of these topics is addressed below.

Recent Code Changes

One consideration is the City Council recently amended the criteria to determine whether a
property would be eligible to apply for the RM zone. Prior to this change, the subject property
was not eligible to be rezoned to the RM zone. The recent code change made properties located
on the west side of Redwood Road and within ½ mile of the Decker Lake or Redwood Junction
light rail stations eligible to be rezoned to RM. The subject property is within ½ mile of both
stations. While the property is eligible for RM zoning, the City may still deny requests for RM
zoning.
Transportation Infrastructure

One of the recent code changes made requires a traffic impact study prepared by a registered
traffic engineer for projects with 100 or more dwelling units that are requesting RM zoning. This
should have been submitted with the application; however, since this is a new requirement, staff
missed this in the initial application review. If the Commission is in favor of residential use on
this site, staff recommends that the required traffic impact study be submitted prior to the
Planning Commission making a recommendation to the City Council.

Neighboring Land Use

Neighboring uses that seem the most compatible with the proposed residential development
include the E Gate Apartments and Decker Lake Park. The Decker Lake Youth Detention
Facility and neighboring office space seem more compatible with the current General Plan
designation of commercial/light manufacturing.

Another land use consideration for the area is the number of existing or proposed apartments.
The neighboring E Gate Apartments include 304 units and the West Pointe Apartments to the
south include 104 units. The City also just received a General Plan/zone change application for
the property on the northwest corner of 3100 S Decker Lake Dr. where 225 apartment units are
proposed. If the two proposed apartment developments are constructed there will be 953 units
within ¼ mile of each other.



Balancing City Objectives Across the City

When reviewing a General Plan/zone change for apartments in a specific area of the City, the
Commission should consider not only site-specific issues but citywide goals as well. Given the
regular inquiries staff receives regarding apartment development, it seems clear that apartments,
if allowed, would be built just about anywhere in the City. However, doing so would preclude
other important objectives from being accomplished and would put our housing stock out of
balance. Therefore, deciding where to approve zoning for new apartment developments takes
careful consideration of both area specific issues and citywide goals.

Below are statements within the General Plan in quotations that highlight the need for a
comprehensive view when considering a zone change for apartments along with commentary
from staff:

      “In areas where retail development is aging (at least 30 years old) and underperforming
       and where transit is readily accessible, consider the addition of some multi-family
       residential as part of a redevelopment strategy to add more retail demand.” This objective
       was part of the reason for approving apartments on the old Kmart site on Redwood Road
       as well as the apartments at Valley Fair Mall.

      “Maintain zoning in areas currently zoned for office or industrial development to allow
       opportunities for new commercial development.” This seems to be the part of the reason
       that a request for apartments on this site was denied in 2013.

       “West Valley City should work to maintain at least one job for each person in the labor
       force within the City.”
      “When considering rezone requests for high density residential development, the
       proximity to transit, the size of the project and the proposed level of quality (i.e. exterior
       and interior finishes and amenities) should be primary factors for evaluation along with
       other site-specific considerations.” The proposed level of quality is addressed later in this
       report.

      “Redevelop blighted multi-family residential with higher density residential.” Examples
       of this would be the redevelopment that occurred on Harvey Street and in Fairbourne
       Station. There are other areas in the City where this approach will be needed.

In summary, when considering where to approve apartment developments, the City should
balance considering area specific issues which have already been discussed, providing retail
support within retail areas, preserving land for job creating uses, supporting transit and
redeveloping blighted areas. This site has no neighboring retail, would consume land that could
be used for job creating uses and is vacant or not needing redevelopment. A residential use on
this site would likely support transit to some degree.



Does the quality of the project further City goals?

City Code requires requests for the RM zone to be accompanied with a development agreement
proposal that addresses dwelling unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, landscaping and
project amenities. Each of these items is addressed below. As a reminder, the recent code
changes mentioned earlier included several changes to the City’s design standards for multiple
unit dwelling residential. In the sections that follow, there are several deficiencies identified in
the applicant’s proposal when compared with the recent code changes. The applicant was made
aware of these deficiencies and is working on a revised proposal.

Dwelling Unit Sizes

The proposed project includes a mix of townhomes with two-bedroom, three-bedroom and four-
bedroom units. Since the fourth bedroom is in the basement and basement area is not considered
for minimum area, we will consider the minimum sizes for two-bedroom and three-bedroom
townhome units. As described below, some of the proposed unit sizes are below the minimum
required for units that are attached to the side of each other.
            Unit Type                  Minimum Size from               Proposed Size (Sq. Ft.)
                                       Ordinance (Sq. Ft.)
 2-bedroom townhome                                   1,100                                   1,037
 3-bedroom townhome                                   1,400           1,348, 1,432, 1,453 and 1,465

As mentioned earlier, details on the unit mix and sizes for the apartments were not provided. If
the Commission is in favor of residential use on this site, staff recommends that these details be
provided before the Planning Commission makes a recommendation to the City Council.

Exterior Materials

For materials, the City’s multi-unit residential design standards state: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With the
exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single material
shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or
fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.
Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials only.”

The applicant is proposing a combination of fiber cement siding, brick/stone, metal paneling and
stucco. Based on the townhome elevations submitted, it does not appear that the minimum of
20% brick or stone is used. If the Commission is in favor of residential use on this site, staff
recommends that the townhome elevations be modified to reflect the 20% brick or stone and that
specific information be provided on the exterior materials for the apartments before the Planning
Commission makes a recommendation to the City Council.



Architecture

Based on a preliminary review of the townhome architecture by staff, the proposed townhomes
appear to be deficient in the required 25% fenestration requirement on the fronts, one garage
treatment and potentially the minimum roof pitch of 6:12. Since the applicant provided two
alternative apartment renderings, staff did not perform a preliminary review of the apartments. If
the Commission is in favor of residential use on this site, staff recommends that the townhome
elevations be amended and that specific information be provided on the proposed apartment
architecture before the Planning Commission makes a recommendation to the City Council.

Landscaping

The ordinance requires a minimum of 15% usable open space with no more than 60% building
coverage. The proposed useable open space is over 18% and the building coverage is below 60%.

Project Amenities

The proposed project exceeds the City’s requirements for amenities. For a project of this size, the
ordinance requires five amenities. The proposed project includes 9 amenities listed in the
ordinance: outdoor pool, club house, fitness room, tot lot, covered pavilion, fire pit, sports courts,
walking trail and hot tub. In addition, the project also includes a dog park, lawn gaming area,
benches throughout and dog waste stations. The applicant is also offering to invest $100,000 in
amenities to improve Decker Lake Park. Ideas for improvements include the following:
playground, outdoor fitness and stretching stations, viewing areas and benches, picnic tables and
dog waste stations.

Interior finishes and amenities include quartz countertops; wood-style flooring; washer and
dryer; Smart Home technology; high-speed internet and large walk-in closets, linen closets
and/or pantry.

Other Issues

      Parking: The overall proposed parking ratio is 2.4 stalls/unit. For townhome
       developments, the Council has recently required more parking (over 3 stalls/unit) by way
       of development agreement. Also, the code was recently changed to state: “All parking
       shall be made available free of charge.” The proposed management standards include this
       statement: “Every unit will be required to rent at least one interior garage parking stall as
       part of the recurring monthly rental charges.” This statement implies that a charge could
       be levied for additional parking spaces.

      Fencing: The development agreement mentions a 6’ white vinyl fence around the
       perimeter of the project. In staff’s view, a solid fence along Decker Lake Dr. is not
       recommended.
      Interior Features: Certain features outlined in the interior finishes portion of the
       development agreement proposal need further clarification. For example, will quartz
       countertops be used in both kitchens and bathrooms and what exactly will be included in
       the “Smart Home technology”?

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Continuance. This application should be continued to allow the applicant time to submit
      the required traffic impact study, provide more details on the proposed apartments, amend
      townhome designs to comply with code requirements and clarify certain provisions of the
      proposed development agreement.

   2. Denial. This application should be denied to preserve the land for job creating uses and to
      focus apartment development in areas that accomplish more citywide objectives.

C-9-2020
Fetzers’ Inc. Warehouse 4850
W 3500 S
M Zone (3.89 acres)

The applicant, Joe Wixom with Fetzers’ Inc., is requesting conditional use approval for
warehouse and light industrial uses. The overall property is 7.73 acres and is zoned both M,
Manufacturing and C-2, General Commercial. The West Valley City General Plan designates
this area as light industrial and mixed use. The surrounding zoning is C-2 to the south and west,
and R-1-8 to the north and east. Adjacent land uses include a dental office and restaurants to
the south, Esparanza Elementary to the west and single unit dwellings to the north and east.

The property was originally approved as a shopping center (C-22-1988) and an approximate
95,000 sq. ft. building was constructed in 1989, which was vacated by Shopko last year. The
applicant applied for a general plan and zone change earlier this year for 3.89 acres of the parcel
in order to operate a warehouse out of the building (GPZ- 1-2020). On March 17th the City
Council approved the request and the zoning was changed from C-2 to M. A development
agreement was also approved on the property, which limits the Manufacturing Zone uses in the
building to warehouse and limited light industrial. Uses listed within the C-2 Zone as outlined
in 7-6-301 of the West Valley City code are also allowed.

Fetzers’ Inc. currently operates from another location in West Valley City where they
manufacture a variety of wood products. The company needs additional space for storage. The
old Shopko building will be used primarily as a warehouse for completed products before they
are distributed. Light industrial uses pertaining to the assembly or disassembly of products
from premanufactured components are also allowed inside the building per the development
agreement. Sanding, cutting, painting or any other use that would require venting to the outside
are prohibited.

The site will mostly remain as it is. The area to the east of the building that was used as the
garden center for Shopko will be converted into additional parking. Parallel parking stalls will
also be added along the north and west sides of the building. Based on the building square
footage, 47 parking stalls are required for the warehouse use. 42 stalls will be provided with the
additional parking added around the building and the other 5 stalls can be accommodated in the
front parking lot. In the future the applicant could subdivide the property and the front parking
lot area may be redeveloped. At that time a shared parking agreement would need to be put in
place to ensure the warehouse building still has all the parking stalls that are required in Title 7.

Much of the landscaping needs repair on the property. The applicant will replace any lawn
that is dead. Some trees that were part of the original landscaping approval have been
removed, and these will be replaced. The applicant will also be redoing the landscaping in
the parking lot islands. The trees will remain, but the lawn will be removed and four shrubs
per tree and at least 50% live plant material will be added.

Most improvements to the building will be on the interior. The building exterior will be
repainted. The development agreement requires that a two-tone paint color scheme be used.
The applicant has indicated that the front of the building will have three-tone paint, and the
sides and back of the building will have two-tone paint. There are some existing loading
docks on the east side of the building. These will remain and the dock area may be expanded
in the future. There is an existing masonry wall in place for screening. Per the development
agreement, all loading and unloading will be conducted between 6 AM and 8 PM, Monday
through Saturday.


Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as
the following:

            1.    All items outlined in the development agreement shall be met.
            2.    The site shall be improved per the approved site plan.
            3.    Parking shall be provided per section 7-9-104 of the West Valley City Code.
            4.    Landscaping shall be installed/maintained per the approved landscape plan,
                  which includes 50% live plant material in all planting areas.
            5.    The building shall be painted per the approved building elevations.
            6.    There shall be no outside storage on the property.
            7.    Requirements of all affected departments and agencies shall be met.
            8.    Subject to review upon valid complaint.

SA-3-2020
Highbury Centre Subdivision – Lot 3 Amended
5432 West High Market Drive
C-2 Zone
2 Lots
11.5 Acres

BACKGROUND

The Utah Department of Facilities Construction and Management is requesting consideration to
amend lot 3 of the Highbury Centre Subdivision. The purpose for the amended plat is to create
an additional building lot within a portion of lot 3. The subject property is located at
approximately 5432 West High Market Drive and is zoned C-2.


ISSUES:

The Highbury Centre Subdivision was recorded with the Salt Lake County Recorder’s Office in
2013. The original subdivision consisted of 10 lots. The proposed amended subdivision plat will
amend lot 3 to create a new building lot.

The newly created lot will be located at the east end of the building that houses Marshalls and
Maurices. The intended use will be a liquor store which will be relocated from Redwood Road.
The new lot will encompass the east end of the building and will extend to the south to provide
parking for the new use. Access to this lot will be gained from both 5370 West and High Market
Drive. Internal access will also be gained from the west which is the Target property. The
original subdivision contained various easements for water/sewer, parking and access. While
these easements are still applicable for the amended plat, the applicant will need to ensure that
these are still viable given that a new lot is being proposed.

Staff is not aware of any improvements that will need to be made to the site as they are already
existing. An Operating Easement Agreement has been recorded between all property owners
allowing the proposed use at this location.

STAFF ALTERNATIVES:

1.     Approve the Amendment to lot 3 of the Highbury Centre Subdivision.

2.     Continue the application for reasons determined in the Planning Commission meeting.
