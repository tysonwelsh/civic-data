1. Welcome and Opening Comment

2. Presentations and Reports

2.1 North Station Area Plan
Christine Richmond with GSBS presented an update on the North Station Area Plan, which is a
state-mandated document focused on creating a vision and goals for the future transit-supported
neighborhood around the planned north Lehi station. The plan aims to establish a unique hub of
activity that is accessible to people of all ages and abilities, with a mix of residential,
commercial, and community spaces. The plan proposes a central hub with gathering spaces, an
interconnected network of roads and paths, and coordination with planned infrastructure projects
like a future I-15 interchange. However, the implementation of the plan is complicated by the
uncertainty around when the actual transit service will arrive, which could be as far as 20 years
away.

2.2 Business License Ordinance Discussion
The city proposed significant updates to its business license regulations to improve enforcement
and protect citizens. The proposed changes include creating a dedicated business license official
with immediate suspension authority for violations like selling to minors, increasing late renewal
penalties from 25% to 35%, and upgrading violations to a class B misdemeanor. The Council
discussed the challenges the city has faced with unlicensed businesses and the need for improved
enforcement and auditing procedures. Exploring software solutions to streamline the renewal
process was also mentioned. Overall, the goal of the ordinance updates is to provide the city with

2.3. Discussion regarding the closure of 1870 N connection to State Street and adding a traffic
signal at 1800 N State Street
Luke Seegmiller, Lehi Traffic Engineer, and Tyler Fadis with UDOT, proposed the closure of
the 1870 North connection to State Street due to safety concerns and increasing traffic incidents.
The plan involves closing the current cut-through route and adding a new traffic signal at 1800
North, with an estimated project cost of $1.45 million. Discussions centered on potential traffic
rerouting, safety improvements, and coordination with UDOT. Council members were generally
supportive, suggesting a temporary closure first in order to monitor traffic pattern changes and
evaluate the impact on surrounding neighborhoods.

2.4 Lehi City Grant Program Update
Matt Lee, Lehi Management Analyst, presented the update. He showcased 20 active grants
totaling $25.5 million, with significant funding allocated to secondary water meters and various
city projects. The presentation highlighted the diversity of grant sources, including federal, state,
and county funding. Upcoming grant applications include projects like an outdoor ice rink and a
bike skills park. The discussion emphasized the city's proactive approach to securing additional
funding and the importance of continuing to pursue grant opportunities to support community
development and infrastructure improvements.

2.5 Discussion of Staffing Plans for Family Park
Jason Walker, City Administrator, asked for clarification on the direction for staffing Family
Park, particularly for Sunday operations, and was discussed at the previous meeting. The
recommendation is to hire a full-time employee that would allow the park be staffed on Sundays.
The key question is whether the council wants the park open on Sundays and if they're willing to
hire a full-time staff member to manage those hours. The mayor expressed support for keeping
the park open on Sundays and believes it needs to be staffed. Councilmembers Paige, Albrecht,
and Condie agreed to hire a full-time employee, open the park on Sundays, and staff the park on
Sundays. Council Stallings dissented stating that she agrees with keeping the park open on
Sundays but doesn’t believe it needs to be staffed and would like to give residents a chance to
show they can take care of it without staff supervision.

5. Community Development Block Grant (CDBG)- Mountainland Association of
   Governments
   Claudia Saldana stated that the grant applicants will present their programs for funding
   consideration.

    a) Applicant Presentations

    1. Family Haven- Camilla Rosas
    Family Haven is a non-profit that provides support to children who have experienced abuse
    and their families. They are starting a new 12-week therapy program to help both the child
    victims and their parents recover from the trauma. The program aims to ensure families can
    continue treatment even if they face financial or other challenges.

    2. Friends of Utah County Justice Center- Heather Allen
    The Children's Justice Center provides a safe, child-friendly environment for forensic
    interviews following allegations of abuse. Beyond the interviews, they also aim to connect
    these families with vital resources, including mental health services. Their funding request of
    $5,000 would help ensure victims and secondary victims receive timely therapeutic support
    to aid in their recovery and healing process.

    3. Lehi City Engineering- Stefanie Daggs
    The Lehi engineering department is seeking CDBG funding to add missing curb, gutter, and
    sidewalk to the west side of 400 East between 300 North and 600 North in downtown Lehi.
    This area is a well-used route for Lehi High School and Lehi Elementary students, as well as
    local businesses. The project aims to improve walkability and safety for residents in the area.

    4. Tabitha’s Way- Wendy Osborne
    Tabitha's Way is a community food pantry that served 3,830 households from Lehi in 2022,
    providing temporary food assistance. They are 98% privately funded but are requesting
    CDBG funding to continue their efforts. With the requested funds, Tabitha's Way could
    provide 792,000 meals and serve 17,600 individuals. The organization relies heavily on
    volunteers, with over 750 volunteers providing 2,384 hours of service at just their Saratoga
    Springs location. The speaker emphasized the growing need for Tabitha's Way's services due
    to factors like inflation and the rollback of other food security programs in the community.

    5. Curtis Center- Michelle Holbrook
    The Holbrook Community Center, which operates the Curtis Center, presented concerns
    about security and accessibility issues at the facility. The handicap accessible door has been
    deactivated due to the fob system, and the front door is difficult to lock. The Curtis Center
    requested $31,000 in CDBG funding to improve the security system, but the staff
    recommendation was only $13,864 towards employee wages, as the security upgrades did not
    fully meet CDBG criteria. The presenter emphasized the importance of the Curtis Center to
    the community and expressed willingness to contribute towards the security improvements.

    6. United Way of Utah County- Katie Hoshino
    The United Way presented their Volunteer Income Tax Assistance (VITA) program, which
    helps low-income families and individuals get their taxes prepared for free. Last year, the
    VITA program in Utah County brought back over $4 million in refunds. The United Way is

    tax preparation assistance more accessible for low-income families in the city. The presenter
    emphasized the importance of getting these tax refunds to families who are struggling.

    b.) Consideration of Project Approval and Funding Allocation for the Community
        Development Block Grant
        The staff recommendation was to allocate $290,000 to the Lehi engineering project for
        sidewalk improvements, and $13,864 to the Curtis Center for employee wages.

        Councilor Condie suggested an alternative, proposing to distribute $5,000 each to Family
        Haven, Tabitha's Way, and the Friends of Children's Justice Center, and reducing the
        engineering project funding. Mayor Johnson and council members expressed appreciation
        for each organization's valuable community work while remaining cautious about altering
        the original recommendation. There was discussion around whether the full $290,000
        was needed for the engineering project, and if any remaining funds could be redistributed
        to other applicants like Family Haven, Tabitha's Way, and the Children's Justice Center.

        Ms. Saldana informed the Council that the CDBG funds may be at risk for reduction by
        the federal government. She stated that if funds are reduced, the Council would have to
        reevaluate the allocation. Mayor Johnson recommended that the engineering department
        reassess the project costs, and if any funds remain, they should be reviewed for potential
        reallocation to the other applicants.

        Motion: Councilor Albrecht moved to approve the staff recommendation for the CDBG
              funds as presented, that we also include a provision that if those funds are cut, that
              the allocations be cut commensurate to the reduction in funding, and that we also
              reconsider reallocation should the project for sidewalks in the downtown area come
              back requiring less than what is presented here. Councilor Newall seconded the
              motion.

        Councilor Condie expressed concern about the proposed motion. He argues that if CDBG
        funding is cut by even 15%, the sidewalk project would not be feasible to complete. He
        suggests that if there's a decrease in federal government budget funding, the sidewalk
        project should be dropped entirely, and the city should fund all other applications as
        presented. Councilor Stallings disagreed and stated that if the funding is cut, then all of it
        should be allocated to the sidewalk project and then the city could fund the request from
        the Curtis Center, noting that it’s a city building. Mayor Johnson suggested moving forward
        with the motion for now, and then returning to this in April if there are any funding changes.
        Councilor Condie was amenable to the Mayor’s proposal if the provision for the funding
        cuts is removed.

        Amended Motion: Council Albrecht amended her motion to remove the provision
        regarding a reduction in funding.

13. Consideration of Ordinance #14-2025 approving a Development Code Amendment to
    Chapter 12 to update buffer and screening requirements for non-residential zones
    adjacent to residential zones.
    Kim Struthers presented the proposed amendments that would alter existing regulations,
    including increased fence heights, restricted material options, expanded buffer spaces, and
    modified landscaping requirements.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public input.

    Mark Skousen, representing Hadco Construction, indicated that while they want to be a good
    neighbor, they are also concerned about preserving the usability of their property. He
    expressed concern regarding the 30-foot buffer, suggesting that such a large setback may be
    unnecessarily restrictive. He expressed a desire to work constructively with the city while
    protecting the interests of the property owner.

    Teresa Gonzalez appreciated the city's efforts to address screening requirements but stressed
    the importance of creating effective barriers that prevent business from seeing into their
    backyards and mitigate sound transmission. She was careful to balance her concerns with an
    understanding of the financial implications for businesses, ultimately seeking a solution that
    provides meaningful protection for residential properties.

    The council discussed practical and aesthetic considerations, noting concerns with property
    height differences, potential cost burden on businesses, sound barrier effectiveness, and water
    conservation.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to table this item and direct staff to go back and listen to
                the comments made during the meeting by the City Council and also by the public,
                and that they work with planning commission to make some adjustments to this
                proposal. Councilor Newall seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Hancock,
    Excused; Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, Yes. The motion to table passed
    with 4 in favor and 1 Excused.

14. Consideration of Ordinance #15-2025 approving a Development Code Amendment to
    Chapter 5 to create the Starter Home Attainability Residential (SHAR) General Plan
    overlay.
    Brittney Harris presented the proposal designed to address housing attainability. She
    highlighted some of the proposal which included a $400,000.00 sales price cap and 1200
    square foot housing footprints, and lot sizes between 4,000-5,999 square feet.

    Mayor Johnson and Council Members discussed the potential implementation challenges,
    examining everything from infrastructure capacity to potential development scenarios. They
    scrutinized the proposal's ability to genuinely create affordable housing, rather than simply
    creating another mechanism for standard development. Councilors expressed both
    enthusiasm for the proposal and careful skepticism about potential unintended consequences.

    The discussion reflected an understanding that affordable housing solutions require careful,
    strategic planning. The Maor emphasized his commitment to developing an effective strategy
    that could potentially serve as a model for other municipalities and potentially influence
    state-level housing policy.

    Mayor Johnson suggested a methodical approach, proposing the creation of detailed case
    studies to fully understand the overlay's potential impacts.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public comments.
