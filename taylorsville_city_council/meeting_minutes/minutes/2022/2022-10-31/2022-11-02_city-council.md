1.      WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

        1.1      Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Councilmember Burgess (Opening
                 Ceremonies for November 16, 2022, to be arranged by Taylorsville Youth Council

Councilmember Ernest Burgess directed the Pledge of Allegiance and Councilmember Knudsen
then offered a reverence.

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Overson reported on both the fun and the challenges at the city’s first annual Trunk
or Treat event. It had been moved inside at the last minute due to high winds, but she mentioned
that going forward, such large events would not be moved inside city hall as it simply would not
accommodate the hundreds of people that were present.

Arts Council had completed a three-day run of Forever Plaid at the Mid-Valley Performing Arts
Center. Additionally, the Utah Philharmonic and Taylorsville/Salt Lake Community College
Orchestras performed together at the same location the previous week.

The Taylorsville Chapter of the Exchange Club held a Day of Service recently, and cleanup work
took place inside Taylorsville’s historic museum. Later in the week they helped Murray City in
their Haunted Woods. Murray’s chapter paid the Taylorsville chapter for their service.

The local group that had been focused on picking up litter along I-215 was done for the winter.
Mayor Overson pointed out what a remarkable difference they had made and indicated what a
shame it was that more litter was left by others almost immediately thereafter.

Long-time Taylorsville volunteer Carlos Moreno was recently sworn in as an American citizen
and had invited the Mayor to attend his swearing-in ceremony at Utah’s capitol building. She said
it was a wonderful opportunity to celebrate with him.

The police department celebrated Red Ribbon Week at Calvin Smith Elementary the week before,
and the Mayor expressed her appreciation to the council members who attended as well. The week
was set aside to recognize our country’s oldest and largest drug prevention awareness program and
was a great opportunity to watch the police officers interact with young students and help them
understand the importance of drug awareness.

The Council of Mayors continued to discuss a temporary overflow shelter for the homeless to use
during the coming winter. It was a continuing project, and each city was trying to do its part along
with the Homeless Coalition and the state legislature.

City staff met with the Food Truck League to hear their thoughts regarding the Starry Nights on
the Plaza that had taken place each Saturday night during the summer. Specifically, it was
important to know if it had been successful for the small businesses. They discussed what worked
well, where changes needed to be made in the future, etc. Everyone seemed to be excited to repeat
it the following summer although the intent was to hold it on Friday nights instead of Saturday.
April 28, 2023 was the tentative beginning date for the following year.

The City would have a tree lighting ceremony on Monday, November 28th. To be frugal with tax
dollars, it would not be a spectacular display but would certainly be lovely to see. A live tree had
been purchased and temporarily planted in front of city hall. It would find a permanent home in
one of the city’s parks in the spring.

After 17 years of service to Taylorsville, Shell Summers had recently retired which created a job
opening in the justice court. A new building inspector had also just been hired and the police
department currently had an opening for a GRAMA Coordinator.

The mayor mentioned that early voting was taking place through the following Friday and then
wrapped up her report by explaining that earlier in the day, the Youth Council had met briefly with
poll workers who spoke to them about voting and the election in general. They even held a mock
election to determine who would have been elected had the Youth Council been old enough to
vote.

       3.1 Taylorsville Police Department Report – Chief Brady Cottam

      Deputy Chief Todd Gray joined Chief Brady Cottam at the podium while the Chief took a
few moments to recognize the recipients of the police department’s Employees of the Month for
June, July, and August 2022.

Sgt. Aaron Cheshire had nominated Det. Evan Keller for the June award. Det. Keller who had in
essence carried the load of three people that month due to various staffing issues. He filled in for
a Special Victim’s Unit detective, and the victim advocate in addition to doing his regular duties.
He was TVPD’s lead defensive tactics instructor and Chief Cottam said that Det. Keller was not
only a great police officer but a great father to his kids and a great all-around human being.

Spencer Stone was nominated for the July Employee of the Month award by at least two different
people. His proactive police work had led him to the recovery of a stolen vehicle which ultimately
resulted in the discovery of a fraud ring. He congratulated Officer Stone for the outstanding job he
had done on this very complex case.

Brandy Stephens, Office Manager and the “true boss” of the department was the August 2022
TVPD Employee of the Month. Chief Cottam said the list of Brandy’s responsibilities was endless,
and he relied on her quite heavily. She put her heart and soul into the department as she had for
many, many years. She had worked in Taylorsville when employed by the Unified Police
Department and for the original TVPD which was in place from 2004 to 2012.

     Chief Cottam thanked Donny Gasu for preparing the statistical data contained within his
report. He explained that because the new police department had now been in place for over twelve
months, there was enough information for a year-to-year comparison.

Although simple assaults were trending upwards, other more violent persons crimes had decreased
in 2022. The Chief displayed data pertaining to not only the types of crimes in the city each year
but also in each individual council district.

After presenting the data for criminal and traffic cases, the Chief explained that the GRAMA
Coordinator position (Government Records Access Management Act) recently posted was a sign
of the current times. While there were 1,013 requests for police records submitted during the first
quarter of 2021, that number jumped to 1,654 for the same time frame in 2022. That number was
expected to continue to increase, so having this new position would free up the records manager
for other tasks.

Chief Cottam then addressed response times for different types of calls in the City. He attributed
the improvement in these times to the fact that officers who had not been terribly familiar with
Taylorsville when they first started with the department had the opportunity to become more so.
Additionally, having a year to become proficient with the new computer system had also had a
positive effect on response times.

Councilmember Knudsen inquired about priority 4 calls which weren’t listed in the 2021 statistics.
(Since the departments’ inception, priority 3 calls had been broken out further, resulting in a fourth
category.)

Councilmember Harker said she and her family had observed that police officers were much more
visible in Taylorsville than they were a couple of years before.

       3.3 Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling – Pam Roberts

       Pam Roberts, General Manager and Chief Executive Officer of Wasatch Front Waste and
Recycling District (WFWRD) explained that Finance Director Paul Korth was also present and
would present some of the information to the council. Ms. Roberts discussed the agency’s 2023
tentative budget and proposed fee increase. Her report addressed the following five priorities
which were thoroughly explained.
     • Sustain current services
     • Implement fee increases beginning January 1, 2023 (last raised in 2018)
     • Continue to improve efficiencies relying on technology
     • Continue to use alternate fuels to keep costs low
     • Find ways to expand the Seasonal Container Reservation Program (SCRP)

The Board had asked Ms. Roberts and her team to analyze what they had done to improve
efficiencies before turning to fee increases which they had done. She explained that installing
forward-facing cameras in each truck had saved the District in liability claims that were
periodically brought forth. Additionally, all the side-loader trucks used compressed natural gas
(CNG) which resulted in over a million dollars in savings per year. Still, the time had come when
a fee increase was essential, and the Board had tentatively approved a fee increase of $2.50 per
household per month.

Ms. Roberts turned the time over to Mr. Korth who shared more detailed information regarding
some of WFWRD’s expenses, many of which had steadily increased since 2018. While the budget
in 2018 was just under $4.2 million, for 2023 it was estimated at over $6.6 million.

The next step in WFWRD’s budget process was a November 14th public hearing to allow affected
citizens to provide their input. It was anticipated that the Board would adopt the final budget on
the morning of December 12.

Chair Barbieri expressed her belief that WFWRD was one of the most well-run and efficient
organizations she had ever seen. She congratulated Ms. Roberts for continuing to do such a great
job. Ms. Roberts in turn thanked Mr. Korth as well as the rest of the WFWRD team.
