1.       A presentation regarding the review of Annual Fraud Risk Assessment. (22-067)
         (0:10:18)

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, presented. Finance staff have completed the self-
assessment for the City and Mr. Borget reviewed the questions included in the assessment and
the City’s responses. In the last few years, the City has implemented some additional measures,
such as a fraud hotline and an internal audit, which have been direct efforts to ensure the City is
protected against fraud risks. The City’s score on the assessment is 384 out of a total 395, which
indicates that there is high confidence in the risk prevention measures in place at the city. Part of
completing this assessment with the state required this presentation before the Council, which
helps also to educate the public about the City’s fraud prevention measures. Presentation only.

2.       A presentation regarding the Fiscal Year 2022 Budget Carryover Report. (22-068)
         (0:20:48)

Kelsey Zarbock, Budget Officer, presented. Ms. Zarbock reviewed the carryover process that
takes place every year. After the conclusion of the fiscal year on June 30, departments submit

encumbered on an old fiscal year purchase order, a historically and universally agreed-upon

Ms. Zarbock highlighted a summary of carryovers last year versus this year. The total difference

the specific items that contributed to the increased requested carryover funds. The higher
requests have largely resulted from encumbrances, vehicle replacement funds, carryover of all
remaining fire wildland revenue (to assist in continued fire mitigation efforts), the new city hall,
innovation funds awarded (but not yet expended), ARPA carryover funds, and about $100,000 to
account for several corrections to the current fiscal year budget.

Ms. Zarbock highlighted additional details regarding the budget corrections. She reviewed the
largest categories of budget carryovers, which were first the carryovers related to ongoing jobs,
projects, programs, or commitments; and second the historically agreed-upon carryovers such as
computer and vehicle replacement funds. The third largest category is CIP budgets, as these
projects are typically multi-year projects with budgets that get moved forward.

Councilor Rachel Whipple asked about the Parks and Recreation CIP list which seemed to have
some mistakes, such as the Covey Center carpet replacement being listed twice. Ms. Zarbock
explained there had been some revisions made to this list and it was available in OnBase.
Councilor Katrice MacKay asked if there were more detailed documents the Council could
review. Ms. Zarbock explained which attachments were made available to the Council.
Presentation only.

3.      A presentation regarding the "Safe Streets For All" grant opportunity- Final
        Discussion. (22-065) (0:35:32)

Dave Decker, Public Works Director, presented. He thanked the project team for their work on
this project which demonstrated their ongoing commitment to public safety. The intent for this
document was that it would be adopted as an appendix to the Transportation Master Plan. Mr.
Decker explained that the intent for this document, beyond its relevance to the current grant
application, was that it be utilized as a living document to reflect the ongoing work to address
various safety concerns with city streets and sidewalks.

document for the City. Vern Kesslar, Parametrix, also presented. They are working on this Safety
Action Plan and the concurrent grant application. Mr. Kesslar reviewed the components of the
Safety Action Plan and shared some details of each component. The City has a Vision Zero
commitment, which means the City commits to implementing proven strategies to reduce fatal
and serious injury traffic crashes in the City by 50% by the year 2040. Charles Allen, Parametrix,

4.      A presentation regarding the city's Moderate-Income Housing Report. (22-069)
        (1:17:19)

Brandon Larsen, Planning Supervisor, presented. Mr. Larsen reviewed the requirements changed
by a recent law passed by the State Legislature. There is an October 1 deadline for adoption of
the revised moderate-income housing plan, which will also be added as an element of the
General Plan. Mr. Larsen noted that one of the requirements of the state law was a plan for
realistically meeting the needs for moderate-income housing units within the next five years. Due
to the presence of the UTA Frontrunner station in Provo, there is an additional element required
of the City as part of the implementation strategies that are identified in the plan.

Councilor Bill Fillmore asked about a housing audit. Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, clarified that
the State legislature funded a housing audit by several reputable data consultants which is being
conducted at a State level. Mr. Larsen reviewed some more of the presentation, noting several of
the prescribed strategies in the state code and several that they felt the City was already fulfilling.

Councilor Katrice MacKay asked about waiving impact fees and whether that was an obligation,
or the City would look into the feasibility of doing so. Her understanding was that impact fees
were not covering the full costs of those utilities. Mr. Larsen explained this would be a high-level
strategy; if the Council did not want to include this, it would be helpful to know now. Councilor
George Handley asked how specific the Council needed to be at this stage. He felt specific
criteria would be needed if the City were going to explore waiving impact fees. Mr. Larsen noted
they could remove this from the implementation plan and incorporate it elsewhere as an option in
the General Plan if the Council did not want to remove it as an option entirely. Councilor Rachel
Whipple thought that providing general fund subsidies should be preserved as an option. Mr.
Handley invited comments from the Council on whether to keep this as an option. Ms. McKay
thought that the “where possible” language was helpful—as long as the policy kept flexibility for
the city, she was fine with it. Several other Councilors agreed. Ms. Whipple suggested narrowing
the strategy to limit the fee waivers to those applying to the moderate-income units.
