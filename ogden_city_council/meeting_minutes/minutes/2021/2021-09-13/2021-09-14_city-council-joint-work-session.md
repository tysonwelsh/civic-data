Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Annual Action Plan Amendment #1:
         As a requirement to receive federal funding from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), the
City's Community and Economic Development Department prepares and submits to the Council what is known as a five-year
Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and an Annual Action Plan. The Annual Action Plan (AAP) is used to outline how federal
Community Development Block Grant funds, HOME funds, and other federal and non-federal program funds will be used by the
City in the upcoming fiscal year. The amendments proposed with this mid-year update include adjusting the HOME
homeownership value limits. When the city provides assistance to homeowners for HOME-assisted housing units, the purchase
price and appraisal value may not exceed 95% of the median purchase price for the city. The HUD-derived formula for
determining home values used data from July 2019 through June 2020 and was implemented beginning in June 2021. These data
lagged behind the market and have limited what the City can do based on these lower values. The City, per HUD guidelines,
performed its own market study to determine more accurate values using more recent data. The proposal is to amend the FY22
Annual Action Plan to include the higher values from the City's study.
         Community Development Manager Ogden and Project Coordinator Fuentes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the purpose of the proposed amendment. The first amendment deals with the HOME Grant program, which
encompasses Own in Ogden down payment assistance, the Quality Neighborhoods Program, and Community Housing
Development Organization (CHDO) projects. The amendment will not have a fiscal impact but is intended to adjust affordable
homeownership limits. Currently, limits are determined by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD); the
price/value for HOME assisted single-family housing units may not exceed 95 percent of median purchase price in the Ogden
area. HUD determines the maximum purchase price based on 95 percent of median purchase price using data on FHA insured

              West Ogden Community Plan Amendment and
              Proposed Rezone at 835- 875 West Binford Street
                        A petitioner has requested to amend the West Ogden Community Plan to allow for a rezone fromR-1-5 to C-3/CO orM-
              1/CO at property located at 835-875 West Binford Street for the purpose of expanding a K-9 kennel assembly and warehousing
              business. The rezone request currently does not meet the requirements ofthe West Ogden Community Plan, so an amendment
              would be necessary to allow the rezone to occur. This is a two-part application, the first part being the amendment to allow C-3
    \         zoning in a specific portion of the community plan area, and the second part of the application is the actual rezone.
                        The petitioner, Mark Kitchen, is requesting to rezone properties abutting his K-9 kennel business so that he can expand
              his business operations. He is requesting to rezone from R-1-5 (Single-family Residential) to C-3/CO (Regional
              Commercial/Conditional overlay) or M-1/CO (Limited Manufacturing/Conditional overlay). This rezone is directly related to the
              requested amendment to the West Ogden Community Plan, which the Planning Connnission recommended denying. This petition
              requires the community plan to be amended to allow the C-3 or M-1 zones, but it was found that the C-3 zone did not meet the
              needs of the petitioner, so the Planning Commission recommended allowing the C-1 or M-1/CO zone in the community plan, with
              the condition that all properties within the block be part of the rezone request. This rezone request does not include all properties
              within the block and would thus isolate residential properties. For this reason, the Planning Commission recommended denying
              the rezone application. Rezones should follow the direction of Community Plans. If the City Council decides not to amend the
              West Ogden Community Plan to allow for either C-3 or M-1 zones, then this rezone request should not be approved, as the rezone
              would not be consistent with the plan.
                        Plarming Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the purpose of the proposed
              Plan amendment; there are two parts to the proposal:
                             •    Amend the West Ogden Community Plan for a rezoning option.
                             •    If plan is amended, then consider rezoning specific property.
                        The petitioner is seeking the ability to rezone his property from R-1-5 to C-3/CO, which would require a development

Southeast Ogden Community Plan Amendment
          Scott Larsen is the owner of a 2.15-acre parcel that has dead end roads on the east and west side of the property. The
Southeast Ogden Community Plan currently states that these two roads-Navajo Drive and 5025 South-should be connected
when development occurs on the property. The property owner would like to build a home on the property but does not want to
install the required road connection because he feels it would not be the best thing for the neighborhood. The roads on each side
are currently stubbed and could provide access between two parts of the neighborhood, but the owner of the land does not think
access should be necessary. In order to remove this requirement, the community plan must be amended. The Planning
Commission determined that access requirements should be based on how much development occurs on the land. They are
recommending that pedestrian and bicycle access should be installed if two or fewer new homes are built on the property; and that
a complete road should be installed if three or more homes are built on the property.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the petition to amend the
Southeast Ogden Community Plan. There is a 265-foot gap between the two ends of Navajo Drive and the City's subdivision
ordinance requires connection of a road if development occurs. The owner of the property abutting the undeveloped portion of
Navajo Drive would like to be able to build a home on the property without installing a connection between 5025 South and
Navajo Drive. The petitioner does not think it is practical to require road construction if a single home is to be built on the
property. The petitioner and 65 other individuals representing 39 households in the Community Plan area have proposed to amend
the Southeast Ogden Community Plan to remove the requirement to connect Navajo Drive at approximately 1500 East Navajo
Drive. Mr. Montgomery presented photographs illustrating present conditions along Navajo Drive and discussed the history of the
area dating back to 1953, including many events that led to the current conditions. He noted connectivity is an important part of
long-range planning. Whether that be pedestrian, bicycle, or vehicular transportation, it is important to consider the long-term
effects of transportation decisions. This does appear to be a unique situation, and the existing traffic patterns may not demand
connection of this road. However, it could also be argued that current homeowners may not want this road to be built, but future
homeowners may appreciate faster access to schools and parks. It is also important to note that while the petitioner is only
proposing to build a single home, up to six homes could potentially be built on the 2.15-acre parcel. He then reported the Planning
Commission determined that pedestrian/bicycle access between Navajo Drive and 5025 South is a more immediate need than
vehicular access. As a result, the Planning Commission has recommended the following:
               •    If two or fewer lots are developed: pedestrian and bike lanes must be provided to connect Navajo Drive with
                    5025 South.
               •    If three or more lots are developed: full vehicular connection must be provided between Navajo Drive and 5025
                    South.
          The Planning Commission found that a pedestrian and bicycle connection between the two roads would be necessary to
provide access to schools and parks. However, if more development occurs (more than two homes), then they feel it would be
necessary to install a complete street to connect the two neighborhoods. The Planning Commission recommended approval of the
amendment. The two dissenting voters gave the following explanations: full neighborhood connectivity of pedestrians and
vehicles is needed for the overall benefit of the area and should not be removed because the last development does not want it
installed; and this is a short-term request, but in the long-term people would wonder why connections were not made to provide
circulation options in the neighborhood.
          Mr. Montgomery facilitated discussion among the Council regarding other similar situations throughout the City and any
precedent that may be set by the Community Plan amendment; ownership and maintenance responsibility for the pedestrian access
that is constructed; and whether additional public outreach should be conducted to determine the position of those other residents
who live along the street that did not sign the petition. Council member Lopez stated he feels that the road is needed, but he
understands that some residents feel differently; he would like to hear from other residents in the Community Plan area to learn
how they feel about the proposal. This is the same process he would like the City to follow if a similar proposal were made for his
own neighborhood. This led to discussion regarding potential unintended outcomes of the Community Plan amendment and
whether the public process for this type of petition is sufficient. Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the noticing that
takes place for this type of action and noted that this Community Plan is only a year old and there was public input on the
requirement for the road connection upon development of the area. Council member Lopez stated that is very meaningful to him
and he wants to uphold the Community Plan.
