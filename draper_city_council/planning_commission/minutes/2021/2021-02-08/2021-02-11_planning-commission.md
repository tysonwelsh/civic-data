6.     Public Hearing: Shipp Barn (Stokes) Development Agreement Request (Legislative
       Action)
On the request of Nate Shipp for a Development Agreement on 2.52 acres of property located at
approximately 570 E Stokes Ave. to allow a single home to be built on the property, with a
subdivision in the future for an additional three lots. Application DA-1112-2020. Staff Contact:
Jennifer Jastremsky, 801-576-6328, Jennifer.jastremsky@draperutah.gov.

Jennifer Jastremsky, Senior Planner, presented an overview of the Development Agreement
Request. She explained that the applicant owned two properties one with a historic home on it and
another that was farmland which currently had animals and barns on it. She noted that both
properties were within the residential low to medium density land use designation and were in the
RA2 zone. She explained that the applicant was planning to develop the property in two phases.
She noted they first wanted to place a single lot behind the exiting home with access to Stokes
Avenue and later add three additional lots on the farmland with access to an existing private road.
She pointed out that deviations were needed in order to move forward with the applicant’s
proposed plan which is why they were proposing a development agreement which included the
deviations to code standards. She noted that this item would also need to go before City Council.

Ms. Jastremsky listed what the applicant has proposed to do in exchange for the deviations
including home preservation of the existing historic home, pasture preservation where the three
lots will eventually go for seven years, addition of right of way improvements along Stokes
Avenue. She pointed out the requested deviations included leaving lots 2, 3, and 4 to be developed
at a later date and allow existing accessory structures to remain on those lots without a primary
structure. She indicated the next deviation was a request to be able to leave the existing private
lane at 20 feet wide and to allow the access lane to count toward the minimum lot size and width
for lot 1.

Ms. Jastremsky indicated that there were still elements of the development agreement that staff
would like to address further including specifics about the home preservation and reduced
separation between accesses. She pointed out that the development agreement needed to specify
that the acreage for the home off Stokes would be determined prior to the dedication the applicant
would be making to the right of way.

Commissioner Squire asked if allowing the applicant to include the driveway in the minimum
square footage of the lot would change the setbacks. She indicated that she was more specifically
concerned about lot one rather than the existing home.

Ms. Jastremsky replied that the Development Agreement did not discuss or allow any deviations
to setbacks. She clarified that the Development Agreement would not impact where they measured
the setbacks from.

7.     Public Hearing: City Initiated Queuing Definition and Bulk Text Amendment
       (Legislative Action)
On the request of Draper City for a Text Amendment to amend Sections 9-3-040, 9-5-090, and 9-
8-020, 9-16-050, 9-22-010, 9-25-070, 9-25-100, 9-27-140, 9-27-200, 9-27-210, and 17-5-020 of
Draper City Municipal Code. This application is known as the City Initiated Queuing Definition
and Bulk Text Amendment. Application TEXTMAP-0004-2021. Staff Contact: Todd Draper, 801-
576-6335, todd.draper@draperutah.gov.

Todd Draper, City Planner, provided an overview of the staff report for the item. He identified the
changes being made which included the addition of a definition of a queuing space, removal of
outdated information, grammatical corrections, and removal of the section on the design guideline
manual.

Mr. Draper indicated the addition of the queuing space definition was the biggest change. He
shared the new definition with the Commission which read, “an area on a property where vehicles
area arranged in a queue to wait for a limited or short duration while waiting to advance to the next
available space in the queue. Such spaces are provided for driver convenience in accessing onsite
services such as drive-up windows, automated teller machines, vehicle service or wash bays, fuel
islands, or designated pick-up or drop-off areas for goods and/or passengers This definition is also
applicable to the use of the terms stacking spaces, stacking isles, drive-through lanes, and drive-
through aisles but does not include freight parking or freight deliveries to a site by commercial
vehicles.”

Mr. Draper shared a visual presentation and pointed out text amendments made to each of the
sections. He noted that many of the changes helped provide greater clarity in the codes. He noted
a change in section 9-27-140(G) to the listed streets where minor collector road setbacks had
exceptions and in section 9-27-200 it was clarified that gates could only go across private streets
and a correction of additional typographical errors. He shared that in section 9-27-210 they
updated the term to queuing spaces for preschools and private schools. He noted the last change
was for a typographical error in section 17-5-020.

Chairman Adams opened the public hearing for the item. No comments were made, and the public
comment portion of the meeting was closed.

Motion: Commissioner Tab Bingham motioned to approve forward a positive
recommendation to the City Council for the Text Amendment as requested by Draper City
for the City initiated Queuing Definition and Bulk Text Amendment, Application
TEXTMAP-0004-2021, based on the findings and subject to the conditions listed in the staff
report dated January 29, 2021.

Second: Commissioner Craig Hawker

Vote: A roll call was taken with the Commissioners voting 5-0 in favor of the motion.
Commissioners Squire, Hawker, Van Hoff, Ogden, and Bingham voted, “Aye”.
