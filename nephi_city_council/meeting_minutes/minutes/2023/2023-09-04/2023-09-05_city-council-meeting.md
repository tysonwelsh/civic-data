NORTH RIDGE PLAT A & B DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT SECOND AMENDMENT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that the developer for North Ridge is ready to record the plat for
Phase B. To assist with future transactions on Phase B, the plat had been renumbered to be more
consistent with Phase A. There was a development agreement amendment that had been approved by
the Council to restrict the development of lots on Phase A & B with electrical line infrastructure that had
not been relocated. This amendment was passed back when the final plats were approved by the
Council.
   For the original development agreement amendment to be accurate with the recorded plat, it was
necessary to change the lot numbers to conform to the updated recorded plat. Mr. Atkinson presented
a draft of a second amendment to the original amendment for approval. A discussion ensued about lot
23 and lot 24 that were outlined to be purchased by the City for a road through that area. Mr.
Messersmith and the City had agreed that lot 23 would be the one used for the future road. Councilor
Memmott moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the second amendment with “Lot 24” from paragraph
3 being omitted from the amendment. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed
on a unanimous vote.

NORTH RIDGE PLAT D TIME EXTENSION APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson explained to the Council that Approximately a year ago, the Planning Commission
approved the North Ridge Phase D preliminary plat. According to the Nephi City Code 11.4.3 (B) (19):

“Any failure to submit a final plat within one year after approval of the preliminary plat shall terminate
all proceedings and render the preliminary plat null and void, unless otherwise approved by the City
Council.”

  Mr. Atkinson stated that the engineer and developer would like to request a three-month extension to
submit the final plat to the Planning Commission. The North Ridge Phase D preliminary plat was a 40-
condominium development on a 5-acre parcel just west of the twin homes from North Ridge Plat A.
   Mr. Atkinson stated that staff had reviewed the request and did not have any concerns about the
time extension if the City Council chooses to grant the request. However, there was a development
agreement that accompanied this phase that had not been signed by the developer. Mr. Atkinson
recommended that if a time extension was granted, that the developer must sign the development
agreement.
   Councilor Ostler moved to approve a three-month extension for North Ridge Plat D if the
development agreement is signed. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion carried.

WALL CONSULTANT GROUP (WCG) SERVICE AGREEMENT AND COST ESTIMATE APPROVED:

    Mr. Atkinson reported that City staff has been working with WCG, the engineering firm selected to
design the preliminary and final plans for the Crimson Flats Subdivision, to put together a design cost.
WCG submitted an estimate for the design costs. The estimate aligns with a subdivision of this size and
the scope of work that the City has requested. The scope of work will include engineered plans with all
utility infrastructure, including connections to a large stormwater facility that will be located on the west
side of the development and possible revisions to the original concept design. It will also include review
by the DRC to ensure it meets with city code and oversight and consulting as the project infrastructure is
being installed. The design cost estimated was approximately $112,000.
   Councilor Callaway moved to go forward with WCG with the estimates provided and authorize the
Mayor to sign the professional services contract. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion
passed on a unanimous vote.

BLIGHT AREA DESIGNATION REQUEST DENIED:

   Mr. Vic Arnold, a homebuilder looking to build a single-family residence in Nephi requested that the
property be designated blight. The property (291 W. 400 N.) fulfills several items on the blight list but is
in an area that is not very prominent or visible. While specific incentives are meant to be determined by
the Planning Commission based on recommendations from the Development Review Committee, this
developer has requested a waiver of landfill fees. If these were recommended by the DRC and approved
by the Planning Commission, the City Council would need to approve any expenditure of funds for
landfill waivers.
   The Council discussed this issue at the last work session. Since that time, the home had been
demolished and the debris removed from the site. Mr. Atkinson state that the policy question for the
Council was in declaring the property blight is, “is there a public interest in seeing the property
redevelop? Is it necessary to use tax-payer funds as incentive to redevelop this property?”
  Councilor Worwood expressed that he was not in favor of declaring a property blight for single-family
residential construction. Mr. Merrill Reed, who was present at the meeting, stated that years ago he
built a house on a lot that was full of junk, and he paid to have the buildings demolished and hauled
away. He stated that the Council would be opening a can of worms that would be difficult to put a lid on
by paying for the landfill fees. Councilor Memmott said he understands the developer’s perspective and
the Council had targeted some specific blight areas within the City and the blight ordinance was not
intended for single family housing but more for commercial property. Councilor Ostler and Mayor Seely
both said they had had residents approach the about the issue from what they had read in the paper
and were against declaring the area blight and waiving landfill fees.
   The Council agreed to deny the blight designation request.

2.    Mr. Atkinson informed the Council about a property owner that had built his fence in a city
     easement. On his fence permit he indicated that the fence would be built on his property line.
     The property owner was requesting that the City pay half of the survey costs so that he could
     find out where his property line was. The Council was not interested in paying ½ of the survey
     costs.

3.    Public Works Director Kyle Marchant explained to the council the different options of dredging
     the holding pond/dam up the canyon that filled with debris from the winter run-off. The dam
     worked as intended and kept the flows at a certain rate, collected debris, and prevented
     sediment from moving downstream. There are some sandbars that existed before but have
     been built up above the current water line. This may or may not affect the ability of the dam to
     do the same job if there is another winter like the 2022-23 season. Judging from historical
     photos, the current amount of water volume in the dam may not be far from where it has been
     in the past.
     Mr. Marchant said that after talking to a couple of different contractors who would use a couple
     of different methods, the costs to dredge the pond and haul excess soil out is probably
     somewhere between $400,000 to $1 million. He said that he did submit a Preliminary Damage
     Assessment to the state who compiled the information for all the State of Utah. They have
     submitted an emergency declaration request from FEMA. It is now on President Biden’s desk
     and awaits his approval. If the emergency is declared, then it is possible that Nephi may be
     eligible for FEMA funds but not guaranteed. Nephi City could expend the funds to dredge the
     dam and may be reimbursed by FEMA. Funds to do this have not been identified.

4. The council discussed the City employee take-home vehicle policy. The city had recently had
   some employee turnover and wages seemed to be playing a factor. It has been difficult for
   Nephi City to compete dollar for dollar with other organizations in Utah County. Mr. Atkinson
   said that one item where the city could level the playing field is in the city’s take-home vehicle
   policy. The city currently does not allow employees to take-home vehicles. For the city’s Police
   department, this may be a way to provide a benefit to retain employees. Allowing police
   officers to take their vehicles home is a common practice among other agencies and was the
   sole question asked to our Police leadership when they were recruiting graduates from POST.

     Mr. Atkinson stated that likewise, the Council may want to explore the option of allowing on-call
     employees to take the utility trucks home to respond to on-call situations. This would again be a
     benefit to the employee and allow them more time with their families instead of additional
     travel time to pick up the utility trucks before and drop off the trucks after a call. While this may
     not prevent all employees from seeking other lucrative job offers, it may provide another perk
     to keep valuable employees working for Nephi City. Chief Morgan said that every other police
     department allows their officers to take home their vehicles if they live within a certain distance.
     Nephi City has lost three officers to different agencies in the last year. He asked for a 35-mile
     radius allowance to take their vehicle home to keep up with what other agencies are allowing.
     Mr. Atkinson showed the council an example of Spanish Fork’s City Vehicle Use policy.

      Mayor Seely had reached out to the Mayor of Manti who has a history in law enforcement. He
     read a statement from this individual about the benefits of allowing police officers to take the
     vehicles home. Councilor Callaway asked about the mileage limitations with the lease police
     vehicles. Councilor Worwood said he is not for raising property taxes to help fund better wages
     and more employees in the police department so he thought if this perk of employment could
     help retain employees, he was in favor of the option of changing the take-home vehicle policy.
     Memmott said that in the past he was not in favor of taking police vehicles home. One of the
     reasons is because of the perception from the public. But he realized that times change, and he
     could see the benefit of allowing the vehicles to be taken home, but he did caution that he was
     not in favor of allowing personal use of the city owned vehicles.

      Councilor Ostler stated that there had been abuse in the Fire District of someone using a district
     vehicle for excessive personal use. The council agreed to study this issue in more detail and
     discuss and address the issue at a future meeting.
