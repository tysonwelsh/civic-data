Cottonwood Galleria - 4998 South Galleria Drive - Mixed Use Project with 639 Multi-family
Residential and 18,900 sq ft of Commercial - Project # 24-083

Tyler Morris, of Cottonwood Residential, was present to represent this request. Zachary Smallwood
presented the request for Design and Master Site Plan Review approval to allow the development of
a mixed-use project, located in the MCMU zone. He showed a map of the property location and
provided a history of the property ownership and uses. He described the uses and requirements of
the MCMU zone. He discussed the location and details within the site plan. He described the design
of the proposed development and number of units and the required commercial use component.

Mr. Smallwood discussed the criteria used to calculate the required amount of commercial space,
stating that the parking provided will exceed the requirements. He stated that the Planning
Commission’s responsibility is to review application for its conformance to the Site Plan/Master Site
Plan requirements of the zone. He indicated that staff have verified that the application does meet
those requirements.

Mr. Smallwood reminded the commissioners that in order to approve the application they must
verify that the application meets four specific criteria. He described how the applicant meets these
criteria. He spoke to the information contained in the traffic impact study. The firm who performed
the study made recommendations to mitigate the impacts to the area. He indicated that each
department within the city said that they can accommodate the potential impacts of this project. He
said that each department made specific comments to assist the applicant in moving forward.
Murray School District was notified of the project. They said they don’t expect an impact from the
potential increase in students.

Mr. Smallwood reviewed the elevations, building heights and materials. He spoke about a
requirement to break up the building at 300 feet with publicly available access. The applicants
addressed this by placing a walkway through the podium building.

Mr. Smallwood stated that notices were sent to property owners within 500 feet of the proposed
project.

Staff finds that the proposed mixed-use development is consistent with the goals and objectives of
the Murray City General Plan Land Use, the number of housing units are permitted in the MCMU
zone, and horizontal mixed-use developments are allowed, subject to the master site plan approval
by the planning commission. He said, with the conditions, the proposed development complies with
the requirements of the MCMU zone. Staff recommends that the Planning Commission approve the
request for Master Site Plan and Design Review for the Cottonwood Galleria and mixed-use project
subject to the conditions.

Commissioner Henrie asked Mr. Smallwood asked about the E level traffic concern and the
application conditions that were reevaluated once the engineer made changes to the flow of traffic.
He was concerned the traffic issues are just being diverted to another location. Mr. Smallwood said
that the city engineer reviewed it and would have made changes to the conditions if needed.

Tyler Morris approached the podium. Chair Patterson asked if he could comply with the conditions.
He said that he could.

Commissioner Pehrson asked Mr. Morris about including the Little Cottonwood Creek Trail as part of
phase one. He said they’re open to the idea, in phase one or phase two.

Chair Patterson opened this agenda item up for public comment.

Sergio Cardenas spoke. He described the increased rate of crime that has occurred over the ten
years he and his wife have lived in the neighborhood. He also described how bad the traffic has
gotten in the neighborhood and anticipates that it will only get worse with this development. He said
that there are increasing issues with the homeless population in the area as well. He asked what will
be done about the increase crime and traffic. He expressed concern for the safety of the
neighborhood children, especially walking to and from school.

Diane Lopez spoke. She expressed her strong dissatisfaction with this project. She said that she’s
very upset about the increased crime in Murray. She feels that a new housing project will lead to
much more crime. She also expressed her concern about the increased traffic. She feels that the
citizens are the ones who lose when developers decided to put in new housing.

Michael Todd spoke. He’s the owner of the Desert Star Playhouse. He pointed out on the map where
his property was in reference to the proposed project. He said he intended to use this land to expand
the Desert Star, but what he wants to accomplish does not fit into the zone’s permitted uses. He
feels that, having the project developed, will make his property become a vacant piece of land. He
wants to work with the applicant to have his land incorporated into this project. He’s concerned
because his property is the frontage of the project and is doesn’t want it to become an eyesore.

Mr. Smallwood read an email received from Kristen Egelhoff. She expressed her concern regarding
the viability in building this size of project. She feels the neighborhood’s infrastructure can’t support
a development of this size, especially in terms of traffic, noise, and pollution – during and after
construction. She foresees an increase in utility costs and taxes. She is also concerned for the
impact on the Jordan River Parkway. She implored the Planning Commission to build single family
homes or a park instead.

Mr. Smallwood read an email from Emma Tanner with Clyde Snow Attorneys at Law. She said that
her firm has been retained by Hunters Woods SPE LLC. She wrote regarding their objection to the
Cottonwood Residential mixed-use development design and Master Site Plan Review Approval. She
said the Hunters Woods presently owns and operates an apartment complex adjacent to the
proposed development. She states that, given the proposed size and limited amount of time
available to review the proposed project, she is notifying the city of her client’s concerns. Their
concerns include the increased density in the area. They take issue with a 2023 decision to deny
Hunter Woods to engage in a minor expansion, stating discriminatory treatment against them. They
anticipate overflow parking of the new development onto adjacent streets and neighborhood
spaces. They state that Murray City is failing to acknowledge the traffic issues that will result in high
density living, this includes the impact on school pick-up and drop-off. They are concerned that the
private water line on their property will need to be utilized to provide utilities to the development.
Hunters Woods desires to obtain independent traffic and other studies so that it can adequately
consider the ramifications of the development. They feel that Murray City did not publish any
substantive information about what may very well be one of the largest developments in state history
until late Friday, July 26th, leaving residents with five calendar days to process and analyze a 140-

Chair Patterson close the public comment period for this agenda item.

Mr. Smallwood addressed the topics of crime and homelessness in the area, stating that there is
statistically more crime and homelessness in areas with open vacant land that isn't being actively
used. He said that a development in the area should lead to a reduction in crime, due to more eyes
on the street and residents being more active and engaged in the community. He said that, just like
those who commented during this meeting, new residents moving into the development want what
current residents want – to call that their home and to feel safe.

Mr. Smallwood addressed the letter from Clyde Snow. He said that it was never stated that there
would be a four-lane road on Murray Boulevard. He also said that there is no stated intention to tie
into private utilities. The property has its own large utility lines. He also stated that the city wouldn’t
allow them to tie into Hunters Woods’ private lines. He addressed the concerns about the impact on
the schools. He said that staff, City Council and the mayor have met with the school superintendent
and the school board. He cited that the city has a letter, signed by the superintendent of Murray
School District, stating that they can accommodate the anticipated students. He stated that UTA is
supportive of the housing project and will be adding bus stops to accommodate the increase in
residents. UTA will also be installing rapid transit stations.

Commissioner Henrie asked if the increased UTA stops will cause traffic congestions. Mr.
Smallwood said that the stops will have pull-offs to reduce the impact.

Commissioner Henrie asked about the concerns of more residents parking along the street. Mr.
Smallwood said that the proposed development exceeds the parking requirements of the zone and
should have ample parking.

Chair Patterson asked Mr. Smallwood to elaborate on how the project will be meeting all the code
requirements.

Mr. Smallwood provided detailed history of the property, including zoning uses. He said that in
2021, the city put in a moratorium on all mixed-use development in the city and went through an
exhaustive review with the city departments. He talked about amendments to the zone based on
that review. He said that those amendments would have been brought before the Planning
Commission and the City Council at the time. This would have included notices sent out to property
owners. He said that this is a private property rights issue, and that the owner of the property is

GENERAL PLAN / ZONE MAP AMENDMENT

Diamond Ridge Development - 4734 South Hanauer Street - Zone Map Amendment from R-1-6, -
Medium Density Single Family to R-M-15, Medium Density Multiple Family - Project # 24-081

Mark Hardy, of Diamond Ridge Development Group, was present to represent this request. Zachary
Smallwood presented the request to amend the zoning of the subject property to facilitate
redevelopment of the property. Mr. Smallwood pointed out the property location on the map. He
discussed the characteristics of the property, such as size and density. He reviewed the differences
between the R-1-6 and R-M-15 zones. He said that, in the current zone, the applicant could have a
maximum of three units, due to height restrictions. The Planning Commission could approve a
height increase in the R-M-15 zone. He discussed the setbacks and that they could be increased in
the R-M-15 zone. He said the parking requirements would also be increased in the R-M-15 zone.
Staff sent out notices to property owners within 300 feet. No comments were received from the
public. Staff recommends that the Planning Commission forward a recommendation of approval to
the City Council for the requested zoning and map amendment .

Commissioner Pehrson and Mr. Smallwood discussed what could be built on this property with
current zoning. Mr. Smallwood said they could only build one single-family home. The applicant
intends to build three homes.

The commissioners and Mr. Smallwood discussed the zoning requirements. He stated that there is a
property currently that is a nonconforming fourplex that was legal when it was built but would not
meet current zone requirements.

Mark Hardy approached the podium. Chair Patterson asked why he is requesting the zone change.
He said that it would increase the property value. He feels that the request matches the area.

The commissioners discussed this being a request for a zoning change, not for a specific project.
Mr. Henrie asked if the applicant made the right kind of request for this type of zone. Mr. Smallwood
said that he did. He said he can talk with the applicant about the number of units he wishes to put
on the property to ensure that it fits with the acreage available. He said that the current request is
for zone change, not the number of units.

The commissioners and Mr. Smallwood had a discussion regarding the buffering in the zone.
Commissioner Milkavich wondered if the buffering provided by the property was sufficient for this
zone.

Chair Patterson opened the agenda item up for public comment. Seeing none, the public comment
period was closed.

Commissioner Pehrson made a motion that the Planning Commission forward a recommendation of
approval to the City Council for the requested amendment to the Zoning Map designation of the
property located at 4734 South Hanauer Street from R-1-6, Single Family Medium Density, to R-M-
15, Multiple Family Medium Density Residential, as described in the Staff Report.
