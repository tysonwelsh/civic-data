I.    Welcome – Mayor Pro-tem Durham.
Mayor Pro-tem Durham called the meeting to order at approximately 6:00 p.m.

II.    Pledge of Allegiance.
The Pledge of Allegiance was recited by those in attendance.

III.   Public Comments.
There were none.

IV.    Consideration of Ordinance 2021-26 Amending Title 13 Regarding Sensitive Lands
       Development Standards Update – FEMA Floodplain & Fault Study
Council Member Fotheringham moved to adopt Ordinance 2021-26. Council Member Petersen
seconded the motion. Vote on motion: Council Member Fotheringham-Aye; Council Member
Petersen-Aye; Council Member Gibbons-Aye; Council Member Quinn-Aye and Council Member
Durham - Aye. Ordinance 2021-26 was adopted by a unanimous vote.

V.     Consideration of Ordinance 2021-27 Amending Title 13.77 Regarding Parking Lot Shade
       Tree Standard
Council Member Petersen moved to adopt Ordinance 2021-27. Council Member Quinn seconded
the motion. Vote on motion: Council Member Fotheringham-Aye; Council Member Petersen-Aye;
Council Member Gibbons-Aye; Council Member Quinn-Aye and Council Member Durham - Aye.
Ordinance 2021-27 was adopted by a unanimous vote.

VI.    Consideration of Resolution 2021-34 Approving an Interlocal Agreement with Unified Fire
       Service Area for Funding Seismic Upgrades to Station 104
Council Member Quinn moved to approve Resolution 2021-34. Council Member Gibbons seconded
the motion. Vote on motion: Council Member Fotheringham-Aye; Council Member Petersen-Aye;
Council Member Gibbons-Aye; Council Member Quinn-Aye and Council Member Durham - Aye.
Resolution 2021-34 was approved by a unanimous vote.

VII. City Manager Report – Gina Chamness.
Ms. Chamness reported that the $150,000 grant for public art has been restored by the County and
the City should be receiving an updated contract. She also stated that the playground mural should
be installed in the next few weeks.

Ms. Chamness noted that the City’s bailiff is leaving to take another position and the Constable
service has informed the city that they will not be able to fulfill the contract and would not longer be
providing service to the City. In the meantime, Chief Hoyal and UPD have agreed to cover court for
the next few weeks while the City looks at other options.

VIII. District Issues and Board Reports.
Council Member Quinn reported that there had been two more major water main breaks. She would
be reaching out again to SLC Public Utilities to see what could be done.

Council Member Gibbons and Council Member Durham had nothing to report

Council Member Petersen has heard from a Mr. Lopez regarding the safety issues at Olympus High
and that the school has some money to spare to help with safety measures. Ms. Chamness reported
that the City and UPD are aware of the issues and Chief Hoyal is putting together recommendations
for the City and school.

Council Member Fotheringham reported on his meeting with the Spring Lane Community Council.
They have a Meet the Masters art program that has been going on for over 17 years and are looking
to hire an instructor and to collaborate with our Arts Council.

V.     Recess City Council into a Work Meeting.
Council Member Petersen moved to recess the City Council Meeting and reconvene in a Work
Meeting. Council Member Quinn seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous
consent of the Council and the Council convened in a work meeting at approximately 6:20 pm.

        a.      Discussion on Updates to the Employee Manual
Ms. Chamness reviewed the three proposed updates to the employee manual, they are: changing from
a fiscal to a calendar year for vacation leave, adding new language to reflect state law changes for
medical cannabis and looking at the employee service award amounts.

Council Member Petersen commented that she had requested to review the serviced award amounts
as she feels they are too low. She would like to double the amount or go with $50/year. The Council
was supportive and going with $50 per year of service.

      b.     Discussion on Budget Amendments
Ms. Chamness went over the proposed budget amendments:
   • A mid-year increase in the amount the City of Holladay pays to the Unified Police Department
      (“UPD”) for law enforcement services;
   • Revenue and expenses associated with Public Safety Appreciation Week events;
   • Funding for a match for a previously approved Interlocal Agreement with the Unified Fire
      Service Area for limited seismic upgrades to Station 104; and

      • Funding for an alternate approach for bailiff services in the Holladay Justice Court as well as
        an increase in Detective services for UPD.
Ms. Chamness also reported that the Historical Committee is asking for funding to study the
possibility of a museum. They are looking at way to tell the story of Holladay by hiring a firm to
help with the exhibit. There is a funding opportunity through a grant for this type of work that is
being applied for. She asked the council if they would support a museum study in the absence of
grant funding. The Council was only supporting if the city could get the grant funding.

          c.   Calendar.
               • Council Meetings – November 4, and 18, 2021.
               • October 26 – Trick or Treat – 5:30 p.m. to 7:00 p.m.
               • November 21 – Interfaith Thanksgiving Service.

The calendar items were reviewed and discussed.

VI.       Closed Session Pursuant to Utah Code Section 52-4-204 and 205 to Discuss Personnel
          Issues, Potential Litigation, and Property Acquisition and Disposition (if needed).

VII. Adjourn.
Council Member Fotheringham moved to adjourn. Council Member Petersen seconded the motion.
The motion passed with the unanimous consent of the Council.

The City Council Meeting adjourned at approximately 6:45 p.m.
