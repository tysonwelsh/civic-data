 5.        Ordinance 2021-24 amending the Zone Map Classification of approximately .25 acres of real
           property, generally located at 790 West. 500 South, from Residential (R1.6) to Very Low
           Density Residential (VLDR) Zone and authorizing a related development agreement. Franklin
           Neighborhood. (PLRZ20210128)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-24, as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, Planner, presented. Mr. Mark Boud requested a zone map amendment from the R1.6A
zone to the VLDR zone for a two-lot subdivision of land in the Franklin Neighborhood. The current zone,
R1.6A, was just short of allowing a lot split since each lot in that zone was required to have a minimum
lot area of six-thousand square feet and the total lot area of the existing lot was just over eleven-
thousand square feet. With the VLDR zone each lot would only need an area of five-thousand square
feet. The concept Mr. Boud was proposing showed a new single-family home on each new lot, with one
facing 500 South and the other facing 800 West. The proposed lot lines shown would meet lot standards
for the VLDR zone and allow the existing garage on the property to remain.

The Planning Commission and Staff forwarded a positive recommendation.

Councilor Hoban asked if there had been any concerns from the neighborhood. Mr. Ardmore said there
was not a neighborhood meeting, but it was posted on Open City Hall and there were no concerns.
Several members of the Planning Commission noted some slight concern because the lot was at least
10,000 square feet, applying the VLDR zone would permit townhomes to be built, but that was not the
intent of this applicant.

The applicant, Mark Boud, was invited to speak to the Council. He said his interactions with the
neighbors regarding the project had been positive. He thought this was a good fit for the neighborhood.

Councilor Harding asked Mr. Boud if he had considered the options that were available with R1.6A zone
and creating an accessory dwelling unit. Mr. Boud said each home would have a basement apartment
that he hoped could be a rental dwelling unit.

Chair Sewell opened public hearing, there was no response.

Councilor Handley liked this project and said it was a modest shift from what was planned for the area
but added newness with sensitivity and aesthetic value. However, there had not been a development
agreement proffered so there was no guarantee this is what would be built if the rezone were to be
granted. Mr. Ardmore explained the applicant had mentioned that he would be willing to discuss a
development agreement.

Councilor Ellsworth also liked the proposal. It added new housing stock and would contribute to a
stronger quality of housing stock. It could elevate this area and add housing where there was not much
being added.

Councilor Harding said when he was first elected there was a two-day planning summit that had
presentations from significant people in the planning and housing industries. One of the speakers was
familiar with urban planning and he instilled that sometimes cities go through the process of
establishing significant plans, but then change them without much consideration. There were strong
reasons to be careful about deviating from the established plan. Neighborhood plans were helpful for
the developers to know what was desired by the cities. Mr. Harding liked the proposal but felt it was
incongruent with the Franklin Neighborhood Plan and so he struggled with his decision. He encouraged
the Council to consider the role of the neighborhood plans.

The applicant, Mr. Boud, confirmed to Council he would agree to proffer a development agreement to
ensure the project did not deviate from the proposal.

       Motion:            Councilor Hoban made a substitute motion to make the rezone contingent upon a
                          development agreement that would require the applicant to move forward with a
                          development that was similar to what had been proposed. The motion was seconded by
                          Mr. Shipley.

Brian Jones, Council Attorney, displayed the version of the ordinance that authorized the execution of a
development agreement.

       Vote:              The substitute motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley,
                          Harding, Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.

Chair Sewell called for a vote on the implied motion which now included the rezone as well as a
development agreement.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 6:1 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Hoban,
                          Sewell, and Shipley in favor. Councilor Harding opposed.

Chair Sewell asked if there was any objection to adjourning the meeting. Councilor Harding objected.

unusual for meetings to begin at noon and go late into the evening. There were many people who
support and enable the Council to serve in this capacity, including the Administration and Staff. Mr.
Harding also thanked the spouses and families at home who support this process and make it possible.
He wished his wife, Julia Harding, a happy anniversary.
