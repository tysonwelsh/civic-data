Kearns Improvement District Bond Waivers, Surety Bond, and Impact Fees

               Pam Gill, General Manager, Kearns Improvement District, stated Salt Lake County
is requiring Kearns Improvement District (KID) to take out a $20,000 bond. The bond covers
excavation and road cuts that KID does in Salt Lake County. If the job does not get finished or
something needs to be fixed later on the bond covers that as well. KID has insurance in place that
does the same thing that the additional bond requirements do, the only difference is, it is requiring
KID to name Salt Lake County as an insured. KID has to pay for the bond each year. If the project
gets up to $15,000, Salt Lake County will require another bond and we will have to expend more
money for the second bond. She does not understand the justification for the additional
requirement because KID has never walked away from a project and if something goes wrong, it
gets fixed. Typically, these types of bonds are for contractors that come into the metro township
to do a project and then leave, which may make it harder to find them if there is a problem with
its work. KID is invested in the community, part of the community, and is not going anywhere;
therefore, any work done by KID will be completed and fixed if there is a problem. The pipeline
license agreement is in place as a safe guard. The agreement states that road cuts that are over
100 feet need a pipeline license agreement. The bond requirement will be in addition to the
pipeline license agreement that KID will have to sign. The pipeline license agreement does the
same thing a bond does; it says the job will be finished, cleaned up, put back to the original
condition, and fix anything that becomes a problem later. KID enters into a pipeline license
agreement for each project that it does. The agreement is signed by herself and reported to the
board of trustees and guaranteed by KID and the board of trustees is aware of those. The
additional bond will eventually be passed on to rate payers in the form of a rate increase. This
bond requirement is an additional cost that KID will have to expend, and it does not feel it is
necessary.

               Council Member Snow asked if this bond requirement is new or did it get
increased.

                Mayor Bush stated the Council is very clear that the metro township is not getting
any money. Cheryl Hatch, KID, said it perfectly, her concern is the district. The Council’s concern
is the community; KID is part of the community, the tax payers, roads and everything else. If the
KID makes cuts in the road and something gets damaged, the Council must protect that. She did
not authorize this additional bond requirement, this was implemented by the director. The Council
understands the money does not come back to Kearns Metro Township. This is not a ploy to try
and get money from KID. We are working from the same bucket of money. KID and the Kearns
Metro Township Council does not seem to be working hand in glove and she does not understand
why. KID is sitting in front of this Council asking for the bond requirement to be rescinded;
however, she came before the KID Board of Trustees a month ago requesting a waiver on an
impact fee, it has the same effect on our tax payers.

                 Ms. Gill reviewed the waiver of fees process. The Kearns community is going to
be stronger if all the agencies work together. There is some confusion on fees paid and fees
waived. Every time the road is cut, KID pays a minimum of $250 with an additional $.50 square
foot above that. KID has not received any fee waivers from Salt Lake County or the Metro
Township; however, KID has received letters of guarantee, which are normally taken out on larger
projects. The letters of guarantee are just like a bond, they guarantee that the work will be
completed, and it is set for the amount of money that the work is going to take to finish. If it did
not finish the work, there would be recourses. Ten percent stays in place for a year, and if the job
is done and there is nothing wrong with it then the ten percent is released. Mayor Bush has
released those letters in 2018. There was no money expended either way. The letters were
released, and KID is no longer under those letters. In 2019 KID asked for a letter of guarantee for
the new two-million-gallon tank, which Mayor Bush signed for KID. The storm drains and drive
approach are $24,000 and the letter of guarantee is still in place because the work is still being
done. From 2015 – 2019 are fees KID has paid to Salt Lake County, Metro Township, and Unified
Fire Authority. In 2016 $14,000 was an impact fee KID paid to Unified Fire Authority. These are
in addition to fees we paid to West Valley City and West Jordan City because we have part those
cities in our boundaries. In 2019 part of the $19,000 was $13,000 for the permit for the two-million-
gallon tank. KID is paying those fees and it is not arguing those charges. Most of the districts in
the valley are paying those fees. Most districts do not waive fees for other governmental entities
or provide free or reduced water. The water that KID provides on 5400 South area has been
provided for free and it will stay that way. KID is invested in the community and it wants to continue
to be invested in the community.
