the Murray City Municipal Council Chambers, 10 East 4800 South, Council Chambers, Murray,
Utah.

The public was able to view the meeting via the live stream at http://www.murraycitylive.com or
https://www.facebook.com/Murraycityutah/. Anyone who wanted to make a comment on an
agenda item may submit comments via email at planningcommission@murray.utah.gov.

SUBDIVISION REVIEWS

Waterstone Subdivision – 6566 South Jefferson Street - #22-012

Susan Nixon reviewed the request for a one-year time extension for Final Subdivision approval
for the Waterstone PUD Subdivision. The Planning Commission granted final subdivision
approval on May 19, 2022. Jacob Ballstaedt, representing Garbett Homes, has requested an
additional one-year limit for recording the plat due to the market's drastic changes since this
property was approved and the market slowdown has created some delays in starting new
construction developments. Staff is recommending the Planning Commission grant the one-year
extension subject to meeting the original conditions of approval.

Murdoch Subdivision – 4580 South Commerce Drive - #22-036

Susan Nixon reviewed the request for a one-year time extension for Final Subdivision approval
for the Murdoch Subdivision. The Planning Commission granted final subdivision approval on
April 7, 2022. The applicant has been working to obtain signatures on the plat but has not been
able to get all the signatures prior to the one-year time limitation. For this reason, they have
requested an extension. Staff is recommendation approval of the one-year time extension
subject to the original conditions of approval.

Commissioner Milkavich asked the applicant if they have read the conditions and whether they
can comply with those conditions.

Carter Maudsley, representing the applicant, responded yes to both questions. He noted the
access is currently on Jensen Lane, which is part of the reason for the delay since they are trying
to figure out whether Jensen is still owned by Murray City, or if it was vacated. The applicant is
accessing the rear property off of Jensen now. In the final plat there was an easement done
through Lot 1 to the rear of Lot 2.

Public comment was opened.

Gary Weston (Resident) said he wasn’t here for this matter, but asked if this is a prescriptive
easement or an easement grant, because you would want to know whether that is a prescriptive
easement that needs to upgraded to a granted easement, or whether it is in fact a granted
easement.

Mr. Smallwood was unsure on the easement, typically the city engineer and team deals with that.
That is one of the delays is that staff is trying to figure that out. In response to Mr. Weston’s
question, it is being taken care of but staff doesn’t have an answer at this time.
Planning Commission
April 20, 2023
Page 4

Ms. Nixon noted that the city engineer brought this up a few months ago, but it was decided that
the city can’t force it; whoever buys Lot 2 will have to work something out, but it will not be on the
plat.

Commissioner Patterson asked if the plat is recorded, is there still a way for the owner to get the
easement/reciprocal agreement in place after the fact. Ms. Nixon stated the owner could sell the
property without an easement, but the buyer would not have access and an access easement
could be obtained at a later date, but should be recorded with the Salt Lake County Recorder’s
office. The city would not approve this type of a situation for a residential lot since they have to
have a recorded access easement or part of a lot as a flag lot, but commercial is different and
the regulations are not the same.

Commissioner Hacker moved to grant the one-year extension for final subdivision approval for
the Murdoch Subdivision on the property addressed 4580 South Commerce Drive, subject to the
original final subdivision conditions of approval:

1.     The properties shall maintain conformance with the requirements of the M-G,
       Manufacturing General Zone as contained in Chapter 17.152 the Murray City Land Use
       Ordinance.
2.     The applicant shall prepare a Final Subdivision Plat which complies with all requirements
       of Title 16, Murray City Subdivision Ordinance.

CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT

Mercantile Towns PUD Subdivision- 861 East Winchester Street - #23-039 & #23-038

Mr. Smallwood presented the application from Scott Verhaaren, of the Boyer Company, to
develop the subject property into 109 townhomes. The subject property is made up of nine (9)
parcels located on the north side of Winchester Street and the west side of 900 East. The
property has been used as RC Willey, a regional furniture store, since the 1970s. The City
Council approved a zone change from C-N to R-M-15 in September 2022. The application
before the Planning Commission is for a Conditional Use Permit for Planned Unit Development
and Preliminary Subdivision approval. If the Planning Commission approves the preliminary
subdivision, the applicant will work with the city staff to prepare a final plat to be reviewed and
approved by the Planning Commission. After final subdivision approval is granted, the applicant
will prepare a final subdivision plat. When the final subdivision plat has been approved and
accepted by the City Engineer, it will be circulated for signatures of approval which will allow the
recordation of the plat at the Salt Lake County Recorder’s Office. Staff is recommending
Planning Commission
April 20, 2023

approval of the planned unit development and granting of the preliminary subdivision approval,
subject to conditions.;:

Commissioner Patterson asked if they are allowed different fencing for different locations on the
property. Mr. Smallwood responded yes. He brought this before the commission to have a
discussion about the options and what the planning commission would like to see.

Commissioner Milkavich asked the applicant if they have read the conditions and can comply
with them.

Scott Verhaaren, applicant representing The Boyer Company, responding to the fencing question
that they typically find that the product type dictates the look of the fencing, and they would like to
have the fencing mimic the architecture and they would like to work with staff on that. He
discussed what they have done on other projects and how they would consider materials based
on the height requested by the commission. Regarding the elevations and height, if they
determine they can get a 26-foot width on certain roads they may come back and ask for some
building heights exceeding 30 feet, but they understand that would only be done if they could
meet the international fire code. The point on the grading and drainage is accurate, their
engineers have worked that out so they can match what is currently there; specifically, over by
the Framing Establishment which has been a bit of a challenge. Nothing presented is of concern
to them, and they think they can work through those issues.

Commissioner Hacker asked what they meant by matching what is currently there. Mr.
Verhaaren said the Fame Establishment to the north has asphalt for the parking and he believes
they are close to figuring out how to make everything work with the same elevation across the
site and having a gradual gain up to the site. He also noted that staff was correct in that they
have a lot of fill to either dispose of or use it on-site. They had no idea that much fill was under
the site until the slab floor was taken out.

Commissioner Nay noted that a concern constantly raised by residents is the drainage on the
west side and the flooding into the neighborhoods seen throughout the years. In the project, they
have added a stormwater retention basin, as well as eliminated many of the hard surfaces, and
he asked if that should remedy the issues for the neighbors.

Mr. Verhaaren said it should, as they have eliminated a bunch of the impervious surface area
and added retention basins designed to city standards. They don’t believe there will be any more
flooding issues there. There are some downstream issues in the stormwater system itself on the
site, so if there is a backup at any time in the future with some of the 90-degree angles he thinks
the detention basin should be able to handle that. Regarding the fencing, he stated they will
work with staff to ensure it is done to the city’s desire; they would just like to have the flexibility,
based on the final designs, to make sure everything matches the homes.

Mr. Smallwood began addressing concerns from the public. Regarding the 9th East access on
the south, currently they are proposing for it to be a full access. However, as one of the
conditions state, the applicant does have to go before UDOT to get those accesses approved
and they may require a right in, right out. If this was something the city could impose as a
condition, staff would have suggested it in their review. Next, this development will only be
residential as the R-M-15 only allows residential with few exceptions like a school or
communication tower. Regarding owner-occupied, these are proposed for sale but the city, as a
government body, cannot dictate owner-occupied versus rental products. Regarding subsidized
housing, some of the project can be subsidized housing, but this applicant is not proposing any.
There are low-income tax credits, and the city would have to write a letter in support of that as
part of the application process; that has not been done for this project. Regarding the fencing, he
agrees with the six or eight-foot fence on the highest side, that is something that is allowed in the
code. He encouraged the applicant to place the fence on the higher side. He discussed the
elevations and how they are shown on the map, noting that the lines on the map do not take into
account the building in terms of grading, they are only describing the land underneath. He
discussed the access easement and possible previous vacation of the easement, noting that
they could have the applicant build a fence along that property line without encroaching in that
easement.

Commissioner Lowry asked if there would be curb and gutter on the north portion. Mr.
Smallwood responded yes.

Ryan Hales, traffic engineer, reviewed his traffic study included in the meeting packet.

Commissioner Hacker asked for Mr. Hales’ thoughts on UDOT’s reaction to the access being as
close as it is to Winchester. Mr. Hales responded that they will look at it, but he believes their
biggest concern will be with the gas station access next to it and they will want to try and
combine the two accesses.

Spencer Moffatt, the applicant representative from The Boyer Company, stated they have met
with UDOT, and they are excited for the consolidation of accesses to 900 East. Regarding the
north access and the Framing Establishment, that will be a shared access so the Framing
Establishment can have access to their parking lot. Curb and gutter will start at their building and
they intend to do a wall, shielding the view of the storage building behind them. The RC Willey
foundation will also be removed in the future and have not had the crews do that yet. He
discussed the collaborative process with the nearby residents and discussed the deed overlap
with some of the shared property lines. They do not intend to take that space back and will work
with the residents to Quit Claim deed that as part of the fencing discussion. They have multiple
vendors for the fencing and in the high-traffic areas, they will definitely want a masonry concrete
fence for safety.

Commissioner Nay acknowledged a project recently done by Brad Reynolds Construction. His
project was required to have an eight-foot fence along his project located further to the west on
Winchester Street which is a terrible area to walk along the street. Mr. Nay recommends along
900 East and Winchester that they extend the sidewalk to six feet, in addition to the two feet for
the park strip already in place; this would take away from some of the setbacks on some units,
but they need to be cognizant of those outside the development as well. He also believes the
eight-foot fence is appropriate and beneficial to the residents fronting those roads.
Commissioner Lowry supports Commissioner Nay’s recommendations.

concessions for the units along both 900 East and Winchester to give more leeway for the

Mr. Verhaaren addressed the comments regarding the uncomfortable experience of walking

Highland, they actually had to go back and re-do fences for those reasons. They have gone
away from eight-foot walls for those reasons. They like to have an open feeling and if they are
setting these on the existing grade plus eight feet, that would result in a 10-foot wall on the north
side.

Mr. Hall shared his feelings about an eight-foot wall around the north and west sections. This is a
for-sale product and they have to market it, so he understands that they have to create
something they think they can sell. It is imperative to him that there is an eight-foot fence for the
existing residents around the project.

Mr. Verhaaren noted that the grading will change, and they are open to the eight-foot fence since
the closest fence in those areas would be 55 feet from the residence. They are platting these as
individual lots, they have not decided if they are for sale or rent, but both options are on the table.
He also added that none of these will be subsidized housing unless the market falls and they
have to subsidize them by selling them.

The commission and applicant discussed their final thoughts on the fencing.
