2.1 #FrontRunner Station UDOT/UTA Update (Presenter, Brian Allen-UDQT).
Brian Allen reported that the FrontRunner 2X Project is a joint venture between the Utah
Department of Transportation ("UDOT") and Utah Transit Authority ("UTA"). State law requires
all state projects to be delivered by UDOT even if they are operated by UTA.

FrontRunner has been in service since 2008 from Ogden to Provo and has moved approximately
50 million people since its inception. Ridership decreased dramatically during COVID but has
made a strong comeback. UTA is currently participating in the Federal Transit Administration
(“FTA”) Core Capacity Program to alleviate congestion. Ridership is at 90% capacity during peak
hour/peak direction, and the project will double train frequency on the existing FrontRunner
corridor to reduce that congestion and increase ridership.
FrontRunner 2X will make transit more convenient, easier to use, and more reliable.

e Trains will run every 15 minutes, so riders no longer need to check schedules.

e Increased double tracking will result in fewer delays. Trains were currently delayed in
Draper due to the single tracks, and double tracking would increase flexibility and
reliability.

e By 2025, FrontRunner travel time will be approximately 20% faster than Interstate 15.
UTA anticipates this percentage to increase with double tracking, acquisition of more
efficient trains, and signaling improvements.

e With the above improvements, ridership is forecasted to increase by 53% by 2050.

The project timeline was reviewed. All environmental studies for double-tracking sections have
been completed, and are moving toward final design. The project will be delivered through the
progressive design-build method, which is similar to the method Bluffdale is utilizing on the 14600
South project. Mr. Allen reported that Union Pacific is a major stakeholder in the project as
FrontRunner runs parallel to their tracks. UTA is currently in the process of procuring more
vehicles to allow for more frequent services. They also selected a designer for the maintenance
facility and submitted a grant application for FTA funding.

Mr. Allen next played a video explaining strategic double tracking and its expected operational

impact on the FrontRunner system. A project map was then reviewed, indicating that a new

double-tracking section will extend 3.1 miles from Draper Station to 15180 South in Bluffdale and

include the new FrontRunner Station. The double-tracking section from Lehi to Bluffdale will

also be extended. Once these items are completed, FrontRunner will operate more efficiently and

with better headways. Their goal is to eventually double-track the entire system.

In coordination with Point of the Mountain State Land Authority and other stakeholders, the station

location had been finalized. They were working to finalize the access road location and

maintenance responsibilities. Minor improvements were anticipated for 14600 South to

accommodate left- and right-turn movement into the station, but they did not anticipate an initial

need for a traffic signal.

Draft renderings of the station were reviewed. A pedestrian bridge will be installed over the Union

Pacific tracks similar to the Farmington station. Union Pacific had approved the overhead

crossing, and the next stage would be the purchase of aerial easements. Pedestrian connections to

the neighborhood to the west of the station were removed based on community feedback.

Mr. Allen reported that the FTA-required National Environmental Policy Act (“NEPA”)

documents had been completed and approved. A Planning and Environmental Linkage (“PEL”)

study had also been completed and was available on the project website. The Categorical
Exclusion (“CATEX”) study, which clears infrastructure improvements for construction, had also

been completed. The primary focuses of the PEL study are noise, vibration, and traffic.

An environmental review was conducted to evaluate potential impacts on the natural and built

environment. No noise or vibration impacts were identified.

2.3 14600 South (Redwood Road to Springview Farms) Corridor Study (Presenter,
Jeremy Searle, WCG).
Jeremy Searle from Wall Consultant Group presented the 14600 South Environmental Feasibility
Study. Environmental Specialist, Marti Hoge, was also present. The culvert project on 14600
South would begin within the next few weeks, and the feasibility study looked at extending those
improvements to Redwood Road.
Project goals include:
e Accommodate future demand and improve safety;
e Enhance active transportation, such as walking and biking;
e 1690 West intersection improvement; and
e Minimize impacts to residents and the existing bridge, river, and canal.
14600 South is currently at or over capacity, with between 11,000 and 13,000 vehicles per day in
the study area and 16,000 to 26,000 near Interstate 15. In the future, between 17,000 and 18,000
vehicles per day were expected in the study area, which would be within the road’s capacity if the
proposed improvements were completed.
A typical road cross-section was reviewed. The study proposed construction of one additional
travel lane in each direction. Similar roadways often include a two-way center turn lane, but turn
lanes were only recommended at key intersections on 14600 South. Active transportation facilities
would include sidewalks for pedestrians, as well as bicycle lanes positioned behind the curb and
gutter to increase safety.
