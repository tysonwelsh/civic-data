Golden Star Stucco & Plaster, LLC 3751
West 4400 South
R-1-8 Zone (.18 Acres)

The applicant, Hilario Figueroa, representing Golden Star Stucco & Plaster, LLC, is requesting
conditional use approval for a major home occupation for storage of a larger vehicle for
property located at 3751 West 4400 South. The zoning for this area is R-1-8, (Single Unit
Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet). The West Valley City General Plan
designates this area as Low Density Residential. The surrounding zone is R-1-8 on all sides and
the surrounding uses include residential.

A business license was submitted for this property in February 2022. The applicant indicated
that there were several vehicles and trailers used for the business and that they were stored off
site. However, when the site review was conducted the vehicles and some additional equipment
and material was found being stored at the property. At that time the home occupation
standards were discussed, the applicant has removed the violations from the property and has
decided to pursue conditional use approval for storage of one of the larger trucks. (2003
Chevrolet C34 Flatbed Truck).

That process is outlined in 7-7-115:
        The maximum size of a vehicle used in association with a Home Occupation shall be
        equivalent to a one-ton pickup truck. If a Conditional Use Permit is obtained and the
        vehicle is screened and parked on private property, a larger commercial vehicle may be
        used if it is not a Heavy Truck.

        A “Heavy Truck” means concrete transport trucks, dump trucks, garbage trucks, log
        carriers, mobile cranes, refrigerator trucks, tractor trucks, tank trucks, and similar
        vehicles.

No other vehicles, trailers or equipment will be stored on site. This property is a corner lot and
there is an existing parking pad on the west side that will accommodate the flat bed truck. The
area is partially fenced so additional screening will be required as part of this application. The
applicant has indicated that a vinyl fence will be added to the north side of the pad. The gate
area also needs screening. Staff recommends that the gate openings be covered with vinyl slats
that match the additional fencing be added. All fencing materials must meet the City Code and
be intended as fencing materials. No fabric, plywood or metal may be used as fencing.

In order to accommodate the clear view requirements the gate must be relocated further north
or swing in to the site and not out into the sidewalk and roadway.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as the
following conditions:

        1. The property shall be used for storage of one vehicle in association with the Home
           Occupation. That vehicle is a larger flat bed pickup truck and can not be a Heavy
           Truck as outlined in the City Code.

        2. The truck must be parked on the subject property on a hard surfaced pad and
           screened as outlined in the staff report.

        3. Additional fencing must be added to the north side of the pad as well as the gate.
           The fencing shall be vinyl or stucco to match the existing fencing. The gate
           screen shall also be vinyl or a similar material. No metal, plywood or fabric
           mesh shall be used.

        4. There shall be no additional outside storage at this site.

        5. Must maintain the required clear views by either relocating the access gate further
           north or having it open in to the site and not out into the sidewalk and roadway.

        6. This use is subject to review upon a valid complaint.

Continuance, to allow for the resolutions of any issues raised at the public hearing and to allow
time for the applicant to submit more detailed information.

CA-4-2022
Valley Collision Outside Storage
2738 S Constitution Blvd
M Zone (1.74 acres)

Valley Collision, located at 2738 S Constitution Blvd, is requesting conditional use approval for
outside storage of vehicles on the property. The property is zoned M, Manufacturing, and the
West Valley City General Plan designates this area as light manufacturing. Zoning to the north
is M, zoning to the west and south is R-1-8, and zoning to the east is RMH, residential mobile
home. Adjacent land uses include light industrial businesses to the north, a Rocky Mountain
Power corridor to the west and south, and a mobile home park to the east.

Valley Collision recently started to operate from the subject property. They are an autobody
repair and painting business. There was a business previously on the property that also did
collision repair, C-46-1997, but that approval did not allow for any outside storage on the
property. There is one area on the north side of the building that was approved for the storage of
vehicles during the day, but the conditional use approval did not allow outside storage. The
applicant would like to use this area to the north, as well as a large portion of the existing
parking lot on the south end for vehicle storage.

Per 7-7-119, outside storage areas are to be screened from public view. The applicant has
installed a chain link fence with slats along the southern area of the existing parking lot that
they would like to use for storage. The area on the north side of the building was previously
fenced with chain link and slats. All areas of the site where vehicle storage will occur was
previously paved with asphalt.

The property has frontage on Constitution Blvd, one of the City’s high image arterial
streets. The site currently has the 20’ required landscaped setback along the frontage and
street trees. Some additional trees were also recently planted as part of a business license
review of the property. All landscaped areas shall be maintained.

The original conditional use approval for the site required 40 parking stalls for one building,
and an additional building was to be built later. A site plan included with that original approval
showed both buildings and indicated there would be 59 total parking stalls on the site. There are
currently 48 parking stalls. With the two outside storage areas proposed on the property, 22
customer and employee parking stalls will remain. The applicant has indicated that in the
building there is 2,000 sq. ft. of office, 4,000 sq. ft. of storage, and 14,000 sf of production
space, which includes 12 auto body bays and 6 paint bays. He also indicated that the highest
employment shift currently has 10 employees, but 15 or 16 employees would be ideal. The
applicant also said that they typically have up to three customers on the site at one time to drop
off or pick-up vehicles.

West Valley City code requires 3 customer and employee parking spaces for each service bay.
The parking code requirement is also 1 stall per 250 sq. ft. of office area and 1 stall per 2,000
sq. ft. of storage. Based on the City code, the building square footage, and number of service
baysin the building, the parking requirement for the site is 64 parking stalls. For reference, staff
has provided parking numbers for similar auto-related uses from ITE’s Parking Generation
Manual:

        Tire Store: 2.85 stalls/1,000 SF Monday – Friday and 4.17 stalls/1,000 SF on Saturday
        Quick Lubrication Vehicle Shop: 5.07 stalls/1,000 SF Monday – Friday
        Auto Parts and Service Center: 1.69 stalls/1,000 SF Monday – Friday and
         1.07 stalls/1,000 SF on Saturday

Going from 64 stalls to 22 is quite a reduction from what is required in the City Code. During
the study meeting some of the Planning Commission members seemed hesitant to allow such a
reduction. There are already on-street parking issues in front of the business. Some of the stalls
in the fenced areas could be treated as somewhat of a flex space, where employees park in
stalls during the day and at night the stalls are used for vehicle storage. Staff has provided three
different parking stall requirement options in the approval. An option for continuance is also
included which could allow the applicant to provide more information or a parking study
justifying the request to the Planning Commission.

Staff Alternatives:


Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as
the following:

             1. All outside storage areas shall be screened from public view using chain
                link fencing with slats and shall be paved with asphalt of concrete.
             2. Landscaping on the property shall be maintained.
             3. Parking on the property shall meet one of the following requirements:
                    a. All 7-9 requirements shall be met, which includes providing enough
                        parking for 3 stalls per service bay, 1 stall per 250 sq. ft. of office space,
                        and 1 stall per 2,000 sq. ft. of storage space. In the current proposal
                        there are 18 service bays, but some of these service bays could be
                        removed from the building, which would reduce the total amount of
                        parking required.
                    b. Parking on the property can be reduced from the requirements of 7-9,
                        but there shall be at least 36 customer and employee parking stalls
                        provided on the property, or 2 stalls per service bay. No additional
                        stall shall be required for the office and storage areas of the building.
                    c. Parking on the property can be reduced from the requirements of 7-9,
                        but there shall be a minimum of 22 parking stalls provided on the
                         property.
              4. All requirements of any affected department or agency must be met, including
                 but not limited to Granger Hunter Improvement District, WVC Public Works,
                 WVC Building Inspections and WVC Fire Department.
              5. Subject to review upon valid complaint.

Continuance, to allow the applicant more time to provide additional information justifying
their request for a parking reduction on the site.

The applicant, Galloway & Company, is requesting conditional use approval for a convenience
store with fuel sales, which will be located on two parcels at 6337 W 3500 S. The property is
zoned C-1, Neighborhood Commercial, and the West Valley City General Plan designates this
area as Neighborhood Commercial. Zoning to the north is Agriculture, west is C-1 and R-1-8,
and zoning to the south and east is R-1-8. The surrounding uses include single unit dwelling and
two-unit dwelling residential to the north, south, east, and west, as well as another convenience
store with fuel sales to the west.

The applicant is proposing to redevelop the property for this project. The existing building is
currently occupied by South Fork Hardware. The applicant is proposing to scrape the site and
construct a new 5,620 sq. ft. building for a convenience store as well as install a new fuel
canopy. The property has three street frontages. Access to the site will be from 3500 South and
6400 West. UDOT will need to approve the 3500 South access. Public Works has determined

residential zone. The building and parking cannot encroach into the required setbacks. A 6’ tall

The building shall meet all the Commercial Design Standards outlined in 7-11-300P. Exterior
materials will be primarily fiber cement panels with some metal accents. The fuel canopy also
needs to include the same architectural elements that were used on the building, as outlined in

C-20-2022
Sage Valley Townhomes
1854 W 4100 S
RM Zone (1.54 acres)


Grey Garza is requesting conditional use approval to construct townhomes at 1854 W 4100
S. The property is zoned RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) and is designated as very
high density residential in the City’s General Plan. The project will include 18 units.


Background
In April of 2018 the site received RM zoning approval, which included a development
agreement. The development agreement, which is included, was later amended by the City
Council. This project is located on the west side of the Sage Valley Apartments. The apartment
project received conditional use approval in November of 2019. The east tower of the project
is nearing completion. Some existing buildings were demolished on the south end of the
subject project area, but a majority of the site has remained undeveloped since K-Mart
developed the property originally.


Project Review
The proposal includes 18, 3-story townhome units, divided between four buildings. There will
be two 5-unit buildings and two four-unit buildings. Access to the property will be from the
apartment project parking lot on the north and south ends of the parcel. On the west side of
the property is a commercial property as well as single unit dwellings. Existing multi-unit
dwelling residential borders the north edge.

Buildings
The units are required to be at least 2,400 sq. ft. of finished floor area per the Development
Agreement. The floor plans reviewed by the Planning Commission in the Study Meeting under
the initial proposal did not meet the minimum square footage requirement. The applicant has
since updated the plans and the unit sizes now vary between 2,424 sq. ft. and 2,451 sq. ft. per
unit. The building’s exterior includes brick, fiber cement siding, stucco, and metal panels. The
building elevations need to comply with all materials, building relief, building design, window
treatments, and roof treatments outlined in 7-11-400 of the City Code. Staff recommends
adding a band between the 2nd and 3rd floors, as was shown in the Development Agreement
elevations. The building is currently lacking one window treatment on the Primary Façade.
The Planning Commission has the discretion to determine if any other treatment meets the
intent of section 7-11-408.

All units will include individual patio and balcony spaces on all three floors. The applicant is
proposing to include roof-top decks on the end units of each building. Due to the existing
single unit dwelling residential units that are adjacent to the property, staff recommends that a
railing is provided to block access to the portion of the roof that extends on the west side of the
door for the building in the north-west corner.

Landscaping
There will be 5’ of landscaping along the west and north edge of the property. A 6’ masonry
wall will also be installed along these edges. All other areas of the site around the buildings
will be landscaped. Landscaped areas need to include at least 50% live plant material.

Amenities
The townhomes will share amenities with the apartment project. The amenity area is located
adjacent to the townhomes on the east edge of the parcel. Amenities will include a swimming
pool, hot tub, basketball court, and a tot lot.

Parking
Each unit will have a 20’x20’ garage. At the time the property was rezoned, townhomes only
required two parking spaces per unit, however it appeared that many of the units would have
driveways. Most of the proposed driveways from the application submittal were only between
10 and 15 feet deep, which would not accommodate a car to park in front of the garage. The
apartment project also provided 8 more parking stalls than were required in their conditional use
proposal, so some of those stalls could be shared with the townhomes. Staff recommends that
the applicant try to add additional parking spaces for the project on the townhome parcel. At the
time this staff report was written the applicant was still working on modifying the site plan to
accommodate for the larger unit sizes as well as adding additional parking.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as the
following:

     1. The applicant shall meet all provisions in the development agreement.
     2. Roof-top decks on the building in the north-west corner of the site shall not extend
        across the full roof. A railing shall be installed on the west side of the door that
        exits the building to prevent full use of the roof-top area.
     3. The building elevations shall comply with the following requirements:
            a. All materials, building relief, building design, and roof treatments outlined in 7-
                11-400 of the City Code shall be met.
            b. A band shall be added along the same line as the patios between the 2nd and
                3rd stories as was shown in Exhibit I of the Development Agreement.
            c. The window treatments as proposed on the primary façade are sufficient.
     4. All landscaping shall include at least 50% live plant material.
     5. Additional surface parking stalls shall be provided on the townhome parcel.
     6. All requirements of any affected department or agency must be met, including but not

C-2 Zone (1.09 Acres)

The property owner at 3497 S 5600 W is requesting modifications to the requirements of
Section 7-10- 103 of Title 7, Standards For Landscaping Along High-Image Arterial Streets.
This property is one of many that was impacted by UDOT’s widening of the intersection at
3500 South and 5600 West, in connection with the Mountain View Corridor project. Both
streets are subject to the regulations outlined in section 7-10 of the West Valley City Municipal
Code, which defines landscaping standards for properties that have frontage on high-image
arterial streets. The right-of-way improvements at the intersection shifted the sidewalk on the
site, leaving about 13’ of landscaping along 3500 South, and just under 20’ of landscaping
along 5600 West. The code requires a minimum of a 20’ landscape buffer adjacent to the
sidewalk. The Planning Commission can grant a modification to the 20’ landscaped buffer
requirement as outlined in 7-10-104.

ft., and the number of employees on the highest employment shift, 18, City Code requires 74
parking spaces. Removing additional parking stalls to accommodate more landscaping on the
site would only make the parking situation more non-conforming. Due to this, the property
owner is requesting that the size of both landscape buffers be allowed to remain as they are.
They have agreed to meet all other requirements of 7-10-103. These requirements include
installing one tree for every 30’ of frontage, 50% lawn or artificial turf and 50% live plant
material in the remaining areas.

M-7-2022
Tunex Landscaping Exception
3549 S 5600 W
C-2-Zone (0.38 acres)

The property owner of 3549 S 5600 W is requesting modifications to the requirements of
Section 7-10- 103 of Title 7, Landscaping Standards Applicable to All High-Image Arterials.
This property is one of many that was impacted by UDOT’s widening of the intersection at
3500 South and 5600 West, in connection with the Mountain View Corridor project. 5600 West
is subject to the regulations outlined in section 7-10 of the West Valley City Municipal Code,
which defines landscaping standards for properties that have frontage on high-image arterial
streets. The right-of-way improvements shifted everything to the east on this site, so some of the
landscaping adjacent to the sidewalk was eliminated. The code requires a minimum of a 20’
landscape buffer adjacent to the sidewalk, but there is currently only about a 14’ buffer
remaining. The Planning Commission can grant a modification to the 20’ landscaped buffer
requirement as outlined in 7-10-104.

The building on the property is 59’ from the edge of the 14’ landscape buffer. Currently there
are two parking stalls in front, including an ADA stall, which leaves 41’ between the parking
and the building. The code requires a 24’ drive aisle and there should also be a 5’ sidewalk in
front of the building.

There is technically enough room to shift the landscaping to the east to meet the full 20’
requirement, however there are two service bay doors on the front of the building. Moving the
landscaping and parking would cut into the maneuvering room in front of the bay doors. Most
of the parking for the business is behind the building. There are two additional service bays on
the east side of the building. With four service bays in the business, the site is required to have
12 parking stalls. There are currently 18 parking stalls on the subject parcel.

Regardless of whether the landscape buffer along the frontage is increased or not, the area
should be modified to meet all other requirements of 7-10-103. These requirements include
installing one tree for every 30’ of frontage, 50% lawn or artificial turf and 50% live plant
material in the remaining areas.

If the Planning Commission feels that it’s reasonable to not require the full 20’ of landscaping
next to the sidewalk, there is a large existing landscaped area to the north of the parking lot in
the front where additional trees and landscaping could be added. The other landscaping on the
site should also meet the approval from the original landscape plan or shall conform to current
code requirements. A landscape plan needs to be submitted to staff for review before any work
is completed.

Staff Alternatives

Approval. Twenty feet of landscaping is not required directly adjacent to the sidewalk,
but the following modifications shall be made to the site as described below.

     1. 1 tree shall be added for every 30’ of frontage and selected from the table in 7-10-
        103(3).
     2. At least 50% of the landscaped area shall be grass or artificial turf that meets the
        standards in 7- 2-127.
     3. All remaining areas between the sidewalk and the building shall be landscaped
        with at least 50% live plant material.
     4. The landscaped area north of the front parking lot shall include 50% live plant material
        and two trees.
     5. The landscaping on the remainder of the site shall meet the requirements of the
        original landscape plan or shall conform to current code requirements, which is 50%
        live plant material and required trees as outlined in 7-9-112.
     6. A landscape plan shall be submitted to staff for review and approval before any
        landscaping installation is done.


Denial. All 7-10-103 requirements shall be met, including the required 20’ of landscaping next
to the sidewalk.
