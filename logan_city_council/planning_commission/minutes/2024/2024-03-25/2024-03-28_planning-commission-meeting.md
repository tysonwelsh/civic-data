PC 24-013 Clark Duplex [Design Review Permit] Berg Engineering/MKR Properties LLC, authorized
agent/owner is requesting a Design Review permit for a new duplex with a detached 4-bay garage
located at 328 South 100 West in the Mixed Residential Medium (MR-12) zone; TIN 02-052-0015
(Woodruff Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Planner reviewed the proposal for a new duplex at 328 S 100 W in the MR-12
zone. The site has an existing 1918 home that was converted to a duplex so there would be 4 units
on this property. The MR-12 zone allows for a maximum density of 12 units per acre, at .34 acres
this site can support up to 4 units, so they are right at the maximum density. The applicant was able
to get an easement on the property to the north for the 20-foot shared driveway to allow for
emergency access.

With the four covered parking stalls and four surface parking stalls being proposed, the project meets
the minimum parking requirements in the Land Development Code (LDC). The project also meets
the LDC requirements for setbacks, lot coverage, and design standards. There does need to be a
sidewalk connecting the proposed duplex with the 100 West sidewalk for pedestrian circulation.

Commissioner Croshaw asked about the proposed location of the carport. R. Holley said that a
carport can be attached or detached, it just has to meet the required setbacks.

PROPONENT: Paul Berg with Berg Engineering said that this project originally came through the
Planning Commission in 2008, but the permit expired so they’re applying again. Mr. Berg said they
are willing to add a sidewalk along the south property line to connect the duplex to the City sidewalk
on 100 West to meet pedestrian connectivity requirements.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

PC 24-014 The Main [Design Review Permit] Jeremy Larsen/MGR 456 LLC, authorized agent/owner
is requesting a Design Review permit for 5, 4-bedroom townhomes with a total of 20 parking stalls in
the garage on the ground floor located at 456 North 600 East in the Campus Residential (CR) zone;
TIN 06-058-0014 (Adams Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Planner reviewed the request for a new 5-unit, townhome-style, student
housing building at 456 North 600 East in the Campus Residential (CR) zone, with 4 bedrooms per
unit and a four-car garage on the ground floor. The applicant is proposing to have 6 occupants per
unit (30 total occupants) which would require 30 parking stalls to meet the LDC requirement of 1 stall
per occupant. As proposed the project includes 22 parking stalls, which only meets 73% of the
minimum parking requirement and would require a 27% parking reduction by the Planning
Commission. The LDC allows up to a 25% parking reduction with an approved parking plan. The
Commission will need to determine how much of a parking reduction is acceptable.

The commission will also need to determine if there are enough design elements on the south fagade
to meet the four-sided architecture requirement in the LDC.

As proposed the project meets the design, layout, pedestrian circulation, height, and outdoor space
Requirements in the LDC.

Commissioner Guth asked about the frontage requirement not being met. R. Holley said that there
is no room on the site to adjust the building location to meet the 75% frontage requirement in the
LDC. The Commission has the authority to approve the building placement as proposed with the
frontage adjustments.

PROPONENT: Jeremy Larsen spoke about the project's proximity to bus stops, USU Campus, and
grocery stores. He said that the owner will offer a reduced lease to students who do not have cars
and spoke about how 5 to 6% of students are from out of the country and do not drive.

Commissioner Lucero asked what would happen if they didn't have any students without a car. Mr.
Larsen said they will only be able to lease to students with cars until the parking stalls are taken. As
proposed they would have 6 leases that state that there is no parking provided and they cannot have


a car on campus. Mr. Larsen spoke about limiting the parking to put pressure on people to use a
public transportation system that is readily available.

Georgia Reeder spoke about her daughter in college whose roommate doesn’t drive and has to rely
on public transportation and said that student housing apartments have a first come first serve basis
with parking.

Commissioner Lewis stated that they cannot control if a student has a car or not, they can only control
if they can park it on their property.

PUBLIC: Ronald Bradford, who lives on the block commented about a 40-bed complex that was
approved a year ago with only 8 parking spots.

Kim Heaton, who lives across the street said he likes the idea of less parking, the area is already
congested with traffic.

R. Holley said that the 40-bedroom building that was approved is an inpatient medical facility for
adolescents who are 16 and under and are not allowed to bring a car. The approved parking was
for the staff.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Guth asked about previous student housing projects that had parking
issues. R. Holley said that The Factory and Millennial Towers were both developed when the LDC
allowed for a 50% parking reduction which created some major parking problems and caused the
LDC to be amended back to the 100% parking requirement with an option for a 25% reduction. Mike
DeSimone, Community Development Director said that the intention is good, but the students are
going to drive whether they have a parking spot or not.

Commissioner Lucero said that even if a lease is signed that states they do not have a parking spot,
that does not guarantee they do not have a car. Due to the location and walkability to the university,
she would be comfortable allowing a 10% parking reduction.

Commissioner Lewis said that this building is a good design with the garages on the main floor and
if the applicant could adjust their double occupancy units they would have enough parking, or at least
need less of a reduction.

Jeremy Larsen spoke about the two tandem parking stalls outside having to belong to the same unit
and requested the commission to allow a parking reduction that would allow them to have 26 total
occupants.

Commissioner Lucero said she would be comfortable allowing a 15% parking reduction to
accommodate a total occupancy of 26.

Commissioner Lewis said he is not in favor of a 15% reduction and feels it will still push cars out into
the community.
