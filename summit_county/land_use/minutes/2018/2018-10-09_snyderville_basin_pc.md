Chair Peck said she will go over the list of questions that was raised by the public.   
 Commercial use and what happens with the next owner.   
Attorney Thomas said if problems arise, it would be a Code Enforcement issue.  The 
Code Enforcement Officer relies heavily on complaints received.  Because the CUP 
runs with the land, it cannot be used as a commercial facility even in the future.  If it 
is found that it is being used commercially, the Code Enforcement officer can issue 
notices of violations to prohibit the activity.  Citations issued would go before the 
Administrator Law Judge who has the power to issue fines.  
 
 What uses are allowed?  What is the process if there is a change in use? 
Attorney Thomas read the uses allowed in the zone.  If the use is changed from what 
is currently allowed, a new CUP must be applied for with another public hearing 
before the Planning Commission.   
 
 The limits on accessory buildings. 
Planner Milliner said currently in the Code, an accessory building less than 2,000 
square feet is allowed.  A structure greater than 2,000 square feet is a low impact 
permit.  An accessory building greater than 10,000 square feet requires a CUP.  
There are no limitations on the number of accessory buildings.  The height and 
setbacks for the zone apply.  Attorney Thomas said the architectural guidelines 
outline the scale and massing of structures.  Landscaping requirements are outlined 
in Chapter 4 of the Code. 
 Are the visual impacts required to be mitigated?   
Planner Milliner said the closest the Code comes to addressing visual mitigation is to 
require the structure to be compatible with the neighborhood.  Attorney Thomas 
added there is a provision in the landscape provisions that talk about the façade and 
the length of the building.

 Can the location of a structure on the property be addressed by the Commission?   
Planner Milliner said the Commission can take into account the proximity of the 
wetlands, sensitive lands, extreme slopes, and access.  Chair Peck said the 
Commission should consider if the location of the building is creating a harmful 
impact.  Attorney Thomas said the criteria for that consideration would be found in 
the CUP.   Assuming setbacks are being met, proximity to a neighbor is not a 
consideration.   
 
 Only one accessory dwelling unit can be built.      
Planner Milliner said that restriction is currently not part of the Code.  
 
 Restrictive Use Covenant  
Attorney Thomas said that is a specialized covenant that is for the benefit of the 
County.   It is recorded against the property and runs with the land.   It prohibits an 
accessory unit from being sold separately from the main unit.  It puts some 
guidelines on what the accessory unit can be.  It can be applied to an accessory 
dwelling unit, but not an accessory structure.  On this particular application, one 
could be put on the dwelling unit, but not the entire building.  The CUP acts as a 
restrictive use covenant for the recreational facility.  
 
 Construction mitigation  
This comes into play when the building permit is obtained.  Engineering has an 
inspector that will note mud on the roads.  Citations have been issued.  Jared Higgins 
said the State also regulates this and the fines are hefty.  
 
Zane Kelly said sometimes they can’t control the subcontractors pulling a trailer in

call and request the mud be removed from the road.

 Lighting for the structure 
The only exterior lighting will be to ensure safety.  They may have some landscaping 
low-voltage lighting.  The safety lighting will be compliant with dark sky ordinances.  
The light will shine down towards the path.  Lighting is reviewed by Staff.  The

 Septic tanks

Planner Strader said that Staff is proposing to amend Chapters 1 & 2 in order to bring it 
into alignment with the General Plan, which was adopted in 2015.  At that time, there 
was a discussion to update the Code to ensure that the needed tools were available.  It 
was decided to remove the regulatory language from the General Plan and put it in the 
Development Code.

Planner Strader said the amendments to Chapter 1 are to simplify the chapter and 
remove language that is more suited to the General Plan.  It establishes the purpose and 
authority which is allowed in the State Code.  Chapter 2 is a new chapter.  It is a user 
guide to the Code.  It is intended to provide instructions for the general public.
