Action Item

Applicant:     Woodbury Corporation
Address:       2252 South 300 West, Times Square Project

Planning Consultant, Alex White Halleck, presented the Staff Report and stated that the application
is for Final Plat approval of property located at 2252 South 300 West. In September 2023, a
Preliminary Subdivision Plat was presented to the Planning Commission for the Times Square
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                 4

Project. She shared a map of the area and identified six existing parcels, several of which have the
same address. The intention was to consolidate and re-subdivide them into three new parcels,
which were shown on the presentation slides. In the end, there would be three individual lots. Lot 1
is a proposed multi-family building and Lots 2 and 3 are vacant land. All of the parcels are in the
Downtown District so there was no request to change the zoning. The only use that was currently
being considered was Lot 1, which was the multi-family building.

The applicant is currently going through the Conditional Use Permit application process.
Ms. White Halleck explained that the Conditional Use Permit application will come to the
Planning Commission at a future meeting. The details of the multi-family building would be
reviewed at that time. Images of the existing conditions were shared. Ms. White Halleck reported
that an ALTA survey was completed as part of the platting process. 300 West, Bugatti Avenue,
and Bearcat Drive are all South Salt Lake City public rights-of-way. There are a lot of utilities in
those rights-of-way, which was part of the analysis done with South Salt Lake Engineering to
ensure that there is adequate infrastructure. There were right-of-way dedications taking place on
the plat, specifically along Bugatti Avenue. There would be a widening of the road and
improvement to the curb, gutter, and sidewalk along the proposed development.

Ms. White Halleck noted that some existing utilities fell through Lot 2 of the proposed plat. Those
would remain in place with public easements over them. At the time the lot was ultimately
redeveloped, the owners will need to design around them or work through the process with South
Salt Lake Engineering. She identified a private utility and stated that it would be relocated. The
yellow on the map represented overhead power, which would be coordinated with Rocky
Mountain Power for relocation. Ms. White Halleck reported that Parley’s Trail comes along the
west side of 300 West and the north side of the proposed development. It would remain in place
and easements were on the plat to account for that.

Lot 1 was the proposed multi-family development. During the Conditional Use Permit process,
additional details about that development would be reviewed. The developer is proposing to phase
the construction of the project partly due to funding needs. The idea was to build Phase I of the
multi-family building along the 300 West corridor. When construction was completed on that half,
the western half would be constructed. Ms. White Halleck reiterated that additional details about
the phased approach would be shared during the Conditional Use Permit process. She reported that
a Utah Transit Authority (“UTA”) South Salt Lake easement is proposed, as there is a UTA
substation in the middle of the development. Access needs to be maintained to that substation. It
is also a good location for fire access for Lots 1 and 2.

Ms. White Halleck reviewed the elevation renderings. When the Conditional Use Permit
application comes to the Planning Commission, there will be updated renderings to show it at full
build-out. The Final Plat was shared. Ms. White Halleck stated that this is a three-lot subdivision
and identified Lots 1, 2, and 3 on the plat. The only thing that was not currently showing was a
Public Utility Easement (“PUE”) where the utilities connect. That would be added through the
engineering review process. The plat was reviewed by South Salt Lake Engineering, third party
engineering, and was in the review process with Salt Lake County for the technical review. General
Plan considerations were reviewed. The Downtown District encourages this type of growth and
density. The proposed uses were found to comply with what the zoning allowed. There were
several examples in the General Plan to support the development.

        Plat, to Construct and Operate an Indoor Climate Controlled Storage Development,
        Located at 2385 South 300 West.

Action Item

Applicant:     A8-South Salt Lake, LLC, Represented by Jarod Hall

Address:       2385 South 300 West

Ms. Ungricht presented the Staff Report and explained that the application was for a Preliminary
Plat for property located at 2385 South 300 West. She stated that an Ordinance Amendment was
approved approximately 1 ½ years ago to allow indoor climate-controlled storage facilities in
South Salt Lake City. There is now a Preliminary Plat application for the property before the
Planning Commission. The Conditional Use Permit would be brought before the Planning
Commission when the item is brought for Final Plat approval.

Ms. Ungricht reported that a legal lot is needed to have any Building Permits, which was the reason
the application was before the Commission for a one-lot subdivision plat. The building on the front
of the parcel that fronts 300 West will remain in place. That building was compliant with parking.
It was more the back half of the parcel, which was outlined in red on the image shown, where the
new project will be built. That area was the focus of the discussion. Images of the existing
conditions were shared. There are currently 140 units. The City no longer allows externally loaded
storage units in the City, so that would be considered a non-conforming use. What was proposed
would be conforming and more secure than the externally loaded units.
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                   14
January 18, 2024


The proposed elevations were shown. Ms. Ungricht reviewed the rendering and explained that
there will be four stories and approximately 110,000 square feet. The lot in total was slightly over
two acres but she reiterated that construction would only occur on the eastern half of the property.
The ALTA survey was shared, which is where the existing storage units were currently. She
explained that it took some time for the application to come before the Planning Commission
because a storm drain was proposed to be relocated. Figuring out all of those details took time.
The storm drain was shown in pink on the ALTA survey. There are easements in the orange shown
and the red represents the property line. In addition, there is a billboard on the site, which can be
seen on the left-hand side of the survey. That billboard would remain in place, as the newly
proposed building will not encroach on that.

Ms. Ungricht reviewed the Proposed Site Plan. The red indicated the fire access on the site. She

        Also to Correct Some Technical Errors in Code, and to Update Definitions.

Action Item

Applicant:     South Salt Lake City

Ms. Ungricht presented the Staff Report and stated that the application pertains to ADUs. The
matter was previously before the Planning Commission during the Work Meeting on December 7,
2023. It was discussed by the City Council on December 13, 2023, during a Work Meeting. A few
changes have been made since the initial presentations. The ordinance would address ADUs and
allow I-ADUs and E-ADUs in the R1 and RM Zones. She clarified that I-ADUs are already
allowed but the ordinance language will add some standards. In addition to the ordinance language,
there were a few typos that would be corrected, and updated definitions proposed. The matter
would be brought to the City Council on January 24, 2024.
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                  18
January 18, 2024

Ms. Ungricht reviewed some of the proposed changes. One of the most significant definition
modifications was the removal of the barber shop definition so there could be a more
comprehensive cosmetology definition instead. Throughout the Code, references to a barber shop
would be replaced with cosmetology. Some definitions would also be added that would apply to
ADUs, such as building footprint, canopy structure, carport, mobile home, primary dwelling,
recreation vehicle, and trailer. Most of those were already defined in the Code but would be
clarified.

One of the definitions was for a short-term rental. It would state that a short-term rental is a unit
that is rented for less than 30 days. As discussed during the Work Meeting, it was recommended
that all ADUs be prohibited from being short-term rentals. That meant an ADU could only be
rented out for 30 days or more. Ms. Ungricht reported that there would also be definitions included
for ADU, I-ADU, and E-ADU. The definitions were as follows:

   •   Dwelling Unit, Accessory (“ADU”) means an additional, self-contained housing unit that
       is secondary to the primary dwelling, including I-ADUs and E-ADUs.
   •   Dwelling Unit, Internal Accessory, means an ADU created:
           o Within a primary dwelling;
           o Within the footprint of the primary dwelling at the time the I-ADU is created; and
           o For the purpose of offering a long-term rental of 30 consecutive days or longer.
   •   Dwelling Unit, External Accessory, means an ADU that is not part of the primary
       dwelling’s footprint or sharing a common wall.

17.03.010 – Land Use Matrix was discussed. Ms. Ungricht explained that I-ADUs and E-ADUs
would be added to the Land Use Matrix as permitted uses in the R1 and RM Districts. Additionally,
the reference from the barber shop/hair salon would be replaced with cosmetology. In the
Permitted Use section, there were standards in place for I-ADUs and E-ADUs. Those would be a
permitted use, but would need to meet the following standards in order to legally operate:

   •   Set a maximum number of ADUs at two per property, one internal and one external;
   •   Prohibit short-term rentals;
   •   Require a Business License;
   •   Prohibit the use of mobile homes, portable containers, carports, canopy structures, and
       other similar structures as an ADU;
   •   Require owner occupancy, with a few exceptions, like active military service.

Commissioner Pechmann asked about portable containers. He assumed that referenced shipping

into a dwelling unit. Ms. Ungricht explained that if someone can refurbish it, have it connected to

there could not be a shipping container used as an ADU without those steps.
