4. Public Hearing, Petition to Rezone, property at 1698 Kiesel from CP-2 to R-1-5.

   As the applicant was not present, Mr. Simpson explained the petition is to rezone a lot from commercial
   CP-2 back to residential. The lot is to the rear of the existing Bott Monument store and was originally a
   residential lot. The home had been removed and the lot has since been used as parking for customers
   of the monument shop. The owners now desire to rezone the lot back to residential in order for a home
   to be constructed.

   Mr. Simpson indicated when reviewing a rezoning petition, the Commission is to consider the
   Community Plan and the Community Policies. Policies indicate an edge should be created and zoning
   should reflect the character of the surrounding community. He stated 17 th Street provides a buffer
   between the residential and commercial zone to the south, and all lots along Kiesel in this block are
   residential in character. The Mountain View Plan indicates the core of the single family neighborhood
   should be strengthened and suggests lots facing local streets should be residential. As Kiesel is a local
   street, the rezoning is supported by the Community Plan. Staff recommendation is for approval of the
   rezoning as requested.

   Commissioner Schade noted when the property was rezoned to commercial in 2011, it was felt it is too
   small for a typical home without requiring variances, and stated that is still the case, suggesting it may
   not work as a residential lot. Mr. Simpson responded the lot is similar to other lots within the block and
   may warrant a smaller home. Commissioner Garner asked about eliminating off-street parking and
   asked if adequate parking is available. Mr. Simpson answered due to required setbacks for parking lots
   in commercial zones, the lot created only two parking spaces. He stated as this is a corner lot, there is
   adequate parking along both the street frontages of 17th Street and Washington. Commissioner
   Southwick stated the business is not busy and felt on-street parking is adequate for its service. He felt
   the residential log is the highest and best use of the lot, and that a home would fit on the lot and fit
   better with the surrounding neighborhood.

As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to close the public
hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Southwick and passed unanimously.

   MOTION:     A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to recommend approval of the rezoning as

6, Request for Encroachment Permit, to allow fence along Old Post Road at rear of property located at
   5289 Winchester.

   Ms. Lacey Wilkinson stated her residential property has frontages on both Winchester and Old Post
   Road. This request is for a fence within the Old post Road right-of-way in order to secure her back yard
   and provide safety to her young children. She indicated she had contacted Weber Basin Water and had
   been informed no fence would be allowed within the aqueduct easement which exists on the site. In
   order to avoid the easement, the request is for the fence to be installed 3’ within the right-of-way.

    Ms. Olson stated the application is to allow a 6’ wooden fence which would extend 3’6” into the right-of-
   way along Old Post Road. She stated the property is in the R-1-10 zone and has frontages on both
   Winchester and Old Post Road, and the BLM owns a water easement which is along the rear property
   line. The BLM will not allow a fence within the easement, and if a fence were placed inside the
   easement, the usable rear yard would be eliminated. The owner is requesting an encroachment permit
   to allow a fence within the Old Post Road right-of-way to protect her family from busy traffic along Old
   Post Road. The Commission is to consider whether the request is reasonable given the physical limits
   of the property, whether there is good cause for the encroachment and whether the encroachment will
   interfere with the use of the public right-of-way.

   Old Post Road is platted as a 66’ right-of-way, with no sidewalk. The owner’s property line is 12.5’ from
   the back of curb, and the easement begins 9’6” from the back of curb. Staff feels options are limited
   and feels there is good cause for the encroachment to provide a secure and safe back yard, While the
   use of the right-of-way along Old Post Road would not be affected, the owner needs to be aware there is
   also a sanitary sewer line 5’ from the back of curb, and if any utility lines need service in the future, the
   fence may be removed by the utility company. Engineering staff has indicated they will approve the
   encroachment permit so long as the applicant signs an agreement indicating the fence will not interfere
   with existing utilities along Old Post Road. Staff feels the request complies with all zoning ordinances
   and review comments and recommends approval of the fence as requested.

   Commissioner Sandau expressed concern about the care of landscaping on the outside of the fence.
   Ms. Wilkinson indicated the intent is to place rocks and native grasses in this area which would reduce
   the need for maintenance, but a gate will be installed to provide access. Commissioner Safsten

                                                      [8]
expressed concern about the appearance of the right-of-way with a fence along Old Post Road,
particularly due to adjacent homes which face the street. He also was concerned with the maintenance
of the area behind the fence. Commissioner Stoker asked if there were other alternatives such as a
hedge which would provide a barrier but be more aesthetic than a fence. Ms. Olson stated the root
system in hedges create problems with water lines and felt a fence is a better option. Commission
asked about the speed limit of traffic on Old Post Road. Commissioner Schade felt the fence should be
limited to 4’ in height in order to better blend with adjacent homes facing Old Post Road. Chair Graf
suggested the fence might be picket or other material rather than being a solid barrier. Commissioner
Safsten asked if the Commission can regulate the size and materials. Ms. Olson stated the
determination is limited to whether there is good cause and whether it complies with all applicable
ordinances. The ordinance allows a fence up to 6’ in height in the rear yard. Mr. Stratford suggested as
the Commission considers the good cause for the fence there could be some findings to regulate the
height and materials, and suggested there might also be a requirement for a gate in order for the
homeowner to maintain landscaping on the outside of the fence.

Ms. Wilkinson stated she is not opposed to a shorter fence but would like it to be solid for privacy. She
indicated the plan is to install a gate at each end.

Mr. Matt Gilbert, 5266 Fillmore, there are a variety of fences in this residential community, including 6’
vinyl. He stated he is in favor of the fence as requested.

Commissioner Southwick felt access gates make sense and that safety is a valid consideration for the
proposed fence. He stated due to potential wind damage, the owner may want to consider an off-set
design if the fence is solid materials. He felt the encroachment permit is a good solution to deal with
the existing pipeline. Commissioner Safsten stated if the fence is to be solid wood at 6’ in height it
would create a separation for the residential area and asked if the 6’ wood as recommended is required.
He also stated maintenance becomes a concern for many wood fences. Ms. Olson stated the request
is for a 6’ tall wooden fence, but any fence material between 4’ and 6’ is allowed by the code. Mr.
Stratford stated while the Code does not regulate the material, if maintenance of any utility is required, a
wood fence might be preferable to both the homeowner and utility company.

9, Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to allow reuse of residential structures in
   nonresidential zones.

   Mr. Brierley stated the proposed amendment is to consider whether to allow residential uses which
   have lost their right as nonconforming uses by being converted to uses which are permitted. He stated
   the Commission had previously discussed the concept at a work session and the amendment is now
   being considered for adoption. There are many existing homes in commercial and industrial zones
   which were initially nonconforming but have since been converted to a use permitted in the zone,
   thereby losing the nonconforming right as a residence. While this is often desirable, the building retains
   the appearance of a home and if the permitted use is discontinued, the building often remains vacant.
   The amendment was felt appropriate by the Commission as it provides an alternative to allowing these
   buildings to deteriorate and provides needed housing for the community. The amendment would allow
   a home in a nonresidential zone which has been converted to a permitted use to revert back to become
   a single-family dwelling, as well as to allow a vacant or deteriorating home in a nonresidential zone to be
   rehabilitated and reused as a nonconforming single family dwelling. Commission had identified some
   requirements for these if they are reused, including removing signs and eliminate front yard parking so
   the buildings have the appearance of a home. Staff also recommends other standards be added which
   prohibit outdoor storage, or storage of vehicles not owned by the owner or their guests. The Ordinance
   also considers the ability for dual use as both a business and residence, but there are some restrictions
   in the Building Code which would be applicable. Staff recommendation is for approval of the proposed
   Ordinance.



                                                    [14]
   Commissioner Safsten asked about outdoor storage. Mr. Brierley responded if the building were
   converted to a single-family home, no outdoor storage would be allowed. Commissioner Southwick
   agreed outdoor storage should be prohibited but felt garage or interior space could store items. He also
   indicated he had received positive feedback relating to this ordinance from the professional community.

   Mr. Dean Stokes, 4204 N 175 W asked about the ability of these buildings to be rebuilt if destroyed. Mr.
   Brierley stated the rebuild ability would be dependent on the use of the structure at the time it were
   damaged. If the use had been converted back to a home, a new single-family home could be built. If
   the building had the appearance of a home but had been used as a business, the new use would be
   required to comply with the existing zone. Mr. Stratford clarified any rebuild would be to the use of the
   building at the time of its destruction.

   Mr. Damon Boccino, 259 E 950 N asked again about the rebuild question. He stated new buyers need a

   order for their loan to be approved. Mr. Stratford indicated such a letter cannot be written until the
   Ordinance is adopted by the City Council and becomes effective.
