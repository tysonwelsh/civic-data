      Discussion: Phebe Brown Trail recommendations Brad Jensen
      Parks and Recreation Director Rhett Ogden said the City received two
      responses to an RFP for the Phebe Brown Trail. He reminded the Council that
      $350,000 was budgeted for the project three years ago, and was still available
      in the CIP Fund. Parks and Recreation Project Manager Brad Jensen presented
      the two options, one of which would involve a fence. Councilmembers said it
      was their understanding fences were not allowed within the easement. Mr.
      Jensen said the canal company would be okay with a fence along the canal.
      Councilmember T. Lowery said she believed neighbors would fight about a
      fence.

      Mr. Jensen explained that three of the thirteen neighboring properties had no
      encroachments. Four of the neighboring properties had grass that caused
      minimal encroachment. Existing fences would need to be removed on two of

6.b   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1630 amending the official Zoning Map of
      Draper City for approximately 1.28 acres of property from the C-2
      (Neighborhood Commercial) to the CG (General Commercial), located at
      approximately 2021 East Village Green Circle, otherwise known as the
      Suncrest Reception Center Zoning Map Amendment
      Community Development Director Jennifer Jastremsky explained the request
      to rezone the subject properties from C-2 to CG Zone. The C-2 Zone was an
      older zone from the 1999 City Code, and could not be amended, since
      amendments could only be made to the current City Code. She said the City
      had received several public comments via email.

      Ms. Jastremsky said a reception center was not a listed use within the 1999
      City Code. She showed use charts for the CG Zone and the CN Zone
      (Neighborhood Commercial Zone in the current Code). A reception center was
      not permitted in the CN Zone but was permitted in the CG Zone. The
      establishment of a CG Zone required a minimum area of one acre. The three
      subject properties together totaled 1.2 acres.

      Ms. Jastremsky listed the following uses allowed in CG, but not C-2: auto, truck,
      RV, and equipment storage; office warehouse; wholesale and warehousing
      limited; general daycare; reception center; tattoo establishment; and
      manufacturing, limited (some specific uses allowed in C-2). She said the



                                                                             4|Page
Planning Commission reviewed the request on December 12, 2024, and
forwarded a positive recommendation with a 4-1 vote.

Responding to a question from Councilmember F. Lowry, Ms. Jastremsky said
the 1999 Code included a requirement that all uses in Commercial/Industrial
Zones within 100 feet of a residential dwelling in a Residential Zone required
conditional use permit approval, but said the current Code did not include
such a buffer requirement.

Adam Koch, the applicant, said the C-2 Zone would only allow a temporary
trailer incidental to construction work on the property. Mr. Koch said the
design of the two buildings on the properties did not lend themselves to any
of the permitted uses within C-2. He said the building on his property was
labeled as office use but was specifically built for large gatherings. He said he
believed the building was initially meant to be turned over to the HOA as office
and gathering space, and said he did not think it was originally meant to be
owned by a private owner.

Mr. Koch said concerns expressed by residents centered around parking, and
possible uses for the property in the future with a zone change. He said he
owned approximately 48 parking spaces on his property, compared to 13
parking spaces owned by the HOA on neighboring property. Mr. Koch said
there was a lot of overlap with parking issues in the summer when the HOA
pool was open. He said he served on the HOA Board, and the parking issue
had been discussed. Mr. Koch expressed the opinion that it was time for
SunCrest to provide for its own parking needs and not rely entirely on what he
owned. He suggested it was not fair for the HOA to use the parking on his
property, yet try to cut him out of being able to use the parking on his property
for his purposes. Mr. Koch emphasized his building averaged three wedding
receptions per month, typically only in the evenings.

Mr. Koch said SunCrest was a unique area, and said SunCrest would not be a
good location for any business reliant on foot traffic and customers passing
the building. He expressed the opinion not many people would drive up the
hill to have their pet groomed or their nails done. Mr. Koch said a wedding
reception was a unique situation with people motivated to attend, and said he
believed the location would be difficult for a retail business. He stated all
wedding receptions would be confined to within the building moving forward,
so noise would not be a problem. Mr. Koch said the HOA Board had plans in

place to solve the parking issue. Responding to a comment from
Councilmember T. Lowery, Mr. Koch said his facility was smaller than Siempre,
with a maximum occupancy of around 81 people.

the current zoning.

6.c   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1631 approving the Hidden Canyon Estates -
      Mercer Mountain Estates Development Agreement 10th Amendment for
      approximately 25.9 acres of property located at approximately 2635 East
      15040 South within Draper City
      Community Development Director Jennifer Jastremsky outlined the history of
      the Mercer Mountain Estates Development Agreement, and explained the
      request to amend the Development Agreement to:
         • allow additional fill in a specific area, reduce roadway width from 56 feet
            to 50 feet;
         • update roadway location and grade in some areas;
         • make minor adjustments to the area of the land swap approved with
            the 8th Amendment;
         • set requirements for temporary fencing during construction;
         • restrict discharge rates and require fencing for stormwater
            infrastructure;
         • set water system and water quality responsibilities; and
         • approve City slope easements for cuts and fills along the proposed
            roadway (3.23 acres).

      Ms. Jastremsky said the Planning Commission reviewed the request and
      forwarded a positive recommendation. She said she recommended requiring
      sidewalks on both sides of the reduced road width and eliminating the park
      strip, instead of eliminating sidewalks on one side of the road as proposed.
      Ms. Jastremsky said engineers had not yet evaluated the effect on the grading
      of the sidewalk on both sides.

      Pratt Dimond, the applicant, emphasized the proposal would allow space for
      snow removal, and said the area would be planted with native vegetation and
      pine trees. Responding to a question from Councilmember T. Lowery
      regarding the safety of streets with sidewalks on both sides versus only one



                                                                           13 | P a g e
side, Mr. Dimond said the street would be within a private, gated community,
and said he doubted many individuals would walk in and out.
