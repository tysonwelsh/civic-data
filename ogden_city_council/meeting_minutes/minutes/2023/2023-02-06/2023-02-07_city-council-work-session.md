Additional Discussion — Ogden School District Property
Council Deputy Director Symes explained on December 13, 2022, the Ogden City Council passed a resolution declaring
the City’s intent to purchase four properties deemed surplus by Ogden School District. Per state law (§53G-4- 902), once the
Council passes the resolution declaring intent to purchase, the City has 90 days to deliver to the school district an earnest money
offer to purchase the surplus property at the purchase price. The initial proposal from the City’s Administration was to purchase | }
three of the four properties for the purpose of developing housing. However, the Council felt that further discussion should be had
in order to explore all options for the future use of the property. If it is determined that not all properties should be purchased and
the City does not deliver an offer to purchase the property, the City forfeits its exclusive right to purchase the property and the
property will be sold by the district on the public real estate market.

Ogden City Flag Project
Council Policy Analyst Watkins reported that at Local Officials Day in January of 2022, the Ogden Youth City Council
(YCC) participated in an activity related to the design and selection of a new flag for the state of Utah. This led to the idea of
adopting a new flag for Ogden as it is the only city out of the 10 largest in the state that does not have a flag. Rather than a flag
that represents Ogden City as a municipality, the purpose of the project is to select a design that is a direct representative of the
Ogden community. In February of 2022, Ogden City Council staff met with members of the Administration to discuss the viability
of designing a flag for the City. Both the Ogden City Council and the Administration expressed support for the project. The
-_ purpose of tonight’s agenda item is for the Council to review and discuss next steps regarding the 10 flag design recommendations
| as provided by Ogden Flag Design Review Committee (DRC). Mr. Watkins then introduced the members of the Youth Council
present, after which he noted there were 81 student submissions, 77 online submissions, and a total of 215 submitted designs. The
Youth Council reviewed all 215 designs using the five principles of good flag design. They forwarded 105 designs to be
considered by the DRC. The DRC has representation from Ogden City, the Ogden Downtown Alliance, Visit Ogden, and Ogden
business owners. The initial review was anonymized, and the field was narrowed from 105 to 32; in the secondreview, each DRC
member was given five votes and the top 10 designs were taken for final consideration. Each DRC member provided their top 10
designs in order. He then presented each of the 10 design options and provided an explanation of the design, colors used, and
symbolism/imagery representations. He engaged in discussion with the Council to solicit their feedback, support, or opposition for
the flag designs; the next step is for Council members to rank their choices and narrow the field to three designs, conduct a public
poll to receive feedback on the final three, and select a design to be designated as the official flag of Ogden City. The Council )
discussed whether there may be an opportunity to hear from the individuals that submitted the flag designs, perhaps once the field
is narrowed to three. Mr. Watkins stated that each designer was able to provide a written description of their flag design. The
Council concluded their discussion by agreeing to rank the 10 designs and by also thanking the Youth Council for their
undertaking of this valuable community project.
Proposed General Plan Amendment — Moderate Income Housing
Implementation Plan
Planning Manager Brierley reported the Planning Division has been working to meet the requirements of Utah state law
with regard to a moderate-income housing plan. The requirements, commonly referred to as requirements of HB 462, require each
City to develop and submit a moderate-income housing plan and update the housing section of the City’s General Plan with
specifics regarding how the City will address moderate income housing. Ogden City has been in compliance with the requirements
of HB 462 in preparing and submitting a moderate-income housing report for the last several years; however, the City did not
meet the October 2022 deadline for updating the General Plan. This proposal would bring the City into compliance with that
requirement. He used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to define terms relating to the subject matter. Moderate income housing
is defined as 80 percent of the County’s area median income (AMD; Weber County’s AMI is $74,345 and 80 percent of that
number is $59,476. Cost burdened is when a household spends more than 30 percent of their gross income on housing. He
})_ presented a formula indicating that $59,476 divided by 12 is $4,956.33 per month; 30 percent of that number is $1,487. Moderate
_/ — income households can afford a $277,000 home, based on a 30-year mortgage with a five percent interest rate. He provided an
overview of the requirements of HB 462 (formerly Senate Bill (SB) 34); cities with a fixed guideway transit station are required to
adopt four strategies. City Administration recommends the adoption of a number of strategies to ensure the City’s compliance
with the legislation and the Council’s role in this process is to adopt a General Plan amendment with the proposed changes to the
Housing Element regarding Moderate-Income Housing with its strategies and implementation plan.
Brief discussion among the Council and staff centered on the amount of moderate- or low-income housing that the City
currently has and the broader plans for comprehensive updates to the General Plan.
Rezone — 1123-1127 Wall Avenue
Planning Manager Brierley explained Cassandra and Victor Galindo has submitted a petition to rezone property at 1123-
1127 Wall Avenue from C-2 to C-3, which would allow a tire sales and service use; the Galindo’s own and operate various
Victor’s Tires locations in the region. He noted the Galindo’s had purchased this property with the hopes of developing a tire
store, which would serve as a second business location in Ogden City. After applying for a business license, they discovered that
the property is located within a C-2 zone which only allows tire stores if they are located on a ten-acre or larger shopping center
property. Since the property is only 0.66-acres, the petitioners are requesting a rezoning to C-3 which does not have a minimum
lot area requirement for tire stores. The property is located within the Lynn Community and adjacent to the 12th Street Corridor.
The adopted plans for these areas do not provide any specific zoning direction for this property but do support C-3 zoning to the
immediate north and south. The properties to the south are already zoned C-3 which would provide continuity with the zone
boundaries if the rezone were to be approved. He concluded the Planning Commission considered the proposal and forwarded a
recommendation of approval to the City Council. The motion was based on the findings that the request:
e Will provide a definite edge to development and buffering between types of uses.
e Will reflect the prevailing character of the overall district.
¢ Will not cut across lots or developments.
e And is supported in the Lynn Community Plan.
