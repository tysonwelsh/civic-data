Planner Caus shared the background. At the last meeting for this item, Staff found that the 
application for the stealth cellular tower met the requirements for the Code. The Planning 
Commission did want to explore the aesthetics further, as well as explore screening the 
equipment entirely and having a better visual idea of what the actual tower will look like in 
the proposed space. The applicant is proposing Trex-style screening or a composite fence 
in a dark brown to screen the equipment entirely. The applicant has provided some visual 
analyses. There have not been any changes relating to Code requirements. Views were 
shown from Lower River Road per the Code. Anything within one-half mile of the site is not 
considered ridgeline because of the close proximity. The height does not apply to utilities 
and towers specifically. Views were shown from the four major surrounding roads to the 
North, South, East and West of the site.  
Commissioner Sargent reminded the public that there was already an extensive meeting 
about this same application, with a lot of detail presented and a lot of information 
discussed. Staff and the applicant were directed to look at four additional points of 
analyses: fully screening the ground equipment, explore the possibility of multiple 
monopines to look like a cluster of trees, show better visual analyses of how the monopole 
will look from various vantage points, and explore other technologies available to lower the 
height of the tower.

the information that was requested. Ms. Walker was presented with public comment and 
asked to respond, which goes beyond the four points that the Planning Commission asked 
for. The site is a permitted use with a Conditional Use Permit within the AG-10 zone. The

Mr. Hunsaker discussed the coverage maps. The coverage map without the proposed site 
was shown compared to the signal level with the proposed site. There is currently no 
coverage along the river bottoms. With the proposed site there would be coverage in 
Francis, Woodland and the river bottoms, while improving coverage westbound towards 
Jordanelle. A sixty-foot tower would not give the coverage needed and would leave 
coverage gaps that the proposed 104-foot tower would cover. There are no existing cellular 
towers in the area available to co-locate to. The nearest tower is in Heber. Twenty-five 
percent of the population within Utah and Idaho are T Mobile customers. 4G and 5G 
technology will be used with this new tower, bringing in broadband internet and television 
as well as other technology capabilities, which will allow better services with reduced 
costs.

Mr. Heine addressed the question of using different technology or micro sites for this area, 
with smaller equipment throughout the valley. The applicants overall design criteria are for 
macro sites. The small cells that are used to offload capacity when a macro site is maxed 
out. A small cell would not be considered from a business or design perspective. T Mobile 
does not use omni antennas. Omni antennas were used in the days of mobilized radio and 
push to talk. Panel antennas help amplify the pressure coming in to gain a greater distance 
than an omni antenna and have greater control over where the signal will go. Analysis of 
height was done to get the most coverage from the site. Typical distance for a tower is three 
to four miles, which the proposed tower would be within. There are some terrain 
challenges to get coverage towards Jordanelle, which necessitates the 100-foot height. 
There is no technology which will cover the same area at a lower height.  
Commissioner Sonntag asked why the proposed location was not at a higher location 
closer to Jordanelle. Mr. Heine responded that the area was not within the search ring and 
the optimal area for service. It is not just about location, but about viability, power and 
accessibility. Commissioner Sonntag stated the proposed area is just better for the 
applicant’s business plan. Ms. Walker stated there are several things that come into play 
with choosing a tower site: willing property owners, appropriate zoning, and access. All of 
those things have to come together. 
Commissioner Wilde stated that lower areas of the tower will be leased to other carriers 
and asked why T Mobile could not run at a lower height if the other carriers can. Mr. 
Hunsaker answered that the benefit of building your own tower is to get the top spot to 
get better coverage. 104 feet is the optimal height to get the needed coverage. The other

Commissioner Sonntag asked Counsel if there is contention that environmental concerns 
cannot be a criterion in the Planning Commission’s decision making and if that negates 
criterion one in the Conditional Use Permit consideration. Attorney Strachan answered 
that under the FTA (Federal Telecommunications Act) health considerations cannot be 
used as a basis for denial. As mitigation it must be tied to the landscape and environmental 
impacts but cannot be tied to the health impacts.
