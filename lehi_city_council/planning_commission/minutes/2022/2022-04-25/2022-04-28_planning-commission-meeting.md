2. Regular Agenda

2.1) Public hearing and consideration of Melvin Radmall’s request for conditional use
approval of an 8’ decorative art wall located at 1818 North Nuttall Drive.
Brittney Harris presented information on the agenda item.

Brittney clarified there needed to be justification for allowing 8 foot is a conditional use; must be
approved unless reasonable and specific conditions that would otherwise prohibit the use.

Code Enforcement Officers had received complaints regarding the art wall. Brittney Harris stated
she did not have all the details from the complaint. Brittney also said public hearing notice
requirements were met for the neighbors.

The applicant, Melvin Radmall, stated the reason for the height of the fence was because it was
very contemporary. He has had the art piece that he would like to hang on the fence for a long time
and this type of fence is appropriate to hang on the fence.

Commissioner Nielsen opened the public hearing.

Grace Flucky was present and wanted to say she loves the fence and thinks it is amazing and
beautiful and that it is a work of art.

Commissioner Nielsen acknowledged the online comments for the item that were against the wall
that were based on concerns for safety and distraction for drivers.

Commissioner Nielsen closed the public hearing.

Melvin Radmall wanted to show pictures that the house comes out further than the wall. He also
said the art pieces are screwed down tightly to the wall now, so they are safely attached.

Commissioner Eyre asked about installing a wall to block lighting. Mr. Radmall stated initially the
wall was installed to block the light pollution from the wall. Commissioner Nielsen asked about
codes and ordinances from the city that addresses residential areas for light pollution.
Commissioner Eyre said the light was a nuisance and similar things have been granted in the past.
Commissioner Nielsen agreed but wanted to clarify that the sight triangle was not blocked.
Commissioner Jackson wanted to clarify whether or not a precedence would be set moving
forward. Craig Chambers stated it could be a permitted use rather than a conditional use.

        Motion: Commissioner Jackson moved to approve Melvin Radmall’s request for
        conditional use approval of an 8’ decorative art wall located at 1818 North Nuttall
        Drive; with the finding that the wall is sufficiently set back at safe distance, and further
        that defined as an art wall that there is a finding that it is prohibiting light pollution.
        Including all other findings stated in the report and DRC comments. Commissioner Newall
        seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett,
        yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Newall, yes;
        Commissioner Britton, yes. The motion passed unanimously.

2.3) Public hearing and recommendation of Kevin & Sarah Gordon’s request for review of
a General Plan amendment on 1.63-acres located at 870 North 2300 West, changing the land
use designation from VLDRA (very low density residential agriculture) to Neighborhood
Commercial.
2.4) Public hearing and consideration of Kevin & Sarah Gordon’s request for approval of
the Lehi Childcare Concept located at 870 North 2300 West.

Items. 2.3 and 2.4 were heard simultaneously.

Brittney Harris presented information on the agenda item.

Ross Dinsdale stated the old General Plan Amendment had an old Neighborhood Commercial
node overly and is now zoned Neighborhood Commercial and said the use could be a good fit with
low traffic and have a low impact on the neighborhood that would fit on the small parcel. The
Neighbors’ concerns are usually traffic, but 2300 West will carry a large volume of traffic.
Commissioner Carlson had concerns about approving 2.3 and moving right to a concept plan
before a zone change.

The applicant, Kevin and Sarah Gordon, were present. They stated when they bought the property
it was zoned NC. They own the childcare across the street from the rodeo grounds and want to
build a similar concept. A childcare is needed and very much family oriented in Lehi, and they
would like the opportunity to build a childcare. Commissioner Eyre clarified it was never zoned
NC but it was an overlay. They are fine with DRC comments and want to take the neighbors into
consideration. They plan to build a double containment and do not want to infringe on the
neighbors and plan on building a nice establishment.

Commissioner Nielsen opened the public hearing for 2.3

Jennalyn Bishop stated the problem she has is the NC was never in the plans and the overlay was
determined in 2019. She believes the daycare will have a high impact on her neighborhood as a
property owner and is highly concerned with the light pollution from the building. She is also
concerned about trucks and garbage's. It will affect her property and is concerned that it will highly
affect her property negatively with spot zoning. She said there are many people who will be
affected who should have their voices heard.

Angie Lethbridge stated she wanted to state her concern the Ivory Homes development is recent
and has been approved as a small singular piece and could appear the City is trying to fix something
they didn't mean to approve. Angie also stated not every City follows the protocol the same way
that Lehi City does with meetings and public hearings.

Grace Flucky stated she does not have a problem with daycare, but she does have a precedence
with the approval. She stated the traffic will affect so many people and wanted to know where and
when will this stop.

Jen (inaudible) stated she loves kids but stated the daycare will increase traffic and will greatly
affect this area. If more vehicles are brought in, traffic and schools will be affected. She looked up
the number of day cares within a 2 mile radius and didn’t see the need for another day care. There
is no commercial beside 2300 West and Main and the 7-11.

Larry Bulshrider wanted to reiterate what Jen Lynn said about the people who can't be here because
the development has not been built. The other concern is the comment of the expansion of the
road. His concern is that it is a bottleneck and if speeds would be increased. He is also concerned
about what the pickup and drop-off would do to traffic.

Bryce Sorenson stated he just found out about the item this afternoon and already experiences light
pollution. He doesn't believe the process would be allowed or heard in other cities without
rezoning, with the component that it will affect quality of life in the neighbors.

Commissioner Nielsen acknowledged the online comments from Brent Bishop and Holly Lewis,
and they were both opposed to the item.

Commissioner Nielsen closed public hearing for 2.3

Commissioner Nielsen opened the public hearing for 2.4

Jennalyn Bishop stated the concept plan should not have been on the agenda and should have an
amendment withe GPA. She stated this is not in the GPA and is opposed to the item.

Mrs. Flucky did want to say she agreed with Mrs. Bishop, and that there wasn't a reason to address
2.4

Commissioner Nielsen acknowledged Brent Bishop and Holly Lewis submitted online comments
that were opposed to the item.

Commissioner Nielsen closed public hearing for 2.4

The applicant stated they were asked by the Planning Department to submit a concept plan. The
day care will not have lighting at night, and they understand the concerns and want to build
something that fits with the neighborhood. The applicant stated there are only two childcare centers
near that area, which are full, and there is a massive lack of childcare. There childcare is not like
Ascent Academy and will not cause the same issues.

Ross Dinsdale wanted to clarify comments he made. The design for 2300 West is slated to start at
the beginning of next year as a five lane road. There are about 12,000 cars per day on 2300 West
as of February 2022. The City has restricted allowances and uses, and lighting and traffic can be
mitigated. He acknowledged that traffic will be impacted, but at a very minimal basis. A road can't
be stubbed to property and there is not enough road for a public street. A flag lot can't be installed
in a new development.

Commissioner Carlson made heavy suggestions of denial.

        Item 2.3 Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to send a negative recommendation Kevin
        & Sarah Gordon’s request for review of a General Plan amendment on 1.63-acres
        located at 870 North 2300 West, changing the land use designation from VLDRA
        (very low density residential agriculture) to Neighborhood Commercial; with the
        findings that the effect of the proposed amendment on the character of the surrounding
        area is not consistent with the surrounding properties, is not consistent with the goals and
        policies of the General Plan or the proposed zone with the General Plan land uses of
        nearby and adjoining properties, that the suitability of the properties for the uses
        requested and their suitability for the existing uses identified by the General Plan is not
        met, change in the uses allowed for the affected properties will unduly affect the uses, or
        proposed uses for nearby and adjoining properties, the gain to the public health, safety,
        and welfare from the existing classification to the proposed amendment will be
        negatively affected. Including all DRC comments. Commissioner Bunker seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett,
        no; Commissioner Bunker, no; Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Newall, yes;
        Commissioner Britton, no. The motion passed 4 – 3.

        Item 2.4 Motion: Commissioner Everett moved to deny Kevin & Sarah Gordon’s
        request for approval of the Lehi Childcare Concept located at 870 North 2300
        West.; with the findings that the proposed concept is not consistent with the Lehi City
        Development Code, and the proposed concept does not conform to the goals and policies
        of the General Plan. Including all DRC comments. Commissioner Jackson seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett,
        no; Commissioner Bunker, no; Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Newall, yes;
        Commissioner Britton, no. The motion passed unanimously.

2.8) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for preliminary subdivision
review of Family Park Estates, a 9-lot development located at approximately 600 East and
Airport Drive in an R-1-15 (residential) zone.
Brittney Harris presented information on the agenda item. The City Engineer granted a city
deviation for a 7 foot planter strip due to the slope and elevation of the lots.

Commissioner Everett asked if the proposal originally had less lots than what is now being
proposed. Commissioner Carlson and Lorin Powell, City Engineer, engaged in conversation where
Commissioner Carlson feels it appears as a conflict of interest with the City approving and
developing the request. Commissioner Carlson also asked about changing code for park strips and
the setback to the front of the house and would recommend reconsidering that decision.
Commissioner Carlson is concerned with the City being the applicant, developer and receiving
revenue. Lorin Powell stated the intent was to develop the subdivision to help build and pay for
Family Park. Craig Chambers, Lehi City Assistant Attorney, stated this is the Planning
Commissions opportunity to act as the hearing body to hear the pros and cons of the request.
Brittney clarified questions from Commissioners and stated the homes and lot sizes still meet code
requirements. Commissioner Nielsen stated the process should’ve been sold off to a private

developer and not to the City. He is concerned about park strips being a safety issue because of
the limited buffer between cars and pedestrians.

Commissioner Nielsen opened the public hearing.

LaNae Stout, a Lehi resident, said she was under the impression the Peck's donated the property
and the City bought the property to build a park, where there was a controversy where the City
couldn’t afford to build the Park. She was under the impression the PARC tax would pay for the
park. She feels like this area is a great secret to this area with a great view of Utah County. Ms.
Stout feels it is short sighted to put houses to block the view. She feels it could be a safety issue to
block the view of the park.

James Slogowsky, a Lehi resident, echoed LaNae's comments. The view is an unintended amenity
at the west end of Airport Drive. If houses are built, the overlook is gone. He would hope the
houses would not be built. He suggested if the houses were built, maybe an intentional overlook
could be built for people to enjoy.

Dave Christofferson, a Lehi resident, stated he has issues with all of it. There is sand where the
houses are being built and there are already complaints of yards eroding. He feels the City knows
it is sand where the City where be building homes. The City would need a wider road to help
maintain the traffic of the park, which would cut into the variance of the lots, and doesn’t know
how it would be a viable road less than 7 feet. He feels that the timing is awful and does not feel
that he agrees with that. He doesn’t know that he agrees with where the money for the park if this
is where it is coming from. He doesn't think it is fair for the city to pick and choose what they
enforce. He doesn’t think the City should be able to pick and choose what works and what doesn’t
just so they can benefit.

Commissioner Nielsen closed public hearing.

Ross Dinsdale stated the city intentionally did not build anything on the north end to maintain the
view and overlook. The road is being widened and will match the width of the existing road. There
is a Geotech company that has been hired to study the area. Kim Struthers wanted to clarify the
City purchase the land from the Pecks, it was not donated, and was a trade as the homes picked up
north of Airport Drive. The concept for Family Park shows a lookout and wanted to preserve a
lookout. Ross Dinsdale stated there were RDA agencies and isn’t out of the norm to be a developer.
The PARC tax is committed and will cover other items that have been underfunded for years. He
stated the other factor is the Mayor said he would find creative ways to fund parks, which is what
he did, and this is a way to pay back the purchase of the land. Lorin Powell said there was about
1.2 million to buy the property and with maintenance and improvements there will be very little,
if any funding, left over.

The Commissioners discussed the pros and cons of the development and stated their
recommendations through the meeting.

                Commissioner Eyre motioned to extend the meeting until all items were heard.
                Commissioner Everett seconded. All in favor.


        Motion: Commissioner Carlson moved to forward a negative recommendation to the City
        Council of Lehi City’s request for preliminary subdivision review of Family Park
        Estates, a 9-lot development located at approximately 600 East and Airport Drive in
        an R-1-15 (residential) zone; with the proposed findings that the subdivision meets the
        requirements of the r-1-15 zone, however a Geotech report has not been provided, the
        proposed zone does not meet requirements of Lehi City code without exception to the park
        strips and do not recommend granting that exception, other findings include that the
        overlook portion is an important element of the park and may interfere with that amenity.
        Including all DRC comments. Commissioner Eyre seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett,
        yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes; Commissioner Newall, yes;
        Commissioner Carlson, yes. The motion passed unanimously.
