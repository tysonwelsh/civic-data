GENERAL SESSION

Chair Snow called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

PUBLIC HEARINGS

     1. Grant Wise requests a Conditional Use Permit approval for the Lotus Ember
        Multifamily Project located at 195 W 7200 S in the Transit-Oriented Development
        Overlay Zone (TODO).

        Grant Wise requests a Conditional Use Permit approval for a multifamily apartment
        project named Lotus Ember which will be located on an existing lot at 195 W 7200 S and
        consist of a four-story building with two levels of underground parking. The project will
        redevelop the entire site of the existing office use and replace it with 106 apartments and
        appurtenant amenities including a club house, a fitness area, a generous outside deck with
        a pool, seating and barbecue facilities, several pergolas, a dog run, and play space.

        Public notice was sent to property owners within 500 feet of the subject parcel;
        additionally, a sign was placed on the property on 12/01/2022 and will remain until city
        council approval of the development agreement. At the time of this writing no public
        comment has been received.

        Staff recommends approval.

DISCUSSION:

Commissioner Liedtke asks a question about the parking availability for residents of the
development. His question asked whether the residents would have assigned parking or street
parking.

Mr. Knobloch responds that the residents would be assigned parking spaces within the parking
structure. This put all the commissioners at ease as this has been a continuing issue for new
developments throughout the city.

Mr. Wilcox does clarify that this is an administrative approval process and so long as the project
meets all zoning requirements, it must be approved.

PUBLIC HEARINGS

   2. IHC Health Services, Inc. requests Conditional Use Permit approval for an
      expansion located at 7302 S Bingham Jct Blvd in the Bingham Junction Subarea
      3/Junction at Midvale Overlay (BJ-3/JMO) zone.

      The applicant requests conditional use permit approval to allow for an expansion to
      IHC’s existing building located at 7302 Bingham Jct. Blvd in the Bingham Junction
      Subarea 3 and the Junction at Midvale Overlay (BJ-3/JMO) zone. The expansion will be
      approximately 50,300 sq. ft. in total and was contemplated as part of the original
      conditional use permit that was approved in 2011. The expansion will endeavor match the
      existing building in height, materials, and colors.

      This proposal has been reviewed by Planning Staff, the City Engineer, and the Unified
      Fire Authority for compliance with the respective guidelines, policies, standards, and
      codes. Staff finds the proposal complies with requirements outlined in Midvale City
      Municipal Code 17-7- 9.9.

      Public notice has been sent to property owners within 500 feet of the subject parcel. No
      written objections have been received as of the writing of this report.

      Staff recommends approval.

      2. State Street Study Presentation by MHTN.
            a. Ryan Wallace, Architect and Planner for MHTN, introduces himself and
                begins to explain the purpose and process of the study. The State Street
                Corridor improvements were at a halt without this study to begin
                redevelopment. The analysis consists of data from existing conditions, land
                use, transportation, economics, and market analysis. An outline of possibilities
                was created for the future of State Street. Each were researched, and
                recommendations made. The ultimate goals would include better connection,
                safety, and an overall welcoming environment. Art, food, and culture along
                with identity and urban design would be at the center of all decisions. State
                Street needs to be memorable within Midvale’s boundaries.
            b. Ms. Emily with MHTN explains the community engagement and public
                outreach portion of the study. Surveys on Engage Midvale were conducted,
                sidewalk spray paintings, advertisements, and informational booths at
                community events were all used to hear from the public, the developers, and
                the city for local input. The inclusion of local opinion is key to the success of
                redevelopment. Transportation concepts, demonstration sites, and potential
                connectivity updates were all analyzed and the data explaining each was
                presented.
            c. Charles Allen with Parametrics discusses the frameworks for transportation in
                Midvale. Mr. Allen will go on to discuss vehicular components for street
                mobility, active transportation, transit, safety, and the linkage for the preferred
                alternative regarding State Street. However, the study is looking at Midvale in
                its entirety. For example, the canal does not currently have pedestrian access,
                but a Canal Trail Study is underway to add to the connectivity that has been
                demonstrated. This also includes bike lanes/paths and sidewalks for pedestrian
                use.
            d. Jennifer Shook with Leeland Consultant Group (LCG) will share the data on
                the economic side of the planning study. State Street will need Zoning Code
