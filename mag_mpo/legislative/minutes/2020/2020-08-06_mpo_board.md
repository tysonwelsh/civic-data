Public Comment None. 2021 Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) Final Adoption – Bob Allen, MAG The TIP is prepared and approved every year. MAG selects and funds projects every two years. MAG is currently selecting projects that will be amended into the TIP in October. The TIP lists transportation projects and programs within the MPO for the fiscal years 2021–2024. Conceptual projects are listed through 2025. Municipalities, Utah County, UDOT, UTA, and others are eligible for MPO funds. All regional projects, regardless of the selection agency, must be approved by the MPO committees and be listed in the TIP. A 30-day public comment period July 6, 2020 and will close August 6, 2020. No significant comments were received. Staff recommends that the 2020 TIP be approved as presented. Mayor Child moved to adopt the 2021 TIP as presented. Mayor Johnson seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. TIP Project Selection Process – 2020 Project Ranking List Approval – Bob Allen, MAG MAG selects projects every two years to be funded using certain Federal State and County funds. There are approximately $85m in funds available. Most of those funds are available in 2023 and 2024. Projects are scored and ranked through an adopted selection process. A virtual tour of each project and TAC scoring was completed. Combined with MAG staff scores the projects are now in a ranked list. RPC will now have until September to review the list. MPO Regional Planning Committee August 6, 2020 Page 3 TAC has recommended that the list to be adopted by RPC in September. Funding and final adoption to the TIP will occur in October. TIP Funding Request - Utah Lakeshore Trail – Bob Allen, MAG The TIP funds regional projects within the MPO area. There are two mechanisms for funding project cost overruns, administrative changes and board approved changes. MAG reserves a contingency account that staff can approve funds for overruns that are under $500,000 and are within 10% of the total approved project cost. All other amounts must be approved by the MPO RPC. The city of Saratoga Springs is the sponsor of the Utah Lakeshore Trail; Hotpots to Amanda Lane trail project. They are requesting an additional $350,000 to cover cost overages due to changes required by the Army Corp of Engineers and to higher than expected bids. This would put the total cost of the project at $1,646,000. The project sponsor requested in March of 2019 an additional $300,000 for anticipated overruns. MPO RPC approved this additional funding. TAC and Staff recommend approval of the additional $350,000 in TAP-MAG funds due to unforeseen requirements by the Army Corp of Engineers. There is a balance in the account for this amount. Mayor Mann moved that $350,000 TAP-MAG funding be added to the Utah Lakeshore Trail – Hotspots to Amanda Land. Mayor Christensen seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Provo River Parkway Trail – Gap Project – Bob Allen, MAG MAG, UDOT, Utah County and Wasatch County are partnering to design and construct the final trail segment that will extend the existing Provo River Parkway Trail from Vivian Park to Deer Creek Reservoir. This three-mile trail segment is the missing link to connect more than 100 miles of existing trail systems in Utah and Wasatch counties. It also completes a critical piece of the Wasatch Loop, a connected system of family-friendly trails encompassing the Wasatch Front and Wasatch Back, home to 80% of Utahns. The partners have been working on this project for at least 20 years but had been unable to secure the necessary ROW and funds, until now. With the cooperation of the Heber Valley Historic Railroad, a viable route has been finalized that will share parts of the railroad ROW. MPO Regional Planning Committee August 6, 2020 Page 4 Four years ago, an initial investment was secured, with Utah County’s $1.5M and UDOT’s $2.9M contributions towards the of the total $14.97M. With critical legislative help from Senator Hemmert of Orem, the state agreed to appropriate $6.1M from the Transportation Investment Fund if MAG would provide the remaining $4.5M in non-state funds. NOTE: The Utah Transportation Commission received 189 distinct comments on the project during its considerations, of which 187 were in favor. Completing this segment will: • Enhance safe recreation access to the river and reduce pedestrian use of the railbed. • Construct a clear span railroad and pedestrian bridge, replacing the old trestle with five piers that pose a hazard to river users. • Provide an alternate route for cyclists in Provo Canyon and enhance safety. • Improve recreation opportunities in Provo Canyon • Reduce the need for vehicular access to Provo Canyon. • Provide a tourism and visitor attraction for both counties. Utah County is requesting approval for the remaining $4.5M in MAG apportioned sales tax funds. Due to the inter-regional nature of the project, the broad partnerships at both state and local levels, and benefits to Provo Canyon, Staff recommends that RPC approve this request. TAC unanimously recommended that this project be approved for funding. Mayor Acerson moved that $4.5M be added to the Provo Canyon Parkway Trail. Mayor Child seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Federal Performance Measures – UDOT Target Adjustments – Bob Allen, MAG The FAST Act required all States and MPOs to set targets for and track certain transportation performance measures. In 2018, UDOT set and MAG adopted, targets for safety, infrastructure, and mobility. After two years, UDOT would like to make minor adjustments to the previously set targets. MAG must adopt those new targets or determine its own targets and how it will achieve those them. TAC and Staff recommends that the MPO adopt the adjusted statewide performance measure targets. MPO Regional Planning Committee August 6, 2020 Page 5 Mayor Rees moved that RPC adopt the adjusted statewide targets for the required federal performance measures. Mayor Brunst seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. TranPlan50 Amendment 02-20 – Shawn Eliot, MAG Every four years, MAG is required to prepare and adopt a Regional Transportation Plan. The current plan was approved in June 2019. An amendment process allows for changes to be made. Amendment 02-20 proposes to remove ambiguous lines in the plan that stated, “Location to be Determined”. UDOT has concerns that these descriptions are not defensible in the environmental process. Though shown as “TBA” in the main TransPlan50 document and map, all these line types were modeled as a project with an exact location spelled out in the technical documentation appendices in the plan. UDOT has proposed changes to two of these projects which are of regional significance for air quality and require a new conformity determination, because their locations are being adjusted from the approved conforming RTP transportation model run. The new model run shows no increases of over 5% for any pollutant. MAG is well within its emissions budgets and can make a positive Air Quality Conformity Determination. A 30-day public comment period on the conformity determination was conducted and no comments were received that would alter the current amendment. Proposed changes: #32: I-15 Improvements: Changes “Location to be Determined” status to I-15 with an interchange at Provo 820 N for environmental purposes. Future study will determine location. #62: I-15 Alternatives Study: The I-15 corridor needs improvements before 2040. The study will identify the future needs of the corridor. This was originally listed in the plan as I- 15/Alternatives and modeled as adding a collector/distributor project to I-15. #71: North Lakeshore FWY: Changes “Location to be Determined” status to Pioneer Crossing for environmental purposes. Future study will determine location. #96: Draper Gravel Pit RD: Remove from plan, Draper is not planning for the corridor. #103: Utah Lake Crossing: Changes “Location to be Determined” status for the Utah Lake Bridge Crossing. Original modeled to join I-15 at 2000 S Orem, now joins I 15 at Provo Center ST #107: Nebo Belt RD: Changes “Location to be Determined” status to an identifiable line for environmental work purposes. Future study will determine location. MPO Regional Planning Committee August 6, 2020 Page 6 #119/125: I-15 Parallel Corridor: The South Wasatch Corridor is renamed I-15 Parallel Corridor and is extended to Lehi as a parallel highway to I-15. It is proposed as a solution to space out traffic between the two corridors. The southern section (south of University PKWY) is moved to phase 2 of the plan, the northern section is needed in phase 2, funded in phase 3. In the original RTP this was the I-15/Alternatives project and was modeled on I- 15 as a collector/distributer project. #126: I-15 Widening: This project continues the 12-lane configuration currently at Orem Center ST from University PKWY to US 6, Spanish Fork and widens the freeway to 8 lanes between US 6 and Payson 800 South. #T12: Central Light Rail Line: Changes “Location to be Determined” status to an identifiable like for environmental work purposes. Future study will determine location. #T13: South Light Rail Line: Changes “Location to be Determined” status to an identifiable like for environmental work purposes. Future study will determine location. The proposed amendments allow UDOT to better conduct environmental projects and conform to air quality conformity requirements. MPO Staff and TAC recommends approval of the proposed amendment 02-20 to TransPlan50 and the associated Air Quality Conformity Determination. Mayor Johnson moved that MPO RPC approve the TransPlan50 amendment #02-20 and the associated Air Quality Conformity Determination to allow for UDOT to better conduct environmental projects as recommended by MPO TAC.
