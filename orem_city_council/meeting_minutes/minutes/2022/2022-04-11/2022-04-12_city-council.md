DISCUSSION — Neighborhood Improvement Team

The Neighborhood Improvement Team introduced themselves and explained the history of code
enforcement in Orem. Ms. Mathews stated that around 20 years ago, there was a unit within the

police department which was set up to deal with basic nuisance complaints. Then in 2021, that
unit was dissolved and reorganized into the current Neighborhood Improvement Team. (NIT)

The current team consists of one police officer and three non-police full time employees.

Mr. Andreason explained why code enforcement is needed in Orem and discussed common
examples of code violations the team deals with. These mainly included junk-filled yards,

homeless encampments, and graffiti.

Mr. Ledoux presented the council with other examples of nuisances the team works with. These
included various code violations such as weeds/landscaping, junk, occupancy violations, and
unregistered vehicles. He stated junk accumulation and parking violations are the two most
common nuisances they encounter.

Mr. Earl explained the process of reporting, contacting, and enforcing nuisance violations with
the council. He stated complaints can be received directly or through 311, at which point the
team will inspect the property, leave a door hanger explaining the violation and what the
property owner needs to change to come into compliance, and then continually follow up. Mr.
Earl expressed the team is as compassionate as possible and often gives people who are actively
working on their violation extra time to come into compliance. He also gave the council multiple
examples of times where the NIT team themselves have helped to clean up or otherwise assist
residents who cannot come into compliance on their own.

Mr. Turner described successes the team has had since July 1. These included the team
addressing 687 cases, obtaining compliance in 442 of those cases, and having 70% of cases reach
compliance within 30 days. He then presented the council with various before and after project
photos.

Ms. Mathews concluded the presentation by showing the council other activities the team hosts,
such as picnics with police, neighborhood leader meetings, back to school nights, preservation
team projects, and the property all-star program.

General discussion ensued regarding neighborhood improvement tactics and goals. Mayor

Young suggested the team look into a more extensive education program to inform residents on
this topic, and common nuisances they can avoid.

DISCUSSION — Water Fee Abatement Program

Mr. Downs summarized the Water Fee Abatement Program stating it provides a monthly credit
towards utility billing for low-income families and builds off of an agreement the city has had
with Community Actions since September of 2017. He stated the program that is already in place
can help families receive up to 300 dollars to pay late utility billing. Mr. Downs then went on to
explain how the city wants to offer an additional program where low-income families can receive
an extra ten dollars per month to help pay for their water bill. He then listed the requirements a
family would need to meet to qualify for this program.

General discussion ensued regarding water conservation education and upcoming changes in
water fees.

The City Council expressed their support for this new program and stated the existing program is
very helpful and they can see the extra monthly credit going a long way as well.

DISCUSSION — Pre-Disaster Mitigation Plan

Mr. Stevenson introduced the Pre-Disaster Mitigation Plan and went through disaster
vulnerabilities and at-risk locations in Orem. These included locations vulnerable to wildfires,
mudslides, strong storms, and earthquakes. He then went through various mitigation projects and
strategies Orem could do to prepare for natural disasters. Some of these included relocating
water pipes to avoid fault lines, retrofitting facilities to meet current building code, practicing
yearly emergency drills, rebuilding the City Center to withstand a major earthquake, “fire wise”
education, and installing a water reuse facility at Sleepy Ridge golf course.

General discussion ensued regarding natural disaster risks, focus areas, and a possible emergency
preparedness center to be built within the new Orem City Center.

DISCUSSION — Budget: Capital Improvement Projects

Mr. Kelly presented the council with capital improvement projects the city plans to undertake
during the upcoming fiscal year. Some of these projects include various park, transportation,
facility, water, street, and lighting improvement projects. After going though these main project
fields, Mr. Kelly presented the council with other miscellaneous capital improvement projects
and concluded his presentation by stating all planned projects add up to a total of $31,385,186
dollars.

General discussion ensued regarding various capital improvement projects and the costs
associated with them.

Alan Johnson expressed he would like to see the choker islands on Palisades Drive removed
while maintaining the installed speedbumps. He expressed the choker islands are unsightly and
can be unsafe because they force cyclists into the middle of the road.

Ralph Olsen stated he felt the choker islands installed on Palisades Drive can be dangerous
because they force cyclists into the center of the road and can make certain turns difficult. He
also expressed neighbors were not properly informed on what was being installed, and the city
should focus on better communication in the future.

Sierra Bradley stated she lives in a rental home with three other people, which violates city code
which states no more than three unrelated parties may live in a home at one time. She expressed
herself and the other residents of the property have done everything they can to meet
requirements and continue living together. She asked the council to review these regulations so
they may be able to continue living within Orem. Mr. Davidson responded and stated this
regulation is consistent with state law.

Jim Hadlock stated with reservoirs being well below normal, the city organization, as the largest
user of water within Orem, should look into conserving their water usage. He stated that as of
last year, the other two largest users of water in the city, Alpine School District and the Church
of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints, did not have any set water conservation policy. Mr. Hadlock
then concluded by suggesting one way of saving water is to not cut grass any shorter than three
inches. That way, water would evaporate less easily and thus scheduled watering could be
reduced.

Douglas Vertell stated there is a development being built around 400 West and 1600 South
which neighbors are opposed to. He then informed the council about a neighborhood meeting the
developers of the project are holding and asked council members to attend.

Lori Tolman stated she is from the Springwater Park Neighborhood where multiple homes in the
area have been facing “settling issues.” She expressed those home are beginning to sink and lean,
causing cracks in walls and other noticeable damage that could cost over $110,000 dollars to
repair. Ms. Tolman stated neighbors feel there is a link between the drilling of new wells and
damage to property. She concluded by asking the council to find answers for the many questions
residents in that area have, and to do an investigation into the matter before more damage occurs.

John Barrick expressed his concern of Northridge Elementary declining in quality over the past
few years, and issues with class sizes and building maintenance. Mr. Barrick then thanked the
council for taking on the school district split feasibility study. He concluded his statements by
explaining how he felt paying $30,000 dollars for the feasibility study isn’t too much considering
how Orem pays $55 million dollars in taxes to the Alpine School District.

Cissy Rasmussen asked the council to limit the public comments to Orem residents only. She
then stated how she was at the meeting to share a petition she started which asks the council to
cancel their feasibility study contract with Discovery Education Consultants, and begin to search
for a new company to conduct the study. Ms. Rasmussen expressed that Orem residents deserve
a study done by an unbiased and experienced company. She then stated that in just two days, her
petition has garnered over 1,200 signatures, and explained how she felt this is a strong indication
of how residents feel about the company which was selected to conduct the study.

Mike Stiles stated he is for a feasibility study, but one done by a more experienced company. He
explained how property taxes in Orem could go up 25% for education that is not guaranteed to
be any better than it is now. Mr. Stiles then explained how he felt Discovery Education
Consultants is unfit to conduct the study, and encouraged the council find a new company to
contract with.

Ada Wilson stated she is one of two Orem representatives on the Alpine Board of Education, and
she specifically represents the Mountain View High School area. She expressed how at the
beginning of March, she sent the council an email containing four presentations regarding Alpine
School District reconfiguration, and stated her sole purpose of involvement is to protect Orem
families right to access the best educational opportunities for their children and to provide fair
and accurate information. Ms. Wilson stated she wants to ensure the 780 teachers, 863 staff, and
41 administrators who work in Orem schools will not be hurt by this decision.

Britney Lindsey from Cedar Hills expressed how she feels the Alpine School District suppresses
parent’s voices and locks them out of school board meetings. She stated Alpine School District is
the largest, wealthiest, and most powerful school district in the state, and needs to be broken up.
Ms. Lindsey continued to explain how she is being criminally charged for honking her car horn
outside of a school board meeting which parents were locked out of.

Bart Francis stated he is a teacher in Orem and asked the council not to rush interpreting the
outcome of the feasibility study. He stated he has been a teacher in Orem for 15 years and
expressed how he would like to see the council take the proper time to understand the study
before they act on it.

Stephanie Grant stated the Alpine School District is encouraging and guiding parents to make the
petition against choosing Discovery Education Consultants to host the feasibility split study, and
asked why the school district is fighting so hard against a split study. Ms. Grant stated that
Alpine School District has shut down multiple schools within Orem and feels the district is much
too big to control what is actually being taught in school. She concluded by expressing how she

feels Alpine School District is trying to stall this process as much as possible so it won’t get on
the ballot.

Elizabeth Ludwig stated she is a teacher at Lakeridge Junior High and is not against the
feasibility study, but she does have some concerns. She expressed if Orem split off from the
district, many teachers would leave to stay with the Alpine School District. Mrs. Ludwig then
stated how she feels Discovery Education Consultants are not fit to conduct a feasibility study
and asked the council to contract instead with an unbiased, out of state company.

Kathy Owens stated she is in favor of the study and the team which the council have chosen to
conduct it. She expressed that Discovery Education Consultants have been involved in split
studies regarding Granite, Canyons, and Draper School Districts. Ms. Owens concluded by
expressing how she feels Alpine School District wants to delay the study and split investigation
because they plan to put a $500 million dollar bond on the ballot.

Kara Ludwig voiced her concern about the action in which the council has taken to hire
Discovery Education Consultants to conduct the split feasibility study. She stated she feels it is
clear that if Orem splits from the district, it would reduce educational resources while raising
property taxes. Ms. Ludwig concluded by expressing how she feels Discovery Education
Consultants is inexperienced in conducting split feasibility studies, and the council should look
elsewhere for the service.

Brook Peterson thanked the council for supporting a feasibility study. She expressed how Alpine
School District wants to put a $500 million dollar bond on the ballot this year, and she feels
Orem residents will be taxed for this bond, and then won’t see their fair share of return from it.
Ms. Peterson concluded by expressing how she feels Alpine School District does not want Orem
to split from the district because they would lose lots of tax dollars.

Keith Wilson expressed how he feels the company selected to host the feasibility study could be
bias and inexperienced with conducting fair studies. Mr. Wilson stated a larger district is more
cost effective and expressed that if Orem splits from the Alpine School District, property tax
could increase significantly. Mr. Wilson concluded his remakes by stating the petition against
contracting with Discovery Education Consultants is not district backed, and the $500 million
dollar bond Alpine School District wants to put on the ballet will not raise property taxes.

Aaron Davidson from Lindon expressed his confusion with people who think the school district
split is a done deal if the council goes forward with the study. He stated this study is just to see if
splitting from Alpine School District would be at all possible, and they are not going into it with
a direction already decided. Mr. Davidson then stated that at a recent County Convention, none
of the current Alpine School Board members received an endorsement from the Republican or
Democrat parties, then stated the school board does not treat parents properly.

Kay Clark expressed how she feels it is not the Orem City Council’s job to decide if Orem
should split and form their own school district. She stated the council should be dealing with
things such as beautification, budgeting, safety, and growth. Ms. Clark expressed how property
taxes could raise significantly if Orem decides to split from the district, and many families would
not be able to afford living in the city anymore. She concluded by asking the council how they
would deal with declining student numbers and disabled students.

Scott Swain expressed that many parents would be willing to pay extra property tax to better
educate their children. He then asked the council to be fiscally minded and cut costs where they
can.

Mayor Young stated he feels there is a group of Orem residents who are against anything the
council wants to do. He stated this group was very vocal and opposed the council hiring their
own attorney, changing the Planning Commission structure, changing the State Street Master
Plan, the school feasibility study, and are now opposed to the council’s choice of who is
conducting that study. Mayor Young expressed how he feels the petition against Discovery
Education Consultants is inaccurate and stated the group against the council’s decision are not
dealing in facts. He expressed that it has been claimed property taxes will go up 35 percent,
educational quality will go down, teachers will leave, and the amount of student resources will
go down. Mayor Young then stated that if any of these were true, he would sign the petition, but
he feels none of them have any basis in fact. He stated no one has any idea if splitting from the
Alpine School District would be feasible, and that’s why the council has voted to conduct a
study.

After the Mayor’s comments, Mr. Spencer went through a list of people from Discovery
Education Consultants, and expressed why the council felt they were qualified to conduct the
feasibility study.

Mayor Young then stated that by signing the petition against Discovery Education Consultants
conducting the split feasibility study, they are saying these individuals are not qualified and
should be disqualified immediately. Mayor Young then asked why Alpine School District is
trying so hard to stop the study. He concluded by stating once the study is complete, the council
will examine the results and see if there is something worth voting on. Mayor Young assured
residents the council is focused on representing the people that elected them and will do what is
best for the tax payers.

PUBLIC HEARING — The City will receive public comment on the Community
Development Block Grant (CDBG) Project Use of Funds for Fiscal Year 2022-2023

Mr. Ransom and Ms. Mathews introduced the presentation and stated the City of Orem will be
receiving approximately $684,750.00 in 2022-2023 CDBG funding plus program income
revenues of $24,808.16. Unspent funding from previous years may also be available for
reallocation during FY 2022-2023, and is estimated at $65,645.95. The total amount for
allocation in FY 2022-2023 is estimated to be approximately $775,214.05 although this amount
is subject to change based on HUD allocation amount and unspent funding from previous

years. The CDBG Advisory Commission recommends that the Orem City Council allocates the
2022-2023 CDBG funding allocation for the following projects: City of Orem Victim Services
$30,000, City of Orem Homeless Services $4,237.50, City of Orem Code Enforcement
$115,000, Section 8 Loan Payment $94,000, City of Orem CDBG Administration $136,950, City
of Orem Neighborhood Preservation $5,000, Orem Senior Friendship Center Roof Replacement
$196,733.45, City of Orem Critical Home Repair and Home Rehabilitation Program $100,000,
Mountainlands Community Health Center $19,745, Utah Valley Family Support $26,580, Kids
on the Move $8,860, and Community Health Connect $13,290.

General discussion ensued regarding the funding vetting process and possible CDBG funding
projects.
