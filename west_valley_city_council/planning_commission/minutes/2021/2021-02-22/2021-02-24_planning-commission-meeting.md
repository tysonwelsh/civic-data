ZT-1-2021
West Valley City
5718 W 3500 S
A zone text change to amend residential landscaping and recreation space standards in
Titles 7 and 9.

At the request of the City Council, City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to the
residential landscaping and recreation space standards in Titles 7 (Zoning Ordinance) and 9
(Condominium Ordinance). After reviewing a rezone application for a townhome
development and then holding several discussions in study meetings, the Council asked staff
to revisit landscaping and recreation space standards and to clearly define what is included
in recreation space.

In recent years the City has seen more residential projects where space for recreation is
being provided in ways not previously seen including higher amounts of indoor space.
Accordingly, the change central to this application is the removal of the term “open space”
and its replacement with the term “recreation space”.

For reference the term “open space” is currently defined as: “landscaped areas that are not
occupied by buildings, structures, parking areas, streets or alleys and are devoted to
recreation use or preservation of natural features.” The proposed definition for the term
“recreation space” is:

        a. outdoor space shared by residents of a residential Development for passive and
        active recreation. Examples include terraces, Courtyards, plazas, shared roof decks,
        sports courts, sports fields, open lawn areas, private parks, swimming pools, hot
        tubs, tot lots, picnic areas, fire pits and trails.
        b. indoor space shared by residents of a residential Development for passive and
        active recreation. Examples include fitness rooms, libraries, computer labs,
        swimming pools, hot tubs, sports courts, lobbies, clubhouses, social rooms,
        classroom space, community centers, recreation centers, community game rooms
        and theater rooms.
        c. private outdoor space provided only for the use of the residents of the Dwelling
        Unit to which it is attached. Examples include private Yards, front porches, patios,
        balconies, decks and roof decks.
       Recreation Space shall not include: public Right-of-way; private curb, gutter, and
       roadway improvements; Parking Spaces and maneuvering areas to Parking Spaces;
       all Building roofs that do not cover a porch or that do not have a roof deck that is
       accessible for recreation use; storm water retention areas; and storm water detention
       areas where the slope exceeds 30% and the depth exceeds 2’ from surrounding areas.


Allowing indoor recreation features to count toward minimum recreation space percentages
will encourage interior features that have year round utility and are more costly than a
similar sized outdoor feature (example indoor sport court versus outdoor sport court). If
indoor recreation features don’t count toward minimum required percentages, there is less
incentive to a developer to include them.

In addition to this overarching change, the proposed ordinance also includes the following
changes:

      establishes minimum landscaping percentages for multi-family residential
       development that vary based on the project density, not the zoning,
      establishes minimum percentages for the combination of landscaping and recreation
       space for multi-family residential development that vary based on the project
       density, not the zoning,
      sets a tree requirement for landscaped areas outside of the minimum required
       setbacks and outside of parking areas,
      updates the definition of “Planned Unit Development” to reflect current practice,
      removes the standards for allowing small condominium developments as these are
       already addressed elsewhere in the code, and
      removes provisions from the Condominium Ordinance that are unenforceable.
Staff Alternatives:

    1. Approval. The ordinance should be approved as proposed by staff.

    2. Continuance.

    3. Denial.

C-2-2021
Krispy Kreme
3370 South 5600 West
C-2 zone, .98 acres

The applicant, Eric Malmberg with Anderson Wahlen & Associates, representing Krispy
Kreme, is requesting conditional use approval for a fast food with drive-up restaurant at
3370 South 5600 West. The zoning for this property is C-2, General Commercial and the
General Plan designation is Hunter Towne Center. The surrounding zone is C-2 on all sides.
The surrounding uses include Alpine Storage to the west that is part of a larger overall
shopping center that continues to the south. There is a Raising Cane’s being developed to
the north, a Pizza Hut/HR Block to the south and a Wendy’s fast food restaurant to the east
across 5600 West.

This property is lot 5 of the Mountain View Business Plaza Subdivision. The building is
proposed to be 3,624 square feet and will contain a drive-up. The building will be
constructed of E.I.F.S with steel canopies as accent features. The building must comply with
the Commercial Design Standards. Staff recommends that the east elevation be the Primary
façade and all other sides be considered secondary facades including the west side of the
building.

Staff has worked with the applicant and the property owner to ensure that the layout of the
facility and the traffic flow works with the adjacent tenants and existing access points. This
includes re-working the Raising Cane’s site to accommodate the elimination of an internal
vehicular connection between these two sites but still maintaining the east-west pedestrian
connection to provide connectivity to the overall shopping center.

Krispy Kreme is also proposing to remove the parking stalls along the north side of the
Pizza Hut/HR Block building and replacing that with landscaping to accommodate their
drive-thru queuing. That work is currently being coordinated with that adjacent land owner
as that is a separate parcel from the overall shopping center. There is also an existing cell
tower that will need to be accommodated and the dumpsters on that site will be relocated
into a masonry enclosure.

This layout also includes a redesign of the existing parking stalls for the Mountain View
Business Plaza. The stalls that are on this site and continue south to another access point are
currently angled and will be re-striped for 90-degree stalls. This realignment will require
compliance with the parking code and landscape islands will be provided throughout this
entire area as outlined in 7-9-112. This work is also being coordinated with the other owners
in the shopping center and the proper easements and documentation are being prepared.

This parcel has frontage along 5600 West so the standards in 7-10-100 are applicable. The
drive-thru lane is also along this frontage so berming and additional landscaping will be
added to help screen the stacking areas for the cars as outlined in 7-7-111.

There is an overall sign package that was approved for this shopping center that includes an
existing pole sign. Therefore, Krispy Kreme will utilize space on that existing pole sign as
well as building/wall signage. No other ground signs are permitted at this location based off
the bonus given for the shopping center pole sign.

The typical hours of operation for this use are 5:30 am – 10:00 pm, 7-days a week.
There will be a dumpster provided for this building that will be located within a 6’ masonry
enclosure that is constructed to match the building.

Staff Alternatives

Approval subject to any issues raised at the public hearing as well as the following
conditions:

   1. The building must be completed per the approved plans and be designed in
      accordance with the Commercial Design Standards including the west side as a
      secondary facade.

   2. The site shall be constructed per the approved plan which includes re-alignment of
      the adjacent parking stalls and landscape islands. This shall be coordinated with the
      adjacent land owners.
   3. The relocation of parking stalls, landscaping and dumpsters for the Camp Six Lakes,

      easements and agreements.

Structural Preservation Systems
Outside Storage 3270 W 2200 S
M Zone (0.25 acres)

Structural Preservation Systems, LLC is requesting conditional use approval for outside
storage. The property is zoned M, Manufacturing, and the West Valley City General Plan
designates this area as light manufacturing. The surrounding zoning is also M. Adjacent
land uses include light industrial businesses to the east and south, and largely undeveloped
land to the north and west. The property directly west of this parcel is currently being
developed for a light industrial use.

The subject property is located just west of a light industrial complex. The 1.9 acre parcel
is largely undeveloped, except for the far northern end which has another outside storage
use approved. This proposal will occupy 0.25 acres of the parcel. The applicant is a
tenant in one of the neighboring building spaces and wants to use the area for a storage
yard. They are proposing to store construction equipment, cargo containers, and
structural and reinforcing steel.

Per 7-7-119, outside storage areas are to be screened from public view. The applicant is
proposing a 6’ tall chain link fence with barbed wire at the top. Slats should be added to
the fence to fully screen the area on all sides. Surfacing for outside storage is to be
evaluated by the Planning Commission. The applicant is proposing to finish the site with
crushed limestone. Historically, we have required vehicles or equipment to be stored on
asphalt or concrete. Staff recommends the same for this proposal as well. The other
equipment and materials can be stored on gravel, which is at least 4 inches deep. The area
should be kept weed free.

The adjacent parcel already meets the required 5% landscaping for the site, so no
additional landscaping is needed. Because the proposed storage area is undeveloped, a
grading and drainage plan is required for review by the Public Works Department.


Staff Alternatives:

the following:

              shall be surfaced with asphalt or concrete. All other storage areas may be
              surfaced with gravel which is at least 4” deep, contained by durable
              borders and kept weed free.

Cabco 5600 West Subdivision – 3rd Amendment
2530 South Anna Caroline Drive
M Zone


BACKGROUND

The West Valley City Engineering Division is requesting preliminary approval for the
Cabco 5600 West Subdivision 3rd Amendment. This application will amend a portion of lot
1A, lot 2 and a portion of Anna Caroline Drive recorded as part of the Cabco 5600 West
Subdivision Lot 1 Amended.

ISSUES:

The application is being submitted at the request of the Engineering Division on behalf of
the Utah Department of Transportation. The purpose for the amended subdivision is to
revise the alignment of the unbuilt portion of Anna Caroline Drive within the Cabco 5600
West Subdivision Lot 1 Amended.

The Cabco 5600 West Subdivision was recorded with the Salt Lake County Recorder’s
Office in 2014. At that time, the construction of Anna Caroline Drive was postponed until
such time that a bridge was installed over the Riter Canal. Once the bridge was installed,
the owner of the Cabco 5600 West Subdivision would be required to build his portion of
Anna Caroline Drive.

In 2019, the Utah Department of Transportation recorded the Anna Caroline Drive
Subdivision which is located immediately to the north of the Cabco 5600 West Subdivision.
The UDOT application created a large lot for UDOT to build an operations center and
building for the storage of material. As part of the Anna Caroline Drive subdivision, the
north end of Anna Caroline Drive was constructed from the north property line of the Cabco
5600 West Subdivision and extended north to 2400 South.

Shortly after the approval of the Anna Caroline Drive plat, the City began preparations to
build the bridge over the Riter Canal. During the initial phases of the bridge project and as
survey work began, the City noticed that the portion of Anna Caroline Drive installed as
part of UDOT’s Subdivision was not built according to plans. The portion of Anna Caroline
Drive constructed by UDOT is approximately 3 feet to the east of where it should be.

The Engineering Division contacted UDOT and the owner of the Cabco 5600 West
Subdivision to discuss the problem. Since the owner of Cabco 5600 West was preparing to
build his portion of Anna Caroline Drive, a resolution to the problem needed to be worked
out sooner than later. The solution that everyone agreed upon, is to re-align the road
slightly to the east in order to make the connection of this road work. In order to do this,
lots 1A, 2 and a portion of Anna Caroline Drive will be amended.
The subdivision plat identifies the area of the existing Anna Caroline Drive that will be
vacated and identifies a portion of the Cabco 5600 West Subdivision that will accommodate
an easement for the new road alignment. Thus, the amendment of Lots 1A, 2 and a portion
of Anna Caroline Drive.


STAFF ALTERNATIVES:

   1. Approve the Cabco 5600 West Subdivision 3rd Amendment subject to a resolution of
      the staff items noted in the analysis.

   2. Continue the application for reasons determined in the Planning Commission
      meeting.
