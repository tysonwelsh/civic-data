The following are the minutes of the Planning Commission Meeting held on Thursday, July 16, 2020 at 6:00
p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 W. Herriman Main Street (12090 South), Herriman, Utah.
Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered
to members of the Commission and media.

Presiding: Chair Chris Berbert

Commissioners Present Electronically: Andrea Bradford, Brody Rypien, Adam Jacobson, Andy Powell, Joy
Kaseke, Heather Garcia

Commission Members Absent: Jackson Ferguson, Lorin Palmer

Staff Present in Person: Communication Specialist Destiny Skinner, Deputy Recorder Wendy Thorpe

Staff Present Electronically: City Planner Michael Maloy, Community Development Director Blake Thomas,
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi, Engineer Josh Peterson, and City Engineer Jonathan Bowers



Chair Chris Berbert called the work meeting to order at 6:04 pm.




City Planner Maloy informed the Planning Commission the City Council discussed, at a work meeting, the
request for a rezone of five acres from A-l to A.5. The Commission had recommended denial of the request.

The City Council was taking time to study the issues, and will further review the item in a future meeting.

                        Commissioner Brody Rypien joined the meeting at 6:07 p.m.

They were to consider the impact of water pressure issues, and whether sidewalk, curb and gutter were the best
options for a rural setting. Council expressed interest to have a Commissioner present at Council meetings for
exchange of information. City Planner Maloy will request further clarification of Council’s expectation and
propose the idea that a stipend should be provided to the Commissioner.

Council approved a one-lot rezone on Pioneer Street that the Commission had recommended for approval.
The layout and shared access were not a condition of approval and were briefly discussed. The subdivision
would come back to the Commission for approval.


Items 3.1 & 3.2 – City Planner Maloy informed the Planning Commission that City Engineer Bowers had
worked closely with Landscape Architect Heidi Shegrud and they were available for answering questions.
Some comments have been received, and both items were conditional use permits.

Commissioner Heather Garcia asked if access to the Wide Hollow Trailhead would be provide from the north
side on 6600 West and Bonnie Jean Road. City Engineer Bowers answered that the neighborhood to the north
did have access. He identified trail access locations. Commissioner Garcia expressed concern with steep
grading. City Engineer Bowers responded the access would be steep but would include native stairs. Graffiti
rock may or may not be moved. Residents were split on the subject. Commissioner Jacobson warned of graffiti
extending beyond graffiti rock. City Engineer Bowers stated the intent was to celebrate it and keep it as part of
the project. Commissioner Rypien inquired as to safety concerns for the Wide Hollow Trailhead. City Planner
Maloy stated traffic study information was included with the meeting Packet. City Engineer Bowers added
traffic committee meeting would discuss the item next week and discuss mitigation measures. He summarized
that traffic calming along wide hollow were recommended. Commissioner Garcia recommended a crosswalk
for the downhill areas on Seely Drive, Clearwater Drive, and Wide Hollow Drive. City Engineer Bowers
indicated a warrant study would be conducted to determine proper traffic calming measures.

Commissioner Garcia asked how the traffic study would calculate usage. City Engineer Bowers stated the
warrant study included traffic counters to determine popularity and continued monitoring for traffic safety
after opening of the trailhead. Commissioner Powell added that stop signs were not good tools for slowing
traffic and that monitoring would be very important. Multiple calming measures may be necessary.
Commissioner Garcia stated many of the houses in that area did not have trees in the park strips. Commission
Jacobson recommended for City Code Enforcement to investigate.

Proposed parking spaces along the street at the pump track and nearby at Big Bend Park were discussed. The
July 16, 2020 – Planning Commission Minutes                                   Page 3 of 9


traffic impact study had not been completed. City Planner Maloy added the parking needs at similar parks in
other cities had been researched. It was theorized that many users would bike to it. Chair Berbert observed a
handful of individuals drive to the existing pump track in Draper. City Engineer Bowers anticipated room for
approximately four spaces along the roadway. Nearby parking lots at adjacent parks would be depended on to
alleviate the need for it. Commissioner Rypien offered this was a huge draw and would attract many people.
He requested the dimensions for the pump track and how many riders it would accommodate.

Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events Anthony Teuscher said the width was around five feet, and
allowed one rider, width-wise. The estimated length was 60 feet by 40 feet, which was the largest that would fit
at the location. The track was comprised of marine grade treated lumber substrate with composite top, which
had a lifespan of twenty to twenty five years. Chair Berbert added it could be a good introduction to the
community and if popular, a larger one could be added later at another location. Deputy Director of Parks,
Recreation and Events Teuscher stated another; similar type of track was planned for the Juniper Canyon
Recreation Area.

Item 3.3 - Commissioner Powell requested more information about the Tide store awning. He drove by when
it was being installed, but it was later removed. City Planner Maloy stated the City inspector noticed it was not
included in the original plan. The developer submitted an amendment for the awning. The franchise company
required the awning over the drive-thru.

Item 3.4 - City Planner Maloy had nothing to add.


Assistant City Manager Maloy appreciated the discussion at the end of last meeting. He proposed changing
work meetings to be a field trip to visit areas before the work meeting. Then having another work session at
the end of the regular meeting. He asked if Commissioners would be interested in doing so when regular in
person meetings resume. Chair Berbert said a field trip might be relevant for some meetings, but not all.
Commissioner Rypien agreed and added it should only be done when clearly needed. He stated time would be
better spent reviewing ordinances, and general plan updates. Commissioner Jacobson cared more about
reviewing ordinances. Chair Berbert mentioned scheduling items together, if they needed a field trip.
Commissioners added that the meeting tonight did not need a field. Trip. City Planner Maloy asked if
Commissioners would come in earlier for a field trip. The majority agreed they would. Commissioner Rypien
proposed riding in different cars. Commissioner Powell thought they should take it upon themselves to review
the areas, if needed, when reviewing the packet. City Planner Maloy proposed meeting at projects after
completion. Commissioner Garcia liked the idea as a chance to review what could have been done better.

Chair Berbert offered a review of the Strategic Planning Committee discussions. Four major areas the city
needed to work on were roads, inadequate fiber, better representation with the County and State, and Public
transit. Fiber should be more widespread in Herriman. He requested to be kept in the loop regarding fiber

Planning Commission work meeting adjourned by consensus at 6:50 p.m.




Chair Chris Berbert called the meeting to order at 7:01 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance.

Full Quorum Present.


No Comments were offered.



Commissioner Heather Garcia moved to approve item 2.4 Approval of Minutes for the May 21, 2020 and June 4, 2020 Planning
Commission meetings and all voted aye.




City Planner Maloy reviewed the zone and location. Two neighborhood meetings were held and comments
were included in the packet. The traffic impact study was included in the packet. Vehicle access, parking stalls
and restroom locations were identified. The City Code approval standards for conditional use were briefly
reviewed. Landscape Architect Heidi Shegrud stated the site plan was changed considerably, based on input
received at the first neighborhood meeting. The latest proposal addressed their concerns and blends well into
the landscape. The traffic study recommended 66 parking stalls for peak use times. The trails would connect
with a north trail. It’s a very balanced site and the Challenge Staircase fits very well with the mountain.
July 16, 2020 – Planning Commission Minutes                                   Page 5 of 9


Challenge Staircases have been very popular in Colorado. Landscape Architect Heidi Shegrud stated the
residents at the neighborhood meeting were excited about it. The fence would be split, white vinyl.
Commissioner Garcia expressed safety concerns with the steep dropoff adjacent to the parking area.
Landscape Architect Heidi Shegrud said a guard rail or boulders would be installed to prevent cars from going
over the cliff. The parking lot would not be paved. Commissioner Garcia stated she could not justify so many
parking stalls. Chair Berbert offered the opposite opinion and stated the number of parking stalls would make
the area safer, and prevent street parking. Commissioner Rypien agreed with Chair Berbert and recommended
angular parking. Commission Powell added the Yellow Fork parking area was always full. Commissioner
Garcia mentioned overflow parking on 6600 West, and that residents have issues with outsiders and don’t
want to share amenities with non-residents. Chair Berbert offered that the parking was needed due to the
growing population in Herriman, and Commissioners Powell and Jacobson concurred.

Commissioners offered split opinions regarding Graffiti Rock. Those against offered the graffit had spread to
other rocks. Commissioner Rypien had concerns with kids going there with spraypaint and offered the
possibility of the City commissioning a local artist to create an art installation there. Other Commissoners
supported this idea and the inclusion of Spirit Rocks. Commissioner Powell added the City should get rid of
the Graffiti Rock due to inappropriate language on the rock. Commissioner Andrea Bradford supported the
addition of security cameras in the area and at other parks. Landscape Architect Heidi Shegrud identified
locations for the two planned surveillance cameras. Neighbors were concerned the parking lot could bring
negative behavior and requested cameras. Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events Anthony Teuscher
said three other parks have cameras and they have unfortunately not deterred vandalism.

Commissioner Garcia asked how traffic would be monitored in the area. City Engineer Jonathan Bowers said
additional traffic was anticipated due to this amenity. He planned to discuss mitigation measures at the traffic
committee meeting next week. Commissioner Garcia asked if Herriman had a fire hazard mitigation plan. City
Engineer Bowers will research and get back to the Commission.

Commissioner Garcia asked what the parking hours would be and how they would be managed. Landscape
Architect Shegrud stated most parks were open dawn to dusk and no changes were anticipated with this one.
No lighting was planned. The installation of a gate was briefly addressed. The City would prefer to avoid the
risk of locking someone in, or denying very early morning access. Landscape Architect Heidi Shegrud
mentioned a light may be on the restroom building, which will have a new system open and lock doors
automatically. Vehicles would be subject to ticketing after hours. City Attorney Chase Andrizzi added the
General Park Ordinance covered additional parking enforcement. It was stated the City has a Community Fire
Protection Plan with UFA and an Incident Response Plan for fire incidents. Commissioner Heather Garcia
requested to see them.

Commissioner Adam Jacobson moved to approve item 3.1 file # C2019-096 Conditional Use Permit for the Wide Hollow

City Planner Maloy reviewed the location and zone. A neighborhood meeting was held June 29, 2020. The
Pump Track consisted of a prefabricated track and is between four and five feet wide.

Chair Berbert asked about parking. City Engineer Bowers stated parking initially would be along the road and
at Big Bend park. Another large park was anticipated to the west, which would include additional parking and
connect to the Pump Track with an asphalt trail. On the East Side of Anthem Park potential open space may
accommodate future parking. City Engineer Bowers anticipated the need for thirty to forty spots, however he
emphasized the parking and traffic studies still needed to be completed. Commissioner Adam Jacobson
expressed the parking study should be completed prior to approval. Chair Berbert mentioned some pocket
parks have no street parking. The size of the Pump Track was approximately 40 by 70 feet. Commissioners
expressed that Pump Tracks in other cities were very popular and more parking may be needed. Commissioner
Jacobson stressed the City should be held to the same level as a developer. Commissioners discussed the size
and capacity of the Pump Track.
