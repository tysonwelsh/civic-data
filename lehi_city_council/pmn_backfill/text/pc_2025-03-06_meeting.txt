Regular Work Session, 5:30 p.m.

1) Call to Order

Commission Chair Jackson welcomed everyone to the meeting.

2) North Lehi Station Area Plan Presentation and Discussion

Christie Richman presented the current North Lehi Station Area Plan.

The current plan covers both developed and undeveloped land. The undeveloped land has several
different owners, and some of the existing plans for it include business offices and a freeway
access point. It is in close proximity to the State Flight Park Recreation Area, Shadow Ridge
Park, and existing trail infrastructure. One goal is to create a new gateway into Lehi City.

Feedback from analyzing the area and from community engagement events have resulted in the
following project vision: to establish and maintain a unique hub of neighborly activity, easily
accessed by people of all ages. Commissioner Eyre had concerns over focusing on small-scale
neighborhood amenities when the ultimate goal is to encourage more people to use the area.
Richman explained that the development will be a transition area between a regional hub and a
transit supportive environment.

The preferred layout currently places mixed-use areas close to the station, with small lot single
family homes or townhomes further away. Several plans for mixed-use blocks were presented,
showcasing varying development intensity. The station will be a central hub connecting the

There will be a public hearing for the plan at the next Planning Commission meeting (March 13),
and it will then proceed to the City Council.

The commission took a brief recess for refreshments at approximately 6:20.

3) Hospital/2100 North Station Area Plan Presentation and Discussion

The commission reconvened at approximately 6:30.

Jordan Swayne presented the area plan, with assistance from Ray Whitchurch and Jonathan
Young.

HB462, which passed last year, requires cities to create plans for a half mile radius around any
light rail stations, and a quarter mile from any BRT (bus rapid transit) stations. The four goals of
the legislation are to (1) create affordable housing around transit stations, (2) encourage
environmentally sustainable infrastructure, (3) provide access to opportunities, especially places
to live, work, and play, and (4) find ways to enhance a multimodal operation of streets.

The combined area plan encapsulates two stations, South Triumph Station, located at the
hospital, and the 2100 station. South Triumph Station is about seven years out, with the
development of the second station being further out. 2100 will be connected to Provo, potentially
by a BRT line. UTA is considering a shuttle that would connect South Triumph Station to the
Lehi Frontrunner station. The gap between the two stations is still being discussed by
UDOT/UTA.

Commissioner Eyre asked how the removal of parking space for the area plan would be
addressed, as the Young Living Campus is a central part of the plan, and currently requires a lot
of parking. Swayne explained that much of the surface parking would be consolidated into
parking structures. He is also hopeful that societal shifts towards remote workability will be
utilized by companies in order to regulate the number of cars in an area at any given time. He
explained that the development will be market-driven, as no part of the plan is dependent on the
other, aside from the train stations.

Commissioner Everett shared concerns about the tensions between walkability and maintaining
traffic flow. Luke Seegmiller said that 1200 West will need to remain an arterial road. Swayne
doesn’t think it will affect the plan too much. Aerial pedestrian bridges have been suggested as a
solution, in addition to the two planned places for pedestrians to cross. Commissioner Everett
was still worried about isolating the northern half of the development from the southern half of
the development, as they will be separated by five lanes of traffic.

The majority of the planned streets will be residential style with 25 mph speed limits. Other
streets are designed to give a ‘village’ style look with more amenities and green space. There
will be dedicated bus lanes to South Triumph Station. Additionally, the development features a
disentangled multi-use path that is separated from streets, but still connected to different areas in
the community in order to provide a safer non-car transportation experience.

The plan is divided into seven different planning districts. The two closest to the stations will be
oriented around the stations to emphasize and encourage public transit. Other districts include a
health and wellness shopping district, an innovation district for startups and rental spaces for
businesses, a commercial corridor district that will carry groceries and other essential goods, and
two transit supportive districts.

Commissioner Everett is concerned about the east side of Lehi which will remain unwalkable.
Jordan Swayne clarified that the area plan is not designed to take larger connections into
consideration. The commission acknowledged this but thinks that the larger connections are
important to consider anyway. Commissioner Jackson explained that it is much harder to
rearrange infrastructure once it’s been put it. Ray Whithouse suggests planning and maintaining
corridors so that infrastructure won’t have to be upset to build more connecting rails.
Commissioner Jackson also shares his opinion that the only way to improve public transit usage
economically is to make public transit just as easy as driving.

There isn’t much room for residential housing north of 1200 W, so the majority of the
development will be commercial and retail. There will be some mixed-use and low density
housing south of 1200 W. Open spaces will be integrated into the designs of the districts, with
the areas near the hospital being more plaza-like, and those in the commercial districts having
outdoor seating for eating.

Commissioner Eyre asked if the South Triumph Station would allow a track crossing. Mike West
explained that it’s vital to have a track crossing there to enable access. Luke Seegmiller asked
about the timeline of the development. Swayne said there is no current deadline, but that the
estimate is seven years. Various factors like the construction of The Point and the Olympics may
accelerate construction.

This item will come before planning commission at the second meeting in March. There will be
more information about implementation at that time.

4) Adjournment

With no further business to come before the Planning Commission at this time, Commissioner
Eyre moved to adjourn the meeting. Commissioner Roberts seconded the motion. The motion
passed unanimously. The meeting adjourned at approximately 7:50 p.m.
