    1.3      (Continued) Presentation on The Point Development by Alan Matheson - Michael Maloy,
             Planning Director
Scott Cuthbertson presented the proposed framework plan of the 600-acre site, on the old state prison location.
They conducted a robust resident feedback process and anticipated demolition of the prison around July 2022.
Full build out was anticipated to take between ten and fifteen years. The mixed-use community included five
forward thinking components: Mobility, Ecology, Energy and Carbon, Water, and Waste. All daily needs may
be met on location within fifteen minutes. The river to range trail was a key feature and included 140 acres of
green space and connections to the Jordan River Trail. The multi-purpose plan included office, commercial,
institutional, rapid transit, residential types and entertainment districts. The developable area was approximately
350 acres, with 112 acres designated for infrastructure and 140 acres for open space. The Commission was
encouraged to visit their website for further updates.

    3.3. Request for Approval to Find for a Compelling, Countervailing Public Interest of Water
          Efficient Standards
          Applicant: Herriman City
City Attorney Andrizzi explained this item would formalize a compelling interest in the conservation of water,
as per the water efficiency standards. City Council recently adopted new water efficiency standards, which have
been codified into code; however, some developments have vested rights that were in place prior to the adoption
of the new standards. State code allowed for an exception to the vested rights doctrine under certain
circumstances. The City may require developments to abide by the new landscaping standards. The City retained
the right to identify compelling, countervailing public interest to require the developments to abide by the new
water efficiency standards. The written finding was a state requirement, and this was intended to be applied
broadly for consistent use. The standards were developed with input from the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy
District. Programmable play space, such as detention basins, may remain lawn. Commissioners recommended
identifying other uses for such basins, like the Rosecrest detention pond which contained a tennis / basketball
court, as possible bonus density spaces.

Commissioner Jacobson moved to approve item 3.3 Request for Approval to find for a Compelling, Countervailing Public Interest
of Water Efficient Standards. Commissioner Rypien seconded the motion.

    4.1. Request to Approve a Zoning Text Amendment to Title 10 Amending Several Chapters and
         Adding Chapter 34 Which Regulate Accessory Buildings in the City (Public Hearing)
         Applicant: Herriman City
         File Number: Z2021-054

Chair Powell reviewed the public hearing guidelines.
November 4, 2021 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                                                            Page 6 of 8
Planning Manager Spencer explained objectives to consolidate standards and reviewed the setback and height
requirements. Per fire code recommendations anything larger than 200 square feet required a five feet setback
from the primary structure.

Chair Powell opened the public hearing.

Pedro Martins stated he disagreed with the City setback recommendation, particularly for pie shaped lots. The
recommended easement would push buildings to the center of the lot. There should be more flexibility for pie
shaped and inverted pie shaped lots, in the exception process. He felt there should be more opportunities for
public comment during planning meetings for unusual situations.

Lyn Egbert thought the recommendations were far too restrictive. Matching to the primary building may look
good to some, but not others. Existing metal buildings and barns look nice but would not comply with the
proposed restrictions. Restricting what can be put on someone’s property was not the purpose of the Planning
Commission.

Chair Powell closed the public hearing.

Commissioners Jacobson recommended allowing small accessory dwellings on the fence line for corner lots, if
no easement setbacks were covered. There should be only one foot setback between the rear property line,
further away for tall buildings. Commissioner Rypien disagreed with the ability to build on the lot line, as runoff
water flow could be impacted. Chair Powell said the point of setbacks was to provide for easement access and
avoid encroachment onto neighboring properties. They should not apply to boundaries without a neighbor.
Commissioner Fenn recommended including the side yard footage when calculating backyard sizes. Canopies
and awnings could blow away and should not be considered part of the main structure. Commissioner Palmer
brought up RV storage. Commissioners debated ideal setback sizes, building heights and runoff water issues. All
special exceptions would come to PC for approval. Commissioners agreed to keep the one- and three-feet
setbacks, allow corner lots to use the one- and three-foot setbacks, with the exclusion of easements, for small
structures. Require three feet easements for medium / large structures, with an increase of one foot for each
additional foot over 16 feet high, even on corner lots. Include side yard areas in the rear yard calculation, and
make all pie shaped lots and corner lots special exceptions.

Commissioner Jacobson moved to recommend approval to the City Council of a proposed Zoning Text Amendment to Title 10
Amending Several Chapters and Adding Chapter 34 Which Regulate Accessory Buildings in the City with three changes: #1 to
include side yard and setback in the percentage area, #2 include the corner lots up to the property line, they’ll keep an easement, but
allow the one and three foot setbacks as agreed upon, #3 and include all pie shaped lots in the list of special exceptions. Commissioner
Rypien seconded the motion.

The vote was recorded as follows:
Commissioner Brody Rypien                   Aye
Commissioner Jackson Ferguson               Aye
Commissioner Andrea Bradford                Aye

Commissioner Lorin Palmer           Aye

    4.2. Request to Approve an Amendment to the Herriman City Official Zoning Map for Property
         Located at 5652 W 13100 S from 2.93 acres of the A-.25 Zone to the C-2 Zone, and 6.06
         acres of the A-.50 and A-.25 Zones to the R-1-10 Zone. (Public Hearing)
         Applicant: Bowler Development L.C. (Public Hearing)
         File Number: Z2021-090
Planner Talia Wright provided an overview of the rezone and explained the purpose of the rezone was to create
a subdivision. She provided the property history and explained the property swap with the Butterfield property.
The proposal was consistent with the general plan map and staff recommended approval to City Council.

Applicant Alex Winder thanked the Commission and staff for their time. He offered to answer questions.

Chair Powell opened the public hearing.

Gary Ballard was not concerned with the rezone but had issues with a previous Commission issue regarding
Grant Mines. He identified the property he owned and a fifteen-foot boundary dispute which caused him to
receive a $6,000 tax bill. He wanted to make sure the owners were notified when similar developments were
approved. He had to pay for a new survey, and it has cost him 10,000 dollars so far.

Lyn Egbert thought the zoning was great, he was concerned the half acre lots were useless as the backyards will
waste a lot of water. He had an issue with 13000 South right of way location, and the property vacated was
wrong, and he should not profit from it. It also caused flooded basements and this development would have the
same problem.

Randy Bowler spoke on behalf of the applicant. He said they were aware of concerns, and they were working
with neighbors and staff to resolve all outstanding issues. They requested approval to continue moving forward.

Chair Powell closed the public hearing.

Community Development Director Thomas said that items such as offset intersections and the potential
vacating of property mentioned be addressed during the site plan process Commissioners discussed possible
roadway options. City Attorney Andrizzi reminded the Commission the rezone was the only topic of discussion
