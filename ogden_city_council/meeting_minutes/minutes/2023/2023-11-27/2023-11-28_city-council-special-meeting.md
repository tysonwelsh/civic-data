; A memo from the Management Services Department regarding a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-2024 budget

$1.5 million from the General Fund balance to the Municipal Building Authority to fund a reserve account and miscellaneous
expenses associated with the WonderBlock parking structures.
Comptroller Sorensen summarized the memo and stated the funds are part of the $4 Million granted to the City by the
Governor’s Office of Economic Development (GOED) for the WonderBlock project. A transfer of $2.5 Million was previously
transferred for use on the project.

Angel Castillo stated this is the Council’s chance to ‘pause’ this project and get more data; Administration cannot
proceed with bonding ifthe transfer is not approved and the fact that the City has received money from GOED is inconsequential.
The fact that GOED is committing money to the project does not mean the City needs to. She is not opposed to the project in
general, but many things have changed in the market since the Council originally approved the project and the Council has the
responsibility to ensure the project is feasible. If the developer fails, the City will be responsible for repaying the debt for the
project. It is irresponsible to move forward without updated data. Administration is recommending proceeding based upon the

‘sunk cost fallacy’; there is no doubt City staff has worked very hard on this project, but it is important to ensure the project can be
successful. She added that when large developers are working on these types of projects, they typically bring anchor tenants with
them, and she feels the City is taking a big risk without having something similar for the WonderBlock project.

Laura Lewis agreed with Ms. Castillo; it seems that Administration recommends these types of things to the Council ona
regular basis without complete or current data. She asked the Council members to consider if they would invest their own personal
money without complete data. The City always seems to have money for these types of projects, but not to address the City’s most
critical need, which is housing. She urged the Council against adopting the ordinance to amend the budget.

Linda Sanchez agreed with Ms. Castillo and Ms. Lewis; she hopes the Council will table this issue until more
information can be provided. She would also like the City to consider placing money in an emergency fund rather than always
funding these types of projects. It is financially irresponsible to move forward at this point and she asked the Council to consider
the worst-case scenario before proceeding.

Heath Satow stated he has little doubt that the Council will approve this budget amendment tonight and he questions why
the Council continues with the pageantry of allowing public input when that input will not be listened to. He stated that at a recent
Union Station open house meeting, he had a conversation with a developer about things that have changed in downtown Ogden in
the last year and the need to consider current data. The developer had completed a study in 2019 and they were unaware of what
has changed since them and this was shocking to him. If that is the mindset of developers who are working in the Ogden
community, he is very concerned about the future of these projects.

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2023-
2034 budget amendment came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Administration has proposed the
appropriation of BDO Lease Revenue Retained Earnings ($250,000), and the transfer of the funds to the Airport Fund ($250,000)
to be used to assist a commercial airline with startup costs associated with new passenger service between Ogden and Orange
County, California.
FE
— Community and Economic Development Director Cooper summarized the memo and stated the new airline is Breeze
) Airways, and they would like to begin operations at the Ogden Hinckley Airport (OGD) in February of 2024. He discussed the
‘ history of Breeze Airways, which began operations in 2021. They have 17 A220’s in their fleet with 63 more on order. They are a
serving 45 communities in 27 states and have flown two million guests in 2023. They will start by flying four weekly flights from i
OGD to John Wayne Airport in Orange County, California, with plans to expand to other locations as demand dictates. The
$250,000 incentive will be broken into three payments: $84,000 in March 2024, $83,000 in July 2024, and $83,000 in November
2024. He discussed the potential revenue benefits to the Ogden Airport, including parking fees, landing fees, fuel flowage fees, car
rentals, restaurant rental, ground handler rent, and TSA rent. He concluded the source of funds, if approved, will be BDO lease
revenue. Breeze is demonstrating their commitment by investing approximately $750,000 for startup costs plus additional ongoing
costs thereafter. There is no further guarantee of passengers. All Airport revenue, including entitlement funds, must ‘stay on the
airport’ and can be used for any airport improvement program eligible project (AIP) that is solely for Airport use such as ramps, .
taxiways, runways, sewer, water, power, etc. :
Council member Hyer addressed fuel flowage; the airplanes used by Breeze will be able to hold enough fuel to complete
around trip from Ogden to Orange County. He asked if the contract includes a requirement for Breeze to buy fuel at the Ogden
Airport. Mr. Cooper deferred to Breeze Airlines Director of Airport Relations Bud Hafer to answer that question. Mr. Hafer stated
that a fuel calculation program is used on each aircraft to calculate the amount of fuel needed and to consider the cost of fuel at
each airport; this program will dictate fuel flowage, and the amount purchased from each airport will vary from day to day. He
also discussed the markets that he is considering locating in; Ogden is an underserved market with demand for affordable airfare :
and that is one reason it is very attractive to Breeze. He then discussed the popularity of flights to Orange County, noting there is
not as much of a demand for flights from Orange County to Ogden; he anticipates that 80 percent of the flights will be from
Ogden to Orange County, with just 20 percent originating from Orange County to Ogden. However, he expects that other markets,
such as Phoenix, Arizona, will be more evenly split. ;
Acting Chair Richey asked Mr. Hafer if the Ogden Airport will draw business from Southern Idaho. Mr. Hafer stated that
the Ogden market will pull from many areas, including parts of Idaho, and this will be due to several different factors.
Council member Nadolski inquired as to Breeze’s marketing plans. Mr. Hafer stated that there will be an initial press
conference announcement about 75 days before the service commences and this will generate a great deal of excitement. There
will also be an email/digital/social media marketing campaign. Council member Nadolski inquired as to what is included in the
_ company’s $750,000 start up costs. Mr. Hafer stated that the company will incur training costs for all the employces who will be
| performing day-to-day operations at the Ogden Airport; equipment; marketing; and general operating costs.
Acting Chair Richey asked Mr. Hafer to address the pilot shortage and if that will have an impact on Breeze’s plans at the
Ogden Airport. Mr. Hafer stated the pilot shortage will be a looming issue for all airlines for some time; however, Breeze has been
able to weather it better than some given that it is a start-up airline where pilots will have greater opportunities at seniority, giving
them greater access to higher salaries. Also, because Breeze is a start-up, it is possible to craft operations in consideration of the
pilot shortage and other factors that impact airlines.
Council member Nadolski asked Mr. Hafer to address the airline’s withdrawal from other markets after just a short time
operating. Mr. Hafer stated that Breeze has ceased to offer certain routes due to insufficient demand, but they have pulled out of
three cities they have operated in. They operate in 45 airports and have left three due to the competitive nature of those markets,
which are vastly different than the Ogden area. Additionally, fuel costs in these markets were very high and made it difficult to be
profitable in the long term. It takes time to develop each route, but after a certain time operating, it sometimes becomes necessary
to shift and cease operating in a certain market or offering a current route.
Council member Lopez asked Mr. Hafer how many other incentive packages he has received from municipalities that
operate an airport. Mr. Hafer stated he cannot disclose specific incentive agreements, but many markets do participate at different
levels. The type of partnership being recommended to Ogden City is fairly common, though the level of support varies in different
markets. Council member Lopez asked Mr. Hafer if he has received some form of financial support from 100 percent of the
airports he operates in. Mr. Hafer stated he has received some sort of local support from a vast majority of the cities he operates in.
This ranges from having airport fees waived for the first year all the way up to a revenue guarantee. He indicated that Ogden’s
incentive is average.
Council member Nadolski asked Mr. Hafer if he would be willing to link the incentive to enplanements directly. Mr.
Hafer stated he has never seen that kind of structure, but he is willing to entertain it. Council member Nadolski stated he is
concerned about the emergency nature of this action; the Council is being asked to approve the budget amendment the day before

are comfortable with relative to the incentive agreement; there was an emphasis for the need for the incentive to be broken into

Council would like the terms of the agreement to change, Administration will renegotiate with Mr. Hafer outside of this meeting.

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
conveying property to the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency (RDA). The memo stated a multi-family residential and
commercial development located at approximately 18" Street and Washington Boulevard is being developed which would contain
up to 115 units in two five-story residential buildings and a small commercial building where the Pioneer Heritage building
(Piccadilly’s building) is located. The overall project approvals include a rezone to C3/CO, a street vacation of River Drive east of
Washington Boulevard, both of which were approved by the Council on September 26, 2023, property sale and/or transfer from ;
the City, RDA, and UDOT, and incentive agreement for public improvements from the RDA. The Council has approved the
rezone and street vacation and has held the public hearing for the sale of several properties at or near 1810 Washington Boulevard
as significant parcels. The Administration is in the process of finalizing the sale of property at 1810 Washington Boulevard to the
petitioner (no further Council action required for the sale). As part of the overall development, the next step is for the City to
approve the transfer of property from Ogden City ownership to Redevelopment Agency ownership. Once that transfer is
completed, the property is then proposed to be conveyed to the petitioner in a separate RDA action and a participation and
incentive agreement is proposed to be approved for the development of the project in another separate RDA action. This action is
the proposed transfer of parcels from the Redevelopment Agency to Parkway Group, LLC. The three properties involved in this
transfer are the same three that are proposed to be transferred from Ogden City to the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency in a

_ separate action. The 1810 Washington Boulevard property and the UDOT properties are being addressed with separate actions.
} The 1810 Washington Boulevard property is being sold to the Parkway Group for $250,000. The UDOT properties are anticipated
to be acquired by the RDA and transferred in a separate action once those acquisitions are completed.

Community and Economic Development Director Cooper summarized the memo and presented conceptual plans for the
proposed development of the subject property and the other parcels owned by the developer. Development of the property will
improve the property along the Ogden River and the area that is publicly accessible as part of the Ogden Parkway Trail system.
