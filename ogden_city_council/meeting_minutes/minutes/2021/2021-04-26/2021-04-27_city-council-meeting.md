Proposed Ordinance 2021-18 amending the Fiscal Year 2021 budget in the
amount of $1,300,000
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Director regarding a proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 budget
amendment came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed budget amendment will increase the
FY2021 City budget by $1 ,300,000; the amendment recognizes revenues from Ogden City Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease
Revenue Retained Earnings ($1,300,000) and appropriates those revenues for an interagency transfer to the Redevelopment
Agency ($1 ,300,000) for environmental remediation at the Swift Building site and for the Weber River restoration project. ·
          Community and Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper summarized the memo and provided a brief overview
of the history of environmental remediation activities performed by the City; the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)
designates certain properties as 'brownfield' sites and these are properties that have real or assumed presence of contamination
that limits or prevents redevelopment potential. There are over 450,000 brownfield sites in the United States and the EPA has
focused on remediation of these sites in order to improve public health and safety; however, an additional outcome of these efforts
has been job creation and infrastructure improvements throughout communities across the nation. He referenced several sites
throughout the community that have been part of the City's environmental remediation efforts; he noted the Swift Building site
was designated as a brownfield site and after the City's acquisition of the property in 2017 the City began to work towards

         Minutes of Regular Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah, April27, 2021 Page 154


enviromnental remediation of the site. This work was being completed in conjunction with the efforts to restore the Weber River
as part of the Trackline Economic Development Project. The Ogden River restoration project has been extremely successful. By
restoring the river's natural channel and appropriate vegetation, the banks withstood a 100-year flood event, even before the
project was completed. The City's Engineering division will be implementing the same type of design and construction along
portions of the Weber River, including the area next to the Swift Building site. This will require soil remediation and removal
along the banks. The $1,300,000 in additional funding will allow for additional environmental site work including subsurface
sampling and site characterization, tarping, river excavation and buried asbestos remediation. As part of the State of Utah
voluntary clean-up program it is likely the City will need to comply with ongoing and additional requirements. As a result,
additional expenditures are expected beyond the current request and as needed the Redevelopment Agency will request additional
appropriations to fund these expenditures.
          Comptroller Stout then reviewed FY20 18 and FY2020 budget appropriations for the subject project. Expenditures to date
include the property purchase and site work; estimated additional costs at the site are $1.4 million, bringing the total project
