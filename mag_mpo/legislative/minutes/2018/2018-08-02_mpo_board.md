Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) Modification - Lehi 1200 West MAG withholds 10% of each project funded as a contingency to pay for cost overruns that may occur. If the city is requesting the contingency fund, and it is less than 10% of total project value and less than $500k, MAG staff can approve it. If it exceeds that amount, then it needs to be approved by Regional Planning Committee. The city of Lehi was awarded $5.4M of County Transportation Sales Tax funds in 2016 to widen Lehi 1200 West to 5 lanes from I-15 to Timpanogos HWY. Since the project was awarded, UDOT has worked with the city on an Active Transportation Plan for the I-15 Technology Corridor project. In developing the plan UDOT, MAG, and the city all agreed that adding bike lanes to the 1200 West project would better serve the area. Adding bike lanes raised the engineers estimate cost of the project, particularly in escalating right-of-way costs. The estimated project cost is $6,628,000 or an additional $1,228,000. UDOT proposes to add $688k state funds to the project. $600k comes from a Region 3 balance, and $88k from a project on Redwood Road that no longer needs these funds. MAG proposes to add $540k in contingency funds. Utah County Commission approval will also be needed because the project is funded with County Transportation Sales Tax Funds. The new project value for the project will be $6,628,000 available in 2018. There is a 6.77% local match on these funds to be paid by Lehi. Any future overruns will be paid by the city of Lehi. There was concern that the TIP is funding several trails and at a high cost. It was asked if it is necessary and why the trails are ranked higher than other projects on the list that appear to be equally as important. Bob stated that the high cost is due to right-of-way costs and the increase in construction cost. Utah County is ahead of other counties in regards to trails because of the investment that has been made for connected bike trails. Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) Final Adoption The MPO funds projects in the TIP every two years. The Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) and Regional Planning Committee (RPC) created a final prioritized project list June 7, 2018 following the process established by RPC. That process included: • Reviewing submitted concept reports • MPO staff scoring each project • Visiting each project for a field review • Voting to create the ranked priority list of projects RPC must approve the funding scenario for the final prioritized project list and adopt the 2019 TIP. MPO staff have created two funding scenarios according to RPC motion. One including project #15 and the other without project #15. Both scenarios include an additional $5M to be added to the contingency due to rising construction costs. Additional funding became available from federal dollars, projects that have been closed-out, and high projections in county transportation sales tax. The total distribution increased from $41M to $55M which will fund seven additional projects. Funding will primarily be available in 2021-2022. Bob Allen reviewed the distribution of MAG/County transportation funds for the past ten years. In 2018, Utah County received $2.1M for three projects, central Utah County received $14.6M for seven projects, north Utah County $20.1M for eight projects, and south Utah County received $17.1M for eight projects. There are cities who have received little to no funding in the past because applications are not being submitted. Cities and towns are encouraged to apply as it is MAG’s goal to help incubate projects that have been submitted so they will be successful. Air Quality Conformity of the Regional Transportation Plan Shauna Mecham stated that the Air Quality Act of 1970 set health-based air quality standards for the nation. If areas in a state exceed those standards by polluting more than is acceptable, the State must create a plan with policies and programs to control air pollution. Part of that plan includes emissions budgets for vehicles. Each time Mountainland prepares a TIP or RTP, it must demonstrate that the plan conforms to the State’s goals for improving air quality. If Mountainland’s models show a proposed transportation plan surpassing the given budget, the plan cannot be approved and any capacity or pollution-adding projects cannot move forward. Senate Bill 136 4th Quarter Cent Sales Tax Options and B&C Gas Tax Revenue Andrew Jackson reviewed the various options, timing, and dollar amounts in regards to 4th quarter cent funds, as well as the need for additional transportation funding. The Committee engaged in a general discussion. A proposed Interlocal Cooperation Agreement has been drafted between Utah Transit Authority (UTA) and Utah County to address the concerns the County has with UTA’s involvement. The agreement will substantially change Proposition 1 and will ensure that the money goes to existing transit, and debt reduction which will save millions of dollars in interest. The MPO Finance Committee recommended that the Utah County Commission enact the 4th quarter cent sales tax as soon as the majority of the cities and towns (13) within Utah County pass resolutions supporting the County Action and approve the Interlocal Agreement with the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) for UTA’s portion of the 4th quarter cent sales tax go to reimburse the 3rd cent for past and future BRT expenses as outlined in the Interlocal Agreement and further that the opinion question not be placed on the ballot November 2018. Concerns were raised about a public vote to approve the 4th quarter cent sales tax because of its complexity and it getting overlooked if there is a large number of items on the ballot.
