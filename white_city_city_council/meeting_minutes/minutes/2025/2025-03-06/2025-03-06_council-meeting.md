2.       Discuss White City Master Drainage Plan

      Colton Smith, Michael Cope and Justin Jeppson from JUB were present to discuss the
White City Master Drainage Plan.

       Michael Cope discussed the White City Master Drainage Plan. He discussed how the
drainage flows as well as their assessment of the gutters throughout the city. Those gutters in
poor condition are on a list for replacement. Overall, the existing storm drain is sufficient for the
City. He said they did not do a specific assessment of the Sandy irrigation canal because it‘s not
maintained so the water capacity varies. He said they looked at the spread of the gutters and
how far the water goes into the streets. They also looked at travel lanes which all looked
acceptable. They looked at some key areas such as Peony Way and Zinia Way and the eastern
side of Sego Lily. They want to keep the stormwater out of the traffic area. Sego Lily has a larger
shoulder so there is more area for the stormwater.

4.    Discuss Justice Court Services for White City

      Council Member Tyler Huish said this one also came up when he was reviewing previous
meeting minutes. He was curious as to how things work for White City and the justice court.

        Paul Ashton said now that the unincorporated areas in the valley are disappearing,
residents in unincorporated Salt Lake County will be less than 1000 residents. Salt Lake County
has petitioned the legislature to abolish the County Justice Court which will take place in 2027.
White City can look at joining another justice court at another city such as Midvale since White
City shares that police precinct. He felt it would be a good idea to change the codes to be heard
by an administrative law judge. Any criminal matters would be sent to the District court so a
justice court may not be needed. He felt that would be a faster approach.

       Paul Ashton said last month he brought before the Council amendments to Title 8 of the
White City Municipal Code. He redrafted the ordinance and reviewed the redlined changes in
the ordinance regarding animals in White City. He said the ordinance is moving the violations
to an administrative law judge. It also allows for photographic evidence of a loose animal. He
said most of the changes were drafted by Smith Hartvigsen who has been hired to work on this
code.

       6.3    CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2025-O-03 AN ORDINANCE SETTING
              FORTH THE ELECTION PROCESS FOR WHITE CITY’S FIVE-MEMBER
              FORM OF GOVERNMENT PURSUANT TO UTAH CODE SECTION 10-1-
              201.5(H.G. 35-2024 LEGISLATIVE SESSION) AND TO ENACT
              CONFORMING EDITS TO TITLE 2 OF THE WHITE CITY MUNICIPAL
              CODE TO COMPLY WITH THIS ORDINANCE

       Paul Ashton said this ordinance is setting forth the election process for White City’s five
member form of government. The mayor stands at large and whoever stands as mayor has to
run for election in November. Two years ago, the Council looked at having the Council run with
specific seats similar to Copperton. At that time, there were questions, so changes were not
made. In looking at it again, he felt it is appropriate for people to know who is running for which
seat.

       Council Member Cardenaz said when this was brought up last time, he was leaning for it,
not seeing why they wouldn’t do it. If the mayor seat is specified, the other seats should be as
well. However, if you were in one council seat and there was another council seat open, it
wouldn’t make sense to run for another council seat.

        Council Member Huish said he was against this proposal because it doesn’t align with the
city size, especially with the culture and inclusion of volunteerism. He said he only sees this as
a way to target another individual. The Council represents the city as a whole and thus the voice
of the election of councilmembers should also be as a whole. He said participation in elections
is already low. He didn’t feel this ordinance will communicate inclusion in the community. Having



                       White City Council Meeting Minutes – Page 5
two individuals up for election each year is very clear and can be communicated to residents to
vote for two individuals.

      Council Member Shelton said he disagreed. People who are getting the most votes are
the most popular, not necessarily the right choice. He said it is worthwhile proceeding with the
new format. He said it is more fair to have a standard election process.

      Council Member Huish said they are voting for an office, not a person. Calling the seats
out means there are separate powers. He said this doesn’t make sense to him.

      Council Member Huish motioned to table item this to send back that 2.40.2 is
removed and it goes back to the way we currently have it and bring it back for
consideration at the next council meeting. The motion died for lack of second.

       Council Member Price said she somewhat agreed with Council Member Huish on this
issue. However, she would be okay either way. She said she does see the other side of it.

     Paul Ashton said there is a typographical error in the Ordinance, but minor changes can
be made after the adoption.

      Council Member Shelton, seconded by Council Member Cardenaz, motioned to
approve Ordinance No. 2025-O-03 An Ordinance setting forth the Election Process for
White City’s Five-Member Form of Government pursuant to Utah Code Section 10-1-
201.5(H.B. 35-2024 Legislative Session) and to Enact Conforming Edits to Title 2
of the White City Municipal Code to Comply with this Ordinance as amended with
corrections. The motion passed 4-1 in favor. Council Member Huish against.
