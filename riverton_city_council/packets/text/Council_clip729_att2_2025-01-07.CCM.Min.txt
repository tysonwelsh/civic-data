18            Ordinance No. 25-02 – Sycamore Glen South Rezone, Rezone of approximately
19            1.04-acres located at 118551 South Redwood Road from RR-22 (Residential ½
20            acre) to RM-14-SD
21
22   Mr. Lethbridge presented the rezone application for a property located south of the 11800 South
23   and Redwood Road intersection, currently zoned RR-22. The surrounding area includes the
24   Sycamore Glen development to the north and east, which has a mixed residential designation
25   (SD). To the south, the property is commercially zoned but remains undeveloped, while the
26   property to the north is vacant but approved for 10 townhomes.
27
28   The general plan for the property designates it for professional office use, though this direction
29   has not been followed in the area's primary development. The applicant, involved in the
30   Sycamore Glen development, proposed rezoning the property to RM-14, with a Special
31   Development (SD) overlay, which limits the property to a maximum of 10 units, mirroring the
32   adjacent development. The SD designation includes a stipulation that the parcel size cannot be
33   reduced below one acre, with no more than five contiguous units permitted.
34
35   The Planning Commission recommended approval with the SD designations, including a
36   condition that shared access points with the adjacent commercial property be coordinated and
37   compliant with existing easements. This condition ensures that the shared access is properly
38   addressed in the development process. Staff recommended adding this condition to the zoning
39   approval, which the Planning Commission had already considered.
40
41   Dave Morton, Applicant, expressed gratitude for the support he received throughout the
42   Sycamore Glen project, which began approximately four years ago. He highlighted efforts to
43   listen to neighborhood concerns and assemble four or five parcels while avoiding duplication of
44   services, particularly regarding detention ponds. He emphasized that this collaboration between
45   public services and private industry created a positive outcome and expressed pride in the
46   Sycamore Glen development and upcoming improvements to the detention pond, which will
47   benefit the local residents.
48

18            Ordinance No. 25-03 – Pelaez Rezone, Amending the General Plan Designation
19            ad Zoning for Approximately 0.96-acres located at 3516 West 12600 South from
20            R-1 Residential Zone to C-G (Commercial Gateway) Zone
21
22   Mr. Lethbridge presented the proposed rezone for the property located at 3516 West 12600
23   South, which is just under one acre in size. The property is currently zoned R-1 (single-family
24   residential with a one-acre minimum lot size) and is part of a block with similar zoning. It lies
25   north of 12600 South, east of 3600 West, and adjacent to a mix of regional commercial,
26   professional office, and residential zones. The applicant is requesting a rezone to Commercial
27   Gateway. The area’s general plan designates the property for estate-density residential use,
28   while nearby properties to the south are commercial gateway and professional office, and to the
29   west are regional commercial.
30
31   Mr. Lethbridge noted that City ordinances allow home-based businesses only within owner-
32   occupied homes and with strict limitations on remodeling, employees, and business operations
33   to preserve the residential character. He also addressed concerns about the perception of
34   predetermined outcomes, emphasizing that no outcomes are guaranteed and that all decisions
35   depend on public hearings and final Council votes. The Planning Commission previously held a
36   public hearing on this application and forwarded a recommendation for denial.
37
38   Rob Freeman, the property owner and representative of First Choice Heating and Air, clarified
39   that while the applicant listed is their contractor, the property and business are owned by him.
40   The purpose of the rezone is to allow the business to operate on the property. He outlined his
41   intentions for the site: to use the existing home as an office and meeting space for customers
42   and to construct a storage facility at the rear of the property for equipment and materials such
43   as furnaces and air conditioners. He also addressed concerns about traffic, emphasizing that
44   their operations would generate minimal traffic.
45
46   Councilmember McCay asked how the home would be remodeled. In response, Mr. Freeman
47   compared his vision to the Apex Insurance property nearby, which was remodeled into a
48   functional and attractive business space. He stated his intention to similarly renovate the house
49   on the property, improving its appearance, updating the landscaping, and addressing existing
50   issues like drainage.
51

 1   Mayor Staggs called for public comments;
 2
 3   Marvin Hagen, owner of Hagen Collision, said his primary concern was not the proposed zoning
 4   change but the possibility of further road widening or additional property being taken from
 5   homeowners, which he believes could harm businesses in the area.
 6
 7   Lawrence Sorge expressed concerns about the gradual reduction of R1 zoning (one-acre
 8   residential lots) in Riverton. Referring to the zoning map, he pointed out that there are only a
 9   few light green (R1) zones left in Riverton, with most located near the golf course on the east
10   side of town. He urged the Council to preserve these areas and avoid eroding the character of
11   Riverton's neighborhoods.
12
13   Troy Anderson read a prepared statement emphasizing his love for Riverton and the rural
14   character of his neighborhood, which has been R1 (one-acre residential lots) with specific
15   CC&R restrictions for over 50 years. He stressed that the zoning change is not aligned with the
16   City's general plan and would negatively impact property values and residents' rights. He urged
17   the Council to preserve the general plan and deny the rezone application, referencing the strong
18   opposition expressed by neighbors in attendance, by proxy, and through submitted letters.
19
20   Wendy Onstott expressed her opposition to the proposed zoning change and emphasized
21   concerns about preserving the long-standing, peaceful nature of the neighborhood. She voiced
22   frustration with the idea of gradually "chipping away" at the existing R1 zoning, pointing out that
23   similar attempts have occurred previously and cautioning against setting a precedent for further
24   zoning changes.
25
26   Marcus Wayman described the peaceful nature of the subdivision, encouraging others to visit,
27   ride a bike through the area, and experience its unique atmosphere away from the busy main
28   roads. He emphasized the importance of preserving the neighborhood's character, stating, "God
29   help us all."
30
31   Daniel Pay, the Applicant, shared his perspective as a small business owner and general
32   contractor, explaining that his role is to help make his clients' dreams come true. He noted the
33   rural feel of the area and the intention to keep the property green with minimal concrete,
34   contrasting the commercial properties to the west. He reassured the Council that the goal was
35   to create an aesthetically pleasing space that the community could appreciate.
36
37   Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
38   Hearing.
39
40   Councilmember Pierucci asked if the developer would be required to build a masonry wall if the
41   zone change was approved. Mr. Lethbridge said the ordinance mandates that in situations
42   where incompatible uses are adjacent, such as commercial next to single-family residential, a
43   solid masonry fence is required around the project area. In the current case, if the zoning is
44   approved to commercial, the requirement for solid masonry fencing on three sides of the
45   property would remain in place.
46
47   Councilmember Buroker expressed concern about lighting when commercial property is
48   adjacent to residential. Mr. Lethbridge explained that the updated code includes restrictions on
49   lighting for commercial developments located near single-family residential areas. Specifically,
50   for any lighting facing residential properties—whether to the north, east, or west—there are time
51   limits on when those lights can be on. Although the amendment initially addressed holiday
     Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     January 7, 2025




 1   lighting, the restrictions apply to all lighting facing residential areas, limiting its duration
 2   throughout the day.
 3
 4   Councilmember McCay explained her vote in favor of the proposed rezone by emphasizing the
 5   minimal impact on traffic, citing data showing that the property is located on a road with 30,000
 6   cars per day, while nearby homes contribute a very small portion of that traffic. She also
 7   highlighted the importance of respecting personal property rights, noting that neighbors should
 8   not have a say in how someone uses their property as long as it does not negatively impact
 9   others. She also pointed out the economic benefits of the rezone, as it would help increase the
10   City’s sales tax base, and noted that Riverton has a history of supporting businesses.
11   Ultimately, she believed that approving the rezone now, rather than waiting for a general plan
12   update, was the best course of action to support the City’s growth and respect property rights.
13
14   Councilmember Haymond expressed concerns about the potential issues of spot zoning,
15   particularly the impact it could have on the integrity of Riverton’s general plan. He cautioned that
16   allowing spot zoning could lead to fractured development, creating isolated pockets of
17   businesses and making the City’s growth more disjointed. This could lead to complications with
18   traffic flow, property access, and future planning. Ultimately, he advocated for addressing
19   development from a broader perspective rather than making piecemeal changes that could have
20   unintended consequences.
21
22   Councilmember McDougal began by thanking the community for their continued involvement in
23   Riverton’s development and emphasized the importance of maintaining the City’s integrity
24   through a well-established general plan and zoning. He stressed that zoning provides a
25   framework that allows for organized, coherent growth, contrasting it with the hodgepodge
26   development seen in other cities that lack a clear structure. He pointed out that when residents
27   invest in Riverton, they do so with the expectation of living in areas designated for specific types
28   of development, such as residential or commercial. Changing zoning from residential to
29   commercial could disrupt this balance, diminish property rights, and lead to inconsistent
30   development that could harm the community's overall appeal and property values. He
31   concluded by reaffirming the importance of respecting Riverton’s master plan, supporting the
32   residents who have already invested in the City, and ensuring that future development aligns
33   with the City’s vision for the next 5-10 years.
34
35   Councilmember Pierucci acknowledged the distinction between properties set back from 12600
36   South and those directly facing it, noting the unique challenges of living along a road designated
37   as a state highway with heavy traffic. While agreeing with the value of adhering to the general
38   plan, he argued that the Council should not delay action on something likely to be addressed in
39   the upcoming general plan review, particularly when a willing investor is prepared to contribute
40   to the City’s economic growth. He highlighted the City's efforts to support businesses while
41   maintaining protections for residents, including requirements for masonry fencing and lighting
42   regulations to mitigate the impact of commercial developments on adjacent residential areas.
43   He stated his intent to vote in favor of the proposal.
44
45   Councilmember Buroker acknowledged the challenges of living in homes directly facing 12600
46   South, suggesting that expecting those homes to remain residential long-term might be
47   unrealistic. She expressed a preference for waiting to address these changes during the general
48   plan review rather than proceeding now and said she would vote against the proposal.
49
50   Councilmember McDougal prefaced his motion by highlighting key distinctions between a recent
51   general plan change on Redwood Road and the current proposal. He explained that the
     Page | 8 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     January 7, 2025




 1   Redwood Road change maintained residential use, merely altering density, which acted as a
 2   buffer between different residential zones. In contrast, the current proposal represented a
 3   significant shift from residential to commercial use, which he argued would disrupt the
 4   established character and feel of the neighborhood. He expressed concern about treating the
 5   general plan update as a predetermined process favoring commercial development, without
 6   adequate consultation with residents.
 7
 8   Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council DENY Ordinance No. 25-03 –
 9   Pelaez Rezone, Amending the General Plan Designation ad Zoning for Approximately
10   0.96-acres located at 3516 West 12600 South from R-1 Residential Zone to C-G
11   (Commercial Gateway) Zone. Councilmember Buroker SECONDED the motion. Mayor
12   Staggs called for discussion on the motion.
13
14   Councilmember Pierucci clarified his stance on the general plan, emphasizing that he was not
15   asserting a predetermined outcome. He referred to the Planning Commission's prior
16   recommendation, where the majority supported transitioning high-traffic corridors to
17   commercial use, including the area in question. He argued that delaying the decision for six
18   months only to likely reach the same conclusion would be inefficient. He emphasized the role
19   of elected representatives in making decisions based not only on resident input but also on
20   broader information and citywide considerations. He expressed optimism about upcoming
21   conversations with residents in each district, emphasizing how this input will shape future
22   discussions on the general plan while acknowledging the need for other forms of learning and
23   deliberation.
24
