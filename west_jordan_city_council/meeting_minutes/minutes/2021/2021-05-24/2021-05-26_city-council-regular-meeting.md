CALL TO ORDER
Chair Jacob called the work session to order at 5:30 p.m., and noted the meeting was open to the
public in Council Chambers, available remotely via Zoom, and streamed live on YouTube.

A.     West Jordan City Budget Discussion
Council Member Worthen proposed an amendment to the FY2022 Budget to include $50,000 for a
gurney lift for the Fire Department. Council Member Whitelock expressed support and proposed
adding gloves for crossing guards to the budget. The Council and Staff discussed funding the two
requests. Fire Chief Maxfield explained the need for the gurney lift. There appeared to be no
objection to allocating $1,500 for gloves and $50,000 for a gurney lift in the FY2022 Budget.

Vice Chair Green expressed the opinion that the City should improve the appearance of the old
sexton office on 1300 West. Public Works Director Brian Clegg responded that the building was not
utilized by the City and should probably be torn down. He suggested including demolition with the
planned 1300 West project in a year or two.

Ms. Steck presented proposed amendments to the FY2022 Budget. She explained changes to the
Water, Sewer, Storm Water, and Fleet Fund Budgets, explained changes to the Consolidated Fee
Schedule, and showed minor corrections made to the Tentative Budget. Responding to a question
from Vice Chair Green, Ms. Steck explained transportation funding from the State.

Ms. Steck shared a spreadsheet with General Fund revenues and expenditures, and showed how
changes in property tax, sales tax, and development fee revenues would affect the General Fund.
Chair Jacob said he preferred an inflation-based or fixed percentage property tax increase and
emphasized that if the City did not implement a property tax increase it would dig itself into a
financial hole that would require a big property tax increase down the road. He said he believed the
City needed to be better at explaining in public communications how property taxes worked. Chair
Jacob said he wanted to open Truth-in-Taxation to put policy in place.

Chair Jacob left the work session at 6:16 pm

Council Member Whitelock said she agreed with Chair Jacob that communication with the residents
was important and suggested the Council should direct Ms. Steck to change the FY2022 Budget to
reflect the intended property tax increase. She emphasized that a lot of public education was needed
and suggested including money for public education in the budget.

Vice Chair Green stated he was not opposed to opening Truth-in-Taxation to look at numbers. He
said he would like a policy for inflationary costs for licenses and permits as well as property tax.
Vice Chair Green asked if the City had ever considered creating a West Jordan City Police District
and a West Jordan City Fire District to create independent tax rates for Police and Fire and increase
government transparency. Chief Administrator Korban Lee responded he and Ms. Steck had long
discussions about different revenue options and potential policy impacts of different approaches.

Council Member McConnehey said he was not opposed to a property tax increase in FY2022, and
not opposed to revisiting the property tax rate each year. However, he said he believed a review
should occur, and would not want to tie the increase to a flat percent or CPI measurement. He
suggested a 1% or 2% increase in FY2022 and suggested putting a portion of the revenue toward
agenda management software. Council Member McConnehey said he believed it was a mistake to
cut agenda management software from the budget.

Council Member Pack said he concurred with Council Member McConnehey on both items, and said
he believed agenda management software would help both the Council Office and the Executive
Branch. He said he believed it was important for costs to reflect an inflationary indexed price. He
said he agreed the rate could adjust every year according to the economy but said he would want
policy to be that the rate would mirror what inflation was doing to costs.

Council Member Worthen said she would be fine with a 2% or 2.5% increase in FY2022, and said
she believed the Council needed to implement something instead of kicking the can down the road.
She agreed that prices went up over time, and the City needed to be able to provide services and
keep West Jordan looking good. Council Member Worthen said she liked Ms. Steck’s suggestion of a
flat annual rate increase. Council Member Pack commented that the long-term cost of deferred
maintenance needed to be considered.

Council Member Whitelock said she believed estimated property tax growth of 2% was pretty
aggressive and suggested estimating 0.5% property tax growth and a 1.5% property tax increase.
She stated she was not comfortable with a flat rate increase or with tying the increase to the CPI
every year. Council Member Whitelock said she agreed West Jordan needed to be able to continue
providing services.

Council Member McConnehey suggested property tax growth might be around 2% in the next few
years considering planned development in the City. Mr. Lee added that the rate of growth would be
largely determined by how quickly the City could build water and sewer infrastructure to support
the planned growth. Responding to a question from Mr. Lee, Ms. Steck confirmed the projection
model she put together did not include an increase in the number of City employees.

The Council discussed the potential purchase of agenda management software, with Council
Member Worthen clearly in favor of removing the purchase from the FY2022 Budget, and Council
Member McConnehey clearly opposed to removing the purchase from the budget.

Ms. Steck asked how many Council Members were in favor of allowing Staff to move forward with
preparing information regarding a potential 2% property tax increase in FY2022. She emphasized
that a final decision to implement the Truth-in-Taxation process would not be made until June 23,
2021.

Council Member Whitelock said she was in favor of preparing information and allowing the public
to chime in. She said she was in favor of budgeting funds for public education materials. Ms. Steck
said she agreed and said the cost of education materials tended to be around $1 per resident. She
suggested budgeting $25,000. Council Member McConnehey questioned the level of detail
necessary in public education materials. Ms. Steck responded that the West Jordan Budget
document included a lot of detail and explanation to help citizens understand.

Vice Chair Green asked if the Council could discuss paying for agenda management software out of
the IT Reserve Fund. Ms. Steck confirmed the use of funds would be allowed. She suggested the IT
Management Fund as another funding option. Council Member Whitelock said she felt they were
growing the Council Office very quickly without allowing time to see what worked and what the
actual needs were. Council Member Worthen said she had understood from explanations that the
software would save the Council Office a significant amount of time and was surprised with the
recent request for an additional employee in the Council Office. She said she would prefer to see
how things went with one or the other but would not want to initiate both at the same time. Council
Member Whitelock said she agreed. Council Member Worthen commented that she would prefer to
start with the software but pointed out that Council Office Director Alan Anderson expressed a
preference for an additional employee.

Council Member Pack commented that council offices in cities of a similar size seemed to have more
employees, as well as agenda management software. He said it was his understanding that the
agenda management software in conjunction with one additional employee would help both the
Council Office and the Executive Branch. He said he wanted the Council Office to be proactive in the
ability to have good policy and analytics. Council Member Pack suggested the Council should also
have their own legal representation. He expressed the opinion the Council Office had been running
too lean.

Council Member McConnehey said he believed agenda management software would free up time in
the Council Office. He suggested the Council leave funds for an additional employee in the Budget,
but start with the software, and authorize the additional employee later in the fiscal year if needed.

Vice Chair Green said Mr. Anderson did mention he would prefer an additional employee over the
software if he had to choose just one. He expressed the opinion the Council was being too
shortsighted and suggested they should authorize an additional employee and funds for agenda
management software. He commented that currently under the new form of government, the
Council was supported by one staff member, where the Council had support of three staff members
and the City Manager under the previous form of government. Vice Chair Green said he would
choose an additional employee if he had to pick just one of the options.

Council Member Pack commented that agenda management software involved a one-time expense
but funding an additional staff member involved an expense year after year. He stated he would
trust Mr. Anderson’s judgement, and would choose adding a new staff member over the software if
the Council had to choose one or the other.

        b. Amending City Code Sections affecting Public Notice Requirements
City Recorder Tangee Sloan stated proposed Ordinance No. 21-17 was prepared to amend sections
of City Code affecting public notice requirements. During the 2021 Legislative Session, SB 201 was
passed, removing newspaper public noticing requirements. During a recent Special Session, SB
1007 was passed to amend SB 201. Assistant City Attorney Duncan Murray explained recommended
amendments to City Code. He explained that SB 1007 set the number of election notices required to
be posted in a city to a maximum of ten.

        d. Landscaping Ordinance Text Amendment Update
The public hearing for proposed Landscaping Ordinance Text Amendments was continued at the
previous meeting to June 9, 2021. Vice Chair Green explained this item was included on the agenda
to discuss any updates made since the last meeting.

Council Member Whitelock expressed concern with the stated ability (under Exceptions) of the
Zoning Administrator to waive or reduce the width of a required landscape buffer if one already
existed. Council Member McConnehey said he appreciated the concern and requested Staff
response. He suggested replacing “Zoning Administrator” with “City Council”.

City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner said he was not sure the particular situation
would ever be applicable. He said he understood the concern. Mr. Gardner commented that most
final site plans would go to the Planning Commission and suggested changing authority to the
Planning Commission. Chair Jacob said he liked the suggestion to change review authority to the
Planning Commission, and requested Staff make the change before the next Council meeting.
Council Member McConnehey suggested adding the language “unless otherwise required by
ordinance or resolution”. City Attorney Rob Wall responded the addition would accomplish what
was desired. Vice Chair Green suggested limiting the ability to reduce a buffer by a certain percent
