Torres Derek Burton Barry McLerran DISCUSSION & AGENDA ITEMS Call to Order (00:00:13) Chair Jered Johnson opened the meeting at 1:30 pm. Minutes - Action (00:02:40) Alma Haskell moved to approve the minutes from January 6, 2025. Brad Kenison seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Orem 1600 N/400 E Roundabout - Scope Change (00:03:21) Bob Allen presented modifications to the Orem City roundabout project at 1600 North and 400 East. The project, originally awarded in 2018 to improve safety at the intersection, was found to be ineffective after further analysis by Orem City. Orem City now requests to use the funds for short-term active transportation improvements and to further develop a grade-separated crossing alternative, either an overpass or underpass. This would not require new funding, but additional funds may be needed if the grade-separated crossing is determined to be the best solution. There was some discussion around the motion, with concerns about implying funding for a study versus just design work. The motion was amended to include a feasibility study and short-term improvements, and it passed with a roll call vote. Jeremy Lapin moved to recommend that the MPO Board approve modifications to the Orem 600 north, 400 East roundabout project to include short term, pedestrian intersection, improvements and development, and an alternative study for a grade separated pedestrian crossing. Richard Nielson seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Page 2 of 4 UTA Park & Ride Lots EM/SS - Additional Funding (00:00:00) Bob Allen presented UTA's request regarding federal funding and park and ride projects. UTA had received $4.685 million in federal funds for two new park and ride lots in Saratoga Springs and Eagle Mountain. Both Park and Ride lots would be along Pony Express. For the Eagle Mountain project, UTA requested to add a hawk signal to improve safety, increasing the project cost to $2.2 million. For the Saratoga Springs project, the location of the park and ride lot was determined after the project was awarded. UTA requested to transfer $1.3 million in previously awarded but unobligated federal funds to these park and ride projects to avoid losing the funding. There was some discussion and concern raised by Lehi about the selected location for the Saratoga Springs park and ride, suggesting a Pioneer Crossing location may be better. UTA and Utah County explained the site selection process and the benefits of the current location for bus service. Brad Kenison moved to recommend the MPO Board approve the adding funds from PINs 19098, 17052, and 18986 totaling $1,339,274, to the Eagle Mountain and Saratoga Springs Park and Ride Lots Project and modifying the scope to add a HAWK signal provided that Lehi and Saratoga Springs would concur on an acceptable park and ride site. Alma Haskell seconded the motion with a roll call vote of 4 Ayes and 11 Nay; the motion failed. Alma Haskell moved to recommend the MPO Board approve the adding funds from PINs 19098, 17052, and 18986 totaling $1,339,274, to the Eagle Mountain and Saratoga Springs Park and Ride Lots Project and modifying the scope to add a HAWK signal at Eagle Mountain and that UTA and Saratoga Springs would consider alternative locations should Saratoga Spring and Lehi find one that suitable; if not it will move go ahead at the existing location in Saratoga Springs, and UTA will work with Lehi on the routing of buses to service the areas. Aaron Wilson seconded the motion with a roll call vote of 9 Ayes and 6 Nays; the motion passed. Maps of the Quarter (00:56:09) Matthew Silski presented two new maps developed by the MAG analytics group - the MAG Active Transportation Map and the TIP Dashboard. The MAG Active Transportation Map shows existing and planned paved trails and bike lanes in the MAG region, with data updated from 2022. It includes features like categorized bike lanes, regional and local trails, trail counters, and planned projects from various sources. Matthew Silski highlighted how this map can help identify trail gaps and coordinate planning efforts. The TIP Dashboard allows users to filter and view transportation improvement projects in Utah County by various criteria, such as project type, status, funding agency, and location. Matthew Silski demonstrated how the Page 3 of 4 dashboard can be used to quickly find information on projects in specific cities, projects funded by MAG, and highway projects not yet under construction in certain cities. Participants asked about the process for updating the local network data on the Active Transportation Map, and Matthew Silski confirmed that cities can email shape files or provide live GIS links to have the data updated. He encouraged continued communication. Overall, these new maps and data resources are intended to support planning efforts and provide quick access to project information for the MAG region.
