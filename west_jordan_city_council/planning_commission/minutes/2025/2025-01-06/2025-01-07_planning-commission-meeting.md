7.     Novva- WJ Power Generation; 6524 West Old Bingham Highway; Preliminary Major
       Subdivision Plat (2 lot on 27.65 acres); M-1 Zone; Novva Data Centers/Chandler Swenson
       (applicant) [#33294; parcel 26-10-276-005]

Chandler Swenson, applicant with Novva Data Centers, said they are a data center operating in West
Jordan and five other western states. There are now three data center buildings in West Jordan. There
will eventually be a fourth data center, but this application deals with a subdivision and construction of
a power generation station for their business. As referenced in the staff report, the proposal follows all
applicable city codes.

Jimmy Anderson disclosed a potential conflict of interest since Novva is a client of his law firm.

Tayler Jensen said Planning Commission approval is required for the subdivision because
approximately 3500 square feet of property is being dedicated to West Jordan for road expansion. Final
plat approval and preliminary and final site plan approvals are all administrative.

Based on the analysis contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission
approve the Preliminary Major Subdivision for Novva West Jordan Power Generation located on
approximately 27.65 acres at 6524 West Old Bingham Highway, with the conditions and requirements
of approval listed in the staff report.

Conditions and Requirements of Approval
1.     Address and correct all redline comments generated by City during Final subdivision reviews.

Trish Hatch asked if this data center is the type that uses water.

Tayler Jensen clarified that this lot will be for power generation, but he thought that all of the Novva
data centers were the type that do not use water.

Ammon Allen opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

MOTION:        Trish Hatch moved to approve a Preliminary Major Subdivision for Novva West

8.     Apollo Burger; 7134 South Redwood Road; Preliminary Site Plan and Conditional Use
       Permit for Restaurant, Fast Food; SC-2 Zone; Tracy Stocking Architect (applicant)
       [#34138; parcel 2127127002)

Tracy Stocking, architect, said that Apollo Burger will be moving from their current site and
constructing a new, modem building. The proposed site plan and photos of a newly constructed
building nearly identical to the proposal were shown. The drive-through scenario is similar to what was
used with the bank previously operating on the property. He stated that the code requirements had been
met. All parking requirements are met, and they have worked carefully on the ingress and egress
layout. The current building on this site will be demolished and everything on the site will be new.

Tayler Jensen said the application is for approval of a site plan and a conditional use permit for the
drive-through component. Six stacking spaces in the drive-through lane are required and seven are
provided. Likewise, 32 parking stalls are required and the site will have 39. The building placement is
closer to Redwood Road than the current building, which is preferred. The elevations are modem and
attractive. The Design Review Committee at their meeting gave a recommendation for approval as
submitted. Commercial zoning on the property allows for this use.

Based on the analysis contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission
approve the Conditional Use Permit for Restaurant, Fast Food (General) and the Preliminary Site Plan
for Apollo Burger located on approximately 0.92 acres at 7134 South Redwood Road, with the
conditions and requirements of approval listed in the staff report.

Conditions and Requirements of Approval: There are no conditions of approval suggested by staff
because staff had not identified any reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of this use.

Tom Hollingsworth said that the drive-through lane exit is close to the entrance to the parking lot. He
wondered if the queue could back up into traffic coming into the parking lot.

Tayler Jensen said that the city traffic engineer had reviewed the layout and after some changes to the
site plan he felt comfortable with the layout. Staff did not have concerns about the location causing
traffic to back up into the parking lot, because Apollo Burger does not usually generate those kinds of
queues. Also, the shopping center does not have big traffic draws that would cause a conflict.

Ammon Allen opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

MOTION:        John Roberts moved to approve a Conditional Use Permit for Restaurant, Fast
               Food (General) for Apollo Burger on approximately 0.92 acres at 7134 South
               Redwood Road subject to all requirements of approval. The motion was seconded
               by Tom Hollingsworth and passed 6-0 in favor. Emily Gonzalez was absent.

MOTION:        John Roberts moved to approve a Preliminary Site Plan for Apollo Burger located
               at 7134 South Redwood Road, subject to all requirements of approval. The motion
               was seconded by Tom Hollingsworth and passed 6-0 in favor. Emily Gonzalez was
               absent.

9.     Wasatch Electric Rezone; approximately 9000 South 6800 West; Rezone 55.35 acres from
       P-F (Public Facilities), SC-2 (Community Shopping Center), MFR (Medium Density,
       Multi-Family Residential), and LSFR (Low Density, Single-Family Residential) Zones to
       SWQ-MU (Southwest Quadrant - Mixed Use) Zone and Master Development Agreement,
       Master Development Plan; Gardner Group/Ben Seastrand and ARCO Design Build/Reed
       Hart & Jessica Escobar (applicant) [#33805; parcel 26-03-326-001; 26-03-300-008]

Ben Seastrand, applicant, said explained that Wasatch Electric is relocating their headquarters from
Salt Lake City to West Jordan, and there will be about 500 employees working out of this office. With
the large amount of vacant land in the area, the city wanted to see a cohesive plan for 55 acres of
property that incorporates the smaller Wasatch Electric site. He stated that the concept plan for future
development around Wasatch Electric preserves the frontage along 9000 South for retail/mixed use
buffering.

Tayler Jensen explained that the future land use designation is Southwest Quadrant. There are
currently several zones on this property, which he explained. The SWQ-MU zoning district meets the
future land use plan for this property and helps to clear up some existing zoning categories that are no
longer used. The proposed zoning also has subzones within it. Two areas at the north are COMU
subzones, which are more retail and residential based. The rest of the site is HTME, which has more
high-tech industrial uses and is where Wasatch Electric will be located. He referred to the concept plan
that showed commercial and residential COMU pods. A total of 117 dwelling units are allowed in the
lower COMU area and the pod designated as retail could have up to 4 7 dwelling units and would cap
this Wasatch Electric area at a total of 164 dwelling units. These figures and placement will be looked
at more closely with site plan and plat review. Staff wanted to allow flexibility to change the density
between the two areas if needed, but the overall cap is 164 dwelling units and no pod can have more
than 20 dwelling units per acre. Being an economic development priority, staff anticipated the site plan
and subdivision plat coming to the Planning Commission quickly, if the rezoning is approved.

Based on the analysis contained in the report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission make
a recommendation of approval (positive recommendation) to the City Council for the Master
Development Agreement/Plan. Staff recommended that the Planning Commission make a
recommendation of approval (positive recommendation) to the City Council for the proposed rezone.

Staff recommended that the following Requirements of Approval be included with any motion that
recommends approval of the Wasatch Electric development to the City Council.

Master Development Agreement/Plan:
1.     The Maximum Dwelling Units in the development is 164.
2.     No development pod that contains residential development may have a density greater than 20
       DU/AC.

Rezone
1.     Wasatch Electric Master Development Agreement (MDA) shall be approved prior to approving
       the Rezone.

2.     The Planning Commission shall recommend approval, denial, or modifications to the proposed
       design standards.
3.     No further piecemeal SWQ-MU rezones will be considered.

Jay Thomas asked if the type of multi-family structure is known at this time.

Tayler Jensen said it is unknown because the road layout is not determined. The applicant has updated
the concept plan with less detail that only shows the pod area.

Jimmy Anderson asked for clarification regarding how residential buildings would be incorporated into
the future retail buildings. He asked if the density could be all in one pod.

Tayler Jensen said the pod would be flex use, which allows retail and residential in the same building
or with horizontal separation. However, they do not have to include residential within the pod. As the
concept is currently drawn, there could only be 117 units on the multi-family pod because of the 20
dwelling units per acre maximum density. Otherwise, they would have to make that pod larger. He
explained the condition that no further piecemeal SWQ-MU rezoning will be considered. Gardner and
their partners are in control of approximately 900 unbuilt acres. Instead of reviewing smaller
rezonings, the ordinance is designed to look at the full acreage to see where the different subzones will
be and then the individual subdivisions and site plans will provide the details in each of the pods.

Ammon Allen said he would like the planning commission to received training on the SWQ zone prior
to hearing the request to rezone the remaining SWQ acreage.

Jay Thomas asked how this piece corresponds with the Jones Ranch master development plan.
Tayler Jensen said the master development plan and agreement they are reviewing tonight is only for
55 acres. The Jones Landing north master plan was used as a template for this project.

Ammon Allen opened the public hearing.
