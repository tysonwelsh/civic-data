6.0   Action Item: Resolution #22-33, a resolution stating the intent of Draper City
      to hold a public hearing on the request to adjust the common boundary line
      with Lehi City. This municipal boundary adjustment would relocate
      approximately 1,035.49 acres of land from Draper City into Lehi City. The
      properties are parcel numbers 110-150-007, 110-150-008, 110-150-002, 110-
      300-024, 110-300-025, 110-300-018, 110-300-066, and 110-140-004

6.1   David Dobbins, City Manager, explained that the boundary between Lehi and
      Draper would change and this resolution initiates the process to hold a public
      hearing to give the public an opportunity for the public to comment on this
      boundary change.
6.2   Scott Bishop with D.R. Horton explained that D.R. Horton had purchased land
      adjacent to the southern border of Draper City, and owned land in Lehi City
      directly across the municipal border. D.R. Horton approached the City about
      adjusting the municipal boundary with Lehi City to relocate approximately
      1,035.49 acres from Draper City to Lehi City. D.R. Horton was looking to
      develop the 1,035.49 acres as part of a larger development within Lehi City.

6.3   Councilmembers expressed an interest in taking a tour of the property.
      Responding to a question from the Council, Mr. Bishop said the intention was
      to include a trail system on the property.

6.3   Councilmember F. Lowry moved to approve                  Resolution     #22-33.
      Councilmember Green seconded the motion.

6.4   A roll call vote was taken. The motion passed unanimously.

10.0   Action Item: Resolution #22-35, a Resolution adopting the 2022 Fraud Risk
       Assessment for the City of Draper.

10.1   Finance Director Bob Wylie explained the State Auditor’s Office implemented
       a Fraud Risk Assessment to be annually reviewed, completed and presented
       to city councils. The assessment was designed to help measure and reduce the
       risk of undetected fraud, abuse, and noncompliance in local governments. For
       FY 2022, Draper City scored in the “low risk” category.
