COMMISSIONER POWERS GARDNER - ANNOUNCED THE MAY 2023 EMPLOYEE OF
THE MONTH, TAD RASMUSSEN (Read Nomination)

I would like to nominate Tad Rasmussen for Employee of the Month. I first met Tad
when we worked together in 2001. Back then, he worked as an Accounting Clerk, quietly
going about his business. He was thorough, prompt, kind and always a team player. I
was happy to find Tad again when I returned to the Auditor’s Office 20 years
later. Once again, I found Tad quietly going about his business. His humble demeanor
makes him approachable. His dedication to the County and its processes means he is a
wealth of knowledge and a great teacher. His institutional knowledge makes him
irreplaceable.

Over the last year or so, Tad has helped me with SO many questions. In the
beginning, he was a wonderful and patient trainer. He was always well prepared and
provided so many additional resources to help me become an independent worker and
good employee. Later, I would see him simply passing by my desk. He was always quick
with a joke or some tidbit to make us smile. As I transitioned away from the Auditor’s
office, Tad was a lifeline. I called him about a range of topics and peppered him with
questions Do you know who does __? Do you know what so and so’s name is? Do you
know how to___? Where do |__? Even when I didn’t want to follow instructions, Tad
was kind, patient, and supportive, saying “I know it’s hard, but that’s the way we have
to do it.” He would repeat this in a calm voice until I got the message.

Tad is truly a humble servant of the taxpayers. He is always willing to help and go out of
his way to move processes forward. I would love to thank him publicly for his years of
dedication and kindness. ~ Monica Critchlow — Program Coordinator, ARPA/Budget

Tad has been helpful to our team. He has made training materials available,
continued to coach through rough spots, and followed up to make sure we understood.
He continues to recommend resources, most recently helping us with travel. He is kind
and prompt when assisting the grants office with our purchasing department needs —
from contracts to vendor registration and RFP (request for proposal) help.

We appreciate Tad and his institutional knowledge; and would like to nominate him for
Employee of the Month. ~Peter Brown — ARPA Budget Manager

Tad has taken the lead to provide Purchasing training County-wide. He developed
online training in Relias, and he frequently reaches out to County employees to give
them the tools they need to succeed. He has done this through Q&A sessions,
contributions to the Purchasing newsletter, and innumerable phone calls. He doesn’t
hesitate to reach out to whomever is needed to get the answers we need.

Tad reaches out to departments on a regular basis to help them keep track of their
many contracts. He advises them when the contracts are due to expire and helps them
navigate the process to either renew the contract or search for a new alternative.

When departments are looking to make a high dollar purchase, Tad springs into
action to help them find State or cooperative contracts that they can use to facilitate the
purchase. He has a thorough knowledge of available resources, and it is not uncommon
for Tad to find alternatives for departments that give them not only a lower price, but
sometimes a better product that they didn’t know existed.

I receive frequent feedback from grateful employees throughout the County that Tad
has helped with resolving sticky and confusing issues. He is often on the front lines
helping people navigate the complexities of Purchasing. ~ Robert Baxter — Purchasing
Manager —

Tad Rasmussen is a lifesaver. I cannot tell you how many plates he has juggling in the
air at one time, and I cannot tell you how he keeps them all spinning! He is so patient
while training and so kind when correcting.

One of his goals is to make some of the Relias purchasing, travel and P-Card training
clear, concise, and easy to understand. He uses screen shots to help us better follow
along.

He has also created a document for County employees who travel to use for any
anticipated conference or trip they may attend — which helps me (or anyone else) create
their travel request. (It is found under Travel/Help/Travel/Training Request form)

Tad reviews all requisitions from the county prior to approving them. He reviews
each for correct coding, purchasing limits and whether an agreement is needed, accuracy
within the requisition description which in turn often leads to a follow up “are you sure
you want to use that object code?” Of course, if I hear that sentence... I know I “chose
poorly”...

He reviews fund/department/object code based on the person submitting the
requisition. He makes sure that JT or PW review boxes are checked if certain items are
being purchased. If food or meals are involved — he reviews that the correct Food &
Meal policy is referenced and that the appropriate documentation is attached. If an
agreement is referenced — he confirms that the agreement is in force and has been signed
by all parties.

Tad sends out a quarterly newsletter to all those who handle Purchase Orders — these are
fun and informative. He also holds quarterly Q&A meetings where we can ask questions
and make suggestions for improvement. From my own experience — he welcomes those
suggestions or has an answer as to why we haven’t seen that suggestion in practice yet.

He manages the fixed assets program for the county and maintains the fixed asset
database for both new purchases and surplus items.

Tad juggles the many P-Cards that have been issued throughout the county. He takes
calls every day to temporarily increase the daily or monthly spend limits and adjusts
them accordingly. That is an amazing spreadsheet in itself.

Tad always looks for ways to improve and clarify processes and procedures with the
intent to make our job easier!

I truly appreciate the guidance I received as a newbie when I joined the (then)
Clerk/Auditor’s office. His teaching has been invaluable — and I know I can ask a
clarifying question any time. Procurement has never run more smoothly. ~ Maureen
Wengert - Sr. Office Specialist

3. PRESENTATION AND DISCUSSION OF POTENTIAL FLOOD PROTECTION DISTRICT.
-Dale Eyre, Attorney

(Timestamp: 43:50)

DALE EYRE, DEPUTY COUNTY ATTORNEY - MADE NOTE OF POSSIBLE FLOOD
PROTECTION ENTITIES SUCH AS AN INTERLOCAL AGENCY CREATED BY THE
LEGISLATIVE BODY, THIS ENTITY WOULD REQUIRE COUNTIES AND CITIES TO
WORK TOGETHER UNDER AN AGREEMENT. IT WOULD NOT BE A SEPARATE
ORGANIZATION. IT WOULD BE FUNDED BY BUDGETS AND GRANTS. THE
DOWNSIDE TO AN INTERLOCAL AGENCY IS IT REQUIRES A COMMITMENT OF
ALREADY STRETCHED RESOURCES, CONTRACTUAL OBLIGATIONS CAN BE
BREACHED BY SUBSEQUENT POLICY MAKERS, AND IT WOULD REQUIRE CONTRACT
NEGOTIATION AND CONTINUED CONTRACT MAINTENANCE BY LAWYERS. NEXT
OPTION, SPECIAL SERVICE DISTRICT - WHERE THE COUNTY COMMISSION HAS
SUPERVISORY AUTHORITY. IT WOULD BE CREATED BY A RESOLUTION, NOT BY
CONTRACT AND NOT BY VOTE. THE FUNDING WOULD COME FROM THOSE WHO
ARE BENEFITTING FROM FLOOD PROTECTION. BONDS, LEVIES, AND FEES COULD
