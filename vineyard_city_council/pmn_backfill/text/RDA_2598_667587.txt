3. BUSINESS ITEMS
   3.1       DISCUSSION AND ACTION – Train Station Improvements
       Community Development Director Morgan Brim will present possible improvements and
       costs for the FrontRunner Train Station. The RDA Board will take appropriate action.

 Chair Fullmer turned the time over to Community Development Director Morgan Brim.
Page 1 of 5; June 24, 2020 Vineyard Redevelopment Agency Minutes
 Mr. Brim explained that the betterment budget went beyond what Utah Transit Authority (UTA)
 would pay to build on the train station platform, such as artwork, photos, etc.

 He said that the platform would have two (2) canopies with six (6) panels each. Each panel
 section had four (4) side panels, for a total of 12 back panels and 16 end panels. He mentioned
 that Freeform Polymers was the company that helped put the budget together.

 Suggested betterments were:
    • End Panels – a pattern
    • Back Panels - could have maps or photos imbedded
           o The cost would include the printing and imbedding the image. Mr. Brim
               mentioned that they wanted to use a polycarbonate design but UTA used a
               pressed glass. The polycarbonate could be patched if damaged and if the glass
               broke you would have to replace the entire panel. He felt the UTA would not
               allow the polycarbonate. He suggested that the old homestead map could be
               imbedded into the panels.
    • Roof Panel - could have color added to them to create a color splash onto the platform
       when the sun shone through them.
    • Projectors – could give the city an opportunity to advertise events and public
       announcements.
    • Art Installation
    • Footings (4’ x 10’)

 Mr. Brim suggested that they could add the footings now and the artwork later.

 Cost Breakdown:




 Mr. Brim suggested that if they went with the larger number it would give them some leeway to
 get the betterments in place. He also suggested that they could take out the public art sculptures
 and install the footings to lower the cost. He felt that there would only be room for two pieces of
 art. He said that they needed to get an approval tonight, so that the betterments would be
 included in the design now. Mr. McHargue mentioned that they did not need to make a decision
 now, on what art they wanted, but did need to make a decision on the footings, the panels, and
 the projectors so they could move forward with the design.
Page 2 of 5; June 24, 2020 Vineyard Redevelopment Agency Minutes
 Boardmember Judd clarified that they did not need to allocate the money for the artwork now but
 just needed to approve the footings. Mr. McHargue replied yes. Mr. Brim suggested that if they
 wanted something for opening day, that they commission a piece of artwork now. Boardmember
 Judd felt that they should get input from residents to help choose the artwork. They should also
 reach out to artists and local schools to see what they could do. Chair Fullmer agreed. Mr.
 McHargue felt it was a wise direction to get the footings done now. He emphasized that they did
 not have to decide on the artwork for the panels tonight, but needed to approve a budget. Chair
 Fullmer felt that there was a better direction than the art sculptures presented.

 Boardmember Flake asked where the projectors would be placed and what they would project
 onto. Mr. Brim replied that they would be placed on the ends of the canopies and would project
 onto the platform but could be moved. He added that people on the train would be able to see
 what was being projected onto the platform. Boardmember Judd asked how the projectors would
 be protected. Mr. Brim replied that the projector would be imbedded under the panel glass to
 protect it from weather. He added that there would be software included which could be accessed
 from an offsite computer. Mr. McHargue reminded the Board that that they were not making a
 decision on the projector today; they were making a decision on the ability to install a projector.

 Mr. McHargue mentioned that not all of the art pieces would work on the footings. Mr. Brim
 explained that the actual footings would be flush with the top surface of the platform but would
 not have the snowmelt system under it. He said that they would be structural footings to support
 the weight. Artists would submit a proposal to fit the dimensions of the footings.

 Boardmember Welsh suggested that the Heritage Commission could create a subcommittee or
 the RDA Board could create a separate committee. Mr. McHargue recommended that they run
 the committee through the RDA Board.

 Boardmember Welsh felt that it was too much money to add color to the top panels. Mr. Brim
 mentioned that the city would have an opportunity to design the Train Plaza.

 Boardmember Welsh asked for clarification on the motion that needed to be made. Mr.
 McHargue replied that they needed to decide if they were okay with the back, end, and roof
 panels, and the footings. He added that a decision on the rest could be made in the future.

 Chair Fullmer asked if the images in the panels would come back to the RDA for approval. Mr.
 McHargue replied yes.

 Boardmember Welsh said that she liked the back and end panels, the foundation for the art, but
 was not in favor of the roof panels. Mr. Brim explained that the roof panels would be pressed
 clear glass but could be upgraded. There was a discussion about the roof panels, which was
 followed by a discussion about the proposed budget.

Chair Fullmer called for a motion.

Motion: BOARDMEMBER JUDD MOVED TO APPROVE THE COST BREAKDOWN FOR
THE VINEYARD TRANSIT STATION BETTERMENT COSTS, AS PRESENTED,
EXCLUDING THE THREE PUBLIC ART SCULPTURES LISTED AND THE BLUE ROOF
PANELS, HAVING THEM BE CLEAR. BOARDMEMBER WELSH SECONDED THE
MOTION. CHAIR FULLMER, BOARDMEMBERS EARNEST, FLAKE, JUDD, AND WELSH
VOTE AYE. THE MOTION CARRIED UNANIMOUSLY.
