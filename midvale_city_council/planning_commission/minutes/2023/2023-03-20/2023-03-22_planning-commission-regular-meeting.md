GENERAL SESSION

Chair Liedtke called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

STAFF UPDATE

      1. Discussion on Planning Commission Bylaws and Relevant Municipal Code
         Sections
            a. Meetings
                Aaron McKnight, Deputy City Attorney suggests changes to Planning
                Commission By-laws. Including regulations regarding Workshop Meetings
                versus Regular Meetings and making small adjustments to the language to
                match what is currently being done. There is an option for Workshop
                Meetings to be called but Regular Meetings will be held unless otherwise
                stated. Workshop Meetings can also be held in conjunction with Regular
                Meetings if needed so long as they are properly noticed. This will be voted
                upon at the next meeting.

                 Special Meetings can also be called, so long as proper noticing was done and
                 all requirements have been met.

                 Chair Liedtke discusses the ability to recuse oneself in case of a possible
                 conflict with an item (ex parte communication). The Commissioners are able
                 to state that they are not allowed to participate in certain conversation or
                 communication outside of official meetings. This is inclusive of media
                 outreach or social media platforms, personal or city.

            b. Alternates
               Chair Liedtke suggests making a recommendation or suggesting a change to
               the allowed number of terms for Commissioners. There is a concern that terms
               ending could cause a lack of eligible/experienced persons applying for the
               position. The Commissioner in question would still have to be voted into their
               position again by the City Council and would allow the possibility to keep a
               trustworthy member as a vital part of the Commission.

               Adam Olsen, Community Development Director, mentions that this is not an
               amendment that is being brought to City Council at this time but is a topic to
               discuss again in the future if needed or desired.

               Elizabeth Arnold, Senior Planner, mentions her concern for unlimited terms.
               This could limit the chances for new members to take their place serving the
               community. Alternates wouldn’t be able to move up as permanent, voting
               members as often. Ms. Arnold suggests the idea instead to discontinue the use
               of alternates and change the required number of voting members.

               Mr. McKnight mentions that this would be another decision made by the City
               Council.

               Ms. Arnold discusses the discontinuation of alternates could possibly ensure
               each district to be represented by at least one Commissioner of each district.
               Equal representation is an important issue to consider.

               Garrett Wilcox, City Attorney, clarifies that if alternates were discontinued,
               the quorum number would grow. The rule for a quorum would be half plus
               one, or large majority vote. In the case of seven voting members, there would
               have to be four members present to pass/deny any motion.

               Mr. McKnight also clarifies that if the quorum is equal to four voting
               members, all members would have to vote unanimously in order for a motion
               to be approved or denied. This could cause a few odd situations in which a
               neutral recommendation would have to be passed on to the City Council due
               to insufficient votes.

               Wendelin Knobloch, Planning Director, questions the possibility of having a
               quorum at the meeting and a vote failing, if a project could be held back.

               Mr. McKnight answers that depending on the type of motion, this could or
               could not become an issue. All administrative votes should be unanimous,
               barring an explanation and justification for the requirements not being met.

               Planning Commissioners appreciate the consideration of including alternates
               as voting members, however it seems that it can leave the door open for
               several issues. Currently, they are not willing to change the size of the
               quorum.
