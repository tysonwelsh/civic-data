2.0     REVIEW OF BUSINESS MEETING AGENDA – Mayor Gay Lynn Bennion.

Mayor Bennion reviewed the Meeting Agenda and stated that the Pledge of Allegiance would be
led by City Recorder, Tiffany Janzen, followed by the Citizen Comment Period.

Two Action Items were on the agenda. Item 4.1 was consideration of Resolution 2026-16
Approving an Agreement with Perkins Engineering, Inc. for Engineering Services. City Engineer,
Matt Shipp, reported that the agreement was part of the Community Redevelopment and Renewal
Area (“CDRA”) project near 3000 East. CDRA funds need to be expended. Most projects in the
area had been completed, but the intersection where Big Cottonwood Trail crosses 3000 East can
be improved. The crosswalk is heavily used, and they would like to improve safety by adding
lights, a textured crosswalk, bump-outs, etc. The project will be funded solely through the CDRA.

In response to a question raised by Council Member Hyland, Mr. Shipp reported that they also
considered the mid-block crossing near the hotel, but there were not enough remaining funds for
both crosswalks and the Big Cottonwood Trail crossing is much more heavily used by walkers,
joggers, and cyclists. In response to a follow-up question, he confirmed that funds were available
for both engineering and construction.

Council Member Birrell spoke with the Active Transportation Subcommittee and their preference
was for a raised crosswalk. A median or other way to create visual friction would also be
beneficial. Mr. Shipp stated that they were considering all alternatives, but that location is not
ideal for a raised crosswalk because it is at a traffic light. They are also considering a refuge island
and bump-outs. Council Member Birrell recommended that the Active Transportation
Subcommittee be included in discussions related to pedestrian and cyclist safety.

Mayor Bennion stated that she spoke about the project with City Manager, Jared Gerber, and was
happy that the funds were being put to good use.

        3.1    Ridgecrest Elementary/Bella Vista Elementary Update - Principal Sarah
        Allen.

Ridgecrest Elementary School Principal, Sarah Allen, reported that Canyons School District voted
to close Bella Vista Elementary and consolidate the two schools at Ridgecrest, which will result
in changes to the school’s boundaries and send some students to East Midvale Elementary. The
transition team includes principals of both schools, community councils, parent-teacher
organizations, and leadership teams, and is working to mitigate concerns and build unity between
the schools.

At the first transition meeting, concerns were expressed over whether the Bella Vista community
would be welcomed at Ridgecrest. An open house was held the next week, and she believes it was
a turning point in the transition. The transition team next considered events that can begin to build
unity. Children from Bella Vista have begun attending the Ridgecrest skate nights, and they plan
to have joint after-school picnics during the year. There will also be a joint talent show on April
17 at 6:00 p.m. at Butler Middle School.

The transition team also discussed possibly renaming or rebranding the school. However, there
was not enough time to rename it by Fall, and the school board was not interested in renaming it.
Instead, they focused on what the schools have in common. They share school colors, orange and
blue. Both school mascots are tigers, and next year, Ridgecrest will have both Bella and Reggie
the Tiger as mascots. The school board had proposed rebuilding Ridgecrest, and that may be the
time to rename the school.

Ms. Allen stated that school visitors are frequently confused by the street name. The street in front
of the school is 7200 South, but people think of it as Fort Union Boulevard. She then played a
video prepared by the fifth graders proposing that the name be changed to Tiger Cub Lane. If the
proposal was approved, they hoped to coordinate it with the refresh of the school’s branding and
announce it in early Fall.

Council Member Newell appreciated the transition team’s efforts in creating unity.

Council Member Birrell stated that when she first heard about Bella Vista closing, her primary
concern was safety. Parents were disappointed that their children would have to travel further to

turned into a mixed-use town center.
