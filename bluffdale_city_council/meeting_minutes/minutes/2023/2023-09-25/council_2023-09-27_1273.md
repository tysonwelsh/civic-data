Notice Is hereby given that the Bluffdale City Council will hold a meeting on Wednesday, September 27, 2023, at Bluffdale
City Hall, 2222 West 14400 South, Bluffdale, Utah. The meeting will begin at 7:00 PM or as soon thereafter as possible. This
meeting will also be broadcast live to the public at: www.bluffdale.com. The public may comment at the meeting or by
emailing comments to councilmeetingcomment@bluffdale.com by 4:00 PM the day ofthe meeting. Emailed comments will
be submitted to the City Council but will not be read at the meeting. Notice is further given that access to this meeting by
the City Council may be by electronic means.
In the event the meeting is disrupted in any way that the City in its sole discretion deems inappropriate,the City reserves the
right to immediately remove the individual(s)from the meeting and, if needed, end virtual access to the meeting. Reasons
for removing an individual or ending virtual access to the meeting include but are not limited to the posting of offensive
pictures, remarks, or making offensive statements, disrespectful statements or actions, and any other action deemed
inappropriate.

       7.3    Resolution 2023-48-Mountainview Corridor Agreement Amendment2(Staff
              Presenter: Dan TracerX


Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer presented a proposed second amendment to the Betterment
Agreement between the City of Bluffdale and the Utah Department of Transportation("UDOT")
regarding the Mountainview Corridor extension to 2100 North in Lehi. It was noted that the Zone
3 water tank in southern Bluffdale off Redwood Road by the Utah Veterans Cemetery and
Memorial Park will be impacted by the project. UDOT was aware of the tank and will maintain
access to the site at no cost to Bluffdale but the City needs a new water tank to serve the Zone 2
West area on the western edge of Bluffdale. This is an opportunity to get some ofthat work done
now with the Mountainview Corridor Project to make the tank construction and installation easier.


The Betterment Agreement includes a new access road connecting to Redwood Road, significant
grading work,retaining walls, drainage improvements, and water infrastructure relaying with stub
outs to the new tank area. Demolition was expected to begin in one to two fiscal years, with
                                BLUFFDALE CITY COUNCIL
                                      MEETING MINUTES
                                Wednesday,September 27,2023

construction to begin soon thereafter. This amendment will allow them to get work on the water
infrastructure done now while contractors are working in the area.

Council Member Kallas asked ifthe access will only be maintained on Redwood Road. Mr.Tracer
confirmed that this is an even more direct access. Currently, traveling further south and
backtracking is required to get to the City's existing tank. This is a more direct route that will
serve both tanks. Council Member Kallas asked if they would construct a bridge over the access
road. Mr. Tracer clarified that the road will go under Mountainview Corridor.

Council Member Hales moved to APPROVE Resolution 2023-48, Mountainview Corridor

       7.4     14400 South Road Project - Discussion and Consideration (Staff Presenter,
               Dan Tracer).


Before Mr. Tracer began his presentation, Mayor Hall noted that the Engineering Department has
worked tirelessly on the project for months and is excited that it went out to bid earlier in the day.
Mayor Hall thanked the Engineering Department for the countless months of work and stated that
it is more of a sewer project than a road project.


Mr. Tracer confirmed that this is a massive project that will hopefully get underway this winter.
The bid was posted earlier in the day and it will close in about three weeks. A mandatory Pre-Bid
Meeting was scheduled for Wednesday, October 4. All pertinent information was available on
SciQuest. City Manager, Mark Reid reported that although the project has gone out to bid, the
City Council can still make necessary changes.


There is currently a sewer main in 14400 South from the Bluffdale/Herriman border to just past
Redwood Road. The pipe is undersized and does not meet the needs ofgrowth taking place in the
area. The Sewer District will replace the existing 10-inch pipe with an 18-inch pipe from 3600
                                BLUFFDALE CITY COUNCIL

West to 2200 West in front ofCity Hall. The pipe is deep and there will be other impacts that will
require mitigation.


This is a great opportunity for the City to cost share on many ofthe associated costs. There is an
old drinking water line in 14400 South from 2200 West to 3400 West and this project will be an
opportunity to replace it and all ofthe services that go with it. The Public Works Department has
been working diligently to replace older services as they deteriorate.

While the road is torn up, they will also install pressure irrigation mains and fill in some of the
gaps in the system. 14400 South is a main trunk line and they will fill in gaps from 2400 West to
3400 West including stubbing new services into the park strips of all nearby properties. They will
also install new residential-style streetlights along the entire corridor to meet the City standard of
250-foot offset spacing. It will alternate on each side of the road with two at each crosswalk or
intersection. The intent is to beautify the corridor and make it a street to be proud of.

Staff was waiting to hear back from Rocky Mountain Power to work out costs for power
undergrounding and determine if it is a good fit. The rough estimate was $400,000 to $600,000
per mile, however, they are waiting on a more detailed estimate that was expected to come out in
the next few weeks. This will be a separate contract with Rocky Mountain Power and will not
impact this project although it will be done at the same time. The tentative completion date was
July 2025. Mr. Reid reported that they opted to underground the power when City Hall was built.
They were pleased with the result and are now considering undergrounding the lines the entire
length ofthe park.


Council Member Aston commented that without numbers there is nothing to talk about. However,
having open power lines at a park where people fly kites and children play could be an issue. She
was very interested in finding out more.


Mayor Hall considered this to be an opportunity to establish a main street in Bluffdale, which is
something the City doesn't have. She commented that it would be nice to do something unique
and beautiful on the street near City Hall. She was interested in seeing how this type of project

could be financed. Mr. Kartchner reported that they are looking into funding and mentioned that
this is the kind ofproject the Capital Projects Fund provides flexibility for. Mr. Reid reported that
the City will pay $5 million for the project and the Sewer District will pay $10 million. Not all of

Mr. Tracer described the project and stated that the road would be torn up with full-depth
replacement and new asphalt, including the sub-base, which is where most asphalt problems come
from. The entire stretch ofroad will be a high-quality product. They will stay within the existing
right-of-way along the entire stretch. They have worked diligently to ensure that all utilities fit
within the existing right-of-way. There will be Americans with Disabilities Act("ADA")required
ramp and sidewalk replacements installed as needed to maintain compliance.

The estimated cost ofthe project is $5 million with the sewer portion being $10 million. Bluffdale
partnered with them on the 1690 West project as well, which was a success for both parties with
cost savings on both sides.


Council Member Kallas remarked that the Council has been discussing this project for a long time
and he was pleased that it had gone out to bid. He agreed that ifthey can underground the power
lines, they should do as much as they can afford. Mayor Hall commented that it is not often that a
road is torn up and this might be their only opportunity to bury the power lines.

Council Member Kallas addressed the issue of lighting. It will be along the entire length of the
project from 2200 West to 3600 West on both sides ofthe road similar to 14400 South from 2200
West to Redwood Road. Council Member Kallas wanted to make sure the lights are situated so
that all crossings are well-lit. Mr.Tracer confirmed that there will be two lights at every crosswalk,
which is the same standard required of all developers. There will be spacing of roughly 250 feet
with two at every crosswalk.


Mr. Reid brought up the mid-block crossing near Hidden Dream and recommended adding
chicanes around it to make it safer for pedestrians crossing at the park. Mayor Hall noted that there

Mr. Reid reported that the City has been having trouble with vandalism in park bathrooms,
including at the new Day Ranch Park. They will ask citizens to help by reporting imusual activity
in the upcoming newsletter.


Public Works Director, Shane Paddock reported that there has been a significant amount of
vandalism, specifically graffiti in the Skate Park one week after Day Ranch Park was opened.

bathrooms. They are down to one working bathroom out offour after the sinks were torn out of
the walls over the weekend. A camera system was installed outside the bathrooms. They have
also had graffiti issues in the bathroom at the All Abilities Park. It was noted that graffiti removal
takes the paint off the walls. They had anti-graffiti installed inside and out of the bathrooms.
Mr. Paddock hoped that getting the message out will help and noted that they will be closing the
bathrooms at the end of October for the year.

It was reported that this year has been pretty good, with vandalism down but they hope to get
messaging out that people can see. Those responsible for the graffiti at Day Ranch Park were
turned in by a witness and arrested. The identity of those involved in removing the sinks was
unknown. Mr. Reid was surprised by the extent of the vandalism and stated that it will require
significant effort to remove the heavy-duty installations from the wall.

The City recently met with the National Security Agency, which has an extensive facility in
