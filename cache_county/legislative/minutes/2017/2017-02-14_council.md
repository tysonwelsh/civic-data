ITEMS OF SPECIAL INTEREST

> Appointment of Interim County Council Member — Cache County Republican
Chairman Boyd Pugmire stated that the Cache County Republican Party held an
election on February 1, 2017 in accordance with Utah Code to replace Val K. Potter who
resigned his seat on the County Council on January 21, 2017. The delegates from the
Cache County Council Northeast District elected Gina Worthen. In accordance, Pugmire
presented the name of Gina Worthen to the Cache County Council for ratification to
serve on the County Council representing the Northeast District seat

ACTION: Motion by council member Tidwell to ratify the action of the Cache County
Republican Party and accept Gina Worthen as the Northeast District Council member.
White seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 5-0. Merrill absent. Northeast
District vacant.

> Swearing-In of New County Council Member — Clerk/Auditor Jill Zollinger
administered the oath of office to Worthen and she was seated on the Council.

UNIT OR COMMITTEE REPORTS

* County Road Condition Report — Director Josh Runhaar reported on the work of the
Road Department during the recent storms followed by warm weather that has created
flooding issues, etc. in the County. Runhaar thanked Janeen Allen for acting as a Public
Information Officer and keeping posts up to date on the County website and Facebook
page. Runhaar displayed the County website to show the Council postings, pictures and
videos of road problems.

There is a lot of damage and Runhaar has contacted FEMA, however, there is not yet
enough damage to qualify for help with the infrastructure repairs, but that may change as
assessments are made. FEMA requires $3.9 million in damages statewide to qualify for
help and Runhaar estimates Cache County has $1.5 to $2 million in damages. Box
Elder has about $400,000.00 in damages. He is also working on funding from Natural
Resources Conservation Service (NRCS) through emergency watershed protection
funds which cover drainage issues and erosion control. Runhaar estimates there is
about 30-35 miles of paved road damage. Gravel roads have not yet been addressed.

Runhaar received a call from Staker Parsons asking what they could do to help. They
showed up with a trailer full of signs and brought over a Picker (picks up concrete
barriers) from Brigham City for County use. Logan city provided flood damage warning
signs — even creating new ones in their sign shop for the County. Runhaar expressed
appreciation to these entities and the Sheriff's Department for many hours of help
“babysitting” damaged road areas until signs or short-term fixes were available.

Runhaar hopes to have more solid numbers next week. The County will start on minor
repairs while awaiting funding assistance. Residents will be living with many of the
damaged/closed areas for months until it dries out and repairs can occur.
