B.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-3-2021,
     FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT
     CHANGE TO ENACT PART 17-2-1200P REGULATING LODGING
     FACILITIES
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled June 1, 2021, in order for the City Council to hear and
     consider public comments regarding Application ZT-3-2021, filed by West Valley City,
     requesting a Zone Text Change to enact part 17-2-1200P regulating lodging facilities.

     Proposed Ordinance 21-29 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
     Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

     ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 21-29, ENACT PART 17-2-1200P
     REGULATING LODGING FACILITIES
     Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 21-29 that would
     enact Part 17-2-1200P regulating lodging facilities.

     Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
     information as follows:

             The proposed ordinance change would prohibit lodging facilities from
             leasing more than half of their rooms to single entity for more than thirty
             days per calendar year. This change addresses two issues. First, lodging
             facilities are intended to provide short-term lodging for business and
             leisure travelers. Having a single entity reserve a majority of the rooms
             within a lodging facility for more than 30 days changes the nature of a
             lodging facility and precludes short-term lodging for business and leisure
             travelers. Second, having the majority of rooms within a lodging facility
             leased by a single entity for an extended period of time could lead to the
             lodging facility effectively becoming an apartment complex. With the
             exceptions of the Mixed Use and City Center zones, apartments and
             lodging facilities are not allowed in the same zone.

     Councilmember Huynh stated that he feels this is a good proposed change and is

     Proposed Ordinances 21-30 and 21-31 related to the proposal to be considered by
     the City Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

     ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 21-30, AMEND THE GENERAL PLAN TO
     SHOW A CHANGE OF LAND USE FROM VARIOUS LAND USE
     DESIGNATIONS TO LARGE LOT RESIDENTIAL, LOW DENSITY
     RESIDENTIAL,         OR      NEIGHBORHOOD             COMMERCIAL          FOR
     PROPERTY LOCATED AT VARIOUS LOCATIONS
     Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 21-30 that would
     amend the General Plan to show a change of land use from various land use
     designations to Large Lot Residential, Low Density Residential, or Neighborhood
     Commercial for property located at various locations.

     Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
     information as follows:

            The Utah Legislature recently passed HB 98 that, among other things,
            removed cities’ ability to regulate most building design standards for a one
            to two family dwelling or townhome where no development agreement
            exists. Most vacant properties in the City planned for residential use either
            have a development agreement that includes building design standards or
            they are within an agricultural zone where staff anticipates a development
            agreement upon a future zone change. The subject properties do not have a
            development agreement and are zoned R-1-6, R-1-8, R-1-12 or RM. The
            properties zoned R-1 could be developed as single unit dwellings without
            many of the City’s building design standards once HB 98 becomes

COMMUNICATIONS
A.  ADMINISTRATIVE AND LEGISLATIVE DECISIONS TRAINING VIDEO
    AND DISCUSSION
    A video was played that displayed Brandon Hill, Deputy City Attorney, discussing
    the difference between Legislative and Administrative decisions made by the City
    Council.

       Councilmember Buhler stated that he has seen occasions where he didn’t like a
       subdivision but had to approve it because it was an administrative action. He
       suggested removing these types of applications from the voting agenda since the
       Council has no discretion. Steve Pastorik explained how zone changes and
       Development Agreement’s are tied to subdivisions. Councilmember Lang stated
       that she likes to have a second chance to view subdivisions and would not be
       comfortable with this change. Councilmember Lang added that she would like to
       hear from the developer in study meeting on zone changes so the Council can
       negotiate any potential changes. Nicole replied that if the Council would like, they
       can allow developers to speak in Study Meeting as part of their policy. She noted
       that the Council has maintained the current policy for the purpose of maintaining a
       clean record. Councilmember Nordfelt noted that he likes the idea of removing
       administrative decisions from the Council agendas. However, he noted that he
       would not be comfortable allowing developers to speak in Study Meeting without
       also allowing the public an opportunity to make comment. Mayor Bigelow agreed
       that this could be poorly perceived by the public. Councilmember Buhler suggested
       lengthening zone change processes by one week. He stated that there could be a
       Study Meeting one week, a public hearing the following week for the developers
       as well as residents, and then a meeting for voting the 3rd week. He indicated that
       he would also like to see administrative actions, such as subdivisions, removed
       from Council agendas since these must legally be approved and the Council has no
       discretion. Mayor Bigelow asked staff to research other Cities that don’t vote on
       administrative actions.
