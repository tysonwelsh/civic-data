A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-7-2020,
     FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT
     CHANGE TO CREATE THE CHESTERFIELD OVERLAY ZONE
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled November 24, 2020, in order for the City Council to
     hear and consider public comments regarding Application ZT-7-2020, filed by West
     Valley City, requesting a Zone Text Change to create the Chesterfield Overlay Zone.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              At the request of the Planning Commission and City Council, City staff is
              requesting a zone text change to create the Chesterfield Overlay Zone.
              Related to this application is application Z-8-2020, which applies the
              proposed overlay zone to the Chesterfield neighborhood.

              The Chesterfield neighborhood is unique in that homes within the
              neighborhood are generally older and smaller than most of the rest of the
              City and many of the neighborhood streets do not include curb, gutter and
              sidewalk. The attached presentation includes information that highlights
              ways that the Chesterfield neighborhood is unique. The proposed
              Chesterfield Overlay Zone includes standards for new development that
              will allow new homes that are more compatible with existing homes.

              Included with this issue paper is a table that compares the proposed overlay
              zone standards with the current ordinance requirements. Also included is an
              email received from a resident of the Chesterfield neighborhood.

          Staff has requested a zone change to add the proposed Chesterfield Overlay
          Zone to properties located between 2420 South and 2920 South and between
          1680 West and the Jordan River. Associated with this application is
          application ZT-7-2020 which creates the proposed Chesterfield Overlay
          Zone within the Zoning Ordinance.

          If approved, the proposed overlay zone would apply to 493 parcels. The
          underlying zoning on all of these parcels is either R-1-6 (single unit

          animal rights if the overlay zone is adopted.

          The proposed overlay zone will apply to all new homes and additions to
          existing homes. The overlay zone standards address housing design and
          right-of-way improvements. It does not change allowed uses, setbacks or
          density regulations.

ORDINANCE 20-49: AMEND SECTIONS 1-2-110 AND 20-7-102 OF THE WEST
VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN STORM WATER
UTILITY FEES
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Ordinance 20-49 that would amend Sections 1-2-110
and 20-7-102 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to amend certain storm water utility
fees.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       This ordinance modifies the Consolidated Fee Schedule to increase Storm Water
       Utility (SWU) fees from $4.00 to $6.00 per month per Equivalent Residential Unit
       (ERU) for most SWU customers. An adjustment is also made in the definition of
       one Equivalent Residential Unit, which increases the impervious area equal to one
       ERU from 2,830 square feet to 3,163 square feet.

       New revenue generated from this fee increase would be used for capital
       improvements, and associated design and construction management costs. An
       analysis has been completed on all needed capital
       improvement projects, including the piping of existing open ditches, replacement
       of old corrugated metal pipe, and upgraded storm drains to alleviate street
       flooding. Assuming 4% annual cost increases, the historical storm water capital
       improvement budget of $1 million annually, leaves $58 million in uncompleted
       projects in 2050.

       It has been 15 years, since this fee was adjusted. We have fallen behind in
       funding storm drainage capital improvements and replacements. Since this fee
 MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING NOVEMBER 24, 2020
                         -6-

       was last increased, the RSMeans Construction Cost Index has increased over
       50%. The condition of the storm drain system will continue to deteriorate without
       additional funds.

       The proposed fee increase generates an additional $1.5 million in new revenue
       annually. Combined with the current annual CIP budget, this new revenue is
       sufficient to complete all anticipated improvements by 2043. All projects and the
       proposed funding year are shown on the attached list.

       Currently all operation and maintenance costs are covered in the existing budget.
       The above scenarios do not account for increased operational costs. Additional
       fee increases will be needed in the future, as those costs increase.

       Since inception of the Storm Water Utility in 2001, storm water utility fees and
       flood control impact fees have been the only funding sources for storm water
       operations and capital improvements. Without the Storm Water Utility, this cost
       would fall back to the General Fund.

       Most Utah cities now charge a Storm Water Utility fee. For the 12 cities
       surveyed, West Valley City has the lowest fee at $4.00 per month. Provo has the
       highest fee at $10.75 per month. The average fee is $6.77 per month. See
       attached bar chart.

       A 50% increase in fees ($4 to $6 per month) is significant for the largest Storm
       Water Utility customers. A reevaluation of the impervious area equal to one ERU
       was done as part of this fee analysis. This increased the area equivalent to one
       ERU from 2,830 SF to 3,163 SF. The result is the number of ERUs per site (and
       subsequently the fee) decreases for non-residential uses. Instead of a 50%
       increase on a $2 per month fee change, non-residential parcels would see a 34%
       increase.

       Adjusting these fees now, with an effective date of July 1, 2021, allows large
       Storm Water Utility customers to plan for the increased fees.

Councilmember Buhler stated that these funds are restricted in their use. He indicated that
these fees haven’t been adjusted in many years and they are an important source for the
City.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of the City

RESOLUTION 20-177: APPROVE THE PURCHASE OF A VARIABLE SPEED
WATER PUMP STATION FOR THE STONEBRIDGE GOLF COURSE
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 20-177 that would approve the purchase of
a variable speed water pump station for the Stonebridge Golf Course.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West Valley City solicited proposals for the replacement of the variable speed water
       pump station for the Stonebridge Golf Club. The pump station is of utmost
       importance as, without it, the course would not have an irrigation system and the
       turf as well as other plants would perish. Four companies responded with Flowtech
       being selected.

       Parks and Recreation publicly advertised for proposals for the replacement of the
       variable speed water pump station for the Stonebridge Golf Club. The current
       pump station is the original unit and is over 20 years old. There have been
       numerous problems with the current station including excessive rusting that will no
       longer allow welding repair work to be done. Affected areas include the filters and
       main manifold as well as deterioration of the metal floor. The system’s electronics
       and computer are also original units that are outdated to the point that technicians
       cannot utilize their sensors to diagnose problems.

       The RFP was posted on the West Valley City website, in the newspaper, on bid
       sites and several companies were contacted by staff. The proposals were evaluated
       and compared against each other in a matrix format by a qualified committee.

       Evaluation criteria included price, warranty, timeline and adherence to
       specifications. The four proposals submitted are as follows:

       Flowtech:                     $274,635
       A.P.E.:                       $277,134.11
       Double D:                     $306,855
       Van Con:                      $563,800

       Out of these proposals, Flowtech was the lowest responsible bidder.
