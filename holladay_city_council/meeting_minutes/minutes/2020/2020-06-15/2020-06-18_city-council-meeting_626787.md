IV.     Consideration of Ordinance 2020-09 Amending the Budget for the Fiscal Year Beginning
        July 1, 2019, and Ending June 30, 2020 (Approving Changes to the Budget for the
        Current Fiscal Year.)
Manager Chamness highlighted an addition made since the last City Council Meeting. $856,000
was added to revenue and expenses as a placeholder reflecting Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and
Economic Security (“CARES”) Act funding that the City is about to receive. She signed an
agreement with Salt Lake County the previous week to receive $906,000. Ms. Chamness reported
that $50,000 will be used this current year for physical improvements made to the buildings,
installation of plexiglass in the courtroom and the Planning Office as well as the purchase of
Personal Protective Equipment (“PPE”) and staff time that can legitimately be charged to that
source of funding.

Mayor Dahle stated that the budget adjustment will reflect the changes in terms of revenue expense
and the $856,000 will be included in the list of changes to the budget for next year.

V.     Consideration of Ordinance 2020-10 Determining the Rate of Tax for 2020 Tax Year and
       Levying Taxes Upon all Real and Personal Property within the City of Holladay.
Ms. Chamness addressed the requirements for setting the Certified Tax Rate under State Statute.
She reported that the Certified Tax Rate is set by independents elected at the County level and the
State Tax Commission. The rate will produce the same amount of revenue for the City plus new
growth. She explained that when valuations increase, the Certified Tax Rate decreases.

Council Member Fotheringham moved to adopt Ordinance 2020-10. Council Member Durham
seconded the motion. The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Fotheringham,
Gibbons, Durham, Quinn, Petersen and Mayor Dahle in favor. Ordinanace 2020-10 was adopted by
a unamimous vote.

VI.    Consideration of Ordinance 2020-11 Adopting a Final Budget for Fiscal Year 2020-2021
       (including General Fund, Capital Improvements, Debt Service, Grants, and Arts Funds).
Ms. Chamness addressed changes to the property tax line item in the General Fund budget detail.
She stated that there was an increase of $53,715 to item G7, which is a formula determination of
new growth based on the definition of new growth provided by State Statute. Historically, new
growth amounts are $50,000 to $60,000. To compensate for the increase, she reduced the use of
Fund Balance from $710,000 to $668,000. A 2% Cost of Living Increase Allowance (“COLA”) for
staff was also included. The Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (“CARES”) Act
funding was added to the expense side of the budget amendment.

Ms. Chamness reported that she increased the Interfaith Council’s budget from $1,000 to $1,500
and funding for the Chamber of Commerce from $5,000 to $7,500. Minor changes were also made
to the Unified Police Department (“UPD”) contract amount and the Unified Fire Authority (“UFA”)
contract amount to match what the Board recently approved. A matching amount of $12,500 was to
be paid for a $183,000 grant the City is receiving from the Wasatch Front Regional Council
(“WFRC”). The grant is to be used for a study of potential improvements on Highland Drive.

In response to a question by Council Member Durham, Ms. Chamness confirmed that the match
amount is shown as a transfer to the Grants Fund. There is also an addition of $12,500 in the grant
funds. The $265,657 reflects the annualized cost increase and the change in Code Enforcement.

Council Member Fotheringham requested Ms. Chamness address restrictions provided regarding
how the CARES Act funding was to be spent. Ms. Chamness stressed that it cannot be used for
revenue replacement.

VII.    Consideration of Resolution 2020-21 Extending the Emergency Declaration and All
        Associated Agenda.
Ms. Chamness described the Emergency Declaration and stated that she was proposing to continue
it as COVID-19 cases do not appear to be declining. She insisted on the need to ensure that they
create an environment where there is continued discussion of possible additional federal funding.

Ms. Chamness further proposed that the signage for curbside pickup and dining on the right-of-way
be extended until the end of August. She requested that the suspension of business license
payments not be extended to the next fiscal year so that businesses are not be required to make two
payments during the next fiscal year.

Council Member Petersen moved to approve Resolution 2020-21. Council Member Quinn
seconded the motion. The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Fotheringham,
Gibbons, Durham, Quinn, Petersen and Mayor Dahle in favor. Resolution 2020-21 was approved
by a unamimous vote.

X.      City Manager’s Report – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness reported on the CARES Act and the receipt of the agreement. Staff will consider
proposals regarding how to spend and share the allocated funds with businesses that may be in need.
Previously the Council approved a Fireworks Map that prohibits fireworks in certain areas of the
City. While it is available at fireworks distribution and sales sites throughout the County, it has not
always been clear to residents where those areas are. The intent was to place 15 signs throughout
the City to identify areas where fireworks are prohibited.

Ms. Chamness reported that Tosh Kano will be leaving the City effective June 30, 2020. Some of
his responsibilities for right-of-way inspections, permitting, and liaison work will be transitioned.
She planned to report back to the Council in July with a proposal for an Engineer Tech position.
She thanked Mr. Kano for his service over the last 20 years. Council Member Petersen valued Mr.
Kano’s service and appreciated his service to the City. She suggested that the Council reevaluate
the way some positions work.

        a.       Discussion on Options for July 4
Mayor Dahle recalled that at the last meeting the Council discussed ways to show patriotism in the

of City Hall and the pavilion at Knudsen Park with patriotic bunting. Banners were also designed

        b.      Discussion on Policing.
Mayor Dahle reported that he received 5 to 10 emails from various constituents who echoed the
standard concerns residents have. He and Chief Hoyal met with a group of residents and mothers
who have adopted African American children who are concerned about the issue. It was a very
productive discussion. With the exception of one incident that took place several years ago, they
were very complimentary of the officers that serve the community. They also want to support the
general consensus opinion that institutional racism and profiling have been part of some policing
practices and embedded within certain police cultures nationally. As an agency, the City needs to
look at the existing policing policies and procedures.

Mayor Dahle insisted that there is a need to engage the Unified Police Department (“UPD”) to
address the existing policing issues. Municipalities will need information on training procedures
and have policies in place to address profiling and violence within the policing community. He
noted that residents have different views on defunding the police. Some residents agree while
others do not.

Council Member Durham reported that the Utah League of Cities and Towns (“ULCT”) and the
State have been tracking ideas at the Legislature and are doing their best to keep the City informed
of any developments. ULCT’s primary interest is preserving local control of the various cities and
towns. This will ensure that they have input in what happens in cities. They also want to maintain
the integrity and authority of municipalities to ensure that they have input over policing in their
communities while addressing valid concerns about inequalities in the law enforcement system.


Holladay City Council Meeting Minutes 06-18-20                                               6
Council Member Durham stated that ULCT’s push is to have an open dialogue with a broad range
of interested parties to discuss issues and concerns that are raised. This will ensure that they do not
jump in with specific proposed reforms or ideas. He further stated that certain communities feel
that taskforces, blue-ribbon commissions, and discussion groups have sometimes intentionally or
inadvertently resulted in frustrating delays. He was interested in hearing more about direct
influences over UPD policies and budgeting. Mayor Dahle stated that the Board sets budget and
policy for the UPD and the Board Members have a lot of influence in directing staff. He offered to
review the budget details with Council Member Durham to address concerns he might have.

Council Member Fotheringham was concerned about the accountability and communication path
used in the event there are behavioral issues or complaints from residents. He asked how the City
Council will be informed of complaints other than getting information from newspapers. Mayor
Dahle reported that all complaints are handled through the UPD’s Human Resources Department.
Chief Hoyal stated that complaints against an officer at the UPD can be reported directly to him, a
supervisor, a sergeant, or a lieutenant at the department. Aside from that, they have a separate
Internal Affairs Unit that carries out investigations. After investigations are complete, all of the
collected facts are sent back to him for review. This information is passed on to the management
team, for the chiefs in the different precincts to review. The information is then forwarded to the
sheriff upon completion of the review.

Council Member Fotheringham asked how the Council feels about the existing chain of command.
He stated that with an Ombudsman scenario, there is someone outside the chain of command who
reports directly to the Board in a governance arrangement. He considered there to be value in that.
Mayor Dahle asked if a Citizen Advisory Committee outside the chain of command and
organization exists within the UPD.

Chief Hoyal confirmed that it does but not with respect to disciplinary matters. He reported that
they are reevaluating existing policies to ensure that they are consistent with public needs. The
previous week they reviewed their Use of Force Policy and created a list of Frequently Asked
Questions. The questions were then presented to the Citizens' Advisory Board for review. He
reported that they have a tracking system where every complaint is logged and documented.

Ms. Chamness asked if a Civilian Review Board structure exists that routinely reviews Use of
Force. Chief Hoyal confirmed that it does not exist. The Sheriff, however, mentioned earlier in the
day at the UPD Board Meeting that they were looking at changing the procedure.

Mayor Dahle commented on the discipline of individuals and felt that 95% of the officers are
excellent and do not use excessive force. He asked if there is access to a file where a background
check could be done especially for officers that are about to join the UPD that may have had
discipline problems in the past. Chief Hoyal confirmed that a personnel file exists. These files are
made available to law enforcement agencies if requested. Extensive and thorough background
checks are conducted on all officers who apply for work at the UPD. Chief Hoyal reported that
every use of force complaint is reviewed by their immediate sergeant, Lieutenant Adamson, and
himself.

Council Member Fotheringham asked if access to the system is limited to the chain of command.
Chief Hoyal confirmed that it is. Council Member Fotheringham stated that such closed systems
could pose potential governance problems. He insisted on the need for outside oversight.

Council Member Gibbons suggested that they not create a system that replicates or duplicates what
is done with internal affairs. He requested that the City initiate use of force reforms and review
processes with the Unified Police Department under the Interlocal Agreement. This will allow the
City Council to review and hear the use of force issues shared by the UPD in a confidential closed
session.

Mayor Dahle reported that there are unhealthy cultures that exist in police departments where bad
behavior is protected and allowed to fester. He suggested there be a check and balance involvement
at the UPD. He stated that the City Council does not want to be involved in an organization that
does not have policies, procedures, and leadership that condones a healthy policing culture.

Council Member Gibbons suggested that the City Council hold a public hearing before making any
decisions on ordinances. This will provide an opportunity for the Council to hear from the public
about police issues.

Mr. Godfrey stated that one of the benefits of the City’s policing model is the shifting of liability.
He stated that the City Council is a contracting entity while UPD officers are employees of the
UPD. He stated that the degree of control the City Council exercises over the UPD can be both a
benefit and a burden. The board structure allows the City Council to influence significant
operational components of the UPD.

Mr. Godfrey stated that he was previously involved in a lot of civil rights defense work for a public
entity in Salt Lake County that handled most excessive force cases. It was important to consider the
interest of people who are on the review boards. He requested that the Mayor be sent back to the
Board with a message that the City Council is vitally interested in this topic and wishes to have
review boards available.

Chief Hoyal felt that the City is quite progressive with its policies and when it comes to staying on
top of things. He stated that a lot of work needs to be done moving forward about the use of force
issues and promised to stay on top of it. Mayor Dahle felt that municipalities will want a standard
statement from UPD that addresses some of the common questions that were being received by the
City Council.

Ms. Chamness confirmed that she was close to providing a statement regarding sharing information
on social media. She asked if the questions presented by Chief Hoyal reflect the policies in place.
Chief Hoyal responded in the affirmative. He stated that most of the questions were related to use
of force by the police.

Council Member Gibbons reported that House Bill 415 under local law enforcement structure states
that a municipality may not establish a board committee or other entity that has authority
independent of the Chief of Police and has authority to overrule a hiring or appointment proposal of
the Chief of Police. He requested that Mr. Godfrey advise the City Council on that.


Holladay City Council Meeting Minutes 06-18-20                                             8
Council Member Durham stated that the most important principle is that there is transparency and
external accountability within the UPD and the County level. This will in turn help avoid problems
caused by the development of an inappropriate culture in the City. An independent view will also
build confidence among people in the community.

Council Member Fotheringham commented that this kind of governance happens in industries all
over and not unique to policing. Every corporation has an audit committee and auditors report
directly to the Board and not the CFO or CEO.

Council Member Gibbons added that House Bill 415 does not prohibit the passing of information
unless deemed confidential. He added that the City Council is prohibited from exerting authority
over the independence of the police under House Bill 415.

Mayor Dahle stated that some citizens were disturbed by the turn that the protest took in Salt Lake
City saying that it did not reflect the true feelings of the protesters in general. The message they
were trying to deliver was that most officers are good and appreciated. He insisted on the need for a
review board that is restricted to top-level incidents only without micromanaging the UPD.

Chief Hoyal reported that there have been discussions to change the Citizens Advisory Board and
make it a different process. He had received emails from citizens who had concerns about certain
existing policies and practices. He thanked the community for the support and appreciation the
UPD has received over the past two weeks.

Council Member Fotheringham was proud of the Chief and his staff for the amazing work they are
doing for the City of Holladay. He appreciated Chief Hoyal’s candor and willingness to listen to
the concerns raised about governance in the UPD. He believed there was a way the Police
Department can be made better without reinventing the wheel.

Council Member Durham commended the Chief for being responsive and helpful in responding to
concerns from residents in his district. Chief Hoyal stated that the UPD is open to making
everything better and there was always room for improvement.

Mayor Dahle suggested that a survey be carried out to better understand what citizens feel is
important, areas that the City Council should improve on, and how much is spent on policing. The
survey would also help enlighten the City Council on how policing is going on in the community
and how citizens felt about it.
