       Temporary Construction Easement with and from the Jordan Valley Water
       Conservancy District for Property Located at 4708 South 4800 West.


       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              The Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District parcel located at 4708 South
              4800 West is one of several parcels affected by the 4700 South
              Reconstruction Project (4000 West to 5600 West). This project is being
              constructed in cooperation with Kearns Metro Township. This project will
              reconstruct the pavement, curb, gutter, sidewalk and decorative street
              lighting on both sides of 4700 South and 4715 South to improve safety and
              ride quality of 4700 South and 4715 South. Compensation for the purchase
              of the Quit Claim Deed, Temporary Construction Easement and
              improvements is $17,700.00 was based upon the appraisal report prepared
              by Van Drimmelen Associates, Inc.

B.    COMMUNITY RENEWABLE ENERGY FOLLOW-UP
      Wayne Pyle, City Manager, stated that the Council requested a follow-up
      discussion on this matter. He indicated that staff is ready to proceed as directed.

      Nicole Cottle, Assistant City Manager, provided a history on HB411.
      Councilmember Nordfelt asked if the City can opt in to be part of the discussion
      and then opt out if prices aren’t manageable. Nicole replied yes but noted that this
      would be negotiated. Councilmember Nordfelt asked what the timeline is before
      the final decision needs to be made. Nicole replied that those who have opted in are
      wanting to move as quickly as possible. Councilmember Christensen stated that
      the City can still continue on the separate path that it is on as well. Nicole agreed
      and stated that the City is already working toward Net Zero 2030. Councilmember
      Nordfelt stated that he likes what the City is doing but noted that HB411 provides
      a different opportunity to residents. He indicated that he feels some businesses and
      residents would like to have the choice to participate. Councilmember Nordfelt
      suggested that the Mayor and Council postpone the decision until May to see if
      there are any changes or movement with different aspects of HB411. He added that
      it may be worth joining and opting out at later date if it doesn’t benefit the City or
      its residents and the cost is a small investment. Wayne indicated that the City does
      not need to pass a resolution to opt out. The only resolution that needs to be passed
      is if the Council wants to opt in. Mayor Lang stated the only benefit to passing a
      formal resolution opting out of the process would be to provide a courtesy to the
      other communities. Wayne agreed and noted that there will be political pressure on
      the Council until a decision is made either way. Mayor Lang stated that she doesn’t
      feel that any additional substantial information that would urge the Council to join
      HB411 has been provided. Wayne agreed and noted that he understands
      Councilmember Nordfelt’s view but indicated that the staff recommendation would
      be not to join at this time. He added that the Council does have time to consider it
      over the next month or so. Councilmember Fitisemanu stated that he sees both good
      and bad with joining the program and it would be prudent to take time to decide.
      He indicated he is confident in the plan staff has provided but would like to see
      more concrete deadlines and accountability for staying on track. Councilmember
      Huynh stated that he feels there is always a challenge when there are multiple cities
      or agencies participating in something like this.
