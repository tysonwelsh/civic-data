Community Renewable Energy Program (HB411) Community Survey

Representatives from Weber State University’s (WSU) Center for Community Engaged Learning are seeking input from
stakeholders regarding the proposed questions for the community survey. Council members will have the opportunity to review
and comment on the proposed questions. Weber State hopes to start the survey in February 2022.

WSU Professor of Economics Jenny Gnagey and Evaluator/Community Research Analyst Cassandra Backman used a
PowerPoint presentation to facilitate a review of the draft survey questions. The purpose of the survey is to determine whether
business owners and residents of Ogden would like to participate in the Community Renewable Energy Program (CREP) and
determine the financial burden business owners and residents of Ogden City are willing to bear to participate in the CREP. Ms.
Gnagey discussed sampling goals, the initial sampling plan for the survey, and sampling methods for residents and businesses.
Screening will be done for the following:

1. Only business owners or residents of Ogden City

2. Only business owner that operate outside their primary residence in Ogden take the business survey
3. Only business owners or residents that pay their electricity either through a bill or rent

4. No residents or business owners that have solar panels installed with net-metering

5. No business owners participating in Schedule 32 or 34

Survey questions will seek to determine what customers are already paying, what they are willing to pay, whether they
would opt out of the program and the price point at which they would decide to opt-out, and the factors that have included their
decisions on the previous survey questions. There will also be questions aimed at determining the demographics of survey
respondents.

The Council engaged in discussion with the WSU representatives regarding the appropriate content and format of the

Gnagey and Ms. Backman for their efforts.

Proposed Honorary Street Name Ordinance

Council Deputy Director Symes explained that Council member Choberka has submitted a Council Initiative form for the
development and consideration of an honorary street name ordinance. The initiative came in response to the discussions from a
petition to assign an honorary street name to a portion of 2" Street. The initiative was submitted with the direction that an
ordinance be drafted that would look at how and who is able to make requests for honorary street names and to work with the
Diversity Commission to review and revise the ordinance to create equity and inclusion in the core of the process. Council staff
have been working with Council member Choberka and members of the Diversity Commission, in conjunction with Legal staff, to
develop a draft proposal aimed at meeting the direction given from the Council initiative. The proposed ordinance was reviewed
by the Council in a work session on October 5, 2021. That work session generally focused on three policy points: petitions and
who may apply, Diversity Commission involvement, and fees and costs. The purpose of tonight’s work session is to focus on the
fees and costs of processing an honorary street name petition. In reviewing the current honorary street name process for barriers
that would make the process less equitable and inclusive, the issue of costs and application fees appears to be a significant issue.
The current ordinance requires a $500 application fee, although the collection of the fee has not been consistent, and signage
printing and installation costs of approximately $75 per sign. At least one sign is required at every intersection of the portion of
road included in the proposed honorary name. These costs can add up and can be prohibitive for residents looking to apply for an
honorary street name. In the proposed ordinance, the application fee remains in place and the costs of signage must be paid by the
petitioner. Application fees are generally put into place to cover the costs associated with City staff time and noticing of any
public hearings for a proposal. In response to requests by Council members for additional information on the cost of processing a
petition, Council staff requested a breakdown of administrative costs associated with the review and processing of a typical
honorary street name petition. Having recently reviewed and processed the Satnam Singh Drive petition, Administrative staff
provided the costs for reviewing that petition as a benchmark. The final estimate provided was $1,666. This amount did not
include Council staff time as this was an analysis of Administrative staff time and noticing costs. Council staffs time for
reviewing and preparing materials as well as Council meeting time costs would add approximately another $200 to the total. The
Council will need to determine what portion of these costs should be borne by the petitioner with the fee. The task for the Council
is to balance the goal of making the process more equitable and inclusive against the financial principles related to user fees.

The Council discussed the proposed application and signage fees and concluded that the fees will not be charged for all
potential honorary street name designations. A resident or group of residents may be able to convince an elected official to
propose the honorary street name designation and in that case the resident group would not pay a fee. For resident or group
initiated honorary street names, the Council indicated they do not feel the suggested petition fees are inappropriate.

Zone Text Amendments — Accessory Dwelling Units

Planning Manager Montgomery explained that in November 2021 the Planning Commission and City Council held a
combined training and discussed amendments to State Code regarding accessory dwelling units. As a result of the State’s
amendments, the City was required to update the zoning code to comply with new state regulations. The most significant change
is the removal of restrictions on how large an accessory dwelling unit (ADU) can be. This zoning code amendment would allow
ADUs to be any size. The amendment would also remove a requirement for owners to get a business license for ADUs. It is
important to note that there were other changes to State law which would allow the City to place more stringent restrictions on
where ADUs may be located. Because Ogden is a college town, it is technically possible to restrict up to 67% of residential zones
from having ADUs. The City already has restrictions on where ADUs may be located, and the Planning Commission did not find
it necessary to increase such restrictions.

The Council briefly discussed limitations placed on ADUs and concluded they were comfortable moving forward with
adoption of the ordinance amendments in a future meeting.

Zone Text Amendments — Mobile Food Trailers

Vice Chair Marcia White submitted a Council initiative form to amend the City’s zoning ordinance to allow mobile food
trailers to operate within City right-of-way in a similar manner to mobile food trucks. The proposed ordinance was reviewed by
the Planning Commission which forwarded a recommendation of approval. The proposal would allow food trailers, as defined in
the Code, to operate with nearly all the same standards as food trucks which are permitted in most commercial zones including the
Central Business District (CBD) zones.

Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed amendments to
mobile food trailer regulations. He first reviewed the history of the current food trailer ordinance, after which he noted the current
proposal is that mobile food trailers be required to meet the same standards as mobile foot trucks. Trailers would be allowed in the
right-of-way with certain conditions:

Council Business

Proposed Anti-idling Ordinance: Council Policy Analyst Watkins used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
the history of past discussions regarding enacting an anti-idling ordinance in the City. There are several policies in existence that
address vehicle emissions, but Utah allows Utah cities to adopt an ordinance that specifically restricts idling. An anti-idling
ordinance must be educational and enforcement options would be based on air quality and/or seasonal conditions. Exceptions to
the ordinance would include public safety vehicles, idling at stop signs or red lights, or idling during certain weather conditions.
At least one warning would be required before a citation could be issued and fines would be equal to parking violations. There are
nine cities in the State with an anti-idling ordinance and at least four focus on educational campaigns. Enforcement is a challenge
and the ordinances in place in other cities are used for messaging purposes. Council staff needs feedback from the Council
regarding whether they would like to proceed with consideration of an anti-idling ordinance; if the Council wishes to proceed, the
matter could be referred to the City’s Sustainability Committee for a formal recommendation.

Chair Blair recessed the meeting at 5:57 p.m. and indicated discussion of this issue will resume following the City
Council meeting. The meeting reconvened at 7:55 p.m.

Discussion regarding the concept of an anti-idling ordinance continued. The Council indicated they are comfortable
pursuing an educational campaign regarding idling, but they do not want to adopt an ordinance that would include enforcement
measures at this time.

Marshall White Center — Proposed Joint Resolution: The Council and Administration engaged in review and discussion
of the proposed joint resolution regarding the Marshall White Center. There was a focus on the separation of powers within the
City’s form of government and the responsibilities assumed by each branch of the government, as well as the Marshall White
Advisory Committee (MWAC) upon adoption of the joint resolution. Several Council members expressed their feelings that the
Marshall White Center should be rebuilt in its present site, but they are not entirely sure of the overall scope of that project. Chair
Blair indicated he is not as concerned about the cost as he is about the funding source for the project once the scope is determined.
Chief Administrative Officer Johnson stated he shares that concern, but Administration is also committed to seeing the MWC
rebuilt in its present location. He is also committed to continuing to work with the MWAC on the project and will plan to provide
a monthly report to the Council along with the MWAC Chair.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith indicated she will work with the City Attorney’s Office and Administration to
adjust the draft of the joint resolution responsive to feedback provided tonight.
