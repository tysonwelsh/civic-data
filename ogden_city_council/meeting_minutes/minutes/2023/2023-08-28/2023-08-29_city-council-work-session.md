Council Business

Council Rules and Norms — Leadership Selection: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the direction :
she received during the August 15 work session meeting regarding leadership selection; the Council indicated they would prefer to
select leadership in February, rather than January, but staff needs to know if the Council would like that to occur during the first
meeting or last meeting of the month. The Council supported taking the action during the last meeting in February each year. Ms.
Eller-Smith stated the Council was split on the idea of a set rotation for leadership that would occur annually; she facilitated
discussion among the Council to determine whether there is broad support for an automatic selection or if the Council would
rather vote each year to choose leadership. There was not a clear consensus and Ms. Eller-Smith asked the Council to continue to
consider the matter in preparation for selection of an option early in the new year. She then discussed the matter of additional
compensation for holding a leadership position; the recommendation from Evergreen was to transition to a straight salary amount
for all Council members and then provide a stipend to cover any differentiation associated with holding a leadership position. She

noted that Salt Lake City has an ordinance that provides for Council members to be paid 25 percent of the Mayor’s salary; if the
‘ — City Council were to adopt a similar ordinance, Council members would receive a nearly $12,000 increase. The Ogden City
Council is currently paid approximately 17 percent of what the Mayor earns. Evergreen recommended that the Council Chair be
paid $6,300, Vice Chair be paid $5,000, and other Council members be paid $3,600 in stipends. She asked if the Council would
like to address changes now or wait for the Compensation Commission to evaluate the issue. This led to discussion among the
Council, with the conclusion being that the Council would like to hear the recommendations of the Compensation Commission.

Draft Elected Officials’ Compensation Commission Ordinance: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith then reviewed
the draft ordinance creating the Elected Officials’ Compensation Commission; the purpose of the Commission is to advise the
Council on elected officials’ compensation. Commissioners must be at least 21 years of age, a resident of Ogden, and have
professional experience in finance, human resources, corporate management, local government management, or a closely related
field. Terms of appointment would be three years and there would be a four-term limit. The Council may provide a per diem for
Commissioners and vacancies on the Commission would be filled in the same way with a new appointee filling the expired
portion of the vacated term. The Commission, on an annual basis, will review results of a salary survey conducted by Ogden City
Human Resources or an independent consultant hired by the City. Standards that the Commission will consider include:
