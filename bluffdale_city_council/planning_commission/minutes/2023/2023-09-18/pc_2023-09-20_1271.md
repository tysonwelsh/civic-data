4. PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION AND RECOMMENDATION on
     Proposed Text Amendments to the Bluffdale City Land Use Ordinances
     rl.11.100.050(R) (2.b) Regarding the Size of Tower Signs Allowed in the SD-X
     Bringhurst Station Zone, CEJ Real Estate LLC and Stasta, LLC, Applicants.
     (Application 2023-30) - Staff Presenter Jennifer Robison.

Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison presented a proposed change to land use ordinances
regarding the size of tower signs at the Special District SD-X Bringhurst Station Zone. She
reported that when it was initially adopted, the current signage language in the Code was taken
from the language for a similar existing Special District. Signage is usually determined during
the permitting process but this language was included in the initial districting.

The current Code allows for one tower sign at the entrance of the project, with a maximum of six
feet in width, 16 feet in height, and 96 square feet in area. The proposal would change the
parameters to nine feet in width, 13 feet in height, and 117 square feet in area. Mrs. Robison
demonstrated what the current dimensions look like compared to the applicants' proposed
dimensions. The applicants want the dimensions for aesthetic reasons as well as to make it
possible to use preexisting concrete panels from the building project of those dimensions to make
the sign. This would exceed the standard six-foot width.

No other signage is planned on the brick face. Some exteriors have brick and wood-form liners
for aesthetic purposes. The applicant is proposing to build the sign using the same materials.
The proposed sign will contain the word "Bringhurst" in backlit letters. The applicant requests
that the text of the Code be amended to allow for this project to go forward as planned.

Commissioner Swanson asked if this would be a change to the Citywide Code or specific to the
SD-X Special Development Zone. It was verified that it will only apply to Bringhurst Station's
specific zone. Commissioner Swanson asked if there have been any other requests for larger
signs. They have not, and there was no specific reasoning for this size other than a precedent for
other zones. No additional signage will be allowed with the proposed project. Commissioner

5. PUBLIC HEARING. CONSIDERATION AND RECOMMENDATION on
     Proposed City-Initiated Text Amendments to the Bluffdale City Land Use
     Ordinances 11.150.080 11.160.290 and Other Related Text Language Regarding
     Assurances for Completion of Required Improvements, City of Bluffdale,
     Applicants (Application 2023-33) - Staff Presenter, Dan Tracer and Jennifer
     Robison.

Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer, reported that per the current ordinances, as a project nears
completion and a Certificate of Occupancy is requested, the owners must post a bond for any
unfinished outside infrastructure. This process has been disruptive and unexpected for many
applicants, who are forced to provide a bond at the last minute as they are preparing to close.
The proposed amendments would change the way the process works for all site plans. Under the
proposal, the City will get an estimate for public improvements only at the beginning of the
project and developers will be required to post a bond before the Building Permit is issued. This
prevents Certificates of Occupancy from being held up by the bond. Once all public
improvements are complete, the bond will be returned before or after obtaining the Certificate of
Occupancy. Providing the bond up front will reduce surprises and complications.

In general, the amended text in the portions of City Code that cover site plans will mimic the
City Code's current process for subdivision bonding requirements. It will cover only public
improvements, which include items that will be owned by the City. Applicants will have the
option of paying a cash bond or entering into a third-party Bond Agreement and posting the bond
at a nearby bank, as is the case with subdivision bonding.

The proposed language addresses the acceptance of improvements as determined by the
Engineering Department. Once all requirements are met and documentation provided, they will
accept the work and release the bond. A warranty in the amount of 10% of the public
improvements is required for a period of one year, as with subdivisions. This protects the City's
public infrastructure against any defects in materials or workmanship.

Commissioner Flynn asked for clarification that the warranty is 10% of the total amount of the
project. They have found that 10% is enough to replace a few things during a project. It differs

from a performance bond where the City finishes the work the applicant didn't do. It assumes
that all of the work is completed but addresses problems that arise within a one-year and require

Chair Cragun asked for clarification that this verbiage was taken from the Subdivision section of
the City Code. Mr. Tracer confirmed that was the case and stated that applying the current Code
for subdivision plat bonding to a site plan.

Mrs. Robison addressed front-yard landscaping and reported that last year, the Legislature passed
several laws that changed several land use codes. The hope was that there would be a significant
amendment in the near future. Front yard landscaping is impacted and is covered here because it
relates to bonding. The current code requires single-family homeowners to complete their yards
within one year of receiving a Certificate of Occupancy. If they are unable to meet this
requirement, homeowners can post a bond and come back and finish the project within the one-
year period, The Legislature has removed the ability to post a bond for private landscaping. It
must fall under the category of public landscaping, the legal definition which includes anything
in a public right-of-way. The proposal removes the Code language requiring homeowners to
post a bond. The landscaping requirement remains but bonding cannot be used for enforcement.

Chair Cragun asked what the consequences would be if owners did not complete the project
within one year. Community and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, reported that
there is not much the City can do currently other than use a tracking log and approach the issue
from a Code Enforcement standpoint. A cash deposit makes enforcement easier for front-yard
landscaping, water projects, or street trees. Code violations are a Class C misdemeanor. The
hope was to make a change in the next year to a civil enforcement procedure for lower-level
violations. Even with a Temporary Certificate of Occupancy, they would be left with the same
enforcement issues. Mrs. Robison reported that language will also be added to reflect the
required water efficiency standards.

6. PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION AND RECOMMENDATION on
     Proposed City-Initiated Text Amendments to the Bluffdale City Code Section 11.300
     Related to Required Notice for Nuisance Enforcement and Abatement, City of
     Bluffdale, Applicants (Application 2023-32)- Staff Presenter, Grant CroweiF.

Mr. Crowell presented the proposed City-initiated text amendment to the Bluffdale City Code
Section 11.300, which is currently ineffective. A Notice of Nuisance is required to be served or
sent via Certified Mail as a prerequisite for criminal prosecution. Servicing costs up to $50 and
Certified Mail costs $8 and is almost always returned. This means that the accused does not
receive the Notice of Violation. The Prosecutor characterizes this as ineffective and
unnecessary, and the proposed change would be more effective and less costly. It would allow
the City to mail notice to the last known address, post notice conspicuously, or allow the City
Code Enforcement Officer to deliver the notice.

The Nuisance Code is currently embedded in zoning ordinances under Section 11.300. It may be
moved in the future, but not under this proposal. With the current proposal, notice will be
considered to have been received three days after mailing or posting the notice.

Commissioner Flynn asked for clarification of the language and if the different methods of
communication must all be employed or just one. The language is "or" meaning that any one
method would be considered adequate.

Commissioner Luker proposed a change to the recommendation. As the mail is not always
reliable, three days post mailing is usually reasonable. He proposed making a conspicuous
posting on the property a requirement to ensure that notice is provided.

Chair Cragun asked if this is standard language in other cities. Mr. Crowell stated that he had
not researched it. Commissioner Luker's only concern was the mail. Commissioner Flynn asked
if they could turn to other options if the mail does not go through. Commissioner Luker pointed
out that under the current proposal, mail would be assumed to have been received and there is no
motivation to try any other means.

Mr. Crowell brainstormed potential issues with each method and explained that they must figure
out how to reach the person responsible for the nuisance who may not live at the property. They
can mail to the last known address or leave notice at the property. The property owner, however,
may not see it if they do not live on the property. Chair Cragun commented that all three
methods can be tried and still fail to reach the property owner. If a property owner lives out of
state and two methods are required, it might be impossible to comply.

Kory Luker moved to forward a POSITIVE RECOMMENDATION to the City Council
for the Text Amendment application as proposed in Application 2023-32, with the addition
that two of the three items be required. Michael Kraupp seconded the motion. Vote on
motion: Ulises Flynn-Aye; Kory Luker-Aye; Erik Swanson-Aye; Michael Kraupp-Aye;
Debbie Cragun-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
