Bird's Eye View
THE LOG LINE Midvale City is looking to rename their historic Main Street area with a
name that appeals to locals in Midvale and the surrounding valley as new developments
revitalize the area.

BIRD'S EYE VIEW THE OBJECTIVE The objective is to fuel the vibrancy of the art, food
and cultural center in Midvale's Main Street area by attracting residents of the Wasatch
Front to live, work and play there.

The Specific Goals
The general objective is to improve and energize the Main Street neighborhood, which is
represented by five goals:
   o Attract new lnvestment
   . Support Existing Businesses
   o Strengthen Residential Areas
   . lmprove Connectivity
   . lmprove Neighborhood ldentity
Sharpening Our Focus
While we intend to appeal to as many Utahans and visitors as possible, catering to every
person's taste in not just impossible, but would lead to bland design and marketing that
appeals to the lowest common denominator. Such results are rarely appealing and always
weak on persuasion. We've strategically narrowed our sights on a more specific slice of
our demographic that is accessible yet aspirational, and who's opinions help sway those
around them       a deliberate tactic for magnifying our efforts and getting more bang for
our marketing -bucks. The names we've developed will appeal differently to every person
in this room yet are selected and fine-tuned to fully resonate with our key target audience.
Let's introduce them by putting faces and names to their personas...

Our Audience
Using the market analysis completed by Webb Mgmt. for the proiect, our target
audiences have been identified as the Young & Resfress, and Bright Young
Professional segments. The aspirational personas sitting at the bullseye of our
target are between 28 and 35 years old. We've named these persorras Camilla and
Cameron.

        A.     DISCUSS MIDVALE COMMUNITY GRANT PROGRAM
Matt Dahl said during the 2022 Budget Retreat, the City Council expressed interest in
developing a formal and open process for providing grants to local non-profit
organizations that serve Midvale. ln the past, the City has dispersed over $125,000
annually in grants to non-profit organizations, including the Midvale Arts Council, Boys
and Girls Club, Midvale Community Building Community, Ute Conference Youth Football
League, and the Midvale Historical Museum. The grant funding provided by Midvale has
been allocated as part of the annual budget process. Historically, the grants have gone
to organizations that have had a longterm record of providing services in Midvale, though
there has not been an open application process for organizations to request funding. ln
order to structure a formal and open program that would allow for non-profit organizations
to apply for grants, the City Council will need to provide staff with direction regarding the
following:

Program Goals: The City Council will need to provide direction regarding the goal or
goals they would like to achieve through the new grant program. These goals may include
maximizing residents served, supporting a particular type of social service, increasing the
types of services provided in Midvale, etc.

Eligibility: The City Council will need to provide direction to the staff regarding the
eligibility criteria of the grant program. Eligibility requirements, and scoring criteria, could
