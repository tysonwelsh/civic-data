   4. PUBLIC COMMENTS

Stan Pappas addressed the council with concerns regarding the local noise ordinance,
specifically focusing on issues related to barking dogs. He presented a letter to the council
(Attachment A) which included a copy of the 2022 noise ordinance, noting its extensive
coverage of various nuisance noises but pointing out the lack of specificity regarding barking
dogs. Mr. Pappas argued that while the county ordinance does mention barking, the language
is vague and subjective, leading to inconsistent enforcement. He cited personal experiences,
including a noise log he maintained and conflicting guidance from three different law
enforcement officers. He urged the council to review and consider amending the ordinance to
provide clearer and more enforceable standards that better protect residents from ongoing
disturbances caused by barking dogs.

Chris Best spoke in opposition to a proposed subdivision of a parcel located directly in front of
his home. He identified himself as a neighbor to Mayor Eric Barney and expressed concern
about increasing density in the area. Best reminisced about moving to Magna for its spacious
feel and stated that ongoing development was leading to overcrowding, comparable to feeling
like "sardines." He criticized developers for not considering the consequences of their projects,
particularly regarding traffic congestion and insufficient parking. Citing issues with vehicles
blocking driveways and overcrowded streets near the area by the former theater and Walmart,
Mr. Best requested that the specific parcel east of 2878 South Bucha Near Drive not be
subdivided. Instead, he proposed an alternative approach where adjacent parcels could have
been combined and divided in a way that would better accommodate residents’ parking and
space needs.

   5. STAKEHOLDER REPORTS
      A. Unified Police Department

Chief Del Craig presented the crime report for May, noting a reduction in both calls for service
and the overall crime rate compared to May of the previous year. He highlighted a noticeable
increase in homelessness within the city and announced that the department would begin
formally tracking encounters with homeless individuals to better quantify and understand the
scope of the issue. Lieutenant Benedict would assist in establishing this tracking process. The
department had recently investigated a homeless encampment near Copper Park, where
individuals had cut holes in the fence to access the golf course area. Although no one was
found at the site during the day, there was concern that the area was being used at night.
Chief Craig expressed hope that the upcoming cleanup of the wooded area would reduce its
appeal for encampments. Plans were also underway to repair the fence lines near both the
park and the dog park to prevent further unauthorized access.

In response to a question from Council Member Audrey Pierce, Chief Craig clarified that the
"public order" crime category encompasses a wide range of disturbances and interpersonal
conflicts, such as keeping the peace and child custody exchanges. He acknowledged that it

for tracking homelessness. Chief Craig confirmed they currently did not, but planned to begin
attributing a specific code to better monitor related encounters. He mentioned a perceived

       B. Financial Report

Dave Sanderson delivered a financial report as of April 30, 2025, noting that 83% of the fiscal
year had elapsed. He reported strong financial performance, particularly on the revenue side.
Sales tax collections were significantly up, placing the city around seventh or eighth when
compared to 62 other entities. Building permit revenues were also higher than expected. On
the expenditure side, spending remained relatively low. Mr. Sanderson highlighted the creation
of a new fund titled the "Magna Designated Fund," which consolidates revenues from the local
option sales tax, Google franchise taxes, and American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funding.
These revenues were moved from the primary fund to this new, exclusive-use fund under the
Municipal Services District (MSD) agreement. He projected that this fund would generate
approximately $3 to $3.5 million annually. If the city intends to allocate any of these funds for
the upcoming fiscal year, a budget adjustment will be necessary; otherwise, the funds will
continue to accrue in reserves. Mr. Sanderson also provided an update on the Pleasant Green
Cemetery fund, clarifying its name after a correction by Mayor Eric Barney. The fund is
projected to operate at a $30,000 annual deficit, which means it has approximately three and a
half years of fund balance remaining under current conditions before depleting its reserves.
Lastly, he mentioned that the community reinvestment area, including the Magna
redevelopment area, has not yet generated property tax revenue but has incurred some
expenditures as loans that will be reimbursed once development occurs. He noted that once
the county completes a financial transfer, an existing fund balance will be added to the
community reinvestment area.

       B. Discussion of Capital Improvement Projects on Existing Roads with Limited Right-of-
          Way, Configuration Discussion and Determination

Jacob Harris reviewed his prepared presentation (Attachment B) and presented information
about an upcoming sidewalk construction project slated for Old Town Magna along 9100 West,
spanning from 2700 South to 2820 South. Scheduled for construction later in the fall, this
Community Development Block Grant-funded project aims to enhance pedestrian
infrastructure by introducing a buffered sidewalk with an adjacent park strip. He explained that
while many Magna streets currently lack curb, gutter, and sidewalk systems, this project would
address those deficiencies. The new design would include a five-foot sidewalk and a five-foot
park strip, aimed at increasing pedestrian safety. This configuration will reduce on-street
parking availability, particularly by prohibiting parking on one side of the street to preserve bi-
directional traffic flow. If parking were allowed on both sides, the roadway would be too narrow
to support two-way traffic. The project is still in the design phase, with bidding anticipated to
open in late July, followed by construction beginning in late summer or early fall. Residents will
be notified once a contractor is selected.

firm specializes in such assessments and recently completed a similar project for the city of
Kearns. The evaluation includes a thorough analysis by a team of engineers who examined
the building's existing conditions, code compliance, operational systems, and requirements for
life safety and seismic improvements. The team has conducted onsite investigations, including
checking above ceilings and within walls, and has produced preliminary findings. These
findings are being compiled into a report that will include detailed cost estimates. In addition to
the technical review, two conceptual designs have been developed for remodeling the
building’s interior to better accommodate city council meetings and public events. The
proposed designs also address improvements for universal accessibility and code compliance.
Mr. Slabbert emphasized that these are early-stage concepts meant to assess the project's
feasibility based on cost and regulatory factors. The study is nearing completion, with about
two weeks remaining until the first draft of the report is finalized. Mr. Slabbert confirmed that he
would return at the next city council meeting to present the final findings and a recommended
scheme.

      A. American Ramp Proposal for Skate Park/Pump Track at Copper Park

The council engaged in an extensive and at times divided discussion about a proposed
investment in new recreational infrastructure at Copper Park, specifically the construction of a
new skate park and a pump track.

City Manager David Brickey summarized a prior presentation from May 13, which outlined a
cost estimate of $217,581 for the skate park, with a potential $17,000 savings through a
particular bid process. The pump track was estimated at $250,000, though that figure
remained uncertain pending site selection.

Council Member Trish Hull strongly supported both projects, citing the importance of providing
safe and enriching spaces for teens, especially given Magna’s lower health indicators
compared to other parts of Salt Lake County. She stressed that the current skate park was out
of compliance and a safety hazard, advocating for immediate action, particularly as the new
skate park design was finalized and ready for implementation.

Council Member Audrey Pierce expressed concerns about starting new projects without
completing existing ones, such as the unfinished dog park, which generated frequent resident
complaints. She also highlighted broader infrastructure needs—such as curb, gutter, sidewalk
repairs, improved lighting, and Hawk crosswalk lights—and questioned the long-term return on
investment if the park remained on a year-to-year lease with Kennecott. She suggested
temporarily removing unsafe skate park elements without committing to full reconstruction at
this time. She acknowledged the importance of teen amenities but echoed the need for
balanced spending, emphasizing that improvements in infrastructure—especially sidewalks
and lighting—were essential safety concerns. She noted that although the skate park might fall
under a “use at your own risk” category, it was still crucial to prioritize basic infrastructure
needs, and she expressed reluctance to support the full scope of both projects immediately.
Pierce also recommended leveraging Redevelopment Agency (RDA) funds for broader
community upgrades and pointed out that not all of Copper Park fell outside the RDA
boundary.

Mayor Eric Barney emphasized that infrastructure in Old Magna, especially roads, sidewalks,
and lighting, remained his top priority. While he acknowledged the benefits of recreational
amenities, he cautioned against heavy investment in park assets on leased land without a
more secure arrangement with Kennecott. He expressed conditional support for the skate park
project only if parallel investments were made in other pressing infrastructure areas.

Ultimately, the council was split on priorities: while some members advocated for immediate
investment in youth-focused recreational projects, others prioritized infrastructure and were
wary of further development on leased property.

Council Member Audrey Pierce raised a procedural question regarding the timeline for
completing the proposed skate park improvements, particularly whether the project would

park would likely not be completed before the holiday, it could be finished by September. David
Brickey added that due to safety concerns and feedback from the Utah Trust, removal of the

Council Member Hull moved to approve the Skate Park as discussed tonight. Motion
was seconded by Council Member Pierce; motion passed with a 3-1 vote, Council
Member Sudbury being the “No” vote and Council Member Prokopis absent from the

Addressing prior concerns raised about park lighting, David Brickey confirmed that the copper
wiring in the park lights had indeed been stolen. The city is exploring replacement with
aluminum wiring, which holds less resale value and is therefore less likely to be targeted. Dan
Torres explained two costed options for replacement: trenching and rewiring with aluminum for
approximately $15,000, or installing solar-powered, dimmable bolt-on lights at around $3,000.
The city is currently determining whether these expenses fall under Magna’s budget or that of
the Municipal Services District (MSD).

Mayor Eric Barney noted that Salt Lake County Public Works, which is contracted to maintain
the park, has an annual budget of $270,000 and questioned whether lighting repairs could be
covered under this existing agreement. Mr. Brickey confirmed ongoing efforts to get clarity
from the county, including on other facility repair costs such as the ballpark lighting. Despite
the urgency, the council confirmed that lighting repairs would not be completed before the
Fourth of July holiday.

      C. Discussion Regarding Pickleball Court at Magna Neighborhood Park
The council discussed the potential funding and development of pickleball courts at Magna
Neighborhood Park, as well as the resurfacing of existing tennis courts at Copper Park.
Dan Torres provided background, explaining that the cost for a new pickleball court—including
concrete, fencing, lighting, and special surfacing—was approximately $67,644, with a
recommended 10% contingency, bringing the total to $74,408. The project location would be at
Magna Neighborhood Park, a site that the city is in the process of acquiring through parcel
transfers from both the RDA and Salt Lake County.
Council Member Audrey Pierce and others expressed general support for both pickleball and
tennis amenities but stressed the need for a comprehensive vision for the entire park area
before committing to specific installations. Mayor Eric Barney and Council Member Trish Hull
agreed, highlighting the value of community input and the potential for broader planning.
Council Member Sudbury reiterated concerns about balancing these recreational
improvements with other urgent city needs, including infrastructure and building expenses,
though she clarified her support for recreational projects in principle.
Mr. Torres clarified that although the grant application deadline was imminent (June 16),
council approval to appropriate funds for the project did not mean immediate execution. The
city could still adjust project specifics after the grant was submitted and even after funds were
awarded. Furthermore, the grant request would be strengthened by showing that the city was
willing to provide matching funds, a factor known to increase the likelihood of approval from the
TRCC board.
Mayor Barney emphasized that the TRCC board was particularly interested in supporting
projects on the west side of the valley and viewed city-backed, shovel-ready proposals

      D. Discussion Regarding Tree/Landscape Plan for Copper and Neighborhood Parks
The council reviewed a proposal to commission a tree planting analysis at Copper Park and
Magna Neighborhood Park at a cost of $19,185 from MGBA, a Salt Lake City-based
consultant.
Dan Torres explained that the scope of work would include assessing soil quality, irrigation
availability, planting locations, and tree species recommendations, though it would focus only
on select areas of each park.
Mayor Eric Barney and multiple council members questioned the necessity of such a cost,
especially considering the availability of similar services from TreeUtah, a nonprofit
organization known for conducting environmental assessments and organizing volunteer-
based planting events at no charge. Mayor Barney highlighted successful past collaborations
with TreeUtah, including a tree planting initiative at Pleasant Green Park, and suggested that
engaging them could both save money and foster community involvement.
Council Member Audrey Pierce expressed a preference for tying tree planting efforts to
community events like Earth Day or local service projects to increase public participation and
awareness.
Council Member Sudbury raised concerns about the aging and deteriorating trees at Copper
Park and proposed exploring a potential partnership with Kennecott, which owns the park land,
to support replanting efforts and maintenance.
The council ultimately agreed to table the item for now, directing staff to engage with TreeUtah
and investigate alternative partnership opportunities, including with Kennecott. Additionally,
Dan Torres noted that a broader parks plan is being pursued as part of a grant application
through the Municipal Services District (MSD), which would help formalize long-term planning
and community input for park improvements specific to Magna-owned properties. It was
clarified that while Salt Lake County maintains authority over regional parks like Pleasant
Green and Magna Regional, the city’s parks plan would focus exclusively on parks under
Magna’s jurisdiction, with shared planning efforts encouraged for consistency across
overlapping usage areas.
      E. Discussion and Potential Action Regarding Ordinance 2025-O-09, Adopting the
         FY2026 Magna City Fee Schedule

David Bricker reviewed the proposed fee schedule (Attachment C) and recapped that the
proposed fee changes were initially introduced to the council on April 8. Due to statutory


    Magna City Council • Webster Community Center • 8952 W Magna Main Street • Magna, UT 84044
requirements from the state legislature, fees related to building costs had to be reviewed by
the Planning Commission before final adoption. This occurred on May 8, and the Planning
Commission gave a positive recommendation. Mr. Brickey also noted that he had previously

adjustments, detailed on page eight of the packet, were developed by a subcommittee and
