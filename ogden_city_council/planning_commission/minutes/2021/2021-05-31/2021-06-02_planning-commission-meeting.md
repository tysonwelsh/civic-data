4. CBD Site Plan, for plaza revisions at “The Corner” at 2501 Washington Boulevard. (Attachment 4)

   Mr. Brierley stated while the Commission had approved improvement plans for The Corner previously, the approval
   has expired and a new design has now been proposed. The intent is to create a gathering place to connect to the
   nearby Arts District. Features of the previous design which are to be maintained include a bulletin wall, seating and
   storage areas while the artistic elements of these features have been changed. A multi-colored metal sculpture
   will create a focal point, with inner seating and space for small groups to gather. LED lighting will be installed into
   the pavement to add interest at night-time. Multi-colored seating of various forms is provided around the plaza
   area with some overhead festival lighting for these areas. The proposed materials will improve the appearance at
   the Corner and will be easily maintained. Both Parks Department and Police have reviewed the design and have
   suggested the bulletin board have an 18” clearance from the ground to allow visibility and that all seating areas
   longer than 4’ have a vertical element to prevent the potential of people sleeping. He reviewed the plans in context
   with the General Plan and indicated the Corner design creates a link between 25 th Street, the Amphitheater and the
   Arts District and would benefit the area. Staff recommendation is for approval subject to design revisions to the
   bulletin board to be off the ground to see activity behind the board and seating be designed so it does not
   encourage sleeping on it.

   Commissioner Schade asked if the lighting will be maintainable, noting the underground lighting at the Junction
   has never functioned properly. Ms. Christy McBride stated they have been working with a design engineer with the
   lighting and feel the new technology will work as proposed, noting lighting at the Junction has recently been
   repaired.

   Mr. Keith Stoddard, 1250 25th Street expressed concern that public art should not be an attractive nuisance,
   suggesting the proposed art feature appears as a jungle gym and felt some would try to climb it. He was
   concerned the 4” openings would allow hands and arms to penetrate and could create a liability to Ogden City in
   terms of potential for injuries. Ms. McBride stated the angles of the metal re designed for safety. Mr. Stratford
   reminded the Commission their review is to based solely on whether the features proposed are compatible with the
   downtown and consistent with the General Plan.

   MOTION:     A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to recommend approval of the plaza based on the
               findings the proposed revisions and additional corrections are consistent with the General Plan in
               “enlivening downtown with artists, concerts, performers and galleries”, and enhancing and expanding
               the “regional open space needs in the urban environment while at the same time meeting needs of the
               residents who live in the downtown/”, the proposed materials with revisions suggested are compatible
               with downtown materials, and the proposed design with the suggested revisions promote a human
               scale, is inviting to the general public and creates a quality space. Approval is subject to design
               revisions to the bulletin board to be off the ground to discourage hiding, and seating be designed so it
               does not encourage sleeping. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed
               unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell, Garner, Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and

               Graf voting aye.

5. CBD Site Plan, for modification of the east wall of the Bigelow Hotel, 2510 Washington Boulevard. (Attachment 5)

   Mr. Craig Widimier stated the Commission had previously approved treatment of the east wall of the Bigelow, but
   as work began and stucco removed, brick has been exposed and is in good shape. The request is now to retain the
   brick as the original material and to install new stucco only on the CMU area of the east wall as well as shielding
   utility equipment in the area with a new screening wall. Some exposed openings will now be covered with matching
   brick. He felt the new design is more in keeping with the historic appearance of the hotel but still provide a modern
   appearance and create interest and fit with the proposed plaza area. Mr. Stratford expressed the owners should
   continue to work with the design of the plaza area.

   Mr. Simpson stated the east wall had been covered with various materials, and the previous approval had been to
   cover the entire area with stucco. The new proposal is to preserve existing brick and install stucco only on the
   existing cinderblock portion of the wall. The previous action included a potential mural on the stucco wall which is
   still appropriate. A new glass entryway is proposed which will be bordered with stucco to better define the
   entrance, and a new 10’’ tall screening wall is proposed on the south portion to screen vents and utility equipment.
   He stated the applicant will be required to obtain an access easement for the entryway as it uses the adjacent
   property. Staff recommendation is for approval, subject to the applicant obtaining an access easement for the new
   east entryway and obtaining and satisfying all department staff review comments.

  MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the CBD site plan based on the
          findings the colors and materials are compatible with the site and surrounding buildings, the architectural
          lines of the building are compatible with the site and surrounding buildings and that adequate screening of
          utilities is provided. Approval is subject to the applicant obtaining an access easement for the new east
          entrance as well as obtaining and satisfying all department staff comments. Motion was seconded by
          Commissioner Sandau and passed unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell, Garner, Safsten, Sandau,
          Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf voting aye.

7. Conditional Use Permit/Preliminary Group Dwelling Approval, to allow Midtown Village, 1450 Washington Boulevard.
   (Attachment 7)

   Ms. Marshae Stokes stated this proposal is for construction of 68 townhome units as well as commercial and
   mixed use buildings along Washington Boulevard. She felt the proposed use would bring life back into this portion
   of Washington Boulevard and felt it is compatible with the surrounding neighborhood. She stated an existing creek
   will remain and is designed as a gathering spot for residents, and presented an updated site plan to commissioners
   based on the recommendations of Staff. She asked the conditional use permit be approved subject to Staff
   recommendations, stating the intent is to be compliant with the Code. Commissioner Southwick asked if the units
   would be owner occupied or rentals. Commissioner Safsten asked about the existing billboard sign, hoping it could
   be removed. Ms. Stokes answered the intent is for units to be rentals at this time and indicated while they are
   working with the owner of the billboard sign, the existing proposal is to build around it for now.

   Ms. Corbridge stated the application is for a group dwelling, consisting of commercial, mixed use and residential
   buildings proposed. A proposed three-story commercial building will also accommodate commercial storefronts as
   well as 12 dwelling units on the second and third floors. Behind the commercial buildings fifteen townhomes are
   proposed, with most facing 14th Street. Building materials are proposed to be brick, stucco and siding, and the units
   will be serviced by interior private roads. Each townhome will include an attached two-car garage, and an additional
   52 uncovered parking spaces are shown on the site. Amenities include the exposed canal and a plaza area along
   Washington Boulevard. The review of the Commission for preliminary group dwelling is to determine whether the
   project can comply with the regulations of the Code, and once these conditions are satisfied, the project would then
   return for final group dwelling approval to assure compliance with the preliminary approval. She reviewed the
   proposed site plan, stating there is adequate landscaping, but the project does not meet the minimum 25% of

13. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to allow minor amendments to approved conditional use
   permits to be approved by the Community Development Director. (Attachment 13)

   Mr. Brierley stated in order to expedite processes, Staff is suggesting minor conditional use amendments be
   reviewed at the Staff level. He stated this is consistent with the General Plan in that it creates more responsive
   local government and promotes a business-friendly environment by reducing bureaucracy. The provision would
   apply only to minor conditional use amendments and due to the State laws governing conditional use permits, the
   review is limited to identifying and mitigating impacts of a particular use. A typical example would be for the
   replacement of cell antennas on an existing cell tower site which would not increase the height or outer dimensions
   of the array. Ground equipment for a cell tower also could be approved at the staff level so long as the previously
   approved equipment area is not enlarged. He identified other situations where a minor amendment might be
   approvable by Staff including changes to a building exterior which does not change the requirements of the original
   permit, a change of use which is within the same category and not create or increase detrimental effects on the
   surrounding community and building additions which would not increase the floor area more than 10% and be less
   than 2,000 square feet. It also might include a modification to parking or loading where no more than 2,000 square
   feet of impervious surface is added. Staff recommendation is for approval of the ordinance amendment as
   proposed.

   Commissioner Blaisdell expressed concern the language allows the Community & Economic Director to approve
   these and would feel more comfortable if it indicated Planning Staff or Planning Manager. Mr. Stratford stated the
   Ordinance often identifies the Director but the definition allows the Director or his designee, which would typically

                                                                                                              Page | 11
   be the Planning Manager or staff. Commissioner Safsten asked if other cities allow staff approval for conditional
   use amendments. Mr. Brierley stated he had contacted some cities and modifications to conditional use permits
   are approved by staff in some cities in Utah. Chair Graf noted the amendment would eliminate some red tape and
   felt it would work within reason, Discussion continued relating to the conditional use process, and it was clarified
   original conditions of approval would remain intact, and would be applicable only if the change of nonconforming
   use did not create any new impacts, and new conditions cannot be set by Staff, and if warranted it would be
   referred to the Commission.

   Mr. Stratford asked the Commission also to identify the appropriate appeal authority for Staff decisions. Unless
   identified otherwise in the Ordinance, appeal goes to the Board of Adjustment and their findings are limited to
   whether Staff made an error in the interpretation of the Ordinance. He asked if the Commission would prefer the
   appeal of a conditional use amendment to go to the Planning Commission rather than the Board. As only one
   appeal is allowed before it would go to the District Court, Commission felt appeals should be directed to the
   Planning Commission, stating the Commission has a better understanding of what a conditional use permit is and
   how impacts can be identified and mitigated.

As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Schade to close the public hearing. Motion
was seconded by Commissioner Garner and passed unanimously.

   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to recommend approval of the Ordinance language as
           proposed based on the findings it is consistent with the objectives of the General Plan and does fit with
           the provisions of the Zoning Ordinance, recommending appeals be made to the Planning Commission.
           Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously with Commissioners
           Blaisdell, Garner, Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Stoker and Graf voting aye.

   State law. (Attachment 14)
