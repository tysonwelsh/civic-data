       b. Ordinance No. 25-51 Petition to Rename the Cul-de-sac at 2290 West (off 7600 South)
          to Poison Spider Circle
          Council Office Director Alan Anderson reported that the Council Office received an
          application to rename the cul-de-sac at 2290 West to Poison Spider Circle. He explained
          that Poison Spider was a trail in Moab, Utah. Mr. Anderson noted that three of the five
          homeowners in the cul-de-sac signed a petition in support of the rename, and one of the
          remaining two homeowners had sent an email in opposition. He mentioned the first name
          proposed, Off-Road Circle, was denied by the County because the word “road” was not
          allowed in a street name, and Poison Spider Circle was found by the County to not pose a
          conflict.

           Tyler Patrick, West Jordan resident, said he petitioned for a name change because of mail
           delivery issues in the cul-de-sac. He believed a lot of houses in the City were numbered

           the same and noted it had been an problem for an ambulance finding his neighbor’s home.
           Mr. Patrick mentioned the Postal Service had suggested renaming the cul-de-sac.
           Responding to a question from Council Member Bloom, he replied that Poison Spider
           Circle was chosen because most of the residents in the cul-de-sac were off roaders.
           Responding to a question from Vice Chair Bedore, Mr. Patrick mentioned receiving a
           response in opposition from one neighbor but not from the other. He said the neighbor in
           opposition did not want the name changed at all because of the effort involved.

           Chair Whitelock opened a public hearing at 7:20 pm.
           Comments:
           Rob Bennett, West Jordan resident, read aloud from City Code that relevant criteria taken
           into consideration for a proposed rename included a high level of community support. He
           said Poison Spider Trail was 232 miles away, and not of significant importance to West
           Jordan City. Mr. Bennett suggested the proposed name could be considered offensive to
           the community, and expressed the opinion that 67% support did not qualify as “widely
           supported.” He suggested property values in the surrounding community would be
           affected.
           Ron Cossure, West Jordan resident, accompanied by his wife, Denise, said they resided in
           the cul-de-sac in question. He spoke of the inconvenience and expense to change their
           address on everything, and asked the Council to deny the proposed change. He suggested
           the petitioner could change their house number by two digits to solve any mail delivery
           issues.
           Josh Standfield, West Jordan resident, lived in the cul-de-sac in question and had emailed
           the Council. He spoke of the financial burden a change of address would impose, and
           commented that updating GIS would take time and possibly pose a safety issue. Mr.
           Standfield felt Poison Spider had nothing to do with West Jordan and he did not think
           people would believe it was a real address. Mr. Standfield noted that the City recently
           placed a sign on the corner to help with directions, which he believed was a good middle
           ground.
           Council Member Lamb explained that some streets in West Jordan had names, and some
           did not. He lives on Highland Hollow Drive, but his mailing address only included
           numbers. He further noted that “Highland Hollow” had nothing specifically to do with
           West Jordan. Council Member Lamb felt that someone in the cul-de-sac would be unhappy
           whether the request was approved or denied, and suggested one solution would be for
           the neighbors to find a name on which they could all agree.

           Council Member Shelton said he lived on Copper Valley Lane, which also had an
           associated number coordinate. He did not understand or feel the significance of the
           requested name, as he was not an off-roader, and he understood comments indicating
           that “poison” could have a negative connotation.

           Chair Whitelock closed the public hearing at 7:29 pm.

           Council Member Bloom asked how the City defined “community support.” She

           process was clear and allow all residents to feel represented. Council Member Bloom

           with the comments from Mr. Bennett and did not believe the proposed name change met

           City Code did not contain language requiring cul-de-sacs to have a name. Council Member
           Green agreed with Council Members Bedore and Jacob. He believed issues with postal
           service, delivery, and ambulance or police response fell outside the ordinance for street
           name change. Council Member Green believed it would be prudent for the City to
           administratively change the address if there were delivery or emergency response issues.

           Chair Whitelock emphasized that the City added a sign to improve the situation. She said
           many streets and circles shared the same name or number. Chair Whitelock did not
           believe the request met City Code.

                        to rename the cul-de-sac at 2290 West (off 7600 South) to Poison

 6. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Ordinance No. 25-54 Amendment to West Jordan City Code Title 7, adding Chapter 6
          (Golf Carts)
          Senior Assistant City Attorney Patrick Boice noted that the Committee of the Whole
          discussed possible amendments to City Code regarding golf carts at two previous
          meetings. The proposed ordinance was a result of those meetings.

           Council Member Lamb did not believe there was demand in the community to allow golf
           carts on City streets, and believed they could pose a safety issue. Council Member Bloom
           felt that residents were already using golf carts in some neighborhoods and expressed the
           opinion it was better to regulate the responsibility rather than pretend the situation was
           not happening. She believed the proposed ordinance set clear safety rules, while
           maintaining neighborhood character and protecting children. She felt there was a need
           for education.

           Council Member Green felt the request had the goal to look at alternative forms of
           transportation. He suggested golf carts could be a clean mode of transportation for

City Council Meeting Minutes                Tuesday, October 14, 2025                          Page 5
           individuals who may not be able to use any other form of transportation. He expressed
           support for residents using alternatives rather than relying on cars. Council Member
           Jacob agreed there had not been a lot of demand or clamor for the proposed ordinance,
           but it would provide more freedom by allowing something not previously allowed. He
           further stated that if the ordinance proved to be a problem, it could be amended.

           MOTION:      Council Member Jacob moved to APPROVE the amendment to West
                        Jordan City Code Title 7, adding Chapter 6 for Golf Carts.
                        Council Member Bloom seconded the motion.

           YES:       Pamela Bloom, Kelvin Green, Zach Jacob, Kent Shelton
