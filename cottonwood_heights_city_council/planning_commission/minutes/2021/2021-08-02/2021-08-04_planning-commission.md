14   Community and Economic Development Director, Mike Johnson reported that the above item
15   involves the rezoning of properties from their current designation of Residential Multi-Family

17   small dirt overflow lot used for trail parking. It was purchased in 2009 from Salt Lake County

19   Bonneville Shoreline Trails. Mr. Johnson reported that plans are well underway and the applicants
20   are required to show substantial progress on construction by the end of 2021. A property rendering
21   was displayed. Mr. Johnson reported that staff intends to create connectivity in the area and they
22   will be encouraging the Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”) to construct a sidewalk.
23   Due to the grade change, the Dog Park will be accessed from the southern portion of the shared-
24   use path. Fencing will include a six-foot split rail with a wood aesthetic.

27                  Possible Recommendation to the City Council on a City-Initiated Zoning Text
28                  Amendment to Chapter 19.89 – “Short-Term Rentals”. Continued from the
29                  July 21, 2021, Planning Commission Meeting.
30
31   Mr. Johnson reported that the above item involves a City-initiated zoning text amendment to
32   Chapter 19.89 “Short-Term Rentals”. The Commission recommended a final draft be prepared
33   that includes the definition of short-term rental being amended to anything less than 30 consecutive
34   days. The previous provision allowing for rentals of less than three days had been removed. The
35   new ordinance clearly states that short-term rentals are strictly prohibited and illegal in the City’s
36   single-family residential zones. The intent of the ordinance was to specify where short-term rentals
37   are allowed.
38

40   who are not concerned that it is against the law. He asked if the owner can be required to post
41   signage either on their website or the property stating it is not a legally permitted rental. City
42   Attorney, Shane Topham, reported the City is limited by statute.
43
44   Enforcement issues were discussed. Mr. Johnson explained that the Accessory Dwelling Unit
45   (“ADU”) legislation specifies that there can be enforcement if a property is licensed as an ADU

 3          3.1     (Project CUP-21-011) – A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Take

 5                  Permit to Operate a Home Preschool for up to 12 Children (Mrs. Dana’s
 6                  Preschool) at 7085 South Watermill Circle.
 7
 8   Senior City Planner, Andy Hulka, presented the staff report and stated that the above request is for
 9   a Conditional Use Permit to operate a home preschool for up to 12 children at the property located
10   at 7085 South Watermill Circle. The preschool will accommodate children aged three to five with
11   alternating classes between 8:45 a.m. and 3:00 p.m. Monday through Friday. Each class will be
12   approximately three hours in duration with no more than 12 children at any one time. A sketch of
13   the general drop-off area was displayed. Mr. Hulka reported that there is a 15-minute window
14   before and after each session for drop-offs and pickups. Children will be walked to and from their
15   vehicles. Based on review of the application, staff recommended approval with conditions set
16   forth in the staff report.
17
18   Mr. Hulka reported that several public comments were received regarding parking and traffic. An
19   email from the applicant, Dana Middlemiss, was read. She previously operated preschools in
20   Montana, Pennsylvania, and Utah for a total of seven years and is experienced with traffic flow
21   issues. She has never had 12 cars lined up at a time and has found that providing a 15-minute
22   window before and after sessions has alleviated the issue. The only congestion she has experienced
23   was with utility trucks being located near her home and garbage cans on curbs on collection day.
24   Noise is often an issue with these types of home occupations and the applicant noted that recess is
25   brief and hours during the day.
26
27   Chair Coutts opened the public hearing.
28
29   Ruth Ellen Bean gave her address as 7113 Watermill Way and stated that 10 years earlier,
30   Ms. Middlemiss operated a preschool in the neighborhood. There was never an issue with traffic.
31   Ms. Bean expressed support for the request and believed Ms. Middlemiss has the experience and
32   education to successfully teach children. She urged the Commission to approve the request.
33
34   Pam Moore gave her address as 3195 East Candy Tuft Street and expressed support for
35   Ms. Middlemiss. She considered her to be an amazing teacher and any children attending her
36   school are fortunate to be under her care. She did not consider traffic to be an issue and expressed
37   support for the request.

38   Mr. Johnson presented the staff report and stated that the above matter involves a City-initiated
39   request to rezone properties located at 7725 South Wasatch Boulevard and 7828 South Prospector
40   Drive from their current designations to Public Facilities (PF). The request also includes a proposal
41   to amend the land use designation from Residential Medium-Density and Residential Low-Density
42   to Civic. The City entered into an Interlocal Agreement with Salt Lake County to construct a trail
43   overflow parking lot and public park on the property. That agreement was extended and requires
44   substantial progress to be made on the site improvements by the end of 2021. The improvements
45   have been budgeted and the work is underway; however, the zoning of the site is not in compliance
46   with the intended use. A rendering of the property was displayed. Mr. Johnson stated that

21          3.4     (Project ZTA-21-002) A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Make a
22                  Possible Recommendation to the City Council on a City-Initiated Zoning Text
23                  Amendment to Chapter 19.89 – “Short-Term Rentals”. Continued from the
24                  July 21, 2021, Planning Commission Meeting.
25
26   Mr. Johnson presented the staff report and stated that the above request involves a public hearing
27   and possible recommendation to the City Council on a City-initiated zoning text amendment to
28   Chapter 19.89 “Short-Term Rentals”. The Ordinance helps provide clarification to language and
29   provides clearer references to potential punishments for the illegal operation of a short-term rental
30   in areas that do not qualify. The Commission recommended a final draft be prepared that included
31   the definition of short-term rental being amended to be anything less than 30 consecutive days in
32   duration. He confirmed that the previous provision specified that anything less than three days
33   was not eligible and was removed. Verbiage was added to the new ordinance to clearly state that
34   short-term rentals are not allowed, are strictly prohibited, and are illegal in the City’s single-family
35   residential zones. The proposed language was reviewed.
36
37   Mr. Johnson reported that penalties were previously included by reference but are not written
38   directly in the body of the ordinance. He confirmed that the penalty for operating a short-term
39   rental in an area where it is illegal is a Class C misdemeanor. Each day a violation continues shall
40   be considered a separate offense. Staff recommended forwarding a positive recommendation to
41   the City Council.
