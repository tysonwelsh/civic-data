1.5. Consideration to Update Action Levels for Arsenic in Soil
Staff Engineer lll Josh Peterson offered a background on the EPA record of decision regarding
contaminated soils in Herriman. lt included the recommendation for allowable ranges for action levels
for remediation. The current action level exceeded the recommended range for certain land uses.

Herriman City code §10-15A-4 states the action levels with respect to residential property located within
a contamination area meant soils having a lead concentration of one thousand six hundred (1,600) mg/kg
or more, and/or an arsenic level of one hundred (100) mg/kg or more. Action levels with respect to
nonresidential property located within a contamination area meant soils having a lead concentration of
five thousand (5,000) mg/kg or more, and/or an arsenic level of eight hundred fifty (850) mg/kg or more.

Engineer Peterson presented the first option to require strict compliance and add verbiage to the code
to provide land uses according to the outlined record of decision. The action levels for arsenic and lead
would conform to the highest recommended value. The second option presented would adjust action
levels for lead on non-residential uses. Engineer Peterson explained the State requested modification to
code specifically regarding lead and noted this option would not consider an amendment to arsenic
levels.

The Commission briefly discussed the highest contamination level which would be acceptable. Engineer
Peterson noted this item would be brought back to the Commission at a future date.

allow additional sign provisions for large commercial, manufacturing, and mixed use
sites. (Public Hearing)

Applicant: Braiden Rindlisbacher

File No.: Z2022-014 & Z2022-015
Planning Manager Clinton Spencer explained the sign overlay zone would place provisions on large
commercial, manufacturing, and mixed-use signage. He explained wholistically, there was a need for
additional signage along transportation corridors. Planning Manager Spencer compared signage
requirements to the proposal and offered clarifications for signage located within 150 feet of Mountain
View Corridor or other arterial roadway displays. The overlay zone could only be placed on properties
with commercial uses which also abut major corridors.

Planning Manager Spencer presented the applicant request to amend the M-1 and C-2 zone to allow
multi-tenant signage which resulted in staff recommending the sign overlay zone. The requirements for
the overlay zone included the development be at least 20 acres of property for commercial,
manufacturing or mixed-use development, and have 200-feet of direct frontage along a state-owned
corridor and have gone through the conditional use process.

Planning Manager Spencer offered an overview of the signage standards:
= Within 50’ of State corridor property line
= 4S’ above average grade of MVC or other State-owned street
= 500 SF for active advertising/700 SF for static
= Maximum 800 SF combined
= Finishing similar to surrounding development
= Building design comes first, then sign
= Stone, brick, steel, other similar materials
= Cannot extend closer than 5’ from property line
"= Comply with §10-27-11 (electronic sign ordinance)

Planning Manager Spencer recommended approval of the proposal.
