Proposed Resolution 2025-23 declaring the intent of Ogden City to
purchase surplus property from the Ogden School District
        VICE CHAIR HYER MOVED TO REMOVE PROPOSED RESOLUTION 2025-23 FROM THE CONSENT
AGENDA. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA, ALL VOTING AYE.
        A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding Ogden School District’s surplus of the
Taylor Canyon Elementary School property came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated on August 21, 2025, the
Board of Education of the Ogden School District officially declared 4.23 acres of property, on which the former Taylor Canyon
Elementary School was located as surplus. As an eligible entity under state code, Ogden City may purchase the surplus property
for the purchase price as determined in state code. In order to facilitate the purchase, the City Council must adopt a resolution
declaring the City’s intent to purchase the property within 90 days of the District’s official notification. Official notification was
received by the City on September 2, 2025.
          Community Development Director Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
expound on the current proposal regarding the District’s surplus of Taylor Canyon Elementary School property; the elementary
school formerly located on the 4.23-acre site was demolished in 2022 and has since been functioning as open space maintained by
the District. On September 2, the Ogden School District notified the City of its intent to surplus the property, giving the City first
right of refusal until today. The resolution before the Council is not a binding purchase agreement, but rather a formal notification
of the City's interest in purchasing the property. If approved, the next steps would include property appraisals by both the City and
School District, which would be averaged to determine a sales price. The property is zoned R-1-6 residential, which allows for
housing as well as park use and while he could not provide a formal proposal regarding future use of the property, the
Administration has expressed a desire to consider maintaining the property as open space to some degree. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          The Council engaged in discussion with Mr. Smith regarding several points of concern related to the proposal. Council
member Blair asked about any restrictions that would remain intact if the City were to purchase the property and later sell a
portion to a developer. Mr. Smith confirmed that state surplus provisions would still apply to any future transfers or land sale.
Council member Blair asked about the urgency of acting on the proposal tonight and what would happen if the Council did not
meet the deadline. Mr. Smith explained that meeting the state-required timeline indicated good faith and prevented potential future
complications but acknowledged that the School District was unlikely to sell the property immediately if the timeline was not met.
It was a recommendation to operate within the minimum state-required timeframes to avoid complications. Mayor Nadolski added
that the timeline for this process had been challenging, as it had not allowed for proper budget planning or completion of an
ongoing parks utilization study.
          Chair White questioned a line in the resolution indicating that the Mayor and Council, through public engagement, had
determined strong community support for maintaining the property as open space. She inquired about the extent of this public
engagement, noting limited turnout at a meeting on-site on a rainy day. Mayor Nadolski clarified that a neighborhood Facebook
page, outreach efforts, and a community survey informed this position. Council member Richey stated it would have been good
for the Council to receive information regarding the public engagement. He then expressed support for the timing of the action and
why an appraisal had not yet been ordered for the property.
          Council member Choberka expressed concern about the resolution specifying open space use while the property would
be appraised at its higher residential zoning value. She wondered if there was room for negotiation with the School District
regarding price if the intended use was for open space.
          Council Member Richey addressed misconceptions, noting that rumors about apartments being built on the site were
unfounded, as the current zoning would not permit it without a rezoning process.
          Vice Chair Hyer suggested exploring an interlocal agreement similar to what was done with the Mount Lewis Park
property, where the school district maintained ownership while the City took responsibility for developing and maintaining it. He
encouraged residents to also make their desires known at the School District meetings, emphasizing the importance of community
input in the decision-making process. He also pointed out that there are other parks in close proximity to this site. Mayor Nadolski
underscored that the City and School District share the same taxpayers and that working together would be advantageous. He
expressed disappointment in the political maneuvering that had prevented straightforward discussions regarding the proposal and
emphasized his support for consolidating open space to foster a larger, more active park area for the neighborhood.
          Council member Myers inquired about the timeline for the City’s Parks Utilization Study, which could provide
information on the best use of the land in the area. Mayor Nadolski responded that although the Taylor Canyon property was not
part of the initial study scope, efforts were being made to include it. Council member Myers expressed interest in understanding
the optimal use of not only this property but other nearby parks as well, to ensure informed decision-making. The Mayor indicated
that preliminary results of the study might be available by the end of summer, with completion expected early the next year,
emphasizing the importance of considering broader context and due diligence in park planning and land use decisions.
          Council member Graf raised concerns about the lack of information available, including the purchase price,
redevelopment costs, and maintenance expenses. He emphasized the need to consider the entire taxpaying public when making
such financial decisions. He thanked the residents that live near the subject property for staying engaged on this matter but asked
for understanding of the Council’s position on the matter given that they first heard about this proposal last week before the
Thanksgiving holiday.
          In conclusion, several Council members expressed discomfort with the language in the resolution that specified the
property would be maintained as open space, suggesting it limited future options. Mayor Nadolski addressed the Council
members' concerns about the resolution language limiting future options by clarifying that the intent was to create a consolidated
open space that could draw and connect the neighborhood in a meaningful way.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2025-23, entitled:
                     “A resolution of the Ogden City Council declaring the intent of Ogden City to purchase surplus property from
                     the Ogden School District.”
          VICE CHAIR HYER MOVED TO AMEND RESOLUTION 2025-23 BY STRIKING THE THIRD
"WHEREAS" CLAUSE THAT STATED, "WHEREAS THE MAYOR AND MEMBERS OF THE OGDEN CITY
COUNCIL, THROUGH PUBLIC ENGAGEMENT, DETERMINED THERE IS A STRONG COMMUNITY SUPPORT
IN MAINTAINING THE PROPERTY FOR OPEN SPACE." COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA SECONDED THE
MOTION. VOTING AYE – COUNCIL MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, RICHEY, VICE CHAIR HYER, AND
CHAIR WHITE. VOTING NO – COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS.
          Council member Myers stated that he opposed the motion because he believed it was important to maintain the property
as open space, in line with the community's interest.
          ON A MOTION BY VICE CHAIR HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF, AMENDED
RESOLUTION 2025-23 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, MYERS, RICHEY, VICE CHAIR HYER. VOTING NO – NONE. Chair
White abstained from voting.
          Chair White explained that she was reluctant to support adoption of the resolution as she only had a few weeks left on the
Council and felt uncomfortable making a decision with long-term financial implications that future Councils would have to
implement.
          Mayor Nadolski expressed appreciation for the genuine engagement from the neighborhoods and highlighted that the
resolution's approval was crucial to keeping open the possibility of consolidating open space while maintaining flexibility for
future decisions. He reiterated his disappointment about the politicization of the surplus property matter. He expressed frustration
that the timelines and State-required minimums had forced the Council into a rushed decision-making process that included
politicized rhetoric and speculation; this type of atmosphere hindered genuine dialogue and sincere engagement with both the
Council and the community. He highlighted his goal of fostering a collaborative environment and good-faith discussions with all
stakeholders moving forward.

Public Comments
          Brian Janroy, a business owner and airport hangar owner, spoke about Airport management, specifically regarding leases.
He encouraged the City to take the lead on Airport leasing policy rather than continuing to rely on consultants.
          Laura Lewis thanked Mayor Nadolski for his community engagement and expressed concerns about the focus on housing
development in the City.
          Rob Allred praised Mayor Nadolski for listening to residents’ concerns but expressed disappointment in the
communication process regarding the Taylor Canyon property. He disputed the notion of "buying the property twice," explaining
that it was a reallocation of resources. He also pointed out that the space had functioned as a park for over 50 years and already
had infrastructure like fencing and sprinklers.
          Kurt Clausse suggested that the City purchase the Taylor Canyon Elementary property now and give Ogden City Schools
first right of refusal for a future school, noting that Polk Elementary was overcrowded. He believed amenities, like restrooms,
could be provided at the property through donations and volunteer labor.
