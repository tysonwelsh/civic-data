NORTH RIDGE FINAL PLAT D PUD SUBDIVISION APPROVAL TABLED:

  City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council that Mt. Peak Development, owned by
Terry Messersmith, was seeking final plat approval for a PUD development that will include five (5)
eight-plex condominiums located at approximately 1500 N. to 1620 N. and 100 E. to 200 E. The property
was rezoned to R-3 with a PUD overlay zone. This development would have to have a mandatory
homeowners association per the PUD code. The open space will be held in common and maintained by
members of the HOA. The access road (identified as 150 East on the plat) will not be a city road and will
therefore not receive snow removal or garbage services.
   Mr. Atkinson explained that the plat was missing some information that the staff would like to see on
the plat and recommended that the final plat approval be tabled. The missing information included a
fence around the perimeter of the development and garbage enclosures along with some other minor
additions. Mr. Atkinson stated that there also needs to be a memorandum of understanding between
Mr. Peak Development and the City that no building permit will be issued for structures under the
power lines that still need to be moved.
   Councilor Memmott asked about the 1620 North road and the points of access into the development.
Mayor Seely asked if the architecture of the units were going to be like Hunter Village Condominiums.
Mr. Messersmith confirmed that they were and expressed his disappointment that the final plat would
not be approved at the meeting.
   Councilor Worwood moved to table the final approval until the plat is cleaned up and to allow the
extension for final approval go to the end of November. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The
motion carried on a unanimous vote.

POLICE VEHICLE USE POLICY ADOPTED:

    Mr. Atkinson updated the council that a couple of years ago the Nephi City Council changed their
policy of requiring all full-time employees to live within the 84648-zip code. This change has allowed
some new hires to live in different areas. It has assisted in the City’s recruiting efforts, particularly
among the police department.
  He stated that many cities in the state have a take-home vehicle policy for their police
department. This allows officers to respond to emergencies in a timelier manner and increases the
visibility of police cars within the community. Nephi City is home to many officers from communities to
the north and their patrol vehicles can be seen in the city. The NCPD has had some officers leave
employment for other opportunities and staff have examined options to help with officer
retention. One of those ways is to allow officers to use their vehicles to commute to their shifts, even if
they live outside the 84648-zip code.
   Mr. Atkinson proposed an updated police vehicle use policy that allows officers to use their vehicles to
commute to their shifts as well as use their vehicle for off-duty travel within the Nephi City limits. This
also improves the visibility of officers in the community and has become a standard practice in many
cities. The policy draft has been reviewed by the City Attorney and by the City Council in the last work
session.
    Councilor Memmott expressed his approval of allowing police officers to commute with the City’s
vehicle but was against it being used for personal use around town. Councilor Callaway moved to adopt
the police vehicle policy as presented. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion passed on
the following roll-call vote:

                Councilor Ostler         Yes
                Councilor Parady         Yes
                Councilor Worwood        Yes
                Councilor Memmott        No
                Councilor Callaway       Yes


AGREEMENT WITH TRAVIS STORY TO RUN TOURNAMENTS AT THE HIVE APPROVED:

    Mr. Atkinson stated that the Hive recreation facility has been open for a year now and several events
have been held there with success. One of the areas that needs improvement is how to hold, promote,
and facilitate tournaments. Tournaments are the best way to raise revenue to help pay off the Hive
debt and make the facility financially stable.
    Mr. Atkinson explained that to achieve the goal of holding tournaments, while also being mindful of
the limited staff resources in the Recreation Department, city staff has spoken with an individual, Travis
Story, who is well-versed in putting on tournaments in several venues around the state. Mr. Story has
expressed his desire to use the Hive and help it achieve its potential. He has facilitated numerous
basketball tournaments in Lehi and Richfield and has helped to promote those tournaments through
social media and web development.
   Mr. Atkinson presented an agreement with Travis Story to assist Nephi City in scheduling and
receiving income from tournaments held at the Hive. The agreement outlines that Mr. Story would
oversee scheduling, promoting, and facilitating the events held at the Hive. He would charge a base fee
for these services, and he would pay the ticket takers and scorekeepers. The city would then receive
net income of up to $3,000 after which net income will be split 50/50 between the City and Mr.
Story. This will provide an incentive to promote the events and get attendance up. The City will also
retain all proceeds from concessions at these tournaments.
  City Attorney Kasey Wright said that he had spoken with Mr. Story about the agreement earlier in the
day and Mr. Story agreed with the conditions of the agreement. Mr. Wright said that the City has the
obligation of making sure the Hive has all the conditions correct for a successful tournament. For
example, the temperature of the building and parking. Both parties need to play a part in the success of
this agreement, Wright stated.
     Mr. Atkinson said that he will give a further update on the Hive at a later date. Councilor Memmott
said this agreement is a step in the right direction. Councilor Memmott moved to authorize the Mayor
to sign the agreement with Travis Story. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed
unanimously. Mr. Atkinson added that the floors at the Hive have had some additional pickle ball
courts painted on them.

CITY COUNCIL AND CITY ADMINISTRATIVE UPDATES:

Work Session 7:56 pm

    1. The Council discussed setback requirements for residential homes. Mr. Atkinson informed the
       council that there was a home last year that violated the setback specifications by 7 feet. The
       contractor brought it to the City’s attention, and the contractor was able to redesign the plans
       to fix the problem. Mr. Atkinson stated that there have been other issues with setback
       enforcement and the staff has come up with some ideas for enforcement. He suggested if the
       setback is off within 24 inches, the violator would get a warning and then if there was a second
       violation within three years, the violator would have to fix it or pay a $5,000 fine. If the setback
       was off from two fee to four feet, the fine would be $10,000 or the violator would have to fix it.
       If the setback is off four feet or more the fine would be $30,000 or fix it with the City’s discretion
       of whether they can leave it be and just pay the fine. Mr. Atkinson said that our building
       inspector is going to really watch and inspect closely the setbacks before the foundation is
       poured. Councilor Parady said he is not a big fan of them paying a fine and leaving it. Councilor
       Ostler agreed with Councilor Parady and asked why the inspector didn’t catch the mistake. City
       Attorney Wright said that we are not a guarantee institution and we can make mistakes.
       Councilor Callaway said he can see a mistake within two feet but when it gets beyond that it
       almost seems intentional. Councilor Memmott said that he thinks it needs to be fixed if the
       setback is off by more than two feet. The council agreed to the warning for the first time the
       contractor is off by two feet or less and then the second time if is off within three years a
       $5,000 fine or fix it and a third violation they need to fix it. Mr. Atkinson said that they will draft
       an official policy for the council to approve.

         change the code to be more direct about vicious dogs. City Attorney Wright asked if the council
         wanted the code to say that a vicious dog owner would have to have a mandatory court
         appearance. The council decided if there are damages caused by the vicious dog then the
