       A.     REZONE, A REQUEST FOR A REZONE OF APPROXIMATELY 1.87-
              ACRES LOCATED AT 12317 & 12325 SOUTH REDWOOD ROAD.
              APPLICANT-CORY GRUNDMAN.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated the above is a request for
a rezone of approximately 1.8-acres located at 12317 and 12325 South Redwood road. The
hearing was noticed within 1,000 feet of this property. The request is for two separate lots
totaling 1.93 acres with 175 feet of frontage and 485 feet deep with an existing home on
each lot. He explained that the corridor in question was changed in the General Plan from
2001 when it was designated as medium-high density residential with the current General
Plan designation of five to eight units per acre. A property map was displayed.
Mr. Prestwich reported that the lot size in the R-22 zone is .50-acre with RM-6 having a
minimum 5,000 square-foot lot with 50-foot frontage and setbacks of 15-5-15. There is a
condition that if adjacent to existing incompatible land uses, the setback is one foot for each
foot in building height. The zone also includes townhomes and condominiums.

Mr. Prestwich stated that at the time of the application, this zone also included townhomes
and condominiums. The City Council recently approved changes to the Code with the
removal of condominiums or townhomes, attached or detached. Detached single-family
units are permitted in the RM-6 zone. The application has no vested rights under the old
ordinance and only single-family detached units will be allowed. Because this is a legislative
recommendation, the Commission must determine whether the application is reasonably
debatable or has a reasonable argument for making the rezone request.

Commissioner Cannon requested clarification of the attached twin homes. Mr. Prestwich
explained that a twin home shares a property line, and each side purchases the lot and the
home. The ordinance allows for a duplex and dual habitation with one lot and one owner.
Density issues were discussed. He confirmed that the Utah Department of Transportation
(“UDOT”) can put requirements on the access to Redwood Road that would potentially stop
the development.

Commissioner Brown expressed concern with the access and increased traffic from the
adjacent development.

City Attorney, Ryan Carter believed that access to the property has been granted to some
extent. UDOT would have to consider to what extent the additional units create an additional
traffic burden on the access that has already been granted. Access would continue to be
allowed and improvements may be required making it difficult for a property owner to get in
and out as a right in or right out.

Vice-Chair Lefgren opened the public hearing.

Lisa Kass read a statement prepared by her father, Jerry Thomas Callicott, who lives directly
south of the proposed development. He is in his 90th year and is raising his sixth generation
of Riverton residents. Progress has taken a portion of his front yard and more recently,
striping took away the shoulder entirely. The turn lane makes left turns nearly impossible
and dangerous. He requested that a concrete barrier wall be constructed rather than a vinyl
fence prior to development. He also requested that the lot sizes be increased and confirmed
that they do not have plans to develop their property.

Vice-Chair Lefgren reassured Ms. Cass that the letter will become part of the record and
forwarded to the City Council who will make the final decision. Mr. Prestwich noted that
additional letters have been included in the Staff Report and will be forwarded to the City
Council.

Clayton Burg believed that the proposed two acres are only the beginning of the remaining
18 acres and this matter will impact the remaining property. He suggested that it be limited
to R-4 zoning. Safety at a nearby crosswalk was of concern.

Susan Callicott Holt identified herself as the daughter of Mr. Callicott and although the
proposed development may look good on paper, she believed it will cause difficulties when
accessing her father’s property. She was concerned about traffic and safety when turning
into the property.

Rod Higley lives adjacent to the proposed development. He emphasized that more homes
would create more sewer and water runoff. He suggested a capture and development of
the sewer system for these homes. He stated that traffic and turnarounds at the school
crosswalk are already a problem.

Kristy Small stated that her home backs the proposed property. She would not be opposed
to six detached homes but was opposed to twin homes. Access was of concern. She
requested that all noxious trees along the fence line be removed.

Dan Ivey resides on Riverton Chase Drive and expressed concern with traffic from the north
using his driveway as a turnaround.

Bethany Dangerfield lives adjacent to the subject property and stated that the traffic using
Riverton Chase Drive as a turnaround is a consistent problem. She was unclear about the
difference between a townhome and a twin home but believed it would cause an increase
in cars with no driveway and street parking.

Dwayne Perra reported that he lives on 12300 South on the west side of Redwood Road.
With the RM-6 designation, he believed it would be appropriate and a fair assessment of
what could be developed.

Cory Grundman, the applicant and owner of Dredge Management, stated the proposed plan
was developed to be less invasive and will include 10 homes. The plan includes a cement
wall surrounding the property. He suggested extending the access median to ensure a right
in and right out only.

Robert Higley commented that UDOT has a strict mandate based on turnarounds and center
medians. The road has a center median but throughout the length of the project,
development is a left-hand turn lane. He stated that townhomes on 12600 South and 1300
West have created congestion and parking along the street. He believed this will overflow
onto Riverton Chase Drive and he expressed his opposition to the project.

Christie Chenault reported that they have an issue with UDOT expanding. When the
mailboxes were placed in their neighborhood, there was no public outreach or
communication. She was opposed to overflow parking in her neighborhood.

Robert Small expressed concern with traffic safety and believed the increase will cause
additional issues, especially with the existing crosswalk.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Cannon stated that UDOT will be controlling the Redwood Road access and
proposing an outcome that will create the least amount of liability and the highest level of
safety.

A question was raised regarding the Historic Preservation Overlay Zone. Mr. Prestwich
explained in the 2020 General Plan update, the Council and Planning Commission
recommended the underlying General Plan designation remain five to eight dwelling units
per acre for medium-high density with the addition of historic preservation.

Counsel Carter recommended that the Planning Commission review the recommendations
regarding how historic preservation could be applied in this case. He believed they were
looking at some type of architectural-style standard.

Vice-Chair Lefgren agreed that the concern is dominated by traffic, which is the biggest

acres of land located at 12317 and 12325 South Redwood Road thus changing the

motion. Vote on motion: Commissioner Brown-Aye, Commissioner Cluff-Aye,
Commissioner Cannon-Nay, Commissioner Breinholt-Nay, Vice-Chair Lefgren-Aye.

      B.     REZONE, A REQUEST FOR A REZONE OF APPROXIMATELY 8 ACRES
             OF PROPERTY LOCATED AT 12761 SOUTH 3600 WEST. APPLICANT-
             SPENCER MOFFAT, REPRESENTING THE BOYER GROUP.

Vice-Chair Lefgren opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that the above request is for a rezone
of approximately eight acres of property located at 12761 South 3600 West. A property
rendering was displayed. The Concept Plan includes a flex warehouse with a storefront and
garage doors in the rear. The underlying zone is Commercial Professional Office with an
Elderly Housing Overlay. He reported that the proposed M-1 zone fits within the General
Plan for Business Park or Light Manufacturing and is an appropriate zoning for that
designation. Properties within 1,000 feet have been noticed and written comments were
included in the packet.

Vice-Chair Lefgren pointed out that the Overlay Zone was changed in early 2021 and the
City Council went ahead with an overlay that was counter to the Planning Commission
recommendation. It was noted that the neighbors would prefer commercial over residential.

The applicant, Spencer Moffitt from The Boyer Group, stated that they understood that the
Elderly Overlay Housing Zone had been approved and the previous developer has no
interest in developing the property when they were denied their requested density. They
were reminded of the history of the property and decided on Light Industrial. He clarified
that there will be truck traffic associated with the buildings during business hours as they are
larger facilities. These types of buildings are typically 32 feet in height with a parapet to
conceal equipment.

Jason Woodbury expressed concern with property access and asked that any traffic not go
through his neighborhood.

There were no further public comments. Vice-Chair Lefgren closed the public hearing.

Zoning issues were discussed. Mr. Prestwich stated that M-1 and CPO zoning differ in terms
of uses, architectural requirements, and signage allowances. CPO allows only minimal uses
for retail and almost no manufacturing. Surrounding properties were detailed.

Commissioner Breinholt recalled the agreement with the nearby plaza regarding trucks
accessing Water Street and asked if there is signage. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that signage
is posted.

Vice-Chair Lefgren stated that he has been involved with this project from the most recent
application and recalled that the Planning Commission forwarded a recommendation to the
City Council with a different proposal. He was inclined to leave it as-is and allow the City
Council to change it back.

Vice-Chair Lefgren moved to recommend DENIAL of PLZ-21-4009 Rezone of 8.0-acres
of land located at 12761 South 3600 West, thus keeping the zoning as C-PO/EHOV-
SD, Professional Office with an Elderly Housing Overlay and Specific Development
Designations. Commissioner Cannon seconded the motion. Vote on motion:
Commissioner Brown-Nay, Commissioner Cluff-Nay, Commissioner Cannon-Aye,
Commissioner Breinholt-Aye, Vice-Chair Lefgren-Aye. The motion passed 3-to-2.

       D.     AMENDED COMMERCIAL SITE PLAN, “SAGE HOME OFFICE BUILDING”
              LOCATED AT 1288 WEST 12700 SOUTH. APPLICANT- DOUG JESSOP
              WITH SAGE HOMES. This item was continued by the Commission at the
              October 28, 2021, Commission Meeting.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that the above request is for an
Amended Commercial Site Plan for the Sage Office Building located at 1288 West 12700
South. The matter was scheduled for a public meeting in September at which time the
applicant wished to make changes. The public hearing remained open and was continued
to the October 28, 2021, meeting. At that time, the applicant submitted drawings after the
deadline and Staff was unable to put the matter on that agenda. Both the matter and the
public hearing were continued.

The property access is along the interior looking northwest and will be expanded into the
adjacent green space. Notices were sent to residents within 300 feet of the property. A
property rendering was displayed. Mr. Prestwich stated that the applicant intends to use the
structure for the storage of home staging furniture. The parking requirements have been
met. The exterior details were to include brick, a pop-out, a high-pitched roof, window
treatments, and were to include earth tones.

Commissioner Brown was in favor of the aesthetics of the building and asked if it was a
concern that it differs from the surrounding structures. Mr. Prestwich stated that
redevelopment is a challenge, and the surrounding projects were built separately with unique
elements of their own.

Commissioner Cannon believed that the project may increase surrounding property values.
The fact that no neighboring property owners were present indicated that they are agreeable
to the proposal. He expressed support for the request.

The applicant, Doug Jessop from Sage Homes, reported that he intends to use the property
as his office. After the improvements are made, it will be something the City will be proud
of. He confirmed that the windows will be replaced and the exterior painted white.

There were no further public comments. Vice-Chair Lefgren closed the public hearing.

Vice-Chair Lefgren was in favor of the request and stated that it will improve the neighboring
offices.

Commissioner Cluff moved to recommend APPROVAL of PLZ-21-8019 Amended Site
Plan for Sage Home Office Building with the following conditions:

      1.    Fix minor Riverton City Staff redline comments on the plans.

      2.    Storm drainage systems shall comply with the Riverton City Standards
            and ordinances, and with the recommendations of the Riverton City
            Engineer.

      3.    Applicant must complete a Long-Term Storm Water Management Plan or
            Covenant to Maintain to reflect the updated site plan as required by City
            Ordinance.

      4.    A Land Disturbance Permit must be issued by the City prior to any
            construction grading on the site.

      5.    The site and structures shall comply with any and all applicable Riverton
            City Standards and ordinances, including the International Building and
            Fire Codes.

      6.    Building elevations shall match those shown to the Commission, with at
            least the lower third of the wall on the addition being brick.

      7.    The site shall have at least 10 trees including existing trees, which shall
            remain.

      8.    Landscaping shall be completed or bonded for under the City’s
            Inclement Weather Policy before a Certificate of Occupancy is granted
            for the addition.

Commissioner Breinholt seconded the motion. Vote on motion: Commissioner
Brown-Aye, Commissioner Cluff-Aye, Commissioner Cannon-Aye, Commissioner
Breinholt-Aye, Vice-Chair Lefgren-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
