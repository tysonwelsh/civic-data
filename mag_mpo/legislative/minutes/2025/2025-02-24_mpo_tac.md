UTA Rescission Request for Item 4b (UTA Park & Ride Lots EM/SS - Additional Funding) from the January 27, 2025 meeting (00:04:39) Bob Allen explained that UTA requested rescinding a motion made at the MPO TAC January 27, 2025 meeting regarding park and ride lot projects in Eagle Mountain and Saratoga Springs. UTA emphasized the importance of moving forward with the existing project plans, particularly a new route connecting Eagle Mountain, Saratoga Springs, and Lehi to the American Fork Front Runner station planned for 2026. The discussion highlighted the complexities of transit planning, the need for coordination between cities, and the importance of maintaining project momentum. Vern Keeslar moved to rescind the motion for item 4b of the January 27th TAC Meeting. Jeff Lewis seconded the motion, and the motion passed. Brad Kenison opposed the motion. Eagle Mountain/Saratoga Springs Park and Ride - Additional Funding (00:18:36) Bob Allen presented a detailed explanation of a tip modification request from UTA to transfer federal funds to park and ride lot projects in Eagle Mountain and Saratoga Springs. He explained that federal funding needs to be obligated within a certain timeframe, and recent FTA notifications require urgent action. The project involves building park and ride lots at two locations: one in Eagle Mountain near Pony Express Parkway and another in Saratoga Springs. UTA proposed adding a hawk signal at the Eagle Mountain location to improve pedestrian Page 2 of 5 crossing safety. The total project cost would increase from $4.685 million to $6.024 million by transferring $1.339 million from previously awarded projects. Bob Allen emphasized that the funds are specifically for these park and ride locations, with the Eagle Mountain portion estimated at $2.2 million and the Saratoga Springs portion at approximately $3.8 million. The discussion highlighted the need to obligate funds quickly to prevent them from being rescinded, and UTA representatives confirmed their commitment to the current project locations. Aaron Wilson moved to recommend that that the MPO Board approve adding funds from PINs 19098, 17052, and 18986, totaling $1,339,274, to the Eagle Mountain and Saratoga Springs Park and Ride Lots Projects and modifying the scope to add a HAWK signal. Jason Bond seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Orem 1200 S 400 W Roundabout - Additional Funding (00:29:58) Bob Allen presented the Orem 1200 South and 400 West Roundabout project update. Originally funded with $2.3 million in exchange funds in 2016 for reconstruction and expansion, the project is now facing increased costs due to construction inflation, additional right-of-way expenses, and utility costs. The roundabout is located near UVU's new engineering building and is designed to improve traﬃc flow and accommodate UVX bus routes. While bids have come in higher than initially estimated, Orem City has done a commendable job of keeping the project close to its original budget. They are requesting an additional $272,000 from the general contingency fund to complete the project, with MAG staff able to award a 10% contingency of $230,000. The project includes acquiring some right-of-way from UVU and has already purchased a house in the project area that will be demolished. Vern Keeslar moved to recommend that the MPO Board add $272,000 to the 1200 South 400 West roundabout project. Brad Kenison seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Safe Streets Check List (00:34:08) Calvin Clark presented the Safe Streets checklist, a new resource developed by MAG to help identify and address safety concerns in transportation projects. The checklist is based on the Safe System Hierarchy, which includes four tiers: removing serious conflicts, reducing vehicle speeds, managing conflicts in time, and increasing attentiveness and awareness. Clark explained that the tool is designed to be user-friendly and can be applied to various types of projects, including city-funded initiatives and major developments. He provided examples of how the checklist works, such as addressing increased crossing distances for pedestrians and bicyclists by suggesting countermeasures like pedestrian refuge islands, hawk signals, and curb extensions. The goal is to help project planners proactively consider safety elements during the scoping process, potentially preventing unforeseen safety issues and additional costs later in the design phase. Clark emphasized that the checklist is currently a resource for member agencies and not a mandatory requirement, with the intention of making safety considerations more accessible and straightforward. Page 3 of 5 MPO Organizational Assessment (00:47:34) LaNiece Davenport presented the MPO Organizational Assessment, which was a comprehensive review of the organization's strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats. The assessment involved interviews, surveys, and focus groups with various stakeholders, including local government staff, elected oﬃcials, legislators, state agencies, and board members. Key findings included the need for staff training, as many employees are new, and a recommendation that the MPO Director spend more time focusing on MPO operations. The assessment highlighted the region's unique collaborative approach among transportation entities like MAG, WFRC, UDOT, UTA, cities, and counties as a significant strength. The review identified challenges such as being understaffed and needing to modernize processes. It emphasized the region's rapid growth as both an opportunity and a threat, particularly in terms of managing growth and securing adequate funding. The assessment noted that while the MPO is doing great work, there are opportunities to improve. LaNiece Davenport explained that staff will now create an action plan to prioritize and address the findings, with the ultimate goal of enhancing the organization's effectiveness. The consultants repeatedly stressed that the MPO is doing excellent work but can continue to improve its operations and strategic approach. MPO Board and TAC Bylaws (00:54:11) LaNiece Davenport presented updates to the MPO Board and TAC Bylaws, which were already approved by the MPO Board. Key changes included updating state agency representatives. For the TAC, the bylaws now require each mayor or county commissioner to formally appoint a voting member to the committee. This member should be a planner, engineer, public works representative, or senior staff member, and they must also designate an alternate. The goal is to formalize representation and ensure clear communication about who represents each jurisdiction. The changes allow for flexibility, such as permitting smaller communities without dedicated planners to send contracted representatives, and allowing voting members to send a designated alternate if they cannot attend. The bylaws also clarify that if a voting member cannot attend, they can send themselves, a city council person, or a senior staff member to be counted as a voting member. The presentation emphasized that the changes are meant to create a more structured and transparent process for committee membership. Legislative Update (01:01:23) LaNiece Davenport provided a comprehensive legislative update covering several key transportation and land use bills. For Senate Bill 195, highlights included a moratorium on highway reduction strategies in Salt Lake City, pushing the transit innovation grant program to 2026, and requiring cities within MPO areas to amend general plans to identify transportation priority connections over waterways, rail crossings, and highways by July 2027. Two transportation utility fee bills were discussed: House Bill 454 by Representative Karen Peterson, which allows municipalities to impose transportation utility fees with differential rates, and Senate Bill 310 by Senator Brammer, which would exempt religious organizations from such fees. ULCT supports HB 454 but opposes SB 310. Page 4 of 5 Additional legislative notes included Salt Lake County passing its fifth local option sales tax, which can be used for public safety expenses, and the current legislative session experiencing lower consensus estimates. She also mentioned Senate Bill 96 on Advanced Air Mobility, which charges UDOT with creating a toolkit for local governments on air mobility. LaNiece Davenport highlighted several housing-related bills that might impact local planning, such as changes to monarch housing planning requirements and annexation processes. She invited attendees to share any additional legislation of interest and noted that the current legislative session was in its final days for bill passage.
