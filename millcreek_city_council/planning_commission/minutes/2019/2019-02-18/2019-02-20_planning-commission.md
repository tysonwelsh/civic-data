3.1 Consideration of ZT-18-013, Amendments to Title 18, Establishing a Procedure
for Condominium Plats and Condominium Conversions Applicant: Hidden Creek
LLC Planner: Blaine Gehring
Blaine Gehring said the Planning Commission reviewed the application at the last
meeting. There was concern about financial stability with conversion condominiums and
the Planning Commission suggested that there be a fund created to maintain the property
and common areas until a home owners association was established. Another proposed
change was limiting conversions to 8 units instead of 4. Vice Chair Stephens asked if
specification was needed between commercial and residential condominiums. Lilly said it
was a subset of multifamily developments, so it did not apply to commercial. Lilly said
the draft ordinance they had distributed to the Commission yesterday was incorrect and
he handed out updated versions. Gehring went over other updates and said the tenant
notification requirement shall not apply to a structure that was vacant and remained so
during the year prior to filing of the developer’s application for conversion; nor shall it
preclude the approval of a project prior to the expiration date where every tenant has
executed a waiver relinquishing his or her right of notice under this provision. Each
current tenant would be given the right to purchase their respective unit upon at least as
favorable terms and conditions as said units are offered to the general public.

Vice Chair Stephens said 60-day notice to tenants was not sufficient. John Brems went
over his recommended changes to the draft ordinance. He said in 18.30.030(B), it was
supposed to put an obligation on the developer to add a reserve fund, so he recommended
adding language similar to, “...prior to developer being relieved of its obligation for
maintenance of common area facilities a minimum...” The developer has to fund the
escrow. In the parking requirement paragraph to add, “to the then current” parking
provisions in the first subsection and to change the word “irrespective” to “regardless.”
He also suggested rewriting paragraph 3 under Tenant Notification and eliminate E.
Tenant Protest Review. New proposed language would be, “ compliance with the notice
to tenant as required in code section referenced shall be a condition of preliminary
approval from the planning commission.” Commissioner LaMar said he wanted clear
language with the tenant notice. Commissioner Allen said paragraph 3 would be the
applicant’s proof to show they did it. Brems said he was not sure what the unintended
consequences were at this point.

Vice Chair Stephens opened the meeting for public comment.

Sean Steinman, 3560 S. 1300 E., said the tenant notice will not supersede leases that are
in place. He said the 60-day notice was a dispute period of time. He said they could
create estoppel certificates for noticing.

Paul King, 4276 Highland Drive, said the minimum unit restriction is unnecessarily
restrictive. There could be good projects less than 8 units. Vice Chair Stephens asked if
he had experience with condo conversions. King said he did. He recommended taking out
the unit limit completely because the Condominium Act does not have one.

Matt Gardner, 2859 Wainwright Road, said it would be a good thing if the Commission
could find the right language to give first rights to tenants to purchase.
