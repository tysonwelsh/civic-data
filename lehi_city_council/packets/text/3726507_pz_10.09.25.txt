43   3.1) Consideration of Clyde Capital Group’s request for approval of revisions to the Vistas
44   at the Point Amenities, removing the amphitheater from their required amenities (Tabled
45   from the September 11th meeting)
46
47   Brittney Harris presented the item. She shared the minutes from the original approval meeting in
48   2021, and explained that the amphitheater was originally just relocated. The overlook park plan

     Lehi City Planning Commission                  1                                   October 9, 2025
49   has been updated and is now more detailed. She stated that regardless of what amenity is decided
50   on, it will be a private amenity, not a city amenity.
51
52   Commissioner Everett asked for more information on the wind study. Harris explained that the
53   wind study group worked with the developer and the flight park, and the information was then
54   passed onto staff in the format found in the packet. Commissioner Everett expressed discontent
55   with the format the wind study was presented in, as he felt it removed a lot of the necessary
56   information.
57
58   Commissioner Kunze clarified that the amenity wouldn’t be a city amenity even though it’s
59   located within a TOD. Harris explained that TOD amenities are a separate requirement in code
60   from residential amenities.
61
62   Commissioner Eyre asked why the open air amphitheater was more problematic for the flight
63   park than the buildings which are being built where it was originally planned. The applicant
64   explained that the amphitheater was moved to replace a building that was causing problems; the
65   amphitheater was never the problem for the flight park. Commissioner Eyre asked what the
66   difference was between the current presentation and the previous presentations. The applicant
67   has more data. It was not included in the packet.
68
69   Commissioner Everett asked if any other amenities were substituted. Harris stated that this is an
70   adjustment of what was previously approved for the subdivision. Commissioner Everett asked if
71   the amenity would be for both the apartments and the townhomes. Harris said that as far as she is
72   aware, it will be for the apartments.
73
74   Commissioner Eyre asked why commercial development wasn’t included in the plan. Harris said
75   that it was in a previous concept plan. She let the Commission know that it is open to
76   interpretation as to whether that meets the TOD standards. There currently isn’t any transit in the
77   area.
78
79   Clayton Rackham was present representing Clyde Capital Group. He is the development
80   manager for this project. He clarified for the Commission that the wind study was an act of good
81   faith, and not a requirement from the city. He explained that the main concerns weren’t with
82   wind patterns, but for beginner parasailers, who are generally the only ones landing at the bottom
83   of the hill at the flight park. Rackham clarified that the amenities would be for the HOA. Clyde
84   Capital Group has done some research on HOA run amphitheaters and doesn’t think that it
85   would be the best amenity for the area given the density. Rackham further explained that cost
86   comparisons are based entirely on estimates, as no project has been approved, but either the dog
87   park or the amphitheater can be built for approximately the same estimated cost of ~$250,000.
88
89   Commissioner Eyre asked about irrigation. The information is included in the irrigation numbers.
90
91   Commissioner Everett asked if the apartments would allow animals. They will. He asked if they
92   will be size limited. Rackham was uncertain. Commissioner Everett asked for Rackham’s
93   thoughts on whether a dog park is a true community space or if it is limited in its use to dog
94   owners. Rackham agreed that a dog park would be limiting as an amenity to those without dogs
95   but argued that it would still be an open green space in the community. Similarly, there would be
96   residents that don’t visit an amphitheater.

161   3.2) Public hearing and consideration of Thanksgiving Park’s request for conditional use
162   approval of a 70-foot flagpole located at 3401 North Thanksgiving Way.
163
164   Brittney Harris presented the item. Any flagpole over 30 feet requires conditional use approval.
165   A 70-foot flagpole is being requested as it would fly as high as or higher than the surrounding
166   buildings.
167
168   Commissioner Lockhart asked if there were any similarly sized flagpoles in the city. Harris said
169   that Macey’s has a flagpole that is around 100 feet tall. There is also a flagpole at the Tag-N-Go
170   carwash that is 70 feet.
171
172   Commissioner Everett asked if lighting for the flagpole would be included. It would. Flag lights
173   are exempt from other lighting requirements in code.
174
175   Mark Ostovo was present representing Thanksgiving Park. He explained that the majority of the
176   building is 70 feet tall, with portions going above that. They would like the flag to be seen from
177   the freeway. He explained that he is ordering a customary height of flagpole, and that they
178   typically come in units of ten feet.
179
180   Kim Struthers clarified that the tallest flagpole can be in a nonresidential zone without approval
181   is 50 feet.
182
183   Ostovo clarified that they plan on lighting the flag in accordance with flag code.
184
185   Commissioner Everett asked if there was a flag currently on the lot, and why it is being asked for
186   now. Ostovo explained that there is no flag currently, and that a tenant recently renewed their
187   lease with the requirement that a flag be installed. The business park also thinks that it is an
188   overall good idea. Commissioner Everett asked if there was any discussion of putting the flag on
189   top of the building. Ostovo explained that it is envisioned as a business park amenity, not
190   something for just one building or client. Commissioner Everett asked if any flags other than the
191   U.S. flag would be flown. They will not.
192
      Lehi City Planning Commission                    4                                    October 9, 2025
193   Commissioner Lockhart asked if there was a business reason for flying the flag. There is not, it is
194   a passion project.
195
196   Commissioner Roberts requested that any lights shine away from the freeway. Ostovo explained
197   that the light wouldn’t be seen from the freeway, and there was only a chance of it being seen
198   from the frontage road. There are specifically designed flag lights that point directly up that they
199   plan on installing.

219   3.3) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a
220   Development Code Amendment to Chapter 4, adding an exhibit for General Plan, Zoning
221   and Development Code Amendments.
222
223   Brittney Harris presented the item. This would codify that applicants are allowed to submit an
224   exhibit for general plan amendment to clarify what they want the amendments for. Exhibits are
225   different than concept plans, as they would not receive feedback from the city in the way a
226   concept plan would.
227
228   Commissioner Everett recommended modifying the language to clarify that exhibits can be
229   provided at any point, rather than just after amendments were requested.
230
231   Commissioner Lockhart asked if this removes the requirements for the concept plan. Harris
232   explained that concept plans aren’t required for a General Plan amendment or zone change.
233   Concept plans would still be required at a later date for any development to happen.
234
235   Commissioner Everett said that he doesn’t see the necessity of the amendment, as applicants
236   could already bring in exhibits. He thinks that leaving it out makes the code more concise and
237   clearer. Commissioner Lockhart shared worries that putting this in code will signal to some
238   developers that the city prefers exhibits over concept plans.
239
240   Commissioner Everett opened public comment.
      Lehi City Planning Commission                     5                                    October 9, 2025
241
242   Candice Clark lives off of Pioneer Crossing. She shared that she may be under the wrong
243   assumption as to what the amendment is meant to do. She asked the Commission to be mindful
244   of what is allowed to be developed in the city.
245
246   Chase Clark expressed his gratitude to live in Lehi and thanked the Commission and city staff.
247
248   Commissioner Everett closed public comment.
249
250   Commissioner Everett shared his stance of not wanting to create unnecessary code. He worries
251   that exhibits will be used as a bait-and-switch by applicants. He advised the Commission that if
252   the code passes, they should see exhibits as something that is a possibility, not a certainty. He
253   recommended that staff simply let people who apply for the applicable changes know that they
254   are allowed to bring in exhibits. Commissioner Lockhart suggested putting it in the application
255   documents rather than the city code. Kim Struthers explained that the goal of the code is to make
256   a definitive statement that exhibits are allowed.
257
258   Nate Purser explained that the way city code is written currently, the Commission wouldn’t be
259   able to consider exhibits if they were brought in. The added code would allow them to be
260   considered. Commissioner Lockhart shared that she likes the ideas of having more examples for
261   the public to see during discussions. Commissioner Everett asked if this could be exploited later
262   on when site plans were brought in.
263
264   Commissioner Eyre expressed confusion over whether concept plans were binding documents or
265   not.
266
267   Commissioner Lockhart suggested striking the last line to distinguish between exhibits and
268   concept plans.
269
270   Commissioner Eyre clarified that the code only confirms that applicants can bring in exhibits.
271   The only things that will be voted on for the applicable amendments would be land uses.
272
273   Brittney Harris explained that part of the problem is that due to State legislation, the Planning
274   Commission is now the end stage for concept plans. Applicants would be allowed to use an
275   exhibit in future meetings, rather than having them finish at Planning Commission.
276
277   Commissioner Eyre asked staff if they were worried that exhibits would come forward primarily
278   for controversial items. He sees it as potentially problematic. Commissioner Lockhart worried
279   that exhibits would be confusing for the Commission, the Council, and for the public. Brittney
280   Harris explained that development agreements could be utilized by the City Council to make
281   things in exhibits enforceable. The Commission still expressed worries over potentially getting
282   the rug pulled out from under the city.
283
284   The Commission and staff discussed different examples of development agreements being paired
285   with General Plan amendments.
286

287   Kim Struthers recommended another option of codifying that the Commission will only look at
288   land use rather than considering other aspects and concurrent applications. General Plan
289   amendments and concept plans can be submitted concurrently.
290
291   Commissioner Eyre and Lockhart both expressed concern over residents being swindled by
292   exhibits that never come to pass.
293
294   Lorin Powell explained that exhibits would be another aspect to deal with for engineering. They
295   would feel obligated to make comments on them in DRC. Commissioner Eyre worried that
296   exhibits would be brought into meetings the night of and wouldn’t have a chance to be reviewed
297   in DRC.
298
299   Commissioner Eyre asked if the code was unfairly in favor of big developers, as they would have
300   more resources to dedicate to exhibits. Commissioner Lockhart questioned if the code was
301   changing anything.
302
303   Lorin Powell mentioned that the code would mix density issues with commercial issues.
304
305   Commissioner Everett said that he could see the use of an exhibit, and if they were currently
306   prohibited, then the code language should be cleared up to allow people to bring them. However,
307   if there is nothing in code preventing exhibits from being brought in, then it’s best to leave it out
308   of code.
309
310   Commissioner Kunze asked if questions about exhibits are asked frequently enough to be a
311   problem. Commissioner Roberts supported the idea of putting it only on the application papers.
312   Brittney Harris explained that as far as she is aware, everything on application paperwork has to
313   tie back to code. Kim Struthers confirmed this and mentioned that staff has gotten in trouble
314   before for having information in paperwork that didn’t connect back to code.
315
316   Commissioner Eyre shared that he thinks the clarification the amendment provides clouds the
317   actual issue of land use. Commissioner Lockhart argued that the most transparency comes from
318   pairing General Plan amendments with concept plans.
319
320   Commissioner Everett asked for advice from legal. Nate Purser explained that the core of the
321   issue is whether the Commission wants to consider an exhibit as a factor or not. He assured the
322   commission that factors can be weighted differently in considerations.
323
324   Commissioner Kunze echoed the feelings of Commissioner Eyre that the code is in favor of
