2.1 Hutchings Museum Tour
The Mayor and Council toured the Hutchings Museum earlier.

2.2 Potential Revisions to Chapter 3 Lehi Municipal Code regarding Public Disturbances
The meeting centered on revising Lehi City's noise ordinance, with city officials proposing a
simplified draft modeled after Provo's recent update. The proposed changes aim to streamline the
current complex noise regulations by creating a more flexible approach that allows enforcement
officers discretion in determining violations. Key discussions explored measurement challenges,
including the high cost of professional sound studies (up to $20,000) and the difficulty of
accurately capturing noise disturbances. Council members expressed concerns about
enforcement, particularly regarding traffic noise, industrial operations, and potential city liability.
They debated whether to focus on creating strict measurement standards or developing proactive
noise mitigation solutions. The group suggested further research into effective noise reduction
techniques, potentially consulting with Provo and exploring ways to help property owners

2.3. Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Discussion.
Claudia Saldana, with Mountainland Association of Governments informed that Council that
there were some CV funds rolled over from last year that can be used this year for additional
Covid related applications. She stated that Tabitha’s Way and Family Haven have shown that
they can tie their requests to covid expenses. United Way would need to amend their application
to show CV expenses. There was further discussion regarding the allocation of other funds for
city sidewalks and how to best allocate the CV funds. The direction was given to use staff’s
recommendations. This will need to come back to the Council for a formal vote.

Marshal Gary was representing a neighbor. He described a noise issue with a downstairs resident
playing loud music with heavy bass. The neighbor has measured noise levels up to 80 decibels,
has called the police multiple times, and is struggling with the impact on her mental health and her
young children.

Mike Bentley expresses concerns with noise issues related on Hadco’s property, a nearby industrial
operation. He complained about noise from trucks, beeping sounds, and how noise measurements

8. Consideration of Ordinance #20-2025 approving a Development Code Amendment to
   Chapter 39, clarifying dwelling units are to be occupied by one family and that
   townhomes and condos can only have one kitchen facility. (Tabled at the 04.08.25)
   The discussion centered on preventing illegal accessory dwelling units in townhomes and
   condos. Kim Struthers, Community Development Director, proposed defining a kitchen as
   having cooking facilities, a sink, and a fridge over five cubic square feet. Council members
   expressed significant concerns about the ordinance's enforceability and potential unintended
   restrictions.

    Councilor Newall suggested creating a document that requires homeowners to sign
    acknowledging they understand they cannot rent out space.


    Motion:     Councilor Hancock moved to deny Ordinance #20-2025 and direct staff to explore
                other options, such as a signature acknowledgment, or stamp on the plans.
                Councilor Newall seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Mayor Pro Tempore Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor
    Hancock, Yes; Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, Yes. The motion to deny

9. Consideration of Ordinance #21-2025 approving the Hospital and 2100 North Station
   Area Plan.
   The Hospital and 2100 North Station Area Plan is a state-required visionary document
   outlining potential transit stations for future development. Kim Struthers explained that the
   plan covers two future stations, is not a construction blueprint, and requires multiple steps
   before implementation. The plan includes transportation, trails, and land use considerations,
   developed through public input, open houses, and collaborative efforts.

    Mayor Pro Tempore Albrecht invited public comments.

    Alan Stevenson expressed skepticism about the plan, arguing it hasn't changed despite public
    feedback and seems to conflate different transit lines. He suggested tabling the plan or
    exploring alternative route options.

    Marshall Gary advocated for the plan's potential benefits, discussing how transit-oriented
    development could improve community dynamics by reducing traffic, creating more walkable
    spaces, and providing better urban design through strategies like public art and car sharing.

    Scott Hilton supported the plan, arguing that transit noise is less disruptive than constant road
    noise and that high-density housing could make living in Lehi more affordable.

    Leah Jay challenged suburban myths about neighborhood permanence, arguing that cities
    naturally change and that density with public transit actually reduces traffic and noise.

    Evan Hilton stated that opposition to density contradicts the region's growth-oriented history
    and that increasing density is necessary as more people want to live and have families in the
    area.

    The Council ultimately approved the plan with conditions, including reserving the right to
    amend the plan, emphasizing no vesting of development rights, and suggesting potential future
    modifications to station locations and accessibility.

    Motion:     Councilor Stallings moved to approve Ordinance #21-2025 approving the Hospital
                and 2100 North Station Area Plan and included that this is a broad brush vision,
                that there are no vesting of rights, development rights that come along with the
                approval of this Station Area Plan, and that we as a city council reserve the right to
                amend the Station Area Plan in the future as we see fit, or if circumstances change,
                and that we include a trigger that reflects the similar resolution we have regarding
                TODs when the residential portion comes online. Councilor Hancock seconded the
                motion.

    Councilor Hancock suggested that the motion also include making the distinction that this
    station area plan is distinctly different than an area plan like we normally think of such as
    Holbrook Farms Area Plan and that the language used to make the distinction in the prior
    approved station area plan also be added to this one.

    Councilor Condie suggested adding to the motion about the detention area too as far as the city
    having the right to add buffering or use that space however they see fit.

    Amended Motion: Councilor Stallings amended the motion to include Councilor Hancock
            and Councilor Condie’s comments, and include that the 2100 North location may
            be revisited to make it more accessible to more people and further away from
            current homes. Councilor Hancock seconded the amendment.
