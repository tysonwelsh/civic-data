Magna Water District

               Clint Dilley, General Manager, Magna Water District, stated Magna Water is
projecting decreased operating expenses from the year prior, for the second year in a row. He
thought it was a testament to employees for looking for ways to increase efficiency and reduce
expenses. Magna Water District hired a Human Resources Manager for the first time, with
experience with unions as well as training and safety programs. It also hired an additional
employee to work on the customer service meter reading crew, and it has already seen an
improvement in customer interaction and engagement, as well as leak detection and identification.
Magna Water will also be transitioning to a new bill pay system that will be more customer friendly.

He reviewed the projects stating the Secondary Water Booster Pump Station is about 90 percent
complete and should be operational for next season. That will provide water to Zone 3. It is being
built on the reservoir property by the D.R. Horton development. Then, the Zone 3 Drinking Water
Booster Pump Station is being expanded and is about 30 percent complete. Zone 3 will need to
be shut down in January, but Magna Water anticipates it can meet the needs from the existing
reservoir system. Information about that will be sent to customers Also, the drinking water line
replacement project on Florence and Edith is about 60 percent complete. Then, the wastewater
reuse treatment plant project is out for contractor pre-qualifications, and those are due this month.
Magna Water is expected to bid that out in January/February. Magna Water is also continuing its
investigation for lead and copper, which is required by the Environmental Protection Agency
(EPA). The EPA is requiring a more detailed inventory, including what the customer is responsible
for (the meter to the home). It will be getting information out to homeowners where there is the
highest likelihood of lead and copper, asking customers to participate in identifying whether they
have lead or copper in their lines.

RESOLUTION NO. 22-12-05                                              DATE: December 13, 2021

       A RESOLUTION OF MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL DETERMINING
       THAT A REFERENCED WALKWAY LOCATED AT 323 JEAN STREET DOES
       NOT EXIST AND RECOGNIZING A PAST VACATION OF SAID WALKWAY BY
       SALT LAKE COUNTY
                                               8
DATE           TUESDAY                             DECEMBER                            13, 2022


      WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) is a Municipality pursuant to Utah
Code §§ 10-2a-401 et seq; and

       WHEREAS, Magna is governed by a five-member elected Council under Utah Code Ann.
§ 10-3-205.5, which Council elects from its members a Mayor, and

       WHEREAS, in approximately 1968, before the municipal incorporation of Magna, a
subdivision plat was recorded with Salt Lake County (“County”) that shows a parcel labeled
“walkway,” whose legal description is attached hereto, marked Attachment “A,” which was
common practice at the time with regard to private lanes and private access rights of way; and

       WHEREAS, subsequent County documents were created that did show the alleged
“walkway” and showed instead that two contiguous parcels to the “walkway” were consolidated
into one parcel upon which a house was constructed with the address of 323 Jean Street, Magna,
Utah (“Subject Property”); and

       WHEREAS, sometime between 1968 and 2009 someone at the County once again
inserted the “walkway” onto the plat but noted thereon that it had been vacated; and

       WHEREAS, the documents of the alleged “vacating” of the “walkway” have been
misplaced by Salt Lake County and not recorded, nor indexed; and

       WHEREAS, Magna was duly incorporated after the occurrence of the forgoing events and
is now the jurisdiction over the Subject Property; and

       WHEREAS, the owners of the Subject Property have asked Manga to assist in clarifying
the confusion arising from documents relating to the “walkway” and desire to sell the Subject
Property; and

       WHEREAS, after consulting with legal counsel, Magna believes there are legitimate
questions about whether or not the “walkway” ever came into existence or was legally and
properly dedicated to Salt Lake County; and

       WHEREAS, Magna further believes that even if the “walkway” had been legally and
properly dedicated to Salt Lake County, then at some point in time Salt Lake County vacated the
“walkway” and the mere fact that documents regarding the same cannot now be found should
not be used to cast a cloud upon the title of the Subject Property.

       NOW THEREFORE IT IS RESOLVED, by the Magna Metro Township Council, Magna,
Utah as follows:

       (1) Any walkway that was contemplated in the initial plat for the subdivision whose legal
description is as set forth in Attachment “A” was never constructed or legally dedicated to Salt
Lake County.

DATE           TUESDAY                              DECEMBER                             13, 2022

       (2) Even if the alleged walkway was property dedicated, it was vacated by action of the
Salt Lake County Council.

Files # REZ2022-000725

Ordinance No. 2022-O-12

               Justin Smith, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, reviewed
File # REZ2022-000725, whereby Joe Colosimo is requesting a zone change on 1.72 acres of
property located at 2802 South 7200 West from an A-1 (Agriculture) zone to an R-M (Residential)
zone, for the purpose of subdividing the lot into 14 lots to develop single-family manufactured
homes. The applicant believes he can make manufactured homes more affordable than a higher
density type of housing. The A-1 zone does not allow for residential uses. The R-M zone is a
medium density residential use that allows for up to 7 units per acre for single family homes, with
higher densities for duplexes, triplexes, and multi-family buildings. The lot borders A-1 zones to
the north, south, and west. The lots to the east are located within West Valley City. The Magna
General Plan calls for a mix of single-family homes and duplexes. The Magna Planning
Commission did not recommend approval of the zone change.

               Council Member Hull stated the number of units on the 1.72 acres, would be
more like 8 or 9 units, not 14 units.

                Joe Colosimo stated he went to the Magna Planning Commission in November
and showed it a site plan of the 14 lots. The Planning Commission was not concerned with the
density, but with the single-family houses on this particular piece of ground. The Planning
Commission was okay with an attached product because it blended better with what is built to the
north. One commissioner had a problem with an access of 7200 South because he thought it
was too close to the corridor. After receiving feedback from the Planning Commission, he created
additional site plans, which he reviewed with the Council, taking into consideration not accessing
along 7200 South, providing fire truck turn arounds, open space, and either manufactured single-
family homes or attached product. His preference would be his initial site plan with the
manufactured homes.

              Todd Richards, Magna Planning Commission, stated the planning commission’s
issue was with the single-family units because it brought the lot size to less than 4,000 square
                                              10
DATE           TUESDAY                                DECEMBER                             13, 2022

feet. It had been 3,500 square feet for the lots. The planning commission was not necessarily
against the density, but wanted to see attached units, such as townhomes rather than single
family homes at that lot size.

                 Council Member Barney stated his initial concern was this corner is an entrance
to the city, and he did not like the idea of a manufactured product there, especially if manufactured
homes were packed in there. He would lean toward attached units, such as townhomes or
condominiums that a person could buy. Townhomes are a lower price than a single-family home

                Council Member Pierce stated she would rather see single-family homes than

                Paul Ashton stated the applicant has presented some layouts tonight that the

Resolution 22-12-06

                 Paul Ashton reviewed the resolution authorizing the Mayor to enter into a petition
and delay agreement with BHI for the installation of public improvements on property located at
2360 South 7200 West. He stated he made some changes to the agreement based on comments
the Council made at its last meeting. He shortened the term from ten to five years and changed
language requiring BHI to come back to the Council at the end of five years to get its money back
if it has given cash or a letter of credit. That language protects the Magna Metro Township and it
is fair.

              Council Member Barney stated he was getting nervous about all these petition
and delay agreements and wondered if the Council could make good on all of them.

               Council Member Prokopis stated the Council called one due about 18 months
ago, but nothing has happened. It was one the County did across from Lake Ridge Elementary.

        A RESOLUTION OF MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL APPROVING THE

        AGENCIES TO CREATE A NEW ENTITY, TO CONSOLIDATE LAW
        ENFORCEMENT SERVICES, AND PROVIDE RULES OF GOVERNANCE

      WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) is a Municipality pursuant to Utah
Code §§ 10-2a-401 et seq; and

       WHEREAS, Magna is governed by a five-member elected Council under Utah Code Ann.
§ 10-3-205.5, which Council elects from its members a Mayor, and

         WHEREAS, the Unified Police Department Board of Directors is comprised of elected
officials from each member entity; and

       WHEREAS, Magna, by and through its Council, has determined approving the Ninth
Amendment to the Cooperative Agreement by Public Agencies to Create a New Entity, to
Consolidate Law Enforcement Service, and Provide Rules of Governance, as set forth in the
attached Cooperative Agreement, Attachment “A,” will benefit the residents of Magna and will
contribute to the prosperity, well-being, peace, and comfort of the community.

        NOW THEREFORE IT IS RESOLVED, by the Magna Metro Township Council, Magna,
Utah:

       Section 1.  The Magna Metro Township Council hereby approves the Ninth
Amendment to the Cooperative Agreement by Public Agencies to Create a New Entity, to
Consolidate Law Enforcement Service, and Provide Rules of Governance as set forth in
Attachment A.

      APPROVED AND ADOPTED by the Magna Metro Township Council, in Magna, Salt Lake
County, Utah this 13th day of December 2022.

Code Enforcement

               Council Member Hull stated she got a complaint phone call from one of Eric’s
constituents yesterday, complaining about an area around 8560 West where some new homes
were being built, saying the construction company was leaving the area very messy and muddy.
She would forward the information to Council Member Barney.

               Mayor Peay stated a new water and sewer line is going in there, and vehicles are
driving through the area and then going back out onto Main Street leaving a muddy mess.

                Council Member Hull asked who would do something about a big trailer parked
on her street that was blocking a snowplow from getting down the road.

                Lieutenant Zach Van Emmerik stated to call the UPD on any parking violations.
An officer will go to the door and try to get the driver to move the vehicle before citing them. It
does not want to be too heavy handed, and just tow vehicles.

               Mayor Peay stated he has gotten complaints from people using the senior center
about dirt haulers that park along the road making it hard to see whether traffic is coming.

                Mr. Van Emmerik stated if the vehicles are obstructing the view of oncoming
traffic, the UPD can do something about them. Otherwise, it would put the vehicles on a 24-hour
watch. Countywide ordinance does not allow a vehicle to park in one position longer than 24
hours.
