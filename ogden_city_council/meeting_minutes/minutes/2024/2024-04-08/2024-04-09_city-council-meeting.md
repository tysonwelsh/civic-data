Proposed Partial Street Vacation – Cahoon Street between F Avenue and
G Avenue (extended from February 20, 2024)
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed partial street vacation came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated petitioners, Tyler Gray, Teresa McCandless, and Rental Investors, LLC,
are requesting to vacate a portion of the Cahoon Street right-of-way between F Avenue and G Avenue. The portion of the public
right-of-way proposed to be vacated is 137.9 feet long, with a total area of 9,320 square feet. The Planning and Engineering
Divisions have recommended that the petition be denied. Ogden Municipal Code 14-7-7 states that the Council shall hold a public
hearing for a proposed vacation of a public street and that the Council may approve the proposed vacation “if it finds that good
cause exists for the vacation and neither the public interest nor any person will be materially injured by the vacation.” The
Community and Economic Development Department has expressed support for the Planning and Engineering Division’s
recommendation to deny the petition. The Planning Commission made a recommendation to deny the petition.
          Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated the subject portion of the right-of-way is unimproved and is
not currently used by the public. The City Engineering Division has expressed the need for the City to reserve the portion of right-
of-way for future utility planning. The petitioners have proposed to provide easements or land dedication for utility routing, but
Planning and Engineering staff still expressed concerns with the proposed vacation because the Utah Department of
Transportation’s (UDOT) plans for the nearby Interstate-15 (I-15)/24th Street interchange are not yet finalized. Mr. Brierley also
used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the implications of the proposed partial street vacation. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet.
          Chair Richey invited input from the petitioner.
          Luke Kennard approached the Council and expressed a willingness on behalf of all the petitioners to collaborate with
UDOT, City staff, and the City Council regarding this petition. The petitioners have worked to reconfigure the site plan and
project concept plan to include two phases in order to be mindful of the need to preserve the Cahoon Street right-of-way for utility
planning.
          Council member Choberka asked if the petitioners would proceed with phase one of the project if they are unable to also
complete phase two if the street vacation is denied. Mr. Kennard answered yes, but he discussed the current condition of Cahoon
Street and whether it is truly the most viable option for a water line extension. Mr. Kennard indicated that proceeding with the
waterline project on Cahoon would require the City to acquire other properties in the area and that would be very costly. He
proposed that a decision on the petition be extended yet again to give additional time for City staff to meet with UDOT to
determine the most viable option for utility projects in conjunction with the I-15/24th Street interchange project.
          Council member Hyer addressed Mr. Brierley and asked him to address Mr. Kennard’s proposal to extend a decision on
the petition. He stated it was the Council’s understanding that the initial extension would give staff time to get more information
from UDOT, but that has not occurred. Council Policy Analyst Burton noted a decision on the petition was initially extended in
February to give the petitioner the opportunity to converse with Planning and Engineering staff. The result of those conversations
was the applicant expressing hope that UDOT could provide Engineering with more concrete plans for the interchange project, but
that has not occurred, and Engineering recommended the petition be denied given they had not received information from UDOT.
Council member Hyer asked if there is a downside to granting another extension regarding the petition. Chair Richey stated that
the City is not aware of UDOT’s timeline for finalizing the interchange plans. Council member Hyer stated that the extension
would not need to be date specific and could be conditioned upon UDOT finalizing their plans, even if that does not occur until
the fall of 2024. Council member Choberka agreed and noted that the extension would help the petitioner avoid the need to pay
another fee for a renewed petition for the street vacation.
          Chair Richey then opened the public hearing regarding the proposed partial street vacation.
          Travis Pate addressed the status of other parcels and streets surrounding the petitioner’s property; other properties and
streets have not been vacated. However, he agreed that an extension is appropriate and could be conditioned upon UDOT’s
finalization of their plans for the interchange. Mr. Pate suggested the Council consider the history of the entire community before
considering this type of petition.
          Angel Castillo spoke in support of Council member Hyer’s proposal to extend consideration of the petition. This would
send a message of transparency and collaboration and help to avoid a situation where the petitioners feel slighted or disregarded.
          Alan Jones stated he is typically opposed to street vacations, but in this case, he does support extending consideration of
the petition. He suggested that a date be set for the petition to be considered as it is not wise to extend it for an infinite or
undetermined amount of time.
          Tyler Gray stated that he is one of the owners of the subject property and he has been working in good faith with the
City. He would also support an extension of the petition rather than denial. Mr. Gray desires to beautify the area and help attract
people to the community. He believes many people will use the I-15/24th Street Interchange to access Ogden City and he wants to
make it a beautiful entrance to the City.
          Teresa Bramwell discussed the street vacation near 18th Street and the Ogden River; she believed the reason that vacation
was permitted is that Ogden City supported it and the subsequent development. She wondered why this experience is different
given that the subject property and the portion of the street to be vacated is much less visible. The property owner is motivated and
willing to pay for the land subject to the vacation and she does not know why they should be treated any differently than the
petitioner of the property near 18th Street and Washington Boulevard.
          There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY VICE CHAIR WHITE, ALL VOTING AYE.
          Mayor Nadolski shared his thoughts about the motivation of the property owners and their development partners. He
stated that City Administration is actually supportive of their proposed project and appreciates their passion for Ogden, but their
petition caused careful examination of the area and staff discovered the need to pursue utility improvements in conjunction with
the UDOT interchange project. He stated it would be nice for staff to have the opportunity to discuss the interchange project with
UDOT; Mayor Nadolski does support extending the petition until those discussions can happen, but he would also be willing to
consider a reduction or waiver of a future petition fee if the Council chooses to take final action tonight and the petitioner is forced
to file a new petition in the future after decisions on the interchange design have been made.
           Vice Chair White asked if the applicant would be able to begin working on phase one of their project if no action on this
petition is taken. Mr. Brierley stated that is dependent upon the applicant’s proposal and whether subsequent applications are
administrative or legislative in nature.
           The Council engaged in discussion of the appropriate action to take this evening based upon the input provided by the
petitioner and City Administration.
           City Engineer Nielsen reported on his participation in a meeting earlier today with UDOT; they have indicated they will
not reach the ‘plan in hand’ phase of the interchange project until September or October of 2024. They also indicated they will
host a series of open houses regarding the interchange project. The City wants to be sure to appropriately plan for improvements
in what is one of the most visible and vital access points to Ogden City and that is the reason for Engineering and Planning staff’s
recommendations regarding this petition. He noted that relative to phase one of the proposed development could not proceed until
the property is rezoned, so there are quite a few steps for the petitioner to pursue in order to proceed. He noted that staff are
willing to work with the property owners to help them complete those steps.
           Vice Chair White stated that there have been past discussions about extending utility improvements through this area all
the way to the Ogden Airport regardless of the 24th Street interchange project. Mr. Nielsen noted that the improvements are
actually made more viable because of the 24th Street interchange because it will be less expensive to install a 24-inch waterline
under the freeway while the area is under construction.
           Council member Hyer suggested that action on the matter be tabled rather than extended and that a date certain in fall of
2024 be selected if the petitioner is amenable to that action. The Council and Administration discussed an appropriate time frame
for tabling and settled on denying the petition at this time and authorizing Administration to work with the petitioner to adjust any
fees for a future street vacation petition. Council members Hyer and Choberka did not agree with the decision to deny the petition
and still voiced their support for tabling or extending action on the petition until after UDOT had finalized their design for the
intersection.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-8 Prohibiting Painting or Covering of Exterior
Brick in Commercial and Multi-Family Zones in the East Central District
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed text amendment pertaining
to exterior brick painting came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Planning Division staff have proposed a
zoning ordinance text amendment regarding the painting of exterior brick in the commercial and multiple-family zones in the East
Central District. The current regulations around brick painting in the East Central District prohibit the painting and covering of
exterior brick in most residential zones, but not the commercial and multiple-family zones.
          Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide context
regarding the topic of painting brick and how the practice can impact the historic character of a home. He noted the current
ordinance prohibits painting brick in the East Central residential zones and Planning staff proposes expanding that prohibition to
the following zones in the East Central district: NC, PI, R-4, R-MFV, R-MFH, C-MU, and R-9. The Planning Commission has
voted unanimously to forward a recommendation to approve the proposed text amendment. For a copy of the PowerPoint
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-8, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by adopting Subsection 15-17-4.I to
                   revise painting of exterior brick regulations in the R-3 Zone; adopting Subsection 15-18-4.I revising painting of
                   exterior brick restrictions in the R-4 Zone; amending Title 15, Chapter 32 by adopting a new Section 7 to revise
                   painting of exterior brick regulations in the R-MFV and R-MFH Zones; amending Subsection 15-33-5.A
                   revising painting of exterior brick regulations in the PI Zone; amending Title 15, Chapter 34 by adopting a new
                   Section 10 to prohibit painting or covering exterior brick in Downtown Commercial Zones; amending
                   Subsection 15-37-5.A revising painting of exterior brick regulations in the R-9 zone; amending Subsection 15-
                   41-5.F revising painting of exterior brick regulations in the NC Zone; and by providing that this ordinance shall
                   become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”
          Chair Richey then called for public input regarding the proposal.
          Al Jones stated that he is typically not supportive of the City telling people what they can and cannot do with their
property; however, in this case, he fully supports this amendment. He owns a brick home, and a brick restoration contractor told
him that the only thing that is keeping the home standing is the fact that the brick has not been painted. He added that he owns a
building on Lincoln Avenue that was tagged with graffiti and the City’s anti-graffiti team was able to remove the paint. He does
not see any reason for painting a brick home; big brick homes are beautiful and should stay that way.
          Steve Jones stated years ago when he first moved to Ogden, the home he purchased had already been painted. He asked
for consideration of grandfathering homes that have already been painted. He noted that the situation he found himself in was
deciding between trying to remove the paint or repainting it and hoping for the best. He stated each building should be considered
on a case-by-case basis because some are being properly maintained and the owner should not be penalized.
          Teresa Bramwell stated she feels passionately about this issue because she just paid a $500 application fee for the
Planning Commission to discuss her property. She later found that she did not need to pay that application fee because she was
already allowed to proceed with her project. She noted she will be painting her home, not because she does not love red brick
homes, but because her home was damaged by a fire, and she is rebuilding it. She will need to take the roof off to fix the damage
and she would like to add a second level to her home, and she was told she must use Hardie board, which is a painted product. She
does not think it would be aesthetically pleasing to have one color of brick on the bottom and another color of a different product
on the top level. She stated there are many properties in Ogden that are not beautiful or are built out of cinderblock; these
buildings would be beautified if they could be painted. She stated she lives in a historic area of Salt Lake City and so many homes
and properties there have been painted and they are so much better than they were before. The reason someone paints something is
to make it more beautiful. She noted the pictures provided by Mr. Brierley were not attractive because the homes were painted all
one color; most people will not pursue that kind of project.
          Travis Pate stated that he and another resident recently petitioned for historic preservation considerations for two
neighborhoods in the community and they were denied; however, the City is now considering this ordinance amendment for the
purpose of preserving a building’s history, but there is no petitioner behind the amendment. He claimed inconsistency on the part
of the City when it comes to historic preservation.
          Angel Castillo stated that she lives in a brick home that is nearly 100 years old; one of the things that caused her to fall in
love with Ogden is its character and the historic buildings. However, she acknowledged that every property owner has rights and
those must be considered. She stated that while preserving the history of a building is important, there must be opportunities for
exceptions, such as in Ms. Bramwell’s case when someone is adding onto a home, and they desire to make the colors and
materials cohesive. The City Council may benefit from more discussion on this topic before taking action; it would be helpful to
consider painting methods that would actually seal and preserve the brick. Enforcement of the ordinance is also something that
must be carefully considered. She suggested the Council table action on this matter to engage the community, specifically
residents who may be impacted by the ordinance.
          Sebastian Benitez stated he also owns a home in Ogden, but he does not have the freedom to do what he wants with it,
including painting the exterior. He believes that painting his home and others in the community would be an upgrade.
          There were no further persons appearing to be heard.
          Chair Richey asked Mr. Brierley to answer the questions raised by the public. He asked if the proposed ordinance
prohibits repainting a home that has already been painted or if it prohibits painting cinderblock, to which Mr. Brierley answered
no to both questions.
          Council member Choberka asked Mr. Brierley to address whether the proposed ordinance will impact Ms. Bramwell’s
property. Mr. Brierley stated that Ms. Bramwell has received approval to proceed with her project because the home was damaged
by a fire; passage of the proposed ordinance will not impact her and her property. He added there are provisions in the ordinance
that give property owners the ability to apply for an exemption. Council member Choberka stated that she agrees with Ms. Castillo
that further community engagement regarding this topic would be helpful, even if the Council chooses to adopt the ordinance
tonight. It is important to educate the public regarding these new regulations before they proceed with a project to paint their
home.
          Council member Blair stated he appreciates the public input that has been provided tonight; he is supportive of historic
preservation but is also supportive of property rights and he believes that some brick painted homes are beautiful. He also agreed
that more community engagement would also be helpful; he plans to vote against the proposed ordinance tonight because he feels
there are other mechanisms for pursuing historic preservation while also being open to different styes and tastes and preserving
property rights. He would be interested to know what other communities with a large number of historic properties are doing.
Council member Hyer stated the ordinance does have language that provides property owners the ability to pursue painting or
sealing their unsealed bricks homes upon evaluation by Planning staff and the Planning Commission. He feels that the proposed
ordinance will address those who are using practices that will harm the historic value of their home.
          Council member Choberka stated that a homeowner is required to obtain a permit for several other types of home
improvements, and she wondered why the City does not require a permit for painting a home; imposing that requirement may help
to address the problems staff is trying to address through the proposed ordinance. She stated she also wants people to be able to do
what they want with their property, but she is very familiar with the historic damage that can occur when improper painting
methods are not used, and she wants to prevent that from happening. She suggested a permit system may be a better tool for
addressing the matter.
          The Council engaged in a discussion about the appropriate action to take, including whether to table/extend action on the
ordinance or adopt as written.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-9 revising organization of the Natural Resources
and Sustainability Stewardship Committee
          A memo from the Management Services Department regarding proposed amendments to the Natural Resources and
Sustainability Stewardship Committee ordinance came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the City’s
Sustainability Coordinator has submitted a proposal to amend the City Code regarding the Natural Resources and Sustainability
Stewardship Committee’s base membership requirements. The proposal is to allow non-resident experts to be on the Committee
and to clarify that the majority of the Committee is to be Ogden residents. The Committee has recommended that the City Council
adopt the proposed ordinance amendment.
          Sustainability Coordinator Long summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
proposal to introduce new language requiring the majority of the Committee to be residents of Ogden, similar to the organization
of the Arts Advisory Committee. The proposed ordinance amendments are meant to expand the potential Committee membership
pool. The proposal will also change the name of the Committee to the Sustainability Committee. For a copy of the presentation in
its entirety, see the information packet.
          Council members expressed their support for the ordinance amendments and thanked Mr. Long for his efforts.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-9, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Chapter 3-31 to revise
                    the organization and name of the Ogden Natural Resources and Sustainability Stewardship Committee; to make
                    technical changes; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after
                    final passage.”
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS,
ORDINANCE 2024-9 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, HYER, MYERS, VICE CHAIR WHITE, AND CHAIR RICHEY. VOTING
