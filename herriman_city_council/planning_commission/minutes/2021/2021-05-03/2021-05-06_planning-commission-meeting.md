2021 at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman,
Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website,
and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

Chair Berbert called the meeting to order at 6:03 p.m.


Assistant City Planner Maloy explained the engineering staff was working with Council to refine engineering
standards, mainly focused on alternates to private road standards. Potential annexation timelines and offsets
to City impacts from the Olympia development were also discussed.
Commissioner Brody Rypien arrived at 6:06

The draft budget was being reviewed and City Planner Maloy expressed appreciation to the Commission for
the feedback provided at the Joint Meeting. Chair Berbert asked for a progress report on different multi-
family design standards. City Planner Maloy responded an update would be provided during presentation of
item 1.3.


Commissioner Rypien thought conditions of approval felt like requirements, not recommendations. He
asked if modifications to code were needed to include them in all approvals. It was explained that conditions
of approval varied per development and often needed modifications.



City Planner Maloy presented pending land development regulatory amendments. The draft General Plan
timeline for approval was reviewed. The consultant has received the latest round of public outreach.
Podcasts, videos, and other electronic methods have received the most feedback. An update would be
provided at the next Commission work meeting. A Public Hearing was planned for a June Planning
Commission meeting, at which time the Commission may make a recommendation to City Council.

In lieu of the proposed Planned Community (PC) zone, the City was reviewing a potential Large Master
Planned Community Enabling Act. This would create a new tool in code to outline the process for pending
master development agreements and would only apply to large areas not yet annexed by the City. Staff were
studying the ideal size for the acreage requirement. It was estimated about twenty five percent of the city had
not gone through a formal subdivision review or was entitled by a master development agreement. This total
did not include open space. City Attorney Andrizzi explained one of the conditions of the sub-zone required
a master development agreement between the city and developer. Commissioners discussed areas that may
be annexed into Herriman in the future. At this time, text amendments may only be initiated by
Councilmembers, Commissioners, or landowners. Staff may ask the Commission to request for staff to
authorize them, as doing so would be helpful when engaging in conversations with developers. City Attorney
Andrizzi explained the enabling act was a tool and not tied to any specific development agreement. They
would require approval of a development agreement by City Council prior to any rights being vested.

Accessory structures code amendments were being worked on by staff. The amendments would unify all
accessory structure information into one chapter, making it more concise and easier to understand, and
clarify placement on non-standard lots. Residents have expressed interest in reusing metal storage containers.
They were allowed with the addition of materials and color to match the dwelling. City Planner Maloy
explained they were already secure structures, and the addition of materials may make them less economical.
Commissioners recommended having fewer restrictions on larger lots, where they would not be very visible.
Commissioners also offered the opinion that enforcement may be challenging.

Multi-family development amendments and land use tables feedback were requested. Commissioners
discussed the size of overall unit finished areas, increasing brick and stone requirements, and allowing more
variety. Additionally, more single-family homes were needed.

City Planner Maloy identified the City Codes being updated to comply with recent Legislative changes and
reviewed Commission training requirements.


No training was presented due to time constraints.

The work meeting was adjourned by consensus at 7:00 p.m.

Planner Sheldon Howa explained the purpose of the plan was to establish alternate routes throughout the City,
provide connectivity of trails, and promote safety for pedestrians, bikers, and hikers.

Staff Engineer I Bryce Terry informed the Commission this plan was financed with a grant from Wasatch
Front Regional Council, in cooperation with UDOT and multiple City departments. The active transportation
plan was for bikes, horses, pedestrians, and trail use. Jeremy Searle from WCG overviewed plan benefits. He
said active transportation plans promote healthy living, better quality of life, improved air quality, and livability.
Jordan King from Kimley Horn highlighted the public opinion survey results. He stated they strived to
understand resident needs, and verified the data matched with the opinion of residents. They utilized social
media, the city website, and board signs for survey distribution. QR survey links were included on trailheads
and parks for easy access. They received 401 survey responses. Public meetings garnered over 1,000 views.
Walking, running, biking, and hiking were priorities and residents requested more trail connections, paved
paths, and bike lanes. They focused on key Herriman destinations, such as Black Ridge reservoir, rodeo, parks,
and canyons. Residents preferred buffered bike lanes and the addition of a bike boulevard around the city
center.

Commissioner Jacobson was frustrated by the lack of north / south paths from around the skate park to old
city hall area. He recommended adding channel paths. Staff Engineer I Bryce Terry responded the old town
ordinance prohibited widening of the street, and the addition of curb, gutter, or sidewalks. That area lacked
walking infrastructure, and parents of kids walking to school have expressed safety concerns. Staff Engineer I
Terry offered to investigate options for adding more side trails through the old town area, but options were
limited by the ordinance. Commissioners requested clarification of which trails were asphalt trails or paved
paths and requested sidewalk gap locations. Consultant Jeremy Searle responded there were multiple areas with
sidewalk gaps, and they focused on key locations near schools. Others would be filled in with future
developments. Ideal connection locations were discussed by the Commissioners. Chair Berbert requested
locations for paved trails that connect to HTC. Trails and bike paths were identified by Consultant Searle.
Commissioner Jacobson expressed concern that only sidewalks were around the recreation and HTC area.
More trails would be preferred. Chair Berbert thought the area should have a ten-foot asphalt trail connecting
east to west. There were no true trail connections through some sections, only bike lanes. Applicant Searle
identified one on Fort Herriman Parkway. Engineer I Bryce Terry added many of the trails follow creek or
open space, and he was not aware of any plans for a trail through HTC. Due to this, most connectivity in the
area was via sidewalks and bike lanes. Commissioner Jacobson recommended widening sidewalks to create a
connected trail feel. Staff Engineer I Terry identified a paved trail east of City Hall and agreed the paved trails,
away from roads, feel more recreational. He added the Commissioners may recommend adding a transit
corridor project, in the area. Chair Berbert made the recommendation a ten-mile bike loop should be
completed. The current proposal contained bike boxes to facilitate safer turn lanes at intersections around
13400 South and MVC. Pedestrian improvements were added near some schools. Commissioner Jacobson
identified a dangerous crossing on 13400 South near Rosalina Drive, where the trail ends. The applicants
thanked the Commissioners for their suggestions. Commissioner Rypien noted the population projections
varied from 2030 to 2050. WRC Jeremy Searle explained projections were based on the statewide model,
which spread totals evenly throughout the state, and did not completely reflect the anticipated population
growth in Herriman.

Chair Berbert opened the public hearing.

Heather Garcia thought overall the plan looked great. She expressed a specific concern with total walk trips
between 600 and 1,000 and identified a sidewalk gap at the corner of 6400 West near the Cove entrance. Bike
lanes have been recommended but the road lacked a sidewalk. She has observed kids walking home through
the dirt in unsafe areas. The other side of the road also had incomplete sidewalks, and she thought sidewalks
should be a higher priority than bike lanes.

Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.

Engineer II Bryce Terry addressed the comment and stated the City applied for funds to put a sidewalk in that
location. When it was established curb, gutter and an equestrian trail were installed. The area may be eligible
for safe walk route grant money. Hopefully, those funds would completely address the issue with the
installation of a sidewalk, among other improvements.

Commissioner Rypien would like to see incomplete sidewalks addressed more. Commissioner Ferguson
identified a slide from the presentation that identified the gaps. The applicant explained those identified were
in key areas that needed to be addressed. Other gaps would be completed when the property was improved.
Commissioner Jacobson thought north, and south trails should be added in areas around 11800 South and
Bacchus highway. The applicant identified proposed trails and stated they would update the maps to make
them easier to read, as it was hard to distinguish between the bike trails and proposed trails. Chair Berbert
recommended creating a look like the Provo canyon trails. Commissioner Jacobson recommended further
discussion at a work meeting and using this plan as a tool to show developers the master vision. Chair Berbert
thought 11800 South was too busy for a buffered bike lane and it should be changed to a paved path.
Commissioners agreed further discussion should take place during a Planning Commission work meeting, to
fine tune the plan.
