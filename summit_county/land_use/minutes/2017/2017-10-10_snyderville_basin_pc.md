WOODWARD/GORGOZA SITE VISIT  
 
Work Session  
Chair Harte asked Planner Milliner if the next meeting will be a public input session or 
another work session.  Planner Milliner said he thinks another work session would be 
helpful.  The applicant, Michael Burrell, said he would like to hear from the Commission, 
particularly about the location.  Attending the meeting with Mr. Burrell was Tim 
Brennwald and Jodi Churich.   
 
Commissioner Peck said she appreciated that the applicant showed the alternate 
location without prejudice.  That is the location she prefers.  She likes that it is set 
against the mountain.  The full visual impact of the hill is still there.  The building will 
over look the pond.  She added that she isn’t sure how that location will impact parking.

Commissioner Peck said she is concerned with Kilby Road and its congestion.  She 
likes the fact that this will provide a third ingress/egress access.  It will avoid creating a 
pressure point for the school.   
 
Commissioner Peck said she realizes the building would need to be cut into the 
mountain, but the building would hide the cuts.  Mr. Burrell agreed.  He said the cut 
behind the building would be addressed with the building itself and its foundation; 
however, a fair amount of cut and fill will be seen at the front of the building.  
Commissioner Peck said she has confidence they can create retaining walls that will 
be attractive.  She doesn’t mind the road that goes by the dam.   
 
Commissioner Peck said she likes the concept and what they are trying to do.  It is the 
massing that has troubled her.  The plan has the building close to the frontage road and 
the Interstate.  This is an entrance corridor.  The building would impede the view shed.  
She thinks placing it against the mountain will be an attractive setting.  There are ways 
they could soften and enhance the look.   
 
Commissioner Peck commented that a statement has been made that there are other 
large buildings close to the road.  She respectfully disagrees.  The Jeremy Clubhouse is 
hardly noticed because it has been set back and has softening features.  Ecker Hill 
Middle School is set back from the frontage road and the highway.  When she drives by, 
it seems to be subtle.  The church, fire station, and Whole Foods have been clustered as 
part of a development.  There are other buildings that have either been set back or the 
impact softened.   
 
Commissioner Peck said she thinks the applicant has shown a lot of integrity.  This is a 
great project, but she prefers that the building be set against the hill.  Mr. Burrell said he 
has taken copious notes on her comments.

Commissioner Klingenstein said the Commission is working with both the General 
Plan and the neighborhood plan.  He said the Apex project is having a lot of cut and fill, 
but once it is completed, he thinks it will blend in well.  Commissioner Peck 
encouraged the applicant to visit this project.   
 
Commissioner Klingenstein said he can’t support the mass of this structure in its 
present location.  If it is built there he would prefer to have the building broken up or 
place it against the hillside.   He thinks an attractive campus look could be created 
instead of a corporate-type building.  He added that the Staff Report states the field 
house is located close to the road and has little vegetation.  As a result, it is highly 
visible from all locations.   
 
Commissioner Cooke said because of the retaining walls that would be needed, he 
doesn’t share the same feelings about the alternate location.  He doesn’t have a problem 
with the massing.  To him, the retaining walls and the amount of disturbance is a deal 
breaker.  The proposed location makes more sense.  The hillside is also part of the view 
shed.  He would like to protect the view of the ridge.   
 
Commissioner Cooke said he would like to know what the view would be like from the 
other side of the interstate.  What will the Jeremy Ranch residence see?  He would 
prefer to break up the 52,000 square-foot building; however, if that can’t be done, he 
likes the proposed location.   
 
Commissioner Klingenstein asked Commissioner Cooke to take a look at Chapters 1, 
2, 9, of the General Plan and the area plan for Summit Park.  These talk about view shed, 
view corridors, and hillside protection.  He doesn’t believe this proposal can honor the 
policy of these documents without amending the General Plan.   
 
Commissioner Klingenstein read a statement that this area is the first impression 
provided to the visitors entering Summit County from the west.  That is why he would

prefer the building to be broken up.  He likes the overall project because this is a resort

Commissioner Cooke responded that in the past, the Commission has considered what 
a visitor is going to see.  For some people the Utah Olympic Park it is a shining example 
of the Olympic legacy.  For others, it is light pollution.  He said a building can 
compromise a view shed, but it can also highlight what the community has to offer, 
making it a benefit.  He thinks this is worth considering.   
 
Mr. Burrell said the 1999 Planning Commission was not only the author of the DA, but 
they wrote a lot of the Code language that is still being used.  They were very aware of 
view sheds and how recreational sports fit in.  That Commission put specific language in 
the DA that says the eastern part of this parcel should be preserved and development 
should be kept to the front where the existing disturbance is located.   
 
Commissioner Klingenstein said he is trying to honor the SPA agreement.  The 
original SPA agreement had a 4,027 square foot building located there.  They are 
proposing one that is 52,000 square feet.  He would like to have something that honors 
both the SPA agreement and the General Plan.   
 
Commissioner Fine said the field trip was very helpful.  He likes both locations.  He 
noted this is a very massive building.  The location closer to Kilby Road would have 
fewer disturbances.  It would look very impressive on the alternate site.  He doesn’t like 
the third proposal.   
 
Chair Harte said he likes both sites and can see advantages for both.  With the location 
closer to Kilby Road, he thinks that the height is more of an issue that the square 
footage.   If the building is close to the highway, it will block the view more.   To him, a 
greater concern is the lights and how it will affect the neighbors.  If it is put against the 
hill, he will be concerned about softening the impact of the retaining walls.  Mr. Burrell

Planner Milliner said the Commission seems to be divided on the location.  He 
encouraged the Commission to let the applicants know which location is preferable so 
they will know how to proceed.  Commissioner Peck said there are two absent

Commissioner Cooke said he found the site visit to be very helpful.  He recommended

Mr. Burrell said there have been two or three Commissioners missing at a number of 
meetings.  He hopes there is a full quorum at the next meeting.  He liked the suggestion

meeting.  They would like to narrow the number of issues.   
 
Commissioner Klingenstein said his preference is to break up the structure at the 
proposed site.  If the Commission prefers the alternative location, he thinks that could 
work.  It will be beneficial to Commissioner Stevens and Commissioner Dickey to hear 
the Commission’s discussion at the upcoming meeting.  It is an opportunity to learn 
from each other, particularly on a complex project like this.  
 
Planner Milliner asked if the Commission would like to review the lighting and noise at 
the next meeting.  Chair Harte said they would be happy to hear these details.  They 
don’t want to slow up the application.  Mr. Burrell said they have briefly shown a 
lighting plan and an exhibit of where the noise could travel.  He thinks lighting and 
noise was anticipated by the DA; however, technology has come a long way in 20 years.
