1.     CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT AND MAJOR HOME OCCUPATION (Public Meeting –
       Administrative). Review an application for a Conditional Use Permit / Major Home
       Occupation in the Veyo area. The property is located at 306 S Leavitt Ln in Veyo. The
       applicant is proposing to rent trailers, which will be stored behind the home in a designated
       drop-off / pick-up location. The applicant is Kurt Staheli.

       Mr. Scott Messel gave an overview of the property and an explanation of what the applicant
       was requesting to do. Mr. Messel explained the applicant is seeking approval to have up to
       10 trailers available for rent, which will be accessible by means of a designated parking area,
       80’ X 80’, located behind the home.

Mr. Messel went over the definition of a Major Home Occupation stating that it shall not
detract from the residential character of the neighborhood. He stated that staff has
reviewed the application and recommends that the Planning Commission approve the
application based on the following findings and conditions:
1.  A trailer rental business is considered a Major Home Occupation.
2.  Parcel 7166-H-NW legally exists in Washington County.
3.  Major home occupations shall be secondary in nature to primary residential use.
4.  The business shall be registered with Utah State prior to receiving the County Business
    License.
5. The home occupations shall maintain the residential character of the area.
6. No more than 10 trailers can be stored, parked, rented, or used in the home occupation
    business.
7. All trailers will be parked behind the residential dwelling fully on the lot.
8. The home occupation shall not significantly impact traffic.
9. No semi-trailers. Trailers limited to size and weight to be towed by passenger vehicles.
10. No employees except family members.
11. No onsite or off-site signage.
12. Business hours of operation are limited to 7:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m.
13. The yard or the area where trailer storage occurs shall be surrounded by a sight obscuring
    fence.

Mr. Messel mentioned that discussions in JUD raised the need for a scope, therefore, the
applicant initially is renting out four trailers but is requesting up to ten trailers. Mr. Messel
also mentioned that guidance from the commissioners was needed regarding the sight
obscuring fence that would need to surround the storage area. He provided examples of
acceptable and unacceptable sight obscuring fences.

Commissioner Brian McMullin queried recommendation # 7 and asked for clarification
whether it referred to the lot or the building.

Mr. Messel answered that this was referring to the lot and wanted to ensure that the trailers
were not to be parked on other family lots in the family compound.

Commissioner Mark Owen asked about condition # 12 and asked if that was 7 days a week?

Mr. Messel confirmed that is was seven days a week.

Commissioner Mark Owen queried about the size of the fenced area and asked the applicant
about the 80’ X 80’ fenced area which seemed like a huge area to fence if he was only renting
10 trailers. He suggested that this area be at the front of the property for easy access to
customers when picking up the trailers and adequate space for a turnaround. Commissioner
Owens also suggested that perhaps an 80’ X 80’ sight- obscuring fence might be too much for
the applicant and that maybe he should consider a smaller area to fence that would fit his ten

     trailers.

     Mr. Kurt Staheli approached the podium and advised the 80’ X 80’ was his suggestion based
     his lot size, but he would be happy to make the changes as suggested by the commissioners.
