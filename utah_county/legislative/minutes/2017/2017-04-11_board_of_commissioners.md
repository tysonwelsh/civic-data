                                            PUBLIC HEARING
         PUBLIC HEARING TO CONSIDER APPROVAL OF THE PROPOSED BECKSTEAD
         AGRICULTURE PROTECTION AREA APPLICATION, WITH THE UTAH COUNTY
         PUBLIC WORKS MODIFICATION REQUEST TO EXCLUDE THE ROAD RIGHTS-OF-
         WAY WIDTHS FROM THE PROPOSAL, REDUCING ACREAGE AND REQUIRING A
         VARIANCE; APPROXIMATELY 40.028 ACRES LOCATED IN SECTION 7, T9S, R2E,
         IN THE WEST PAYSON AREA OF UNINCORPORATED UTAH COUNTY
(Track A, 26:15)
Peggy Kelsey of the Utah County Community Development Office presented slides (copies of which can be found
attached to these minutes) summarizing the Beckstead Agriculture Protection Area application for the ‘raising and
ranching of mink, and hay or grain crops.’ She reviewed the location of the proposed area which is currently in
production. Ms. Kelsey noted the Utah County Public Works Department has requested a modification to the
Beckstead application, and the Utah County Planning Commission has recommended the Board of Commissioners
deny the Agriculture Protection Area.

(Track A, 36:10)
Sergeant Gregory Knapp of the Utah County Attorney’s Office pointed out he had visited the site at the request of
the commissioners. He described the mink ranching, including the cages, cleaning process, and his observations on
two separate visits. Sgt. Knapp noted there are wild mink in the area. Commissioner Ivie asked whether there are
safety concerns in the way the Beckstead ranch is managed, and Sgt. Knapp replied “no;” he does not think there are
any safety concerns in their operation.

(Track A, 48:40)
Brent Beckstead, the applicant, came forward to explain he has been in full compliance with county requirements to
be granted agricultural protection for his mink farm. Commissioner Lee asked Mr. Beckstead for a response to the
Utah County Public Works modification request for road rights-of-way widths, and Mr. Beckstead stated he is
willing to work with the County Engineer. Mr. Beckstead said he will not sell the property because he would not
meet the 40-acre-minimum requirement if that happens, but he is willing to grant easements as asked. Mr.
Beckstead also mentioned spending thousands of dollars to reduce the flies on the property, and reviewed some of
the veterinary certification and good management requirements at Commissioner Ivie’s prompting. Veterinarian
Jason Loft out of Morgan, Utah also commented on ranch certification and called the Beckstead farm a model
operation.

(Track A, 55:55)
Jeff Cooper owns property near the Becksteads, and considers them good and decent people. He reviewed a series
of property purchases wherein he had hoped to develop that land, but was unable to complete the transaction due to
several factors. He asked the Board to deny agricultural protection, citing proximity to Payson City limits as one of
the reasons.

(Track A, 1:01:55)
Sterling Brown, Vice President of Public Policy for the Utah Farm Bureau Federation, talked about hardships facing
farmers. He cited state code and asked the Board to grant agricultural protection for the Beckstead operation.

(Track A, 1:09:00)
Dennis Stone noted that while the Beckstead family consists of generational farmers, this specific operation is new
and is encroaching upon their neighbors. He talked about potential future nuisance and asked the Board to protect
the existing rights of the neighbors by denying agricultural protection.

(Track A, 1:10:55)
Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore spoke on nuisance laws, mentioning that agriculture protection laws
are meant to help protect farmers from eminent domain, zoning changes, and annexation. A rancher is only offered
enhanced protection from nuisance claims so long as they are operating within good farming practices; they may
still be subject to nuisance complaints if outside of those good practices.

(Track A, 1:12:25)
Melanie Peck lives with her husband near the Beckstead farm. She read a letter from her husband Austin Peck in
opposition to the agriculture protection area. Ms. Peck has been cornered by mink on her property, and is also
opposed to the agricultural protection. She spoke about accountability and farmers needing to take responsibility for
their animals.

(Track A, 1:15:50)
Payson resident Karen Stephens is the next-door neighbor of previous commenter Melanie Peck. Ms. Stephens
loves agriculture and farming, but is unable to use her own barn to house her horses because of the excessive
number of flies. Ms. Stephens shared stories of having to keep one-hundred flies in her own home every day, and
expressed concern over potential health issues caused by the nearby mink farm.

(Track A, 1:20:30)
David Davis comes from three generations of mink ranchers; he expressed it is a family lifestyle that he hopes to see
his grandchildren continue, and is in favor of agricultural protection.

(Track A, 1:21:45)
Margaret Cooper recalled a previous meeting three years ago regarding agricultural protection for Brent Beckstead’s
mink operation. Ms. Cooper noted Mr. Beckstead had claimed at that time his neighbors would be ashamed for
opposing the area – he was going to prove to be a wonderful addition to the community. Ms. Cooper pointed out the
application was denied then by a 2-1 vote and Mr. Beckstead was encouraged to re-apply for the protection once that
positive relationship was established. Ms. Cooper presented 59 signatures of community residents that are adversely
not happy with Mr. Beckstead’s mink farm, and asked the Board to deny the agriculture protection area. She stated,
“It’s their livelihood; it’s our life.”

(Track A, 1:24:00)
Tony Bishop grew up on a farm in the area, and only remembers seeing two mink his entire childhood. He is now
surrounded by the odor and all his chickens have been killed, presumably by mink from the Beckstead ranch. Mr.
Bishop understands agriculture and needing to make a living, but the residents should also have protection.

(Track A, 1:26:15)
Bonnie Palfreyman lives in Payson and is concerned about Brent Beckstead taking accountability for his livestock.
She spoke about escaped mink, and how they become wards of the state as soon as they leave the property. Ms.
Palfreyman shared how one mink almost entered her home. She also commented on the unbearable flies, and the
manure containing mink carcasses – this is against code. Ms. Palfreyman linked the rise of rabies in bats to potential
rise of rabies in mink, noting there are two schools within two miles of the Beckstead operation. She shared a news
article from September 2003 in which eight children at Reese Elementary School in Spanish Fork needed to receive
rabies vaccinations after encountering mink.

(Track A, 1:36:45)
Trevor Brisk lives in Payson and is no stranger to farming; his family is responsible for 1,800 acres in the Roosevelt
area. Mr. Brisk is concerned about not being able to allow his pet chickens to forage because they may encounter
escaped mink. He is also worried about the smell, and health issues created by the flies attracted to the mink farm.
Mr. Brisk cheekily invited the commissioners to a picnic at his home for firsthand experience of the problems.

(Track A, 1:39:05)
Paul Westwood has a mink farm within Salem City limits, and comes from a line of mink ranchers. Mr. Westwood
noted some of the stories being told are exaggerated, as he has never seen mink behave in some of the ways claimed
today. He acknowledged that flies are a problem but may not necessarily be coming from the mink farm. Mr.
Westwood supports the Beckstead agricultural protection area.

(Track A, 1:41:40)
Cheri Bean lives just north of the Beckstead farm, and would like to be considered a viable part of the agriculture
community. Ms. Bean commented on subdivisions and acreage requirements, and the impracticability of placing a
commercial operation so near residential populations. She spoke of the hardships in trying to convince her children
and grandchildren to visit during Summer months; they refuse because of the terrible odor from the mink farm. Ms.
Bean asked the Board to deny the agricultural protection area so as not to negatively impact the rest of the
community for twenty years.

(Track A, 1:50:40)
Marlin Oldham lives south of the Beckstead farm. He commented on the unbearable smell of the mink, and the fact
that they are still able to escape the ranch despite the farmer’s best efforts. Mr. Oldham pointed out the community
residents were established prior to the mink farm, noting Brent Beckstead himself lives a full two miles from his
own Lehi operation. He asked the Board to deny the agricultural protection area application.

(Track A, 1:55:20)
Payson City Attorney Mark Sorensen addressed the factual basis for the Utah County Planning Commission’s
recommendation to deny the Beckstead application. He talked about the county nuisance ordinance and decreased
property values, asking the Board to deny the agricultural protection area because the farm can still operate without
that extra safeguard.

(Track A, 2:05:25)
Jason Rasblocker is in favor of agricultural protection. He noted Brent Beckstead continues to spend thousands of
dollars to minimize flies. Mr. Rasblocker also pointed out there are beautiful quarter-million-dollar properties in
Lehi, where additional mink farms are located. He also is not concerned about children being faced by four-pound
mink when there are more serious dangers on other ranches: getting kicked by a horse, or mowed down by a cow.
Mr. Rasblocker summarized, “This is the United States of America; they lawfully and legally bought 41 acres to
raise mink, and they can raise mink.” He asked the Board not to be distracted by issues unrelated to their legal right
to agricultural protection.

(Track A, 2:07:55)
Brent Beckstead returned for final comment. Mr. Beckstead noted he has not broken any laws regarding the farm
operation, and is within his rights according to statute to be granted agricultural protection. He responded to the
earlier comment about living two miles from his own ranch – that is true, but Mr. Beckstead pointed out he is within
half a mile of another mink ranch as well as other livestock farms and his family has a barbecue almost every
Sunday.

(Track A, 2:09:15)
Commissioner Graves asked Brent Beckstead to clarify earlier statements regarding easements. Commissioner
Graves asked if it is accurate to state Mr. Beckstead would like the Board to grant the agricultural protection without
the modification request from Utah County Public Works; this would mean the road rights-of-way widths are
included within the protection area. Mr. Beckstead confirmed, stating he can work with the County Engineer once
the protection is granted. Mr. Beckstead also mentioned he would be willing to grant or sell right-of-ways and

easements to the county if the Board would grant a variance on the acreage requirement for his protection area.
Commissioner Graves thanked him for the explanation, and Commissioner Lee closed the Public Hearing, returning
the discussion to the bench.

1.     APPROVE, MODIFY AND APPROVE, OR REJECT THE PROPOSED BECKSTEAD
       AGRICULTURE PROTECTION AREA APPLICATION, AND THE UTAH COUNTY PUBLIC
       WORKS MODIFICATION REQUEST TO EXCLUDE THE ROAD RIGHTS-OF-WAY WIDTHS
       FROM THE PROPOSAL, REDUCING THE ACREAGE AND REQUIRING A VARIANCE;
       APPROXIMATELY 40.028 ACRES LOCATED IN SECTION 7, T9S, R2E, IN THE WEST PAYSON
       AREA OF UNINCORPORATED UTAH COUNTY
(Track A, 2:11:25)
Commissioner Ivie reviewed the considerations for agricultural protection areas as defined by county ordinances.
This includes whether the property is being used for agriculture, contained in the correct zoning, has viable
production, and proposed or existing improvements. Commissioner Ivie talked about the importance of supporting
farmers, as forty acres of agricultural land are consistently lost hourly in this country – reasonable measures must be
taken to protect family farms.

(Track A, 2:15:10)
Commissioner Graves questioned whether RA-5 Zones were modified to allow mink farming, or if it had always
been included as a permitted use. Utah County Civil Division Chief Deputy Attorney David Shawcroft noted he
does not remember a time when it was not allowed. Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore reminded the
Board their decision on the proposed agriculture protection area is a legislative action; this means the criteria must
be acknowledged, but it does not have to be the only consideration. The commissioners discussed rights-of-way and
road exceptions with input from Peggy Kelsey of the Utah County Community Development Office.

10.    RATIFY A NOTICE OF TERMINATION BETWEEN UTAH COUNTY AND PROVO CITY
       RELATING TO THE ICE SHEET ARENA FACILITY IN PROVO CITY, UTAH COUNTY, UTAH
(Track B, 10:25)
Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore explained he drafted a termination letter to Provo City after speaking
to each commissioner individually. Commissioner Lee has signed the letter; it has already been sent to and received
by the city. Commissioner Graves noted Commissioner Lee signed it on behalf of the County Commission. Mr.
Moore added the notice of termination begins a 180-day countdown. Within the first 60 days of that period, Provo
City must declare in writing whether they will take control of the Ice Sheet Arena Facility. Commissioner Lee
mentioned the county has not yet received a written response from the city. Mr. Moore noted if the sixty days
passes without written response, there are options to sell to a third party or by auction per the terms of the
agreement, but that has not been discussed by the Board at this point.

(Track B, 14:10)
Acknowledging the crowded audience, Commissioner Lee invited comment on this issue. Pleasant Grove resident
Brian Post-Jones was the first to come forward and ask, “Why are you trying to get rid of the ice rink?” He asked
the commissioners not to take it away from the people. Grace Post-Jones, and Serenity Post-Jones, both echoed that
comment. Tracy Stout, who described herself as a single parent from Springville, also wants the ice rink to stay
open; she mentioned her nine-year-old cried upon hearing it may close. Provo resident Robert Stevens would like
the ice rink to stay open but also understands the government responsibility in spending tax dollars. Mr. Stevens
also began to request the suspension of a county employee, but was interrupted by the Board as this is not the
appropriate venue to voice that concern – he was asked to follow up directly with the Commission Office.

(Track B, 21:25)
Payson resident Erin Short started a Facebook page to support keeping the ice rink open. She explained how she
regularly utilizes the arena, and expressed it would be detrimental to Utah County and the community youth to pull
out of the agreement with Provo City. Ms. Short noted the fees and facility would no longer be viable without
county support. Pleasant Grove resident Brian Blum serves on his local high school hockey board, and has seen
how the ice rink has provided a safe place for kids. Mr. Blum noted hockey kept him out of trouble as a high school
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS

student, and asked the Board to consider their approval today of a new position in the Department of Drug and
Alcohol Prevention; the rink also helps teenagers and families. Mr. Blum asked the commissioners to rescind the
termination notice, pointing out 75% of the ice rink users are outside of Provo City and serves many in our fast-
growing county.

Eagle Mountain resident Laurie Gilson’s family has been skating at the Peaks Ice Arena for eight years, and it is the
best in the state with well-maintained ice, fantastic staff, and reasonable prices. She suggested asking for taxpayer
input, as Ms. Gilson is confident the community would not want the rink to be privatized. Shelley Mercato

find a solution. Church groups, family gatherings, and many others use the rink for many purposes. Ms. Woodland

and even those outside of the county is worth finding a solution if the county could provide a reason for wanting to
terminate the contract.

(Track B, 36:15)
Salem resident Jeffrey Redden is worried that if Provo City is solely responsible for the ice rink, he and his family

Lindon resident Karen Danielson has four children who have gone through skating programs at the ice rink, and her

Fork resident Kate Jones explained she spends 5 days a week at the ice rink; it is her second home. Ms. Jones
became emotional when explaining her problems melt away when she is on the ice, and there is no way her parents
would be able to take her to the next closest facility, in South Jordan, if this ice rink closes. Nineteen-year-old Sarah
Lyle is a competitive figure skater who has been using this rink for 14 years, and has seen it grow and improve from
being a private facility to management by Provo City and Utah County. Provo resident Belki Tolman’s daughter
plays hockey, and all of her friends are from different cities but have this ice rink in common. Ms. Tolman hopes
the commissioners will find a solution that does not require terminating financial support; the community will not be
able to afford privatized prices, and Provo City alone will not be able to keep the rink open.

(Track B, 45:10)
Spanish Fork resident Marilyn Jones commented on the youth in the audience wearing their uniforms and medals to
express passion and support for keeping the ice rink open. Brothers Cole Redden and Ryan Redden out of Salem
have loved learning how to play hockey, and hope to continue at this arena. Pleasant Grove High School Junior, and
Assistant Captain of UCI (Utah County Independent) North Warriors, Hunter Blum explained the hockey players on
their team pay their own fees; privatizing the Peaks Arena would dramatically increase prices and prevent some of

Orem resident and ‘proud hockey mom’ Vicki Wiesenberg explained this is a great place for everyone to play and
feel included. Hockey is a sport that does not rely on the usual ‘starting five’ in many other sport – every kid gets a
chance to skate and make goals. Ms. Wiesenberg pointed out Peaks Ice Arena is renowned even outside of this
state, drawing in competitions and in turn economic base for Utah County. She asked the Board not to pull out
resources from this Olympic level rink. Spanish Fork resident Belle Campbell has devoted her entire life to figure
skating. She became emotional while explaining she has made many friends that she will not able to see if the ice
rink closes. Ms. Campbell stated the first time she visited the rink, she realized her dream of going to the Olympics.

Team and the tremendous progress they have made. Commissioner Ivie stated he hates political fear-mongering,
emphasizing he does not have any intention of shutting down the ice rink.

amounts from the county to the Ice Sheet, noting the county has invested $2,000,000 into this facility in the last
seven years while acknowledging the contributions have steadily declined since 2010. Commissioner Ivie
questioned how to justify continued subsidization for ice rink users while other youth with Olympic dreams in other

discussion today was facilitated because of the Board sending the letter.

Commissioner Graves reminded those present that he was a teacher before a politician, and applauded the parents of

termination notice. Commissioner Lee added the conversation will continue once Provo City replies, and suggested
the audience ask Provo City to respond quickly.
