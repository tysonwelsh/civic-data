REASONING FOR VARIATIONS BETWEEN REQUESTED & RECOMMENDATION AMOUNTS:

The PARC Committee is recommending fully funding 23 applications and partially funding 2 applications for this year’s
Cultural Organization and Recreation/Cultural Facility Grants. The committee determines their recommendations by
reviewing each application in reference to the PARC Tax Policy and the PARC Committee scoring criteria. The scoring
criteria includes four weighted categories: local impact, community need, capacity and funding, and measurable
outcomes. While the committee recognizes the benefits of all 32 applications, due to various reasons the committee has
recommended the following variations between requested and recommended amounts.


        Heritage Arts Foundation
        The PARC Committee is recommending not funding the Heritage Arts Foundation’s application. The Heritage Arts
        Foundation is working to install themed statues at the Lehi Family Park. While the committee supports their
        broader project of installing art at Family Park, the application they submitted was presented to the committee as
        follows: “A community-building program that engages Lehi youth to facilitate legacy storytelling and donor
        engagement, resulting in the placement of sculptures at Lehi’s new Family Park.” The PARC Committee scored
        their project low in local impact and community need, determining that training youth in fundraising skills does not
        align with the grant's intended purpose.


        Lehi Historical Society & Archives
        The PARC Committee recommends fully funding all of the Lehi Historical Society’s applications. Please note that
        the Facility Department approved the application titled 'Banners for Lehi Historical Society Building and Main
        Street Light Poles,' but requested a condition that funding will only be disbursed once an approved streetlight pole
        attachment has been chosen.


        Lehi Area Music Association
        The PARC Committee recommends fully funding the application titled, “Low Notes for Higher Ground” and
        partially funding the application entitled, “Space, the Final Frontier.” This application, in part, proposed replacing a
        door within the facility, but the Facility Department did not approve this change, thus the partial recommendation.


        Lehi Arts Council
        The Lehi Arts Council submitted applications for 9 projects totaling $179,849. Seven of those projects were facility
        upgrades to the Lehi Arts Center. Only two of the seven facility projects were approved by the Facility Department
        (glass replacement and clay and paint trap). The PARC Committee is recommending following the Facility
        Department’s directive in relation to the facility projects as well as fully funding the Show Rights and Music
        Program projects.




ORGANIZATIONS IN QUESTION:
Please note the PARC Committee called into question whether or not 4 organizations’ meet the definition of cultural
organization as defined in section 2.5 of the policy; “– a private nonprofit organization or institution having as its primary
purpose the advancement and preservation of: (1) natural history; (2) art; (3) music; (4) 2 theater; (5) dance; or (6) cultural
arts, including literature, a motion picture, or storytelling; and an administrative unit.”
As we have funded several of these organizations in the past, we have included extra details below to help the Council
better understand the PARC Committee’s recommendation. Each of the four organizations in question were asked to
defend their organization and its primary purpose against the definitions in the policy. We have included their responses
below along with the PARC Committee’s recommendation.



        Chabad of Utah County
        From Rabbi Chaim: “Our non-profit is not listed as a cultural organization, as being a source of Jewish culture is
        just one of the responsibilities that we bear. We are the single source for Judaism in Lehi, which carries with it
        many types of responsibilities, including education, programming, and others. One of the needs that has seen
        increasing demand is to also provide cultural events that are open to all citizens. That is why our grant request is
        for funding for a specific cultural event, and not for operational budget which would serve other non-profit activities
        as well.”


        The PARC Committee recommends fully funding their application.


        Just For Kids of Utah County, Inc.
        From Michelle Holbrook: “Just for Kids is a private 501(c)3 non-profit organization organized in 2007. We serve
        adults with disabilities at the Curtis Center for Arts and Education. Our curriculum includes humanities, general
        music, choir, theater, musical theater, arts and crafts, sharing a musical class at Covington Manor in a memory
        care unit, dance at a local studio, geography including history of the 50 states in America, and monthly literature-
        based lessons at the Highland library. Lehi High school graciously invites us to 4 theatrical or dance programs
        each year. Implementing our curricular learning we put on a Christmas musical based talent show and a spring
        musical theater production of Wizard of Oz. Just for Kids of Utah County is preserving and advancing history,
        music, art, theater, and dance through our classes for adults with disabilities. The primary focus of our instruction
        is cultural arts. Our participants LOVE the arts. This is where they excel! Academics are more difficult due to
        intellectual restrictions.”


        The PARC Committee recommends fully funding their application.


        United Angels Foundation
        From Silvia Lam: “Thank you so much for taking the time to thoroughly review our application and for considering
        United Angels Foundation for a grant. Our organization is dedicated to promoting the advancement of disability
        culture, a rich and vital cultural perspective that is historically underrepresented in mainstream narratives.
        Disability culture encompasses the shared experiences, values, expressions, and histories of individuals with
        disabilities, and we work to ensure these voices are heard, respected, and celebrated. Through education, shared
        experiences and other opportunities to connect, our organization preserves and promotes cultural expression
        within the disability community and ensures that these narratives become a valued and visible part of our shared
        cultural landscape. This directly aligns with the eligibility requirement of advancing cultural arts and storytelling,
        as we are actively engaged in preserving and elevating an important cultural tradition that enriches the entire
        community.”


        The PARC Committee recommends fully funding their application.



        Wrap the World with Quilts, Inc.
        From Gina Halladay and Beth Hawkins: “Thank you for allowing us the opportunity to clarify the primary purpose
        of Wrap the World with Quilts and how it meets the definition of a cultural organization. At Wrap the World with
        Quilts, we have two primary purposes:


        1 - to promote, teach, and preserve the art of quilt making,
2 - to provide those in need with a quilt.


We believe that this meets the definition of a cultural organization, as we are a private non-profit organization
working to advance and preserve the art and culture of quilts and quiltmaking. When our organization first came
to be, we hoped to provide a small measure of comfort to refugees fleeing their homes by providing them with a
handmade quilt. Quilts offer comfort as they have a handmade touch, provide warmth, and add beauty to one's
environment. As our organization grew, there became a significant interest in learning the art and skills of
quiltmaking. With this realization, we quickly discovered that our mission to collect and distribute quilts evolved as
our community (from kids to seniors) wanted to serve others by learning how to quilt. This resulted in the
establishment of several sewing centers in Lehi, Poland, Ukraine and Hawaii. At these centers, we provide
volunteers with knowledgeable teachers, the required sewing materials, and an energetic atmosphere. Those who
come to our centers and attend our workshops will learn a thing or two about sewing and quilting, which is
eventually given back to us in the form of a quilt, as part of the cultural heritage of quiltmaking involves gifting
those quilts… which perfectly aligns with our efforts to bring comfort to those in need. Over the last few years we
have worked diligently to engage the Lehi community in learning how to sew and quilt, in an effort to keep our
pioneer heritage thriving. We have expanded our sewing centers to accommodate more people wanting to sew
with us, we have taught classes and workshops across the city, state, and nation, and we have been guest
speakers and instructors at schools, churches, universities, businesses, and homes, with our efforts dedicated to
teaching people of all ages how to quilt and how to serve. As such, we believe that we qualify as a cultural
organization with the primary purpose of advancing and preserving both an art and a culture. We advance the art
of quiltmaking through teaching and we preserve the culture of quilts by gifting them to those in need both locally
and across the world. Both the skills we teach and the quilts we make have the ability to be passed down from
generation to generation and keep the art of quilting alive.”


The PARC Committee has concluded that Wrap the World with Quilts, Inc. serves a dual purpose as both a
humanitarian and an arts education organization. We recommend funding only those portions of their applications
that specifically advance the art of quilt making. We recommend fully funding the Heritage Day Quilting Workshop
and the Community Workspace Expansion applications. The committee recommends not funding the vehicle
purchase application, as it scored low in all categories. We recommend partially funding the Operating Expenses
application excluding all salaries and all shipping fees.
