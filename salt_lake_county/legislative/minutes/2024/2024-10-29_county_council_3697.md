Council Member Stringham read the proclamation for Salt Lake County ’s 
Health Literacy Month.  
Salt Lake County
WHEREAS, health literacy is defined as the degree to which individuals have 
the ability to find, understand, and use information and services to inform 
health-related decisions and actions for themselves and others (personal 
health literacy ); and the degree to which organizations enable individuals to 
find, understand and use information and services to inform health -related 
decisions and actions for themselves and others (organizational health 
literacy); and 
WHEREAS, health literacy is essential for individuals to make informed 
health decisions, effectively navigate the healthcare system, and manage their 
health; and 
WHEREAS, health literacy empowers individuals to understand medical 
information, follow treatment plans, and engage in preventive health 
practices; and 
WHEREAS, inadequate health literacy has been associated with poorer 
health outcomes, increased hospitalizations, and higher healthcare costs; and 
WHEREAS, addressing overwhelming health literacy needs can reduce 
healthcare costs, improve the accessibility, quality, safety, and patient 
satisfaction of healthcare, and improve the health and quality of life for 
millions of Utahns; and 
WHEREAS, public schools, librarians, healthcare professionals, insurance 
providers, public health professionals, community organizations, social 
workers, and employers play a critical role in improving health literacy 
through clear communication, education, accessible health information, and 
routinely teaching and using health literacy interventions, such as plain 
language and teach-back; and

WHEREAS, The U .S. Department of Health and Human Services includes 
health literacy in one of its overarching Healthy People 2030 goals "Eliminate 
health disparities, achieve health equity, and attain health literacy to improve 
the health and well-being of all.”; and 
WHEREAS, October is recognized nationally as Health Literacy Month, 
providing an opportunity to raise awareness about the importance of health 
literacy and to promote actions that improve health communication and 
outcomes; 
Now, Therefore, I Jenny Wilson, Mayor of Salt Lake County, and the Salt 
Lake County Council, do hereby proclaim the month of October as Health 
Literacy Month in Salt Lake County. I call upon all librarians, healthcare 
professionals, educators, employers, and community organizations to work 
together to improve health literacy, enhance communication, and ensure that 
all individuals have the tools and knowledge to make informed health 
decisions.

2. PUBLIC HEARINGS AND ISSUANCE OF PUBLIC NOTICES
2.1 Public Hearing to Receive Public Comment About Salt Lake 
County’s Agreement to Purchase 18.6 Acres of Real Property 
Located at Approximately 799 W. Coliseum Way in Midvale, 
Utah
24-2266
Attachments: Staff Report
Presenter: Megan Hillyard, SLCo Department Director of Admin. Services.
Informational
A motion was made by Council Member Bradley, seconded by Council 
Member Theodore, to open the public hearing.  Council Member Harrison 
was absent for the vote.  
Ms. Megan Hillyard , Director, Administrative Services Department, 
delivered a PowerPoint presentation on the Midvale office campus purchase .  
She reviewed an asset summary; the County ’s options (remodel, rebuild, or 
relocate); a facility condition assessment for the Salt Lake County 
Government Center; and a list of due diligence tasks for the County.
Mr. John Tanner , Sandy Hills Community Council, spoke in opposition to 
the Midvale purchase.  He stated even though the building was 40 years old, 
there were many other buildings in the County that were older.  He stated age 
was incidental, due to the expense of moving.  He wondered how much 
maintenance had been neglected over the years.  He stated many people in 
his neighborhood are struggling and cannot afford a tax increase.  He 
suggested renting out one of the buildings in the Government Center, as rent 
would be easier to collect than taxes.
Council Member Winder Newton  stated this was less about the age of the 
building and more about what would be the most cost effective.  Purchasing 
the building in Midvale, even with the price tag, the cost for renovations, and 
the cost for moving, would still cost less than renovating the Government 
Center.  This would not result in a tax increase because of the high value of 
the Government Center property.  The property could either be sold or 
leased, and that revenue would pay for any bond payments.

Ms. Rachelle Morris  spoke in support of the Midvale purchase.  She 
stated she has met with thousands of County residents this year.  One thing 
that has been repeatedly brought up is that there are individuals who would 
like County Government to be closer to where they live.  Additionally, many 
residents have difficulty distinguishing between Salt Lake County and Salt 
Lake City functions.  She felt this move to Midvale City would help with 
those concerns.
Mr. Bart Barker stated he served as a County Commissioner in the 1980s.  
He spoke about the history of the Government Center and some of the 
challenges at the time.  Prior to building the Government Center, all County 
elected offices were housed at the City and County Building.  The County 
wanted to move away from leasing and toward ownership.  Being closer to 
the center of the valley was also a priority at the time.  Rather than speaking 
in favor or opposition to the purchase, his intent was to watch and listen to 
the due diligence process and gather more information to make an informed 
decision.
Council Member Bradley  stated members of a community can sometimes 
be quick to tear down buildings because they are “in the way .”  Restoration 
is not always a bad idea.  However, there were other considerations with this 
decision.
Ms. Samantha [last name not given] asked if the County had any plans 
for accommodating transgender individuals, given the passage of HB 257, 
sex-based designations for privacy, anti-bullying, and women’ s 
opportunities.  She wondered if any accommodations would be made for 
individuals who were not openly transgender to all their colleagues.  
Ms. Megan Hillyard  stated the building was designed as a call center .  
Readying the building for County use will require some renovation.  During 
that stage, the varied agency needs will be taken into consideration. 
A motion was made by Council Member Bradley, seconded by Council 
Member Theodore, to close the public hearing.  Council Member Harrison 
was absent for the vote.
