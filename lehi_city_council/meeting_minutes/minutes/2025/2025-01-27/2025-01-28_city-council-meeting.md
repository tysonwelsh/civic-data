2. Hearing to consider whether to revoke the Business License for the Corner Shop.
Ryan Wood, City Attorney, provided some context around the business license revocation hearing.
He explained that the business owner's attorney had notified the city the previous Tuesday that
they would be voluntarily surrendering their business license, so they would not be opposing the
revocation. Wood noted that the city's code does not have a specific mechanism for a voluntary
surrender, so they wanted to still make a brief record of the findings to properly document the
revocation process. This included noting that they had received the email from the business owner's
attorney about the voluntary surrender. Wood indicated they wanted to ensure that proper
procedures were followed, even though the business was not contesting the revocation.

Craig Chambers, Assistant City Attorney, stated that Lehi Municipal Code 4-1-6 lists the
conditions for which a business license may be suspended or revoked for one or more violations.
The city is asking for a revocation of license for violating the following conditions: 1. Because
the license should not have been issued; 2. Because of licensee's failure or refusal to permit or
cooperate with an audit or inspection; 3. If continued operation of the licensed activity would
constitute a nuisance, or present a danger to the health, safety or general welfare of the community;

and 4. Because of a violation of this title or any other applicable provision of law. Chambers stated
that his hope was to provide enough evidence to the Council that the business violated 4 of the
conditions for revocation.
Chambers continued by stating that a 10 day notice dated January 16, 2025 was sent to the owner
of the business listed on the business license application, and the city received confirmation that
the letter was received by her attorney, Mr. Brown.

Craig Chambers then presented evidence for each violation. First, Chambers asserted the license
should never have been issued in the first place. He explained that based on the business's own
application, they indicated they would not be selling e-cigarettes, vaping products, or tobacco.
However, the city later determined the Corner Shop was operating as an illegal "smoke shop"
within 1,000 feet of a school, which is prohibited by city ordinances. Chambers said the city had
evidence that when staff followed up with the business, they claimed they would be operating as
a convenience store, not a smoke shop. He argued this was false information provided on the initial
license application. Secondly, Chambers stated that during their investigation in December, the
Corner Shop refused to cooperate with required inspections by the Utah State Health Department
and Utah Department of Agriculture. Lastly, Chambers asserted the continued operation of the
Corner Shop would constitute a nuisance and present a danger to the health, safety, and welfare of
the community. He indicated that Sergeant Lewis, Lehi Police Department, would speak to the
finding of their investigation to support this claim.

Sergeant Lewis stated that the Lehi Police Department became aware of the Corner Shop around
December 10th. They received a report of some juveniles who had purchased e-cigarettes and
marijuana gummies from the store. This prompted the police to conduct a sting operation over the
course of about 4 days. During this sting, the police observed numerous underage individuals, as
young as 14 years old, going into the Corner Shop and leaving with vaping products and THC
edibles. The officers would then stop the juveniles' vehicles and the kids would admit to purchasing
these items without being ID'd by the store clerks. Sergeant Lewis said they served two search
warrants on the Corner Shop and confiscated around $13,000 worth of illegal products like THC
gummies. However, they noticed that as soon as the police left, more underage customers would
go right back into the store to make purchases. The police cited the store clerks, both a daytime
and nighttime employee, with numerous counts of selling nicotine and tobacco products to minors.
Sergeant Lewis described the Corner Shop as essentially operating as a "smoke shop" rather than
a convenience store, with the majority of its inventory being drug paraphernalia and illicit
substances.

Mayor Johnson asked if there was anyone present that would like to respond in defense. No one
spoke in response.


    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to revoke the business permit for the Corner Shop with
                the findings that they have violated the conditions of Lehi City Municipal Code 4-
                1-6 and include that the license should not have been issued, the business refused
                to cooperate with inspections, the business is a nuisance or presents danger to the
                health, safety or general welfare of the community, and the business is in violation
                of Title 4-1-6 or any other applicable provision of law, therefore the business
                license is revoked. Councilor Hancock seconded the motion.

3. Presentations and Reports

3.1 Financial Audit Presentation
Dana Howell, the city's auditor, provided an overview of the annual financial report. She stated
that the main goal of the audit was to ensure the financial statements were presented fairly in
accordance with accounting principles. Howell spoke to some of the financial highlights from the
from the analysis, such as the bonds issued in the electric fund and changes to the net pension
asset and liability. She also discussed the single audit package, which includes reports on internal
control, compliance with federal grants, and state legal compliance. Howell noted there were no
significant issues identified in these reports. Regarding the governance letter, she explained this
is a standard letter outlining things like no changes to accounting policies, use of estimates, and
lack of any disagreements with management. She stated there were no material matters to report.
Overall, she indicated the audit went smoothly and the financial statements were properly
presented. She thanked the city staff for their cooperation throughout the audit process.

   3.1) Presentation and Public Input on priorities for the Community Development Block
   Program (CDBG)- Claudia Saldana, Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG)
   The CDBG funds prioritization discussion focused on Lehi City's annual $300,000 allocation
   from HUD to invest in lower to moderate income neighborhoods. Claudia Saldana explained
   the process for approving the projects. The city council had previously provided their
   priorities for CDBG funding, with the top items being sidewalks, street improvements, water
   and sewer improvements, parks and recreation projects, and housing rehabilitation programs.

    Mayor Johnson asked if anyone from the public would like to provide their input.

    Residents Jenny Allred and Geraldine Daly expressed concerns about the lack of sidewalks
    on 400 East, citing safety risks for children and high school students. The mayor and staff
    discussed the process for obtaining funding through the Safe Routes to School program and
    the challenges of meeting the criteria for CDBG projects in that area.

    The Council discussion highlighted the importance of citizen input in determining the
    priorities for the CDBG funds and the ongoing efforts to address infrastructure needs in the
    community.
