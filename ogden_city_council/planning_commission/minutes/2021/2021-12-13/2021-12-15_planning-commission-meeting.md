1. MU Site Plan/Development Agreement, for Moda Greenwell mixed use building at approximately 2459
   Quincy Avenue. (Attachment 1)
   Mr. Jake Wood explained he has been working closely with the RDA Staff and is proposing to develop
   a new multi-family building at the corner of 25th and Quincy. The ground floor on 25th Street will be
   commercial or retail space with 176 apartments on the upper levels. The intent is for the building to
   connect with future development within this block as well as the with pedestrian walkways running
   north and south and a green belt running east and west. The architecture is designed to fit with the
   historic fabric of the community, replicating the Prairie Style. He indicated the plan shows a pedestrian
   walkway area on the east side of the building but he is working with Staff to identify the best place for
   pedestrian activity which will extend into future phases of the project. He described the architecture
   and materials stating some variation is proposed to provide visual interest along the Quincy side of the
   building due to its length. Materials are brick masonry and hardi panels, with large, framed windows.
   Entry canopies and awnings will be provided at entrances, and the main entries to the building will be
   facing the street. The building is also proposed to be energy efficient and meet the standards of
   Enterprise Green certification with Energy Star appliances.
   Commissioner Blaisdell asked about the proposed podium parking. Mr. Wood explained the first floor
   of the structure is to be concrete and will contain most of the parking for the project. The first floor also
   is proposed to house storefronts facing 25th Street, potentially containing a coffee shop or other small
   food vendors which would service users of the BRT transportation line. He also described the
   entrance into the parking area which will be on the Quincy side of the building. Interior parking will be
   limited to tenants and will be secure. He also indicated the entryway will be enhanced, which could
   result in loss of one or two parking spaces but hoped the enhancement can be done with architecture
   so needed on-site parking can remain.
   Mr. Brierley explained the proposal is for 176 apartment units with 5,400 square feet of commercial
   space. The plan shows a total of 202 parking spaces consisting of podium parking and exterior
   surface parking. Amenities to the project include two rooftop gathering areas and an interior courtyard
   as well as direct access to the greenbelt area for the overall project. This review is to determine
   whether the proposal is consistent with the approved master plan for the block and satisfies the
   standards of the MU zone. While the proposal fits with the master plan with its brownstone
   appearance on the north side, assurance needs to be made that the north-south walkway remains
   available for users from the neighborhood, the proposed apartments and the BRT. The pathway also
   should be enhanced to create a comfortable walkway with pedestrian lighting. He stated the MU zone
   requires specific types of architecture, and the proposed Prairie style fits those standards as well as fits
   with the fabric of the community. The brownstone appearance on the north provides access to the

  exterior plaza which is important in that it creates a synergy between the different buildings within the
  project as well as the pedestrian walkway. He described work which still must be done to satisfy both
  the MU code and the existing master plan, including stairways, porches and entrances to the west.
  The green building concept fits with the policies of the East Central Community Plan, and with easy
  access to the downtown and the BRT, provides an environmentally friendly and walkable community
  atmosphere. Staff recommendation is for approval of the project, subject to the items identified in the
  Project Corrections Report and approval by the Redevelopment Agency.
  Commissioner Sandau expressed concern that there is only one entrance into the parking garage to
  service 174 apartments. Mr. Wood explained due to the grade change on the site, there is no other
  location where vehicle access to the structure can be provided. He stated other projects similar to this
  have worked well with no conflicts with residents entering or exiting the garage. Chair Graf noted the
  masterplan identifies there will be live/work space within the block and asked if this project could
  accommodate this. He also asked whether the outdoor plaza will serve the residents or become
  outdoor dining and seems inadequate. Mr. Brierley stated the project is not designed for live/work
  space. He further described the patio areas and the recessed area on 25th Street which are intended
  to provide gathering space for residents and users of the BRT. Mr. Wood stated the recess on 25th
  Street provides a secondary access to the building as well as a transition space between the building
  and the sidewalk which could also accommodate café tables. Chair Graf also noted the development
  agreement requires a 10’ plaza area, but the plan shows 8’. Mr. Brierley explained there was an error
  in the development agreement, and it will be changed to 8’. Chair Graf also asked about the
  appearance of the east side of the building, noting it appears to be monolithic and doesn’t appear to fit
  with the surrounding residential neighborhood. Mr. Brierley explained there is a 40’ setback for the
  garage entry area which provides some relief. Mr. Stratford explained it appears the building is flipped
  from its original appearance with the entrances on the east side and the more monolithic side facing
  the interior of the block. Commissioner Southwick felt he building complements the existing brick of
  the State buildings to the east. Chair Graf stated due to the length of the building, there should be
  some design features to break up the flat plane. Mr. Brock Loomis presented a rendering of what the
  proposed building would look like from the east, noting some visual relief is provided. Mr. Brierley
  explained some ornamentation could be added such as entry features or sidewalk art to improve the
  pedestrian experience. Commissioner Sandau felt these details could be worked out between the
  applicant and the Staff.
  MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Sandau to recommend approval of the site plan
               and development agreement based on the findings the proposed plan can comply with
               the Capitol Square MU zone standards, is in substantial conformity to the approved
               project master plan, provides adequate utilities and maximizes safety of vehicle and
               pedestrian movement. Approval is subject to the Staff recommended conditions,
               identified in Attachment 7 approval by the Ogden Redevelopment Agency and the
               applicant work with Staff in providing visual relief to the façade of the structure,
               particularly along the east side facing Quincy Avenue. Motion was seconded by
               Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously, with Commissioners Blaisdell, Garner,
               Sandau, Southwick and Graf voting aye.

There being no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at 6:20pm.
