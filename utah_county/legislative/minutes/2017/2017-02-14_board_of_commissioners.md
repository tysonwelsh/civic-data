Acknowledging those with comment regarding Consent Agenda Item No. 1 on the calendar, Commissioner Lee
opened the floor to the public at this time.

(Track A, 04:45)
Cheri Bean has lived in the area for 25 years and shares a fence with the Beckstead property, whose owners are
currently seeking agricultural protection for their mink farm operation. Ms. Bean explained she has previously
argued against agricultural protection for this property, and prior complaints to the Utah County Health Department
and Utah County Sheriff’s Office go unresolved. She noted the gate has been left wide open for several hours at a
time which is against code. Ms. Bean also commented on the decreased fish and other wildlife in the nearby stream,
and the stress of having to shoot animals that pose a threat to her hens and turkeys. She stated the mink are not
marked and it is therefore impossible to prove they belong to the Beckstead family, but Ms. Bean has lost more than
60 hens and 15 turkeys since their operation began. She questioned why the farm was allowed to set up so near to
an already established community.

(Track A, 08:53)
Payson resident Martin Ewell’s property is adjacent to the Beckstead property, and he has lived there for forty years
with no issue of flies or mink until the recent acquisition for the mink farm operation. Mr. Ewell questioned the
safety of public health where uncontrolled mink are concerned, since they are very aggressive and may carry rabies.
He stated it would be unfair to the community to grant amnesty to the Beckstead family and not insist they be held
accountable.

(Track A, 10:40)
Mark Sorensen came forward to represent Payson City and the city’s objection to the proposed agriculture
protection area for the Beckstead property. Mr. Sorensen talked about the hardships the city would face if the
protection is approved, including severe impacts on and perhaps completely stopping subdivision developments
which have begun. He acknowledged the referral to the Utah County Planning Commission is only one of several
steps needed for protection to be granted, but asked for the record to reflect Payson City as being opposed.

(Track A, 12:20)
Payson resident Ann Christiansen explained her mother has lived in the area for nearly 40 years, and had let Ms.
Christiansen know how upset she was after the initial meetings regarding the incoming Beckstead mink farm
operation. Now that Ms. Christiansen has moved in with her mother, she is astonished by how much the quality of
life has deteriorated because of the mink farm. Ms. Christiansen noted there are nine structures on the Beckstead
property instead of the permitted three buildings, which demonstrates the farmers are ignoring the rules. She noted
it would be problematic to allow the business to be immune from consequences when they are already violating
standards. Ms. Christiansen talked about the fear in facing mink in her carport, and damage to property in the form
of shredded window screens. She mentioned her family is no longer able to enjoy nice weather because of the
horrible smell intensified by mink carcasses which are not properly disposed. Ms. Christiansen noted the farmers
will not accept returns of their mink upon escape, and also questioned why the business was allowed to set up where
it is currently located rather than the other end of the county with similar operations. She pleaded with the Board to
support their constituents and consider the legacy being left behind.

(Track A, 16:30)
Mink farm owner Brent Beckstead came forward. Mr. Beckstead explained he and his family are second and third
generation mink farmers; they purchased the land for this purpose and are within their legal rights to operate there.
He noted his original intent to purchase land further west and way from established residents, but concerns for water
transportation prohibited that plan. Mr. Beckstead also explained he had not previously met acreage requirements to
apply for agricultural protection, but can now satisfy those standards. He talked about the expense of fencing the
property, noting the gate is sometimes left open by employees feeding the mink. Mr. Beckstead mentioned a
machine which cost $58,000 and was purchased to clean the sheds every few weeks to help minimize odors; he is
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS

trying to be a good neighbor and a good farmer by addressing problems like the smell and flies. He explained his
family had been robbed after a couple of years in operations – 80 of their animals were harmed and killed, and dogs
were purchased as a deterrent against criminals. Mr. Beckstead questioned the earlier claim that his farm is
responsible for decreased aquatic life, since he is more than 100 yards away from the stream and other farms
operating upstream may be spilling pesticides or otherwise negatively impacting the water flow. He repeated his
intent to be a good neighbor, mentioning his family often helps with snow removal and other community tasks.

(Track A, 21:00)
Commissioner Lee summarized this issue has been approved to refer to the Utah County Planning Commission and
the Utah County Agricultural Area Advisory Board for additional dialogue, and those entities will in turn make a
recommendation to this Board. He noted the public would continue to have opportunities to speak at those meetings
and future Commission meetings. Brent Beckstead interjected that each building on his property went through the
appropriate building permit process, which led Martin Ewell to ask Mr. Beckstead how large the operation is going
to become. The commissioners suggested they continue that conversation amongst themselves outside of this
meeting. Cheri Bean asked about the process to require animals be marked so that she could prove it is the mink
that are killing her fowl, and Commissioner Graves responded that would be a state issue.

                                          REGULAR AGENDA
1.     ADOPT AN ORDINANCE REGARDING THE COMPOSITION OF THE UTAH COUNTY
       TOURISM TAX ADVISORY BOARD
(Track A, 26:25)
Utah County Deputy Attorney Paul Jones summarized the purpose of this ordinance is to ensure no individual can
serve concurrently on the Utah County Tourism Tax Advisory Board, and the Board of Directors for the Utah Valley
Convention and Visitors Bureau. Commissioner Ivie noted there are currently a couple of members serving on both
boards, and asked how often that occurs; Mr. Jones was not sure of the answer. Commissioner Ivie acknowledged
the attempt to protect from conflicts of interest since the Tourism Tax Advisory Board makes recommendations to
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS
MINUTES – February 14, 2017
Page 5

the commissioners on where to invest tax dollars. He expressed concerns over unintentionally reducing the qualified
individuals on the Tourism Tax Advisory Board (TTAB) – there should be board members who understand tourism
as it relates to hotels, the Convention and Visitors Bureau, art museums, and other local attractions. Commissioner
Ivie suggested TTAB members could instead voluntarily excuse themselves from being involved in proposals
wherein their organization could financially profit. Commissioner Graves pointed out the Utah Valley Convention
and Visitors Bureau will soon be reporting directly to the TTAB for one such recommendation.

(Track A, 30:48)
At Commissioner Lee’s request, Paul Jones reviewed the functions of the Tourism Tax Advisory Board: providing
the commissioners with a priority list for tourism taxes, including TRT and TRCC monies. Commissioner Graves
added the Utah Valley Convention and Visitors Bureau (UVCVB) is a 501(c)6 organization. Mr. Jones clarified
state law which designates at least two percent must go to the function of tourism in the county; the UVCVB is not
designated as the sole recipient of that two percent, but the commissioners have historically given it to the UVCVB
since they provide that service.

(Track A, 34:20)
Commissioner Ivie stated he is not comfortable with singling out the Utah Valley Convention and Visitors Bureau as
the only group wherein a member cannot serve on their Board of Directors and the TTAB, but strongly recommends
the ‘excuse yourself from grading proposals if you stand to benefit’ route. The commissioners discussed having
checks and balances in place, with Commissioner Lee noting the final decision on recommendations from the TTAB
remains with the commissioners. Commissioner Lee added the Tourism Tax Advisory Board has years of
experience in a field which he does not, and he enjoys seeing their presentations and having projects explained.

                 Commissioner Ivie made the motion to deny Regular Agenda Item No. 1. The
                 motion was seconded by Commissioner Graves and carried with the following
                 vote:
                                                                            AYE: William C. Lee
                                                                                    Nathan Ivie
                                                                            NAY: Greg Graves
DENIED

2.     AUTHORIZE AN ADDITION TO THE SURVEYOR STAFFING PLAN OF ONE FULL-TIME,
       CAREER SERVICE MAPPING SPECIALIST POSITION (ADDING JOB CODE 3580 TO
       SURVEYOR)
(Track A, 39:25)
Utah County Surveyor Gary Ratcliffe came forward, noting he had promised previous Board members that he would
never stand before the Commission and ask for something that is not needed. Mr. Ratcliffe talked about the two
sides of his office which serve statutory responsibilities, and provided some history demonstrating the staff has not
increased since 1980 when the county population was roughly thirty percent of the current residency. A new
employee is needed for several projects, including catching up on the backlog of section tie sheets, and creating
records of survey plats as required by the state.

(Track A, 49:05)
Commissioner Graves said he appreciates Gary Ratcliffe and what his office does for the county, and stated he is
ready to move forward with this addition since it was approved during the budget process. Commissioner Lee
expressed his concerns about increasing government, and questioned why the work cannot be handled by the county
Information Systems Department. When Commissioner Lee mentioned he would like Human Resources (HR) be
given the chance to measure the metrics of the position, Commissioner Graves replied the proposed change has
already gone through Human Resources. Commissioner Lee countered he had not been given any updated
documents. Commissioner Graves explained the Information Systems portion is a completely different component
from what is needed in this job, noting he is the Portfolio Commissioner for Mr. Ratcliffe’s department.
Commissioner Lee reiterated he would like additional information from HR and Commissioner Graves presented the
paperwork. Commissioner Graves added the function of government is to protect the exact records which the
Surveyor’s Office maintains. Commissioner Graves pointed out HR is devoting a lot of time and attention in
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS

tasks they have not been given the tools to address. The conversation continued in this vein, and Commissioner Ivie
