2. PUBLIC COMMENT
Ms. Barbara Cameron  stated she was one of the original proponents for the Town of 
Brighton because of what can happen when a little extra money goes toward the canyon .  
She was grateful to the County for its past contributions that funded trails and restrooms 
and hoped it would continue to show its support for the canyon.  By becoming a town, 
Brighton is now able to provide additional money, plans, and focus to improve the 
environment, transportation, and visitor experience in the canyon.  She asked that the 
County not carve Brighton out of the recreation area, but instead help it support its two 
million visitors and the stewardship needed for a healthy forest and watershed.  She 
provided a handout to the Council detailing how Brighton spent its money, as well as a 
handout showing photos of wildflowers, which the Big Cottonwood Community Council 
puts together every year for the upcoming wildflower season.    
Mr. Jeff Bossard , Councilman, Town of Brighton, stated he hoped the Council would 
approve the proposal to extend the County ’s contribution to the Unified Fire Service Area 
by 18 months.  Big Cottonwood Canyon gets two million visitors a year, and that canyon 
is a resource for the County.  As Brighton ’s representative, he looked forward to figuring 
out a solution that would satisfy everyone.

3.2 Consideration of a Resolution of the Salt Lake County 
Council Providing Notice that it Intends to Remove the Town 
of Brighton from the Recreation Area Established by Salt 
Lake County Council Resolution No. 5644, and Further 
Intends to Cease Providing County General Fund Dollars for 
Public Safety Services in the Town of Brighton After Fiscal 
Year 2024
23-0529
Attachments: Staff Report
Brighton Public Safety Resolution (1).pdf
Presenter: Councilmember Bradshaw and Chair Winder Newton (Approx. 45 
mins)
Discussion/Direction
Council Member Bradshaw  reviewed the resolution, which he and Council 
Member Winder Newton came up with, providing notice that the Council 
intends to remove the Town of Brighton from the Recreation Area and cease 
providing County General Fund Dollars for public safety services in the 
Town of Brighton after fiscal year 2024.  
He stated the language in the resolution is not meant to be a fully prescriptive 
document of all potential solutions, but rather to make it clear the Council 
intends to end the subsidy at the end of the 18 months, so all affected 
jurisdictions know what to expect.  This is the initial step, not the final step 
for reaching a resolution on this issue.  He noted that every member of the 
Council is committed to public safety in the canyon and would not take 
action that would detrimentally harm public safety within the Town of 
Brighton, but there were complicated issues that played into what the 
County’s role should be now that Brighton is an incorporated town, and 
what was fair to all County residents in terms of general fund expenditures on 
municipal services. 
Council Member Theodore stated she met with members of the Unified 
Page 2 of 12

Fire Service Area (UFSA) and learned the canyon has been funded since the 
1970s because the Legislature felt it was important to fund public safety in 
the canyons since it is a recreation area.  The proposed resolution carves 
Brighton out of the recreation area, which was illogical.  Brighton, with its 
300 residents, could not be responsible for all the visitors who recreate there .  
Pulling any funding from Brighton will impact other cities that are part of 
UFSA, which could have an impact on services provided in the canyon .  
Cutting public safety and reducing services did not make sense.  This issue 
needed further discussion.  
She proposed an alternate resolution, which provides 18 months of funding 
while trying to determine an adequate amount of funding for these services, 
and requires the County to continue working with all stakeholders to provide 
an equitable mechanism to mitigate disproportionate impacts to public safety 
services and the review of public safety needs generated by recreation, as 
well as possible new revenue streams.   
Council Member Alvord asked which entity the $2 million subsidy went to.
Ms. Catherine Kanter , Deputy Mayor of Regional Operations stated that 
went to the Unified Fire Authority (UFA) to provide fire and medical 
services.  The contribution from the County ’s General Fund goes to the 
UFSA, which is a special service district that collects revenues from its 
members.  If the County pulls funding from Brighton, the members of the 
UFSA will have to pick up the additional costs.
Council Member Stewart  stated in 2019, the State Legislature modified 
language for recreation areas to include incorporated areas that have specific 
requirements.  Brighton matches all the requirements the Legislature put forth, 
and so does the Town of Alta, and Butterfield Canyon.  Carving Brighton 
out would set an inappropriate precedence.  He did not know what 
mechanism was used to levy the fees for services; the calculation has not 
been looked at in many years.  
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated the County provided a General Fund 
subsidy for Brighton when it incorporated, even though the bill is derived due 
to the service area within the footprint of the town, not the broader area.  The 
question going forward is who should pick up the bill.  She felt it should be 
the canyon users, not General Fund taxpayers, nor Brighton taxpayers.  The 
most viable option was to figure out, with the Legislature, what amount the 
Page 3 of 12

canyon users should pay.  She wanted to be a partner and advocate for a 
solution.  
Council Member Stewart  suggested working toward a better resolution for 
the next six months to resolve the disagreements.
Council Member Winder Newton  stated the UFA and UFSA would like 
some idea what they need to put forward in their budgets.  She wanted to 
vote on a resolution today.
Council Member Granato  stated the Council sets funding through June, 
but it can move that funding to December or another time.  The cutoff date 
for what the UFA needed to set its budget was not set in stone in June.
Mr. Mitchell Park , Legal Counsel, Council Office, stated for the first six 
months of the year, the County decided its contribution would be provided 
in a manner consistent with past years, and that the Council would revisit this 
in its June budget to determine what, if any, contribution would be 
appropriate for the following six months of the year.  That decision needed 
to be made by the end of June because that was when the County had its 
budget hearing.  At that time, the Council would have the availability to add 
additional funds for the rest of 2023 if it wanted to.
Council Member Theodore, seconded by Council Member Stewart, moved to 
approve the resolution of the Salt Lake County Council providing notice of its 
intent to remove the Town of Brighton from the Recreation Area established by 
Salt Lake County Council Resolution No. 5644, and its intent to cease 
providing County General funds to mitigate the disproportionate impacts on 
statutorily eligible public safety services caused by county residents recreating 
within the town of Brighton after January 1, 2025.  The motion failed 3 to 6 by 
the following votes:

Council Member Bradshaw stated when attorneys drafted his and Council Member 
Winder Newton ’s resolution, it made the most sense to remove Brighton from the

recreation zone if the County was going to cease its General Fund subsidy.  Clearly 
Brighton is a recreation area, but “recreation area” in the resolution is a legal designation 
of a geographic area for this specific purpose.  If anyone was hung up on that language, 
he could strike that language around recreation zone and just say that the funding was 
going away.   
Council Member Alvord  stated that language was a hangup for him. The recreation zone 
is a very useful zone as recreation areas get high tourism traffic.  He would like to extend 
the conversation to consider adding Butterfield Canyon and the Town of Alta to the 
recreation zone.  He would also like to work out a funding mechanism with the Legislature 
to fund the recreation area, such as exploring using Transient Room Tax funds or some 
other funding source.  
Council Member Stringham  stated for her it came down to County policy.  At the time 
the recreation area was set up, only entities the County had land use authority over were 
included in the recreation area, even though others met the requirements of the law.  The 
County’s policy is at odds with what is occurring now.  The policy needed to be clear on 
what should be included in the recreation area, and who should pay for countywide 
services in all of the canyons, not just Brighton.  Another concern was the amount of the 
County’s contribution, which was a flat, agreed -upon amount, with no justification for that 
amount.  She did not know where that number came from, but it was never vetted .  
Additionally, the General Fund is not the appropriate fund for that to come out of .  
Anything paid for out of the General Fund had to meet criteria.  She would like all of that 
clarified before passing a resolution.   
Council Member Bradshaw stated the resolution does not preclude anyone from 
discussing the concerns, nor is it counter to finding solutions.   He would like to pass a 
resolution today to provide some direction to the UFSA.
Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member Winder Newton, moved to 
approve the resolution providing notice that the Council intends to remove the Town of 
Brighton from the recreation area established by Salt Lake County Council Resolution No . 
5644, and further intends to cease providing County General Fund dollars for public safety 
services in the Town of Brighton after fiscal year 2024.  The motion failed 4 to 5 by the 
following votes:

A motion was made by Council Member Winder Newton to approve the following provisions 
in the resolution: 1) The Council, desiring to be a good partner and to provide adequate time 
for local policy and budget decisions, hereby provides notice to the Town of Brighton and 
the Unified Fire Service Area that the Council intends to remove the Town of Brighton from 
the recreational area created by Salt Lake County Council Resolution No. 5644, and further 
intends to cease contributing County general funds for the provision of public safety services 
within the Town of Brighton. The Council intends to effectuate these changes following the 
close of the County ’s 2024 fiscal year, for the period beginning January 1, 2025. 2) The 
Council desires to see the Town of Brighton and the Unified Fire Service Area, before asking 
the County for any further contribution to subsidize a public safety funding gap in the Town 
of Brighton, take action to reduce such gap, potentially including but not limited to the 
following: redrawing municipal boundaries; maximizing currently available revenue streams 
such as resort sales and use tax, municipal energy sales and use tax, and general property tax; 
and seeking new revenue streams from the Utah State Legislature in the 2024 legislative 
session.  The motion failed due to the lack of a second.
A motion was made by Council Member Theodore to continue funding Brighton for the rest 
of the year, and to work with all the stakeholders to come up with a mechanism and a 
solution for Brighton and the UFSA.  The motion failed due to the lack of a second.

3.4 Informational Presentation on the Reorganization of the 
Public Works Operations Division an Enterprise Fund
23-0509
Attachments: Staff Report
PW Reorg Ops. Council Briefing Memo 05.09.2399
PWOps Reclassification 05.09.2023
PW ReOrg Appendix 05.02.2023
Presenter: Lisa Hartman, Associate Deputy Mayor. Crystal Hulbert, Division 
Director of Public Works Operations. Sharon Roux, Division Director of 
Human Resources (Approx. 10 mins)
Informational
Ms. Lisa Hartman , Associate Deputy Mayor, stated back in July, the 
Mayor and Council gave the Public Works & Municipal Services Department 
direction to evaluate Public Works Operations holistically, as it had been 
having issues with retention and hiring employees, which made it difficult to 
meet contractual obligations.  Public Works Operations is an Enterprise 
Fund, with four clients - the cities of Taylorsville, Holladay, and Millcreek, 
and the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD), with its 
communities.  Public Works in collaboration with the Human Resources 
Division evaluated Public Works Operations, and they are recommending the 
following: reclassifying 67 out of the 106 positions in Public Works 
Operations; potentially increasing salaries for 87 of those positions; and 
Page 7 of 12
Council Work Session Meeting Minutes May 9, 2023
accommodating salary increases in the contracts with the municipalities, 
starting July 1, 2023, so there would not be a general fund impact, and all the 
contract agencies supported this.
Ms. Chrystal Hulbert , Director, Public Works Operations, delivered a 
PowerPoint presentation on the Public Works Reorganization, including 
reclassifying positions and salary comparisons, reviewing why it needed to 
do this; reclassifying job descriptions for 66 positions and modifying 
descriptions for 17 positions; reclassifying positions for road maintenance 
workers levels I -IV; a Vactor Certification; a salary comparison; the flow 
chart; and an excerpt from draft job descriptions.
Council Member Stewart  asked why this was not part of the Greater Salt 
Lake Municipal Services District.   
Ms. Catherine Kanter , Deputy Mayor of Regional Operations, stated the 
interlocal agreement with the MSD does contemplate that it could take on 
Public Works, but it would be an extraordinary operation to have to move 
that.  Last year, a group studied just moving Public Works Engineering to the 
MSD, but that was not economically viable for either the MSD or the 
County, partly due to some overlap between Public Works Engineering and 
Flood Control, which the County is statutorily required to provide.

Council Member Stringham  asked if there was a plan to still expand the 
Utah State Hospital.

behavioral health specialists, and the Utah Department of Health and Human 
Services will be formed to evaluate that.  Dallas Earnshaw, Director, Utah

provide quality of life and care for people who could not integrate back into

Council Member Granato  stated she assumed a certain percentage of 
long-term patients are at the Utah State Hospital through adjudication or 
came from jails or prisons.  
Ms. Bridget Romano , Deputy District Attorney, stated that is correct.  The 
individuals Mr. Whalen mentioned would never be able to meet the Olmstead 
standard because their needs are so acute, so their only benefit is to be in a 
long-term placement.  The Utah State Hospital does not provide long -term 
care.  Building a long -term facility would satisfy a critical need both in terms 
of that population and availing the County of more civil and forensic beds, 
which would help it on the jail side as well as the mental health side.
