 F.1. PRESENTATION OF THE 3RD QUARTER 2019 FINANCIAL REPORT
      Scott Parke, County Comptroller, presented the highlights, which included that the county had budgeted 5%
      for sales taxes which currently are at 4.8%, had estimated property tax growth between 3-3½%, and they are
      right where expected. The county had spent about 60% of its budget through the third quarter, which is typical.
      Expenditures for the Culture Parks & Recreation Fund are also right where expected. The revenues are slightly
      higher in the Municipal Services Fund primarily due to some additional road charges to cities, and expenses
      are right where expected for this time of year. Higher tonnage has been coming into the Transfer Station, thus
      revenues are slightly higher than normal, expenditures are slightly higher, and the margins are right where they
      should be. Expenditures are where expected for the Garage and revenues are slightly lower due to incomplete
      tracking of the time that vehicles were being repaired but adjustments have been made to charge for all of the
      time spent repairing them. Mr. Parke responded to Chair Jenkins’ question that at this point he can see nothing
      that the county should be doing differently.

    6. AGREEMENT WITH THE FOLLOWING FOR LEGAL REPRESENTATION TO INDIGENT DEFENDANTS IN A
       PATTERN OF UNLAWFUL ACTIVITY CASE: CLIFFORD VENABLE AND GREGORY FERBRACHE.

         Bryan Baron, Deputy County Attorney, stated that this item, together with #8 and #9, arise from the same
         situation. The County Attorney’s Office is prosecuting five defendants with pattern of unlawful activity
         and as a result of the five defendants, all the witnesses, and the sheer volume of evidence in this case the
         trial is scheduled for six weeks. This is a big burden for the County Attorney’s Office and an even bigger
         burden for the public defenders. This case would take up five of the six full contracted attorneys for 6-8
         weeks and those attorneys said that they would not be able to handle this on top of their regular calendars.
         Mr. Baron said that one discussed option was for the county to hire attorneys to come in and help out with
         this case but it came with a half million dollar price tag. He and Commissioner Harvey worked on a grant
         application to the State’s Indigent Defense Commission to receive some funding. They denied the initial
         request and asked the county to modify it; they did not want to fund just one case but to have ongoing
         relationships with counties to track cases, etc. The revised application requested hiring two attorneys to
         handle difficult cases and these patterns of unlawful activity cases and was approved for $270,000 to hire
         two attorneys. This grant money only goes through the end of June and reapplication will have to be made.
