   1.3. Presentation and discussion pertaining to the development of the Butterfield Canyon Trail
        Head and trails improvements located generally at 8101 W U-111 Highway – Salt Lake
        County/Madeline Francisco-Galang
Madeline Francisco-Galang and Dustin Wyberg, from Salt Lake County provided an overview of the
Butterfield, Rose and Yellow fork Canyons open space master plan and trails. Twelve miles of new
multi-use non-motorized, single-track trails were planned within upper and lower Butterfield Canyon.
Parking locations for events and everyday use were identified. Phase one of construction was
scheduled to begin summer of 2022, with completion anticipated around Spring 2023. The completion
timeline was dependent upon greenbelt taxes. They would work with the Olympia developers to
identify safe trail crossing locations. At least two events were anticipated per year and, if necessary,
a parking study would be completed. Community Development Director Blake Thomas added the City
and County would work closely to provide adequate staggered parking locations for daily and event
usage.

    1.4. Discussion of possible text amendment for signs in the M-1 and C-2 zones
Planning Manager Spencer provided the concept and size of a possible sign for Rockwell Landing and
requested input from the Commission regarding the best way to move forward with a sign text
amendment. Currently, the City sign ordinance limited planned center signs in the C-2 Zone to a
maximum of sixteen feet high and 200 square feet. The M-1 Zone signs were limited to six feet high
and sixty-four square feet. The applicant was preparing for future development in both zones and
applied to increase the sign height and size to forty feet and 560 square feet. The sites for future
development were largely along Mountain View Corridor, Porter Rockwell and Redwood Road.
Recently, the City approved a sign for the Academy Village commercial center, which allowed up to
forty-five feet above the grade of Mountain View and up to 500 square feet. The Commissioners
stressed the importance of signs fitting both with current and future frontage road configurations of
Mountain View Corridor (MVC).

Applicants Braiden and Kim Rindlisbacher identified locations with similar signs around the state. They
were in favor of large signs with many placards for identification of onsite businesses. Commissioners
asked if tenants had the option to advertise on the scrolling sign. Applicant Kim Rindlisbacher
responded affirmatively. Commissioner Jacobson was not a fan of the massive wall of labels and
expressed concern they would be difficult to read from moving vehicles. Commissioner Ferguson
agreed. He proposed one large active sign scrolling the business names like the Mountain View Village
sign. Commissioner Ferguson identified locations with similar signs. Chair Berbert agreed a sign was
needed, was it better to have a bunch of small signs, or one larger active sign rotating. Commissioner
Jacobson offered the opinion most shoppers see the name on the business itself and don’t look at the
large sign near the road with lots of little hard to read placards. Chair Berbert agreed. Commissioner
Sickles questioned who the target audience was. Chair Berbert was in favor of approving the sign
height. Commissioner Jacobson agreed.

Planning Manager Spencer mentioned there was no requirement to apply the sign change to an entire
zone. The Commission could recommend approval of signs within an overlay zone near MVC.

        3.1 Request to Approve the Preliminary Plat of Academy Village Pod 39 Located Generally
                at Real Vista Drive and Mountain View Corridor in the C-2 (Commercial) Zone
         Applicant: Alex Dahlstrom (authorized agent)
         File: S2022-010
Planning Manager Spencer reviewed the simple amended plat request near the Real SL Center. He
identified the subject location and provided the plat overview. The applicant proposed the creation of
17 commercial plots and the vacation of the existing subdivision. The C-2 zone had no minimum lot size
or width, all lots were sized between .42 and 2.73 acres, and must also comply with the South Hills
MDA. He identified lot F needed additional street frontage. The Commission had the option of
removing the lot from the final plat or allowing the applicant to work with staff to resolve the issue.

Applicant Alex Dahlstrom approached the podium and stated they would either combine lot F with lots
G or E to create a larger parcel or work with staff to provide a cross access agreement. Commissioner
Powell requested utility easement clarification. The applicant responded it would remain in place as it
followed the road. Commissioner Sickles asked how lot O was accessed. Planning Manager Spencer
replied access was from MVC, and applicant Dahlstrom stated they would work with staff to acquire
any needed easements. Engineer Petersen confirmed all street layouts and utilities were already
approved.

        4.1 Request: Consideration to Amend 10-16 of the City Ordinance to Allow Internal
                Accessory Dwelling Units as a Permitted Use in the R-2 Zones
         Applicant: Jason Brown
         File: Z2022-025
Planner I Talia Wright reviewed the Utah State Legislature adoption of House Bill (HB) 0082, regarding
single family housing modifications. It’s adoption during the 2021 Legislative Session allowed Internal
Accessory Dwelling Units (I-ADUs) in the R-1 zones. In October of 2021, the City Council adopted
changes to the Herriman City Code to be consistent with the changes made in the State. One of these
changes included allowing internal accessory dwelling (I-ADU) units to be a permitted use in the R-1
Residential Zones. The applicant has requested to allow I-ADUs in the R-2-10 Zone. As staff has
reviewed this request, it seems beneficial to consider the R-2-15 Zone, too. These zones consist of low
to medium density and a mix of single-family dwellings and multi-family complexes such as
townhomes. Only single-family homes that meet the other requirements listed in section 10-29-51 of
the Herriman City Code would be eligible to have an I-ADU. Current standards in the code restrict I-
ADUs on lots 6,000 square feet or less and have other requirements such as requirement of an extra
parking space. These restrictions naturally filter out lots that would not be appropriate for an ADU. Of
4,500 homes about 1200 would be eligible. Single family must have 4 spots (2 in garage and 2 in
driveway) one extra would need to be added to accommodate the I-ADU.
