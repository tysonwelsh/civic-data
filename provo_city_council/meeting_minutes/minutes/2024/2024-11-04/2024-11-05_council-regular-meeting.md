Public Comment 0:08:56

Chair MacKay read the public comment preamble and opened the public comment period.

Joey Franklin, of Provo, shared his positive experience with the special use permit policy for Accessory
Dwelling Units (ADUs) in Provo. He recently used the policy to build an ADU in a zone where it would
typically not be allowed. The process was smooth, involving meetings with neighbors to discuss the
impact on the neighborhood, securing their approval, and complying with building codes. He
emphasized that the legal and transparent process led to a successful outcome, with a family now living
in the ADU, contributing positively to the neighborhood. Mr. Franklin expressed strong support for the
special use permits, highlighting the benefits of legal ADUs over illegal ones, and advocating for a
process that includes neighbor engagement and ensures safety and compliance.

Melanie McCoard, of Provo, expressed frustration with the inadequate staff briefing provided at the
recent work session, which she felt was misleading and incomplete. She criticized the presenter for
failing to provide a full history of the issue, which left the council lacking essential information. She also
pointed out that the briefing did not address past mistakes made by previous councils and
administrations, potentially leading to the repetition of those errors. Additionally, she was upset that
the consequences of these past decisions were downplayed or ignored, which she felt misled the council
into thinking that only positive outcomes had resulted from previous actions. Ms. McCoard voiced her
discontent by initially drafting a press release but later opted to address her concerns through email,
urging the council to read her statement and avoid similar issues in the future.

Action Agenda

1.         A resolution approving the transfer of $220,000 from the General CIP Fund to the Legacy
           CIP fund, and the associated appropriation of $220,000 in the Legacy CIP Fund to pay for
           Fire Station #1 construction costs. (24-084) 0:13:46

     Motion:       An implied motion to approve Resolution 2024-49, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Chief Headman, Fire Chief, presented. He discussed the progress and funding needs for Fire Station One.
He highlighted that the station, located downtown, has been without fire apparatus for over 20 years.
The new station will provide a fifth fire apparatus and a paramedic ambulance, enhancing the city's
emergency response capabilities. The project team delivered an economical building designed to last up
to 50 years. However, challenges like redesign costs, underground issues, and supply chain delays
increased expenses. He requested an additional $220,000 in fire impact fees to complete the project,
bringing the total cost to $1.5 million. The project has been funded without using the general fund,
relying instead on impact fees and federal funds. The station is expected to open in December, with an
open house planned for December 7.

Councilor Whipple asked about the asphalt work done by Public Works, specifically whether the city
only covered the material costs. She inquired how much it would have cost a private entity to remove
the asphalt.

Chief Headman responded that he was not sure how much a private entity would charge, as they did not
receive a bill for the work. However, he emphasized that Public Works provided significant assistance by
doing half of the work for free. The city only had to pay for the asphalt and the labor to lay it down. He
expressed appreciation for Public Works' support, describing it as a great partnership.

Councilor Garrett asked for a reminder of the overall budget for the project and inquired about the
contingency amount, assuming it had already been used up.

Chief Headman explained that the initial contingency was just shy of $294,000. After the additional
$220,000 requested today, the total contingency would be less than 8% of the overall project cost.

Chair MacKay confirmed that usually the contingency of a project is 10% but this project started at 5%.

With no other questions, Chair MacKay opened the public comment period.

Melanie McCoard, of Provo, asked for assistance in connecting to the ProvoGuest Wi-Fi network.

Tana Hoover, of Provo, expressed concern that the new fire station does not include a proposed gym for
officers, which may not be sufficient to accommodate the increased number of officers using the current
facility. She asked if funding would be allocated to improve the current accommodation and whether
officers would have access to local recreation facilities.

2.         A public hearing for consolidated plan priorities. (24-091) 0:23:18

Melissa McNalley, Community Grant Administrator, explained that the purpose of the public hearing
was to gather input from residents as the city prepares to create the Consolidated Plan. This five-year
plan, required by the Department of Housing and Urban Development, is designed to help direct priority
funding for the CDBG and HOME programs.

Chair MacKay mentioned this was thoroughly discussed during the work session. She opened the public
comment period. With none, she invited a council discussion.

Councilor Whipple emphasized the importance of the work in distributing funds to the community and

information and share their feedback on funding priorities by emailing the council or providing input.
While the council has a sense of how to prioritize these opportunities, she welcomed the perspectives of
the community.

Redevelopment Agency of Provo

3.         A resolution of the Redevelopment Agency of Provo City designating a Survey Area for the
           Proposed City Center Community Reinvestment Project Area. (24-082) 0:25:46

Keith Morey, Economic and Redevelopment Division Director, presented. He discussed the potential
expansion of a downtown project area, initially focused on City Hall, to include more areas for future
development and infrastructure updates. The project area grew to encompass surrounding areas like
Center Street, First South, University Avenue, and even First North, in anticipation of potential
improvements and redevelopment opportunities. He emphasized that the map presented is not set in
stone and could be adjusted. The expansion also considered future needs, like utility upgrades and
traffic flow changes. While he is open to adjustments, he urged that key areas, like Center Street and the
old City Hall block, be preserved in the project boundary to accommodate potential future
redevelopment.

Board Member Hoban asked for clarification on the philosophical reasons for addressing the project
area now, rather than waiting until the need arises. He inquired about the potential downsides of
including an area larger than what is necessary.

Mr. Morey said that his primary concern is the redevelopment of the Old City Hall block. He suggested
that if there are concerns about expanding the project area, focusing on the two blocks around City Hall
would be sufficient for now. He explained that while the developer has not yet identified a funding gap,
future plans may require tools like a parking structure, and having the redevelopment area in place
would help facilitate such improvements, particularly in relation to the Covey Center's impact.

Board Member Hoban acknowledged the reasoning behind including the additional areas in the
redevelopment zone, understanding that it helps initiate discussions, reassures potential developers,
and avoids needing future approvals. However, he expressed concern about the potential downsides of
expanding the area too broadly and questioned if the Board might want to take a more conservative
approach.

Mr. Morey explained that the map’s purpose is to inform the public that the city is evaluating a large
area due to perceived deficiencies, though the specifics of any projects are not yet determined. The goal
is to signal that the city is looking at potential redevelopment opportunities, particularly along areas like
Center Street. He emphasized that this broad analysis is meant to address infrastructure needs and
encourage public input, inviting residents to voice any concerns or ideas now, before specific projects
are defined.

Board Member Christensen expressed strong support for the expanded focus on downtown, highlighting
the area's uniqueness and importance as the "heartbeat" of the city. While he appreciated the broader
approach, he cautioned that expanding the area too much could lead to a loss of focus. He emphasized
the importance of maintaining attention on downtown, which he believes is central to the city's identity.

Chair Whipple asked about the rationale for including the half blocks on the north side of the
redevelopment area, specifically the Discovery Academy building and areas north of Sherwin-Williams
and Fresh Market, including the former Blockbuster location. She sought clarification on the justification
for their inclusion.

Mr. Morey explained that the inclusion of the half blocks, such as the Harmons block, was to consider
potential development opportunities. He noted that the area, particularly where car dealerships have
struggled, has open space that could be revitalized in a meaningful way. The intent was to identify
properties with development potential rather than restricting the focus solely to the immediate right-of-
way. Mr. Morey highlighted challenges faced by the car dealership downtown, noting that its hidden
location limits its visibility and access compared to typical car dealership locations in Utah and
nationwide. While the dealership has made efforts to succeed, its position off main traffic corridors
hinders its potential. He contrasted this with another dealership on 300 South and University Avenue,
which thrives due to its high visibility and traffic flow.

Chair Whipple opened public comment. With none, she closed public comment and invited a motion.

     Motion:       Board Member Christensen made a motion to approve the resolution as written.
                   Board Member Bogdin seconded.

Chair Whipple invited a discussion on the motion. With none, she called for a vote.
