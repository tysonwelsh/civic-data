Washington County Planning Commission Meeting
                             September 24, 2019
The Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting was held on Wednesday, September 24,
2019 in the Council Chambers of the Washington County Administration Building at 197 E.
Tabernacle, St. George, UT. Vice Chairman Doug Solstad opened the meeting at 1:30 p.m. He led
the Pledge of Allegiance and then explained meeting protocol.

Commissioners present: Brandon Anderson, Dan Briggs (Alternate acting as a regular member),
Shayne Durrant, Kevin Jones, Mark Owens, Reed Scow (Alternate), and Doug Solstad.

Excused: Jon Walter.

Staff present: Scott Messel, Community Development Director; Natalie Nelson, Deputy County
Attorney; Doreen Bowers-Irons, Administrative Assistant; Kurt Gardner, Building Official; Todd
Edwards, County Engineer.


1.     PUBLIC HEARING/REVIEW AND DISCUSSION OF COUNTY CODES. Update
and review of County Ordinances: Title 10, Chapter 4: Definitions; and Title 10, Chapter 9:
Planned Development.

Mr. Messel stated at the last meeting we reviewed the draft ordinances. The commission was
uncomfortable moving forward with the changes as they felt they did not have enough time to
review them. The motion was made to table the recommendation to approve the ordinance changes
and a they requested that a second public hearing be held in September. The meeting was
rescheduled and the public hearing was again noticed for this date. He explained that the PD
ordinance was somewhat cumbersome and was reworked to try to streamline it and clean up the
language for approval. The County Commission placed a moratorium on Rural Recreation
Grounds and Facilities until such time as the ordinance could be reworked. He then reviewed the
rules and laws of a moratorium. The Rural Recreation Grounds and Facilities was taken out of the
conditional use process and moved into the PD Zone which, will provide better oversite on that
type of use. He informed the commission that with the moratorium about to expire and the county
needing something in place, the County Commission adopted the Ordinance pertaining to the Rural
Recreation Grounds and Facilities at their September 17, 2019. The documents presented now are
the recommended changes that staff feels comfortable moving forward with. He asked the
commissioners to review the reworked ordinances and provide comments, feedback or suggestions
in order to have better oversight. Also, to provide information and directions for large
developments including commercial and other types of uses. When the commission is comfortable
with the document changes, they should make a recommendation to the County Commission to
approve the suggested ordinance updates.

Commissioner Solstad asked some questions clarifying the changes to the ordinances and where
they are in the process. Ms. Nelson identified a few of the changes. She reported that after the
ordinance was passed, staff met and went over the document and made some corrections which,
was mostly for clarification purposes. This body should review the changes to the code and if they
are comfortable with them, staff will take them back to the county commission for approval.

Vice Chairman Solstad announced Chairman Slade Hughes had arrived and deferred the meeting
to him. Chairman Slade Hughes took over the meeting. He explained the public hearing guidelines.

Chairman Hughes opened the floor to the public hearing.

Jon Richter thanked the commission for their time. He introduced himself as a planner and that he
was representing himself and variety of entity’s throughout Washington County. For the record,
he wanted his concerns noted. In speaking with our counsel and engineers we are concerned about
several of the processes, standards, and overlap between the plans that were presented by this
ordinance. We are especially concerned that some of the changes are overly vague and possibly
illegal. He was advised by counsel to appear today and respectfully request that this item be tabled.
It is our hope to put together a working group of community members who would have a stake
and to discuss their concerns of ordinance changes going forward. The biggest generalization and
concern is that it’s impossible to flow chart this ordinance in anyway so we know what is expected
and when. In reviewing the changes, the standards are difficult to discern and the processes are not
clear what the expected outcomes and procedures should be. He realized the importance of the
ordinance moving forward and voiced appreciation for the efforts of putting something together
as it would be better but respectively requested that the county slow down and allow for a working
group to discuss and make recommendations to allow them to address specific concerns. Those
concerns would then be provided to the county.

Commissioner Owens asked Mr. Richter for specific concerns he had. Mr. Richter replied under
Utah law, staff does not have the authority to reject the proposed plans. They can only return for
the completeness of the application. In several sections of the code, it gives staff the authority to
reject or approve the plan. He pointed out the code sections to which he referenced. The planning
commission can veto the plan without making a positive recommendation. He additionally
provided references of other concerns.

Commissioner Solstad commented that he would like to see the concerns Mr. Richter had.
However, the ordinance was adopted by the county commission because they needed to have
something in place before the moratorium expired. Currently, we are looking at the ordinance
changes to address the changes and concerns. We are not making a motion to approve or adopt an
ordinance today. We are looking at reviewing and making recommendations and amendments to
the recently adopted ordinance.

Additional comments and clarification of the adopted and pending ordinances occurred between
staff and the commission.

Ian Crowe stated he had a couple of points he would like to make. He made reference to the natural
futures of the land and how that is not part of open space. All of our land is natural and wondered
if there is something specific of the futures that would be incorporated into the ordinance.

Commissioner Solstad reported he had seen something that talked about lava flows, steep slopes
and natural washes. There were a few items listed but not necessarily topographies. Mr. Messel
stated they may need to go back in to the ordinance and better define the futures. He spoke about
the challenge Santa Clara had with a development in the lava flows.

Mr. Crowe questioned the reasoning of the two access points. He and the commission discussed
the access points. He also would like to have a work meeting and group to review the ordinances
and suggest any changes.

Richard Kolar reported he was a professional architect and planner. He had been following this
issue at a distance. This ordinance covers a lot of land. First, normally zoning changes implement
general plans. In this case, a zone change occurred without changing the general plan. There should
be some objectives. Changing the rural recreation zone to the planned development zone, there
should be some type of document or map that indicates different objectives and locations
throughout the county. Secondly, as it got moved in, the language of the planned development that
implements rural recreation. Rural Recreation was previously rural areas and that has different
needs in terms of services. Now it gives an assumption of urbanism.

Chairman Hughes closed the floor to the public hearing.

The commission and staff thoroughly reviewed, discussed, asked and answered questions, and
clarified the proposed ordinance updates and changes. The planning commissioners deferred the
changes to staff who will being the item back to the commission at a later date.


Commissioner Solstad MOVED to adjourn the meeting at 3:45 p.m. Commissioner Jones
SECONDED. The meeting then adjourned.
