Reconsideration - Airport General Plan Amendments
          Council Deputy Director Symes explained the amendments to the City's General Plan are related to the recent adoption
of the 2020 Ogden-Hinckley Airport Master Plan. The Master Plan, adopted May 26, 2020, provided direction for future
development of the airport. As part of the master plan development, Administrative staff identified areas of the City's General
Plan that needed to be amended to reflect updated information, including recommendations made in the Master Plan. The
proposed amendments were adopted by the Council on June 23, 2020. Discussion among Council members took place after the
adoption regarding language related to placing the airport tower and tenninal on the local historic register. At the July 7, 2020
regular Council meeting, Council members voted to reconsider the adoption of the amendments and voted to review the language
at a future work session.
          Assistant City Attorney Stratford provided the Council with an overview of the differences between the General Plan and
a zoning ordinance; the General Plan is meant to be a guiding document that is not binding in nature, but the City Council should
be advised against proceeding with a project or land use change that is in conflict with the General Plan. The statement that was
added to the Plan based upon the Council's recent action calls for consideration of placing the terminal building at the Airport,
with its tower, on the national and local register of historic places. This is a suggestion and not required. However, as long as that
language is in the document, if City Administration came to the Council with a recommendation to remove the terminal building
and tower at the Airpmt, that project would need to be evaluated for its consistency with the General Plan. That is one example of
how the General Plan amendments could impact future changes at the Airport.
          Council member Hyer wondered about the benefits of including the statement in the General Plan. Mr. Stratford stated
that some could argue that there is a need to include the tenninal building and tower on national and local historic registers and it
is appropriate for that consideration to be included in the General Plan.
          Community and Economic Development Director Christopulos stated that Administration has not formulated future
development plans for the Airport that could impact the terminal or tower structure; he is willing to accept removal of the
statement from the General Plan, but he is also not opposed to leaving the language in the document.

Proposed Text Amendments -Accessory Apartments in Central Business
District (CBD) Zone
          Ogden City Planning is proposing an amendment that would allow accessory apa11lnents in the Central Business District
(CBD) zones, which include the CED-Intensive zones. An accessory apmilnent is a dwelling unit that is contained in a structure
that was originally designed and is still used as a commercial building. The current CBD and CED-Intensive zoning standards
allow for additional dwelling units to be located above first floor commercial uses but not on the same floor in a single-story
building. The proposed amendment is intended to provide additional flexibility and reuse of existing single-story buildings in the
CBD area.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the intent of the proposed text
amendments. The item being presented for consideration is to allow an accessory apartment in a single-story building in the CBD
Zone of the City; the dwelling unit would be allowed so long as it is located above first floor commercial use and one additional
on-site parking space is provided for each unit. There are existing single-story commercial buildings in the CBD and the CBD
ordinance does not allow for an existing single-story structure to have an accessory apartment incidental to commercial use. The
ordinance amendment would allow options for the reuse of the existing buildings in downtown Ogden. The Planning Commission
evaluated the proposed text amendments for consistency with the General Plan, which provides for horizontal and vertical mixed-
use development in specific areas of the downtown area and for new development to embody sound urban form and respect the
context of Ogden's already built environment. The proposed ordinance amendments would provide for a 24-hour downtown
presence, creation of additional housing options, provide options for reuse of commercial space, and retain architectural character
of the CBD. The General Plan encourages downtown development to build-up rather than out; the ordinance amendment would
still supp01t this for new development projects and require that accessory residential units be located above the commercial first
floor. The Planning Commission ultimately voted eight to one to recommend approval of allowing accessory apartments in a
single-story commercial building.
          Council discussion centered on the number of properties in the CBD that could include an access my apartment if the
proposed ordinance ainendments were approved. Council member White asked if there will be a limit on the number of accessory
apartments that would be allowed. Mr. Montgomery answered no, but indicated that this is a self-limiting issue given that many
commercial spaces would need to be altered significantly in order to comply with residential development regulations and some of
those alterations would be cost prohibitive for property owners.
          Mr. Symes indicated that if there is no opposition to proposal, an action item can be included on the August 18, 2020
agenda.

Proposed Street Vacation -Approximately 3317 South Monroe Boulevard
         Ogden City is proposing the vacation of a portion of Monroe Boulevard at approximately 3317 South. The portion is
located on the T.O. Smith Elementary School site and would assist in the reconstruction of that school. The small portion joins
Monroe Boulevard and Liberty A venue and is not currently developed as a vehicular street. The vacation would eliminate this
portion ofright-of-way from the school site.
         Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the intent of the proposed street
vacation. This request is to vacate approximately 18,128 square feet of the existing right-of-way located at 3317 Monroe
Boulevard. There are currently some portions of Monroe Boulevard that occupy the existing school grounds of the T.O. Smith
Elementary School. The City is requesting those areas to be vacated for the reconstruction ofT. 0. Smith Elementaiy School. The
Planning Commission evaluated compliance with the policies in the General Plan and Zoning Ordinance by considering the
following:

owned property changes, action is taken to declare the property as surplus. Surplus property is then managed by City
Administration, up to and including any detennination to sell the property. Administration is required to sell the property for
market value unless they receive approval from the Council to sell it for less. He would discourage the Council from getting
involved in decisions to sell the property to a certain party or for a certain amount. The decision for the Council is whether the
property still serves a public use. He noted that the School District cannot construct a new building that would cross property
lines; in order for them to rebuilt T.O. Smith Elementary, they must own the property.
          Mr. Symes indicated that a public hearing for the proposed street vacation can be scheduled for September 1, 2020.

Proposed Development Agreement Amendments - 100 West 2nd Street
          Shawn Strong, developer of the townhome project at 100 West 2nd Street, is proposing several amendments to the
Development Agreement for the project. The property was rezoned with a development agreement in February of2019 for the
development of 30 townhomes. A request to amend the Lynn Community Plan and rezone additional property to the west was
denied by the Council in February of 2020. As such, the project remains limited to the original property and to the 30 units
approved with the original Development Agreement. The petitioner is requesting several minor changes to that agreement
including a reduction of required trees per unit, an amended project timeline and an update to the owner name.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the request to amend the
Development Agreement. He presented a map to orient the Council to the location of the property subject to the Development
Agreement. The Agreement was first approved in February of 2019. The developer is seeking an amendment to change the
developer name from Lucky Development, LLC to Parkridge, Incorporated; reduce tree requirements from three to two per unit
(sixty trees); and revise the completion dates from February of2020 and February of2021 for commencement and completion,
respectively to August of202 l and August of2022 for commencement and completion, respectively. Mr. Montgomery presented
a conceptual plan of the project to be constructed on the site and illustrated the proposed tree reduction. He noted the Planning
Commission voted five to two to recommend approval of the Development Agreement amendments. Those that voted in
opposition did not agree with the request to reduce the number of trees on the site and they were not in favor of the project to
begin with.
          Council member Stephens asked if the Council needed more infonnation regarding the reasons that two Commissioners
voted in opposition to the project. Mr. Montgomery stated that the opposing vote based on not being in favor of the project to
begin with is an attempt for those Commissioners to be consistent with the votes they have taken in the past regarding this project.
He noted that the Council has been presented with all of that infonnation over the course of their multiple reviews and actions on
this project.
          Council member White asked if an action to amend the development will set a precedent for other developments in the
City; she wondered if parties to other development agreements will seek amendments that are favorable to them based upon
Council support of this proposed amendment. Mr. Montgomery stated that the City has amended many different development
agreements for projects in the City based on transfer of ownership. The proposed amendment regarding the number of trees to be
included in the project may be more significant, but staff supported the amendment because it does not impact the density of the
project and may actually be an improvement because the number of trees initially required could not be fit onto the site.
          Discussion centered briefly on the history of the Development Agreement, after which Mr. Montgome1y noted that the
Planning Commission has granted final plat approval for the project, contingent upon final approval of the Development
Agreement amendment.
          Council member White asked if the City's Urban Forester has reviewed the proposed amendment and offered his opinion
regarding the number of trees to be included on the site. Applicant Shawn Strong stated that he has met with the Urban Forester on
site and he approved the tree plan and supports the proposed placement of the trees. Chair Choberka inquired as to the number of
trees on the site. Mr. Montgomery stated that he is unsure, but noted that the existing trees will be removed before the
development commences. There was brief discussion about the other planting materials included in the landscape plan, with
Council member White requesting that the developer consider water-wise planting methods. She also asked that a written
recommendation from the City's Urban Forester be presented before final action on the amendment.
