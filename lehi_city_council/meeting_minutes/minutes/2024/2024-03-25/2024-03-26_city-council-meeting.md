Pre Council, 5:30 p.m.
1. Welcome and Opening Comment
Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present except for
Councilor Hancock is excused. Councilor Albrecht gave the opening comment.

2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Project overview and update on the Mountain View Corridor- Andrew Jordan, UDOT.
Andrew Jordan updated the Council on progress with the Mountainview Corridor project which
will include bridge construction and trail system development. He inquired about the Council’s
preferences for aesthetic improvements, noting that $100,000 has been allocated for the project.
He displayed options for bridge designs which included functionality and visual appeal. The
Council emphasized a commitment to improving infrastructure while considering budgetary
constraints.

2.2 Fiber Network Update- Shaye Ruitenbeek, Lehi Fiber.
Mrs. Ruitenbeek presented an update to the Council which included promising statistics,
including preregistration is up 40% and the take rate is at 28% with over 1000 total
subscriptions. She also expressed disappointment that the Affordable Connectivity program has
ceased accepting new enrollments and will only offer discounts until April, unless circumstances
change.

2.3 Discussion of Pioneer Village overlay- Kim Struthers, Community Development.
Mike West, Lehi Community Development, presented and led a discussion regarding plans for
the Pioneer Village overlay zone currently designated in southwest Lehi. He explained urban

7. Consideration of Concept approval for the updated Skyridge Subdivision Concept Plan,
   a 5-lot residential development located at approximately 260 East 2750 North.
   Brett Ventura, the applicant, was seeking to downscale to five lots and remove park strips to
   better fit the surrounding area. He also asked for an exception to the connectivity standard
   that requires a pedestrian access to the school. He also requested an exception to sidewalk
   installation. Reno Mahe also spoke for the applicant regarding the pedestrian access
   exception.

    Mr. Struthers stated that the access to the school is important for connectivity, and it reduces
    driving traffic to and from the school; however, they do need permission from the school
    allowing the access.

    There was confusion regarding the school district’s preference for the access. Two different
    school authorities offered differing preferences. Mr. Struthers stated that if the school district
    wants the access, then it should be a requirement, but if the school district wants to close off
    that access then that’s within their rights, and granting a connectivity exception would be
    justified.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to approve the Concept for the updated Skyridge
                Subdivision Concept Plan, a 5-lot residential development located at approximately
                260 East 2750 North; granting the exception to the park strip, the sidewalk and the
                connectivity standards. Councilor Albrecht seconded the motion.

14. Consideration of Ordinance #11-2024 a Development Code Amendment to Chapter 11,
    Application Requirements, to include all common and limited common areas in condo
    and townhome projects as public utility easements to allow access for
    fiber/telecommunications.
    Shaye Ruitenbeek, Lehi Fiber Network Manager, explained that this amendment to the
    development code would define all common and limited common areas within condominium
    and townhome projects as public utility easements for the fiber network and would be
    applied to any new developments. She stated that this was aimed at facilitating access for
    telecommunications installation while ensuring other utilities were accounted for.

    Councilor Stallings expressed concerns about easements potentially limiting property owners'
    rights. She emphasized the importance of mutual agreement and property owner consent. She
    was also concerned with designating all common areas as public utility easements without
    proper justification and declaring private property as public utility easements without proper
    compensation or agreement.

    Mayor Johnson explained that the subdivision plat, which designates easements, acts as an
    agreement between the city and developer, and is signed by all parties including property
    owners.

    Ryan Wood, City Attorney, stated that the process of the city acquiring an easement in a new
    subdivision is the same. Easements are a requirement of development. He stated that what
    differentiates the fiber easement from others is that it is declaring a broader area.

    Motion:     Councilor Albrecht moved to approve Ordinance #11-2024 a Development Code
                Amendment to Chapter 11, Application Requirements, to include all common and
                limited common areas in condo and townhome projects as public utility easements
                to allow access for fiber/telecommunications; and include the change in language
                from “drive” to “access points and alleyways”; and include all Development
                Review Committee Comments. Councilor Newall seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Hancock,
    Absent; Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, No. The motion passed with 3 in
    favor, 1 opposed, and 1 absent.

    Councilor Stallings requested that her reasoning be added to the minutes. She stated that she
    has concerns that this is a constitutional takings issue because the city is declaring private
