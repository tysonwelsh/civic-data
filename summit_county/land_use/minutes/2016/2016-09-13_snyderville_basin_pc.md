Engineer Horton said they discussed if an aerial photographic survey would improve

Engineer Horton said the next question was about the geo-tech reports.  In his 
experience, most geo-technical engineers will provide a report validating only a specific 
location where a hole was drilled.  What is important is what happens on site during 
construction.  He related an experience where a drilled hole indicated the land was 
good, but part of the location had an underground spring.  This would not have been 
discovered unless multiple holes had been drilled.  The potential to add additional 
drillings would not give him any more comfort than what he has at this time.   
 
Engineer Horton said one of the solutions they talked about was requiring the 
Engineering Staff to be on site during excavation to verify that the land is suitable.  If 
not, the applicant would propose a plan that Staff would review.  If this plan wasn’t 
acceptable, Staff would work with the applicant.  If needed, a third party would give a 
recommendation of what should be done.  Commissioner Klingenstein said this same 
plan was used by Engineering with the Apex slope issue.
