14            Resolution No. 25-36 – Approving the Non-Reciprocal Utilities Transactions for
15            Fiscal Year 2025-2026
16
17   Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, explained that the item on the agenda was a
18   routine, state-required approval for a non-reciprocal transfer involving the City’s Enterprise
19   Fund, specifically the water fund. While this action shows activity between City funds, no actual
20   money changes hands. It simply reflects that the City has chosen not to charge itself for internal
21   water use—such as watering parks or other municipal properties. Because of that decision, the
22   state requires public notice and Council approval.
23
24   Mr. Geer clarified for residents that even though no money is being transferred, water use is still
25   being tracked, monitored, and reported. The City maintains oversight of consumption through
26   regular reports and system checks to ensure responsible use and conservation efforts are
27   upheld. This accounting step is not a loophole or a way to avoid tracking—it’s a technical
28   compliance matter to satisfy state reporting rules.
29
30   Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker called for public comments;
31
32   Jill Bateman emailed the Council expressing concern over the City not billing its own
33   departments for culinary water and said this might reduce the incentive to conserve.

26            TransJordan Landfill Discussion
27
28   Councilmember McDougal briefed the Council on the resolution to approve Herriman City as a
29   full member of the Trans-Jordan Landfill. Currently, there are seven member cities, including
30   Riverton, and all must approve the same interlocal agreement for Herriman’s inclusion.
31   Herriman is presently using the landfill but paying the higher commercial (gate) rate of $41 per
32   ton, compared to the $26 per ton that member cities pay. As part of the proposed agreement,
33   Herriman will continue paying the $41 rate, with the $15 per ton difference credited toward
34   their membership buy-in over 15 years. Additionally, Herriman will pay $400,000 annually for
35   the next four years toward their $44 million share of the landfill’s estimated value. Allowing
36   Herriman to join now would not only secure a growing revenue source but also help
37   strengthen the long-term fiscal health of the landfill. He emphasized that other landfill options
38   exist, and losing Herriman could significantly impact Trans-Jordan financially.
