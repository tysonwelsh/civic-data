Item 1: A resolution approving the appropriation of $65,057 in the general fund for a
contractual sales tax increment post-performance payment to East Bay Shopping Center.
(25-045) 0:07:19

Melissa McNalley, Redevelopment Agency Director, presented the request for an appropriation
of $65,057 to fund a post-performance sales tax increment reimbursement to East Bay Shopping
Center. She explained that in June 2018, the Council approved an agreement with East Bay
Shopping Center to provide this reimbursement in support of remodeling and reconstructing the
Ross store, a project intended to help stimulate activity among other retailers in the shopping
center. The agreement outlines a target reimbursement total of $1,154,385 over a ten-year period.
This request represents the fifth annual payment under that agreement, bringing the cumulative
amount reimbursed to $306,155.

Item 2: An ordinance amending the zone map classification of real property, generally
located at 2601 West 280 South, from the Agricultural (A1.1) zone to the Agricultural with
Agritourism Overlay (A1.1(AT)) zone. Provo Bay Neighborhood. (PLRZ20230196) 0:09:05

Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented the proposed zone change, explaining that the
matter dates back several years when the Council passed the Agritourism Overlay Zone to
facilitate the development of an event center on the property. Ardmore stated that the applicant
has now satisfied the conditions required under the overlay and is seeking final approval.

Ardmore displayed the property's location near an elementary school and indicated that the
proposed event center would occupy approximately one-third of the parcel, with the remainder
allocated to parking and open space. He noted that the Planning Commission had reviewed the
request and issued a recommendation for approval. The item was scheduled for formal
consideration during the Council Meeting later that evening.

Councilor Bogdin voiced concerns regarding the condition of existing structures on the site, and
inquired whether they would be removed. Ardmore responded that he was unsure about the
future of those accessory buildings but confirmed that none were located within the distance that
would conflict with the event center.

Councilor MacKay questioned whether all buildings on the property would be required to meet
code compliance if the rezoning were approved. Ardmore clarified that the current application
does not involve review of the other existing structures.

Councilors expressed collective concern for public safety due to the state of the structures,
particularly given the property's proximity to an elementary school. They discussed the
possibility of tying approval to remediation of those concerns. City Attorney Brian Jones advised
that if the zoning change were approved, the owner would have legal rights to the permitted uses
in that zone, regardless of any informal commitments.

Councilor Whipple observed inconsistencies between the aerial imagery and the submitted site
plan regarding which buildings currently exist on the property. Ardmore acknowledged the
discrepancy and stated that full information on which structures would remain was not yet
available.

The Council further discussed enforcement difficulties and the property owner's past compliance
issues. They debated whether to proceed with the approval or consider additional conditions to
address ongoing concerns.

Item 3: A presentation regarding a proposed public infrastructure district (PID) for Slate
Canyon development with Lennar Homes. (25-016) 0:20:40

Betsy Fowler Russon and colleagues from WBA presented on the proposed Public Infrastructure
District (PID) for the Slate Canyon development. Russon explained that PIDs are independent
governmental entities authorized to use capital financing tools to fund public improvements. She




Provo City Municipal Council Work Meeting –March 25, 2025                                Page 2 of 8
emphasized that any debt incurred by a PID is not an obligation of the City and that the City
Council serves as the gatekeeper in deciding whether to approve the formation of a PID.

Russon outlined several advantages of using a PID, such as enabling the timely construction of
public amenities without affecting the City's credit or financial obligations. She reviewed the
application vetting process, which includes evaluating the developer's performance history,
alignment with the City's plans, and financial reliability.

Lennar Homes, the developer for the proposed Slate Canyon project, was introduced as one of
the largest homebuilders in Utah with a track record of successfully implementing hundreds of
PIDs nationwide. The proposed PID would help finance public infrastructure with estimated
costs of $4.7 million. Bond proceeds would range from $1–2 million, subject to a $5 million cap.

Councilors asked questions concerning the PID’s financial structure, tax implications, and the
transparency of associated costs for homebuyers. There was discussion about whether this
financing tool could inadvertently create an equity issue by obscuring long-term financial
commitments.

In response, Russon noted that state law mandates robust disclosure requirements, including a
dedicated, colored document explaining the PID tax as part of the closing paperwork for
homebuyers. She added that this financing structure allows for a lower upfront cost of homes by
spreading infrastructure costs over a 30-year period.

While some councilors raised concerns regarding enforcement and potential misuse, others
acknowledged the PID as a helpful mechanism to advance City goals related to development and
housing affordability.

Russon concluded by stating that the team is seeking to move toward a vote on April 8, 2025,
depending on Council feedback and direction.

Item 4: A discussion regarding the future land use maps for the Station Area Plans.
(25-032) 1:12:37

Hannah Salzl, Planner and Sustainability Coordinator, presented updated future land use maps
related to the Station Area Plans and requested Council feedback. She addressed questions about
the potential impact of upzoning on property values, explaining that pre-zoning typically helps
reduce costs for developers by streamlining the entitlement process.

Councilors discussed several specific areas in the draft maps. For the 2230 North area, questions
were raised about the designation and protection of open space along the river corridor. In the
Joaquin Neighborhood, Councilors debated the proposed increase in density south of 500 North.
Some expressed interest in maintaining existing single-family zoning, while others supported
higher density due to the neighborhood's proximity to Brigham Young University and the
prevalence of rental housing. Salzl noted that only six of the thirty-six parcels in that section of
Joaquin are currently single-family homes.

Item 5: A discussion regarding Reimagining Code Enforcement and Civil Fines (25-011)
1:55:27

Scott Johnson, Zoning Administrator, presented ongoing efforts to improve code enforcement
procedures and implement a system of civil fines. He outlined internal process enhancements,
including more frequent case review meetings, heightened expectations for staff communication
with the public, and improved collaboration with the Legal Department.

Johnson explained that the City is beginning to implement civil fines, particularly targeting
repeat offenders and individuals who fail to comply within reasonable timeframes. He proposed
a new tiered fine structure: $100 per day for days 1–30, $250 per day for days 31–60, and $500
per day after 60 days, with no cap on the maximum fine amount.

Councilors expressed general support for the proposed direction but discussed concerns about
equity, including the ability of low-income residents to pay fines. They also noted potential
enforcement challenges. Council feedback included a desire to explore additional refinements to
the policy, such as creating tiered structures for repeat violations and differentiating between
types of code infractions.

Item 7: A discussion regarding Provo City Code 14.34.450 Elderly Persons – Extra Living
Space (25-040) 2:45:41

Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, and Michael Sanders, Policy Analyst, presented a discussion on
possible amendments to Provo City Code 14.34.450 concerning extra living space for elderly
persons. They explained that the current code permits individuals aged 60 and older to house
additional occupants within their homes in certain residential zones.

Staff reviewed several options to expand eligibility criteria, including the possibility of
incorporating economic hardship or similar considerations. However, they raised legal concerns
about the enforceability of such provisions and the risk of potential misuse.

Following discussion, Councilors agreed not to pursue changes to the qualifying criteria at this
time, citing enforcement difficulties. However, they expressed interest in introducing a
requirement for safety inspections for these units.

Councilor MacKay made a motion to maintain the existing qualifications for extra living space
while adding a requirement for safety inspections.

The motion was seconded by Councilor Bogdin.

The motion passed 7:0.

Item 8: A discussion regarding establishing a nominating commission for city constable.
(25-042) 3:08:38

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, presented a proposal to establish a nominating
commission for the city constable position. He explained that Bob Connor, the current constable
and longtime overseer of operations, is recommending a transition plan due to strategic changes.
The plan involves appointing a new constable from his organization, All Provo Security (APS),
to ensure continuity of service. Connor has proposed Danny Mitchell as his successor.

The proposal prompted significant discussion among Council members regarding the proper
process for selecting a new constable. Some Councilors raised concerns that appointing a
successor from within the existing organization without broader consideration may limit
competition and transparency. They expressed interest in initiating an open request for proposals
(RFP) process that would allow other qualified individuals or organizations to apply, ensuring a
fair and competitive selection.

Mr. Connor addressed the Council and detailed the complexity of the services his company
provides, which include court security, prisoner transport, and law enforcement support for 28
municipalities. He noted that APS employs 60 deputy constables and has developed specialized

expertise, particularly in court security and prisoner transport, that would be difficult for a new
provider to replicate quickly. He emphasized the importance of continuity and voiced concern
that transitioning outside his organization could disrupt services.

Council discussion also considered the logistical implications if the appointment were awarded
to a non-APS constable. Some Councilors debated whether appointing two constables might help
ensure continuity during any potential transition.

Following deliberation, Councilor Whipple made a motion to convene a nominating commission.
The motion was seconded by Councilor Christensen.

The motion passed 6:1, with Councilor Bogdin opposed.

Item 9: A resolution approving a transfer of $1 million from the General Fund to the
insurance and claims fund. (25-043) 3:37:17

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, presented a recommendation to transfer $1
million from the General Fund to the insurance and claims fund. He explained that the City
experienced several significant claims over the past year, which placed considerable strain on the
insurance and claims fund, necessitating additional financial support.

Borget reported that the General Fund currently maintains a healthy balance, with reserves
totaling 32.91% of revenue—near the state-imposed maximum of 35%. The proposed transfer
would reduce the balance to 31.84%, still well above the state’s minimum requirement. He
emphasized that the fund balance is intended to be used in such situations and that this transfer
represents a fiscally responsible action to address unforeseen financial needs.

Borget added that a one-time transfer of this size is equivalent to approximately a 1% decrease in
the overall fund balance, noting that the City would continue to be in a strong fiscal position. The
Administration supports the transfer as a prudent step in maintaining the City’s ability to meet
insurance obligations and future claims.

Item 14: A resolution appropriating $175,000 in the general fund for fire department
needs. (25-044) 5:14:27

Fire Chief Jeremy Headman discussed the urgent operational needs driving the request for an
appropriation of $175,000 from the General Fund. He emphasized that the department continues
to face staffing challenges, falling below national standards, and highlighted the growing need to
train additional Emergency Medical Technicians (EMTs) to the paramedic level due to a limited
pool of available paramedics.

Chief Headman explained that $110,000 of the requested appropriation would be used to cover
the cost of paramedic training, which totals approximately $11,000 per EMT. This investment is
essential for sustaining service levels and maintaining high-quality emergency response
capabilities in the city.

The remaining $65,000 would be used to purchase new turnout gear for firefighters. This
equipment is necessary to meet current safety standards, protect firefighter health, and ensure
readiness for emergency situations.

Chief Headman also referenced the department’s long-term objective of establishing a new
northwest fire station, though the current appropriation is focused solely on immediate, one-time
needs.

Councilors acknowledged the time-sensitive nature of the request and discussed incorporating
specific elements, such as AED and CPR equipment purchases, directly into the resolution rather
than deferring them to the next budget cycle.

Councilor Handley made a motion to include the amounts for the purchase of AED and
CPR devices, as presented in Item 13, in the proposed appropriation.
Second by Councilor MacKay
Passed: 6–0, Councilor Christensen excused

The resolution will be placed on a future Council Meeting agenda for formal approval.
