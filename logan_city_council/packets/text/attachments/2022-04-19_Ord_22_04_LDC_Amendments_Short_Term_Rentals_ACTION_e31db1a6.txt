Ordinance 22-04 Land Development Code Amendments

On March 1, 2022, the Municipal Council held a public hearing on the proposed Land Development Code Amendments (17.08, 17.11, 17.37 & 17.62) governing Short Term Rentals. A final decision was continued to April 19, 2022 to allow time to reach out to the Neighborhood Councils soliciting their input on this issue. We sent out a packet of information (Council's packet & power point) to all Neighborhood Councils with a comment deadline of 4/8/22. The comments that I have received are included with this report. Throughout the Planning Commission and Council processes, there have been a variety of opinions about short term rentals, most of which have been favorable towards permitting short term rentals, but with conditions.

Based on comments received and discussions at the last public hearing, we modified the proposed language in LDC 17.37. Under Subsection B, we added a CUP requirement for all short term rentals in residential zones, established a minimum distance between short term rentals of 500', and placed a citywide limit on the total number of licenses available (3 licenses per 1,000 people). The CUP requirement will notify adjoining property owners of a proposed short term rental and give them the opportunity to bring any issues to the Planning Commission in their decision making process, while the minimum distance between short term rentals and the maximum number of licenses will limit the overall density of within the City. The parking component in Subsection C was updated to clearly state that all parking requirements necessary for a specific short term rental shall be accommodated on site. Also added language about the appearance/character of the residential unit not changing and a limitation on the number of consecutive rental contracts by the same person or groups. The updated LDC Chapter 17.37 is below:

I did not change the occupancy standards, nor did I include a requirement for owner occupancy in residential zones. One of the common themes that we have heard is that the draw of short term rentals is the ability to bring families together under one roof. I think that we can manage occupancy on an individual basis when the City licenses the short term rental based on designated bedrooms, designated sleeping areas, and designated on-site parking areas. We can stipulate on the license, which is required

[page 3]

to be posted, what the maximum permitted occupant load of a specific residential unit at time of licensing. If we receive complaints about over occupancy, and can substantiate the violation, then violations would be used to revoke a person's license.

Ordinance 22-08 Logan Municipal Code Amendments

Included with this packet and listed as a separate workshop item on the Council agenda is the proposed Section 5.15 in the Logan Municipal Code contained in Ordinance 22-08 (Attached). This proposed section deals with the business licensing aspect of short term rentals which is different than the land use aspect covered in the LDC amendments. The reason why this is trailing the LDC amendments considered under Ord. 22-04 is that we couldn't finalize the licensing requirements until we knew the direction of the general land use requirements.

In summary, proposed LMC Section 5.15 establishes the following:

•  An annual business license is required to conduct a short term rental in the City
•  Minimum license application requirements & submittals
•  Licensing fees (Proposed $100.00 new/$50.00 renewal)
•  Annual license renewal requirements
•  Requires a local property manager
•  Requires posting of rental license in unit
•  Limitation on total numbers of short term rental licenses – 3/1,000 population, annual adjustments, waiting lists, sales & transfer of property, new licenses
•  Minimum requirements – meets building codes, CUP required
•  Minimum inspections – one at time of application & as needed
•  Penalties – 3 violations & loss of license

While our concerns still remain regarding the potential use of a short term rental to circumvent long term residential usage and occupancy standards as well as potential impacts to residential neighborhoods, the proposed approach outlined in both Ordinance #22-04 & #22-08 should temper potential problems, provides a clear & legitimate path for property owners who want to use their residential properties for short term rentals, provides an opportunity for public input, and gives the City the ability to revoke licenses if we do find problematic short term rentals.

Any questions, let me know.

ORDINANCE NO.  22-08 (Draft 4/19/22)


AN ORDINANCE AMENDING SECTIONS OF LOGAN MUNICIPAL CODE
REGARDING SHORT TERM RENTALS


        WHEREAS, the state legislature has granted general welfare power to the City Council, independent, apart from, and in addition to, its specific grants of legislative authority, which enables the City of Logan to pass ordinances as are necessary and proper to provide for the safety, promote the prosperity, improve the peace and good order, comfort, and convenience of the city and its inhabitants, and for the protection of property in the city; and

        WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code § 10-1-203, a municipality may license for the purpose of regulation and revenue any business within the limits of the municipality and may regulate that business by ordinance; and

        WHEREAS, the City Council has determined that the following ordinance is in the City's best interest;

        NOW THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE MUNICIPAL COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF LOGAN, UTAH, AS FOLLOWS:


SECTION 1:

                        Chapter 5.15
                        SHORT TERM RENTALS


5.15.010. Definitions.

5.15.050. License Fee.

5.15.010: DEFINITIONS: In addition to the definitions set forth in section 5.02.020 of this title, the following words and phrases used in this chapter shall have the meanings prescribed in this chapter:

[page 18]

SHORT TERM RENTAL: Temporary lodging typically located in a residential dwelling for charge or fee and for a rental period of less than 30 continuous days. A short term rental excludes hotels, motels, or bed & breakfast establishments.

5.15.020: PURPOSE

The purpose of this chapter is to accomplish the following:

A.    To promote the public health, safety and welfare by requiring short-term rental to be safe and fit for human occupancy;

B.    To provide contact information, through licensing, for each short-term rental to allow problems to be resolved expeditiously; and

C.    To provide consistency with licensing of all other businesses in the city by requiring licensing of short-term rentals.

5.15.030: LICENSE REQUIRED

A.    It is unlawful for any person or firm to keep, conduct, operate or maintain a short-term rental within the city without a business license.

B.    A business license for a short-term rental is not transferable. Any owner holding a license shall give written notice within thirty (30) days to the business license official after having transferred or otherwise disposed of legal or equitable control of any short-term rental. Such notice of transferred interest shall include the name, address, and information regarding the person(s) or entity succeeding to the ownership or control thereof. The new owner shall obtain a business license within 30 days of the date of purchase and as required by this Chapter.

5.15.040: LICENSE APPLICATON

An application for a short term rental business license shall conform to the requirements of Section 5.02.050 of this title and shall include the following additional information:

A.    A detailed floor plan drawn to scale with labels on rooms indicating uses or proposed uses;

B.    A detailed site plan identifying location and numbers of off-street parking spaces;

C.    A copy of the Utah State tax license number for remittance of transient lodging taxes;

D.    The name and contact information of the owner, and if applicable, the property manager, residing in the state of Utah, who is authorized by the owner for service of process.

E.    If located in a residential zone, a copy of the Conditional Use Permit issued by Logan City for the short term vacation rental.

5.15.050: LICENSE FEE

A.    The fee for a short-term rental license shall be as set forth in the consolidated fee schedule adopted by the municipal council.

[page 19]

B.    The business license shall be paid annually and shall be due and payable on or before the license expiration date of owner's application for a business license. A license shall be delinquent if paid after said date in the year for which it is due.

5.15.060: LICENSE RENEWAL

A.    A renewal application for a business license shall conform to the requirements of Section 5.02.050 of this title. The owner shall include with their renewal application evidence that minimum Utah State Sales Tax and Transient Taxes were submitted for the prior year.

B.    Failure to renew the business license within 30 days of the license expiration date may result in revocation of the license and subject the property to the license limitations in Section 5.15.090.

5.15.070: LOCAL PROPERTY MANAGER REQUIRED

All short-term rental properties shall have a designated local property manager that resides within one (1) hour travel time of the property. The local property manager shall be available twenty-four (24) hours per day to respond to questions or concerns.

5.15.080: REQUIRED POSTING

The following information must be posted in a clear, concise, and unambiguous manner in a conspicuous location inside all short term rentals:

A.    A copy of the short term rental license.

B.    The name, address, and phone number of the owner and/or property manager.

C.    Emergency exit plan and the location of all fire extinguishers.

D.    A list of all rules applicable to short term rentals.

E.    The maximum occupancy of the short term rental, the maximum number of vehicles allowed, and the location of all parking stalls.

5.15.090: LIMITATION ON TOTAL NUMBER OF SHORT TERM RENTAL LICENSES

The total number of short term rental licenses issued shall be limited in accordance with the following:

A.    The maximum number of short term rental licenses issued citywide shall be proportionate to the total population of the City and is limited to three (3) licenses for every 1,000 of total population.

B.    The total number of licenses available each year shall be re-calculated based on the City's most current population as of July 1st using the American Community Survey (ACS) data, U.S. Census data, or official State of Utah population numbers.

C.    If, after the maximum number of licenses has been issued, an application meeting all other requirements for issuance of a license shall be placed on a waiting list in order of the date of receipt. No fees will be due until a license becomes available. When a license becomes available, the first applicant on the waiting list shall be notified of the license availability, and the applicant shall have 30 days from the date of such notice to file an

[page 20]

updated & complete application, pay the filing fee, and 60 days to complete the licensing process. In the event that the applicant fails to file a complete application and filing fee within 30 days or fails to complete the licensing process within 60 days, the application shall be deemed to have expired, and the next applicant will be notified of the license availability, and the same procedure shall apply.

D.    In the event of a sale or other transfer of any property containing a dwelling licensed as a short term vacation rental, the purchaser or transferee of the property shall be required to apply for a new license within 30 days of the date of purchase. In the event that the purchaser or transferee fails to apply for a new license within said 30 days, the license will be forfeited, and the owner must reapply for any available license or be placed on the waiting list.

5.15.100: MINIMUM REQUIREMENTS

A.    Each short-term rental unit shall meet the zoning and building code requirements for installation, maintenance, and operation in effect at the time the short-term rental unit was legally permitted.

B.    Each sleeping room must meet current international code requirements for egress and be equipped with smoke and CO2 detectors. A fire exiting plan and maximum occupancy number must be posted in each sleeping room.

C.    A Conditional Use Permit shall be issued for all short term rentals in residential zones prior to the issuance of a business license.

5.15.110: INSPECTIONS

Inspections of short-term rental units may be conducted pursuant to Chapter 5.02.090, Logan Municipal Code.

A.    The City or other authorized agency shall conduct a life safety inspection of the short term rental at the time of application for the initial short term rental license. License renewals will not automatically require subsequent re-inspections unless subsection 5.15.110.B necessitates additional re-inspections.

B.    The City or other authorized agency shall be permitted to make an inspection of any short-term rental unit to enforce any part of this title or any other applicable statute or ordinance if the short-term rental unit has a recorded and valid complaint.            Proposed Land Development Code Amendments (Ordinance #22-04)

As some of you may be aware, Logan City is in the process of reviewing proposed amendments to the City's Land Development Code governing Short Term Rentals (vacation rentals/airbnb's). These proposed amendments have been reviewed by the Planning Commission and are now under consideration by the Logan Municipal Council. The Municipal Council held a public hearing on March 1, 2022 but delayed making a decision until the Neighborhood Council's could weigh in on this topic with any comments or concerns. This matter is scheduled for the City Council April 19, 2022 agenda.

Attached with this memo are the minutes from the 3/1/22 Council hearing, the proposed Land Development Code changes, staff reports to the Planning Commission and the City Council, the proposed Ordinance #22-04, and a PowerPoint presentation that highlights the general issues surrounding Short Term Rentals.

Please share and discuss this information with residents in your respective neighborhoods. If you have any comments or concerns that you would forwarded on to the City Council, please send me an email (mike.desimone@loganutah.org) by April 8, 2022 as I will be collating comments and presenting them to the City Council at their April 19th hearing.

Here is a brief overview of the Short Term Rental Issue & Discussion to this point

Our current regulations recognize short term rentals in a category similar to a hotel or motel, and only permit them in commercial zoning districts. We currently have minimal operational, life safety and licensing standards for short term rentals.

There are a number of short term rentals currently operating in residential areas, with most co-existing just fine with their neighbors and having very little impact on surrounding residential properties. Current State Law prohibits the City from using listing platforms as a tool for enforcement, so we are forced to rely on complaints to make us aware of problematic short term rentals.

The City recognizes there are both positive as well as negative attributes associated with short term rentals in residential areas. Our primary concerns are how to balance the desire to permit them throughout the City while minimizing any potential neighborhood degradation, preventing long term over-occupancy of residential dwellings, and limiting nuisances such as parking, noise, parties, etc.

The original approach that was presented to the Planning Commission was to only permit short term rentals in the commercial zones and in the multi-family zones. The Planning Commission concluded that the impacts from short term rentals are relatively minor, that there is not a significant demand for short term rentals in Logan thereby total numbers are self-limiting, that nuisance concerns can be adequately managed, and recommended the City Council consider allowing them in all zones.

The operational standards center around licensing requirements, parking requirements, occupancy limitations, and compliance with other City requirements for nuisance, health & safety, animals, signs, etc.

The Planning Commission held a public hearing on the proposed short term rental code amendments on January 27th while the City Council conducted a workshop on February 15th and held a public hearing on March 1st. All of these meetings were publicly noticed. The public input has been a mix of opinions and ideas.

If you have any general questions on this material, please don't hesitate to contact me at (435) 716-9022 or mike.desimone@loganutah.org. And please be aware of the April 8th date for submitting comments that you want included in the packet of information going to the City Council for their April 19th meeting.

Staff recommends that the Planning Commission recommend approval to the Municipal Council of the proposed amendments to Chapters 17.08, 17.11, 17.37 and 17.62 of the Land Development Code.

BACKGROUND
The proposed code amendments define minimum operational standards for Short Term Rentals and what zones they are appropriate to be located in.

What is a short-term rental?
A short-term rental, also referred to as a vacation rental, is a transient rental accommodation, typically within a single family residential dwelling (but not always), that is rented out for a period of time not exceeding 30 days. This short-term occupancy is viewed and regulated differently than a traditional rental (long term occupancy) where the term of rental is longer than 30 days.

The use of short term rentals has grown in popularity in many locations primarily because of their location and draw, although many communities are experiencing a significant pressure on maintaining affordable housing for residents. Logan is an entirely different rental and attraction market than a Moab or a Park City or even Bear Lake, all of which function more as "designations" due to their proximity to National Parks, significant ski areas, or other outdoor recreational opportunities. The demand in Logan City for short term rentals is not that great and has, over the past few years, remained relatively constant. The typical number of short term rentals being advertised in Logan on the larger short term rental platforms (vrbo & Airbnb) averages around 25 at any given time which is in contrast to other jurisdictions having thousands of short term rentals. The Utah State Legislature stepped in a few years ago but failed to provide any real guidance on how a community could, or could not, regulate short term rentals short of legislating that a city could not use the listing platforms as a basis for enforcement.

What are the issues associated with short-term rentals (STR)?

Pros:

A short term rental:

• May contribute supplementary financial assistance to homeowners, especially elderly homeowners on fixed incomes, enabling them to stay in their homes longer;

1

• Makes efficient use of space in a home that is otherwise empty or may go unused;
• Enable travelers to find less expensive alternatives to a traditional lodging such as a hotel room;
• Provide a unique "experience" for both host's and guests that may not be available in more traditional forms of lodging (e.g., hosting international guests or guests experiencing a unique residential neighborhood);
• Helps property owners maximize the economic potential of their property by renting out unused bedroom space;
• Provide additional lodging during peak demand events;
• Provide an incentive to keeping properties from being blighted; and
• Contribute more money into the local economy as it is believed individuals staying in STR's end up staying longer and spend more money in the communities in which they stay.

Cons:

A short term rental:

• Can have an adverse impact on neighborhood character as the residential nature of a neighborhood changes from long-term neighbors with a vested interest in their neighborhood to short-term or transient "guests" who don't share the same the long-term interest;
• Can create a number of nuisance problems involving loud music, parties, trash, inconsiderate guests, excessive traffic, inadequate parking, and the commercialization of residential properties for private events;
• May not pay their fair share of local taxes (lodging taxes) nor do they have the same licensing, inspection and accountability as do more traditional forms of transient lodging;
• May be problematic in a "college town" in that they could be viewed as a tool to circumvent student occupancy limitations; and
• Can negatively impact the availability and affordability of the housing stock by removing available residential units from the long-term housing market and driving up the cost of existing units as residential values become more commercialized due to the potential revenue.

What do other communities do?
The approach to short term rentals is varied. A large number of jurisdictions have not adopted any specific regulations for short term rentals, and as a general rule, prohibit them in the residential zones as they are treated as transient lodging. However, because most jurisdictions operate on a complaint driven code enforcement system, there are probably more short-term rentals operating throughout residential neighborhoods than most jurisdictions realize.

Some jurisdictions permit a short-term rental where the owner/occupant just rents out a spare bedroom of their house while others permit the rental of an entire dwelling without the owner present. Other cities limit the concentration or total number of short-term rentals citywide. For example, the City of Hurricane (UT) permits short term rentals with a limitation that one short term rental cannot be located within 300' of another short-term rental. In their residential zones, they will only license three (3) short term rentals per 1,000 of population limiting the total number citywide. Park City permits short-term rentals in most of their zones as a permitted or conditional use. Other areas such as Moab have banned them from residential zones altogether as they drive up the price of housing making it unaffordable for many residents to live in certain neighborhoods or communities.

Staff's position on prohibiting short term rentals in single family residential areas is consistent with City policy on trying to maintain and protect the integrity of single-family neighborhoods and with the adopted neighborhood plans. We believe that permitting short term rentals in single family residential areas is not beneficial for the existing residents or for the neighborhood as a whole. As discussed during the Accessory Dwelling Units code amendment process, many of the core neighborhoods have seen a dramatic decline in owner occupancy while the remaining residents deal with the problems and issues associated with long term rentals near a major university (noise, parking, occupancy). Short term rentals could potentially lead to additional degradation and increased problems with parking, occupancy, noise, etc., and could negatively impact Logan's housing market as new home buyers are competing with investors for limited housing stock.
