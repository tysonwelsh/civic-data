Presentations

         Transit Fresh Look Study Presentation

Ted Knowlton, Deputy Director of Wasatch Front Regional, Carlton Christensen, Chair of Utah
Transit Authority, and Lauren Victor, WFRC Transit Fresh Look Project Manager attended the
work session to present the “Transit Fresh Look” Study.

Mr. Knowlton provided an overview of the "Transit Fresh Look" initiative and discussed its focus
on regionally significant transit connections, such as bus rapid transit and potential rail
improvements. This project is part of a broader effort to address both long-term and short-term
transit needs in the region.

The "Transit Fresh Look" initiative is distinct from other transit planning efforts, as it emphasizes
regional connections. However, Mr. Knowlton explained that this is only one aspect of the transit
planning process. UTA is also working on a five-year service plan, which includes more
immediate improvements to local bus routes, specifically in Riverton, with plans for
implementation around 2025. Two specific routes, Route 126 and Route 218, were discussed
as being in development. These routes are expected to connect to key areas and provide bi-
directional service with the possibility of increasing service frequency based on demand.

Mr. Knowlton noted that these local transit services are not included in the regional
transportation plan but are essential for addressing current transit needs. UTA has made
progress in staffing, which allows for the expansion of services that were previously suspended
due to shortages. He emphasized that public input is still being sought, with further opportunities
for feedback in the coming months before final decisions are made by the local advisory council
and UTA board later in the year.


Discussion Items

         Traffic Calming Discussion

Mayor Staggs explained that in 2018, a resolution was passed prohibiting speed bumps in the
City, but there's been a significant influx of requests from residents asking either to bring them
back or to explore alternative solutions. One such alternative is the implementation of speed
dips, which might not require a change to the existing resolution or ordinance. City staff has
been gathering information on the costs associated with these measures, and it has been
estimated that installing a speed dip would cost around $5,000, while a properly constructed
concrete speed bump would cost about $8,000.

Given the challenges associated with snow plows and the maintenance of speed bumps, the
staff's preference seems to be leaning towards speed dips as a more practical solution. This
topic is expected to be further discussed in upcoming meetings, especially as the City continues

Mayor/Council Reports

Councilmember Haymond said that several residents had expressed concerns about the state
of their yards following the installation of secondary water metering. He planned to collaborate
with Public Works to address these concerns.

Councilmember McDougal reported that Trans-Jordan had begun its audit process. He also
provided an update on the landfill's status, confirming that it likely had about five years left
before reaching capacity. The timeline could vary depending on the amount of trash received.
He mentioned that construction was slightly ahead of schedule for the new landfill.

Councilmember McCay said she attended a Broadway kids production at the Sandra Lloyd
Center, noting it was positive to see the new space being utilized. She also toured Riverton
Hospital, which is approaching its 15-year anniversary in the City. She was pleased to learn that
Riverton Hospital leads the nation in the number of robotic surgeries performed and that the
hospital will be acquiring another robotic system, which enhances recovery times and minimizes
invasiveness.

Councilmember Pierucci addressed two issues during the meeting. First, he informed residents
about an earthy or musty smell in tap water caused by increased levels of algae present in Deer
Creek Reservoir. He emphasized that the water is safe to drink despite the odor and mentioned
that the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District is actively educating the public. Secondly, he
expressed gratitude to the Police Department and the Unified Fire Authority (UFA) for their
response to a recent tragedy involving a homicide in his neighborhood.

Councilmember Buroker said she attended the Riverton Library's 25th anniversary celebration,
which took place at the library and the adjacent plaza. She provided an update from the Unified
Fire meeting, mentioning that it was uneventful especially with no major concerns following the
Fourth of July and Pioneer Day (24th of July) celebrations. She also discussed mosquito
abatement efforts, reporting that there have been no cases of West Nile virus detected so far,
and regular testing continues as the season nears its end. Finally, she reminded the Council
about the upcoming "Get to the River" festival scheduled for September 23rd, an event focused
on educating the public about the plants, animals, and activities along the river.

Mayor Staggs provided an update on meetings with the county regarding the golf course, where
plans are underway to submit a third Request for Proposal (RFP) for renovations. The proposed
upgrades include a new clubhouse, a pool, and a reception center similar to facilities at South
Mountain Golf Course, aiming to enhance the amenities on the over 40-year-old property.

Mayor Staggs also noted the recent opening of a signalized intersection at 13200 South, which
now provides improved access for both Riverton and Herriman residents to areas west of
Mountain View, including Costco and Mountain View Village. He said the Green Well project,
which involves installing a reverse osmosis facility near the Jordan River to supply culinary
water to about half the community, has experienced slight delays. The project, largely funded by
Salt Lake County's America Rescue Plan funds, is expected to save the City approximately $1
million in the upcoming budget year by reducing the need to purchase water from the
Conservancy District. The system has been undergoing testing and flushing due to sediment
buildup, with an anticipated resolution soon.

Mayor Staggs also shared a personal highlight of recognizing a centenarian in the community,
Phyllis Madison, who recently turned 102 years old. Lastly, he mentioned attending a
Legislative Policy Committee meeting in preparation for the upcoming legislative session, where
he anticipates addressing growth issues and density concerns raised by the development
community.

         Resolution No. 24-79 – Approving Amendments to the Municipal Fee Schedule for
         the 2024-2025 Fiscal Year

Nick Geer, Administrative Services Director, discussed the proposed increase in the event
security fee to help cover the rising costs of having police officers provide security at events.
Additionally, he mentioned that revisions were made to the dog license section, not only to
address necessary changes but also to streamline and condense outdated language.

Councilmember Pierucci asked for clarification on the vaccine fee. In response, Mr. Geer
explained that the new vaccine requirement fee was designed to simplify the payment process
and cover the costs of vaccinations. Previously, there were multiple incremental fees for
different vaccination stages, which have now been consolidated into a single flat fee. This flat
fee covers approximately $10 more than the original contract price for vaccinations. The cost to
the City varies depending on the type of animal and is based on a contract with a third-party
provider. This fee structure aims to account for the range of costs associated with different

animals, including both high and low ends. However, it does not cover all administrative costs
related to animal control; it primarily reflects the charges from the contract with the third-party
provider.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 24-79 –
Approving Amendments to the Municipal Fee Schedule for the 2024-2025 Fiscal Year.
Councilmember Buroker SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the

Councilmember McCay expressed concerns about the intake fee rising from $65 to $100. She
made a suggestion to increase the initial intake fee to $75 instead, with subsequent intakes set
at $100.

Councilmember McDougal and Haymond expressed support for the proposed fee changes,
emphasizing the importance of fiscal responsibility for pet owners. It was noted that the increase
in the intake fee helps address the costs associated with animal control and licensing.

Councilmember Pierucci proposed postponing the decision on the animal intake fee increase to
the next council meeting. He requested additional data on the impact of the fee change,
including how many residents it affects, to make a more informed decision. He expressed
willingness to proceed with other fee changes but emphasized the need for more information
specifically on the intake fee increase before making a final decision.

Approving Amendments to the Municipal Fee Schedule for the 2024-2025 Fiscal Year with

September 3, 2024. Councilmember Pierucci SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for
discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows:
Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed
