 1           A ceremony recognizing Chief Troy Beebe as Utah Chiefs of Police Association's Chief of the
             year (26-007) 00:01:34

Mayor Judkins recognized Troy Beebe for being named the 2026 Large Department Chief of the Year by
the Utah Chiefs of Police Association. She noted that the award had been presented at the Association’s
conference in St. George and expressed the City’s desire to also recognize him publicly at the Council
meeting. Mayor Judkins highlighted Chief Beebe’s leadership, including efforts to improve department
staffing, support the language incentive program, and advance innovation and modernization within the
department. She also acknowledged his work in strengthening the statewide law enforcement radio
system, collaborating with agencies across Utah to apprehend a dangerous fugitive, and expanding
community partnerships. Under his leadership, the Provo Police Department has enhanced community
policing, improved response times, and strengthened public trust. The Mayor further noted Chief
Beebe’s hands-on leadership style and willingness to address challenges of all kinds, sharing an example
of his direct involvement in resolving a public safety concern along the Provo River Trail. She concluded
by expressing appreciation on behalf of the City and Council and congratulated Chief Beebe on the well-
deserved recognition.

 Public Comment

Chair MacKay read the public comment preamble and opened the public comment period.

Angie Carter, East Neighborhood District Chair, spoke regarding a proposal discussed during the
Council’s work session related to a potential conservation easement for Slate Canyon. She reminded the
Council of strong community interest in preserving the canyon, referencing significant public turnout at
a neighborhood meeting the previous year when development of the area had been proposed. Ms.
Carter expressed that residents value Slate Canyon for its natural beauty, recreational opportunities,
and its role as a gateway to the Bonneville Shoreline Trail. She noted that the area already provides
amenities such as dirt bike trails and a disc golf course, and that it has long been identified in the City’s
General Plan as a future park. While acknowledging that the conservation easement was not part of the
formal agenda, she urged the Council to give it careful consideration as a means of preserving the
canyon for future generations. She emphasized the importance of maintaining access to natural spaces
and the positive impact such environments have on residents’ well-being.

Taudine Andrew, a Provo resident and owner of Curvature, a plus-size thrift store, invited the Council to
attend an upcoming ribbon cutting ceremony celebrating the expansion of her business. She noted that
the business is growing and has recently acquired the adjacent property, effectively doubling its size.
Ms. Andrew also raised concerns regarding the City’s utility deposit requirements. She explained that
she was asked to pay a $400 deposit to establish utilities for the expanded space, which would be held
for three years. She stated that this timeframe is longer than in many other Utah municipalities, where
deposits are typically held for one year, and suggested the Council consider revisiting this policy to
better support small business growth. Additionally, she noted that despite being an existing business
with a strong payment history, she was initially required to provide a second deposit for the expansion.
While she expressed appreciation that the City ultimately granted a waiver, she suggested that a more
consistent policy for existing businesses could simplify the process for both staff and business owners.
Ms. Andrew indicated she would follow up via email with additional information.

 3           A resolution consenting to the mayor’s appointment of the energy services director for
             Provo City. (26-028) 00:18:22

       Motion:        An implied motion to approve Resolution 2026-8, as currently constituted, has been
                      made by council rule.

Mayor Judkins recommended the appointment of Patricio Hernandez as the City’s Energy Services
Director and requested the Council’s advice and consent. She noted that Mr. Hernandez was selected
following a nationwide search that included 24 applicants and expressed full confidence in his
qualifications. The Mayor highlighted Mr. Hernandez’s prior experience with Provo City as a power
systems control operator, where he monitored the City’s energy distribution system. She also outlined
his subsequent work with Rocky Mountain Power and Heber Light & Power, where he gained additional
technical and managerial experience. Mayor Judkins emphasized Mr. Hernandez’s leadership
background, including roles as an engineer, project manager, and his recognition as an award-winning
safety committee chair with the American Public Power Association. She concluded by recommending
his appointment for the Council’s consideration.

With no council discussion, Chair MacKay called for a vote.

       Vote:          The motion passed 6:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Garrett, Hoban, MacKay, Whipple, and
                      Whitlock in favor. Councilor Christensen excused.

Judge Schreiner administered the oath of office to Director Hernandez.

Hannah Salzl, City Planner and Sustainability Coordinator, presented proposed amendments to the City’s
data center code, which had originally been adopted in September. She explained that, after
implementation and coordination with departments and the Utah Municipal Power Agency, staff
identified updates intended to better protect the City’s power grid, air quality, and water resources. Ms.
Salzl outlined three primary components of the proposed amendments. First, the ordinance would
remove power generation as a permitted use for data centers. She explained that existing requirements
from the power provider would effectively limit facilities to 50 megawatts, which would significantly
reduce potential air quality impacts. Second, the amendments would require non-evaporative cooling
systems. While these systems may increase energy use, they reduce water consumption and would
prohibit discharge into the municipal wastewater system. Third, the proposal would remove certain
third-party environmental analysis requirements due to the reduced environmental impact, while still
maintaining Council discretion in reviewing applications. Ms. Salzl also noted that the Planning
Commission had reviewed the proposal and recommended against fully prohibiting power generation as
a permitted use. Instead, the Commission suggested allowing it as a conditional use with performance-
based standards, recognizing the potential economic benefits of data center development.

Chair MacKay opened public comment. With none, and no council discussion, she called for a vote.

       Vote:          The motion passed 5:1 with Councilors Garrett, Hoban, MacKay, Whipple, and
                      Whitlock in favor. Councilor Bogdin opposed and Councilor Christensen excused.
