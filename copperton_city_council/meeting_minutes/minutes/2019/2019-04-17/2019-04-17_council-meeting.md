Rio Tinto

                Matt Howes, Rio Tinto, stated he needed community feedback regarding the
deterioration of the railroad overpass at the entrance of Copperton. Debris has been falling from
the overpass and is a significant safety concern. There are three options to tackle the problem:

   1) Put a net underneath to catch debris (not recommended and very temporary).
   2) Remove all the concrete and any structure but leave the iron in place.
   3) Completely remove all the overpass but leaving the pillars.

He recognized that many people consider the overpass to be the entrance to Copperton and it
might have sentimental value to the community. The Rio Tinto engineers recommend the full
removal, which is also the safest option. Leaving the metal framework would take the longest
and would require sandblasting. This would cause more inconvenience to the community.

              Council Member Pazell asked if the town could purchase something in its place,
some kind of gateway.

                 Mr. Howes stated Rio Tinto would not want to do that, but if the town wanted to,
that was okay.

                 Council Member Severson stated leaving the iron beams in place would be a
safety hazard.

                 Mr. Howes stated he would need some specifics. Removal would be the quickest.

               Ed Walden, Unified Fire Authority (UFA), stated UFA’s concern has always been
that the overpass will fall on someone.

South Valley Sewer District

                Michael Hayes, Attorney, South Valley Sewer District, stated the state legislature
passed a law that changes the ability to have a representative on the board of a special service
district. Well over 98 percent of Copperton lies within the South Valley Sewer District. There are
a few edges that need to be cleaned up. Once those areas join the district, Copperton would
have the right to appoint a member to the board. It could be the Mayor or a Council Member.

                 Council Member Pazell stated he has met with Rio Tinto is looking at how big an
impact it will be. Copperton has asked Rio Tinto to weigh in prior to any annexation because they
do not want them opposed to an annexation. Rio Tinto will get back to the Council next week.

              Mr. Hayes explained the complications involved in the 2019 board election if
Copperton did not decide on annexation rather quickly.

              Council Member Pazell stated Rio Tinto will weigh in and the board will act at a
special meeting.

               Nathan Bracken stated it was important to have a voice on a public utility that
provides services to Copperton. Annexation could be done by either Rio Tinto filing a petition or
through Metro Township Council resolution. Council resolution would be the easiest option.

           Salt Lake County Animal Services Advisory Council

               Council Member Severson stated the advisory council has had a lot of discussion
about bylaws. There is concern about private entities that provide dog watching services because
people often do not understand the liability of dog bites. The owner of the dog is responsible for
any dog bite, even when the dog is in the care of another. Millcreek is working on an ordinance
that may be a model for other communities. He will put this information in the newsletter.

This is also the time of the year when skunks can become pests. The Division of Wildlife Services
will not do anything with skunks. Instead, it is something Animal Control takes care of by providing
traps. Once a skunk is in the trap, an Animal Control officer will pick it up.

He also noted an Animal Control Officer periodically patrols the park.
