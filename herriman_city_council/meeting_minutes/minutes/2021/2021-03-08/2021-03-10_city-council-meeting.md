    2.2. 2021 Legislative Session Review - Tami Moody, Assistant City Manager, Cody Stromberg,
         Deputy Police Chief and Chris Bleak, Lobbyist
Assistant City Manager Tami Moody explained the 2021 legislative session had several bills proposed which
posed a significant impact on local control and public safety. There were also bills which addressed mental
health, impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic, business assistance, economic development as well as voting
procedures. There was a total of 1,216 bills requested of which 503 bills had been approved.

Assistant City Manager Moody highlighted the sessions overall outcomes including the $23.8 million in ongoing
money for economic development, $39 million for trails, outdoor recreation and parks, over $200 million for
building construction for colleges and universities, $50 million for homelessness and housing, and roughly $1.1
billion for infrastructure.

Lobbyist Chris Bleak touched on HB 244 and HB 433 relating to transportation and infrastructure. HB 244
allocated first class county highway road funds to cities for an annual distribution. This bill proposed $700,000
to Herriman City as well as $1 million to Herriman Boulevard from 6800 West to 7300 West. HB 433 offered
amendments related to infrastructure funding which authorized the issuance of a bond for specified
transportation and transit projects. This would be a significant investment in infrastructure projects.

Deputy Chief of Police Cody Stromberg noted 101 bills presented has a law enforcement or criminal justice
component. He explained they were able to successfully navigate to standardize the baseline standards.

   2.3. Discussion regarding the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District creation of a local
        district - Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn
Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn explained the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District were looking to
change from a special service district to a local district which would save the taxpayers money and provide a
more efficient public process. She asked the Council if they would support the District with the change. The
Council agreed.

    2.5. Bonneville Study Update - Wendy Thomas, Interim City Manager
Interim City Manager Wendy Thomas informed the Council it would cost $21,900 for Bonneville Research to

3. Budget Report
    3.1. Budget Report Discussion - Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae presented the 2021-2022 major revenue projections expected to be available. He
presented property tax projections for the General Fund, Herriman Towne Center, Anthem Business Center,
Herriman Business Center, Herriman City Safety Enforcement Area, and the Herriman City Fire Service Area.
Sales tax increased by 33-percent in the current year as online sales have grown. Finance Director Rae then
moved to the franchise tax projection increases while the building permit revenues would continue to decrease.
