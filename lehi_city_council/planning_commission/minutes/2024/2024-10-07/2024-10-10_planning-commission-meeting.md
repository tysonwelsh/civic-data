3. Regular Agenda

3.1) Public hearing and consideration of Steve Kelly’s request for approval of the Ember
Hotel Concept with an exception for a reduction in the landscape buffer located at 26 East
Main Street.
Brittney Harris presented information on the item. She noted that with the current plan, the south
and west parking do not have the required 10 foot buffer.


The applicant, Steven Kelly, was present. He petitioned for a buffer distance of 0 feet on the west
side of the parking lot to widen the space on the east side of the lot, which would provide a park
use area. This area would ideally keep noise away from residents that are on the west side of the
property. He also asked for a waiver of the 10 foot distance on the south side, as there will be a
six-foot privacy fence.

Acting Chair Everett opened public hearing.

Michelle Hopkinson (also representing her husband Dana) neighbors the property. She expressed
concerns over privacy and safety if the parking buffer is allowed to be 0 feet. She also expressed
concerns about the privacy of hotel clients.

Larry Webb is a neighbor of the property to the west. He pointed out that the driveway doesn’t
have the regulation 20 foot setback and expressed worries over movability in the parking lot and
the possibility of cars blocking the sidewalk. He also expressed concern over shade from the
hotel in the winter preventing snow from melting, and concern that the sewage system isn’t
prepared for what the hotel will produce.

Ron Wall, a Lehi resident, neighbors the property to the West. He expressed worries about
increased traffic along Main Street.

Brada Mayor neighbors the property. She expressed concerns for privacy in her backyard.
Additionally, she mentioned that there would likely be increased concerns about the nearby fire
station and their ability to leave and return with additional traffic.

Tona Wall, a Lehi resident, expressed worries about RVs potentially parking on the street.

Janys Hutchings was not able to make it to the meeting, but submitted comments that expressed
worries about traffic, offsite parking, and garbage. She suggested a right in, right out system for
the parking lot.

Ben Matthews was not able to make it to the meeting but submitted comments expressing that he
supports it as a business endeavor.

Acting Chair Everett closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Eyre clarified that the issue at hand was the parking buffer, and that other
complaints were beyond the scope of the current meeting. Commissioner Lockhart asked for a
clarification of what Kelly was requesting. She also expressed frustration that properties that
were originally zoned residentially no longer have access to residential zoning law now that they
have been rezoned to be mixed-use. Kelly clarified that his main goal was to be granted
permission to have less than a 10 foot buffer for his parking lot, and that he would like concrete
numbers from the Commission as to how much of a buffer he is required to have, so that he can
work them into his next set of plans for presentation.

                Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to grant approval for the concept of the
                Ember Hotel located at 26 East Main Street and deny the exception for reduction
                in the landscape buffer on the south and west sides of the property, with the
                parameters that an exception will be granted for a 0 foot buffer on the east side of
                the property. There will need to be a minimum of 10 feet buffer per code on all
                other sides of the property. Commissioner Lockhart seconded the motion.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, no; Commissioner Lockhart, yes, Commissioner
                Eyre, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes, Commissioner Roberts, yes. The motion
                passed 4-1.

3.2) Public hearing and consideration of Jeff Ackley’s request for conditional use approval
for the conversion of an accessory building into a detached ADU located at 1088 North
Trinnaman Lane.
Brittney Harris presented information on the item. She explained that this type of property would
usually be allowed, but since the house was built after 2021, it is a conditional use situation.

The applicant, Jeff Ackley, was present. He clarified that no special exceptions are being
requested, he just wanted to make sure there wouldn’t be any zoning issues.

Acting Chair Everett opened and closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Roberts asked if the ADU will pass along to the next resident in the case that
Ackley chooses not to remain on the property. Harris explained that if there is a new owner, they
will need to fill out the paperwork again for possession of the ADU.

                Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to approve the conditional use for the
                conversion of an accessory building into a detached ADU located at 1088 North
                Trinnaman Lane. Commissioner Eyre seconded the motion. Commissioner
                Jackson seconded the motion as well.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Lockhart, yes; Commissioner
                Eyre, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes; Commissioner Roberts, yes. The motion
                passed unanimously.

4. City Business

4.1) Continuation of the Work Session from October 3, 2024
Kim Struthers emphasized that this is the solution that the State will hold the city of Lehi to, and
that the discussion should be focused on investigating the proposed solution.

The commission discussed the five-year equity delay and how it will be enforced.

Commissioners Lockhart and Everett both express concerns over CHOZ being an overlay zone,
rather than a selected area to test out the solution.

Commissioner Eyre suggested limiting the size of homes as well as limiting lot size to naturally
drive down prices. He also suggested getting rid of the equity delay entirely.

Kim Struthers suggested looking into enforcement methods before the next meeting.
