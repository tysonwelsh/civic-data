The following are the minutes of the Herriman Planning Commission. The meeting was held on Thursday,
March 4, 2021 at 6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main Street,
Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s
website, and delivered to members of the Council, Commission, media, and interested citizens.

Presiding: Chair Chris Berbert

Commissioners Present In Person: Brody Rypien and Heather Garcia

Ferguson, and Joy Kaseke

Chair Berbert called the meeting to order at 6:04 p.m.


Community Development Director Blake Thomas reported two key Council decisions. The Academy Village
VMU overlay zone for signs was approved with Planning Commission recommendations. The Reserve at Sky
Ranch general plan amendment was approved with Commission recommendations. The rezone was also
approved with a change from one acre to quarter acres, allowing two lots per acre. Eighty percent of the
western lots must be half-acre in size.

The Rosecrest MDA and PUD amendment were reviewed, and changes were being made to the parcels. The
Council and Staff were analyzing the impact Olympia Hills would have on Herriman. So far, the discussion has
been focused on roadways.


Planning Manager Clinton Spencer offered to answer questions for the Valhalla and SLCC subdivisions.
Commissioner Palmer asked for information regarding the ownership of lots as discussed in the email sent to
Commissioners from staff. City Attorney Andrizzi informed the Commission the property in question was
removed from the development. The developer has owner affidavits for the property included in the discussion
tonight. Commissioners requested for City Attorney Andrizzi to state the issue had been resolved during the
regular meeting. Commissioners questioned the total land used to calculate the density bonus for the Valhalla
subdivision. Planning Manager Clint Spencer stated the descriptions were pulled from county records and the
exact calculations were included in the presentation. The bonus density was not available to the A-1 zone.
Commissioner Jacobson does not understand how they received so much density. Installing improvements
was a required part of the development and should not provide bonus density points. He questioned how the
ten-foot park strip benefitted the community outside of the development. Community Development Director
Blake responded if continued all the way to the south on the steep road it provided an extra safety buffer for
pedestrians. The location of the extra property was between the sidewalk and the precast wall and he identified
the larger lot locations. Commissioner Jacobson stated they were not enough for density bonus as they were
just transitional zone buffering. Planning Manager Spencer stated the Ordinance did not specifically state the
required number of lots bordering an agricultural zone. Commissioner Jacobson did not think their locations
provided buffering. One was next to Knapper Ridge Cove which was not bigger. They do not meet the intent
of transitioning to larger lots. Community Development Thomas reviewed staff interpretation of the code and
stated the Planning Commission makes the final decision for bonus density calculations. All stormwater
detention was onsite. The non-reimbursable offsite improvement was an offsite road that was not completed
in 2012 at the corner of 14300 South and Solemn Way. Commissioner Jacobson argued completion of it should
have been required. Community Development Director Thomas offered they could have run Solemn Way
through with no intersection. Commissioners were concerned with the lack of connectivity to the land west of
the subdivision. Commissioner Jacobson does not see that as a system improvement or betterment for the
community. Full improvements need to be stubbed to the property line and has been done in other
developments without bonus density. Community Development Director Thomas replied the Ordinance may
not be strong enough to require the road connectivity. Commissioners thought the Ordinance should be
redone to limit further subdivision and the bonus density tables needed to be more specific. Commissioners
discussed previous offsite improvements. Commissioners stressed the importance of identifying specific
instructions when making a motion. Commissioners reviewed in detail the bonus density point items and
calculations and stated some proposed by staff should not qualify. The Commissioners were encouraged to
make changes as they see fit. City Engineer Bowers explained the maximum slope allowed on City streets was
ten percent. This developer submitted a deviation for consideration of a twelve percent slope from UFA. Public
works would have to approve also. The staff would continue to work with them regarding the slope.
Community Development Director Thomas stated the Parkside development installed fire doors through the
breezeways. Per building code, they were not allowed. The City worked with the developer, the doors were
removed, and the openings were enlarged.


Planning Manager Clinton Spencer reviewed staff reports and options for making a motion. Commissioners
felt the current packet outline and layout were useful. Items should not be on an agenda if they were not ready,
and problems should only be included with recommendations for mitigation.

Planning Manager Clinton Spencer reviewed the history of the application for 35 single-family lots in the Valhalla
Subdivision. Thirty-one of the proposed lots were in the A-.25 zone, which accounted for 12.5 acres of the
project. The other four proposed lots were in the A-1ZC zone and account for the additional five acres. Part of
those lots were within the Hillside Overlay zone. The applicant recently rezoned a portion of the property within
the proposed subdivision to the A-1ZC zone with the condition that only four lots could be approved in that
area.

Engineering issues were still being addressed to determine if the drainage provided on lots 1, 16 and 30 would
be adequate. If not, drainage may need to be added. Access for the existing home at the intersection of Solomon
way and 14300 South needed to be established to provide entry to the side entry garage and provide access to
the detention pond. No retention walls or buildings would be allowed on the hillside overlay zone with slope
greater than thirty percent. The City had no issues with the buildable area proposed by the applicant. A section
of the area designated open space by the General Plan had been rezoned A-1 and was limited to four lots total.
Three of the four lots proposed in the A-1 zone do not meet the width requirement. They either need to be
changed to 100 feet wide, to comply with the standard, or the appeals authority may approve a variance. Other
details to be addressed by the applicant include providing a tree planting plan, working with the owner to
determine access to lot 17 and maintenance of the cul-de-sac configuration. He explained the Commission shall
determine whether the applicant has complied with necessary design components and shall determine the
density, per City Ordinance.

Community Development Director Blake Thomas explained the density criteria table was used to calculate the
bonus density, and to request a density greater than baseline of 1.8 in A-.25. Bonus density .2 credit was acquired
by installing roadway improvements, off-site on the north side of 14300 South, which was outside of the
requirement for this project. Additional .2 bonus density was due to the developer paying fee in lieu which would
provide landscape improvements to the offsite Shoshone detention pond. Additionally, the applicant provided
onsite storm water drainage and three landscaped, onsite detention ponds. Additional 0.05 bonus density was
acquired by combining two or more parcels into one project. Another 0.1 was due to 10% of the subdivision
being half acre lots. Providing larger lots adjacent to larger parcels added another 0.1 density bonus, per
Ordinance. Dedicating the ten-foot park strip behind the sidewalk adjacent to 6600 West accounted for another
0.1 bonus density. Future developments along 6600 West would have the same opportunity to add bonus density
in the same manner, if zoning requirements were met. Additional width would be moved from lots 18 and 19 to
lot 20, to make it 85 feet. With these calculations the applicant exceeded the requirement for 2.5 maximum
allowable density.

Planning Manager Clint Spencer explained a note on the recorded plat would be required to state the applicant
met criteria to reach the approved density, and no additional subdividing of lots would be allowed. He displayed
the proposed conditions of approval. City Attorney Chase Andrizzi explained ownership of the property was
reviewed with each application. For affected property not owned by the applicant affidavits were required to
allow the applicant to act as an agent for the property owners. He stated this applicant has provided the necessary
affidavits for all property in this development.

City Engineer Jonathan Bowers stated the standard maximum slope allowed for roadways was ten percent. The
proposed plan contained a five-hundred-foot portion with a twelve percent slope. Any deviations would
normally be discussed during the engineering phase review. Lowering the road slope to ten percent may be
needed. Further Engineering and Public Works department review may allow the twelve percent slope with
additional safety measures and possibly retaining walls.

Applicant James Horsley with Riverside Development approached the podium. The cul-de-sac was added, based
on previous Commission recommendations. They will accommodate the required building envelopes, and they
plan to apply for a variance for frontage on lots 18, 19 and 20 and will comply with the decision. They would
continue to work with the owners of the existing homes to ensure access. The tree planting plan will be provided.
He expressed appreciation to the Commission for the feedback they provided four months ago. They will work
with Engineering regarding the slope. He requested to address issues after the public hearing.
