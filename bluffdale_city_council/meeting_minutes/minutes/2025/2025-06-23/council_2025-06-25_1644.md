5.     Public Comment.


Chris Harding identified himself as the Salt Lake County Auditor. He is visiting cities and towns
to discuss property taxes. He passed out a signup sheet for e-notifications, which will include an
electronic version ofthe Notice of Valuation. The notice includes the property valuation as well
as information on the school district, town, county, abatement district, and other taxing entities'
property taxes, whether they will stay the same or increase. The paper version will have a QR
code to the electronic version with hyperlinks to more detailed information regarding the reason
for any increases. That will help residents be better informed on the issues discussed at the Truth
in Taxation meeting. They are also working to implement an appeals tracker. If a property owner
files an appeal to their property taxes,they sometimes have to wait weeks or months for a response.
The County is working on a system to notify appellants ofeach step ofthe process to better provide
them with information. The notice will also include a reminder to please report any fraud, waste,
or abuse in the County. They recently had a report about a facility that was writing off family
expenses. Because an employee reported it, the County was able to take action.

Finance Director, Stephanie Thayer, presented the Amended Consolidated Fee Schedule and
reported that it is reviewed by the City Council annually. Changes to the schedule were then
reviewed. She reported that Arena User Fees were adjusted based on rates for neighboring cities,
and language was added to specify that the arena is for equestrian use only. Fees for use of arena
lights were also added.


Pavilion User Fees were amended to be based on the specific parks and their amenities. Fees are
broken down by resident and non-resident, and pavilions may be rented by the day or half day.
The deposit was also removed. Mr. Reid added that the City does not reserve all its pavilions. For
example, the large pavilion near the playgroimd in Day Ranch Park is not available for rental. A
cleaning deposit was previously required for the pavilions that are rented, but historically, the City
has never charged a cleaning fee and always returned the full deposit. The requirement was
removed to save time and paperwork.


The Stormwater Maintenance Fee will be increased by $1.50 per month to help cover infrastructure
replacement costs. Garbage Collection Fees were increased by Waste Management, and that

amount was being passed through to users. Water Fees were raised due to an increase by the
Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District. Engineering Fees were added for Subdivision
Inspections, Unscheduled Quality Acceptance, and Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan
("SWPPP")Violations.


In response to a question from Mayor Pro Tem Aston, City Attorney, Fred Donaldson, provided
background on the requirement for SWPPP Violations. In 2024,the State noticed that fees charged
by counties, municipalities, and other SWPPP enforcement agencies varied considerably. In
response, they disallowed all fees. In 2025, they specified that the enforcement agencies are
allowed to charge for violations. The fees included in the schedule were taken directly from Utah
State Code.


Mr. Reid stated that the most common SWPPP violation is from construction vehicles tracking
mud onto roadways, which then enters the City's storm drains. Ifthose violations are not mitigated
with the use oftrack-out material, a fine is assessed to cover cleanup costs.

                Fiscal Year Budget for all Funds except the Fire and Police Protection Fund.

Mayor Pro Tem Aston reported that the public hearings for the LBA,RDA,and Fire and Police
Protection Fund would be held in conjunction.


Ms. Thayer reviewed the final Budget Adjustment for the fiscal year ending June 30,2025. There
will be a $2 million contribution to the Capital Projects Fimd, which is a standard end-of-year
distribution to fund future capital projects. The number is a placeholder. Staff will not know the
exact amount until August,and it is important that the amount be between 5% and 35% as required
by State law.


Assistant City Manager, Bruce Kartchner, reported that the budgeted amounts for expenses are
maximums that cannot be exceeded. If there is not enough money budgeted for this line item, it
cannot be transferred out of the General Fund. It can be challenging to understand because the
entire amount will likely not be transferred,but they need to ensure that the number is large enough
to meet the required target.

Ms. Thayer explained that revenues are conservatively forecasted one year in advance, and the
exact amounts are not known until the funds are received. For example. Building Permit revenues
were $125,000 higher than budgeted, which offsets some increased expenditures.               Small
adjustments were made to other funds, including recalculation of administrative charges in the
RDA and LBA. The Fire and Police Protection Fund includes a reallocation of$150,000 in salaries
and wages from the part-time to full-time employee line items.
