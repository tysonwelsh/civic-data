       a.     HOME OCCUPATION, LAURA WATSON IS REQUESTING A
              CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR A HOME OCCUPATION FOR “DAZZLING
              ACCESSORIES” TO BE OPERATED FROM HER HOME ON 12866 SOUTH
              MAPLE SPRINGS ROAD.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the subject property
is located in the Cottages of Western Springs, in an R-4 SD Zone. 10,000 square-foot lot is
the R-4 zone and the SD takes them down to about 7,000 square-foot lots. The frontage on
the lots is 61 to 62 feet, which is about 20 feet less than what a typical R-4 is and 30 feet
less than R-3.

Mr. Prestwich reported that Dazzling Accessories sells items such as jewelry, home décor,
clothing accessories, and auction pallet items. The applicant purchases pallets of
merchandise that are returns from online vendors or freight that is undeliverable. She brings
the pallets home, breaks them down, and lists individual items for sale on the internet.
People come to the home to pick the items up. She has no employees and five or fewer
customers come and go each day and arrive at different times. Parking is available in the
three-car driveway and business is usually conducted in the garage or on the front porch of
the home. Mr. Prestwich reported that the ordinance specifies that the applicant needs to
provide off-street parking, but if the customers chose not to use it, it cannot be enforced on
a public street.

Mr. Prestwich reported that several comments were received from the public and included
in the Commission packet. The comments included concerns regarding the narrow streets
and the number of cars that come and go from the site. Another concern was whether the
surge in crime in the neighborhood could be attributed to people coming and going from the
business from outside the neighborhood.

Mr. Prestwich reported that staff felt that the home occupation could qualify and meet the
fixed standards. He spoke with the neighbors and informed them tonight’s meeting was not
a punishment or a judgment for what the applicant has done in the past. If the applicant can
demonstrate that they can potentially meet the ordinance, they are entitled to approval if the
Commission finds that they are capable of following the ordinance. Staff recommended
approval subject to the conditions outlined in the staff report.

Mr. Prestwich referenced condition number four and reported that the applicant indicated
that she could operate her business with no more than five visitors per day, however, the
ordinance allows up to eight. Given that that was one of the neighbors’ concerns and that
the frontage is narrower, staff included it as a condition.

Commissioner Russell asked about the restriction on the business hours. Mr. Prestwich
explained that they would be 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m., which is defined as one of the
qualifications. The Commission could alter the hours to 9:00 p.m. or restrict it if they can
attribute it to the impact on the neighborhood.

Commissioner James noticed a discrepancy between the application and some of the letters
received. He asked if the Commission was aware that alcohol is being sold. Mr. Prestwich
stated that he had spoken to the applicant about that and explained that it is not allowed by
the Home Occupation ordinance or by the Division of Alcohol. If it happened in the past it
cannot happen in the future as it would violate State Code.

Commissioner James stated that one of the letters received expressed concern regarding
the garage door being left open. In one case, the claim was that it was open for two days
before being closed. His understanding of the ordinance was that all activity in a home
occupation has to be conducted within the structure. Mr. Prestwich stated that the Planning
Commission can approve something outside.

Commissioner Rushton asked if there is an ordinance regarding yard sales. Mr. Prestwich
stated that it is part of the Home Occupation Ordinance as an exception. He explained that
a yard sale can take place four days in any given year.

The applicant, Laura Watson, from Dazzling Accessories, reported that since being notified
on October 29, she tracked all of her pickups over a 15-day period. She did not change how
she operates her business but documented the times, what was sold, and the number of
pickups. During the 15-day period, there have been 22 pickups, with the latest being at 7:15
p.m. and the earliest pickup was at 9:47 a.m. Ms. Watson was informed that she could do
business until 8:00 p.m. Those wishing to meet after hours would be met outside of the
neighborhood.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that short-term rentals were discussed
last fall before a recommendation was made to the City Council. The Legal Department
worked on an ordinance that was presented to the Planning Commission, a few changes
were made, and ultimately the City Council approved an ordinance. This is the first
application that has been submitted and is a conditional use. A copy of the ordinance was
provided to the Commission Members. The property is located on the west side of the City

near 3600 West in the Summerwood Subdivision, where 40% of the lots are required to be
one-third acre or greater.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the conditional use is licensed as a business, which differs from
an ADU. The request can be approved by staff if the ordinance requirements are met. The
owner must live in the home 185 nights per year. Rentals are limited to 150 nights per year.
The request was similar to an Airbnb model. Rentals are limited to one rental agreement at
a time with up to eight people who cannot stay longer than 29 days. If the property is greater
than one-third acre, the back yard must be fenced. The new ordinance requires the use of
off-street parking. The applicant lives in the home and will be renting out the basement on
Airbnb-type of business.

Mr. Prestwich indicated that because it is a conditional use, the matter was noticed to
neighbors living within 300 feet. The process was described. One of the requirements is
that the applicant provide proof of compliance with existing CC&Rs associated with the
property. Enforcement and legal issues were discussed. The applicant indicated that there
are no CC&Rs and no HOA. Mr. Prestwich spoke to two neighbors about the request and
they did not mention an HOA or CC&Rs.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the applicant, Justin Whitney, has a large driveway that will
accommodate at least three cars in addition to a three-car garage. Two notices were sent
to the neighbors. The first announced the public hearing and the second indicated that
public comments would be not taken but invited written comments. Two phone calls were
received from neighbors with questions but no one expressed objection.

Mr. Whitney reported that he works from home and will be present when renters are on site.
His personal vehicles will be parked in the garage and there will be plenty of parking on-site
for guests. In response to a question raised by Commissioner James, Mr. Whitney indicated
that he had read the recently adopted addendum to the ordinance and he is in compliance
with the requirements.

Commissioner Hansen urged Mr. Whitney to be courteous to his neighbors because if there
are a significant number of complaints, the permit could be revoked. Mr. Whitney stated that
he is close with his neighbors and they have known his intent for months and have been
supportive.

Commissioner Rushton questioned how the City will determine 150 nights per year.
Mr. Prestwich stated that it will be dictated by neighbor complaints. The applicant must
provide an information sheet to all neighbors within 300 feet describing the activity that will
take place on-site and provide his phone number if there are issues. There is also a
provision that specifies that if there is a noise violation, the rental contract must be canceled.
If there are more than three violations within any 12-month period, the business license will
be revoked.

             TO ITS ACCESSORY STRUCTURE ORDINANCE ADDRESSING SQUARE
             FOOTAGE REQUIREMENTS.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the staff report and stated that the proposed text change pertains
to the maximum allowed size for accessory structures. There are two standards in the
ordinance that include the following:

       1.     Individual structures can exceed two times the footprint of the house; and

       2.     The total amount of accessory structure square footage cannot exceed 25%
              of the rear yard.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the ordinance states that the rear yard area is measured from
the closest point of the home to the rear property line. The challenge is that there are several
different interpretations. It is also a very challenging measurement for most people to make.

motion. The motion passed with the unanimous consent of the Commission.

5.     DISCUSSION ITEMS

       a.     PARKING STANDARDS IN MULTI-FAMILY ZONES.

Mr. Prestwich reported that staff was asked to investigate parking standards in multi-family
developments. Over the past three to five years there has been an increasing number of
multi-family developments. In some areas, the parking has worked out while it has not in
others. Feedback was invited from the Commission.

Mr. Prestwich first addressed the Carden Square Subdivision located on the south end of
the City. The project met the parking ordinance at the time, however, it is an area where
parking has been an issue. Guest parking had also been problematic. Staff suggested that
a specific standard be set. Several Commission members suggested the standard be 1.2
parking spaces per unit. Grant Lefgren commented that it should depend on whether the
road is public or private. A public road that is wide enough for people to park on tends to
alleviate problems. One option beyond a metric is to have the road have enough width to
accommodate on-street parking. Commissioner Lefgren stated that a two-car garage with
space for two cars in front is generally sufficient and alleviates the need for guest parking.
A Commission Member disagreed and stated that garages are often used for storage rather
than for the parking of cars, which forces parking onto the street.

It was noted that rooms are frequently rented out in townhome units, which results in too
little parking. It was suggested that enforcing a certain number of parking stalls will in reality
reduce the density. Parking issues and possible solutions were discussed. Commissioner
Lefgren suggested that in areas where there is a problem, the number of parking spaces be
increased to at least three. Wider streets will also be beneficial and accommodate on-street
parking. It was noted that developments of a certain type will have different parking needs.
It was suggested that a fair compromise was to increase the number of spaces and include
on-street parking. Mr. Prestwich suggested setting a hard number but allow an alternative
for someone to apply for a Conditional Use Permit if they feel they need less.
