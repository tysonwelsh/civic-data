Proposed Resolution 2024-2 adopting the budget for the Redevelopment
Agency for Fiscal Year 2024-2025
         A memo from Board staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year 2024-2025 Redevelopment Agency budget came before the
Board for consideration. The memo stated the proposed budget totals $20,280,075 and the current proposal is to accept the budget
for review and set a public hearing for June 11, 2024 to consider adoption of the final budget.
         Comptroller Sorensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed FY 2024-2025 budget. Tax
revenues and other revenues are expected to increase when compared to the FY2024 budget and this is related to the debt issued in
the Continental Redevelopment Area related to the WonderBlock project. Mr. Sorensen presented a list of current Redevelopment
Agency areas, noting that the Fairmount and Lincoln areas will expire in the coming Fiscal Year. He identified transfers in and out
of the Redevelopment Agency budget for housing and administration costs; he also highlighted outstanding bond and loan debt in
the Redevelopment Agency budget. Outstanding bond debt is $7.4 million.
         Vice Chair White inquired as to the timeline of debt service for the St. Anne’s property. Mr. Sorensen stated he is unsure
the maturity date of that loan, but he will provide the answer to Vice Chair White’s question via email.
         Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2024-2, entitled:
                  “A resolution of the Board of Directors of the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency adopting the annual budget
                  for Fiscal Year 2024-2025 as required by Section 17C-1-601.5, Utah Code Annotated, 1953.”
          BOARD MEMBER HYER MOVED TO ACCEPT THE PROPOSED FISCAL YEAR 2024-2025
REDEVELOPMENT AGENCY BUDGET FOR REVIEW AND SET A PUBLIC HEARING FOR JUNE 11, 2024 TO
CONSIDER ADOPTION FOR THE FINAL BUDGET. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY BOARD MEMBER GRAF,
ALL VOTING AYE. Board member Myers was not present when this vote was taken.

Public Comments
           Teresa Bramwell expressed her opinion that the practice of ‘gatekeeping’ is occurring in Ogden on a regular basis. She
noted gatekeeping is the ‘activity of controlling and usually limiting access to something’. She feels it has happened to her a lot in
the City of Ogden, though it has not happened to her in any other place. She is a woman of the world and has lived in many places
throughout the world; but she was born and raised in Ogden, and she does not have access to information that others do. In order
for gatekeeping to occur, there must be a gatekeeper and she has been trying to find out who that is in Ogden government; she
believes there are a few, but she knows who one of them definitely is. She had an interaction with this gatekeeper when she was
calling Ogden Police Department about crime that was occurring at her property. Most Police Officers in Ogden are great, but one
is not, and this is the one that has responded to her property; he brought a new recruit with him to her property, and she found it
odd that he was new because he appeared to be fairly old. She had another interaction with this gatekeeper when her home was on
fire; this person had moved from the Police Department to the Fire Department.
           Linda Sanchez referenced the currently underway annual budget development process in the City and cited discussion
during tonight’s work session meeting about potential tax or fee increases that could impact residents. She encouraged the elected
officials of the City to keep in mind the elderly population who usually do not even receive a one-percent increase in their income.
A cost-of-living increase could put them at risk of deciding whether to buy food or medicine over paying their taxes. She added
that she has not received a pay increase recently and she does not receive an increase every year. She is also concerned about the
lack of an emergency fund in the City; the City must prepare for inevitable situations.
           Chair Richey stated that a one percent property tax increase is proposed; the information presented to the Board by
Administration indicates that there has been a four-percent increase in wages in the area and a three percent inflation rate; the
difference between those two is one percent and that is the basis for recommending a one percent tax increase. He added that the
City does have a ‘rainy day fund’ and State law regulates the amount of money that can be held in that account each year. Chief
Administrative Officer Brown added that the minimum the City can keep in its ‘rainy day fund’ is five percent of the general fund
budget and the maximum is 35 percent of the general fund budget. Currently, the City has about 20 percent of its general fund
budget in its ‘rainy day fund’, which would cover three months of employee wages.
