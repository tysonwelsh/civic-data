8. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to allow online auto sales as a permitted use in commercial
  and CBD Zones. (Attachment K)

  Ms. Olson stated at the previous meeting, the Planning Commission had reviewed a conditional use permit for
  an online auto sales. Given these are typically only office space uses with a limited amount of vehicle display,
  Staff is suggesting this use might be made permitted in commercial and CBD zones as a typical office use.
  She reviewed the standards in each of the zones for both new and used auto sales and stated while used auto
  sales are not allowed in the CBD, C-1 or C-2 zones, online auto sales may be acceptable as there is little effect
  on surrounding land uses. Staff has prepared ordinance language which would allow this use in all
  commercial zones so long as display is limited to three vehicles, and allows no maintenance or repair. All
  display vehicles must be operable and offered for sale only. She then reviewed the General Plan policies,
  stating the CBD zone allows a mix of commercial, office and residential uses, while commercial uses are
  typically for the sale of goods. She stated it could be determined that online auto sales fit the objectives of
  each of these zones and would typically function as an office rather than a typical auto dealer. Staff
  recommendation is for approval of the language as proposed.

  Chair Southwick asked if there is any limit to the number of these, suggesting if there were several in the same
  area it would have the appearance of a used car lot. Discussion continued among the Commission relating to
  how the use could be limited and what effects it could have if not regulated by the City. Ms. Olson answered
  the State regulates used car sales, and online sales operations are required to have a display area for at least
  three vehicles. She felt if the use were limited to three, the impact of the use would be limited. Commission
  expressed concern relating to the delivery and pick-up of vehicles, stating a vehicle may be sold and not
  picked up as anticipated and a replacement vehicle made available for display would violate the ordinance.
  Additional parking also needs to be available for those coming to pick up a car. It was indicated existing
  approved car lots do sell vehicles using the internet, but this regulation would be limited to those who use only
  on-line sales. Commission also asked if there is a need for these and if there are many applications for online
  car sales. Mr. Montgomery responded Staff is asked about the regulations for online vehicle sales and after
  the applicant finds a conditional use is required, they reconsider and either don’t open the business or go to a
  different city. Commissioner Schade asked if there is a definition of used car sales and if more detail should
  be added to the proposed ordinance language. Discussion continued relating to other information to be
  considered, such as whether detail work would be considered as maintenance and suggesting the item be
  tabled for more information to be researched by Staff.

  MOTION;       A motion was made by Commissioner Graf to table action and request Staff to consider the
                ideas discussed, including a better definition of online auto sales in relation to used car sales,

                                                    Page 7 of 10
                 whether the number of online sales should be limited. Motion was seconded by Commissioner
                 Safsten and passed unanimously, with Commissioners Blaisdell, Boykin, Garner, Graf, Safsten,
                 Schade, Stoker and Southwick voting aye.

9. Public Hearing, Petition 2020-5 to revise square footage requirements for townhomes in the MU zone.
   (Attachment L)

  Mr. Joe Torman, representing Lotus, stated they are desiring to develop property to the west of Slackwater as
  residential units in the MU zone consisting of 30 townhomes. The proposed architecture is a mix of traditional
  and modern, with Victorian elements on the corners. The proposal would be for a mix of one- two- and three-
  bedroom units, based on the design of the building, with one-bedroom units comprising about 25% of the
  project. He stated the intent is to maximize the design of the Grant Avenue Promenade and create a walkable
  community.

  Mr. Spencer stated the intent of the MU Ordinance is to provide a variety of uses, including residential. The
  current code required a minimum of 1,500 square feet of floor area while the applicant is requesting 940
  square feet of floor area for some of the single-bedroom units. He stated Staff has prepared language which
  would allow up to 25% of one-bedroom units to have a smaller unit size, between 950-1,300 square feet,
  feeling this would add variety while preserving the quality of development in the MU zone. He stated market
  studies indicate the size of one-bedroom units is smaller than they were in 2013, and the variety may increase
  incentives for development, improve the tax base by adding density and would have no effect on parking. He
  noted there is a lack of studio or one-bedroom apartments in the downtown. He reviewed the proposed
  language in relation to the River Project Plan, the MU zone and the CBD Plan and recommended approval of
  the language as proposed.

  Commissioner Garner stated while the unit size may have some effect on the quality of development, the
  appearance of the structure and interior amenities may be just as important. Commissioner Stoker asked why
  unit size is limited. Mr. Spencer responded the larger units create stability and cause a higher quality project
  to be built, but felt allowing some variety for reduced size does have merit, as it gives flexibility to the
  developer while providing some variety for tenants, stating market standards have changed since the MU
  Ordinance was first adopted.

  Mr. Brian Wrigley stated there are some new one-bedroom apartments in Salt Lake that were immediately
  filled upon construction, indicating there is a need for these. He stated there is a market for rental
  townhomes, stating these are often rented to young couples.

As there were no additional comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to close the public hearing.
   Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously.

   MOTION: A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the petition to amend
           the MU Ordinance to allow up to 25% of one-bedroom townhomes units to have a minimum size of
           940 square feet based on the findings the amendment is consistent with the intent and purpose of
           the MU zone, allows for the implementation of the Ogden River Redevelopment Plan and complies
           with both the CBD Plan and the General Plan. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and
           passed unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell, Boykin, Garner, Graf, Safsten, Schade, Stoker
           and Southwick voting aye.

10. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment to 15-2, and 15-38 regarding warehousing and personal
   storage uses in commercial zones. (Attachment N)

  Mr. Montgomery stated Staff has been concerned with the limited definitions of warehousing and personal

                                                   Page 8 of 10
  storage, stating while warehousing is allowed in some zones where storage is not, some applicants have
  argued storage the use of warehouse space. He explained typical warehousing may store items, but are
  usually products to be offered for sale, while storage units are typically for long-term storage of personal
  items. He stated because warehousing is allowed in the C-3 zone, retail space could be converted to indoor
  self-storage and retail opportunities are lost. These create dead zones and eliminate the potential taxes for
  sold commercial items. He identified areas where personal storage is allowed as well as where warehousing
  is allowed, stating there is concern that existing vacant big box buildings are not allowed to be converted to
  self-storage space. He stated Staff is proposing to create separate definitions of each of these as well as
  allowing them as accessory to a main permitted use. He stated commercial zones were not intended to be
  marketed for personal storage units and stated this use is not temporary and there is no future opportunity for
  redevelopment. These often become dead zones with little activity and eliminate the interaction of people
  which is promoted by retail uses. Staff recommendation is to better define both personal storage and
  warehousing, to remove warehousing the C-3 zone and clarifying personal storage is not allowed in the C- 3
  zone, but to continue to allow both as uses in the M-1 and M-2 zones.

  Commissioner Blaisdell asked about the effect on existing facilities. Mr. Montgomery stated they would be
  allowed to continue as a nonconforming use. It was indicated these are often designed such that there is no
  view into the site from the street and often become a magnet for crime. It also was noted Staff also has been
  made aware of their being used as living space and auto repair. Chair Southwick stated there is a demand for
  self-storage and expressed concern the language as proposed would affect private property rights by
  changing rules and property owners may have purchased commercial property with the intent for self-storage.

As there were no additional public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Schade to close the public
   hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously.
