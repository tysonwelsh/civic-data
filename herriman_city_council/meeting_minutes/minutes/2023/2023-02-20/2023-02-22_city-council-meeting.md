       2.1. City Council roundtable with County Councilman Sheldon Stewart
County Councilmember Sheldon Stewart hoped to have this meeting as a quarterly discussion.
He introduced his Policy Advisor, Kim Coleman. His goal was to have open dialogue and keep
fighting for the southwest part of the county. He attended yesterday’s Unified Police
Department’s presentation about shared services and believed HB-374 would bring more control
of the sheriff back into the county. They were trying to convolute what was considered shared
services compared to what county-wide services included and were going to relabel the services.
Policy Advisor Kim Coleman would continue to push for elected officials to keep accountability.
Councilmember Stewart disclosed the Corridor Preservation Funding was approved and money
should be distributed soon.

Councilmember Stewart wanted the City Council’s opinion on areas of focus. Councilmember
Shields signified the items Councilmember Stewart was passionate about, were aligned with the
desires of the Herriman Council including advocating for a fair share of money and services to be
allocated to the fastest growing area of the county and checking into how the budget was being
expended. Councilmember Shields alluded there had been a lack of scrutiny with how the budget
has been applied.

                           Councilmember Hodges arrived at 5:37 p.m.

Councilmember Shields recounted previously talking to the County Arts Director Matt Castillo,
who are looking at developing a facility for the performing arts in the southwest. Herriman City
completed a feasibility study which included aspects suggested for this facility. He asked what
needed to be done to encourage funding of the facility in this area. Councilmember Stewart voiced
that this project was in the early phases of discussion, and suggested opening it up to an inner-
city project, working together with other communities, and offered an example. He wanted to
make sure it was presented as a regional facility as it would help capture support of multiple cities
would bring a major benefit.

Councilmember Ohrn implied transportation funding for infrastructure was always a big subject.
There was never a lack of growth but always a lack of money. Property tax bills show a lot of
taxes going to the county yet, more land is annexed out of the county into the cities each year.
For example, the county was wanting to spend $500,000 on shuttle services up the canyon, she
opined shared services were being wasted and noted those services were not the proper role of

government. She argued the county needed to get back to providing essential services, not
entertainment. County Councilmember Stewart provided the example of the library tax increase
which had been approved, but patrons were not using these facilities as digital usage has
continually increased. He testified there had been a lot of concern with mismanagement of funds.
He suggested the County Council needed to look at a centralized system and manage those costs.
Councilmember Ohrn revealed that unfortunately, Herriman City gets less in property taxes than

Councilmember Ohrn voiced a need for discussion on the regional park. They need partnership,

development where the regional park would be constructed.

Councilmember Shields suggested due to the amount of land the county was managing keeps
decreasing there was a need for a redundancy analysis. Shared services needed to be reviewed
to determine if there was a duplication of efforts and fees within the county and the city.
Councilmember Stewart wanted the momentum to have the discussion for redundancy. There
was a need to show disparity in the reporting. He related more gang activity reported in Millcreek
by almost 99-percent, yet the southwest was comparable in size. There was gang activity in the
southwest, but it was not being reported nor was it receiving responses. The southwest was not
getting the attention but continued to fund shared services. Moving forward, the disproportionate
share needed to be reviewed. The southwest needed to look at the canyon and UFA fee, and
possibly investigate contracting Herriman City into policing the Butterfield Canyon. The UPD
regional hub was not available within the needed areas for service in the southwest.

Councilmember Stewart specified the UFA regional service did not have a boundary. Police
services do have regional boundaries. He believed what the southwest had set up for police and
SWAT team was amazing. He was encouraged with local control and operating and seeing what
has been happening with what was created together. The cities have created a great partnership.

            comprised of two lots located at 5143 W Miller Crossing Drive and 12252 S Herriman
            Auto Row in the C-2 Commercial Zone – Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy oriented the council of the location of the project and highlighted the
conceptual plan. The property currently has an approved Master Development Agreement, and
the developer looked to amend the site based on market changes. The amended plan included
office buildings, restaurant pads and multi-use buildings. City Planner Maloy relayed the MDA,

was quite simple. Staff felt the project should include a recreation element. The developer looked
to have shared parking for all uses.

Community Development Director Blake Thomas specified allowing a drive-thru in the
development would require either the definition of restaurant to be changed to include a drive-
thru or allowing fast food restaurant to be added into the MDA. 2020 changed how the restaurant
business remains profitable. Councilmember Ohrn preferred a more upscale restaurant, not

      2.3. Cemetery Rules and Regulations and Cemetery Expansion – Anthony Teuscher,
           Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events
Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events Anthony Teuscher conveyed the city was
running out of plots to sell in the cemetery. This doesn’t mean the plots have been interred, just
purchased. There was a need to expand the cemetery to the west. There was a possible plan for
a road to go through the expansion area, but it didn’t need to be added. The west section would
be able to add approximately 500 additional plots. Councilmember Ohrn felt the need to have
access to the actual grave site with the roadway. Mayor Palmer questioned if the road needed to
be added immediately. Deputy Director Teuscher disclosed the road could be marked out and
then can be added in the future. He offered the engineering estimate for the asphalt road with no
curb or gutter. He didn’t consider the road to be necessary and suggested it could also serve as a
pedestrian pathway. The Council asked for a comparison with other cemeteries in the
surrounding area. Deputy Director Teuscher informed the distance without the road was
comparable to other nearby cemeteries. Councilmember Hodges questioned parking issues.
Deputy Director Teuscher conveyed parking has not been an issue with parking along the road
and parking next to the park.

Deputy Director Teuscher informed the staff would like to provide a double stacking option in the
new section. The first burial would be at 8 feet with the second at 5 feet. He also offered policy

        2.4. Discussion the Parks ordinance regarding the E-collar approval process – Anthony
             Teuscher, Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events
Deputy Director Teuscher offered a brief background of the park’s ordinance update. Staff
investigated credentialing dogs to be on the trail system with e-collars. In their efforts, they
reached out to many organizations and came back with the K9 Good Citizen Program. An AKC
representative indicated this may not be the best method. Staff was still looking for a more viable
option.

City Manager Cherpeski expressed concern with the city’s liability if something happened with the
e-collar dog. Councilmember Ohrn thanked staff for their work, she specified wanting to make an
informed decision and would still want to leave it open for discussion. Animal Services Coordinator
Debbie Pedersen explained residents see dogs using e-collar, but assume the dogs were off-leash
and have concerns with dogs physically off leashes. There was an option of an ordinance like
Millcreek allowing dogs to be off leash on hiking trails but not on improved trails on odd days. Even
days, dogs are strictly on leash. Councilmember Shields signified his preference to leave the
liability left with the dog owner, if the ordinance was changed the city might be liable.


        2.5. Pothole Repair and Pavement Preservation Report – Justun Edwards, Public Works
             Director
Public Works Director Justun Edwards explained potholes begin with imperfections in the asphalt
when the water penetrates the structural base. The freeze and thaw cycles and traffic loading is
what initially creates the potholes. This also places limitations on when the potholes can be
repaired. Cold patches are faster to install but are a temporary fix. Hot patches are permanent but
require a machine, longer labor, and a higher cost. The city recently purchased a used hot patch
machine. Previously, this machine was rented annually, in the long run, the machine would save
the city money. Hot patch and cold patch need about the same outdoor temperature to be used.

Pavement Preservation goals included preserving the good roads and maintaining or improving
overall PCI (Pavement Condition Index) rating. Councilmember Shields inquired about the city’s
