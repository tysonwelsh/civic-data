Ogden Water Utility Manager Brady Herd will discuss applicable provisions
of Ogden's Water Shortage Management Plan and outline steps residents
should take to conserve water
          Ogden Water Utility Manager Brady Herd indicated thatthe City's Water Shortage Management Plan provides the City
the authority to issue a Declaration ofModerate Water Shortage in times of drought; the City issued a declaration on June 2, 2021,
which will be effective through October 15 or until further notice. He used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss current
water conditions in the City; precipitation in the Weber and Ogden River basins has been just 46 percent of average this spring
and 64 percent of average over the 2020-2021 winter months. Current soil moisture is 72 percent of normal and was 74 percent of
normal last year. Both water and soil temperatures are well above nonnal and water availability in the Ogden River and Weber
River is currently at the fifth and third percentile, respectively. Pineview Reservoir's storage is at 56 percent of capacity,
compared to 92 percent at this same time last year. Pineview Water will need to shut off its residential secondary water system as
early as the first week in August. He provided several graphs illustrating the water availability index for Ogden and Weber Rivers,
after which he discussed phased responses to water shortages and provided the press release that as published to communicate the
declaration to residents and businesses in the City. As a result of the declaration, applicable provisions of Ogden's Water Shortage
Management Plan are in force, and all residents are asked to take the following steps to conserve water for the remainder of the
summer:
                •   Outdoor watering is prohibited between 10:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m. A violation is enforceable by fmes beginning
                    at $50 for the first violation and ranging up with subsequent violations.
                •   All residential water customers of Ogden City are encouraged to reduce their water use by at least five percent,
                    and commercial water customers are encouraged to reduce their water use by at least fifteen percent.
                •   Maintain and properly adjust your irrigation system to avoid wasting water. Adjust watering times based on
                    weather.
                •   A void hard-surface washing of sidewalks and driveways (except for health or safety).
                •   Use a positive pressure nozzle when washing vehicles.
                •   Use a swimming pool cover when the pool is not in use. Lower the pool water level by four inches to minimize
                    water loss from splashing.
          Mr. Herd then provided websites that include water conservation resources and educational materials for residents and
businesses; additionally, there are several rebate programs that residents can access that provide incentives for making
improvements to their properties or irrigation systems in an effort to conserve water. He also discussed the efforts of City
Administration to clearly communicate the declaration to residents, with a focus on the importance of determining whether turf is
dead or just dormant and the amount of water that is needed to keep turf from dying.
          Council discussion with Mr. Herd centered on communication efforts and opportunities for individual Council members
to communicate with their constituents about the matter. Additionally, Council member Nadolski suggested the Council consider
adopting a resolution urging the Utah Governor to issue an Executive Order prohibiting fireworks throughout the duration of the
drought. Chief Administrative Officer Johnson stated he does not believe the Governor has that authority and the resolution should
be directed to the State Legislature.
          Vice Chair White stated it is important to communicate to commercial property owners as well as industrial water users
as they are a significant portion of the City's total water users. Mr. Herd agreed and stated that the City can increase its efforts at
communicating with commercial property owners. Mr. Johnson added that The Boyer Company, manager of Business Depot
Ogden sent a letter to all of their tenants to infonn them of their efforts to reduce water consmnption for the remainder of the
growing season.
          Continued discussion centered on the City's plans for enforcing the declaration and citing residents who are violating the
directives of the declaration. Mr. Johnson stated that the City is primarily interested in compliance and not generating revenue
through enforcement actions.

Ogden Fire Marshall Kevin Brown will review restrictions on the use of
fireworks and open flames in specific areas of Ogden City
          Ogden City Fire ChiefMathieu reviewed the Firework and Open Flame Restrictions order issued June 1, 2021. The order
prohibits the use of fireworks and open flames in the following areas of Ogden City:
                1. All areas east ofHanison Boulevard from the north city boundary to the south city boundary.
                2. All wooded areas along the Ogden and Weber River Parkways beginning at Harrison Blvd and continuing
                    along the pathways to the west city boundary and the south city boundary. This also includes all associated
                    parks along the River conidor.
                3. All of Fort Buenaventura, the city baseball park, and dog park area located off A Avenue.
                4. The old landfill property located at approximately 2550 A A venue, near Fort Buenaventura.
                5. All open fields, vacant lots, wooded areas, and brush covered hillsides throughout the city.
          ChiefMathieu then discussed present conditions and data that led the City to issue the restrictions; present conditions are
rare for this time of year and more closely resemble conditions that are typically not seen until August or September. Fire officials
in the area are very concerned about conditions worsening and resources being taxed if restrictions are not observed. He briefly
touched State laws and regulations that defme the authority oflocal government officials to enact restrictions, and he emphasized
no City official has the authority to implement a complete ban on open flames or fireworks. That action must be taken by the State
Legislature.
          Chief Administrative Officer Johnson added that the City's Police Department will be actively enforcing this order and
the associated restrictions; given the cUITent drought conditions, frres resulting from open flames or frreworks in violation of the
order are very taxing on the City's water systems.
          There was then discussion among the Council and Chief Mathieu regarding recent frre events and the impact those events
have had on the water supply and vegetation/structures in the City.
