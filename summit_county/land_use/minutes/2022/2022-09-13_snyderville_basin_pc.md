Background:
In accordance with recent amendments to t!E±§g State Resource Management Plan
Amendments, it has been requested by the Utah Public Lands Policy Coordinating Office
(PLPCO) that Summit County review our Summit County Resource Mana ement Plan (RMP) to
revise and add language deemed necessary in order to align with the H8160 amendments to
the State RMP.  The amendments are relate to land access, energy resources (including
renewable energy), and mining and mineral resources (including critical minerals).  Additionally,
two new sections on utility corridors and pipelines, and infrastructure have been added and
have been considered for inclusion.
Analysis and Findings:
Staff has reviewed the current Summit County RMP, an element of the Snyderville Basin
General plan, and has drafted proposed modifications to the plan that address the request
related to land access, energy resources (including renewable energy), and mining and mineral
resources (including critical minerals) as well as the addition of two new sections on utility
corridors and pipelines, and infrastructure.
It is important to note that this amendment is not meant to be a rewrite, or a full revision, of
the RMP. A future opportunity is forthcoming, accompanied by state funding to assist the
process, that will allow for a deeper understanding of needed RMP changes, updates and
additions.  Also, noteworthy is recognizing that the RMP is integrated with the Eastern Summit
County General  Plan and any modifications to the RMP will also require an update to that
General Plan as well.

additions to the Summit County RMP and vote to forward a positive recommendation for to the

RECOMMENDATION:  Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the proposed
language to amend Chapter 10-10-1:  Engineering Standards in the Snyderville Basin
Development Code conduct a public hearing and forward a positive recommendation to the
County Council per the findings of fact, conclusions of law and conditions of approval in this
staff report.
Proposal
The purpose of the amendments is to bring Summit County into compliance with the
requirements of the State of Utah's issued  Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4)
permit, in chapter 10-10-1 which reference Section 4-5-1 through 4-5-19 and Section 9-3-1
through 9-3-12 of the Snyderville Development Code that regulates stormwater. The updates
will prevent Summit County from potential fines from the Department of Environmental Quality
Division of Water Quality (DEQ-DWQ) or the Environmental  Protection Agency (EPA).
Background
ln 2016 Summit County was designated as a MS4 and issued a general MS4 stormwater permit
from the DEQ-DWQ. The County had a five year grace period to come into full compliance of all
permit requirements without penalties.
In January of 2022 Summit County received an audit from the DWQ Division for the compliance
with the State issued MS4 permit requirements. The conclusion of the audit included Action
Items that were required to be submitted to be in full compliance with the MS4 permit. One of

Analysis
Section 10-7-3 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code states that whenever there is
initiated an amendment to the Code, it must be reviewed by the planning Commission who will
deliver a recommendation to the County Council. The County Council, after holding a public
hearing, shall approve, approve with modifications, or deny the amendment according to the
following criteria.
Criteria 1: The amendment shall be consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the
general  plan.  COMPLIES
Analysis: The proposed amendment will bring Summit County into compliance with
current MS4 permit, as well as continue to protect water quality.
Criteria 2: The amendment shall not permit the use of land that is not consistent with the uses
of properties nearby. COMPLIES
Analysis: The proposed change will not create any inconsistencies between uses on any
land  in the Snyderville  Basin.
Criteria 3: The amendment will not permit suitability of the properties affected by the proposed
amendment for the uses to which they have been restricted. COMPLIES
Analysis:  The amendment will not permit suitability of the properties affected by the

Criteria 4: The amendment will not permit the removal of the then existing restrictions which
will  unduly affect nearby property. COMPLIES
