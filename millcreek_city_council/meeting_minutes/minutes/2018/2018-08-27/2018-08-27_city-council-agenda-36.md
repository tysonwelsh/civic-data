1. Discussion on Employee Insurance Waiver Stipend Program

Mayor Silvestrini said a number of other cities provide a program for their employees for those
who choose not to take the City’s insurance benefit. This stipend compensates them for not
taking the benefit. The City saves money with employees using the stipend compared to using
the insurance. He said Laurie Johnson had a survey of what other cities do, but she has been
excused from the meeting. John Geilmann said the stipend is the amount the City pays for the
employees now, but the percentage is lower than the whole premium. This would put Millcreek
in line with what other cities are doing. Mayor Silvestrini said this is a measure to save the City
money but also to be attractive as an employer. Council Member Marchant asked if he could
waive the stipend if he did not want it. Geilmann said yes. Council Member Jackson said it is an
equity issue.

2. Presentation on International Property Maintenance Code; Director of City Services

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 27 August 2018 Page 2 of 10

Kurt Hansen said he wanted to introduce a new code to the Council and get their direction on it.
He said the information is published by the International Code Council, who publishes the
international building code. Hansen said the building code takes you through the construction,
this code applies afterwards. He said code enforcement officers find dilapidated houses
throughout the City. The code gives the City the ability to inspect structures and notify residents.
Hansen went through the code which included definitions, noticing, appeals, and process. Mayor
Silvestrini said attractive nuisances draw transients. Hansen said the City could abate some of the
properties. Hansen said there is a provision for weeds, rodents, and vehicles. Mayor Silvestrini
said the code should cross-reference other areas in code. Council Member Marchant asked what
other remedies there are than to board up the structure, disconnect utilities, or lien the property.
Hansen said the structure can only be boarded up for a year. Hansen said it could also be torn
down.

Mayor Silvestrini asked if the City should adopt a policy about the form of notice for code
compliance. He thinks the wording should be less threatening and more encouraging to fix the
problem. Hansen said if the City had to abate the nuisance, the bill would be sent to the property
owner. John Brems said there is a specific process to put a lien on property. He said by way of
notices of violation, some areas do not comply, so the officers may just issue the citation instead
of notices. Mayor Silvestrini said he would like to have an attitude of assistance. Hansen said the
City could soften the procedure. Mayor Silvestrini said there are some instances when more
experience is required to determine structural soundness. Hansen said the building inspector
would verify those issues. Mayor Silvestrini asked how the inspectors do inspections on the
interior. Hansen said the inspectors would gain permission to enter the premises when the
structure appears to have an issue. Mayor Silvestrini asked how the City gets probable cause to
get a warrant. Hansen said it usually involves tenants reporting on a landlord. Brems said there
may be an order to enter with lawsuits. Hansen said the code applies to residential and
commercial. Hansen said the standard is to maintain homes to the level at which they were
constructed. Council Member Catten asked about trees encroaching in others’ yards. Hansen said
it is not covered in this code and those incidents are civil disputes between neighbors. The
Council said they were in favor of pursuing the ordinance.

3. Discussion on Ordinance Amending Millcreek Code to Provide that Rental of One or
More Residential Dwellings is Included in the Definition of Business and Thereby Requires
a Business License

Kurt Hansen said the code says a business license is only required for a 3-plex or higher density.
The code change would be to require a license with one rental or more. He illustrated an affidavit
which allows the owner to sign the check sheet verifying compliance with code on an honor
system instead of using City resources on inspectors. Council Member Jackson asked if the
ordinance included Airbnb’s. Hansen said this ordinance is for rentals longer than 30 days.
Mayor Silvestrini said he would like to implement a good landlord policy but state code does not
allow the City to rebate for single family dwelling rentals. He said this new City requirement
would be to get a handle on rentals to make sure they meet certain standards and the license
would be the tool to do that. Council Member Jackson asked about parking, because if 4
unrelated adults are allowed then two parking spaces is not enough. Hansen said the business
license code states that there needs to be enough parking for the appropriate number of adults.
Council Member Marchant asked if there was any grandfathering to what rentals currently
provide. Brems said licenses do not allow grandfathering. Council Member Marchant asked what
the fee for the license is. Hansen said the annual fee right now for a business license is $150.

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 27 August 2018 Page 3 of 10

Mayor Silvestrini said the City will not make money on this but will expend on resources to
enforce it. Council Member Jackson expressed concern about penalizing the honest people who
comply and pay for licenses when many will not obtain them. Brems said the City will look at
where the property tax notices are sent, if it is elsewhere than the residence then it is probably a
rental. Hansen said the enforcement is complaint-driven and Unified Police Department (UPD)
would get involved. Mayor Silvestrini said the City could reduce the license fee to something
more palatable. Hansen said the base fee is $150 then an additional fee per unit. Brems
recommended the City do an analysis to see how much it would cost to then know what to
charge. Council Member Jackson asked if the City would track down houses with a different tax
notice mailing address then notify them of the ordinance. Hansen said the program could start in
January with a grace period then start enforcement. Mayor Silvestrini said Hansen could bring
this ordinance back with more information at another work session.

Mayor Silvestrini asked about adding yard maintenance to the owner checklist on the affidavit.
Council Member Jackson said it would be a good reminder to the landlord to maintain the yard.
Mayor Silvestrini said it would make enforcement easier if the owner certifies they will maintain
the yard. Council Member Jackson asked about including snow removal from the sidewalk on
the affidavit too. Council Member Uipi wants to make sure the checklist is equitable. Hansen
asked about the difference between room rental versus a basement rental. Mayor Silvestrini said
safety equipment still applies. Brems said renting a room is still a business, so a license should
be required. Council Member Jackson asked if there are multiple roommates, does each room
count as a unit. Council Member Uipi said yes. Mayor Silvestrini said the City would only know
that based on complaints which may be seen by excess parking.

4. Discussion on Property Located Near City Hall for Use as City Vehicle Parking Lot
Kurt Hansen said a piece of property behind City Hall is not owned by Woodbury Corp. He said
he sees it being a parking lot for city vehicles. It would cost about $30,000 to fix it up. He said
staff has drafted a preliminary offer to the owner. Mayor Silvestrini said it would get the City’s
vehicles out of the parking lot, which would free up parking and keep the vehicles in a more
secure area. He said there have been issues with transients, so it may make City Hall safer.
Hansen said Woodbury Corp. owns the access property from this piece to 3300 S. Hansen said
the City sent them a non-binding letter of intent, offering $2 a foot to lease the space. The
Council said Hansen could move forward with negotiations.

2. Planning Matters
2.1 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-48 and Ordinance 18-49, Rezone
from R-M to C-2 for Mixed Use for an 8-unit Multifamily Building with 1,150
square feet of Ground Level Retail and Adoption of a Development Agreement for
Property Located at 2780 E. 3300 S.
Francis Lilly said the project has 1,350 square feet of retail not 1,150. Lilly said this
application came to the Council in May and it was remanded to the Planning Commission
for lack of public notice. Lilly said the Planning Commission recommended approval of
the rezone subject to a development agreement. Lilly said the development agreement
included issues raised by the public; it reduces the height of the building to 26.5 feet with
three 28-foot tower elements, reduces the number of units to 8, adds an eight-foot wall
that will be measured from the highest grade, and that the developer will seek input from
the residents to the south on trees. Lilly said the front yard setback is still 15 feet with
enhanced landscaping though the Council recently amended code to remove that
provision. There will also be 24 parking spaces that benefits the commercial and
residential, 28% open space, bike storage, private balconies, and a BBQ area. The East
Millcreek Community Council recommended that the project be approved, subject to a
development agreement that limits height to 26.5 feet, with an allowance for the 28-foot
towers, columnar trees in the rear yard buffer, an 8-foot masonry wall along the
residential zone boundary, and a design that utilizes quality materials. Lilly said the
Planning Commission recommended approval with the considerations of 2-inch caliper
trees, lighting be installed along the bottom of the second floor along the 3300 South
facade, and to illuminate the sidewalk, and prohibition of short-term rentals. Mayor
Silvestrini asked about the basis for curbing short-term rentals. Lilly said there was a
concern about parking and the balcony usage creating a nuisance to the neighbors. Lilly
said during the term of this agreement, the developer will commence with all required
permits and will complete the project. If the developer fails to complete the project within
the term of this agreement, the City may unilaterally pursue a rezone of the property to R-
1-10, and the developer hereby waives any objection.

Mayor Silvestrini asked applicant Joe Johnsen if the development agreement was
acceptable to him. Johnsen said yes. Mayor Silvestrini asked for public comment. There
was none.
