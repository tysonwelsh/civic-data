                                              CONDITIONAL USE


  3.        Consideration of a Non-Administrative Conditional Use Permit to Allow SNG Auto Sales to Operate a Used
            Car Dealership at 4515 South Redwood Road. (Karyn Kerdolff/GIS Planner)

 3.1      Ms. Kerdolff presented this item. George Marks, on behalf of SNG Auto Sales 100, is requesting a conditional
          use permit for the operation of a used car dealership. The applicant is proposing to run a car dealership for
          used vehicles. There will only be sales as the applicant is not proposing any repair work or car washing at the
          location. The applicant is proposing to have 12 vehicles at the location at any one time. Previously a non-
          administrative conditional use was approved at this location on June 11, 2013 (File #26C13) with the following
          conditions:
               • Comply with the requirements of all reviewing agencies.
               • Obtain a City business license and keep it current.
               • Public access to the rear (east) of the building is prohibited.
               • Building permits must be obtained for remodeling or signs as required by the International Building
                   Code.
               • Any signage used on the property must be in compliance with Chapter 26 of the Taylorsville Land
                   Development Code.
               • No parking or display of vehicles will be allowed on the landscaping.

3.2       FINDINGS:
          3.2.1  This application was initiated by George Marks on behalf of SNG Auto Sales 100, at 4515 South
                 Redwood Road.
          3.2.2  The subject property is zoned Boulevard Commercial.
          3.2.3  The applicant is requesting a conditional use for the operation of a used car dealership.
                 3.2.4    A non-administrative conditional use was approved at this location on June 11, 2013 (File
                 #26C13) for the same land use applicant is requesting.
          3.2.5  During the previous approval for the same land use type (File #26C13) parking requirements were
                 not set.
          3.2.6  [Changed by Motion – 04-13-21] The applicant is proposing no more than 12 vehicles at the location
                 at any one time.
          3.2.7  The applicant has indicated that the location will not be used for vehicle maintenance or the washing
                 of vehicles.

3.3       CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL:
          3.3.1  Comply with the requirements of all reviewing agencies.
          3.3.2  The Conditional Use Permit is subject to review upon substantiated and unresolved complaints.
                 Complaints which cannot be resolved by Staff may be grounds for permit revocation.
          3.3.3  The applicant must adhere to Taylorsville Municipal Code. This includes but is not limited to:
                 13.24.050 (F) – “Parking Lot Surface: Every parcel of land used as a parking or storage area shall be
                 paved with asphalt, brick, or concrete surfacing and shall be arranged and striped to provide orderly
                 and safe loading, unloading, parking, and storage of vehicles.” (Ord. 12-15, 7-11-2012)
          3.3.4  The requisite state agency must issue a vehicle dealer license in addition to the City issued business
                 license prior to commencing operations.
          3.3.5  The requisite vehicle dealer license must be always displayed.
          3.3.6  [Changed by Motion – 04-13-21] Public vehicular access to the rear (east) of the building is
                 prohibited.
          3.3.7  Building permits must be obtained for remodeling or signs as required by the International Building
                 Code.

         3.3.8     Any signage used on the property must be in compliance with Chapter 26 of the Taylorsville Land

         3.3.9     No parking or display of vehicles will be allowed on the landscaping.

3.4      STAFF RECOMMENDATION: Staff recommends that the Planning Commission approves the Conditional Use
         Permit if all conditions are met, for SNG Auto Sales 100 to operate a used car dealership on the property
         located at 4515 South Redwood Road.

3.5      DISCUSSION:
         3.5.1  Commissioner Russell reference Conditions of Approval #9 said he was not sure what “no display of
                vehicles” means. Ms. Kerdolff said that reference was specific to “landscaping”. They cannot
                   display vehicles on the landscaping.

         3.5.2     Commissioner Wright said he was curious about the previous permit. Did the ownership change or
                   operation change and if so, would that have needed a new conditional use permit there? He was
                   asked how it went from auto sales to whatever interim use was there and now they are asking for
                   auto sales again. Ms. Kerdolff said her understanding is that the land ownership did not change but
                   the business changed. It is a new business requesting to operate there now, so they needed a
                   conditional use for that current operation. Commissioner Wright said that then the previous
                   business did not need a conditional use. Ms. Kerdolff said that was correct because the intermediate
                   one did not need one. It was a coffee shop, which is a permitted use in the zone.

         3.5.3     Commissioner Burggraaf said to Staff that in two different spots they bring up that the applicant had
                   said there would not be any use to wash vehicles on the lot. The applicant did explain how they
                   intended to keep the vehicles clean. He wanted to know if that would be a separate application if
                   they were to subsequently use the lot for washing vehicles. Ms. Kerdolff said not for washing
                   vehicles but the maintenance or working on cars would need it being looked at further. That washing
                   is not looked at as minor or major car maintenance in the Code. Commissioner Burggraaf said the
                   Code obviously deals with car washes but that is usually a business done just for that. In this sense
                   they may wash the cars to make them presentable for sale. He wanted to know if there was a
                   “harm” that Staff feels the Commission ought to be addressing by a condition related to washing
                   vehicles on that lot. What he was getting at was there was obviously a canal in the back and so he
                   did not know if the run-off from washing a vehicle would constitute a concern or not. He wanted to
                   know if Staff had considered that. Ms. Kerdolff said they had not because basically the applicant has
                   specifically said they are not going to wash the vehicles on site. Ms. Kerdolff said she would defer to
                   the City Engineer to make that call along with having Taylorsville-Bennion Improvement District
                   personnel review and place any requirements to deal with that type of discharge should that become
                   an issue.

         3.5.4     Mr. Harper interjected that the intent of that was to assure it was not a commercial vehicle washing
                   operation. The vacuuming and light washing that they normally do next to the building into the
                   existing drain system is part of the normal business operation. Staff wanted to make sure that there
                   wasn’t a commercial aspect involved. Commissioner Burggraaf said maybe it is something the
                   applicant can speak to in that this is a smaller car dealership. Larger car dealerships usually have a
                   bay specifically for washing vehicles where there is adequate drainage. He was not familiar with this
                   particular lot but did not think they have that type of set up. Mr. Harper said that they do not have a
                   drive-through, but they do have a place for the runoff water to go to.

3.6      APPLICANT ADDRESS: George Marks.

         3.6.1     Mr. Marks addressed the issue of washing cars by saying that there might occasionally be one or two
                   cars that need to be sprayed off and wiped dry, which is the only thing that would be done on site.
                   That would involve maybe one or two vehicles every six months or so. Cars needing more than that
                   would be taken to a car wash in the local area. For information, he added that there are drains in
                   the front and rear of the property. Commissioner Wright asked where those drains empty into. Mr.
                   Marks did not know. Commissioner Wright wanted clarification on that saying it seems they have
                   moved from not washing vehicles on site to maybe washing just a few. Commissioner Burggraaf said
                   his question about the “washing” was more because Staff brought it up twice but didn’t include a
                   condition related to washing.

         3.6.2     Commissioner Willardson added that he had walked this site and noticed there were four parking
                   stalls over where the handicap stalls are. There are two that would be going the opposite way on the
                   other side. That accounts for six and there could probably be a couple more stalls squeezed in but
                   there needs to be access provided for emergency vehicles to get to the back. Mr. Marks said there
                   would probably only be four to five cars in the front and the rest would be in the back on the patio.
                   Commissioner Willardson said if he understood it right, the public is not allowed in the back area.
                   Ms. Kerdolff said that had been a previous condition of the previous approval that no public access
                   to rear (east} of the building was allowed. Mr. Marks said an easy way to fix that is that he could just
                   pull the vehicle they are interested in up so they could look at it. Commissioner Willardson said that
                   the back area is presently in pretty rough shape. Mr. Marks said it is a cement area and weeds right
                   now. He has been working on cleaning that up. He asked if he could bring in a couple of truck-loads
                   of gravel to fill in that area. Currently there are no black top finished surfaces back there.
                   Commissioner Burggraaf said that is a question for Staff to answer but he was under the impression
                   that needed to be paved. Ms. Kerdolff said that for the storage of vehicles it would need to be
                   paved.      Commissioner Burggraaf said that in this scenario it would need to be paved for vehicles
                   to be stored thereon as opposed to just doing gravel. That gravel would probably be considered
                   “landscaping” and vehicles cannot be parked on landscaping.

         3.6.3     Commissioner Wendel           needed clarification on access to the back. She didn’t think it was being
                   said that customers could not go into the back, but rather saying they can’t park in the back. If they
                   were using the back lot as their showcase to display their vehicles, then it would be fine for
                   customers to walk back there. Ms. Kerdolff said that was right. The condition does not stop public
                   access but rather was meant to be no vehicular access allowed. She agreed that still isn’t very clear
                   and might need clarification. Commissioner Burggraaf suggested talking about what the harm might
                   be as a point of discussion when it goes back to the Commission. Commissioner Wendel said she
                   had more questions for the applicant. It sounds like the applicant is already in business. Mr. Marks
                   said he has been in business at another location and has now moved everything to this location.
                   Commissioner Wendel said when he talks about having 12 cars, she wanted to make sure the
                   amount of asphalt he currently has will accommodate that many cars in the back. Mr. Marks said
                   that would be no problem. Commissioner Wendel asserted that in looking at the space it looks on
                   the image that it could accommodate a much larger number of vehicles than 12. The point of the
                   conditional use permit is to make sure that the applicant has a good understanding that the City
                   wants the business to thrive but can’t have cars parked on anything that is not safe for that. Mr.
                   Marks said he understood that, and which is why he asked about the gravel but does know now why
                   that is not possible and that he will have to pave it. He continued on to say that customers actually
                   don’t have to go in the back because of being such a small dealership, they can rotate cars from the
                   back to the front and vice versa easily and often. Commissioner Wendel wanted to make sure the
                   applicant knows that the City wants them to be successful. Mr. Marks said if he subsequently wants
                   to expand, he will come back to the Commission to get permission to have more paving installed.

         3.6.4     Commissioner Wright wanted to discuss the use of gravel and felt the assumption was that gravel
                   was landscaping and not paving. He advised he has seen gravel used in many parts of the United
                   States as paving and wondered why it was considered not appropriate here for paving.
                   Commissioner Burggraaf said he may have mis-spoken when he said that gravel is landscaping and
                   asked Staff if cars are allowed to be stored on gravel. Ms. Kerdolff advised the answer to that is no,
                   per Code. Mr. Harper clarified that saying that sometimes there is vehicle leakage while parked and
                   if it is gravel it permeates through to the soil and into the ground water. While on asphalt or
                   concrete it can be better managed.

         3.6.5     Commissioner Burggraaf said if the Commission were to contemplate business growth here, in his
                   mind when he contemplates car dealerships if only three or four vehicles are being displayed in front
                   that means there will be less enticement for people to come in and buy a car. He felt foot traffic
                   from the public should be allowed. He asked the applicant if the Commission were to put a condition
                   in place that says the applicant can only park vehicles on paved areas and must insure there is
                   emergency access around the building would that be a feasible condition for him to comply with. To
                   which, Mr. Marks replied in the affirmative.

         NOTE: At this point Commissioner McElreath had to leave and Commissioner Burggraaf excused him to do
         so.

3.7      Commissioner Burggraaf opened the meeting for public comments. No one came forward but Ms. Kerdolff
         advised she had received one E-mail comment from Karen Quinn who said she was not in favor of this
         because there were already too many car dealerships in that same area. There being no further comments,
         Commissioner Burggraaf closed the public hearing and opened the meeting for discussion by the Commission.

3.8      DISCUSSION:

         3.8.1     Commissioner Wendel              said she was really happy with the applicant coming in today and
                   wished him and his wife well with this new business. Her concern was to go back to Condition of
                   Approval #2 and how to adjust that. Just for clarification, it was many years ago that the Commission
                   said they needed solid surfaces for parking, which applies to residential, commercial, essentially
                   everything. She felt that would probably answer Commissioner Wright’s question on that matter.
                   She wanted to discuss Condition #2 and discuss Findings #6 on how there are 12 cars limited and get
                   the details together in order to be able to create an appropriate motion.

         3.8.2     Commissioner Wright                addressed Commissioner Burggraaf as having brought up the topic of
                   Condition #2 being subject to review. He felt simplifying that might be a way to address that
                   particular item, just saying it is subject to review as is the case generally. That may be redundant, but
                   it is a little easier to word it that way than to add the additional items or complaints which cannot be
                   resolved by Staff may be grounds for permit revocation. He was in favor of simplifying that to make
                   sense to him. He was curious about the applicant proposing no more than 12 vehicles at this location
                   at any one time. He was curious why that was included in Findings #6. Commissioner Burggraaf
                   asked Commissioner Wright if he had thought what harm having a specific number of cars would
                   address. Commissioner Wright said the number of cars, if he were to do a quick measurement it
                   looks like it would only fit them on the concrete pad in the back right now. Anymore would overflow
                   into the landscape/gravel area. Commissioner Burggraaf asked what would happen if the applicant
                   were to go into the bigger size trucks, extended cabs, etc. Commissioner Wright said that was a
                   good question. Commissioner Burggraaf said that is why he wonders if the number of vehicles
                   makes sense. Another scenario would be if it were compact cars, then more vehicles could fit into
                   that same space than would a mid-size sedan. That is why he brought it up really trying to mitigate a

                   “harm” meaning to just say it is allowed on the paved area at his discretion as to how many and how
                   they are parked. Commissioner Wright was in favor of that idea as well as accommodating safety
                   issues.

         3.8.3     Commissioner Russell said he was curious if the applicant owned any other used car dealerships
                   around the valley. Mr. Marks replied that he doesn’t, but his family does.

         3.8.4     Commissioner Burggraaf suggested that the Commission outright strike #2 as a Condition of
                   Approval because of what has been discussed as not addressing a harm. As Commissioner Wright
                   said, conditional use permits are subject to review and the applicant hearing this ought to know that
                   if the City discovers a condition is not being complied with, such as cars are being parked off
                   pavement, then that potentially may be grounds for revoking the conditional use permit. It would
                   then be brought before the Commission to consider that. He would also suggest that condition of
                   approval #6 be clarified to allow public access by way of foot traffic in the rear but no public vehicular
                   traffic.       His suggestion is to say no public vehicular traffic, which would imply that the public
                   would not be driving vehicles back there. Cars could be displayed back there, the public park up front
                   and walk to the back. As business grows, cars may be parked very close to each other back there,
                   which is another reason why the public should not be driving back there. Another reason why is if
                   the public is going back there, they may impede an emergency lane.

         3.8.5     Mr. Harper wanted to make sure what exactly is being proposed and asked if the proposal is to Strike
                   Condition of Approval #2, and on #6 change it to say, “Public vehicular access to the rear, (east) of
                   the building is prohibited.” Commissioner Burggraaf said that was correct.

         3.8.6     Commissioner Wright said that would allow if the applicant hired someone to come to bring in cars.
                   Commissioner Burggraaf said those instances are not members of the public but essentially
                   employees, delivery people, etc.

         3.8.7     Commissioner Burggraaf said it is brought up in Findings #6 that the applicant is proposing no more
                   than 12 vehicles but that is not a binding issue. What is being decided would be based on that but
                   not necessarily a binding condition for proposal. He just wanted to be clear that it is a finding
                   compared to a condition. He felt the “pavement” condition is sufficient. He asked Staff if there was
                   a need to add a condition that all vehicles must be parked on pavement. Ms. Kerdolff said that it is
                   already a requirement so would not be needed to be added as a condition. Commissioner Burggraaf
                   said then by Code, if the business grows and needs more space, within the restrictions of the Code
                   pavement could be added for additional parking. Ms. Kerdolff said that was correct.

         3.8.8     Commissioner Wendel asked if the Commission completely strikes Condition of Approval #2 where is
                   it in the agreement or information provided to the applicant that should they fail to comply with the
                   conditions of approval that they may be subject to permit revocation if it is not in the conditions of
                   approval? Is it available on an application form or somewhere else? Mr. Harper said it is in other
                   sections of the Code, which location he could not exactly remember. Commissioner Wendel advised
                   that she just wanted to make sure the Commission is doing their due diligence and assuring an
                   applicant is informed that revocation is possible if conditions are not complied with. Commissioner
                   Burggraaf said even phrasing it that way, it is not technically a condition to have that in that section.
                   He did recall in the ordinance that conditional use permits are subject to review and revocation.
                   Therefore, he believed it is covered. Commissioner Wendel said she just felt it was an issue of good
                   customer service being provided to the applicant.
