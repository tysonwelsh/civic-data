5.     PUBLIC HEARING.CONSIDERATION AND RECOMMENDATION on Proposed
       Text Amendments to the Bluffdale City Code 11.110.140 SD-X The Bluff at Point
       Crossing Zone Reducing the Number of Entitled Townhome Units from 50 to 40 and
       Reducing the Amount of Commercial Area from 62.000 Square Feet to 40,000 Square
       Feet Along with an Updated Conceptual Site Plan, Plans for Hillside Revegetation
       and Other Related Administrative Items. Geneva Rock Products, Applicant
       Represented by Ciavton Rackham (Application 2024-17)»                 Caitlvn Tubbs, Staff
       Presenter.


Planning Manager, Caitlyn Tubbs, presented the Staff Report and stated that the project is located
on 20 acres north ofPorter Rockwell Boulevard and south of Day Ranch Park. Approximately 1
i/2 years earlier, the City Council passed Ordinance 2023-03 enacting the Special District Mixed
Use Bluffs at Point Cross Zone, which entitled the property to 50 townhome units and at least
62,000 square feet of commercial space. The project approval also included the Site Plan, which
placed the townhome development on the western portion ofthe property with commercial space
and parking interspaced throughout the rest ofthe property and along Porter Rockwell Boulevard.


The applicant completed some site work and grading and found there was a need for slope
stabilization and reduced the developable area by approximately 30%,resulting in a reduction in
overall entitled units and required commercial square footage. They proposed reducing the entitled
townhome units from 50 to 40 and the commercial space from 62,000 square feet to 40,000 square
feet. That reflects a 20% reduction in the residential component and a 30% reduction in the
commercial component, which is in line with the reduction in developable area.


The applicant provided an updated Site Plan for review. Ms. Tubbs noted that the overall layout
has not changed drastically. The townhome units are still located on the western side of the
property and the commercial uses are spread along Porter Rockwell Boulevard. The applicant has
also proposed a private park amenity near the townhomes as well as trail space and pedestrian
access through the commercial area to ensure that residents are able to safely access those spaces
without impeding traffic. Aerial views of the subject property in 2021 and 2023 were displayed
to indicate the efforts the applicant put in to stabilize the slope for development.

Staff recommended a provision be included that the applicant provide a revegetation plan as part
oftheir landscaping plan when Site Plans are submitted for the townhomes and commercial spaces.
Revegetation ofthe hillside areas will be required ifthey are impacted by grading or construction
ofthe project.


Notice ofthe public hearing was posted as required and no public comments were received. Staff
recommended that the Planning Commission hold a public hearing and consider forwarding a
positive recommendation to the City Council for the proposed Amendment.


Commissioner Swanson asked if the move from 62,000 to 40,000 square feet would preserve the
minimum language included in the original plan. Ms. Tubbs stated that the minimum requirement
will not be included. It will be entitled for approximately 40,000 square feet.

In response to a question from Commissioner Flynn, Ms. Tubbs clarified that the revegetation
standard is enacted as part ofthe Streets and Public Right-of-Way Ordinance, which specifies the
species ofgrass that must be planted on hillsides to retain those soils and keep them from moving.
It also outlines methods and timing. The Site Plan change does not affect the streets. There is a
reconfiguration ofthe street's curvature because the townhomes are being built farther to the east,
but accesses along Porter Rockwell Boulevard will not change.


Chair Cragun asked for confirmation that the new Site Plan meets all Code requirements.
Ms. Tubbs confirmed that it does, and Staff will re-verify that the Site Plan complies will all
adopted standards during Site Plan Review.


The applicant's representative, Clayton Rackham, thanked Ms. Tubbs for the presentation and
stated that she did a great job explaining the reasoning for the change. In response to a question
from Council Member Flynn, Mr. Rackham reported that they have not yet determined if the
townhomes will be rentals or for sale, but he believed there would be a mix.


Chair Cragun opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.

Commissioner Swanson stated that his intention in adding that language was to encourage the

has been used as an example ofhow Bluffdale is working to achieve fair housing,so both elements
ofthe project are important.

Ms.Tubbs presented the Staff Report regarding the proposed Text Amendments and indicated that
at least once each year, a series of Code updates is required based on actions taken in the most
recent legislative session. Senate Bill 43 created two classes of notices, Class A and Class B,in
an effort to streamline the system. Additionally, House Bill 256 requires cities and counties to
provide notice to military installations when they receive Land Use Applications within 5,000 feet
ofthe military boundary.


Class A notices require that notice be posted on the Utah Public Notice website, as well as
Bluffdale's official website. It also requires that the notice be posted in a public location. Bluffdale
posts such notices at City Hall. Depending on the application, a physical sign on the property may
also be required.


Class B notices have the same requirements as Class A notices, plus a mailing to affected entities
and all property owners within 1,000 feet of the subject property. Affected entities can include
utility or signage companies. For example,ifit is a Text Amendment it would affect where utilities
can be installed or signage configurations, notice would be sent to those entities.


At the previous Planning Commission Meeting, the Utah State Ombudsman reviewed
administrative versus legislative items, which correlate with Class A and Class B notices in that
Class A notices go for administrative items and Class B notices are for legislative applications
where a broader amount ofpublic input is desirable.


An additional change that Staff proposed as part ofthe amendments pertaining to the Conditional
Use process. Under state statute, conditional uses are considered an Administrative Land Use
action, and a public hearing is not required. The statute does not prohibit the City from holding a
public hearing if it so desires. However, since Bluffdale recently removed public hearing
requirements from administrative items such as Site Plans and subdivision plats. Staff proposed
the removal ofthe public hearing requirement for Conditional Use Permits as well.


With regard to providing notice to Camp Wiliams, the City Council recently adopted the West
Traverse Mountain Compatibility Area Study as Bluffdale's Compatible Use Plan, which governs

all properties within 5,000 feet of Camp Williams. When any Land Use Application is received,
excluding Building Permits but including Site Plans,Zone Map Amendments,and Special District
Amendments, notice will be provided to Camp Williams. They will then have a 90-day review
period in which the City cannot take action on those applications. Currently,the practice is for the
Utah Department of Veterans and Military Affairs to review the proposal and determine if they
consider it to be compatible with their military operations. If not, they provide feedback for the
City's consideration. By statute, they have up to 90 days, but the most recent notice was reviewed
within 18 days.


Ms. Tubbs presented a map showing all affected Bluffdale properties within 5,000 feet of Camp
Williams. It includes the southern half of Perry Farms, some of the gravel pits, and from
approximately 15200 South to the City's southern border. Any Land Use Applications received
for property within those areas will have the additional noticing requirement.

In summary. Staff revised the noticing table to include the Class A and Class B designations for
notices, proposed the removal of the public hearing requirement from the Conditional Use Permit
process, and added the Camp Williams notice to the noticing table, as well as various grammatical
changes.


Notice was posted in accordance with state law and Bluffdale City requirements and no public
input was received. Staff recommended the Planning Commission hold a public hearing and
consider forwarding a positive recommendation to the City Council.

In response to a question from Chair Cragun, Ms. Tubbs clarified that the Application is 2024-29
and the Ordinance is 2024-19.
