39          Discussion of Review Criteria for Development Permitting
40
41   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, discussed the role of homeowner
42   associations (HOAs) and the relationship between HOA rules and city ordinances. He
43   mentioned that generally, HOAs can establish rules that are stricter than city ordinances but
44   cannot create more lenient standards. He explained that the enforcement of HOA rules, often
45   referred to as Conditions, Covenants, and Restrictions (CC&Rs), is typically the responsibility of
46   the HOA itself.
47
48   Mr. Lethbridge provided examples of how different cities handle the notification of HOA
49   requirements during the building permit process. Park City requires a notification form to be
50   submitted with building permits but emphasizes that CC&R enforcement is the responsibility of
51   the HOA. South Jordan mandates architectural committee approval for certain areas like
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     October 3, 2023




 1   Daybreak. However, the majority of cities, as exemplified by Herriman, follow a hands-off
 2   approach, deferring CC&R enforcement to the HOA.
 3
 4   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, clarified the role of the City in cases involving development
 5   agreements, such as those with Suburban Land Reserve (SLR) or Daybreak. In these
 6   situations, he explained that the City's primary concern is ensuring that the necessary approvals
 7   have been obtained from the entity with design review authority over the property, whether it's
 8   SLR or Daybreak. The City's role is not to directly enforce CC&Rs on behalf of these entities but
 9   to confirm that the appropriate approvals have been granted. The City then evaluates the land
10   use application against the development standards outlined in the development agreement.
11
12   Mr. Lethbridge said that Riverton includes language on permits and the City's website, directing
13   residents to their mortgage documents, title reports, or the Salt Lake County Recorder's Office
14   to find information about their HOAs.
15
16   Mr. Carter said the responsibility lies with property owners to be aware of their CC&Rs, as the
17   City does not have the authority to enforce CC&Rs adopted by homeowner associations. He
18   mentioned that in some communities with CC&Rs in place, homeowners' associations may not
19   actively maintain their roles and responsibilities due to the associated costs, leading to the
20   burden of enforcement falling on homeowners.
21
22   Councilmember McDougal agreed with the idea that the City should not involve itself in disputes
23   related to CC&Rs since it lacks legal standing to do so. He expressed support for the language
24   that places the responsibility on homeowners. He raised a concern regarding the gap that often
25   exists between a development being completed and the homeowners' association (HOA) being
26   established. He noted that developers often make representations about architectural styles and
27   other aspects to the City, which leads to density approvals. Still, sometimes the developer
28   doesn't transition to an HOA efficiently. He emphasized the need to address this gap and
29   questioned how the City could manage this situation effectively.
30
31   Mr. Carter clarified that the homeowners' association (HOA) is created at the moment the
32   subdivision application plat is filed because the CC&Rs are filed with it. Typically, the developer
33   designates themselves as the board at that time. Developers often maintain control over the
34   HOA until the subdivision is complete because they want to avoid activist neighbors interfering
35   with their business. The transition from the developer to the residents is a mixed process, and it
36   varies from case to case, involving elections and other factors.
37
38   Councilmember McDougal mentioned that in Councilmember McCay's area, residents are
39   expressing a desire for more say in their HOA, but the developer has not transitioned control to
40   them. He inquired if there was any way to ensure that the initial transition from developer to
41   residents occurs, possibly tied to bond release or other mechanisms. In response, Mr. Carter
42   explained that ensuring a smooth transition from developer control to residents in an HOA could
43   be addressed through tools like development agreements. He recommended that, in cases
44   where developers promise specific architectural standards or home products with increased
45   density during the rezoning phase, the City should request a development agreement that
46   guarantees these standards. Development agreements can serve as contractual obligations for
47   developers to fulfill their promises, even if the property changes ownership. This approach could
48   substitute for relying solely on homeowner's associations to enforce architectural standards.
49
50   Mr. Lethbridge highlighted two scenarios regarding the transition of responsibility from
51   developers to homeowners associations. In gated communities where the HOA becomes
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   responsible for maintaining streets, this transition is typically made as the developer wants to
 2   relinquish these responsibilities. However, in subdivisions without common spaces or
 3   maintenance responsibilities, this transition may not occur smoothly. He mentioned that
 4   Riverton could work with development teams to ensure more effective handling of this transition,
 5   especially in subdivisions at different stages of development.
 6
 7   Councilmember Buroker pointed out that adding a notice on building permits might not
 8   effectively address the issue of homeowners not realizing they are part of an association until
 9   they want to build something that requires HOA approval. She also highlighted the importance
10   of development agreements, which can help maintain architectural standards even if the
11   property changes hands. She cited an example of a project on Redwood Road where such an
12   agreement was used to preserve the historic look of the area.

16   during the permitting process, further helping to address concerns and raise awareness about
17   their obligations in this regard.

19            Summary Report of Code Enforcement and Animal Control
20
21   Chief Blair Barfuss said that Deputy Chief Shane Taylor had been asked to speak about the
22   process of integrating Animal Services and Code Enforcement into the police department.
23
24   Deputy Chief Taylor discussed the various aspects of integrating Animal Services and Code
25   Enforcement into the police department. He mentioned the creation of standards and policies,
26   including uniforms, to guide code enforcement and Animal Control. He also highlighted efforts to
27   strengthen relationships with veterinary clinics and discussed the possibility of having Officer
28   Watson obtain the necessary certifications for euthanization services if needed.
29
30   Deputy Chief Taylor mentioned reaching out to neighboring police departments like Draper,
31   South Jordan, and Herriman to explore options for collaboration and shared facilities,
32   particularly for euthanization services. However, these options seemed limited due to the high
33   demand on their resources.
34
35   Regarding adoptions, Deputy Chief Taylor emphasized the importance of socializing animals
36   and spending time with them to improve their adoptability. He mentioned the challenges of
37   having to rely on Stone Ridge's availability and facilities for this purpose. He expressed the
38   belief that having their own shelter would greatly enhance their ability to interact with and
39   present animals for adoption successfully. Additionally, Deputy Chief Taylor discussed the
40   credentials required for euthanization and the need for secure storage of medications.
41
42   Mayor Staggs thanked Deputy Chief Taylor for providing that background context on the
43   situation with Animal Control Services and the partnership with Stone Ridge vet clinic. He said
44   it's clear that there are challenges related to euthanization, the length of time animals are being
45   housed, and the desire to maintain a no-kill status.
46
47   Chief Barfuss said the potential for a termination notice further underscores the need to explore
48   alternative solutions and potentially bringing Animal Control Services in-house. It's crucial to
49   ensure that the City can effectively and compassionately manage its animal services while
50   considering future growth.

44            Presentation to Riverton Community Arts Organization
45
46   Mayor Staggs explained that the Riverton Community Arts organization, a newly established
47   501(c)(3) nonprofit organization was created by Riverton residents to meet the diverse needs of
48   the local arts community. The existing Riverton Arts Council has focused more on theatrical
49   productions, making it challenging to access certain grants designated for broader arts
50   programs.
51
     Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     October 3, 2023




 1   Mayor Staggs said the goal is for Riverton Community Arts to replace the Riverton Arts Council
 2   as the formally recognized arts institution in the City. Formal recognition would grant Riverton
 3   Community Arts funding of $7,500 from the City's budget, as well as access to the auditorium
 4   and future facilities like the outdoor pavilion.
 5
 6   Scott Reed, Riverton Community Arts, explained that his extensive background in volunteering,
 7   especially with the Riverton Arts Council in his earlier years, has fueled his passion for
 8   community involvement through arts events and programs. His vision for Riverton Community
 9   Arts is to create a centralized hub that includes various art groups, such as orchestra, dance,
10   performing arts, and theater, with the aim of uniting these diverse arts within the community.
11
12   Mr. Reed clarified that Riverton Community Arts currently has four members, including himself.
13   After the council meeting, they will begin onboarding more members. The existing members
14   include Pam Henderson, who was present at the meeting, and Dave Martin and Dennis Page.
15   Mr. Reed expressed the group's excitement in contributing their expertise to the organization.
16   He mentioned that he had a productive discussion with members of the Riverton Arts Council to
17   alleviate any tensions. He believes that the structural changes and new regulations will provide
18   a better long-term solution for the community as the City continues to grow in a more scalable
19   format.
20
21   Councilmember Pierucci asked what the vision was for programming and whether they planned
22   to attract art programs to the City. In response, Mr. Reed emphasized that the main goal of
23   Riverton Community Arts is not to become a professional organization. Their aim is to provide
24   opportunities for individuals who are not professionals in the arts, allowing them to explore their
25   passions and potentially develop their skills.
26
27   Councilmember Pierucci also asked about sources of funding for the organization and whether
28   they would seek additional funding besides the money from the City. Mr. Reed said that there
29   are various grants available, including the ZAP grant, which Riverton Community Arts plans to
30   pursue. Some grants may require the organization to be formally recognized before they can
31   apply. The goal is to expand the organization's programming and improve its portfolio to
32   enhance its chances of securing funding through grants and partnerships.
33
34   Councilmember Pierucci said he wanted a clearer understanding of the organization's mission,
35   vision, and a strategic plan before granting formal recognition and potential funding. Mr. Reed
36   said Riverton Community Arts has a mission statement that he could forward to the Council.
37
38   Mayor Staggs thanked Mr. Reed for all of his efforts in organizing the Riverton Community Arts.

28            Resolution No. 23-78 – Recognizing the Riverton Community Arts, a Non-Profit
29            Corporation, as a Partner in Providing Arts Programs to Riverton City Residents
30
31   Councilmember Pierucci raised a question about how the funding and payment process would
32   work upon recognizing Riverton Community Arts. He was seeking clarification on whether the
33   organization would be authorized to access a pool of funds, or if they would need to submit
34   requests for funding in a formal process.
35
36   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, shared information about the City’s historical
37   approach to funding such organizations. He explained that typically, the funding is provided at
38   the end of the organization's fiscal period after they have shown how the money has been
39   spent. He also mentioned that in this particular case, as it's a new organization, he would
40   request a budget and follow up at the end of the fiscal period to ensure the funds were used as
41   intended. He emphasized the importance of accountability and ensuring that funds are not
42   accumulated but rather spent as intended.
43
44   Councilmember McCay asked if the City would still be funding other arts organizations. In
45   response Sheril Garn, Events and Operations Director, explained that the Jazz band would
46   continue to receive their allocated $2,500 from the current year's budget. The Riverton
47   Community Arts organization would receive the $7,500 instead of the Riverton Arts Council, but
48   they would collaborate on projects and share resources, which would help the new organization
49   as they apply for grants and show financial stability.
50
51   Mr. Carter said the resolution has a two-fold effect. It recognizes a new group while
     Page | 9 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     October 3, 2023




 1   discontinuing recognition of the old one. This means that funding will not be allocated to the old
 2   organization. However, the jazz band is a separate and independent group, so it remains
 3   unaffected by these changes.
 4
 5   Councilmember McDougal said the decision to recognize the Riverton Community Arts
 6   organization has been a result of a comprehensive process and discussions over a significant
 7   period. The changes are intended to provide greater oversight and a more comprehensive
 8   approach to meeting the diverse arts and cultural needs of the residents. This shift in focus will
 9   enable the City to better align with its vision and serve a wider range of residents.
