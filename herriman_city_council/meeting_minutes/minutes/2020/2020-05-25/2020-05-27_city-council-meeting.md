    2.1. Discussion regarding Rio Tinto Dust Mitigation Efforts - Piper Rhodes, Rio Tinto
         Communications Manager and Samantha Julian, Rio Tinto External Affairs Senior Advisor
Rio Tinto Senior Advisor of Communications Virginia Alexander introduced herself and stated she worked on
the communications team with Rio Tinto Communications Manager Piper Rhodes. She stated she helped
support the mine team that would be offering input during the presentation.

Rio Tinto Mine Manager Thomas Francis went through the dust mitigation strategy. He stated aside from the
safety of the operators, nothing was more important to him as the Manager than mitigating dust. He stated they
had a number of pieces of equipment and resources, including an on-site water supply. Over the years they had
used other additives to help with dust suppression, which they monitored on an annual basis. He thanked
Council for the opportunity to present and stated this project was high on their priority list.

Rio Tinto Mine Affiliate Tyler Jolley referenced a slide in the PowerPoint Presentation to explain water strategy
areas and priorities, which included Butterfield canyon. He stated they had eight water trucks running
throughout the pit and mine, and each truck held around 50,000 gallons of water. It took them 10 to 15 minutes
to top off at the watering pump stations, and each truck could run with a full water tank for a little under an
hour. He identified the priority areas and explained they were breaking it into two separate areas: upper and a
lower-pit area, where they could prioritize trucks to handle the dust. He explained they had close access to water
near visible dust areas, which he identified using the aid of an aerial map. They had also built a temporary water
stand at 6190 West in case they ran into any issues near the upper pit closer to the front dumps.

Mr. Tyler Jolley stated they used chemical dust suppression throughout the area. They used a minimal amount
consisting of a surfactant similar to a dish soap that was mixed directly into the water trucks as part of the
refueling process. This surfactant helped keep the water on the roads for a little bit longer. He stated the other
chemical suppressant they used was magnesium chloride. Using the aid of an aerial map, he identified a main
area that was a large gravel in front of the mine they would hit with magnesium chloride. He explained they
were planning a big project and there was a lot of work going on right off of U-111, for which they had brought
in several contractors and equipment. They were in the process of getting the area on their list for magnesium
chloride because of it being a high traffic area. He stated one downside with magnesium chloride was after it
rained it would start to lose its life; therefore, it had to be reapplied quote regularly. They used chemicals all
over the pit so they could apply surfactant from any of the water stand areas.

Mr. Tyler Jolley stated as part of their license to operate, they had explored options using cameras. However,
they also found that having someone down in the valley right off of U-111 was more successful than cameras
because that person could monitor the dust. On an aerial map, he identified Gate 47 between the Jordan Landfill
and 11800 South off of U-111. From the vantage point the dust spotter could see almost the entire mine face
and what was visible to the rest of the valley. He noted there was a second place by the security area where
employees could meet and where cameras were installed. He stated the dust spotters arrived at 9:00 in the
morning and were there until sunset monitoring the area. The spotters ran a log of conditions every hour.
Additionally, there was also a log kept by dispatchers of all calls received from spotters or outside sources. Both
of these logbooks helped employees respond to any issues that came up. For example, that year they had seen
May 27, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                       Page 3 of 13
an increase in high temperatures and winds and had shut down some areas that the trucks were running to
minimize some of the dust.

Mr. Jolley explained they were looking more into a technology-based camera system, and they had worked with
other mines to explore running filters through thermal imaging to pick up dust and create alarms and triggers.
He then referenced a flow chart to further address the dust mitigation process. He explained it was every
employee’s responsibility to help monitor and minimize the dust.

Mr. Jolley stated there was a process flow for outside of the mine. They had a team that handled all
environmental issues, including management of a hotline and community relations. He explained they handled
calls and got a hold of reduction control. Anytime they received a call, they would shut down the areas, log the
occurrence, and take pictures of the area. He stated if they got a dust complaint, they would do a full investigation
to see if there was anything they had missed or an area they could improve.

    10.1. Discussion and Consideration of a Resolution adopting and approving a Public Improvement
          District (PID) Policy - Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae explained the Public Improvement District (PID) policy would create a public
infrastructure district that would allow a certain area, if it were created, to assess taxes for infrastructure. He
asked the Council if they had any additional questions.

City Attorney Chase Andrizzi supported the drafted policy that was proposed by Finance Director Alan Rae. It
allowed the City to come up with its own procedure for applicants to follow within the City. The policy drafted
allowed the City to exercise discretion and help applicants understand the process they needed to follow. The
Act provided the City Council ultimate discretion to approve or deny a public infrastructure district. Having a
policy allowed the City to whittle away at bad applications and could save those applicants significant time and
money when submitting an application that would not be approved. The policy did not require Council to
approve anything that came to them, but they were willing to have a discussion based on conditions and
parameters set forth in their policy.

Councilmember Shields said a concern from the previous meeting was that this policy was a bit of a “pet project”
for the Utah Legislature. There was a feeling if a policy were adopted, the State could alter the parameters of
the policy, undermining the policy they put in place. City Attorney Chase Andrizzi said the Act as currently
drafted meant the City had discretion. If it were amended, the City could implement changes. The
recommendation was based on the current language; the legislature would still get absolute discretion over their
jurisdiction. Councilmember Shields asked if it also granted the City discretion to repeal a policy completely, to
which Attorney Andrizzi answered in the affirmative. The policy indicated it could be changed at any time and
was in line with the statutory language of the act.

Councilmember Ohrn noted this sounded like a policy that they did not have to follow because it could be
altered easily; this seemed like it was guiding document. City Attorney Chase Andrizzi said staff could be
instructed to narrow down the scope of parameters the City would be willing to consider. They could provide
additional information in the policy and State language could also be changed. Councilmember Ohrn felt it was
strange legislation in the first place. The constitutionality seemed off and she was concerned by the taxation
without representation context of the policy. City Attorney Chase Andrizzi said this was part of the District act
and Councilmember Ohrn was not alone in this concern. This was something that could be addressed in the
governing documents. If this were approved by the City Council, they could move parameters to have certain
governances and taxation rights that could address some of those concerns.

Councilmember Shields noted an important aspect of PID was that the City was not necessarily involved after
initial set-up. A few ideas presented were that the City would retain a small portion of property inside a PID to
allow the City to retain representation. He asked if there was a way in the governing documents to require a
representative of the City to be involved on the board. City Attorney Chase Andrizzi said it could go either way
and was unprecedented territory in the governing documents. They could include it as a requirement if the
applicant agreed.
