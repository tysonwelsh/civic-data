Proposed Ordinance 2025-2 amending the Ogden Municipal Code by
amending Chapter 4-2A to revise provisions relating to contracts; by
amending Chapter 4-2B to revise provisions relating to contracting for
professional services; and by making technical changes
         A memo from the Management Services Department regarding proposed purchasing code amendments came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed changes to the City’s procurement code would increase thresholds that
determine when a formal bid process is required. The proposed threshold changes affect bid limits for the purchase of general
goods and services, building improvements, public works projects, and professional service contracts. The Administration is
recommending the proposed amendments to account for inflationary pressures on the city’s purchasing power and to align with
some state code requirements or thresholds. The City’s procurement code has not been updated in decades.
         Purchasing Coordinator Puertollano summarized the memo and stated the City’s procurement code has not been updated
for decades; proposed amendments to the code account for current market conditions and will effectively enhance the City’s
purchasing power that aligns with the State of Utah Code and purchasing threshold levels of surrounding municipalities. The
amendments will also create greater efficiencies in the procurement process. She used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
discuss the City’s current procurement code and highlight language changes to the ordinance; review the State’s purchasing
requirements and benchmark data for other cities; and to detail the proposed dollar amount changes within the procurement code.
She concluded the primary goals of the proposed amendments are:
              • Create greater efficiencies while conducting a responsible procurement process.
              • Balance the need for flexibility and speed with the need for fairness, transparency, and good stewardship of
                  public funds.
              • Time saved in completing previous purchasing formalities will give opportunity to address other operational
                  needs.
         Ms. Puertollano noted the expectation is that the approved updates will positively impact policies and procedures and
improve the City’s overall procurement and contracting process. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information
packet for the meeting.
         Vice Chair White thanked Ms. Puertollano for the manner in which she explained the rationalization behind increasing
the purchasing thresholds and for the extensive work done with the committee assembled to study this issue.
         Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-2, entitled:
                  “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Chapter 4-2a to revise
                  provisions relating to contracts; by amending Chapter 4-2b to revise provisions relating to contracting for
                  professional services; by making technical changes; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective
                  immediately upon posting after final passage.”

Public Comments.
          Andrea Baltazar thanked Council members and City staff for meeting with residents to address concerns regarding the
recently completed 20th Street reconstruction project.
          Maris Hogue also expressed gratitude to City officials for their willingness to listen to residents living on 20 th Street
regarding their concerns about the elimination of on-street parking in front of their homes.
          Robert Hunter expressed gratitude to Council staff for publishing the communication plan regarding the Weber Housing
Authority (WHA) permanent housing project; he emphasized that there is $2.6 million of funding that is at stake, as well as the
potential loss of an operating subsidy and American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds if the project is not approved and commences
in a timely manner. He added that the property that has been chosen is the most financially feasible property because it already has
a structure that can be reused; other properties will require complete construction of a facility, which will be much more expensive
and take much longer.
          Heath Satow also thanked Council staff for the communications plan regarding the WHA resolution; however, he
expressed disappointment in last-minute delays, citing difficulty in attracting businesses and developers to Ogden due to frequent
changes.
          Curtis Smith stated he does not reside in Ogden, but some of his youth sports group members do and he was just chosen
as a spokesperson for them. They have noticed that there has been a decline in youth sports participation that's affecting academic
lives and social behavior, especially of those that are in high school and elementary school. He asked what community resources,
if any, are present to combat this challenge, what can be done, and, more specifically, what the public can do to contribute to the
solution.
          Linda Sanchez offered the Council a ‘shout out’ for participating in a recent town hall meeting; she acknowledged that
the Council is often criticized by the public, but she does appreciate them and asked them to consider that those who are vocal
about City issues are simply trying to advocate for themselves or an entity they represent. She stated many are often frustrated and
they do not know how to express themselves in a positive way.
          Mike Jordan thanked the Council for recognizing public servants earlier in the meeting. He then stated that he owns a few
lots on 24th Street and A Avenue and has maintained them for over a decade. In May of last year, he cut the alfalfa, but he later
became ill with COVID, and it took him a while to recover. He received civil penalties for his properties, which he appealed but
never received a response. He eventually received an invoice for a large amount of money in December. He is hopeful the City
will consider the special circumstances for his case, noting that he did not receive adequate notification of the issues at his
property, and he does not live in Ogden. He stated that he does try hard to maintain his property, but he hopes the City will follow
the lead of South Ogden City, which considers hardships such as a health condition.
          Laura Lewis also addressed the WHA permanent housing project and stated she still does not have a clear understanding
of how the issue has gotten to this point. She has heard some say that the project has been in the works for five years and she does
not know how that much time and effort has been spent on the matter only to come to the current situation of some feeling that the
selected property is not the right place. She then stated last week there were concerns about the imposition of different rules
pertaining to the time limits on the public who is addressing the Council. The Rules of Order state that each member of the public
has three minutes to address the Council, but an arbitrary decision was made to limit speakers to one -minute and that seems
unfair. She then addressed wildfires in California, which caused her to think about instances in the past when fire hydrants in her
neighborhood were drained due to a water problem. She asked what precautions the City is taking to ensure that something like
what happened in California cannot happen in Ogden.

Council member Comments
          Chair Richey addressed Ms. Lewis’s comments about wildfire risk in Ogden and noted the Council has asked the Fire
Chief to attend a future meeting to discuss these issues with the Council and the public. He then stated that according to State
statute, the City Council is only required to allow public input during a public hearing and that is what the three-minute rule
applies to. However, the Council values public input and when they invite public input, they are not required to allow three
minutes. The Rules of Procedure state the Council may allow up to three minutes, which means that less time may be afforded to
each speaker. Regarding the Weber Housing Authority (WHA) permanent housing project, the Council has already heard a great
deal of public input and did not feel it necessary to allow each person to speak for another three minutes. City Attorney Williams
added that the manner in which the Council handled the public input period last week was consistent with what other governing
bodies are doing; the Chair has the authority to run meetings in an effective and efficient manner and that is what he did last week.
He added that it may be helpful to clarify the Rules of Order language regarding public input to ensure that the public has clear
expectations. Chair Richey then addressed the timeline of the WHA project; the WHA may have been working on the project for
years, but the City Council was only notified of the project in November of 2024. WHA had the expectation that they could move
forward with the project, but Council staff discovered a requirement for the City Council to grant approval of the project. The
Council wanted to have a full understanding of the project to learn of the impacts it could have on the community. He looks
forward to continued dialogue with all interested parties and stakeholders before a decision is made. He then addressed Mr.
Jordan’s comments and asked Administration to reach out to him to get more information about his situation and provide
guidance. He then asked Mayor Nadolski to respond to Mr. Smith’s comments about recreation opportunities in the community.
Mayor Nadolski stated that the City has seen a recent increase in youth recreation participation, but one of the biggest struggles
the City has is limited gymnasium space, which requires a cap on participation in certain programs. He added the City is always
looking for high caliber individuals to mentor and coach participants in the City’s recreation programs. He added Administration
is preparing for an upcoming work session meeting with the Council to discuss wildfire risks in the community, and he has invited
Public Services Director Anderson to also attend that meeting to address the Council regarding public infrastructure improvements
that have helped the City to be more prepared for a significant fire event.
          Council members expressed gratitude to City staff for responding to the concerns of residents who live on 20 th Street and
for facilitating a tour of the project area for Council members who were interested in learning more about the situation.

Mayor Comments
          Mayor Nadolski thanked the members of the public who took the time to come to tonight’s meeting to express gratitude
for a response they have received from the City or for the positive manner in which elected officials are representing them. He
stated that elected and appointed officials commonly hear negative feedback, and it is nice to hear positive feedback. He then
stated that the Weber Housing Authority (WHA) permanent housing project has been in the planning stage for some time, but
anyone involved in the project must admit there has been a poor relationship between the City and the WHA in the past and the
City was not involved in the years of planning the project. He is trying to do things differently and improve the relationship with
WHA to ensure that something similar does not occur again in regard to future projects. He added he does not fault the Council
for exercising their authority to examine the project in greater detail and withholding their approval until all questions are
answered and more study of the subject property and other optional properties can be completed.
