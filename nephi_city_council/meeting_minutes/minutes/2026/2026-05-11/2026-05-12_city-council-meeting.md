   1. WILDLAND URBAN INTERFACE DOCUMENTS (WUI)
         a. City Administrator, Seth Atkinson, stated that staff met with Thomas Peterson, the
             Central Area Manager for the state with the Utah Division of Natural Resources. He sent
             the documents necessary for the city to participate in the state’s Wildland Urban
             Interface program.
         b. With recent changes in state laws, municipalities are held responsible for fire protection
             rather than special service districts. This means that the city needs to be the entity to
             sign a cooperative agreement with the state to ensure that wildfire suppression efforts
             continue to be funded through the state rather than the Juab County Fire Protection
             Special Service District.
         c. Because this agreement will place responsibilities on the city that would normally be
             placed upon the fire district, staff advises that prior to signing anything with the state,
             that an agreement be drafted and approved by the Fire Board to perform all the services
             and fund any requirements found in the cooperative agreement.
         d. In addition to the cooperative agreement, the city can draft its own Wildland Urban
             Interface or WUI map. This map would be for different purposes than the state’s WUI
             map. While the state’s map is used for insurance purposes, the city’s WUI map would
             determine whether a structure would need to follow certain building codes.
         e. The council agreed to have staff draft an agreement with the fire district and work with
             the local fire chief to create a city WUI map.
   2. POLICE STATION PROJECT AND POLICE STAFFING DISCUSSION
         a. Mr. Atkinson explained the police project status. The deal to use an existing building as a
             shell for the police station fell through resulting in the need to build and thus increasing
             costs to fund the project.
         b. The council expressed concern that with increased costs, the funds were now allocated
             to the police station project only and not funding additional police officers. The
             statement was made that a new building does not improve public safety—officers do.
         c. Discussion included suggestions to lower costs for the building construction:
                   i. Build the building for functional purposes and not aesthetics and save costs
                      through different materials and design;
                  ii. Use a phased approach to the project by building a shell and then finishing the
                      interior as needed;

   3. FIXTURES FOR PARK BATHROOMS
          a. The bathroom fixtures at the city park were vandalized and need replacement. The sinks
             have been replaced multiple times due to vandalism.
          b. In the FY 2026-27 Budget the Council had a goal of installing steel fixtures in the park
             bathrooms to discourage vandalism. It was an unfunded request, so it was dependent on
             surplus funding from the prior fiscal year end. Those surplus funds were used up in
             previously approved operational requests and subsidies to the Hive. The current fiscal
             year shows signs that there will be an operational surplus. There will also be
             contributions from the PAR tax to parks also. Cameras have been installed and are being
             used to find the perpetrator of the most current vandalism.
          c. The council discussed the option of replacing fixtures in all city owned park bathrooms.
             The last quote for installing these fixtures in all the city parks was approximately
             $10,000. Putting automatic locks on all park bathroom doors had a similar cost. The idea
             was presented to replace the damaged sink, install automatic locks and then phase the
             replacement of the fixtures to steel. The council considered replacing the cameras if
             they were not working or relocating them to be more helpful.
   4. ROUNDABOUT ON 400 EAST AND 700 NORTH
          a. The council had wanted to revisit the topic of a roundabout near the jr. high and high
             school at the intersection at 400 East and 700 north. The topic had been discussed at a
             previous work session. Mr. Atkinson summarized what was discussed previously.
          b. The mayor and council members proposed holding a public meeting to discuss the
             suggestion and its potential impact. It was recommended that a traffic impact study be
             conducted and additional right-of-way be acquired if necessary.
          c. The benefits and challenges of implementing a roundabout were mentioned. Support of
             the school district would be helpful in gaining community support. The staff and council
             want to involve the school’s art department in designing a structure for the center.
          d. Next steps were recommended to move forward with the potential project. A traffic
             study will be conducted at the beginning of next school year since the current school
             year is ending next week. That will allow an accurate account to be collected during peak
             use.
   5. DENSITY DISCUSSION
          a. Developers have been requesting higher densities in various zones. This is due to the
             housing pressures that exist in Utah and the difficulty in finding a supply of housing for
             younger generations.
          b. Previous Planning Commissions and City Councils have been clear about keeping density
             limits and even reducing density in the city’s residential zones.

   6. CITIZEN’S ACADEMY CURRICULUM
          a. Mayor Seely would like to do a citizen’s academy to educate the public about city
              services and operations.
          b. The proposal included a series of short videos that people could watch and take a quiz
              on. Results could be brought in and then having a graduation ceremony for participants.
          c. The need for better public engagement and education through social media and in-
              person session was emphasized. The council talked about using the 4th and 5th Tuesdays
              when there is no council meetings for in-person sessions. The group discussed the
              coordination of implementing the Citizen’s Academy, including staff time and involving
              high school students who are part of the Center for Advanced Professional Studies
              (CAPS) program.
   7. ADMINISTRATIVE AND COUNCIL UPDATES
          a. Mr. Atkinson raised concerns about broadcasting work sessions, suggesting it might
              cause unnecessary confusion. The council members support broadcasting to ensure
              transparency but acknowledged the potential for public misinterpretation due to
              discussion items that are not action items. Further discussion ensued concerning the
              importance of maintaining an open forum for public comment and the potential for
              recessing meetings if they become disruptive. Experiences shared about other cities
              highlighted our need for balanced public engagement and transparency.
          b. The National Guard open house is on June 2 at 2:00 pm.
          c. Ms. Cowan announced the ribbon cutting ceremony for Beans and Brews for Saturday,
              May 16 at 11:00 am.
          d. Ms. Cowan attended the Miss Nephi workshop last night. There are thirteen contestants:
              six for Miss Nephi and seven for Miss Nephi Teen.
          e. Craig Oswald, Recreation Director announced that the city was approved for the CPR
              grant totaling $150,000. The grant will be used to replace the lights at the Orgill Ball
              Fields.
          f. Mr. Oswald stated the Bell family used one of the wheelchairs to take their daughter on
              a hike. They had sent pictures and said the wheelchair worked great.
