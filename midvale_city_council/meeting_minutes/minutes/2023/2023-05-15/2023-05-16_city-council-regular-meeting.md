       A.     CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO.2O23-R-19 AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR
              TO EXECUTE A 2023 TRCC INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT WITH SALT
              LAKE COUNTY FOR CANAL TRAILS IMPLEMENTATION
Kate Andrus said in July 2022, the Redevelopment Agency applied for funding in the
amount of $240,000.00 through the Salt Lake County Tourism, Recreation, Culture and
Convention (TRCC) Support Program to fund the development of bicycle/pedestrian trails
along the East Jordan and Jordan & Salt Lake City Canal right of ways as part of its goals
to improve active transportation in the City. Midvale City has been awarded grant funds
from the Transportation lnvestment Fund (TlF) Active Funding of $600,000.00. The
requested $240,000.00 awarded through the TRCC Support Program will provide the
required matching funds Midvale City will need to move this project fonarard. Furthermore,
the Canal Trails Feasibility Study is taking place, and the results will guide the design and
construction of the two noted trails.

Salt Lake County approved Midvale's grant request for the project in December 2 022 and
committed $240,000.00 towards it. To receive the grant funds, Midvale City must enter
into an interlocal agreement with Salt Lake County that specifies the terms and conditions
for using the funds and reporting expenses. The attached interlocal agreement outlines
the substantive terms of the grant, including the grant amount, period of performance,
scope of project, and method of disbursement. The agreement has been reviewed by the
Legal Department and requires the Mayor's signature upon approval by the City Council.
The interlocal agreement includes the following substantive terms:
       . Grant Amount: $240,000.00
       . Period of Performance: March 31 , 2023 - March 31 , 2024
       . Scope of Project: The development of bicycle/pedestrian trails along the East
       Jordan and Jordan & Salt Lake City Canal right of ways.
       . Method of Disbursement: Lump sum reimbursement

FISCAL IMPACT:
The project's total estimated cost is $840,000.00. Midvale City was awarded $600,000.00
in TIF Active Funding with the requirement that Midvale City match $240,00.00. The
matching funds of $240,000.00 are being funded by the TRCC Support Program and will
be provided as a lump sum reimbursement. The budget for the Midvale City Canal Trails
project has been included in Midvale City's draft Fiscal Year 2024 budget.

Adam Olsen said the canal trail study will come before the Council at the June 6th during
the City Council work session. At that time, the study will be close to wrapping up and
then will move onto the next phase.

Mayor Stevenson asked what would happen if the study is not feasible.

Kate Andrus said if the City is not able to fulfill the obligations, therefore would not be able
to expend the money.

council Member Bryant Brown said what if some of the studies come back feasible for
some cities and not feasible for other cities.

Nate Rockwood said UDOT has done a generalized feasibility study. They determined
that it ls feasible, which is why the grant money was awarded. The tricky part of this
process is to deal with all the different canal companies.

Matt Dahl said if the City doesn't do a feasibility study and something goes wrong, then
residents can come back to the City and ask why the City didn't look into this before
making any decisions.

Council Member Dustin Gettel asked what the $840,000 goes towards.

Kate Andrus said that money goes to the actual construction of the trail.

Council Member Dustin Gettel asked what the cost of the feasibility study is.

Nate Rockwood said the study is about $75,000 and the City's portion of the study was
about $6,000. The $840,000 is nowhere near what the total trail will cost. The study and
design work will allow the City to continue to get more money. The trail will eventually
lead to other trails and connect to other cities. The state will continue to award money for
the trails.

Council Member Bryant Brown said he is hoping to keep the trails safer.

Mayor Stevenson asked about money to complete the trail

Nate Rockwood said through the feasibility study, the City will need to prioritize areas of
the trail. There will be phases.

Council Member Dustin Gettel said he is in favor of improving trails; he just wants to make
sure the money is being spent wisely.
