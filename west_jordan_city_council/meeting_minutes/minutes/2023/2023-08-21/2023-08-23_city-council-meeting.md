 7. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Ordinance No. 23-22 adopting the property tax rate for tax year 2023.
          Council Member Green said he could support a 3% inflationary property tax increase, but
          not a 4.8% increase. He expressed the opinion that truth in taxation did not make much
          sense. Council Member Whitelock felt 4.8% was more than what was needed. Council
          Member Worthen said she could not support a property tax increase with “wants”
          included in the budget. Council Member Green commented that a reduction to 3% would
          simply reduce the contribution to CIP.

           Council Member Jacob expressed support for an annual inflationary tax increase and
           commented that a 4.8% increase was less than the inflation rate in the last year. He said
           he would support the proposed budget as presented. Chair McConnehey said he would
           want any adjustment to the proposed property tax increase to be meaningful and
           impactful. He expressed the opinion that a small adjustment would not be felt or noticed
           by residents but would be felt in terms of the services the City was able to provide. Chair
           McConnehey said he wanted the City to be able to provide services the residents were
           asking for and expressed support for the proposed property tax rate.

           MOTION: Chair McConnehey moved to APPROVE Ordinance No. 23-22 adopting a
                   .001499 property tax rate for tax year 2023.
                   Vice Chair Bloom seconded the motion.

           Council Member Pack said he was in favor of the increase to be able to provide City
           services and keep good employees with institutional knowledge, and to avoid kicking the
           can down the road. He said he preferred regular incremental adjustments over huge
           occasional adjustments. Council Member Pack commented that the West Jordan tax rate
           was lower than most cities.

           Council Member Green said he believed the growth of government and the budget needed
           to be moderated and limited. He commented that property tax was a regressive tax that
           hit those on fixed incomes and said he would vote against the motion.

           Council Member Jacob spoke of market forces and increased costs for goods and
           materials, and said he was in favor of the proposed tax rate to support needed capital
           projects.

       b. Ordinance No. 23-23 adopting the Final Budget for Fiscal Year 2024
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck explained three changes to the FY 2024
          Budget since last reviewed by the Council. Council Member Green said he was not in favor
          of the proposed budget nor the sales tax revenue projection. Council Member Green noted
          that the number of staff members grew faster than the City’s revenue. He believed growth
          in government needed to be kept in check. He did not like the way the wage scale had
          been “fixed”. Council Member Green felt that while there were things in the budget he did
          not like, he understood the bad had to be taken with the good, and the City needed to
          continue to operate. He commented that he would probably vote in favor of the budget
          but felt the Council would need to be careful moving forward. He suggested for FY 2025
          that the Council consider eliminating the Street Light Fund transfer and consider directly
          using Enterprise Funds for streets and roads, water, and public safety. Council Member
          Green agreed with Council Member Jacob that the Council should start discussions
          regarding tax policy in January.

           Council Member Worthen said she would vote against the FY 2024 Final Budget. She
           emphasized that her dissenting vote was not directed at staff. Council Member Worthen
           did not believe building a cultural arts building was a good investment for the City as it
           stood financially. She noted that there was no plan in place for ongoing maintenance
           expenses of a it, and although she was not against the idea of a cultural arts building, she
           did not believe construction was financially responsible without a better plan in place.

           Council Member Pack commented that he knew an increase in property taxes would
           impact property owners, as well as renters through increased rent. He said it was hard to
           make a decision without factoring in all contributing information. Council Member Pack
           believed the City did not exist as an entity to make money but was there to provide
           amenities. He commented that constructing a community arts building now would cost
           less than waiting considering the rising costs of construction, and would vote in favor of
           the FY 2024 Budget.
