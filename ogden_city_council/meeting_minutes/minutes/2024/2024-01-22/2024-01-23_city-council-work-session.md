        The purpose of the work session was to discuss a proposed zone text amendment regarding group dwellings; discuss
Council business, including a Council Code of Conduct and video captioning; and hear Council comments.

Zone Text Amendments – Group Dwellings
          Senior Planner Rypien explained petitioner Mike Bastian has submitted a petition to update the City’s group dwelling
regulations. The City’s group dwelling ordinance, Chapter 10 of the City’s zoning code, provides standards for the development of
multiple residential units on a single lot. This is different from the subdivision process of developing one unit on a single lot. The
City’s group dwelling ordinance provides standards for three types of group dwellings. This includes small lot group dwellings,
neighborhood group dwellings, and mixed-use group dwellings. The petition requests that several specific elements of the Code be
amended. These include reducing the square footage calculation for determining density and reducing the setbacks to those that
are more consistent with other residential zones. The proposed amendments incorporate changes that are intended to meet the
petitioned request as well as other elements of the development code. The Council reviewed the proposal at the work session of
January 9, 2024. After discussion, the Council requested an additional work session to review the changes related to building type
requirements. Mr. Rypien used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to address the questions asked by the Council during the
January 9, 2024, work session, including:
               • Should we require a variety of building types?
                         o Staff favors a variety of building types/styles/architectural elements/color schemes.
               • What should rear-to-rear building separation be?
                         o Staff recommends a 30-foot rear-to-rear building separation.
               • How many trees are required for open space?
                         o The current formula is two trees per unit and five five-gallon shrubs per unit.
               • Should we consider an adjustment to the architectural detailing requirements?
                         o Staff recommends requiring three architectural detailing elements rather than four, which is the
                              current requirement. This is due to the fact that a front porch is always required and that has been
                              considered to be an architectural detailing element.
          For a copy of the PowerPoint presentation in its entirety see the information packet.
          Discussion among the Council, staff, and Mr. Bastian centered on the items that make up the list of optional architectural
detailing elements; whether the rear-to-rear building separation will be adjusted based upon building heights; and the difference
between a building type and building style. The Council concluded they were comfortable proceeding with consideration and
possible action on the proposed ordinance amendments. Council Deputy Director Symes indicated he will work with Planning
staff to address the issues that have been raised regarding the proposal and schedule the item for action on an upcoming Council
agenda.

Council Business
          Council Code of Conduct: Council Policy Analyst Burton explained that at the request of a Council member, staff has
conducted research regarding the possibility of the Council adopting a code of conduct. Mr. Burton used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to present a model code of conduct and compare it to the Council’s current Rules and Norms. For a copy of the
PowerPoint presentation in its entirety see the information packet.
          The Council engaged in discussion regarding whether to pursue a code of conduct in addition to the current Rules and
Norms document; they ultimately concluded a code of conduct document should be explored further.
          Video Captioning: Council Deputy Director stated that Council staff received a request to provide video captioning
during Council meetings; the captioning was provided, but Council staff felt it would be appropriate to determine if this is a
service the Council wishes to provide for all future meetings. The Council broadly voiced support for the captioning and thanked
Council staff for looking into the matter.
          Winter parking ordinance: Chief Administrative Officer Brown provided a report of the City’s efforts to communicate
the changes to the winter parking ordinance to residents and business owners; she also noted that the City has worked with the
Ogden Downtown Alliance to communicate to all businesses that it is their responsibility to keep the sidewalk in front of their
business cleared after a snowstorm.

Council Comments
         Council member White asked how Council staff and Administration keep track of requests for information that are
received during the public comment period of a meeting. Council staff and Ms. Brown indicated they both keep notes, but they
could develop a tracking mechanism to follow up on those requests.


         The meeting adjourned at 6:35 p.m.
