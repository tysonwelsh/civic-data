MEMORANDUM 
 
To:   Eastern Summit County Planning Commission   
From:   Jennifer Leslie, County Planner 
Date of Memo:  April 24, 2025 
Date of Meeting: May 1, 2025 
Type of Item:  Conditional Use Permit - Discussion, Possible Action 
Process:  Administrative Review 
File #:   25-047 
 
 
 
On April 17, 2025, the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission (ESCPC) reviewed a 
Conditional Use Permit (CUP) for Henefer Town’s secondary irrigation system, which consists of 
a water pipe, dam, and water storage reservoir located on Parcel NS-836-X (38.52 acres). The 
ESCPC opened and closed a public hearing during the meeting.  
 
Commissioners recognized the importance of the infrastructure project for the Town and 
hoped to move the project along quickly. However, there was insufficient documentation to 
show the potential risks to County infrastructure and residences if the dam were to break, 
overflow, or need to be drained. Without the documentation to outline the potential risks, the 
ESCPC was unable to condition the project in a way that could mitigate those risks. The ESCPC 
tabled the item to the next available meeting, May 1, 2025, to give the applicant time to 
provide additional information.  
 
Since the April 17
th meeting, the applicants have corresponded and met with County Staff. 
However, as of 12:00pm on the date of this Memo, the applicant did not provide the requested 
information. Therefore, the information included in the April 17th Staff Report is still the most 
current analysis of project. Should the applicant provide the requested information to Staff 
prior to the scheduled May 1
st ESCPC meeting, Staff will correct the agenda with an updated 
Staff Report.  
 
As of the date of this Memo, Staff recommends the following:  
 
Staff recommends that the ESCPC discuss the proposed  Conditional Use Permit  and 
vote to table the application until the applicant provides supplementary information to 
demonstrate compliance with all standards and public safety concerns as expressed in 
this staff report.

Proposal 
Henefer Town is proposing a diversion from the Weber River for secondary irrigation water. 
Water will be funneled from the Weber through an underground water pipe in the S. Henefer 
Rd. right of way (ROW) to a dam and storage reservoir located on Parcel NS-836-X. The project 
begins within the Town boundaries around Interstate 84 and goes into the unincorporated 
County where Old Ditch Rd. meets S. Henefer Rd.

Background 
Currently, Henefer residents use culinary water for irrigation. Because of water shortage 
concerns the Town had a moratorium on new building permits from 2018 through November 
2024. Henefer Town proposes a secondary water system that includes an underground water 
pipe, dam, and storage reservoir to free up the culinary water supply. The project straddles 
Henefer Town and the unincorporated County. Any proposed work within the unincorporated 
County is within Henefer’s Annexation Declaration Area.  
 
The dam, access to the dam, and most of the reservoir are proposed within the unincorporated 
County on Parcel NS-836-X, which is owned by the Town of Henefer. The proposed reservoir 
will hold about 3.5-acre feet of water at capacity. The dam will be accessed from Peaks Rd. by a 
proposed 12 ft. wide gravel road with 3’ shoulders. Peaks Rd. is a private road. A small portion 
of the reservoir will extend into Parcel PEAKS-AG, which is within Henefer Town.  
 
The water pipe will run from NS-836-X into the ROW of Peaks Rd. on an unnamed Parcel that 
includes the Henefer Cemetery. Then the pipe will follow the ROW of S. Henefer Rd. where it 
will cross into the Henefer Town boundary along Old Ditch Rd. on Parcel HT-142. Both S. 
Henefer Rd. and Old Ditch Rd. are County roads. The pipe will follow the Weber River within the 
Town along Parcels HT-142 and HT-142-B where it will terminate at a pumphouse on parcel HT-
846-1-X. Only work within the unincorporated County is subject to this Conditional Use Permit 
(CUP).
  
 
The proposed dam is classified in the Eastern Summit County Development Code (Code) as a 
Utility Structure and Related Facilities. The water pipe is considered an Underground 
transmission line exceeding 12 inches diameter. Both uses are considered Conditional Uses in 
the AG-5 Zone.  
 
Construction of the project began without appropriate County permits, and Summit County 
Code Enforcement has instructed all construction related to this CUP to stop until appropriate 
permits have been obtained. Additionally, Henefer intends to annex Parcel NS-836-X and the 
unnamed Parcel into the municipal boundaries. A complete annexation petition was received 
on April 4, 2025 for both properties (Exhibit F). 
 
The purpose of this CUP is to
 allow construction to begin again as soon as possible for the 
benefit of County and Henefer residents while the annexation is ongoing. Additionally, a 
portion of the project, particularly the water pipe along S. Henefer Rd., will remain in the 
unincorporated County even after the annexation is approved, therefore, the CUP is still 
necessary.  
 
The applicants have now obtained appropriate ROW Permits for work within all County ROWs 
from the Summit County Engineering Department. However, the Engineering Department 
raised public health and safety concerns surrounding the flooding potential and damage to 
properties within the unincorporated County should the dam break or the spillway channel be

Background 
 
The Promontory Development Agreement was approved on January 2, 2001. It provided for 
885 estate lots as part of a base density, 716 incentive density lots, two hotels/300 resort units, 
equestrian facility, up to five golf club houses, and other accessory uses on approximately 6,500 
acres with no less than 3,900 acres remaining as passive open space. Of the 1901 total units in 
the density pool, 1,481 units have been approved. The development parameters for this project 
are specifically set forth in the Development Agreement. 
 
Analysis and Findings 
 
The applicant is using density from the existing pool of density. All building pads have been 
located outside of critical lands.  
 
Due to a recent increase in the amount of disturbance per lot and to encourage better practices 
to design homes with the land rather than modifying land to suit the home, Promontory is 
implementing a limitation on the amount of grading that can take place within each building 
pad. While larger building pads do give site flexibility to applicants, the amount of grading will 
be limited. Promontory has adopted this in their Architectural Guidelines and all subdivisions 
moving forward will include plat notes for all future subdivisions.

Staff has received approval from the Promontory Conservancy for the proposal. 
 
Development Agreement Requirements 
 
The Development Agreement delegates decision making powers to the Board of County 
Commissioners for all Final Plats. With the change of government that took place, the former 
Board of County Commissioner’s administrative duties diverted to the County Manager and the 
legislative duties diverted to the County Council. The Final Plat is considered an administrative 
action. The Eastern Summit County Planning Commission remains as the recommending body.

recommendation to the Summit County Manager to approve the proposed Promontory Scenic 
Valley Final Subdivision Plat, according to the following Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law and

885 estate lots as part of a base density, 716 incentive density lots, two hotels/300 resort units, 
equestrian facility, up to five golf club houses, and other accessory uses on approximately 6,500

the density pool, 1,481 units have been approved. The development parameters for this project 
are specifically set forth in the Development Agreement.

Analysis and Findings 
 
The applicant is using density from the existing pool of density. All building pads have been 
located outside of critical lands.  
 
Due to a recent increase in the amount of disturbance per lot and to encourage better practices
