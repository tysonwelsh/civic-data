A. Kearns Right-of-Way Agreement
Nathan Bracken, the city attorney, initially sought clarification on which agreement was under
consideration, confirming it was the one involving Rocky Mountain Power. The agreement was
related to work being done at David Gourley Park, which is undergoing significant renovations
including new restrooms, pavilions, benches, and a playground. The agreement had not been
included in the council packet because it remained unresolved at the time. He outlined several
concerns with the proposed agreement, stating that it was heavily one-sided in favor of Rocky
Mountain Power and lacked key provisions typically included in such contracts. These
omissions included clauses on governmental immunity, liability, indemnity, and utility
relocation. He explained that the utility company had not been receptive to incorporating the
city’s suggested changes, likely because the project had been initiated by the city. Despite
these issues, he noted the agreement was legally sufficient, though it did not include all the
customary protections.
Council Member Tina Snow asked if approving the agreement would affect other projects, to
which Nathan Bracken clarified that it would apply only to the David Gourley Park project. She
expressed support for proceeding under that condition.

Curtis Woodward presented an ordinance amendment designed to address a gap in the

municipal code related to commercial vehicle and equipment rentals. He reviewed his

prepared presentation (Attachment A) and explained that while the code already included

provisions for commercial vehicle and equipment repair and sales, it lacked language

permitting rental businesses, which are similar in nature. This issue came to light following the

arrival of a successful heavy equipment rental company in the Camp Kearns area, prompting

the need to revise the code to ensure the business was operating within appropriate zoning

parameters.

Mr. Woodward detailed the specific changes to the ordinance. The existing definition of vehicle

Commission also recommended removing the phrase “other heavy” from the definition to make

newly revised definition was proposed as a permitted use in the M-1 and M-2 industrial zones,
which are primarily located in Camp Kearns and partially along 6200 South. He pointed out
that existing development standards, such as a required minimum lot size of 20,000 square
feet and a 100-foot setback from residential properties, would remain in effect but were
amended to include rental operations. One significant adjustment to the code was the
modification of a requirement that such businesses be located on an arterial or major collector
street. Due to limited frontage on 4700 South in the industrial zones, the standard was
changed to allow such uses within 1,500 feet of these roads, measured along the shortest
direct vehicular route. He also raised a planning concern for future consideration, involving the
movement of heavy equipment through residential neighborhoods. He highlighted the difficulty
in regulating whether equipment use on residential streets was for legitimate job site deliveries
or just inappropriate traffic shortcuts. He noted that this matter may fall under traffic
enforcement rather than land use regulation and suggested it could be addressed later in
consultation with legal counsel.
Mayor Bush acknowledged the challenges with access routes in the area and expressed the
city's intent to reduce backdoor traffic through residential zones.
Mr. Woodward concluded by reporting that the Planning Commission had given a positive
recommendation for the ordinance and that all M-1 and M-2 property owners had been
notified. While there were a few inquiries, no opposition was received.
Council Member Snow moved to approve Ordinance 2025-0-07, amending specific
areas of Section 19 as noted above to include and regulate the rental of commercial
vehicles and equipment in M-1 and M-2 Zones. Council Member Butterfield seconded

D. Discussion Regarding Adjustment of Procurement Amount, currently set at $100,000
Mayor Kelly Bush initiated a discussion regarding a proposed adjustment to the city’s
procurement policy, specifically to increase the threshold for construction-related projects. She
explained that the current limit of $100,000 was proving to be restrictive, particularly because
the Metro Services District (MSD) lacked a pre-qualified vendor pool for services like asphalt
and concrete work. The result was a burdensome bidding process for even minor construction
tasks, such as small segments of cement work, due to rising costs. She proposed raising the
procurement threshold for construction projects to $250,000 to streamline project execution
and avoid delays.

Nathan Bracken clarified that any change would require a formal resolution and amendment to
the purchasing policy, and that the discussion item was not up for a vote during the meeting.
He inquired whether the proposed increase would apply to all purchases or be limited to
construction. The mayor confirmed it would be exclusive to construction-related work to
prevent unnecessary public bidding for relatively small but high-cost projects.
Chad Anderson, in agreement with the mayor, stated that the proposed threshold would be
sufficient for the projects under consideration.
Mr. Bracken outlined the current procurement structure, which includes categories for small
purchases, intermediate purchases requiring two to three informal bids, and sealed bids for
higher amounts. He suggested the city could adopt new thresholds: $50,000 for small
purchases, $100,000 for intermediate bids, and $250,000 for sealed bids specific to
construction.
Mr. Anderson supported continuing to gather multiple bids for smaller construction projects but
without the full public bidding process, noting that this would allow the city to remain cost-
effective without the delays of formal procurement procedures.
Mayor Bush confirmed that the city typically does solicit multiple bids even without a formal
process, but the comprehensive bidding process is too time-consuming for frequent, smaller-
scale projects.
Mr. Bracken also introduced the concept of establishing an “approved vendor list,” or
contractor pool, created through a request for qualifications (RFQ) process. Qualified firms
would be selected by an evaluation committee, potentially including council members, and
could be used for streamlined bidding on future projects. Mayor Bush asked whether
contractors listed on the state’s procurement site could be automatically included, but Mr.
Bracken explained that while the city’s code references the state list, it would still be beneficial
to develop a Kearns-specific pool.

E. Resolution R2025-06, Interlocal Agreement Between Salt Lake County and Kearns

City for the 2025 Municipal Elections

Diana Baun presented the resolutioon concerning an interlocal agreement between Salt Lake
County and the City of Kearns for the administration of municipal elections. She explained that
this agreement, which is standard and renewed for each election cycle, authorizes the county
to handle all ballot-related and behind-the-scenes election logistics. The city retains
responsibility for managing candidate declarations. She confirmed that cost details were
included in the council packet and noted that the estimated cost for election services is
typically around $35,000. She mentioned that in the previous election cycle, the county had
provided inaccurate cost estimates, which prompted them to recommend budgeting at the
higher end this time to avoid underfunding. She also raised a procedural question regarding
the city’s fee schedule, asking whether Kearns currently charges a fee for filing a declaration of
candidacy. She noted that in some cities the county had previously collected the fee, leading to
omissions in municipal fee schedules.
Mayor Bush confirmed that the absence of this fee from Kearns’s current fee schedule was a
necessary correction and acknowledged it needed to be included.
Council Member Snow moved to approve Resolution R2025-06, Approving an Interlocal
Agreement with Salt Lake County for the Administration of Election Services. Council
Member Schaeffer seconded the motion; vote was 5-0, unanimous in favor.

C. Wasatch Front Waste Recycling District (WFWRD) - Renee Plant, Manager
Mayor Kelly Bush addressed an increase in resident complaints regarding waste collection
fees, noting that several community members had expressed a desire to cancel their garbage
service due to rising costs. Some residents indicated plans to either share containers with
neighbors or transport their own waste to the dump in an effort to avoid the higher fees.
Renee Plant provided background on the fee increases, explaining they were driven by
substantial cost hikes in the industry, particularly a 37% increase in the price of waste
collection trucks. She emphasized that the service provider continues to reduce internal
expenses wherever possible while striving to be responsible stewards of public funds. She also
introduced a new tiered reservation system for the SCRAP (Special Collection, Recycling, and
Pickup) area cleanup program aimed at improving fairness and accessibility. Kearns will
remain divided into three zones, with each zone now offering three reservation tiers. The first
tier, “Early Bird Access,” will be available to residents who were on the cancellation waitlist last
year but were unable to secure a container. These individuals will receive a special postcard
and a dedicated one-day reservation opportunity. Two days later, a second tier, “Early
Access,” will open for residents who did not participate in the program at all last year. The final
tier, “General Access,” will be open to all remaining residents, including those who received a
container last year. She noted that in other cities where this new model has been tested,
availability remained for general access participants, indicating the system's potential to more
equitably distribute resources. Full details, including personalized reservation dates, are
available on the official website and will also be included in mailed postcards. She concluded
by reminding residents that recycling bins are designated exclusively for paper and cardboard.
Yard waste such as grass and wood chips is not permitted. For such materials, residents are
encouraged to enroll in the optional green waste subscription service, which operates on the
same schedule as regular waste collection.

C. Unified Police Department (UPD) - Chief Hughes
Chief Levi Hughes of the Kearns Precinct delivered a comprehensive report (Attachment C) on
recent law enforcement activity and departmental updates. He began by expressing
condolences on behalf of Chief Mazuran and the Unified Police Department (UPD) to Mayor
Kelly Bush and her family following the recent passing of her father-in-law. He also
acknowledged Carlos Morenos in attendance, who had recently met with UPD leadership and
offered support. He reported that warmer weather had coincided with an uptick in road activity
and traffic-related issues, including road rage incidents, which had become more visible on
social media. In response, UPD plans to deploy unmarked vehicles for traffic enforcement in
the coming weeks. He noted that officers had successfully identified suspects and made
arrests related to several recent incidents. He also described a serious domestic violence
incident in Kearns that required a coordinated SWAT team and negotiator response. Although
the situation lasted approximately eight hours, it was resolved with an arrest and without
further injury beyond the harm suffered by the victim. He emphasized the strength of UPD’s
regional support system, which allowed them to rapidly deploy resources for high-demand
incidents. A major highlight of the report was the recognition of Detective Angie Rowe, who
received the District Attorney’s Office "Screener of the Month" award for her work on a large-
scale burglary case. The case involved a suspect responsible for hundreds of vehicle and
home burglaries. Ms. Rowe uncovered more than 100 stolen IDs and documents, initiating
over 120 felony charges. Initially, the District Attorney's Office filed 60 felony charges and
halted due to the volume, but she continued the investigation, leading to 60 additional charges.
Chief Hughes praised her thoroughness and dedication to ensuring every victim received
proper attention. In terms of crime statistics, he noted several positive trends for the past
month. Overall calls for service had declined, with significant decreases in property damage,
drug-related offenses, family-related offenses including domestic violence, thefts, and larceny.
The only category with a slight increase was vehicle thefts, with six reported cases. However,
he shared optimistic news that Officer Kirk Powell would be returning to service. Mr. Powell,
who previously retired, had earned a reputation for effectively recovering stolen vehicles and
was expected to have a strong impact upon his return.
