ORDINANCE NO. 20-02-01                                                                DATE: March 5, 2020

AN ORDINANCE AMENDING TITLE 5, ENTITLED “BUSINESS LICENSES AND REGULATIONS,” OF
 THE WHITE CITY METRO TOWNSHIP CODE OF ORDINANCES, BY ADDING PUBLIC NUISANCE,
  CRIME-MAGNET, AND GENERAL WELFARE JUSTIFICATIONS FOR REVOKING A BUSINESS
LICENSE AND PROVIDING GREATER DUE PROCESS FOR BUSINESS LICENSE SUPENSIONS AND
                                 REVOCATIONS

                                                  RECITALS

      WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. §§ 10-1-203(2) and 10-8-84 authorize the White City Metro Township
Council (the “Council”) to regulate businesses within the Township through the enactment of city ordinances;
and

        WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has recognized that a city’s municipal authority to issue licenses
and promulgate licensing policies is a principal “means of regulating businesses” pursuant to the “exercise of its
police powers” (Consolidation Coal Co. v. Emery County, 702 P.3d 121, 123 (1985)); and

       WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has declared that a city “has broad discretion in granting, denying,
or revoking [business] licenses” (Whiting v. Clayton, 617 P.2d 364, 364 (1980)); and

        WHEREAS, Utah Code Ann. § 10-8-60 authorizes the Council to regulate public nuisances by
“declare[ing] what shall be a nuisance, . . . abate[ing] the same, and impose[ing] fines upon persons who may
create, continue or suffer nuisances to exist”; and

       WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has affirmed that “cities have been granted the specific authority to
pass ordinances to regulate, by means of business licensing, offensive businesses that have become nuisances”
(Dairy Prod. Servs., Inc. v. City of Wellsville, 2000 UT 81, ¶ 38; 13 P.3d 581, 591 (2000)); and

       WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has further declared that “a [business] license revocation may be
based on the existence of a nuisance as defined by [city] ordinance” (Whiting, 617 P.2d at 365); and

        WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has stated that a business license represents “a substantial property
interest” and therefore “should not be destroyed nor disrupted arbitrarily, nor without following fundamental
standards of due process of law to guard against capricious or oppressive administrative action” (Anderson v.
Utah County Bd. of County Comm’rs, 589 P.2d 1214, 1216 (1979)); and

       WHEREAS, the Utah Supreme Court has noted that the “minimum requirements” for due process in
revoking a business license are “adequate notice and an opportunity to be heard in a meaningful manner” (Dairy
Prod. Servs., Inc., 2000 UT at ¶ 49; 13 P.3d at 593); and

        WHEREAS, the White City Metro Township Council desires to amend the business license revocation
provisions of the White City Municipal Code to provide for greater due process in business license suspension
and revocation proceedings and to authorize business license revocation or suspension in situations where
businesses that become public or private nuisances, encourage and facilitate crime, or operate in a manner
injurious or manifestly inimical to the general welfare and prosperity of the community.

       NOW, THEREFORE, the Council ordains as follows:

        Section 1: Section 5.07.020, entitled “Grounds For License Suspension Or Revocation,” is repealed in its
entirety and replaced as follows:

       5.07.020 Grounds for License/Permit Suspension or Revocation

       In addition to any fine imposed, every license or permit issued by the license official or council may be
       revoked or suspended by the council for any of the following reasons:

           A. Licensee or permittee commits a felony or any crime involving moral turpitude after having
              received a license;

           B. Licensee or permittee has obtained, or aided another to obtain, a license by fraud or deceit;

           C. Licensee or permittee has failed to pay personal property taxes, Utah state sales taxes, or other
              required fees;

           D. Licensee or permittee has refused to permit authorized officers or employees to make an inspection
              or to take a sample of a commodity, or has interfered with such officer or employee while making
              such inspection;

           E. Licensee or permittee has filed or encouraged another to file false information with the license
              office, the local law enforcement agency or any other agency as part of the license application;

           F. Licensee or permittee has contracted with two or more independent contractors whose business
              licenses were subsequently revoked in final unappealable decisions during a twelve-month period
              for engaging in unlawful conduct under the Utah Massage Therapy Practice Act or Prostitution
              under the Utah Criminal Code on the licensee or permittee's leased or owned premises;

           G. Any conduct or act on the business premises, or any conduct, act, or condition permitted by the
              licensee, permittee, or any employee on the business premises, which renders or tends to render
              such business or its premises: (a) a public or private nuisance, as defined by applicable law,
              regulation, or ordinance; or (b) a menace to the health, peace, safety, morals, or general welfare of
              the City or its residents;

           H. Licensee or permittee is responsible for unlawful activities conducted on the business premises
              that are contrary to the laws of the United States, the State of Utah, the ordinances of the metro, or
              the rules and regulations of any agency or another governmental entity governing the operation of
              the business holding the license or permit;

           I. Licensee or permittee has knowingly permitted its employees, agents, or patrons to engage in
              illegal activities on the business premises or to use the premises for any unlawful purpose or
              enterprise or the maintenance of a public or private nuisance, as defined by applicable law,
              regulation, or ordinance;
                                                         2
          J. Licensee’s or permittee’s business activities or premises are conducted or kept in an illegal,
             disorderly, unsanitary, hazardous, or ill-governed manner;

          K. The failure of the licensee or permittee to comply with any condition or requirement under local,
             state, or federal law; or

          L. Any other reason, not arbitrary or capricious and permitted by law under the metro’s police power
             that justifies suspension or revocation of a license or permit.

       Section 2: Section 5.07.030, entitled “License Suspension Or Revocation – Procedure,” is repealed and
replaced as follows:

       5.07.030 License/Permit Suspension Or Revocation – Procedure

          A. If the license official has good cause to believe that a license or permit should be suspended or
             revoked, the official shall schedule a hearing before the council by providing notice to the licensee
             and permittee which notice shall contain the following:

                  1. The time, place, and date of the hearing;

                  2. A statement of the purpose of the hearing; and

                  3. A reasonably definite statement setting forth the alleged grounds under Section 5.07.020
                     justifying suspension or revocation of the license or permit.

          B. The hearing shall take place at least fourteen (14) days and not more than thirty (30) days from the
             date written notice is sent.

          C. At the hearing, the licensee or permittee shall have the right: (a) to appear personally or by counsel;
             (b) to hear the evidence against him; (c) to confront and cross examine any witnesses; (d) to present
             evidence and witnesses on his behalf; and (e) to state his case as to why the license or permit
             should not be suspended or revoked.

          D. After conducting a hearing pursuant to this section, the council may suspend or revoke any license
             or permit if the licensee or permittee fails to show cause by a preponderance of the evidence why
             the license or permit should not be suspended or revoked.

          E. If the licensee or permittee fails, neglects, or refuses to appear at the hour and date set for such
             hearing, the council may proceed to determine the matter in the absence of the licensee or
             permittee, or may, in its sole discretion, continue the date of such hearing to some later date, and
             at the later date proceed to act on the matter.

          F. After due deliberation, the council shall announce its decision, and shall issue a written decision
             notifying the licensee or permittee of its findings and determination and an explanation of how the
             licensee or permittee may appeal the council’s decision.

       Section 3: Section 5.07.040, entitled “Appeals,” is repealed and amended as follows:

5.07.040 Appeals

   A. A licensee or permittee may appeal any decision rendered by the council under this chapter by
      requesting an administrative hearing with the metro’s administrative law judge pursuant to the
      process set forth in chapter 1.16, which shall govern appeals filed under this chapter.

   B. In addition to the provisions of chapter 1.16, the administrative law judge shall:

       1. Review the record, and may not accept or consider any evidence outside the record unless the
          evidence was offered to and was excluded by the council, the license official or license
          official’s designee and the administrative law judge determines that it was improperly
          excluded;

       2. Review the council’s or the license official’s actions to determine whether the council’s
          decision was arbitrary, capricious, or illegal; and

       3. After reviewing the record and written and oral argument on both sides, affirm, reverse, alter,
          or remand the council’s decision for further review and consideration.

   C. The filing of an appeal does not automatically stay the council’s decision; however, the
      administrative law judge has the authority to stay the decision while the appeal is pending.

   D. A decision issued by the administrative law judge pursuant to this section is a final administrative
      order that is appealable to the district court pursuant to section 1.15.130.

Section 4: Section 5.07.050, entitled “Effect of Revocation,” is amended as follows:

5.07.050 Effect of Suspension or Revocation

   A. If a license or permit is suspended or revoked, it shall thereafter be unlawful for any person to
      engage in or use, or permit to be used, any property for any business with respect to which the
      license or permit has been suspended or revoked.

   B. In the event of suspension or revocation as provided in this chapter, there shall be no refund or
      rebate of any part of the original license or permit fee paid by the licensee or permittee.

Section 5: Section 5.07.060, entitled “Effect of Revocation,” is amended as follows:

5.07.060 Waiting Period For New License Or Permit

   A. No person whose license or permit has been suspended or revoked under the provisions of this
      chapter, and no person associated or connected with such person in the conduct of such business,
      shall be granted a license or permit for the same purpose for a period of six months after the
      suspension or revocation has occurred.
                                                4
           B. The council may, for good cause, waive the prohibition against persons formerly associated or
              connected with an individual who has had a license or permit suspended or revoked.

       Section 6: Section 5.02.180, entitled “License – Effect of Denial,” is amended as follows:

       5.02.180 License – Effect of Denial and Waiting Period for New License

           A. If at any time a license or renewal is denied under the provisions of this chapter, it shall thereafter
              be unlawful for any person to engage in or carry on or operate or use or permit to be operated or
              used, any property for any business with respect to which the license or renewal has been denied,
              until a license or renewal shall be granted by the council upon appeal.

           B. No person denied a license under the provisions of this chapter, and no person associated or
              connected with such person in the conduct of such business, shall be granted a license for the same
              purpose for a period of six months after the denial has occurred.
       Section 7: Pursuant to Utah Code Ann. §§ 10-3-711(1)(b) & 10-3-712, this ordinance shall take effect
twenty (20) days after: (a) depositing a copy of the ordinance in the White City Metro Township’s recorder office;
and (b) publishing a short summary of the ordinance in a newspaper of general circulation within the metro
township.
