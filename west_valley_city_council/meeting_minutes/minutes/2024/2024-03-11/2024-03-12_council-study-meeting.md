 Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
 information as follows:

        This 7,293 square foot parcel, known as Lot 1A of the Hunter Cove
        Mountain View Corridor Subdivision, was created from Lots 1 and 2 of
        the Hunter Cove Subdivision. As part of the Mountain View Corridor
        project, UDOT acquired Lots 1 and 2 of the Hunter Cove Subdivision and
        demolished the homes on these lots to facilitate the widening of 4100
        South, the termination of Rushford Court in a cul-de-sac, and the
        construction of the shared use path west of the Mountain View Corridor.
        Following the completion of the Mountain View Corridor project, UDOT
        sold the subject parcel in March of 2023.

        Since the subject parcel is below the current minimum lot size of 8,000
        square feet, the applicant is requesting to rezone the property to the R-1-7
        zone so that it will comply with the minimum lot size requirement.

        If this application is approved, the applicant intends to build a new home
        on the subject property. The applicant has submitted a conceptual site
        plan, front elevation, and floor plans for the property. The proposed home
        would be 1,420 square feet in area and include an unfinished basement
        and a two-car garage. The proposed exterior materials include brick and
        fiber cement siding. The proposed roof pitch is 6:12 with laminated
        architectural shingles. Based on a preliminary review by staff, the
        proposed home meets the City’s current design standards.

 Councilmember Nordfelt asked if this would be built to the City’s current standards.
 Steve replied that the State removed the ability for cities to have specific regulations
 in place, however, the applicant will meet the architectural standards that were in
 place in City Code prior to the legislative changes. Steve noted that there will be a
 2-car garage which is also in line with current ordinances since the lot existed prior
 to April 2015 and therefore doesn’t require the new 3-car garage standard.
 Councilmember Nordfelt noted that the size of the lot would restrict home size and
 garage size anyway. Mayor Lang asked if the basement would be finished at the
 time of build. Steve replied that he is unsure but would clarify. Mayor Lang asked
 if the front door would be next to the sidewalk. Steve replied no and indicated that
 setback requirements will be met. Mayor Lang stated that she feels this is a good
 solution for this property.

RESOLUTION 24-42: AWARD A CONTRACT TO QUALITY CARPORTS, INC.
FOR A CARPORT FOR FIRE STATION 74
Jake Arslanian, Facilities, presented proposed Resolution 24-42 that would award a
Contract to Quality Carports, Inc. for a Carport for Fire Station 74

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       For many years now several fire apparatuses at station #74 have been parked
       outside in the weather. Fire Station #74 is a very busy station and has a lot of
       apparatus to provide the necessary service demands of the city in this area. Several
       of this apparatus can’t be housed in the existing station bays due to space.
       Consequently, several expensive large and small apparatus have been parked
       outside and being subjected to the harsh environment of summer and winter
       seasons. For this reason, the Facilities Division has designed a space adjacent to the
       existing bays at station #74. The Location and structure design is approved by the
       building official and the planning and Zoning Staff.
       The facilities have received three bids for this structure.
           1. Quality Carports Inc. $45,264.11
           2. Elevate Steel Carports $54,600.00
           3. Awnings Unlimited $88,500.00

Mayor Lang asked if access will still be available around the building. Jake replied yes.
Mayor Lang asked if the generators will also be stored under the carport and asked if there
is a concern for theft. Jake replied that they are large and would be difficult for unwanted
removal. Councilmember Fitisemanu clarified that this purchase was addressed in a
previous program mod and doesn’t require a budget opening. Jake replied yes.
Councilmember Harmon asked if a lot line adjustment should be considered. Jake

A.  RESOLUTION 24-45: ACCEPT A STORM DRAINAGE EASEMENT
    FROM GRANGER-HUNTER IMPROVEMENT DISTRICT FOR
    PROPERTY LOCATED AT 2155 WEST PRINTERS ROW
    Mayor Lang discussed proposed Resolution 24-45 that would accept a Storm
    Drainage Easement from Granger-Hunter Improvement District for Property Located
    at 2155 West Printers Row

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

               The Granger-Hunter Improvement District parcel located at approximately
               2155 W. Printers Row is one of several properties affected by the Brighton
               Canal Storm Drain Project; 2600 West to Redwood Road, scheduled to be
               constructed in 2024. This project will include construction of storm drain
               piping through portions of the former Brighton Canal and eliminate any
               open storm water ditches through the project area.

               Decker Lake Business Park was developed prior to the incorporation of
               West Valley City. Storm water was historically allowed to drain into two
               ditches on either side of the Brighton Canal. This existing system does not
               function very well and has caused ongoing maintenance and flooding
               problems. The Brighton Canal no longer operates in this area and has
               largely been abandoned, which now provides the opportunity to consolidate
               and pipe the storm water into one system.

       D.     CARBON MONOXIDE DETECTOR DISCUSSION
              Mayor Lang stated a memo was provided. Jerry provided a summary as follows:

                     -   West Valley City currently has 29 buildings in its inventory. 6 of these
                         are Fire Stations.
                     -   Both the Building code (as well as Fire code) and Environmental
                         Protection Agency only require Carbon Monoxide detection in
                         residential dwellings, schools, and “I” occupancies (Assisted Living,
                         Group homes, Adult & Child Day Care, Hospitals, etc.). In all these
                         cases, the requirement is at least one device per floor of a building with
                         a “fuel burning appliance”. Because of this requirement, all 6 Fire
                         Stations have them due to the fact that employees sleep there.
                     -   The cost of installation would greatly depend on what the preferred
                         placement and connection is.
                     -   One factor to consider is that all the office and recreation buildings have
                         air handling units that bring in at least 15% outside air each hour. This
                         also creates a positive air environment inside that is constantly pushing
                         air outside by drawing in fresh and expelling old, thus creating a
                         constant mixture and comfortable environment.
                     -   One last note: all of our garages have a detector attached to a fan that
                         activate when emissions exceed preset acceptable levels.
