        1.2      Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Overson reported that ChamberWest’s Fall conference had been held the previous
week. She and Chair Barbieri attended the one-day event that involved dynamic speakers, and
great break-out sessions. She explained it was a good opportunity to network with the city’s
business partners and officials from West Jordan, West Valley, and Kearns.

There was recently a ribbon-cutting held at Sierra, an outdoor apparel retailer that had recently
opened in Taylorsville. She thanked Chair Barbieri and Councilmember Cochran for attending as
well as various city staff members. The Crossroads at Taylorsville shopping center contained
many opportunities to shop local.

Both the Mayor and Chief Cottam had the opportunity to speak to Chair Harker’s class of third
graders about the importance of being good citizens and explained how the children could make
a difference in their community.

Mayor Overson offered her congratulations to Sen. Karen Mayne, Rep. Karen Kwan, Rep. Jim
Dunnigan, Rep. Mark Wheatley, and Rep. Ashley Mathews who were all successful in the general
election held earlier in the month. The City communicated with each legislator regularly, and they
helped advocate for Taylorsville residents. Additionally, Salt Lake County Councilmember Aimee
Winder Newton had been reelected and Suzanne Harrison was newly elected to the Commission
as well. Finally, Connie Burgess was reelected to the Granite School District Board of Education.
The Mayor appreciated the partnership and friendship that the city had with each of these
individuals.

Veterans were celebrated the previous week on Veteran’s Day, and it had been Mayor Overson’s
pleasure to speak at both Salt Lake Community College and Truewood Assisted Living Facility in
Taylorsville. Her remarks were focused on the Taylorsville Cemetery and some of the veterans
interred there. She felt that sharing the stories helped to keep veterans’ legacies alive.

Utah’s legislature, the Division of Housing and Community Development, and Workforce
Services sponsored a workshop on housing, growth, and land use regulations. Many stakeholders

The City had recently hired a planner as well as a judicial assistant in the justice court. There were

Mayor Overson mentioned two important opportunities for giving in the community. The annual

3.      REPORTS

       3.1 Historic Preservation Committee Report – Susan Yadeskie

      Committee Chair Susan Yadeskie described many of the activities that the committee had
taken part in over the previous eight months. She pointed out how critical volunteerism was to the
success of the historic preservation of Taylorsville. She also expressed her appreciation for Jay
Ziolkowski who had organized a service project on September 10 in commemoration of 9/11. She
had expected about 20 people but many more people than that arrived to help at not only that event
but throughout the year. She mentioned many of the groups that had volunteered at the Heritage
Center.

The Committee had been awarded funds that made it possible to purchase new lighting which was
used during the Tombstone Tales event wherein community and committee members participated
in acting out the stories of many city residents who had been interred at the cemetery.

Back in 2019, the Utah Islamic Center had met with Councilmember Cochran and Ms. Yadeskie
and expressed interest in preserving their building because they were aware of its historic value.
They submitted a grant for funds to pay for a study on how best to preserve it and in hopes of
getting the building on the National Register of Historic Places. They were notified in March that
the building had been added to the register and it was the first building in the city to be placed on
the list. Since then, the museum and pump house had also been added to the register.

        3.2 Unified Fire Authority Report – Captain Richard Rich

       Captain Richard Rich of the Unified Fire Authority presented information regarding the 3rd
quarter of 2022 (July through September) and the statistics had remained fairly consistent.
However, the flex traffic lanes on 5400 South had a significant impact on traffic accident numbers
when there was construction in the area. He pointed out that it would be very important to keep
that in mind for any future UDOT projects that affected those lanes.

Councilmember Knudsen asked that future reports include quarterly totals on the slide that broke
out the number of incidents by month.

Captain Rich provided statistical data regarding the types of calls received (both fire-related and
medical), the types of apparatus used and fire stations that responded to different calls. He pointed
out that on average, responding units arrived on half of the emergent calls within 4 minutes and
40 seconds of their dispatch time and in 90% of those calls, arrived in just over seven minutes.

Councilmember Cochran was curious to know why Station No. 117 responded to so many more
calls than Station No. 118. Captain Rich explained that Station No. 117 was a “dual house”,
meaning that it had two engine companies since their area was a bit busier. In addition, a large
portion of Taylorsville was handled by Kearns Station No. 109.

Captain Rich explained that the Wildland Division, which handled the western half of the United
States, had wrapped up their season which included at least 75 different fires, several of which had
been quite complex. Another wildfire team worked out of Camp Williams and the UFA was in the
process of extending employment offers to five members of that crew. Seven paramedics (lateral
hires) were sworn in, had completed their 4-week orientation, and were continuing their
acclimation in the field. He expected that they would be working independently by the first week
of December. The top firefighter applicants were currently undergoing background investigations
and the next recruit camp would start on February 1st with an anticipated class of 36.

The Captain provided an update regarding fire stations under construction elsewhere in the Salt
Lake Valley and explained that the supply chain issues, and the increasing cost of construction
materials and labor were delaying some of that work.

The summer event season had wrapped up and the department had participated in 99 events
including Taylorsville Dayzz. The majority were city sponsored and all the events resulted in
1,830 person hours worked. He had hoped to get about 250 residents to show up to the Community
Pancake Breakfast held the month before but there were more than 850 people. UFA loved seeing

2023.

Unified Fire continued to work on their Standards of Cover document which would assess the risk

7.      OTHER MATTERS

        7.1      Update on Volta Development – Mayor Overson

      Mayor Overson explained how excited the city administration was about the Volta
development planned for 5400 South Bangerter. She thought it would be a good opportunity for
the developer to update the council on the project status and invited John Thackeray, Johnny
Thackeray, and Mark Isaac to address the City Council.

John R. Thackeray began his comments by admitting that the current economy presented
challenges. However, he and his team continued to work closely with city administration, and he
pointed out how much he appreciated the open dialogue, even though the administration did not
always agree with everything his company proposed. He explained that the city administrators
were passionate about their responsibility to Taylorsville and he and his team were just as
passionate about bringing a high-quality project to the city. He believed that Volta would rival any
other project in Utah.

Mr. Thackeray then turned the time over to Mark Issac who explained that his role on the Volta
project was to oversee the architecture and engineering, the design team, and the collaboration
with the City. He stated that his team had “bombarded” city staff with over a thousand pages of
design over the last 6-7 months. The off-site sewer design for Taylorsville Bennion Improvement
District was finished, as were the plat and the civil engineering. The architecture was complete
apart from a few ingress/egress issues and fire marshal requirements. They received bids for
demolition earlier that day and he would work with both UFA and TVPD to see if either agency
Taylorsville City Council Minutes

wished to do any training in the buildings prior to demolition which he hoped would take place
before Christmas.

Mr. Issac stated that they were in the final stages of financing with a local, well-known lender, and
he expected construction to begin in March which was preferable to doing the work in the winter
months. He anticipated having the project’s first phase (350 units) completed 24 months after that
March commencement date.

Rocky Mountain Power had wanted to relocate utilities to the easements in the backyards of every
home on the southside of the Volta project site. Instead, the Thackeray Company asked RMP to
move the utility easement onto the Volta property rather than adversely impact sheds, patios,
landscaping, etc. which homeowners had in place for many years. That caused some delay since it
changed the project’s civil design. Although he understood that not every neighbor was happy
about the project, his firm was doing everything it could to manage the disruption.

Councilmember Cochran was pleased to hear that progress was being made. Vice-Chair Harker
expressed her excitement that the first buildings would be in place within 24 months from the
coming spring.

Councilmember Burgess appreciated what the Thackeray Co. had done to mitigate the negative
impact on the neighbors by asking that the utility easement be moved to its own property. He also
appreciated the update, explaining that the Council now had a better idea of what progress was
being made.

John Thackeray admitted they might have over-designed the project, but said they were thrilled
with the plans. One challenge they had not yet been able to resolve was ensuring that there was a
certain percentage of “essential” (affordable) housing included in the design. It was very important
to him, and with interest rates and the cost of construction materials increasing, it was becoming
even more difficult to make a reality. They intended to continue working with the City as well as
Salt Lake County to ensure that a certain number of units were affordable, perhaps for public
employees such as first responders and school teachers. He admitted they had not yet solved that
very important “piece of the puzzle.”

Mayor Overson appreciated the Thackeray Company’s willingness to update the City Council and
felt that face-to-face communication was important. She appreciated their kind words about staff
and felt that there was a great working relationship between the City and the developer. She looked
forward to the magnificent development.

Councilmember Burgess encouraged Mr. Thackeray and Mr. Issac to continue to find a way to
include affordable housing in their plans.

10’ wide public easement to remain with the property.

so Vice Chair Harker closed the public hearing. She then invited the applicant to address the
