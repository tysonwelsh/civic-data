1.     WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

Chair Cochran called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance.
A roll call was conducted wherein all council members were present.

       1.1     Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Council Member Barbieri
               (Opening Ceremonies for March 6, 2024 to be arranged by Mayor Overson)

Council Member Barbieri led the Pledge of Allegiance before commenting on the work taking
place at the Utah State Legislature. She expressed gratitude for Mayor Overson and John
Hiskey before concluding her remarks with a humorous quote in honor of President’s Day.

       1.2     Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Kristie Overson explained that there were nine more days left in the current
legislative session. Activity was fast and furious, and she agreed with Council Member
Barbieri John Hiskey did an exemplary job of representing Taylorsville and its interests.

Earlier in the day the Youth Council had met and finished presenting reports of their job
shadowing experiences with various city staff members. They were currently preparing for a
visit to Utah State University for a leadership conference.

As a member of Unified Fire’s Board of Trustees, Mayor Overson received some statistics
regarding how busy various stations were across the valley. Station No. 109 in Kearns, which
handled quite a few Taylorsville calls for service was the second busiest UFA station in the
valley, while Taylorsville stations #117 and #118 came in third and fifth place respectively.
She greatly appreciated the work that UFA firefighters and paramedics did for city residents.

An Evening of Art was held for the third consecutive year, this time at the Mid-Valley
Performing Arts Center. Doug and Dianne Adams were the presenters for the evening and
led a fascinating evening as part of the city’s Plaza+Arts program. Incidentally, the program
had its own Facebook page, and Mayor Overson encouraged everyone to pay it a visit.

       3.1     Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation, Valley Regional Park Master Plan
               Update – Mathew Winward/Liz Sollis

     Salt Lake County’s Walt Gilmore, Associate Division Director for Park Planning & Park
Development introduced himself as well as Liz Sollis of the County’s Parks and Recreation
Department and Mathew Winward of G. Brown Design. Mr. Gilmore explained that they were
in the process of updating several regional parks in Salt Lake County, including Valley
Regional Park. They had been gathering input from residents near the park as well as from

others. There had been a public open house, and a second one was scheduled for March
19th. These forums helped them understand as much about what residents didn’t want as
what they did.

Mr. Gilmore then turned the time over to Mr. Winward who explained that he was the
Managing Principal at G. Brown Design, which was a landscape architecture firm. His firm
had contracted with Salt Lake County in late 2022 to update the master plan for Valley
Regional. They assembled a stakeholder group which included Taylorsville residents, Parks
and Recreation committee members, city staffers, parks operations staff, etc. In short,
there was a broad spectrum of people to help inform the process. He explained that in
addition to the open house(s), a survey had been conducted to gain insight into citizen
priorities for the park system. The online survey garnered almost 500 responses regarding
how people currently used the park and what they would like to see in the future. Armed
with the feedback they had garnered, they developed several different concepts for the park
before whittling them down with the stakeholders and ultimately presenting two at an
August 2023 open house. They then had an additional public comment period to solicit
further feedback before beginning to mold the two concepts into one cohesive model. The
next open house was planned for March wherein additional community feedback would be
sought.

Mr. Winward first reviewed the Preferred Alternative A which would include covering the
outdoor pool so that it could be used year-round. The plan also called for two new multi-use
sports fields, possibly with astroturf, depending on the public feedback that was received.
Additionally, there would be a bicycle skills area, a small playground, a 9-hole disc golf
course (reduced from the current 18-hole course), a maintenance building, more
landscaping, twelve pickleball courts, and two tennis courts.

A slide depicting Preferred Alternative B was then displayed which included a field house for
a variety of indoor recreational activities. It could house eight basketball courts, ten indoor
pickleball courts (as opposed to the twelve in Alternative A), and no tennis courts.

There had been quite a few discussions regarding the effect all this would have on
Taylorsville Dayzz. They would continue to have discussions, including with the Taylorsville
Dayzz committee and Jim Dunnigan.

Liz Sollis, Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation’s Associate Director of Community
Engagement then addressed what could be expected going forward. A public open house
would be held on March 19 from 4-6 pm at the library in Taylorsville. She pointed out that

         3.2   Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling Report – Pam Roberts

    1.         General Manager/CEO Pam Roberts mentioned HB107 which would require
         WFWRD to be transparent about the recycling they picked up. She pointed out that
         WFWRD already did that. It would also require municipalities to publish a report of
         their recycling activities and WFWRD already provided those for each city. She was
         in favor of the bill.

Ms. Roberts reported on 2023 collections in Taylorsville. Specifically,
    825 tons of green curbside waste diverted from the landfill (from 1,548 subscribers
      in that program)
    16 tons of curbside glass diverted (from 124 subscribers)
    2,980 tons of recycling diverted
    18,726 tons of garbage delivered to the landfill at an average cost of $35 per ton
    17% diversion rate for Taylorsville which was slightly below the 18% average

       3.3     Taylorsville Police Department Victim’s Advocate Report

     Sgt. Jake Hill explained that he supervised the Violent Crimes Unit as well as the Victim
Advocate Division. He was joined by Merari Lopez and Alex Perez who together had almost
thirty-years of experience in victim advocacy.

Ms. Perez presented information regarding the services provided by her division during Q4
2023. This included:
   o Serving 211 clients
   o Providing referrals and information 614 times
   o Providing personal advocacy 25 times
   o Providing emotional support 84 times
   o Providing shelter/housing services 24 times
   o Providing assistance with navigating the justice system 206 times
   o 265 instances of “other” services such as transportation

All these services were provided only by Lopez and Perez.

Ms. Lopez recounted an incident where she was able to assist a crime victim who had been
hesitant to cooperate with the police but ultimately agreed to do so.

Council Members Barbieri and Harker thanked Ms. Lopez and Ms. Perez for doing the work
they did for Taylorsville victims. Council Member Burgess wished to ensure that they had
access to services that would help them deal with the stressors that came with their work.

      Judge Christopher Bown explained that since he last reported to the Council, he had
been appointed to the Board of Justice Court judges which gave him the opportunity to affect
justice court policy at a state-wide level. He was also assisting the District Court by
reviewing arrest warrant requests.

Clerk of Court Jeff Gallegos was retiring on March 1. He explained that Jeff was instrumental
in getting the judge acclimated to his new position in 2021. He pointed out how effective Jeff
was in dealing with challenging people—possibly from his years working as a bailiff.

Chair Cochran asked that Mr. Gallegos approach the podium. He did so, and the Council
and City Administration offered their congratulations and thanked him for his service to the
Taylorsville community.

Judge Bown continued his report by explaining that there had been an increase in need for
interpreters during court, although it was not yet to the extent that the budget needed to be
increased. He said the prosecutors were doing a wonderful job, as were Stowell & Crayk who
provided defense work for indigent defendants. The Judge particularly enjoyed the fact that
they were willing to take their cases to jury trial, which wasn’t always the case with many
other indigent defense firms. Judge Bown pointed out that his interactions with TVPD officers
had always been positive and that they were always well-prepared when they appeared
before him.

The Judge then provided some statistical data regarding the disposition of criminal, traffic,
and small claims matters. For criminal cases, his goal was to dispose of them within 180
days of filing. The disposition rate had steadily improved from 67% in late 2021 to 89% as of
this month. Regarding traffic cases, the goal was 90 days, and that rate was now being met
for 91% of those cases. Finally, he sought to dispose of small claims cases within 270 days
from the date of filing, and that was happening for 99% of them.

Judge Bown concluded his report by saying how much he enjoyed his job, ensuring that
defendants in his court felt hopeful and heard, while he did what justice required.
