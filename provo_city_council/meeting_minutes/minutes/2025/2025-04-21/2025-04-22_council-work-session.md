Item 3: A Resolution Approving the First Amendment to the Lakeview Fields Development
Agreement to Allow Updated Plans, Optional Basements, Vinyl Fencing, and a Separate
HOA for the Subject Property. (25-052) 0:09:58

Jessica Dahneke, Planner, presented a resolution to approve the first amendment to the Lakeview
Fields Development Agreement. She provided background on the development, noting that the
original agreement had been approved in 2008 and that the initial phase of the project had
already been constructed. Ms. Dahneke stated that the proposed amendments had undergone
review by the Coordinators Review Committee (CRC) and City Attorney Brian Jones. She also
relayed that Public Works expressed concerns about the language related to basements,
particularly due to the high groundwater levels in the area.

Councilor Bogdin raised concerns that the amended agreement did not include a homeowner
occupancy requirement, which she identified as a Council goal and long-standing standard. Ms.
Dahneke confirmed that the amendments did not include any increase in density. Councilor
Whipple expressed appreciation for the updated designs, noting the variety of housing types
proposed as a positive change from the original 2008 plans.

Councilor Handley emphasized the significance of offering a range of housing sizes to support
affordability. Tyler Horan, the developer, clarified that while the amended agreement would
permit basements, their inclusion was unlikely due to feasibility issues. Mr. Jones explained that
the amendment did not mandate basements but would allow them if they complied with city
standards.

The Council discussed the possibility of including a homeowner occupancy requirement in the
agreement. Mr. Horan proposed a compromise of requiring 60–70% owner occupancy. Council
members deliberated the appropriate percentage, with some advocating for a higher threshold.
They also discussed the duration of the occupancy requirement and potential enforcement
mechanisms.

Item 5: An Ordinance Amending Provo City Code Regarding Permitted Uses and Floor
Area Ratio in the Specialty Support Commercial (SSC) Zone. City Wide Application.
(PLOTA20250083) 0:42:20

Mary Barnes, Planner, presented an ordinance to amend the Provo City Code relating to
permitted uses and floor area ratio in the Specialty Support Commercial (SSC) Zone. She
explained that the applicant, Doug Nielsen, was requesting to add indoor storage units and drive-
through facilities to the list of permitted uses within the SSC zone. Additionally, Mr. Nielsen

The Council discussed the proposed amendments and their potential impact on future
development patterns within SSC-zoned areas. Councilors also inquired about the distinctions
between the SSC zone and other commercial zoning designations in the city to better understand
the broader implications of the proposed changes.

Item 8: An Update Regarding Re-Imagining Code Enforcement. (25-053) 1:01:45

Scott Johnson, Zoning Administrator, provided an update on efforts to reimagine code
enforcement practices in Provo. He outlined several initiatives aimed at improving operations
and enhancing enforcement strategies.

Utilizing County Data
Mr. Johnson explained that staff had been comparing county data with city records to identify
unlicensed rental properties. By isolating residential properties with mailing addresses differing
from the site addresses, they reduced an initial dataset of 40,000 entries to 12,000. Of those, at



Provo City Municipal Council Work Meeting – April 22, 2025                               Page 4 of 11
least 6,000 properties were confirmed to have rental licenses. Staff is in the process of verifying
the remaining properties, after which letters will be sent to noncompliant property owners
requesting licensure or justification for exemption.

Drafting an RFP for Software
An RFP is being prepared for software that would identify long-term rental advertisements on
public platforms and cross-check them with city licensing data. Although the process has been
delayed pending finance department approval, the launch will proceed as soon as possible.

Budget Line Item for Cleaning Vacant Lots
Mr. Johnson discussed a proposed budget item to fund initial cleanup efforts on vacant lots,
particularly those with overgrown vegetation. After cleanup, the city would place a lien on the
property for reimbursement.

Exploring Technology and Privacy Concerns
He also reported that the use of cell phone aggregation data was explored as a way to identify
high-occupancy sites. However, privacy limitations restrict the usefulness of such tools, and
individual tracking was deemed infeasible.

Implementing In-Field Printers and Body Cameras
Field officers are being equipped with mobile printers to issue on-site notices more efficiently.
Additionally, body cameras have been ordered for officers, with training to begin upon their
arrival.

Public Education and Outreach
Efforts are underway to increase public awareness, with neighborhood meetings planned to start
in June or July. The Public Information Office is collaborating with the department to develop
digital media outreach, with an initial focus on rental dwelling licensing.

Legislative Amendments and Collaboration
Text amendments are being drafted to address civil fines and pest infestation abatement. Mr.
Johnson also noted ongoing collaboration with Provo Police and Utah County officials for meth
contamination testing at problem properties.

Council members expressed appreciation for the comprehensive and data-driven nature of these
initiatives. They asked follow-up questions on specific strategies and encouraged continued
progress on code enforcement enhancements.

reinvested into critical facility improvements, including new blinds for the library ballroom.

collections budget, upgrade technology, and introduce professional art exhibitions in the attic
space.

Item 15: A Resolution Approving the Memorandum of Agreement Between the Provo City
Redevelopment Agency and BCP Ironton, LLC for Property Located Within the Mountain
Vista Business Park. (25-055) 3:56:52

Melissa McNalley, Director of the Redevelopment Agency (RDA), presented a resolution to
approve a memorandum of agreement between the Provo City Redevelopment Agency and BCP
Ironton, LLC concerning property located in the Mountain Vista Business Park. The property
was originally owned by Hall Labs and is part of a 2016 agreement concerning the
environmental remediation of parcels formerly used as an ironworks site.

Under the original agreement, Hall Labs assumed responsibility for the environmental
remediation of the property, which has been partially completed. The new agreement proposes
the sale of the property to BCP Ironton, LLC, with the condition that the new owner will assume
responsibility for completing the remediation.

Council members engaged in a comprehensive discussion of the potential risks associated with
the remediation process, particularly in light of the two quotes, respectively $2.1 million and
$3.2 million. A key component of the agreement is a sunset clause, which requires that the
remediation be completed by 2028.

Councilors expressed concerns regarding the adequacy of the approximately $1.9 million
purchase price credit relative to the potentially higher remediation costs. They also raised
questions about enforcement mechanisms and legal safeguards in place should BCP Ironton,
LLC fail to fulfill its obligations. City Attorney Brian Jones clarified that the City holds a trust
deed on the property, which provides a level of protection against default.

While acknowledging the complexity and potential liabilities involved, the Council recognized
that the proposed agreement offered the best opportunity to advance the remediation and
development of the site while minimizing Provo City's exposure to risk. Before proceeding to a
vote, which was deferred for two weeks, the Council requested additional details and
clarifications regarding financial and legal elements of the agreement.
