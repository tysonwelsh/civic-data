Zoning Text Amendment

       4.     3Z22 - Public Hearing and Recommendation to the City Council for a Zoning Text Amendment to
              Taylorsville Municipal code Sections 13.07.020; 13.08.020; 13.08.020; 13.09.030; 13.10.020;
              13.11.040; 13.26.080; and Chapter 13.36: Definitions, Related to Home Occupation Standards. (Jim
              Spung/AICP/Senior Planner)

4.1         This item was presented by Jim Spung.              File #3Z22 is a revisit of the same item heard on Dec 13,
            2022, wherein the Planning Commission needed clarification on some portions before making a decision.
            As a result, Staff has made revisions to the text which Mr. Spung went over during tonight’s meeting.

            Revisions about Vehicles and Trailers include:

            4.1.1   Addressing the employees visiting the site of the home occupation. A new sentence has been
                    included saying, “Offsite employees may visit the residence in accordance with 13.11.040 C(4).
                    Letter B which says, “Excepting residential child daycare, preschools, and home instruction; home
                    occupations shall have no more than two business related visitors per hour”. Any employee would
                    be subject to that same standard – no more than two per hour. Otherwise, they would be allowed
                    on the site. Commissioner Quigley said that accomplished the intent of what had been discussed
                    in that previous meeting and clarified that offsite employees can visit yet puts some teeth in the
                    Code that if there is a neighbor who complains because there are a lot of cars on a regular basis
                    then there is the ability to control that. This was apparently approved by Code Enforcement and
                    seems to be a good solution.

            4.1.2   Mr. Spung continued with the issue of trailers. That is the topic that was discussed at length with
                    Code Enforcement and most of their complaints dealt with parking of trailers. He had initially
                    asked them about potentially regulating home businesses based on the size of the trailers, size of
                    the lots and allowing larger lots to have larger trailers. It is difficult to parse that out because
                    people have trailers that aren’t associated with the home occupation for personal use, so it is
                    difficult to have a different set of rules for different intensive for trailers or vehicles because then
                    the City has to ascertain if it is a business or personal vehicle. Staff prefers not to have that
                    entanglement for simplicity. He then reviewed some of the redlines on the document.

            4.1.3   Letter B was clarification for Code Enforcement that vehicles and trailers used in association with
                    the home occupation must be parked in an approved off-street parking location (added: and shall
                    not encroach or overhang on any sidewalk or pedestrian way). Letter E calls out tow trucks,
                    tanker trucks, box vans, delivery vans and similar vehicles. Staff removed verbiage “that exceeds
                    10,000 pound gross vehicle weight ratings” by just saying they are prohibited and may not be
                    stored onsite. Such vehicles may be located offsite in an approved storage location.       Letter
                    F says “Only one trailer with no more than two axles” may be used in association with a home
                    occupation. Letter G features an addition to The maximum body length of an enclosed trailer is
                    20 feet. The maximum body length of an open trailer is 16 feet. (added – “For purposes of this
                    standard, trailer length does not include the trailer tongue”. Letter H was added to say “Auto
                    transport trailers designed to transport more than one vehicle or that exceed the body length
                    requirements are prohibited and shall not be stored on-site. Such trailers may be located off-site
                    in an approved storage location”.

4.2      DISCUSSION:

         4.2.1           Commissioner McElreath asked about the tow trucks and wanted to know if it only applies
                   to a home-based business. Mr. Spung said that was correct. That as part of the larger Code update
                   these standards will probably be mirrored therein and make them just general residential
                   standards for any residence anywhere, even if there is no home occupation involved. But right
                   now, the standards are only for home occupations.

         4.2.2     Commissioner Quigley           expressed concerns about both paragraph Letters B and E. His
                   memory was there was discussion about trying to force them to park their service vehicles
                   elsewhere, particularly of concern being food trucks parking in front of the residence. He did not
                   see any language in this change that talks about that. It does say, “in an approved off-street
                   parking”. His concern was what the definition of that was. He wondered if that would help with
                   the concern of having the service vehicles parking in front of a residential home. He also wondered
                   if it is somewhat addressed in paragraph E where it talks about box and delivery vans and if there
                   was a difference in the verbiage – one being referred to as “stored” and the other as “parked”.
                   With that someone could say they are parked there today but it is not being stored. That could
                   mean someone is parking their food truck every night in their driveway, which could be very
                   unattractive to the neighborhood and yet fall under the definition of not being stored.

         4.2.3     Mr. Spung answered the question of what an approved off-street parking location is, as being any
                   area of the lot that allows parking. Generally speaking that would be a paved surface in the front,
                   side or rear yard or in a garage. It basically is to say no parking is allowed in landscaping, on dirt
                   or gravel, etc. He addressed the second question about the location of the parking. He did talk to
                   Code Enforcement about that and it was their opinion that if there was a requirement for any
                   home occupation vehicle or trailer to be required to park on the side or behind, that it would be
                   difficult for most of them to comply with that standard. They either simply don’t have anywhere
                   on the side of the home or behind it to park. The intent was to avoid creating that problem for
                   those businesses it was decided it best to say they must be in an approved location but not
                   necessarily on the side or behind the house. Regarding the terminology of “stored” or “parked”,
                   he felt it was a good idea for Letter E to replace “stored” with “parked” for clarity. Staff could
                   make that edit easily if the Commission agrees to it. Commissioner Quigley advised his only
                   concern was substituting the word “parked” with “stored” is you have a contradiction because it
                   says it is okay for a box van or delivery van to be parked out front but in Letter E it is saying that
                   they cannot park them on site. It is a conflict because “out front” is “onsite”. There are vehicles
                   identified in Letter E that aren’t allowed as a home occupation but any vehicles that are allowed
                   can be parked onsite.      Commissioner Quigley asked if “food trucks” could be included in that
                   language. Mr. Spung said they could do that but he asked Code Enforcement about food trucks
                   and they advised they hadn’t seen many during their daily travels. He asked them about the
                   signage question in having business vehicles with big racks or obnoxious signage advertising the
                   business and that was something they hadn’t seen much of either. They also have not had

                   However, it would not be all inclusive because there are so many varieties of vehicles.         Staff
                   would like to defer to Code Enforcement to make the determination on individual instances.
                   That, aside from tow trucks, the City doesn’t receive many complaints in this regard.
                   Commissioner McElreath felt it is fine the way it is, and Commissioner Wright agreed with that.


         4.2.5     Mr. Spung continued on to say there had been a sentence added to Letter G which says, “For
                   purposes of this standard, trailer length does not include the trailer tongue”.      Mr. Spung said
                   that Code Enforcement has expressed issues and the following sentence was added in that regard
                   as Letter H, “Auto transport trailers designed to transport more than one vehicle or that exceed the
                   body length requirements are prohibited and shall not be stored on-site. (he advised that could be
                   clarified by just saying “parked” instead of “stored”. Such trailers may be located off-site in an
                   approved storage location”. Another concern for Code Enforcement is apparently there are
                   problems with double-decker car trailers that are pulled by big trucks and are not suitable for
                   residential neighborhoods. Therefore, that was added in the not allowed section. Commissioner
                   Young said he has seen the double decker trailers but there are also others that are single layer
                   trailers. He understood why they would not be allowed for a business but would be for personal
                   homeowners to use for hauling ATVs, etc. He wondered if that was being considered. Mr. Spung
                   advised that for right now this just involves home occupations and wouldn’t address private
                   recreational use.        That issue can be discussed when doing the larger re-write of the Code
                   update is done and right now it just involves a business registered to a house.

         4.2.6     Mr. Spung said the next portion is related to daycares, preschools and other high intensity home
                   occupations which involve instruction.        There was a lot of discussion during the last meeting
                   about the location requirements for where these are and are not allowed. He read existing
                   sentence #2, “A residential child day care/preschool home instruction with five or more kids, shall
                   only be allowed in a detached single-family dwelling that fronts on a public or private street with
                   a minimum pavement width of 25 feet that has curb, gutter, and sidewalk. (added) “The minimum
                   25-foot pavement width and curb, gutter, and sidewalk shall extend from the home occupation lot
                   or parcel to the nearest through street with those same improvements”.        That answers the
                   question if they install curb, gutter, and sidewalk in front of their home, would they then be
                   allowed to have that business. The answer is no because those improvements must connect to

                   the next through street with curb, gutter, and sidewalk. He displayed a map showing areas of the
                   City that don’t have sidewalks, which turn out to be older residential areas and generally don’t
                   have through streets or the high traffic volume. Commissioner Wilkey asked if there is an older
                   area of town that was built prior to Taylorsville incorporating where curb, gutter, and sidewalks
                   was not required but now the requirement would be instituted for this requirement in order to
                   operate a daycare. She wanted to know if there were an argument that indicates grandfathering
                   would apply to not need those requirements for a new daycare because of pre-existing conditions.
                         Mr. Spung addressed the grandfathering issue by saying, if they have an existing daycare or
                   pre-school, etc., then they could certainly continue that use even after this change is adopted but
                   anyone currently having that use that wants to start a new business would not be eligible for
                   grandfathering conditions based on this standard. The City’s view on that is that the character of
                   those areas are generally more rural in nature, a little bit older, and people generally don’t want a
                   lot of traffic anyway. Daycares and pre-schools generate quite a bit of traffic. Another concern is
                   the safety and access involved. There are presently a couple of daycares that are on a dead end
                   street on the City’s east side which present safety concerns, for example for emergency vehicles.
                   Commissioner Wilkey said that she supports this change but is trying to anticipate what the
                   residents are going to say. Like if the City requires curb, gutter and sidewalk their question might
                   be why has the City not made those improvements for them.              Mr. Spung advised that in his
                   experience he has noted that people who live in these particular areas do not want curb, gutter
                   and sidewalk, so if the City were to say they were going to do that, there would probably be push-
                   back by the citizens in not wanting that to be done in order to stay rural. For all new applications
                   for daycares/pre-schools, surrounding neighbors are notified by letter of that intent in order to
                   allow for them to respond to Staff either in favor or opposition. Mr. Spung said that during his
                   conversations with City Administration, other Staff members, and citizens in the community, it was
                   determined that there are several areas of the City where a daycare is a great idea and could be
                   facilitated and infrastructure supports that type of intensity of use. But there are other areas of
                   the City that would not support that type of use. The biggest question is the intensity and number
                   of children involved to generate the number of trips dropping off and picking up. The intent is to
                   let the citizens know that the City hears their concerns and understands that each area is different
                   and have Code standards reflect what is appropriate in the neighborhoods.

         4.2.7     Mr. Spung advised that he would bring up with the City Council, the issue of food trucks being
                   “parked” or “stored” on private property and high intensity instruction uses. Commissioner
                   Wright asked Mr. Spung if there needed to be a public hearing tonight, to which he replied that
                   the public hearing was already done but since the notice was advertised as a public hearing, it
                   could be opened up tonight for comment.

         4.2.8     Commissioner Quigley commented that the Planning Commission recently approved a daycare
                   on Player’s Ridge that did not have curb, gutter and sidewalk and wanted to know if that would
                   have been approved with this change in place. Mr. Spung said it would not. That along with no
                   curb, gutter and sidewalk, it is in an Accessory Dwelling Unit and is within 150’ of another existing
                   daycare; none of which would be allowed under this change. He explained that Staff’s goal is to
                   listen to citizens and understand their views and base the Code on what is best for the
                   neighborhoods. Commissioner Quigley advised he just wanted to make that a point of record
                   that the Commission recently approved a daycare that under this new Code, would not have been
                   approved.

Other Matters

       5.     Planning Commission Review of the Taylorsville Land Development Code Update Project – Chapter
              13.01: General Provisions. (Jim Spung/AICP/Senior Planner)


5.1              Mr. Spung opened a discussion of Chapter 13.01 of the Taylorsville Land Development Code update
            project. He discussed the purpose and need for having such a document                He conducted his review of
            Chapter 13.01 – General Provisions, explained what had been added to the Code, the reasons in so doing
            and answered Commissioner’s questions. There was a lengthy discussion following and Mr. Spung
            explained that they would be reviewing individual chapters in the next few weeks during work sessions
            before a review draft of the document is made. A few of the bigger changes coming to the new Code
            include defining Brew Pubs, to see if they fit within this City, to establish a more streamlined appeal process
            and establish the need/means to build meaningful places, which include aesthetics and beauty themes to
            identify this City. Mr. Spung suggested that adding purpose statements would be really helpful and have
            been included throughout the Code document. That he had taken notes of their comments tonight, which
            will be incorporated into the next work session for clarity. Chapter 2 is the next one up for review and will
            be presented at the next work session. Commissioner Wright suggested they take more time in reviewing
            Chapter 2 during the next work session. That it is more important to get it right than fast. He wanted to
            make sure the general ideas they forward are sound.

5.2         Mr. Spung advised that the table is easier to read than the numerous pages of footnotes. He explained
            how to use the links he had incorporated into the tables to make it easier to navigate.
