37          Animal Control Discussion
38
39   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, provided a presentation on proposed options for establishing a City-
40   operated animal shelter, highlighting the need for a Council decision to facilitate a budget
41   adjustment by December 3 and expedite the project. The presentation included a brief history of
42   the City's animal shelter services, noting a seven-year partnership with Stone Ridge Veterinary
43   Services that saved the City approximately $1.5 million compared to contracting with Salt Lake
44   County Animal Services.
45
46   The proposal outlined five potential locations for the shelter, all leveraging existing City-owned
47   land or rental options to minimize costs. These included:
48
49         Station 120: Retrofitting part of an existing fire station while maintaining ambulance
50          operations. However, it was noted this location may not serve as a long-term solution
51          due to future projections for fire service expansions by 2030.
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1           Water Shop Land: Building a new facility on City-owned land near the Old Farm Park
 2            and fishing pond.
 3
 4           Water Reception Area (Variant of Option 2): Incorporating the water reception area as
 5            the public-facing component of a new shelter on the same land.
 6
 7           Garamandi Well Site: Repurposing an abandoned well site, which is already budgeted
 8            for decommissioning.
 9
10           Flex Space Facility: Renting and renovating a local flex space building, following a model
11            currently used by Herriman.
12
13   Mr. Hicks emphasized the importance of deciding quickly, with temporary solutions such as
14   continued use of Stone Ridge or temporary support from Herriman available through June 2024.
15
16   Mayor Staggs favored the option to have the animal shelter at the Water Shop with the
17   reception area used as the public entrance. He said this modified approach would reduce costs
18   rather than building a separate structure for customer entry.

20   Councilmember McCay said she leaned toward Option B (Water Shop) as the most viable
21   choice. She cited its affordability compared to other permanent options and its use of an existing
22   City-owned site, which would maximize utility without additional land acquisition. She
23   expressed concerns about potential impacts on residents near Sanborn Drive if public access
24   were routed there and suggested positioning the entrance near the park and pond area.

28   explored the option of a nonprofit partnership and highlighted key concerns with outsourcing
29   shelter management, including potential boarding costs and loss of direct control over the
30   shelter's operations and public interactions.
31
32   Councilmember Pierucci emphasized his discomfort with the high costs associated with any of
33   the proposed options relative to the small number of animals and residents served. He
34   expressed concerns about the cost-effectiveness of the investment, describing it as a point of
35   personal struggle.

38   drawing on a family member’s experience in the sector. He explained that these nonprofits

40   boarding or shelter services. Many operate with a "no-kill" policy, which could place Riverton in
41   a similar situation as its current model, with limited capacity for handling animals.

39   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, presented a development agreement for a
40   vacant property located at the northeast corner of 13400 South and 2700 West, a parcel
41   approximately 0.32 acres in size. This property is surrounded by developed properties, with
42   the large undeveloped tract located to the south across 13400 South. The agreement
43   proposes to modify the land use and zoning of the property.
44
45   Mr. Lethbridge explained that a development agreement combines zoning and specific
46   standards, including restrictions or allowances, tailored to a specific parcel. In this case, the
47   proposal is to change the zoning to RM-6, which typically requires a minimum of half an acre.
48   The application also includes requests for additional units, modifications to setbacks, and
49   adjustments to fencing requirements, specifically opting for existing fencing standards instead
50   of the typical solid masonry requirement. Development agreements are supported by Riverton
51   City and Utah State code but require public hearings, just like a rezone. The Planning
     Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   Commission held multiple hearings and ultimately recommended denial of the proposal.

 3   Dan McCay, Applicant, presented the proposed development on a 0.32-acre lot at 2682 West,
 4   13400 South and discussed several factors related to traffic and density, which were concerns
 5   raised during the Planning Commission’s review process.
 6
 7   Regarding traffic, Mr. McCay acknowledged that traffic is a concern in the area, noting that the
 8   Wasatch Front Regional Council’s traffic projections may not account for further development
 9   in the area, such as the nearby Bangerter interchange. He shared data showing that cul-de-
10   sacs generally generate fewer daily trips compared to streets like 13400 South and 2700
11   West, which have much higher traffic volumes. A traffic engineer had advised that the
12   westward exit would be safer for ingress and egress due to better visibility and lower speeds,
13   while the eastward exit would pose more challenges due to traffic congestion and limited
14   visibility.
15
16   Mr. McCay also addressed concerns about density, noting that current zoning allows for
17   single-family homes and accessory dwelling units (ADUs), which could be built without
18   needing the development agreement. However, he proposed a layout that would allow for two
19   single-family homes with shared walls, increasing yard space while also ensuring safer traffic
20   flow on the lot. The design also took into account maintaining visibility at the corner, which had
21   been a concern for traffic safety.

23   Councilmember Pierucci asked the applicant why he was seeking a development agreement
24   as opposed to a rezone.

26   In response, Mr. McCay shared concerns about the potential issues that might arise from
27   modifying zoning regulations for a single lot. He expressed support for higher-density zoning
28   along high-traffic corridors like Redwood Road. He also highlighted the difficulty of adjusting
29   zoning codes for this one lot without unintentionally creating inconsistencies or opening the
30   door for broader, less controlled zoning changes throughout the City. He concluded that a
31   development agreement would be a practical tool to handle the specific issues related to this
32   lot, while still allowing for flexibility in addressing its unique circumstances.

 9            Organizations, Actions, and Recommendations of the Planning Commission,
10            Amendments Proposed by Riverton City
11
12   Mr. Lethbridge explained that the proposed update would clarify inconsistencies in the
13   Planning Commission code. The current code mandates bi-monthly meetings using the term
14   "shall," which implies meetings must occur, even during holidays. The proposed amendment
15   specifies that meetings are generally held on the second and fourth Thursdays but may be
16   rescheduled for reasons such as holiday conflicts, lack of agenda items, or joint discussions
17   with the Council, provided proper notice is given.
18
19   Additionally, the code includes language about Planning Commission members issuing
20   concurring or dissenting reports or recommendations, which has not been practiced in over 25
21   years. Instead, members express their agreement or dissent through votes and comments
22   without separate recommendations or motions.
23
24   The update, not classified as a land use item, did not require Planning Commission input and
25   was presented directly to the Council for approval.

 2   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that the ordinance allowed for the creation of bylaws to
 3   address operational details after the program is established. He supported the idea that the

 5   roles and positions within the program. He emphasized that bylaws should handle day-to-day
