The applicant is Dean Schulte.  The property is owned by his son, Stephen Schulte.  The

acres.  The location of the structure on the property was demonstrated on a slide.      
 
Planner Northrup-Robinson said the structure is approximately 960 square feet and is a

garage/shop.  The building was completed on April 29, 2015.  Since that time, it was

approval for a CUP.  She said that Staff recommends approval with the findings of fact,

Planner Strader explained under the cluster-bonus subdivision program, there is a 
provision that if at least 80% of the parcel is preserved for contiguous agricultural use, 
the landowner is eligible for two times the density that would otherwise be allowed.  
She added this is only allowed in the AP-40 zone district.   
 
Planner Strader said this property has three different zone districts.  There is the 
highway corridor (HC) zone, the AP-40 zone, and the AG-100 zone.  Taking everything 
into account, the applicant is eligible for 20 lots.  An aerial photo of the property was 
shown.     
 
Planner Strader said an additional requirement for the cluster bonus program is that a 
portion of the property has to be actively used for agriculture.  Another is that a note 
will be placed on the plat and that a deed restriction will be recorded with the plat to 
ensure the continued agricultural use of the property.   
 
Planner Strader said the southwest side of the road has been used for hay production 
since 1932.  The proposed plat note and deed restriction can be found in the packet as 
Exhibit B.  A site map was shown.  It showed that seven lots are being located on the 
north side of Highway 35.  Three lots are located on the south side of the road.  There 
are slopes over 30% on the north side of the property.  Because of this, the applicant 
has placed building pads at the toe of the hill.  The building pads will ensure that houses 
will not be built on a ridgeline or located on steep slopes.   
 
Planner Strader said there is also a small area of wetlands on the property.  The 
building pads have been located outside of the required 40-foot wetland setback.  She 
said there will be an access off Highway 35 that will serve lots 1-6.  There is an existing 
access for Lot 7 on the south end.  
 
A slide of the plat map was shown.  Three lots were located on the south side of 
Highway 35.  Planner Strader said to ensure that the agricultural use of the property 
will remain, building pads were established.  There are two ditches that run through the 
property.   Both ditches have a 40-foot easement.  Notes were placed on the plat 
regarding the ditches and what can and cannot be done in the easement area.   
 
Planner Strader said the applicant is proposing individual wells and septic systems on 
the lots.  The septic systems have to be approved by the Eastern Summit County Sewer 
Advisory Committee (ESAC) before final approval is received from the Planning 
Commission.  The Health Department has reviewed the application and finds it 
complies with their requirements.  Staff requests the Planning Commission discuss any 
issues that should be addressed prior to moving forward with a public hearing.

Commissioner Clyde asked Planner Strader to explain what ESAC is and how it 
operates.  She said this committee was established a number of years ago.  It is a special 
service district that covers Eastern Summit County with the exception of the Indian 
Hollow subdivision, which has its own special service district. Anytime a subdivision is 
proposed, ESAC reviews the waste water options.   
 
Chair Ure asked if ESAC can require that a sewer system be installed.  Planner Strader

the State requirements are met, the Health Department must approve them.  ESAC 
works with the Health Department and follows their lead.
