1.           FIRST Lego League – Cookies and Milk Presentation (23-011) (10:45)

Chair MacKay introduced Rachel Argyle, Sadie Berges, Maylin McGowan, Adelaide Wingate, and Hope
Wingate, from Centennial Middle School and Timpview High School. She said she heard the presentation
at the UMPA conference and was impressed.

The group explained what the Lego League is and said this year’s theme was based on answering the
question “How do we improve the energy journey in our community?”. They discussed the challenges
that Utah faces when it comes to energy. Through their research, they discovered that Utah is one of the
best solar areas in the United States. They shared the solar options available and decided to focus on the
benefits of Floating Solar.

After the presentation, Councilor Ellsworth asked how long they researched and what the most
challenging part was.

They said they had been researching since last fall and finding the right resources was the most
challenging part of the project.

Councilor Ellsworth congratulated them on their hard work and said she is excited to see them become
electrical engineers and come work for Provo Power.

6.         A presentation on the proposed tentative budget for Provo City for Fiscal Year 2023-2024.
           (23-008) (49:15)

Mayor Kaufusi read and discussed the goals of this year’s budget.

John Borget, Director of Administrative Services, shared that the budget is available to view on the city’s
website. For the public’s benefit, he said the council has met with all departments to discuss their
budget needs. He then discussed the highlights of this year’s budget. He encouraged feedback from the
council and from residents.

Councilor Ellsworth asked about dependency on increased taxes.

Mr. Borget responded that the budget does not include a property tax increase. He discussed the library
rates and how they relate to tax increases.

Councilor Shipley asked about sales tax increase assumptions.

Mr. Borget discussed the sales tax revenue and how this year is trending compared to last year.

Councilor Shipley said he would like to see ways departments are looking to reduce headcount and debt.
He stated he would like to see that included in the summary statement of the budget.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With none, she closed public comment.

With no objections, the Provo Municipal Council adjourned and reconvened as the Governing Board of
the Redevelopment Agency of Provo with Chair Ellsworth conducting.

9.         A resolution authorizing the Mayor to submit an application for a Utah County Municipal
           Recreation Grant to be used for restroom and playground improvements at Rock Canyon
           Park. (23-044) (1:21:55)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-17, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Doug Robins, Assistant Parks and Recreation Director, presented. He explained the annual grant is from
Utah County and he shared what this grant has done for the city in previous years. He said this year,
they are requesting to use the funds for restroom and playground improvements at Rock Canyon Park.

Councilor Hoban asked if this grant could be used for restrooms at other parks that do not currently
have restrooms.

Mr. Robins said this could be used anywhere in the city.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With none, she closed public comment.

Councilor Whipple expressed her thanks for the Parks and Recreation department for finding these
grants to use instead of city funds. She echoed Councilor Hoban’s remarks about adding bathrooms to
parks that do not have restrooms instead of upgrading one that does. She said she hopes to see those
projects high on the priority list in the coming years.

Councilor Ellsworth requested a discussion of the Parks and Recreation Capital Improvement Plan in a
future work session.

Councilor Hoban shared he would be voting against this resolution.

10.         A resolution appropriating $1,060,000 in the Mountain Vista Fund for completing the
            Mountain Vista Business Park Road construction project. (23-006) (1:29:50)

       Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-18, as currently constituted, has been
                    made by council rule.

Ms. Zarbock presented.

Councilor Shipley asked for a brief description of this fund and what the plan is for the future.

Mr. Borget discussed the Mountain Vista Fund’s history of being owned by USX. He said the city has an
agreement where the city will be paying USX back, which is being tracked. He mentioned there was an
interfund loan for the improvements at the golf course which will be paid back by receiving increment
payments from the housing that is to be built adjacent to the medical school. He said those payments
will pay the loan back plus some funds that can be used for other capital improvement projects. He said
the only money that has been used from the Mountain Vista Fund has been for road infrastructure and
improvements.

Councilor Shipley asked if there is a set amount that is due to USX or if the amount is contingent on the
completion of the whole project.

Mr. Borget said there is a set amount due. He stated the finance department has requested some
information from USX prior to sending any payments and they are waiting to hear back from them. He
added that the amount that is in the fund will cover the full cost of what is owed, while leaving some in
the fund.

Chair MacKay opened public comment. With no comments, and no council discussion, she called for a
vote.

       Vote:        The motion was approved 5:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Hoban, Mackay, Shipley, and
                    Whipple in favor. Councilors Bill Fillmore and George Handley excused.

12.         An ordinance amending the General Plan Map classification of approx. 0.23 acres of real
            property, generally located at 2050 North Canyon Road, from Commercial (C) to
            Residential (R). Pleasant View Neighborhood. (PLRZ20220301) (1:37:35)

       Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-16, as currently constituted, has been
                    made by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented. He discussed the concept plan that went through the
Planning Commission and shared some of the feedback from staff. He commended the applicants’
willingness to listen to neighborhood and staff feedback and recommendations. He described what
these Ordinances would accomplish.

Ryan Salmon, applicant and resident of Provo, shared the desired outcome of this project.

Chair MacKay thanked Mr. Salmon for listening to neighborhood feedback. She said she is thrilled by this
development. She asked what his plan is for ensuring they stay owner occupied.

Mr. Salmon said he does not have a definite plan but has explored options.

Councilor Whipple asked if these would be condominiums with an HOA.

Mr. Salmon said yes, it would be condos governed by an HOA. He mentioned the possibility of having
the neighboring complexes under the same HOA since they will face similar obstacles.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment.

West side of the city.
