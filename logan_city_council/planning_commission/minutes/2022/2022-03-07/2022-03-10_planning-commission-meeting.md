                                  PLANNING COMMISSION MINUTES

Minutes of the meeting for the Logan City Planning Commission convened in regular session

p.m.

PUBLIC HEARING

 PC 22-009 Foothill Lofts Residential Subdivision-Plat Modification -Continued from the
 February 10th meeting [Subdivision Permit] Rick Champlin/Foothill Lofts Residential LLC,
 authorized agent/owner, request a subdivision permit to amend their previously approved plan
 and remove the required third access off 1300 East. The project is located at approximately 1250
 North 1300 East in the Suburban Neighborhood Residential (NR-4) zone; TIN 05-011-0005.
 (Hillcrest Neighborhood)


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a 25-lot single-family subdivision on 8.37 acres located
at approximately 1250 North 1300 East. The proposal is to amend their previously approved project
and remove the third required access off 1300 East. This is the fourth subdivision proposal from this
owner for this property in the last two years. First, in August of 2020 (PC #20-011), a 23-lot
subdivision was proposed with a northeast street stub with the potential of connecting to 1400 East.
After debating neighborhood issues, the proposal was denied by the Planning Commission. Second,
in October of 2020 (PC #20-052), a 25-lot subdivision was proposed showing no street stubs to the
east and only two street connections onto 1300 East. After debating street connectivity standards,
the Planning Commission also denied that application. Thirdly, in December of 2020 (PC-#20-059),
a 25-lot subdivision was submitted showing three street connections onto 1300 East. The Planning
Commission approved this application. In December of 2021, PC #20-059 expired since the final plat
had not been approved and recorded.
Previously, in 2017 and under different ownership, A 19-lot Subdivision was approved as part of the
multi-family Design Review Permit. The 19-lot subdivision did not propose stubbing streets or a third
street connection. That subdivision permit expired in 2018.


The land development code requires subdivisions with more than 20 units to have 3 street
connections, 9-20 units to have 2 connections, and 1-8 units to have 1 connection. The applicant is
requesting a deviation from the street connections standards due to the constraints associated with
this project site. The commission will need to determine if the deviation proposal meets the
connectivity standards in the Land Development Code:

§17.30.170    Connectivity Standards
              A. All streets must connect to other streets, forming a grid street pattern that extends Logan’s
                   historic street grid. Mini-blocks are the preferred road configuration (See Figure
                   17.30.170.A.1), except where physical conditions of the site or abutting properties
                   preclude street connections. Wherever the street layout cannot conform exactly to the grid
                   pattern due to topographic constraints, pre-existing development patterns, or the presence
                   of critical areas, a modified grid which provides a close match shall be achieved.
             D. The hearing body may grant adjustments to the standards in Section 17.30.160 upon
                  finding that the proposal is consistent with the purpose of the zone in which it is located, is
                 consistent with the intent of this section, and there are no practical design alternatives for
                 complying with the subject standard.

Commissioner Lewis asked about the increase in traffic and the intersections. R. Holley said that
1300 North and 1300 East are considered local streets that do not accommodate large volumes of


traffic or high speeds. The engineering department has determined that the current intersections are
sufficient.

PROPONENT: Rick Champlin said that their proposal meets the grid street pattern, the third
access is unnecessary and not practical, the property is accessible and will be a nicer community
without three accesses so close to each other. He also stated that there have been several
exemptions allowed on subdivisions throughout Logan, allowing them to have less than three
access points.

PUBLIC: Jeanie Simmonds asked the commission to consider the ramifications of an exemption and
asked what other properties received an exemption for less than three access points.

Craig Champlin said that it does not make sense to have three exits right in a row. They are not
asking for an additional building lot, the center lot where the third access was will be a non-buildable
lot used for a landscaped area.

Ian Troesoyer said that the previous plan with the access to the Northeast better meets the general
plan, the grid system, and better meets connectivity. The proposed loop does not improve walkability
in the area. Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director said that the previous plan with the
access to the Northeast is no longer a viable option.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Lewis asked if other subdivisions received an exemption on the
number of entrances. R. Holley recalled one subdivision, Harvest Meadows near Bridger Elementary
that did exceed 20 units and had constraints that did not allow the third access. This project was
approved with an exemption allowing two connections.

Eldon stated that he is in favor of the proposed plan, it seems reasonable considering there are no
other connection options. The third connection onto 1300 East does not benefit the subdivision and
is not a practical option. Commissioner Lewis said that the third access seems ridiculous. He does
not like the idea of setting a precedence of allowing exemptions, but the physical restraints of this
site make a good argument for the approval of two street connections.

 PC 22-015 Roofers Supply [Design Review & Conditional Use Permit] Michael McHugh/Pappas
 Limited Company, authorized agent/owner request a design review permit to build a new 23,160
 SF warehouse and a 3,600 SF office space. The conditional use permit is for outdoor storage
 needs. The project is located at 765 West 200 North in the Commercial (COM) zone; TIN 05-
 062-0038. (Ellis Neighborhood)


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a Conditional Use and Design Review Permit to construct
a new 26,700 SF Roofers Supply office and warehouse building with outdoor merchandise storage,
parking areas, and landscaping. The existing 2.97-acre site has four smaller structures that are
planned for demolition for the new development. The proposed L-shaped building sits near the west
side of the property with a parking lot near the south side and the 200 North driveway access.

The Commercial zone conditionally permits wholesale sales and services and parking lots to be
located in front yards. The Commission will need to determine if the proposed parking and outdoor
storage comply with the Land Development Code Section 17.42.050.

Conditional Use Permits allow the Planning Commission to appropriately mitigate any unique
adverse impacts created by the land use and specific proposal.

§17.42.050 Planning Commission Action
The Commission may approve or conditionally approve a conditional use permit that is based on an objective
standard in compliance with Utah Code (10-9a-507) and only upon substantiating the following findings:

A. The proposed use is consistent with the allowable maximum densities of the underlying zone.
B. The proposed use is consistent with the requirements of the Land Development Code.
C. The use is compatible with surrounding land uses and will not interfere with the use and enjoyment of
   adjoining properties.
D. The site will be served by infrastructure having sufficient capacities to meet the service demands of the
   proposed use.
E. The proposed use is compatible with the surrounding neighborhood character as defined in Section
   17.62.
F. The proposed access is consistent with Logan City access and roadway standards and Utah Department
   of Transportation requirements where applicable.
G. The conditional use is aimed at mitigating the possible negative impacts of excessive light, noise, and
   traffic.

The outdoor storage if limited in height and bulk could be considered compatible by limiting the
product staking to less than six feet in height, which is the same height as the fencing, visual
impacts can be mitigated. The front yard parking lot location also requires a Conditional Use
Permit. To approve this, the surrounding neighborhood character and similar adjacent parking
layout should be considered. Both properties to the east and west have some parking stalls
located in the front yard. A parking lot in the front yard for this property will not significantly alter the
existing character of the area. By limiting the parking lot in the front to no more than 50% of the
required stalls, as the LDC states, the inconspicuous size and visual impact will remain minimal.
The applicant is currently working to resolve some boundary line and access issues that will have
to be completed before a building permit is obtained. If there are any substantial changes to the
site plan the project will return to the Planning Commission for approval.
The warehouse portion of the “L” shaped building located approximately 230 feet away (north side)
from the street does not contain 30% fenestration, addressed in condition number 4.
Commissioner Lewis asked where the fence will be located. R. Holley said that the fence is proposed
along the front of the property, in the southeast corner, and along the east side of the site, and can
be up to 8’ tall. Commissioner Heare asked what the access issue is and if there is a proposed plan.
R. Holley said that U DOT will not allow access off 200 North, so the applicant is currently working
with the neighboring property owner on a joint access agreement.

PROPONENT: John Pappas said that they are planning on putting up a rod iron fence along the
front, the storage is far enough from the street that it shouldn’t be an issue.


PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Eldon said that the rod iron fence is attractive, but it does not completely shield or
hide the storage area. Commissioner Lewis said that the landscaping will help block the view of the
storage and he is also ok with transparency as proposed. Commissioner Heare said that this will be
a huge improvement to the site and agreed that the landscaping will help to screen the storage. It
was discussed to amend condition number 2, to strike “storage no taller than 6 FT” and replace it
with “storage no taller than the fence”. It was also discussed to remove condition number 4 requiring
30% transparency.
