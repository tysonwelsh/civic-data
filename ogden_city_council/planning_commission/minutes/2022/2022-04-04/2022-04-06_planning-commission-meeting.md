3. CBD Site Plan, for exterior revisions to 2420 Washington Boulevard. (Attachment 3)

  Mr. George Daines indicated Cache Valley Bank has purchased the historic bank building as well as the
  commercial building to the south. This request is to remove the façade from the front of the south
  building to determine its original finish material and to return with a request for its front wall treatment.
  In addition, they are proposing to demolish the annex building at the rear of the main bank building in
  order to create an entryway plaza and stairwell while retaining the underground portion of that building.
  They also are working to assure the building remains secure and provide weather barriers for any part of
  the building which becomes exposed.

  Mr. Brierley stated the first request is to remove the east portion of building to the south and expose the
  original wall, as well as the two above-ground stories of the eastern annex building. As the bank
  building is on the Ogden City Register of Historic Resources, the Landmarks Commission has previously
  reviewed and approved the removal of the east annex building subject to care being taken to not
  damage the main building as well as retaining the windows to be reinstalled in the main historic
  building. This action is based on their findings the annex building has no historic value as its design
  does not match the original bank tower. There is no pictorial evidence of the façade on the adjacent
  building, but it appears the existing façade was installed during the 1970’s. The determination of the
  Commission is assure the finish is compatible with the character of the downtown in terms of material
  compatibility and architectural lines. He stated this existing façade is top-heavy and overhangs into the
  public right-of-way, making it structurally unsound. It also is not typical of the architectural theme of the
  downtown. Staff recommends approval subject to a plan being provided to address temporary
  protection of building surface after demolition and if a permanent replacement of these surfaces has
  not begun within one year of the demolition permit, a plan for durable replacement and protection of the
  building surfaces which meets the CBD criteria be submitted.

  Commissioner Safsten commented the time period is appropriate and security of the building is
  important. Commissioner Schade asked about the existing alley to assure continued access to other
  existing buildings on the block. It was suggested a condition of approval could be added to assure
  vehicular access is maintained.

  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the proposed
                site plan based on the findings it complies with the purpose, intent and requirements of
                the CBD zone, maximizes safety of vehicle and pedestrian movement and provides
                adequate screening. Approval is subject to the following conditions:
                1. A plan be provided for temporary protection of the exposed building surfaces once the
                     demolition is complete;
                2. If a permanent replacement of the exposed building surfaces has not begun within
                     one year of the demolition permit being issued, a plan be submitted to identify a
                     durable replacement and protection of the building surfaces that meet the CBD site

                3. If possible, the existing second-story windows on the annex building be salvaged and

4. Proposed PRUD Amendment, for Sunset Ridge PRUD located at 5755 Harrison Boulevard. (Attachment 4)

  Mr. Vaughn Jacobsen stated he is requesting the required wrought iron fence at the entry to the project
  be eliminated as it provides no security to the project and allow a solid fence to be installed with its
  location to be worked out between the property owner and the HOA. He is also requesting trails which
  were proposed adjacent to the sidewalk be eliminated, allowing the sidewalk to be used for access to
  the trail. He is also requesting the trail location to be relocated behind the clubhouse and that the fence
  requirement along Harrison be eliminated in favor of grasses, trees and shrubs to be approved by the
  Ogden City arborist as the wall would serve little purpose in reducing noise from traffic on Harrison. He
  stated due to topography of the site, there is no need for a sound wall, and the approved wall would
  provide no visual or noise buffer. He suggested there may be potential for a wrought iron fence to
  define the western boundary of the project.

  Commissioner Safsten asked Mr. Jacobsen to identify what has changed since the original approval of
  this project which would warrant an amendment. Chair Graf asked about landscaping maintenance on
  this western boundary noting it is too steep for lawn to be cared for. Mr. Jacobsen stated while there
  have been no physical changes to the property, its topography is a concern. He also suggested there
  may be potential for graffiti on a solid fence. Mr. Daniel Stephenson stated he has purchased the
  property identified as phases 3 and 4 and after reviewing the original drawings in relation to the existing
  topography, as some of the proposed trails were adjacent to sidewalks, he felt they served no purpose
  and is requesting they be eliminated, allowing residents to access the trail from the sidewalks. He also
  is requesting the solid fence on Harrison not be required, allowing the area to be finished with rocks,
  shrubs and trees. He also stated a storm water facility is required and is suggesting some of the
  approved trail amenities be relocated to this area to provide a gathering place. He felt a more natural
  treatment of the western edge along Harrison would be better than the approved solid wall, noting that
  plantings could help to stabilize the hillside and absorb ground water. He is considering a plan to
  provide trees, shrubs and groundcover plantings.

  nearly completed on the first two phases but no work has been done on the other two phases. He
  reviewed the requested revisions with the Staff recommendations as follows:
   1. Delete the approved 4’ iron fence along Shadow Valley Drive and approve the 6’ tall solid privacy

           home. As this is a PRUD, the Commission may consider a variance if a finding can be made
           that it does not isolate surrounding neighborhoods, is consistent with the development theme
           of the project and is compatible with the neighborhood. Staff recommends the solid fence be
           retained.

5. CBD Site Plan, for Wall Avenue Apartments, 2023 Wall Avenue. (Attachment 5)

  Mr. Pierre Langue stated this project is for a four-story apartment building containing 28 units. The
  building is designed close to the street with parking hidden behind the building while allowing vehicular
  access around the building. The architecture contains shadow boxes on the north an east sides and
  extended balconies. Horizontal lines exist between the 2nd and 3rd stories. He felt the project complies
  with existing zoning regulations while there may be conflict with the downtown guidelines. He stated
  while the property is in the CBD zone, it is on the outside edge and existing buildings in the area are
  industrial in character.

  Ms. Cassandra Tavolarella stated as the design guidelines are not adopted by ordinance, they should
  not be applicable.

  Mr. Brierley stated the apartment building is proposed for the corner of Wall and 20th, with Rushton to
  the south. The plan shows an L-shaped building with 78 units and 118 parking spaces. The building
  fronts on both Wall and 20th Street and parking is located in the rear, The façade proposed is a dark
  gray brick base with gray and white concrete panels and cedar accents on the upper floors. While the
  property is on the edge of the CBD, it is still within its boundaries and the regulations of the ordinance
  are applicable. The purpose statement in the CBD Ordinance direction is to promote orderly growth and
  blend with the character of downtown buildings, with particular attention to material compatibility and
  architectural lines. He reviewed existing buildings along both Wall Avenue and within the CBD, stating
  while some variety in materials is acceptable, most of the downtown buildings have a more traditional
  character with brick facades. The Commission also is to consider both pedestrian and vehicular traffic
  circulation and landscaping. He stated due to the speed of traffic along Wall Avenue the sidewalk
  should be moved away from the street to improve safety of pedestrians and tenants. He stated the site
  plan is acceptable with some minor revisions but expressed concern that the architecture does not fit
  with the character or theme of the downtown. He reviewed items on the façade which were concerning
  to Staff and recommended denial.

  Commissioner Schade suggested if the building were moved away from Wall Avenue there would be
  better walking space for pedestrians. He also asked about the transition area between the garage doors
  and asked if on-site landscaping complies with the ordinance stating it appears to be minimal. He also
  suggested the long edge of the building could be flipped so it is on Rushton Street so residential units
  are further from the noise of the adjacent streets. Commissioner Safsten while there is a benefit to
  facing Wall Avenue and he envisions mixed use and multi-family along the Wall Avenue corridor
  between 20th Street and the Intermodal Hub. He stated while the proposal is an improvement to the

  downtown, stating the City is working with a consultant to establish ordinances which would

6. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to consider allowing manufacture of food products in
   the BEI zone. (Attachment 6)

  Mr. Grant Ellis stated he has been operating an ice cream manufacturing facility in Cache County and
  desires to relocate to Ogden. He has selected this area as he has the option to purchase the property
  where in other industrial areas leases are required. He stated while food sales is allowed in this zone, its
  manufacture is not. The use produces no offensive odor or noise, and he felt the use would be a good
  fit for this area. He reviewed the manufacturing process using nonfat dry milk and butter and no silos
  are required for food storage.

  Mr. Brierley stated this request is to amend the regulations of the BEI zone to allow food manufacturing.
  He identified the location of the only BEI zone and stated its purpose is to create an area for light
  industrial research and development of outdoor recreation products and uses. He stated food
  manufacturing is allowed in the BEH zone and stated the use fits with the general area. He identified
  the location of other existing food manufacturing uses and stated Ogden has a long history of food-
  related businesses. He noted there is only one vacant lot in the BEI zone and the proposed use would
  contribute to the economy by providing jobs and would occupy an existing vacant lot. Staff
  recommendation is for approval.

  Commission asked why food production was excluded from this zone yet allowed in the BEH zone. Mr.
  Brierley responded the original intent of the BEI zone was to attract outdoor recreation uses, which has
  mostly been accomplished. He felt the proposed ice cream manufacturing business would be a good
  complement to those uses. Commissioner Schade expressed concern about the limited vehicular
  access to this area and asked if there is any consideration for improving the route. Mr. Simpson
  responded direction is given in the West Ogden Plan for improved traffic connections and suggested


                                                      8
  these may be awaiting the anticipated improvements associated with the full I-15 interchange at 24th
  Street, and until those road alignments are determined by UDOT, the City will be aware of its options for
  this area.

As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to close the public
hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Schade and passed unanimously.

  MOTION:        A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to recommend approval of the ordinance
                 amendment as proposed based on the findings it is consistent with the purposes of the
                 BEI zone and the General Plan. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Southwick and
                 passed unanimously with Commissioners Safsten, Schade, Southwick, Stoker and Graf
                 voting aye.
