GPZ-3-2026 Petition by Robbie Thomson requesting a General Plan Change
from Non-Retail Commercial to Medium Density Residential (7 to 12 units/acre)
and a zone change from RB (Residential Business) to RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling
Residential). The property is located at 5548 West 4100 South on 1.9 acres. (Staff
- Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov.


Discussion: Steve presented the petition by Robbie Thomson requesting a General
Plan Change from Non-Retail Commercial to Medium Density Residential (7 to 12
units/acre) and a zone change from RB (Residential Business) to RM (Multiple Unit
Dwelling Residential). The property is located at 5548 West 4100 South on 1.9 acres.
Commissioner Durfee asked for clarification regarding the elimination of some of the
property to get to the two acres but not considering the road. Steve responded that
looking at the area for the rezone on the map, all the units are within that 1.9-acre
rectangle, so it’s the, for lack of a better term, the fingers that are excluded.
Commissioner Durfee asked if all the units were for sale, not rent. Steve replies yes.
Commissioner Durfee then brought up the fencing saying that along the business side
he would like to see the block wall continued and on the residential side maybe having
vinyl.
Commissioner Lovato said that he thinks that makes sense, because when we did the
Starbucks, we had a conversation like that and pointed out that there is a huge setback
for the AutoZone. The order board noise would be reduced with a wall.
Commissioner Durfee asked if the two fingers that go out will just be open for public
access and be walkways.
Steve said they would be owned by the HOA so technically the public could use it, but it
would still be private property.
Commissioner Lovato brought up the house in between the fingers and asked if a
homeowner would want walkways on both sides of their property. Commissioner Durfee
agreed.
Steve agreed that it is awkward but, in his view, having at least one would be a good
thing, particularly for people who want to get down to the intersection instead of having
to go around the block.
Commissioner Durfee said it doesn’t even make sense for a playground area because
it’s too close to the major arterial roads.
Commissioner Lovato said he was just thinking of protecting the one home there, but if
it’s part of the application it makes sense.
Commissioner Lovato asked if the street proposed is to be at 54 feet all the way through
and if the circle was all normal regulation size with no private street or anything along
those lines.
Steve explained that it is a public street. With a townhome development, the code does
allow that to be at 44 feet.
Commissioner Durfee asked if the townhomes would require a type A unit? Steve asked
what he meant by that and Commissioner Durfee replied, an accessible unit. Steve said
he could check with Jerry Thompson from building inspections.
Steve said that the sidewalk is roughly 11 feet off the property line, so the back of the
curb is not right on this wall, it’s centered so you have a little bit of landscaping on both
sides of the road.
Commissioner Lovato asked if Steve had received any concerns from residents around
the area regarding the townhomes. Steve said so far he had not.
Commissioner Drozdek asked if the poles in the pictures of the surrounding area were
telephone lines, and if it was inside the property or outside. She also asked if they were
planning on putting it underground or if it was going to stay as it is, above ground.
Steve was not sure if it was power or some other utility, but that he would find out.


Z-1-2026    Petition by Granger Hunter Improvement District requesting a zone
change from A-2 (Agriculture, minimum lot size 2 acres) to R-1-10 (Single Unit
Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 10,000 SF). The property is located at 5901
W Parkway Blvd on 0.29 acres. (Staff- Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3454 or
steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov).


Discussion: Steve presented the petition by Granger Hunter Improvement District
requesting a zone change from A-2 (Agriculture, minimum lot size 2 acres) to R-1-10
(Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 10,000 SF). The property is located
at 5901 W Parkway Blvd on 0.29 acres.
Commissioner Durfee asked if residential homes did go up, can’t they just change the
fence at that point?
Steve said he would hate to have Granger Hunter put up one type of fence now and
then have to change it later. His preference was that if we think masonry makes sense
that we have them do that up front.
Commissioner Lovato said right now it looks like the fence is chain link with barbed wire
on top and wanted to know if they are proposing the same sort of look when doing the
upgrade or is it going to be different.
 Steve said he believed they were doing more of a wrought iron type fence along the
north, east, and south sides.
Commissioner Durfee stated if they are anything like the ones in his city, they are tall
wrought iron with pointed tops.
Steve said right now it is chain link with barbed wire on top.
Commissioner Durfee said his guess is that the fence was probably put in before the
neighborhood was there.
Steve said yes, it’s been there a long time.
Commissioner Lovato asked if they were going to keep the weeds down.
Steve said it does need some TLC.
Commissioner Durfee said he would think if they were going to do a block wall and
you’re potentially going to have homes there, he would put a block wall across, and
wrought iron on the other two and then you are looking in two ways. Then if you
potentially have homes it seems to make sense. Steve said he agreed with that.
Commissioner Lovato asked what the small utility building is on the right? Steve said he
believed it was a small Utopia facility for fiber optics.
Commissioner Lovato said it looks like it has a generator hooked to it, or natural gas, so
he was wondering if it was part of Granger’s, but it sounds like it’s not. Steve said it is
not Granger Hunter. He is pretty sure it’s Utopia.
Commissioner Lovato asked if there was another one that is similar to this that was
upgraded with two really big lots. Steve said yes. It’s across the street from the Maverik
Center on 31st South and Decker Lake Drive. They had one right on the corner and
they demolished that and built another one next to it. They have a 50-year life, so they
build a new one, wait 50 years, tear it down, and build a new one next to it.
Commissioner Lovato said he didn’t understand why they needed the whole driveway.
Steve said they technically don’t need the whole driveway, but essentially, they are
going to hang onto this because in another 50 years this will get demolished and they’ll
build it again on the other side.
Commissioner Durfee said they have one in Sandy and the façade on it looks just like a
house.
Steve said he thinks they have some elevations, and he can include them in the
information.
