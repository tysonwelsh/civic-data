BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Text Amendment to Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC)
Sections 9-23-110 and 9-3-040. The applicant is requesting that a Text Amendment be approved to amend
fencing requirements and other aspects of land use buffers for commercial, multifamily and industrial
properties.


ANALYSIS
Land Use Buffers.
The intent of the land use buffer provisions in DCMC Section 9-23-110 is to enhance the ‘community
appearance and welfare of residents and visitors from the traffic, noise, glare, trash, activity, vibration,
odor, visual disorder and other adverse or harmful effects typically associated with commercial,
multifamily and industrial properties’. Fencing requirements in the current ordinance intended to require
the use of high quality materials that are both durable and sight obscuring. In practice, proposals for
fencing with lightweight polyethylene panels have included fencing containing decorative details that are
non-sight obscuring, and with steel reinforcements limited to the fence posts only. The result is a less
sight obscuring and less durable fence. The proposed ordinance changes clarify that sight obscuring
fencing is required, and remove polyethylene fencing from the list of allowable fencing materials.

The proposal clarifies in the intent statement at the beginning of DCMC Section 9-23-110 that land use
buffers are to be provided along the side and rear property lines of commercial, office, multifamily, and

industrial properties. Grammatical corrections are also made to the intent statement including replacement
of the word affects with effects.

Clarification is added to DCMC Section 9-23-110(A) to indicate that the section regulates both the
amount of landscaping and the type of fencing. Additional references are proposed to be added to DCMC
Section 9-23-110(A)(3) to denote that trees are required in both land use buffer areas described in DCMC
Section 9-23-110(A)(1) and (2). The requirements of DCMC Section 9-23-110(A)(3)(b) are proposed to
be adjusted to clarify the intent of the ordinance to require screening with trees between commercial,
office and industrial uses and adjacent residential lots and uses.

New proposed fencing specifications in DCMC Section 9-23-110(A)(5) include changes to use more
uniform terms such as tall and sight obscuring, as used elsewhere in this section and other sections of the
DCMC. The use of lightweight steel reinforced polyethylene plastic walls is proposed to be removed from
the list of allowable fencing materials under the same section. Additional clarifications regarding the
height measurement of fences not including the gap under fence panels has been added to the proposed
ordinance. Additional limits on the amount of gap allowable under fencing panels has also been proposed
reducing the allowance from eight (8) inches found in the definition of a fence in DCMC Section 9-3-040
down to a maximum of four (4) inches. An allowance for height reductions to the required fencing when
done for the purposes of complying with clear view areas outlined in DCMC Section 9-27-080 is also
included in the proposed revision of DCMC Section 9-23-110(A)(5).

Under the allowable reductions specified in DCMC Section 9-23-110(C) the proposed ordinance revisions
include revisions to reference the fencing specifications of DCMC Section 9-23-110(A)(5) to make clear
that reductions to the fencing material and design standards are not allowable reductions that can be made
by either the zoning administrator or the planning commission. Ordinance language using the term wall
have also been replaced for the purposes of uniformity to use the defined term sight obscuring fence.

The new proposed language of DCMC Section 9-23-110(E) intends to remove use of ambiguous terms
such as appropriate and natural, and to clarify that the Planning Commission can consider the type of
vegetation proposed for preservation when considering whether or not existing trees and vegetation on the
site qualify towards the satisfaction of land use buffer standards. Additionally, DCMC Section 9-23-
110(E)(4) provides additional clarification and updates necessary to prohibit the relocation of undesirable
existing vegetation that is unhealthy, invasive, or not drought tolerant into land use buffers.

The use of berms in landscape buffers as indicated in DCMC Section 9-23-110(F) is proposed to be
removed to eliminate language stating that they are preferable to fencing. As berms have no known
definition in ordinance or applicable standards relative to this section, the current ordinance could have
been interpreted in such a manner as to undermine many other prescribed or desirable aspects of DCMC
Section 9-23-110. Berms may still be used within a land use buffer design.

Definitions.
A small change to the definition of a fence found in DCMC Section 9-3-040 is being proposed to reduce
the allowable size of gaps at the base of a sight obscuring fence to no more than four (4) inches. This is
being done to provide continuity with the same requirements proposed for DCMC section 9-23-110
(A)(5).

9-23-110: LAND USE BUFFERS:
Land use buffers are shall be provided along the side and rear property lines of
commercial, multifamily, and industrial properties intended to enhance community
appearance and welfare by protecting residents and visitors from the traffic, noise,
glare, trash, activity, vibration, odor, visual disorder and other adverse or harmful affects
effects typically associated with some such land uses.
  A. Land Use Buffer Requirements: The amount of landscaping and type of
fencing required for a land use buffer shall be as follows:
     1. A landscape buffer of twenty feet (20') shall be provided between all commercial
and office uses when located adjacent to a residential zone and when a multi-family use
is located adjacent to a single-family zone.
   2. A landscape buffer of thirty feet (30') shall be provided between industrial uses
and residential uses or zones.
    3. Trees within the buffer area described in 9-23-110(A)(1) and (2) above shall be
planted at one tree per four hundred (400) square feet of buffer area.
      a. Trees may be grouped.
       b. Each adjacent residential property shall have at At least one
adjacent buffer tree shall be planted between commercial, office, multi-family and
industrial uses and each single-family residential lot. Trees shall be arranged to provide
linear coverage between commercial, office and industrial uses and adjacent residential
units.
    4. The rest of the buffer area shall consist of ground covers, shrubs and planting
beds, wherein a maximum of seventy five percent (75%) of the buffer area may be
planted with ground covers.
    5. A six foot (6') high tall, solid, sight obscuring fence constructed of masonry,
precast concrete, or lightweight precast fiber reinforced concrete, or lightweight steel
reinforced polyethylene plastic wall shall be used between abutting commercial,
industrial, or office land uses and residential zones. Except for required footings or
foundations, the fence shall extend from the adjacent finish grade of the property
upward to the required height. Any gaps between the bottom of the fence panel and the
adjacent finish grade of the property may not exceed four inches (4”). Such gaps are not
counted towards the total required height of the fence. The height of fences shall be
reduced in clear view triangle areas or where they may impact sight visibility with a
driveway as specified in 9-27-080.
  B. Exemptions: No land use buffer shall be required between land uses located in
the same zone.
  C. Reductions:
   1. The zoning administrator or designee may reduce land use buffer requirements
based on the character of specific uses, building orientations, topographic features, or
other unique characteristics of adjoining uses. A reduced buffer shall consist of a
minimum ten foot (10') wide landscape buffer and an eight foot (8') tall, masonry, precast
concrete, lightweight precast fiber reinforced concrete, or lightweight steel reinforced
polyethylene plastic wall solid, sight obscuring fence in compliance with all other
specifications of 9-23-110(A)(5).
    2. The planning commission may approve additional deviations to
landscape buffer requirements if all of the following criteria are met:
     a. Physical site features preclude strict compliance. In this case an alternative
landscaping scheme shall be provided which meets or exceeds the intent of the code.;
      b. Additional landscaping shall be added to the overall site to compensate for any
approved reduction in buffer area widths, up to the amount of trees required for the
original buffer width.;
       c. An eight foot (8') tall, wall solid, sight obscuring fence or a hedgerow of at least
five feet (5') tall at maturity has been provided. If a fence is utilized it shall be in
compliance with all other specifications of 9-23-110(A)(5); and
      d. In no case shall a landscape buffer be less than five feet (5') in width.
  D. Relationship To Other Requirements: When the requirements of a land
use buffer exceed those of other requirements along a property line, the land
use buffer requirements shall apply.
  E. Existing Buffer Vegetation And Trees: Retention of appropriate existing plant
material is strongly encouraged in land use buffers. Approved eExisting trees and
natural vegetative ground covers should be retained where possible by avoiding
scraping, grading, sodding and other construction activity within the land
use buffer. Where land use buffer standards call for additional trees or shrubs to be
installed in an existing natural landscaped area, it should be done in a manner which
minimizes disturbance to existing plants. In determining whether existing trees and
vegetation qualify as satisfying a land use buffer standards, the planning commission
shall consider all of the following factors:
    1. The type and quality of the vegetation being preserved;
    2. The effectiveness of the visual screening that will be provided;
   3. The extent to which the proposed buffer makes use of existing topography and
vegetation; and
    4. The ability and availability to relocateion to the land use buffer area of healthy
native and existing, healthy, non-invasive, drought tolerant natural vegetation which that
would otherwise be removed from areas of the site to be developed to a land use buffer.
 F. Berms: The use of well designed berms is preferred rather than fences and walls
when possible, and when walls or fences are not otherwise required. Berms may be
required in land use buffer designs and should be designed to integrate with the natural
topography of the site.
  GF. Compatibility Of Plantings: Supplemental buffer plantings shall be compatible
with any existing vegetation within the buffer area.
   HG. Development Within Land Use Buffers: Land use buffers may contain
nonmotorized trails. In no event shall structures or uses, such as, but not limited to, play
fields, stables, swimming pools, golf courses, tennis courts, parking lots and vehicular
use areas, equipment storage and other open storage, or buildings be permitted within
land use buffers.
