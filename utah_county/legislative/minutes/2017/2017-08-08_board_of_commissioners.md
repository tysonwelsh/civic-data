1.     APPROVE REQUESTED EXEMPTIONS ON LIST DATED JULY 26, 2017, APPLIED FOR BY
       TAXPAYERS, FOR PAYMENT OF TAXES WHERE THE MARKET VALUE OF EQUIPMENT
       WAS LESS THAN $10,300 AS PROVIDED BY THE UTAH STATE LEGISLATURE FOR 2017
       (Continued from the August 1, 2017 Commission meeting)
(Track A, 03:45)
Commissioner Lee questioned why the Board would need to approve exemptions that have been determined by state
code. Utah County Deputy Attorney Paul Jones explained the current exemption application process requires a
mode of approval, but the Board could choose to adopt an ordinance delegating that responsibility – perhaps to the
County Assessor’s Office. He and the commissioners discussed whether it would be more appropriate to handle
these items through the Board of Equalizations since it concerns property taxes, and they decided to begin defining a
process to approve exemptions administratively through the Assessor’s Office.

           Commissioner Graves made the motion to approve the requested exemptions on
           list dated July 26, 2017 for payment of taxes where the market value of
           equipment was less than $10,300 as detailed in Regular Agenda Item No. 1. The
           motion was seconded by Commissioner Ivie and carried with the following vote:

3.      ADOPT (OR DENY) AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND THE UTAH COUNTY LAND USE
        ORDINANCE, SECTIONS 6-3, 6-4, 6-5, AND 6-6, RELATED TO SETBACK AND NOXIOUS
        WEEDS CERTIFICATION REQUIREMENTS FOR PLATS
(Track A, 12:50)
Commissioner Ivie asked why the noxious weed certification would be eliminated from the ordinance, when it is a
built-in protection for agricultural protection and critical environment zones. Brandon Larsen of the Utah County
Community Development Office explained it was not included prior to 2009, when Mr. Larsen thinks it was
accidentally added as part of the Planned Unit Development (PUD) section update. He clarified there is no proposal
to delete noxious weed certification for planned subdivisions, but it is not necessary in the canyon areas since those
developments usually prioritize natural vegetation.

           Commissioner Ivie made the motion to adopt the ordinance amending the Utah
           County Land Use Ordinance Sections 6-3, 6-4, 6-5 and 6-6 as outlined in
           Regular Agenda Item No. 3. The motion was seconded by Commissioner

10.   RECEIVE THE RECOMMENDED TOP THREE PROPOSALS FOR RFP 2017-12 FOR A
      RETIREMENT PLAN CONSULTANT, AND RECOMMEND A PROPOSAL FOR FURTHER
      NEGOTIATIONS OR AWARD OF CONTRACT
(Track A, 35:25)
Commissioner Lee invited public comment, and none came forward. The top three ranked proposals for a
Retirement Plan Consultant were NFP, Soltis, and AndCo. He suggested in the future perhaps a summary sheet
could be included to present to the Board with additional details on the differences between the recommendations.

Commissioner Graves stated he has a major concern with this agenda item, as the Request for Proposals (RFP) was
seeking a “partner that will proactively assist Utah County with plan administration consulting, investment
consulting, and individual financial planning.” Commissioner Graves pointed out three of the five applicants
responding to this RFP did not address individual financial planning with their proposals. He questioned how a
contract can be awarded when requirements have not been met. Commissioner Graves went on to note the
committee members estimated costs to rank the companies, and he considers that an incomplete bid. The score
sheet seems very subjective, making the results invalid and the process unfair. Commissioner Graves said there is
no reason to spend taxpayer money on this as it can be employee-funded. He is also offended that he was not
consulted, and instead was asked specifically to not attend the meetings.

Commissioner Lee acknowledged there were some problematic points, but ultimately thinks the expertise of an
outside group is beneficial for the county. Commissioner Lee appreciated the responsiveness of his first choice for
Retirement Plan Consultant. Commissioner Graves said he will not be comfortable moving forward; his integrity
was attacked and this entire process was flawed. Commissioner Ivie shared his experience was different from that
of Commissioner Graves’ – he was informed and involved in the process, and received answers to all of his
questions. Commissioner Graves replied when the committee asks the Chair to rescind an invitation for a Board
member, it seems covert, which is not good when the Board is ultimately held accountable to the public for their
vote.

Commissioner Ivie expressed a preference for NFP as the County Retirement Plan Consultant, and Commissioner
Lee stated he would choose Soltis. They discussed what they liked and disliked about the companies, with
Commissioner Ivie asking if it is possible to ask for a best and final offer from the two contenders. Utah County
Civil Division Chief Deputy Attorney David Shawcroft noted that is an option, as well as meeting with Soltis and
NFP individually. Commissioner Lee asked Commissioner Graves whether he would participate in such a
discussion, and Commissioner Graves stated no. Commissioner Graves added he brought documents outlining his
concerns today specifically so they could be requested through the Governmental Records Access and Management
Act (GRAMA) if needed; he has already detailed them to the county attorneys. Utah County Purchasing Manager
Robert Baxter received clarification from the commissioners on the next steps: one more interview each with NFP
and Soltis.

                 Commissioner Ivie made the motion to receive the top three proposals for RFP
                 2017-12 regarding a Retirement Plan Consultant, with further interviews
                 conducted between the commissioners and the top two recommended companies
                 of NFP and Soltis as discussed in Regular Agenda Item No. 10.
