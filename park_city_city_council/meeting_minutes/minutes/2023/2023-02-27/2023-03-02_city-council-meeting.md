IV. NATIONAL COMMUNITY SURVEY RESULTS
Linda Jager, Community Engagement Manager, and Brandon Bennett with Polco, who
administered the survey, were present for this item. Jager stated the survey results
helped Council determine City priorities. Bennett stated over 500 communities
participated in the survey and it included comparable resort communities to Park City.
He reviewed the outreach efforts made to notify residents of the survey and encourage
them to take it. He noted 972 total surveys were received. Bennett explained how his
team determined priorities of respondents.

Council Member Rubell joined the meeting at 9:35 a.m.

Bennett stated 90% gave the City excellent or good as a good place to live. Affordability
was an area of concern. Transportation was a category that respondents felt needed
improvement. Community Design was lower than scores received in 2017. Safety
scored well, but there was some concern regarding questions on natural disasters and
fire. The Recreation and Natural Environment scored high. Some key takeaways
included the City was still a desirable place to live. The cost of living, affordability of
quality childcare, affordable housing, and mental health resources were concerns.

Mayor Worel stated the overall direction Park City was taking decreased yet there was
overall confidence in the local government, and she asked how that was reconciled.
Bennett stated there was a good connection in the government, but there appeared to
be decisions made in 2015 that hadn’t been resolved. Council Member Gerber asked
about the cost of living. Bennett stated the drop in cost of living was not significant but in
looking nationwide, affordability was trending downward.

V. CRITICAL COMMUNITY PRIORITIES
Mayor Worel reviewed there was discussion among Council members that the Council
priorities were unclear. She thought this exercise would clarify and cement Council
priorities. Linda Jager, Community Engagement Manager, reviewed the history of the
City’s visioning processes in 2009 and 2020 and the priorities that resulted from those.
She noted in looking at peer communities, they had similar priorities. Jed Briggs, Budget
Manager, stated the purpose of having critical priorities could be summed up by saying,
“If we don’t get these right, it could have a significant negative impact on our
community.” He stressed the priorities should be a clear and shared vision on where the
City should go in the next five years. They should inform the budgeting process, add
relevance and clearly communicate projects and initiatives, be a lens to evaluate
process, initiatives and the budget, and be the City’s North Star. He also noted there
should only be a few and they should be easy to share with others and be easily
understood.

VII. TRANSPORTATON STRATEGY AND PLANNING
Matt Neeley, Johnny Wasden, Kim Fjeldsted, and Andrew Leatham presented this item.
Neeley reviewed he presented to Council on February 16th and today he would follow
up. He distributed a parking stall sheet and he stated there were about 125 stalls in the
City. At the last meeting, they also discussed the Silver bus route. Neeley stated there
was a lot of use at the Richardson Flat Park and Ride during the sold-out ski days. That
showed the lot was used when it was promoted. He indicated there was outreach to
know how the Silver route was being used. The recommendation was to use the Silver
route throughout the shoulder season seven days a week from 6:45 a.m. to 7:45 p.m.
with a 40 minute frequency through November. He noted the community liked the route,
but the complaint was it didn’t go directly to the resort.

Mayor Worel stated this route was an asset to PC Heights. She asked if microtransit
would take residents home if the bus route ended at 7:45 p.m. Neeley stated
microtransit would end mid-April and noted he would come back to Council in May with
an end-of-season update and would report on the use then. Council Member Rubell
wanted consistency with all bus routes and wanted the hours to extend to match other
routes. Council Members Toly, Gerber, and Doilney supported the proposal. Council
Member Dickey asked if the frequency was ridership driven, to which Neeley affirmed.
Council Member Rubell asked for more outreach to see if it would be used more at 20-
minute frequency. Neeley stated they were trying to manage cost with usage. Council
Member Rubell stated it wasn’t just PC Heights. Many people in town liked the route
because it was more direct to the high school. Neeley summarized they would ensure
the survey was broad enough.

places where proposed solutions worked. He wanted to see examples of successes or
failures. He didn’t want the City to start from scratch. Council Member Toly stated she
wanted to see a project tracker so when one topic was discussed, she didn’t have to
wonder where other projects were in the process.

Council Members Doilney and Gerber requested seeing a strong recommendation in
staff reports. Shields asked what metrics and measurables Council wanted to see data
on. Council Member Gerber noted there wasn’t an easily communicable goal for traffic.
She asked for staff to propose goals for ridership or cars coming into town. It was
indicated that having something to measure against would help. There was further
discussion on what was desired in staff reports.

Pearce indicated there was no time for disruptive transportation ideas at this meeting.
She suggested a community meeting where the public, staff and Council could discuss
and suggest ideas. Council Member Toly asked if this was what the study was doing.
Pearce stated the study had specific parameters, but she thought the consultant could
attend this meeting and listen. Council Member Dickey wanted to be purposeful and
clear on what the City would get.

VIII. ADJOURNMENT

With no further business, the meeting was adjourned.
