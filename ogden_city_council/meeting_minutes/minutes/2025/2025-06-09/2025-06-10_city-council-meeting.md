Proposed Resolution 2025–11 adopting amendment #2 to the Consolidated
Plan for the period July 1, 2020 through June 30, 2025, and Amendment #2
to the Annual Action Plan for the period July 1, 2020 through June 30, 2021
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2021-2025
Consolidated Plan (ConPlan) and FY 2021 Annual Action Plan (AAP) amendments came before the Council for consideration.
The memo stated the ConPlan and AAP are the mechanisms by which the City receives federal funding from the U.S. Department
of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). The AAP is used to outline how federal Community Development Block Grant
funds, HOME funds and other federal and non-federal program funds will be used by the City in the upcoming fiscal year.
         Community Development Director Smith summarized the memo and stated City Administration is proposing to amend
both the Five-year Consolidated Plan and the FY21 Annual Action Plan. The two are proposed to be amended together because
they were originally approved in 2020 as a combined document. The amendments proposed with this update address the
following:
              • Modify the “Target Areas Public Improvements” project in the FY21-25 Five-Year Consolidated Plan.
                      o Add language to the project scope that indicates the city will use HUD funds to “create, expand, or
                          enhance infrastructure and public facilities to help prepare for future Coronavirus outbreaks.”
                      o Re-allocate $500,000 Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) CV (COVID) funding to Target
                          Areas Public Improvements.
                      o Increase goals to one project to be completed in FY2026.
              •    The re-allocation of CDGB CV funding is proposed for Target Areas Public Improvements. Administration has
                   indicated that the intended use of these unused emergency funds will be for recreation and park improvement
                   projects, and that the proposed amendment is needed for the city to retain the funding.
          Mr. Smith also used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the dollar amounts associated with the
amendments; for a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council member Choberka asked if small businesses can apply for funding for exterior improvements, or if the money is
set aside for public space improvement. Mr. Smith stated that any improvement must relate to preparation for or response to the
COVID-19 pandemic; exterior business improvements would be difficult to justify under those parameters. Public open space
improvements would be a qualifying expense because they would help people gain access to outdoor spaces during a pandemic.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2025-11, entitled:
                   “A resolution of the Ogden City Council adopting Amendment #2 to the Consolidated Plan for the period July
                   1, 2020 through June 30, 2025, and Amendment #2 to the Annual Action Plan for the period July 1, 2020
                   through June 30, 2021, and directing that this document be submitted to the U.S. Department of Housing and
                   Urban development.”
          Chair White then opened the public hearing.
          Theresa Bramwell stated this is the first time she has heard of this program; she owns a commercial property at the corner
of 25th Street and Monroe Boulevard that was burned by homeless people in 2021. Her neighbor’s building, Topper’s Bakery, was
also burned in 2021. Both she and her neighbor are in need of public assistance to rehabilitate their properties, but she has never
heard of this program. However, she may not be inclined to take out a loan for her property. She asked if the City is receiving a
loan or grant from the federal government. She also asked if the funds can be used for improvements of park strips; her park strip
is very messy because it is used for snow removal purposes by her neighbor. She feels the properties around 25 th Street and
Monroe Boulevard need a lot of attention.

Public Comments
           Debra Darrington expressed concerns regarding housing issues in the community; a property two doors to the north of
her was for sale recently and it was a great opportunity, but she did not purchase the home. She now has regrets because the
person who did purchase the property is not a good landlord and does not respond to maintenance requests from tenants, does not
perform background checks for residents, and is not a resident of the community and is not aware of the problems occurring at his
property. She has made significant investment in the community and her experience when trying to move through the City’s
development application process has been that there is ‘a lot of unnecessary red tape’; many other property owners are not
following the City’s permitting process, so they likely do not have the same experience as her, but they are conducting business
improperly in the community. Many are out-of-state investors, and they have turned the properties into low-income housing that is
not well maintained. She asked if the City can require property developers to go through a Good Landlord Program or a specific
background check; it is important to ‘get a grasp’ on the community and the people developing here.
           Theresa Bramwell also discussed housing issues in Ogden; she has experienced similar ‘red tape’, but it appears that big
developers are able to bypass those regulations. She attended the auto show over the weekend, and it was great; at the event she
learned of a project at the corner of 25th Street and Quincy Avenue, which is less than a block from her own property. It looks like
a nice property, so she visited the website for the project to learn about project. The website advertises rental opportunities, but
she recalled attending the meetings during which that project was discussed and there were statements made that the income levels
for residents who would be living in the project would be 80 percent of the average median income (AMI); this would be tenants
that are paying their own rent rather than receiving a subsidy. This would be great for her neighborhood, but that is not what is
happening. The website advertises units available to those with an income of 60 percent of AMI. Rent rates are the same price
regardless of the size of the unit and every unit can have two pets. There are 176 units in the building, which means there can be
352 dogs at Quincy Avenue and 25th Street. Her concern is that no one will be able to rent the property at the prices listed and the
developer will be in a situation where they are ‘going broke’ because they cannot secure tenants and the project will immediately
be converted to a Section 8 housing development. This is very concerning to her.
           Holly, no last name given, stated she lives in Section 8 housing, and she does not appreciate Ms. Bramwell’s comments;
she is a functional adult, but she has struggled in her life. She discussed some of the issues she has encountered in the community
and encouraged the City Council to work to address the problems that many residents are facing at the Lantern House property.
           Another resident, no name given, also spoke of her experience at Lantern House; she has experienced seizures while
staying at the facility but does not receive the help she needs during these seizure events. People are treated horribly at Lantern
House, so much so that they choose to sleep on the street rather than be housed at the facility.
         Norene Jordan also discussed the problems she has encountered at Lantern House; she stayed at the facility for four
months and was treated badly. Her medicine was thrown away and the facility does not have the appropriate equipment to keep
and store needed medicines. She asked the Council to explore options for assisting the segment of the population that relies on
resources at the Lantern House.

Mayor Comments
          Mayor Nadolski thanked Ms. Darrington for her remarks about the housing issues she has experienced; he agreed that the
increase in out-of-state landowners has caused many problems, one being that local residents are not able to purchase residential
units in Ogden. The City has proposed a pilot program to the State Legislature that would work to address this issue, and he
expounded briefly on the anticipated benefits of the pilot program. He also addressed the comments regarding the Lantern House;
the City has been working to secure additional funding for homelessness programs that will be aimed at improving the facility and
keep the doors open; Administration is also working on short term solutions until additional funding can be secured.

Council member Comments
        Council member Choberka thanked Administration for exploring homeless mitigation funding and programs. She is a
Board Member on the Lantern House Board and will pass the comments made by the public on to the entire Board.


     There being no further business to come before the Council, VICE CHAIR HYER MOVED THE MEETING
