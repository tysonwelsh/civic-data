E. PRESENTATION ON THE OGDEN CANYON STUDY

    Kris Peterson, UDOT Region I Director, gave an overview of the Ogden Canyon study emphasizing that this
    is a planning study for long term transportation opportunities in the Canyon. Phase I issues that were looked
    at included geotechnical, right-of-way, environmental and roadway/traffic/safety/utilities. Phase II is
    currently underway and includes management committee meetings, focus groups and technical committees.
    Mr. Peterson said that significant efforts were made to involve the public at large—comments were solicited
    along with a public opinion survey.           The major issue identified in the survey is safety (for
    pedestrian/motorist/bicycle) in the Canyon due to its tight curves, no shoulders, etc. He said that 70% of
    respondents are most concerned about bicycle and pedestrian safety, 60% are in favor of safety
    improvements, 55% do not want bicycles on the roadway, 78% are in favor of a trail if impacts to residents
    can be minimized and 75% want a trail that allows bicycles up and down the Canyon. Canyon residents are
    divided on having a trail system and are extremely concerned about privacy, trespassing, vandalism, crime,
    impacts to property right-of-ways, environmental and aesthetics protection.

    The roadway group recommends 35 mph for segment 1 & 2 (lower segments) and 30 mph for segment 3/
    narrowest area of the canyon, no 45 mph and no 4-lane roadway. They recommend 4-foot shoulders in both
    directions, and for sharp curves to be improved. The transit group’s recommendation thus far is for a paved
    trail from the mouth of the Canyon to the vicinity of the Narrows or Peery Camp, then for unpaved/gravel
    trails the remainder of the way up the Canyon. Several potential alignments have been identified for this
    latter trail. This is not a final recommendation and the study will not recommend one specific location.
    Commissioner Bell said that there may be bridges and/or underground tunnels traversing the roadway.
    Regarding bikes in the Narrows, Mr. Peterson said that the environmental group prefers going over the river
    rather than cutting into the mountain. He noted that no decisions have been made; there are several more
    months of studies and meetings. They are identifying locations and will then turn it over to the county.
