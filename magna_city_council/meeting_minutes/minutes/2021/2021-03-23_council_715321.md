determination:

Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-207(4), and acting in my capacity as the Mayor of the Magna Metro

Public Hearing

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the 2021 Magna General Plan.

             Ms. Kate Davies, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(GSLMSD) stated the updated 2021 Magna General Plan has been posted on GSLMSD’s Long-
Range Planning website for the public to access.

                                                    3
DATE            TUESDAY                                  MARCH                                23, 2021

                 Ms. Christine Richman, Economic Analyst, GSBS Architects, delivered a
PowerPoint presentation on the 2021 Magna General Plan stating the plan has several elements
to it, three of which are required by state statute, i.e., a land use plan, a transportation and traffic
circulation plan, and a moderate-income housing plan. Other elements are to help meet the goals
and vision of the community. These elements include catalyst areas and economic stability;
parks, trails, recreation, and open space; natural hazards; community services and facilities;
sustainability; and implementation and work plan. She reviewed the planning process, an
overview and the goals and strategies for each of the elements, and the appendix, which includes
the Magna Metro Township’s moderate-income housing plan and draft transportation plan.

               Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Peel, moved to open the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

               No one spoke in favor of or in opposition to the 2021 Magna General Plan.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to close the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

Resolution No. 21-03-02

            Mayor Peay reviewed the following resolution approving and adopting the 2021
Magna General Plan:

RESOLUTION NO. 21-03-02                                                         Date: March 23, 2021

      A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL APPROVING AND
      ADOPTING THE 2021 MAGNA GENERAL PLAN

                                              RECITALS

      WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township was incorporated and commenced operations on
January 2, 2017 pursuant to State Law; and

       WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code Annotated (“UCA”) §10-2a-414, Magna inherited its
Code of Ordinances, and with it the Magna General Plan from Salt Lake County as a part of its
incorporation process; and

        WHEREAS, the Magna General Plan inherited by the municipality was over 10 years old;
and

        WHEREAS, as the inherited Magna General Plan had not been updated in over 10 years,
it was determined by the Council Magna needed a new Magna General Plan; and

       WHEREAS, the 2019 Magna General Plan Steering Committee was created to review the
old General Plan, look into new information and data regarding Magna as it pertains to land use,
                                                   4
DATE           TUESDAY                                MARCH                              23, 2021

transportation, economic development, historic preservation, and moderate income housing, and
to make recommendations to the Planning Commission and Council on the new General Plan;
and

       WHEREAS, the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, in support of the Magna
General Plan process, hired GSBS Consulting (the “Consultant”) to provide data collection and
information to for the steering committee, planning commission and council to consider in creating
the new General Plan; and

       WHEREAS, due to the COVID-19 pandemic, all work conducted on the Magna General
Plan and its chapters since March 13, 2020 were required to be done via electronic meetings and
through electronic citizen engagement portals; and

       WHEREAS, the consultant created www.thefutureofmagna.org (website) where residents
could provide comments and feedback on land use, transportation, and other components in the
plan; and

      WHEREAS, the website was able to capture information, comments, and feedback to a
number of community stakeholders and hundreds of residents regarding the plan; and

       WHEREAS, from the provided feedback, the steering committee, consultants and
planning staff were able to develop the 2021 Magna General Plan for consideration by the
Planning Commission and Council; and

       WHEREAS, on February 11, 2021 the Planning Commission conducted a public hearing
on the 2021 Magna General Plan; and

       WHEREAS, after the public hearing, the planning commission proposed a number of
amendments and recommended the Magna General Plan to the Council for its consideration and
possible adoption; and

      WHEREAS, on March 9, 2021 the Council set an additional public hearing to be held on
March 23, 2021 regarding the 2021 Magna General Plan; and

         WHEREAS, all electronic links to the 2021 Magna General Plan were posted to the Utah
Public Meetings Notice, Magna Metro Township, and Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services
District’s websites (https://magna-lrp-gslmsd.hub.arcgis.com/); now therefore be it

                                         RESOLUTION

       RESOLVED, the Magna Metro Township Council hereby adopts the 2021 Magna General
Plan including all exhibits, attachments and maps.

       APPROVED AND ADOPTED in the Magna Metro Township, Salt Lake County, Utah this
23rd day of March, 2021.

Magna United / Magna Community that Cares (CTC)

                Kellen Schalter delivered a PowerPoint presentation on the Community Profile
Report compiled of the Community Assessment, which she shared with the Council about two
months ago, and the Resources Assessment. In the Resources Assessment, the coalition
prioritized three risk factors – low commitment to school, low neighborhood attachment, and

                                               6
DATE            TUESDAY                                 MARCH                              23, 2021

parental attitudes favorable to drug use, and one protective factor – opportunities for prosocial
involvement. Then, a group of people looked at resources in Magna that addressed the priority
risk and protective factors that met two criteria, i.e., they had to be tested and effective, and had
to meet the risk and protective factors identified. The group also looked for gaps and barriers in
the implementation, and made recommendations addressing those. The resources selected
include: Botvin LikeSkills, After School Programming (provided by Salt Lake County Youth
Services and Utah State University Extension), Communities that Care, Guiding Good Choices,
and Staying Connected with Your Teen. Last Friday, the coalition started a community action
plan, and it is prioritizing which programs it wants to implement and which policies it wants to
focus on.

               Ms. Schalter delivered a presentation on the proposal to contract for the Halo
Vape Detectors at Matheson Junior High School. The coalition was awarded a $40,000 grant
through the Salt Lake County Health Department to put vape detectors in the school and also for
marketing for vape prevention education. The collation has been working with Utah Yamas
Controls, and it has given her a quote of $23,864 for 12 vape detectors and the installation of
them in the school to cover all the bathrooms. The coalition will also do some training for the
administrative staff there on how the vape detectors work. She needed approval from the Council
to move forward with the contract.

             Council Member Peel asked what the remaining amount of the $40,000 grant
would be used for.

                Ms. Schalter stated that would go towards advertising and education, and the
youth coalition, which is doing a project on vape prevention. Also, if Matheson Junior High School
wanted to, additional vape detectors could be purchased and installed in the locker rooms. Each
extra vape detector would cost $1,000, with a $300 cost to install it.

             Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to accept the bid
and allow the coalition to spend the grant money as it needed to. The motion passed
unanimously.

Council Consent for Audrey Pierce to Remain as Council Member from District #5 pursuant to
Utah Code § 10-3-301(6)

                 Paul Ashton stated Utah Code 10-3-301 says when an elected official represents
a district, they need to maintain residency within that district. There is an exception, whereby an
elected official who has not maintained residency can seek consent of the Council to extend their
time to enable them to reestablish residency. Council Member Pierce lost her home and has had
to move outside of District #5. However, she has been making efforts to find another place within
District #5, although that has not been an easy task given the cost of housing. Therefore, she
asked that she be given additional time to reestablish residency.

What the Council needs to discuss is the final date of the extension. The latest date it can extend
that is November 16, 2021. However, this is an election year, and if a seat is declared taken and
then vacated, it would take about two months to get a replacement. He asked whether the Council
would want to move that date in front of the election.

               Council Member Hull stated Council Member Pierce’s seat is not up for
reelection.



                                                10
DATE           TUESDAY                              MARCH                             23, 2021

              Council Member Peel stated he could not see any advantage in having the date
be before the election. A newly elected Council would not go into place until January 1, 2022,
anyway.

             Council Member Prokopis asked Council Member Pierce what she felt like a
reasonable amount of time was.

                Council Member Pierce asked that the date be November 16th so as not to limit
her capacity to reestablish residency. She was shocked at how hard it was to find affordable
housing, and it will be even more difficult in the upcoming months because summer is the time a
lot of people try to move.

               Council Member Hull stated maybe housing prices will drop in the fall once more
people are settled throughout the summer. She did not see a reason not to extend the date until
November 16th

               Council Member Peel asked Council Member Pierce if she could satisfy her
obligation as a Council Member while looking for a house.

              Council Member Pierce stated yes.

RESOLUTION NO. 21-03-03                                                  Date: March 23, 2021

    A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL, PURSUANT TO
    UTAH CODE §10-3-301(6), CONSENTING FOR AUDREY PIERCE TO REMAIN AS
    COUNCIL MEMBER FROM DISTRICT #5 PENDING TEMPORARY ABSENCE FROM
    DISTRICT DUE TO CHANGE IN RESIDENCY

                                          RECITALS

        WHEREAS, Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) is a Utah municipality subject to Title 10
of the Utah Code, including the requirements of Section 10-3-301 of said Code requiring Council
members to live within the boundaries of their respective districts, with minor exceptions, and

      WHEREAS, Audrey Pierce is the duly elected Council Member representing District #5 on
the Magna Metro Township Council, and

                                              11
DATE           TUESDAY                                MARCH                              23, 2021

         WHEREAS, due to no fault of her own Audrey Pierce lost her home within the boundaries
of District #5 and has, thus far, not been able to move back into a home within the boundaries of
District #5, and

       WHEREAS, Audrey Pierce continues to live within the boundaries of Magna, to faithfully
represent District #5 interests in the Council, and is in the process of finding a home within the
boundaries of District #5 to move into, and

        WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code §10-3-301(c), Audrey Pierce has requested additional
time to reestablish her residence within District #5, and

       WHEREAS, the Magna Council can grant additional time for an elected officer of the
municipality to reestablish residency within a district by following the requirements of Utah Code
§10-3-301(6)(b), and

        WHEREAS, after a public meeting on March 23, 2021, duly noticed and the receiving
public comment, if any, at such meeting, the Council has determined it is in the best interest of
both Magna and District #5 that Audrey Pierce be given additional time to reestablish her
residency within District #5. NOW THEREFORE BE IT

                                         RESOLUTION

       RESOLVED, the Magna Metro Township Council hereby consents and agrees that Audrey
Pierce, the duly elected Council Member for District #5, may continue to serve on the Council
while she seeks to reestablish residency within District #5, which consent shall continue through
November 16, 2021.

       APPROVED AND ADOPTED in the Magna Metro Township, Salt Lake County, Utah this
23rd day of March, 2021.
