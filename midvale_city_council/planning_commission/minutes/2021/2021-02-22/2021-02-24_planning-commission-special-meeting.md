Mr. Alex Murphy stated that Glen Kennedy, Midvale City Public Works Director, requests a
rezone of 0.21 acres of property located at 460 W. Oak St. from the Single Family Residential
Zone (SF-1) to the Single Family Residential with Public Facilities Overlay (SF-1/PFO). The
property currently contains several buildings that have been in place for many years and operate
as part of the City’s water supply system. The proposed rezone is requested to allow construction
of a new building to house an improved chlorination facility.
PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION FEBRUARY 24,2021 = |

Rezones are legislative decisions required whenever a change in designation on the City’s
Zoning Map is requested. Rezones are first heard in a public hearing by the Planning
Commission, who respond with a recommendation to the City Council. The Council then hears
the request in a second public hearing and decides to approve or deny the request. The Zoning
Ordinance includes requirements for findings and procedures necessary to consider and adopt a
rezone. The General Plan provides direction on the City’s future land use goals and must be
considered whenever a rezone request is made.

ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS:

Section 17-3-1 of the Midvale City Municipal Code requires the Planning Commission hold a
public hearing and adopt a written recommendation to the City Council, advising the Council to
approve, disapprove, or modify a request on all zoning map amendments. The Planning
Commission may only recommend approval of a rezone application if it determines, in written
findings, that the proposed rezoning is consistent with the policies and goals of the general plan
and that the applicant has demonstrated the following:

1. The proposed rezoning is necessary either to comply with the general plan proposed land
use map or to provide land for a community need that was not anticipated at the time of
adoption of the general plan;

2. The existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or that it
failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the natural
characteristics of the land including, but not limited to, steep slopes, floodplain, unstable
soils, and inadequate drainage; or

3. The land or its surrounding environs has changed or is changing to such a degree that it is
in the public interest to encourage redevelopment of the area or to recognize the changed
character of the area.

When the General Plan was adopted, this property was placed in a Stability Area. Stability Areas
are those areas of the City where the existing development pattern is intended to remain. This
site has functioned as a part of the City’s water infrastructure network for many years and is
necessary for the City to continue to supply water to its residents. The SF-1 zone was an odd
choice for this property and now hampers the City’s efforts to improve its water network.
Rezoning to the Public Facilities Overlay would allow the site to continue operation and expand
as needed to improve water service in the City.

Section 17-7-16.4 indicates the Public Facilities Overlay is only appropriate if the following
additional findings are made:

1. There is a demonstrated need for the public facility within the community at large and it
is not contrary to the public interest.

2. The public facility is consistent with the goals and policies of the general plan, and
applicable ordinances of the city where feasible.

The Planning Commission held a public hearing regarding the application on May 13, 2020. A
copy of the minutes for that agenda item have been attached. After considering public input and
Staff recommendation, the Planning Commission tabled a decision on the requests and directed
the applicant to address several concerns. The request was brought back to the Planning

Commission on September 9, 2020 where it was tabled pending a decision from the City Council
on a rezone requested for the same property.

The City Attorney determined the proper decisionmaker for this Large Scale Master Plan is the
City Council instead of the Planning Commission. As a result, this item is being presented to the
Planning Commission in this meeting for a recommendation to the City Council and not a final
decision.

ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS:

Part of the applicant’s request includes a rezone from the SF-1/AO zone to the RM-12 zone. The
Planning Commission considered this request in previous meetings and recommended denial of
the rezone to the Council. The Council will be considering the rezone request in a later meeting,
alongside the Large Scale Master Plan and Development Agreement requests.

Large Scale Master Plans are reviewed as a conditional use permit in the RM-12 zone. The Staff
Reports from May 13, 2020 and September 9, 2020 analyzed this project with respect to the
applicable requirements and have been attached to this report. The required public hearing for
the project was conducted on May 13. The relevant meeting minutes from May and September,
including public comment and Commission discussion, have also been attached.

Following Planning Commission consideration of the request in May and September, the project
has been revised as follows:

e The boundary of the project has been reduced to approximately 10.4 acres in size.

e The townhome portion, occupying the property where the church building sits today, has
been removed from the project. Approximately 13,000 SF of that property has been
turned into a community park space and the remainder has been removed from the
application.

e Communal guest parking spaces have been removed from the park areas. Guest parking
is still available in the driveway for each unit.

Reducing the area and scope of the request does not negatively impact the previous analysis. The
density has been reduced from 9.67 units/acre to 6.91 units/acre. Parking has been reduced from
4.28 stalls/unit to 4.0 stalls/unit (requirement determined by Planning Commission, typically
2.25 stalls/unit). Traffic counts will be lower than the Traffic Impact Study determined due to the
loss of 50 units. Total open space has increased from 50.1% to 56.7% (50% required).
While improved common open space appears to have decreased from 15.5% to 15.0% (15%
required), the previous calculation included areas that do not count as open space, so the total
amount provided has actually increased. The areas counting towards improved common open
space are the 4 community park spaces spread throughout the project and a trail connection to
150 E, connecting the project to other planned trail routes in Sandy and Midvale. To mitigate
safety concerns along the proposed trail, Staff recommends requiring lighting be provided along
the length and signs be posted at significant intersections (each end and the Ashby driveway).
This requirement can be included in the project’s Development Agreement and reviewed as part
of the Small Scale Master Plan for Phase 2.
