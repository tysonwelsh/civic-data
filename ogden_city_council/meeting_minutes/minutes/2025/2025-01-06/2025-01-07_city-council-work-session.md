        The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
discuss a proposed Weber Housing Authority Resolution; discuss proposed purchasing code amendments; receive an
administrative update regarding the Ogden Airport; discuss Council business; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair Richey and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Proposed Weber Housing Authority Resolution
         Weber Housing Authority is requesting a resolution finding a need for its permanent supportive housing project at 2325
Madison. Utah Code Ann. §35A-8-401 states that a county housing authority may not undertake a project within a city’s
boundaries unless “the governing body of the city [adopts a resolution] . . . declaring that there is need for the county authority to
exercise its powers within that city.” The project has been reviewed by the Planning Commission and found to be consistent with
the General Plan. After considering the matter in the December 3, 2024, Council meeting, the Council requested a work session on
January 7, 2024 to allow for additional discussion.
         Chair Richey introduced WHA Executive Director Andi Beadles; he noted that Council staff emailed her a list of
questions the Council had about the proposed permanent housing project, and he invited her to approach the Council and respond
to the questions.
         Ms. Beadles provided each Council member with a handout describing the Weber Housing Authority Supportive
Housing Tenant Screening Criteria. She then reviewed the Council member’s questions dealing with the following aspects of the
project:
               • Core elements of the housing project;
               • WHA supportive housing eligibility criteria;
               • The WHA housing application procedure;
               • The process for notifying an applicant that they have been denied housing;
               • The supportive housing appeal process;
               • WHA supportive housing consideration of circumstances/reasonable accommodations;
               • Availability of the tenant selection plan; and
               • Modification of the WHA supportive housing plan.
          Throughout Ms. Beadles’ review, she engaged in discussion with the Council and the Mayor regarding instances in an
individual’s past that would prevent them from being eligible for housing; activities, occurrences, or events that could result in an
individuals eviction from supportive housing; the separation of WHA from Weber County; staffing of the housing facility; the
status of renovation/maintenance of the subject property and building; the maximum number of individuals who can be housed at
the facility; whether those who may potentially receive housing are currently housed or homeless; the total number of supportive
housing units throughout Weber County; the reasons behind the selection of the subject property; representation of Ogden City on
the WHA Executive Board; access to medical services and medicines for residents of the housing project; the impact the project
could have on public safety services in the Ogden community; active participation requirements for residents; the history of
similar projects in the community and plans for additional supportive housing projects in the future; differences between the WHA
and Ogden Housing Authority (OHA); and the involvement of City Administration in the planning of this project thus far. The
Council and Mayor concluded by requesting that WHA consider an alternative property in the community for the supportive
housing project and that they involve the public and specifically the people living in the neighborhood surrounding whatever
property is selected to ensure there is some level of public support.
          For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

Proposed Purchasing Code Amendments
          The Council will review and discuss proposed changes to the City’s procurement code that increase thresholds that
determine when a formal bid process is required. The proposed threshold changes affect bid limits for the purchase of general
goods and services, building improvements, public works projects, and professional service contracts. The Administration is
recommending the proposed amendments to account for inflationary pressures on the City’s purchasing power and to align with
some state code requirements or thresholds. The City’s procurement code has not been updated in decades.
          Purchasing Coordinator Puertollano reported the City’s procurement code has not been updated for decades; proposed
amendments to the code account for current market conditions and will effectively enhance the City’s purchasing power that
aligns with the State of Utah Code and purchasing threshold levels of surrounding municipalities. The amendments will also
create greater efficiencies in the procurement process. She used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the City’s current
procurement code and highlight language changes to the ordinance; review the State’s purchasing requirements and benchmark
data for other cities; and to detail the proposed dollar amount changes within the procurement code. She concluded the primary
goals of the proposed amendments are:
              • Create greater efficiencies while conducting a responsible procurement process.
              • Balance the need for flexibility and speed with the need for fairness, transparency, and good stewardship of
                    public funds.
              • Time saved in completing previous purchasing formalities will give us the opportunity to address other
                    operational needs.
          Ms. Puertollano noted the expectation is that the approved updates will positively impact policies and procedures and
improve the City’s overall procurement and contracting process. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information
packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Ms. Puertollano, as well as members of the advisory committee that was involved in
developing this proposal, centered on the basis for some of the proposed adjustments to bidding requirements as well as
administrative processes surrounding procurement and contract awarding. Chair Richey concluded that an action item on the
proposed amendments will be included on the January 21, 2025 meeting agenda.

Administrative Update – Airport
         The Council received an administrative update from Airport Director Condie regarding the Ogden Airport, including the
following:
              • Promotion of local general aviation;
              • Commercial air service, including capital improvements and beautification of the Airport terminal;
              • Economic growth at and surrounding the Airport.
         Mayor Nadolski emphasized his support for the Ogden Airport and thanked the Council for their attention to Mr. Condie
this evening.
