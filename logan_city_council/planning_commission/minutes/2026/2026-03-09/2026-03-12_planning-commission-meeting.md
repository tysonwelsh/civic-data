PUBLIC HEARING:

PC_ 26-012 Brio Office [Design Review] Ryan Short/Brio Home Builders LLC, authorized
agent/owner is requesting a design review permit to construct a new 19,000 square foot
office/warehouse building development consisting of 15 units, located at the southeast corner of 600
West and 200 North in the Commercial Service (CS) Zone (TIN 06-004-0010) within the Ellis
Neighborhood.

Staff: Aaron Smith, Logan City Planner, reviewed the applicant proposal, explaining that the CS zone
serves as a transition between commercial and industrial uses and permits general office, flex space,
and warehousing.

The proposed site plan includes a main office building at the corner of 600 West and 200 North, with
two warehouse buildings located toward the southeast portion of the site.

Due to the residential setback transition along the eastern property line, parking must be adjusted to
meet the required 15-foot setback.

Required right-of-way improvements along 600 West are not shown on the site plan. Staff
recommended adding to condition number two, that all buildings must meet minimum setbacks
following right-of-way dedication, installation of improvements, and final surveyed boundary
locations.

Pedestrian connections between the buildings and public sidewalks are not shown and are
addressed in Condition number three.

Parking requirements vary by use under the Land Development Code. Staff recommended an

average of one stall per 500 square feet for office/flex space. Final parking compliance will be verified
at the building permit and business licensing stages.


A surface stormwater facility is proposed in the front yard. The Land Development Code requires
such facilities to be located in side or rear yards unless integrated into the landscape design and
approved by the Planning Commission.

The proposed buildings meet height and general design standards. However, the west fagades of
the warehouse buildings lack fenestration. The Commission will need to determine whether the 30%
fenestration requirement should apply, given the buildings are approximately 130 feet from the street.

Proponent: Ryan Short, project owner, stated that the corner building along 200 north will serve as
the Brio office, with the remaining units available for lease. He indicated efforts have been made to
comply with all applicable design standards.

Public: Jeanne Jones, a nearby resident, expressed concerns about existing weeds on the property
and potential future expansion displacing neighborhood residents.

Ezra Eames, owner of an adjacent property surrounded by the development, requested a minimum
10-foot setback from his building.

Russell Goodwin raised concerns about traffic on 600 West, particularly southbound vehicles turning
left into the site near the intersection.

Commission: The commission responded to public comments, stating the current proposal does not
include expansion into nearby residential properties and that any future development would require
additional approval.

Staff clarified that the side setback to the existing building along 600 West is five feet, and the revised
driveway location provides adequate clearance. The driveway was relocated from the north to the
south side of the adjacent building to meet intersection spacing requirements. Darren Farar, Logan
City Engineer, confirmed the driveway meets City standards.

The Commission discussed fenestration on the west fagades of the warehouse buildings and
determined that they are setback far enough that fenestration does not need to be required and that
condition number four can be removed.

The Commission discussed the lack of fenestration on the west facades of the warehouse buildings
and determined that, due to the distance from the street, the requirement is not necessary. Condition
number four will be removed.

The Commission agreed to amend condition number two, to include staff's recommended language
regarding right-of-way and setbacks and to allow the proposed stormwater facility to remain in the
front yard (condition number 7).

PC 26-013 Logan 2045 General Plan [Plan] Russ Holley/Logan City is requesting the approval of

the Logan 2045 General Plan, a long-term vision document intended to guide growth and
development over the next 20 years.

Staff/Proponent: Russ Holley, Community Development Director introduced Cody Ferguson of
Houseal Lavigne, the consulting firm contracted to prepare the Logan 2045 General Plan.

Cody Ferguson explained that the plan has been developed over 15-month period with extensive
public involvement. Outreach efforts included 15 public workshops and neighborhood meetings with
over 390 participants; input from 63 residents and stakeholders through focus groups and interviews;
more than 300 community survey responses; 189 map-based comments submitted through the “Map
Social” tool; two pop-up events with over 40 participants; 47 online comments on the draft plan; six
Steering Committee meetings; and three meetings with the Planning Commission and Municipal
Council. In total, the City recorded more than 800 points of engagement.

Mr. Ferguson reviewed the fourth draft of the plan, noting it meets all State statutory requirements,
including elements related to Moderate Income Housing, Water Use and Preservation, and
Transportation Connectivity. The Plan identifies key community priorities such as affordable housing,
walkability, traffic congestion, water resources, infrastructure capacity, managing growth, economic
opportunity,.and preservation of open space. He stated the draft incorporates public feedback and
is intended to be botfi accessible and implementable.

Commissioner Maughan asked whether a comparison table showing current zoning versus proposed
future land use could be created. Mr. Ferguson confirmed that such a table could be prepared.

Commissioner Doutre indicated that this information would be helpful for future zoning decisions and
asked whether the City maintains data of dwelling units by zone. Russ Holley confirmed that
information is available.

Commissioner Maughan expressed interest in identifying areas designated for future higher density
development and agricultural preservation.

Commissioner Heare asked whether maintain agricultural zoning is a priority. R. Holley responded
that agricultural designations are limited within the current future land use map, and that increasing
density within the City may help preserve agricultural land in surrounding county areas.
Commissioner Doutre requested additional time to review the Action Matrix before making a
recommendation to the Municipal Council

Public: Russell Goodwin suggested the City consider an east bench arterial road rather than
focusing on a corridor at 1800 West. He discussed the potential for connecting Canyon Road to the


cliffside area and coordinating it with planned water line improvements. He also advocated for
increased use of roundabouts, citing safety and traffic flow benefits.

Commission: R. Holley explained that the concept of an east bench arterial has been considered
for decades, including prior study of a 400 East corridor. However, due to challenging topography
and other constraints, the concept has not advanced. Current transportation planning and modeling
anticipate that west-side corridors will accommodate regional traffic, including travel from Sardine
Canyon north toward Smithfield. Recent Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) studies have
also explored alternatives to Main Street, including improvements along 200 East and 200 West.
