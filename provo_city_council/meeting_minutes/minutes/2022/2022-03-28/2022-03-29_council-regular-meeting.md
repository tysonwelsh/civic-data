 Public Comment

Melanie McCoard, of Provo, asked why the Parks or Arts Board Members were not present in today’s
work meeting. She proposed that no briefing be provided to Council unless board members have been
asked or provided input. She also shared her belief that a Public Works board is necessary. She added
that the Airport needs to be a separate department.

With no other comments, Chair Hoban closed public comment.

 Presentations, Proclamations, and Awards

 1.           A presentation of 2020 Utah Firefighters of the Year. (22-034) (13:25)

Lynn Schofield, Fire Battalion Chief, introduced other participants to council and asked them to join him
at the front. Mr. Schofield discussed an incident that happened in February 2021.

David Larsen, Chair of the Joint Council of Fire Service Organizations, recognized and presented the
award to the following members of the Fire Department: Jeff Wise, Jeremy Moon, Nick Blackburn, Koby
Hubbs, Todd Glassford, Brenin Anderson, Jake Dennison, Chad Chapman, and Spencer Long.

 4.           A resolution adopting the 2021 Provo Foothills Trail Master Plan. (22-024) (29:04)

        Motion:       An implied motion to approve Resolution 2022-15, as currently constituted, has been
                      made by council rule.

Doug Robins, Assistant Director of Parks and Recreation, presented the Master Plan.

Councilor Fillmore asked if they are anticipating any federal funding.

Mr. Robins responded that funding would be provided.

Councilor MacKay applauded those who put the plan together. She shared her concern for private
property and suggested a clause be added to acknowledge property rights would not be taken away.

Mr. Robins said their focus is to work with property owners.

Brian Jones read suggested text and said a motion would be required prior to the addition to passing the
resolution.

Councilor Ellsworth discussed a law that was passed last year regarding eminent domain.

 6.           An ordinance regarding redistricting adjustments to City Council District Maps. (22-003)
              (1:01:27)

        Motion:       An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2022-13, as currently constituted, has been
                      made by council rule.

Brian Jones presented the 3 Maps that Council recommended be voted on during the work session. He
explained how it would look if they decided to use ranked choice voting.

Councilor Handley shared his opinion of rank choice voting.

Karen Tapahe, Community Relations Coordinator, provided history of how we got to this point.

Chair Hoban expressed his opinion to get rid of Map 33 and only discuss the other two Maps after
seeing the results of the initial vote.

Councilor Ellsworth pointed out the differences between Maps 34 and 113 regarding her district.

Councilor Handley asked about timeline of applicability.

Mr. Jones proposed the effective date be at the end of the year to prepare for next year’s elections.

Councilor Handley asked how it affects council members after the restructuring of the districts.

Mr. Jones responded that regardless of districts, council members represent and make decisions for the
city. He said the only difference it makes is who will be voting for each council member during the next
election.

Councilor Ellsworth asked if that meant she is an at large council member for the duration of the next
year.

Mr. Jones responded.

Councilor Handley suggested Map 34 be selected and provided reasoning to why it would benefit
multiple districts.

Councilor Whipple asked about Map 113 and how the colors match the district numbers.

Mr. Jones explained that after the Map is selected, the council could determine which district is which.

Karen Tapahe added that the staff could change the numbers to reflect the correct districts.

Councilor Handley asked clarifying questions regarding his district and the area around BYU.

Councilor Whipple asked if it was possible to move one precinct from a section and move it to another.

Ms. Tapahe responded that it is something staff could look at.

Staff pulled up the Map to view how the population would become unbalanced if the precinct in
question was changed to a different district.

Chair Hoban suggested the council look at a Map that was provided by Eric Chase.

Councilor Ellsworth shared that it might not be the best idea to design from the dais after the public was
presented with the other Maps.

Staff brought up the Map that Mr. Chase designed.

Chair Hoban opened public comment.

Melanie McCoard asked what street the boundary for District 5 is. She stated she is in favor of seven at
large council members.

Jeremy Rieske, of Provo, encouraged Map 34 since Map 113 splits the Franklin neighborhood.

Chair Hoban closed public comment.

Councilor Ellsworth stated her preference is Map 34.

      Motion:       A motion to remove Map 33 for consideration was made by Chair Hoban. Seconded
                    by Councilor Ellsworth.

      Vote:         The motion was approved 4:3 with Councilors Ellsworth, Handley, Hoban, and
                    Whipple in favor. Councilors Fillmore, MacKay, and Shipley opposed.

Chair Hoban said the council now has two maps to consider, Map 34 and Map 113.

      Motion:       A motion to adopt Map 34 was made by Councilor Ellsworth. Seconded by Councilor
                    Whipple.

Mr. Jones clarified what the correct procedure would be as far as amended motions go and said a final
vote would need to be taken.

Councilor Fillmore asked which maps are being considered and said it looks like there are three.

Chair Hoban explained how the rank choice voting affected the decision and how Map 113b would
replace 113a if council decided.

      Motion:       A motion to adopt Map 113b was made by Councilor Fillmore. Seconded by Councilor
                    Shipley.

Councilor Ellsworth questioned Councilor Handley’s district.

Councilor Handley responded that he feels like Map 34 is a modest improvement and does not make
dramatic changes to District 3.

Councilor Ellsworth echoed Councilor Handley’s statements. She also addressed Councilor Fillmore’s
concerns.

Councilor Fillmore disclosed why he does not support Map 34. He also discussed his rationale for Map
113b.

Councilor Shipley shared his experience with living on the westside and said the westside deserves more
representation and connectivity.

Councilor Ellsworth asked about the impact Grandview has in relation to the westside.

Councilor Shipley responded.

Councilor Whipple asked Councilor Shipley about the Franklin Neighborhood vs the Grandview
Neighborhood.

Councilor Shipley responded.

Councilor Whipple added that any of the options will have uniquely shaped districts and hopes that the
public will understand that they are due to the precincts.

Councilor Handley asked Councilor Shipley to clarify his comments regarding the westside being
inconvenienced.

Councilor Shipley shared his belief of shared communities making more sense than others.

Councilor MacKay shared experience while campaigning for her seat in the Grandview Neighborhood.

Mr. Jones simplified the differences between Map 113a vs 113b and said that neither one kept
Councilor Ellsworth in her district.

Councilor Fillmore asked about the effective date of these changes.

Councilor Handley asked for Chair Hoban’s view of Map 34 in relation to Grandview.

Councilor Ellsworth reminded that the job at hand is to decide the boundaries of the districts and name
them based on when the election will be for that district councilmember.

Chair Hoban asked Councilor Whipple which she preferred in relation to her district between Map 113a
and Map 113b.

Councilor Whipple responded that she preferred Map 113b.

      Motion:       A substitute motion to supplant Map 113a with Map 113b was made by Chair Hoban.
                    Seconded by Councilor Ellsworth.

Councilor Ellsworth questioned if Councilor Whipple felt like there is enough stability or permanence to
facilitate candidates over time.

Councilor Whipple said that there have been many people investing in properties to stay in downtown
area. She also said that there are new apartment buildings being constructed whose residents will be
eligible to run for office.

Chair Hoban called for a vote on the substitute motion.

      Vote:         The motion was approved 5:2 with Councilors Fillmore, Hoban, Mackay, Shipley, and
                    Whipple in favor. Councilors Ellsworth and Handley opposed.

Chair Hoban asked for Councilor Whipple’s feedback on which district the Grandview Neighborhood
should be part of.

Councilor Whipple answered that she did not know what was best.

Chair Hoban asked Councilor Ellsworth and Councilor Handley what they liked about each option.

Councilor Ellsworth shared thoughts on how the council should do what is best for the entire city, not
necessarily what is best for their own districts.

      Vote:         The motion to adopt Map 113b was approved 4:3 with Councilors Fillmore, Hoban,
                    Mackay, and Shipley in favor. Councilors Ellsworth, Handley, and Whipple opposed.

Mr. Jones discussed the need to rename the colors on the Map to reflect the correct districts.

Councilor Ellsworth asked questions regarding the elections.

Mr. Jones responded.

      Motion:       A motion to label the purple section on the map District 3 was made by Chair Hoban.
                    Seconded by Councilor Fillmore.

      Vote:         The motion was approved 4:3 with Councilors Fillmore, Hoban, Mackay, and Shipley in
                    favor. Councilors Ellsworth, Handley, and Whipple opposed.

Councilor Whipple asked if Councilor Handley could resign prior to his term expiring to allow for
Councilor Ellsworth to run in that district.

Councilor Handley shared it does not affect him much as he does not intend to run again or resign prior

Chair Hoban called for a five-minute recess. (2:49:00)

Mr. Jones reviewed the finalized Ordinance.

Councilor Fillmore suggested a press release be presented to public prior to elections for clarification.

Councilor Whipple asked to confirm the precincts.

Councilor Ellsworth shared that she hopes the council did the right thing disregarding her personal
stance.

Councilor Whipple reviewed the process and echoed Councilor Ellsworth’s comments of doing the right
thing. She also applauded council members for their teamwork and respect for each other’s opinions
and viewpoints.

Councilor Handley shared that the boundary lines are less significant than the political culture that is
built throughout the city.

Chair Hoban called for a vote.

      Vote:     The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Hoban,
                Mackay, Shipley, and Whipple in favor.

 Adjournment
