Pre Council, 5:00 p.m.
1. Welcome and Opening Comment
Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present. Councilor
Condie gave the opening comment.

2. Presentations and Reports

2.1 Presentation and Discussion of the Hospital / 2100 North Station Area Plan- Planning
Department
Christine Richmond with GSBS presented an update on the North Station Area Plan, which is a
state-mandated document focused on creating a vision and goals for the future transit-supported
neighborhood around the planned north Lehi station. The plan aims to establish a unique hub of
activity that is accessible to people of all ages and abilities, with a mix of residential,
commercial, and community spaces. The plan proposes a central hub with gathering spaces, an
interconnected network of roads and paths, and coordination with planned infrastructure projects
like a future I-15 interchange. The implementation of the plan is complicated by the uncertainty
around when the actual transit service will arrive.

2.2 Lehi City Finance Report- Dean Lundell, Finance Director
Dean Lundell, Lehi Finance Director, presented the financial report, highlighting the general
fund balance and the impact of large claims. The sales tax revenue was discussed, with
fluctuations due to online sales and the overall budget being ahead of expectations.

2.3. Discussion to Extend Waste Management Contract- Blake Leonelli, Waste Management
Blake Leonelli stated that the contract with Waste Management will be up for renewal soon and
they would like to continue working with Lehi City. He said that Lehi benefited from the

2.4 Presentation and Discussion of a Green Waste Facility at 1700 W 1400 S
Steve Marchbanck, Lehi City Parks and Facilities Manager, presented a proposal to add a green
waste facility at Mellor-Rhoads Park. He stated that the facility would handle green waste from
tree removal and provide a service for city residents because they could drop off their green
waste and purchase product. It would also improve the soil quality and provide the needed
topsoil for the future park. The council discussed the benefits and logistics of the green waste
facility, with concerns about the impact on neighbors. The Council also discussed mitigation
options.

Human Resources Director
David Kitchen, Human Recourses Director, presented the proposed revisions to the workplace
harassment policy to include all protected classes. Changes to the parental leave policy was also

9. Public Hearing to receive input on a public green waste facility use, located at
   approximately 1700 West 1400 South in a Public Facilities zone.
   Mayor Johnson explained the proposed green waste facility as a temporary solution to prepare
   for the future park by clearing trees, adding fill, improving soil conditions. A contractor offered
   to remove invasive Russian olive trees and grind the material on-site at no cost to the city.
   Citizens would be allowed to drop off green waste for free. The facility would be moved as the
   park is constructed from south to north. Mayor Johnson emphasized this as a cost-saving
   measure that could benefit the city by avoiding expensive tree removal, improving soil quality,
   and providing a green waste service. He stressed that this was not a permanent solution and
   was intended to help clear the land for park construction.

    The Mayor and Council discussed concerns such as noise and traffic and how to potentially
    mitigate those concerns.

    Mayor Johnson opened the public hearing.

    Jonathan Cavender: Concerned about increased truck noise, traffic, and the long-term impact
    on the neighborhood. Worried about a 5-year timeline for the facility.

    Ryan Hess: Questioned the length of the project and its impact on traffic, especially near school
    bus stops.

    Warren Johnson: Asked about potential environmental impacts and water contamination.
Lehi City Council Meeting                         4                                       April 8, 2025
    Dan Logan: Expressed frustration about existing traffic problems on 1700 West, concerned
    about additional trucks and safety issues.

    Steve Steadman: Highlighted traffic congestion and road limitations.

    Anne Bailey: Raised concerns about potential methane gas buildup and impact on nearby
    schools and well water.

    Ernie Jones: Questioned the potential conflict of interest.

    Jeff Dixon: Concerned about dust control, and proximity to houses.

    Nicole Melling: Requested clarification on traffic situation.

    Annette Gom: Questioned the need for the facility and the lack of public input.

    Bob Richardson: Asked about the long-term solution and commitment to making this truly
    temporary.

    The contractor proposed limiting truck movements to avoid peak hours. He stated that there
    would be no additional traffic. He explained that they would grind the material and remove it
    quickly to prevent methane buildup. He was committed to submitting a fugitive dust control
    plan and using water trucks.

    Kenneth Webb: Asked about weed control for the remaining area not covered by the green
    waste facility and questioned how many cities would utilize the free green waste drop-off.

    Warren Johnson: Argued the project seemed long-term.

    Mrs. Richardson: expressing concerns about traffic and environmental impact.

    Kate Peterson: Questioned the budget allocation for the portable ice rink, suggested a specific
    contract end date, and asked about potential health impacts for her daughter with asthma.

    David Johnson: Inquired about the current green waste disposal methods and the need for the
    facility.

    Ryan Hess- Questioned the assertion that no additional traffic will be generated by the green
    waste facility.

    Mayor Johnson and the City Council carefully considered the green waste facility proposal,
    ultimately concluding that while the concept had merit, the proposed location was unsuitable.
    They acknowledged the significant traffic problems in the area and appreciated the contractor's
    good-faith effort to provide a cost-effective solution. They emphasized the importance of
    saving money for the city without creating additional burdens for residents. The council
    unanimously agreed to reject the current proposal, encouraging the developer to explore
    alternative sites.
