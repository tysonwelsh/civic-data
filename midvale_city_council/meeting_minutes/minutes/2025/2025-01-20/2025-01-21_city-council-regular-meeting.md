Jonathan Anderson said the current property at 7444 S Union Park Ave was developed
under the Neighborhood Commercial-2 (NC-II) standards of the SF-1 zone which limits
total parking stalls to 8. Rezoning to RC would allow the applicant to construct more
parking stalls as shown in the proposed site plan. The project will be subject to a Site
Plan application prior to construction to ensure compliance with the standards of the RC
zone for the expansion of the parking lot.

Midvale City Code 17-3-1(E) outlines the criteria necessary for granting a rezone as
follows:

17-3-1(E). Zoning Map Amendment Criteria. The city’s zoning is the result of a detailed
and comprehensive appraisal of the city’s present and future land use allocation needs.
A zoning map amendment application may only be approved if the reviewing body
determines, in written findings, that the proposed amendment demonstrates one or more
of the following:

1. Proposed rezoning promotes objectives of the general plan;

2. Existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or that it
failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the natural
characteristics of the land, including but not limited to steep slopes, flood plain, unstable
soils, and inadequate drainage;

3. Land or its surrounding environs have changed or are changing to such a degree that
it is in the public interest to encourage redevelopment of the area or to recognize the
changed character of the area; or

4. Proposed rezoning promotes the purposes outlined in Utah Code Annotated 10-9a-
102.

The proposed rezone request satisfies #1 and #4 listed above. Goal #1 of Commercial
Development and Economic Sustainability in the 2016 General Plan identifies the need
to “1. Plan for a balanced mix of commercial and residential land uses in Midvale to

        A.   CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2025-R-03 AMENDING THE VETO
             AUTHORITY IN THE HARVEST DAYS FESTIVAL COMMITTEE
             BYLAWS.

Erinn Summers said Harvest Days, a longstanding Midvale tradition, was originally
started by the city's farming community as a celebration of the fall harvest. The local
Midvale Kiwanis Club sponsored the first Harvest Days in 1938. Supported by other civic
organizations and the cooperation of surrounding towns, the Midvale Kiwanis Club
organized a parade, queen contest, car raffle and fireworks show. More than 85 years
later the Midvale Harvest Days celebration is enjoyed by thousands of residents from
Midvale and surrounding communities.

For many years prior to the creation of the Harvest Days Festival Committee (Committee)
in March of 2022, the Harvest Days Festival (Festival) has been an administrative
responsibility of the City, under the direction and management of the City Manager. The
event was a huge undertaking for City staff each year. The City Council established the
Harvest Days Festival Committee (Committee) through the Harvest Days Festival
Committee Bylaws (Bylaws) to support the City in planning and executing the festival.
Under the Bylaws, the Committee is made up of individuals who live or work in Midvale.
It consists of up to 4 members and 2 alternate members, appointed by the Mayor, and a
Committee Chair who is appointed by the Mayor and confirmed by the Council. The
Committee members and Chair work with ex officio members, including the City Manager,
City staff, up to 2 City Council members, and an event planner. Only Committee members
are able to vote on Committee actions, with alternates voting in the absence of other
Committee members. The City Manager has the authority to veto any, and all, decisions
made by the Committee, if deemed that the decision(s) are against the policies or best
interest of the City. Under the current Bylaws, vetoes are at the sole discretion of the City
Manager.

On October 15th, City Council instructed City staff to investigate options for a formalized
appeal process for vetoes to be added to the Bylaws. To meet the direction provided by
Council, City staff prepared three options for City Council to consider and to facilitate
discussion on this topic during the December 3rd City Council Meeting. Through that
discussion, the City Council asked staff to finalize language in the Harvest Days Festival
Committee Bylaws to outline the process by which the City Manager informs City Council
of any veto made of a Harvest Days Festival Committee decision and gives City Council
the authority to consider an appeal of the veto. The following language has been
proposed to adhere to the guidance provided by City Council: “The City Manager retains
authority to veto any decisions made by the Committee if, in the judgment of the City
Manager, a decision is contrary to the best interests of the City or community, or is not
aligned with City policies.

Upon exercising veto authority, the City Manager shall notify the City Council, by the close
of the next business day, of the veto, the context of the decision made by the Committee,
and the reason(s) for the veto. Each City Council member may add the review of the veto
and consideration of an appeal to the City Council agenda. All City Council decisions on
appeals shall be final,” (Section 9).

Jonathan Anderson said Midvale City was awarded financial assistance through a
Transportation and Land Use Connection (TLC) grant for the Porter Rockwell Trail Plan.
The project will determine the feasibility of connecting to and continuing the Porter
Rockwell trail from the southern border at Sandy, to the northern border with Murray City.
This trail is identified as #142 in the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan, and on
WFRC’s Regional Transportation Plan as a planned connection. This would be an
important connection for a regional active transportation facility. Members of the Trails
Division at UDOT suggested pursuing this to complete the northern portion of the Porter
Rockwell trail.

The scope of the project includes, among other things, an analysis of existing conditions,
developing concept plans for three different trail routes, including a preferred option, and
providing cost estimates. Opportunity for public input and project updates are included in
both the scope and proposed timeline.

The Transportation and Land Use Connection Consultant Agreement outlines services to
be provided with the Porter Rockwell Trail Plan. A page detailing the budget and the
parties responsible is included. As the City was awarded a TLC grant through WFRC,

Mayor’s signature on the attached agreement is recognition that the City understands the
parameters of the project and will work with both WFRC and the consultant in preparation
of the Porter Rockwell Trail Plan.

The City’s required match is $10,000. This amount has been included in the FY 2025
budget.

       C. DISCUSS 2025 MUNICIPAL ELECTION.

Rori Andreason said the City of Midvale has participated in the Alternative Voting Method
Pilot project of Ranked Choice Voting (RCV) for the past two municipal elections. This
approach replaced the traditional municipal election format, which includes a primary
election followed by a general election.

In order to be in compliance with Utah Code 17B-1-305, a notice of election must be sent
to the County Clerk by February 1, 2025. The notice must include the offices to be filled
at the next election and the dates for candidate declarations. The dates for candidate
declarations for the traditional method will be June 2-6. The dates for candidate
declarations for the ranked choice voting will be August 12-19. If the City wants to
participate in Ranked Choice Voting, the Council must pass a resolution to participate
and provide written notice to the Lieutenant Governor by May 1, 2025.

Seats Up for Election in 2025:
      • Mayor
      • City Council Districts 4 and 5

Key Differences:

       1. Traditional Municipal Election:
          o Includes two voting events: a primary election (to narrow the field of
             candidates) and a general election.
          o Requires voters to select one candidate per race.
          o Potential for higher election costs due to the dual-election structure.

       2. Ranked Choice Voting:
          o Conducted as a single election with no primary.
          o Voters rank candidates in order of preference.
          o Ensures the winning candidate has broad support by reallocating votes from
            eliminated candidates until a majority is achieved.
          o Streamlines the election process, potentially reducing costs and increasing
            voter engagement.
          o Election costs depend on how many cities participate in the RCV pilot
            program. While there is an additional cost associated with RCV, it eliminates
            the need for a primary, potentially offsetting expenses.
