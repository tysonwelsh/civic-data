5.     PUBLIC HEARING.CONSIDERATION.AND RECOMMENDATION on a Proposed
       Land Use Ordinance Text Amendment to Section 1L120.060 to Allow Gravel Parking
       Lots for Conditional Uses Along with Proposed Conditions for Consideration.
       Strangling Brothers,LLC Applicant Represented by Scott Knight Caitlvn Tubbs.Staff
       Presenter. (Application 2024-511- Continued from February 19.2025 Meeting.

Chair Cragun reported that this item was continued from the February 19, 2025, Planning
Commission Meeting.


Planning Manager, Caitlyn Tubbs indicated that the Planning Commission's comments had been
incorporated into the Text Amendment. As the changes were outside the scope ofthe original notice,
the matter was re-noticed to all affected property owners.

The Applicant intends to open a seasonal haunted circus at approximately 734 West 14600 South in
the Heavy Commercial ("HC") Zone. The Applicant's proposed use falls under the definition of
Outdoor Recreation and Entertainment, which is a Conditional Use in the zone. Site development
standards were discussed during pre-application meetings,including the requirement that a solid, hard
surface be utilized for access and parking areas. The Applicant proposed amendments to Bluffdale
City Code in order to allow a gravel surface. A photograph ofthe Applicant's current haimted circus
in American Fork was displayed, indicating a similar gravel surface and seasonal storage of the
trailers used as haunted structures.


Pursuant to Bluffdale City Code 11.120.060(A), all parking lots and parking structures shall be hard-
surfaced with asphalt or concrete. The Staff Report indicates that this has been the standard since
the City first adopted its Zoning Ordinances in 1979.


Ms. Tubbs reviewed the proposed Text Amendments. As originally proposed, gravel parking lots
would have also been allowed for hospitals and manufacturing uses. Based on Planning Commission
comments at the February 19, 2025 meeting, the change was limited to Outdoor Recreation and
Entertainment uses. Base preparation and gravel size requirements were also added. The Applicant
originally proposed a smaller gravel size and depth, but the Engineering Department specified an
angular one-inch gradation at a depth offour inches.

Amendments to Section 11.120.060 Design and Construction Standards are as follows:

       2.       Gravel parking lots may be permitted in place ofpaved parking surfaces for Recreation
                and Entertainment(Outdoor)projects that require a Conditional Use permit,subject to
                the standards in Chapter 11.200.040(B(2)(1).
                A.     The proposed gravel parking lot must meet all City safety and drainage
                       standards, and be comprised ofthe following:
       1.       Base Preparation: A compacted base layer capable of supporting a fire apparatus,
                based upon a recommendation prepared by a licensed geotechnical engineer and
                accepted by the city engineer, shall be placed once the site has been graded according
                to the approved grading and drainage plan; and
       2.       Gravel shall be angular one-inch gradation (1") and shall be installed at a minimum
                depth offour inches(4").


Amendments to Section 11.200.40(2) Approval Standards are as follows:

       1.       For proposals including gravel parking lots and/or accesses:
                A.     Detrimental effects on transportation corridors, wetlands, utilities, or
                       stormwater and drainage features stemming from gravel trackout.


Ms. Tubbs reported that Staff research indicated that the majority of concerns brought up by the
Planning Commission could be addressed through consideration of existing standards, but the above
addition was also required.

Commissioner Luker referenced the pond that was required to have an asphalt surface and asked if
the amendment would allow other outdoor recreation facilities to install gravel. Ms. Tubbs reported
that the pond proposal did not go through the Conditional Use Permit process. However, if the
Planning Commission forwards a positive recommendation for the Text Amendment and the City
Council approves it, any Outdoor Recreation and Entertainment use could have gravel accesses and
parking lots.

In response to a question from Commissioner Flynn, Ms. Tubbs reported that ifthe Text Amendment
is approved and an application is made for a Conditional Use Permit, a likely Condition of Approval
will be that the site drainage be directed away from any stormwater drainage features or wetlands.
Because gravel is a fairly dynamic material, the Applicant will be required to minimize impacts on
other systems.


Chair Cragun indicated that concerns were expressed in the last meeting about track out onto the two-
lane road. She asked if cities, where the Applicant has similar gravel lots, have experienced any
issues with trackout. Ms. Tubbs stated that there were no documented complaints or statements, and
the Applicant has stated that they have not had problems in American Fork. Community and
Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, indicated that he contacted American Fork when
the idea was first brought up, and they cautioned him about trackout and overflow parking. If the
Text Amendment was approved, those items would need to be addressed with the Conditional Use
Permit.


In response to a question from Chair Cragun, Mr. Crowell reported that parking is not allowed on
14600 South. If people parked illegally,they would be in the bicycle lane. He noted that Staff would
need to analyze the anticipated parking demand to determine if there will be adequate parking. Ms.
Tubbs added that the purpose of the Text Amendments is to ensure that those concerns can be
addressed during the Conditional Use Permit process. She noted that the Applicant has indicated that
the ability to utilize a grav^-parking lot and accesses is pivotal to the project.

The Applicant,Zac Tillotson stated that the drainage will be addressed by the engineering firm. The
property will have approximately 200 parking spaces, which is three times more available parking
than the American Fork property. Regarding concerns with trackout,they do not intend to allow cars
to exit onto 14600 South. They are working with the owner ofthe fish pond property to the north for
access through that property to 800 West. They hope to avoid curb cuts and other requirements for
direct entry from 14600 South. Mr. Tillotson is confident that an agreement will be reached. Due to
cost constraints, they cannot move forward with their plans if a paved surface is required.
Additionally, that would require utilities to be installed that would need to be demolished when the
land is eventually developed.

6.     Planning Commission Training - Integrated Land Use and Water General Plan
       Element


Chair Cragun stated that she will be attending the Utah Land Use Institute Conference online, which
will help fulfill a portion ofthe training requirements. She encouraged her fellow Commissioners to
attend either online or in person.


Ms. Tubbs reported that SB 76, which was enacted in 2023, requires all first through fourth-class
municipalities and all counties in Utah to create an Integrated Water Preservation and Land Use
Element in their General Plan. Water and land use planning have historically been siloed, and the
legislation is the State's effort to bring the elements together. The Element must be adopted on or
before December 31, 2025. No ongoing reporting is currently required, but there may be changes or
reporting requirements in the future.


A video was viewed in which Rick Webster fi:om the Utah Division of Water Resources("DWR")
discussed the Integrated Land Use and Water General Plan Element. Ms. Tubbs reported that the
water conservation plan was last updated in 2022, and it already addresses many of the new
requirements.


Chair Cragun expressed finstration with the current rebate program. To qualify for the rebate, she
would have been required to cover 50% of the subject area in waterwise vegetation. However, she
was not eligible for a rebate if she had no vegetation and therefore did not need to water. She asked
if the City could find ways to supplement that program and offer rebates for other scenarios.
Commissioner Flynn stated that he had the same issue. Ms. Tubbs reported that she will pass the
suggestion on to the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District("JVWCD")for consideration. The
Planning Commission can also recommend that the City Council consider offering additional rebates.


Commissioner Luker asked if the County's recommendations were available. Ms. Tubbs reported
that the County is responsible for the development ofthe Element for unincorporated townships and
County-owned land, so their plan would not affect Bluffdale. However, Staff coordinates with the
JVWCD to meet the goals outlined in their Regional Service Plan, which was updated in December

2024. Ms. Tubbs and Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer, have conducted preliminary research and
determined that the City is already exceeding goals set for 2040.

Ms. Tubbs noted that a requirement for the Integrated Water Preservation and Land Use Element is
recommendations for water conservation policies. The City has met that requirement by adopting the
JVWCD's waterwise landscaping standards, but the Planning Commission can consider whether the
standards need to be modified in order to fit Bluffdale's specific needs.

Partnership consultations are also required, and City Staff met with the DWR and JVWCD in
February 2025. Ms. Tubbs reported that the DWR offers some technical assistance, but as they have
been appointed as the reviewing body, that assistance is limited. The City is working with the
JVWCD to consider regional conservation goals, as well as the effect ofdevelopment patterns on the
Great Salt Lake. In response to a question from Chair Cragun, Ms.Tubbs confirmed that Staff would
be bringing related items to the Commission at future meetings.


As part of the strategy for developing the Element, Staff will update user counts and number of
connections, determine ifthere are any new large water users in the City,review progress on existing
goals, and identify areas for improvement and future demand. They will also coordinate with the
Parks Department to ensure that all irrigation is optimized.

As this will be a new element ofthe General Plan, a Notice to Prepare will be sent to the community
and the Wasatch Front Regional County in March or April. The data will be reviewed and language
drafted in Spring and Summer. Work sessions will begin in July, and the Element is scheduled for
final review and adoption in the Fall.


Commissioner Flynn asked if the Element would include construction of tanks and water storage
facilities to buffer future demand. Ms. Tubbs confirmed that it will consider recommendations for

maintenance and expansion ofthe infrastructure if needed.

In response to a question from Chair Cragun, Ms. Tubbs stated that it is always beneficial to have a
few joint sessions to discuss larger items like the Integrated Water Preservation and Land Use
Element, and Staff will coordinate that with the City Council.
