BRYCE’S LANDING PHASE B SUBDIVISION APPROVED:

     City Administrator Seth Atkinson updated the council about Bryce’s Landing Phase B Subdivision which is west of the commercial Phase A.  The subdivision had been approved by the Planning Commission.  He stated that the development is in the Combined Use zone which allows for both commercial and residential development.  Phase B proposes 46 units in a townhome style development.  The road system would be a loop to allow for pickup of garbage and snow plowing services.  The developer and builder will be separate entities, but one half of the water and electric impact fees would be paid in order to schedule a pre-construction meeting.
    The council had many concerns with the development.  A discussion ensued about putting a pause on these type of developments within the Combined Use zone.  Council directed staff to bring back an ordinance to pause development within the Combined Use zone so that they can revise the code to meet their vision of the city. City Attorney Kasey Wright informed the council that approving the development is not really a legislative function where they have a lot of choice.  It is an administrative function, where if it checks off all the boxes of the code, they have to say yes to the development.  
     Councilor Memmott moved to approve the Bryce’s Landing Phase B Subdivision.  The motion passed on the following roll call vote.

ORDINANCE 10-02-2021 ADOPTING PARKS AND RECREATION IMPACT FEES APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed the process that had been taken to come to this point in time to adopt an ordinance establishing parks and recreation impact fees.   He stated that several months ago, the Council directed staff to begin the process of developing an impact fee facility plan and analysis for a parks and recreation impact fee.  The city contracted with Lewis, Young, Robertson, and Burningham to develop these documents.  
Based on the findings in the analysis, the impact fee is estimated to be at $2,802 for a single-family home and $2,016 for multi-family.  Projects included in the analysis were:
Juab Fieldhouse (The Hive)
Red Cliffs Ball Fields Park
Quad Plex Ball Fields South City
Canyon View Curb & Gutter
Town Square Bowery
Orgill Lighting
Land Acquisition for Old Mill to Orgill Fields Trail
These impact fees could also be used for future investments and improvements to parks that add capacity to the system.  

     A public hearing was held on October 19th to receive public input.  Some questions were answered at the hearing.  One question involved how the fees could be used to help create open space and parks in new development.  If the parks and recreation impact fees are charged to the developer, based on the number of units, then these fees could be used in negotiations for open space or land acquisition for parks.  If the ordinance is adopted, a 90-day waiting period would begin before the fees would take effect on January 31, 2022.  The council could adopt the full recommended fee in the analysis or a percentage of the impact fee.
     Councilor Jones moved to adopt the full impact fee in Ordinance 11-02-2021.  Councilor Ostler seconded the motion.  The motion passed on a unanimous vote.    

REVISED EMPLOYMENT RESIDENCY POLICY ADOPTED:
 
      Mr. Atkinson explained that the current personnel policy required that a full-time employee must live within the 84648-zip code area.  Because of the lack of affordable  and limited housing options that currently exist, the council has been concerned about the limited recruiting efforts of full-time positions because of this policy.   Mr. Atkinson presented a revised policy to help alleviate these problems.  The new policy is as follows:

“Residency is required within the 84648, 84639, and 84645 zip codes for all new, full-time, year-round, on-call employees beginning November 2, 2021.   Residency is a condition of successfully completing the probationary period.  If a new on-call hire is not a resident of the 84648, 84639, or 84645 zip codes by the end of the probationary period, he or she cannot successfully complete probation and cannot become a regular employee but will be subject to termination.  In addition, if a full-time, on-call employee has chosen to move outside of the 84648, 84639, and 84645 zip codes after meeting these requirements, the employee may be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including termination.  The residency requirement does not allow for a vehicle take-home practice unless the employee lives within the 84648-zip code.  The City Administrator and Police Chief must reside within the Nephi City limits.   Public Works Superintendents must live in the 84648-zip code”

Councilor Ostler moved to adopt the new residency requirements revisions.  Councilor Worwood seconded the motion.  The motion passed on a unanimous vote. 

UDOT PROJECT COOPERATIVE AGREEMENT APPROVED:

    Mr. Atkinson updated the council about a UDOT project that will begin next summer.  UDOT will be doing a mill and fill project along Highway 132 from the Fairgrounds to the middle freeway exit.  Along this path, the city has several drainage ways that need to be refurbished.  This project provides an excellent opportunity to do so.  This would smooth out the drainage dips that meet with 100 North.  UDOT has provided a cost for these improvements and staff recommends funding the amount from the Street Improvements line in the Capital Projects Fund.  
     Councilor Jones had invited some residents that live in the area of this project to come to the meeting and voice their concerns about speeding on Highway 132 and the approach to the railroad tracks.  Mariah Sperry handed out  some pictures  and reported of many accidents happening in the area.    Bryce Gardner spoke of his concern about the dangerous approach to railroad tracks.  Ms. Sperry suggested having some flashing speed signs installed.  UDOT officials said that many of the issues are the responsibility of Union Pacific Railroad.  The residents said they have contacted the Railroad before with very little response from them about the dangerous situation.  The UDOT officials agreed to bring up these concerns at the next quarterly meeting with Nephi City officials. 
     Councilor Worwood moved to ratify the UDOT Project Agreement.  Councilor Ostler seconded the motion.  The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
