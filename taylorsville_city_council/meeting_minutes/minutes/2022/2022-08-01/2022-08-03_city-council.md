         1. 2    Mayor' s Report


  Alal   Mayor Kristie Overson reported that she walked the annual Days of '47 Parade with the
Youth Council      on   Taylorsville' s float. She expressed her appreciation to UTA' s Carlton
Christensen who provided an air-conditioned bus for them to remain cool in since they were the
91st of 99 parade entrants on a very hot day. She also wished to thank Ray Havens who had driven
the float.


The Mayor thanked Chief Cottam for participating in Chamber West' s Board of Governor' s
meeting. He joined the police chiefs of West Jordan and West Valley, sharing information about
current crime trends, staffing challenges, mental health issues and ways that local businesses can
better help police departments protect their communities.

It was nearly time for school to start and Taylorsville' s Human Resources Director Stephanie Snow
had recently conducted training sessions for incoming crossing guards who were tasked with
watching over children as they made their way to school. These trainings educated the guards on
their roles and responsibilities.



Fireworks season had recently wrapped up and Mayor Overson wished to thank the first responders
at both Stations # 117 and# 118. She explained that the fire crews were constantly at the ready to

respond to any incidents, particularly during such a major drought.

Mayor Overson stated that a huge debt of gratitude was owed to Sen. Daniel Thatcher who had
represented portions of Taylorsville in the past. Sen. Thatcher had sponsored the new 9- 8- 8 hotline
which provided immediate access to crisis intervention and support for mental health. The creation
of a three- digit hotline for Utah had led to the creation of one throughout the United States.


That evening there would be discussion of the transfer of ownership of two properties from Salt
Lake County to Taylorsville. With ownership came responsibility. The administration had been

working internally on final design plans for a Master Parks Plan which had been received after a
great deal of input. She anticipated robust discussions about parks, budgets, construction, etc. as
the city addressed what they wanted to see on these properties. She pointed out that one of the
properties involved in agenda item# 6. 2 was not a park but instead, a retention basin.

         3. 3    West Valley Animal Services Report—Melanie Bennett


 El West Valley Animal Services Director Melanie Bennett reported on animal services activities
from January through June of 2022.        There had been slightly more animal licenses issued as
compared to those in 2021 although Millrace Park licenses had decreased a bit from the previous
years.   The clerks had handled 3006 contacts ( either walk-ins or phone calls) and although the
number of animals impounded had increased significantly, most of those impounded animals were
either adopted/ rescued or returned to owners. Assigning animal services officers to specific areas
of the city had resulted in a significant reduction in response times, particularly for Priority 3- 5
calls.  There were currently 165 animals in the shelter when the report was created two weeks
before. The daily average in 2022 was 132 animals which was up significantly from 95 in 2021.
The shelter' s staffing level was not sufficient for the current workload which unfortunately left
staff no time for enrichment, behavior training or adoption meets. An animal intake comparison
showed a 17. 60% increase over 2021 and the number of surgeries completed was up 34.48%.




Ms. Bennett mentioned that the shelter had begun accepting rabbits ( assuming they were already
contained), each ofwhich needed to be quarantined for two weeks before having contact with other
animals.

          6. 2    Resolution No. 22- 12 A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville approving an
          Interlocal Cooperation Agreement between the City and Salt Lake County to transfer
          ownership of two parks to the City— John Taylor


        Mr. Taylor explained that the first of the two parks was Taylorsville Park or" missile park"
at 4700    South Redwood Road.     Salt Lake County and the city attorney' s office had successfully
negotiated a transfer of the park to Taylorsville. The city would pick up a little extra cost from the
county to continue maintaining the portion that Salt Lake County had been taking care of. One of
the best clauses in the agreement spelled out that once the property was transferred to the city, if
the city chose not to use it anymore and was unable to sell it for public use, the property would go
back to Salt Lake County and the county would have to compensate the city for the depreciated
value of its capital investment in park improvements, excluding any funds received from the
county. Mr. Taylor stated that was the case for both properties addressed in the resolution.

Councilmember Harker asked Mr. Taylor to address the matter brought up by Mr. McElreath
during the citizen comment period ofthe meeting. Mr. Taylor explained that prior to the discussion
about     transferring parks, the city secured $ 1. 5 million from the TRCC program for capital
improvements at this park which needed new landscaping, irrigation, new structures, etc. In
exchange for those funds, Taylorsville had to match that amount which resulted in a total of
 3, 000,000 now allocated for improvements at that park. The County had indicated that they would
do what they could to prioritize any future funding requests for this park without making any actual
additional promises.




Councilmember Cochran stated it was his understanding that this funding was not included in the
current    FY22- 23 budget. If that were the case, did that mean that any additional expenses would

anticipated expenses in the budget, and they would reconcile it at year-end when the funds

The Skyview Detention Basin was the second " park" referenced in Resolution No. 22- 12 and it
collected water from Salt Lake County, Kearns, West Valley, West Jordan, Taylorsville, etc. It
was approximately six feet deep at times and drained from several inlets. He felt it was a regional
storm drain facility basin. However, Salt Lake County Flood Control no longer wished to own,

operate, or maintain     it.
                          The city could decline it, but in that event, another entity could take

Mayor Overson wished to make it clear that she did not consider this basin a park even though it
was referred to as such in the property transfer agreement. The city had no plans to turn it into a
park and Mr. Taylor pointed out that it was fenced to keep people out.

Mr. Taylor said Salt Lake County would be getting rid of the basin, whether it was to go to
Taylorsville or some other entity. He recommended Taylorsville accept it to ensure it was properly
maintained. Their immediate goal was to get Google to remove the name " park" from the on- line
description of the property.

Mr. White agreed that it was in the best interest of the city to accept the basin.
