       a.      DAI Master Plan Update, Nate Shipp.

Nate Shipp, Principal of DAI, introduced his partner, Bryan Flamm, and provided the history and
background of the Independence Master Plan Community. Mr. Shipp provided aerial photos of
the development and the Comprehensive Master Plan from 2017 which now includes Heritage
Crest. He reported that the homes in the area have been built, the park is complete, the schools
have been opened, and the apartments are being leased. The project is in the final phase and the
latest plat has been approved for townhomes. Mr. Shipp noted that some of the areas have
nicknames such as ''pork chop" due to the shape of the area and "sore thumb" due to the way the
area stands out. Wendy Aston asked when the development will be completed. Mr. Shipp hoped
it would be completed within two to four years.

Mr. Shipp provided the approved transportation plan and explained that the original plan was to
have a full intersection tying into Heritage 76 Road, however, the light was shifted further south,
which removes the original connection point. Mayor Timothy asked Assistant City Engineer,
Trevor Andra if he knew about the light being shifted. Mr. Andra believed the decision was made
by the City and DAI.

Ryan Hales from Hales Engineering explained that they met with UDOT to discuss the signal
locations. He explained that UDOT wanted to tie the interchange at Noell Nelson Drive however,
in the environmental document they completed, they identified Day Ranch Road as the signal
location. Mr. Hales stated that the question now was whether to signalize one or both locations.
His recommendation was to signalize both locations.

Mr. Shipp commented that the signal change has had a significant impact on the original design.
He identified the challenges it has created with connecting the roads. Mr. Shipp reported that he
met with staff and came up with ideas that were outlined in the Transportation Plan. Mr. Shipp
asked the Council Members to table approval of the Transportation Plan for at least two weeks to
give him time to meet with UDOT. He explained that the road will go further north and the
buildings will be pulled back further south. Jeff Gaston asked if Mr. Shipp was asking for a rezone.
Mr. Shipp replied that they intend to work with staff to reach a resolution and was unsure if
rezoning will be necessary.

The Council discussed the location of the intersection, the number of units currently entitled, and
whether two weeks was enough time to table the item. Staff would work through the issues with
DAI and provide a plan for Council consideration over the next two weeks.

Bryan Flamm provided information on the "sore thumb" area, which is currently zoned for sand
and gravel to help get the project through the process. He showed large berms going to the back
and coming down both sides of the pits at Geneva. He pointed out that the traffic flow is primarily
on the 14600 South off-ramp and Porter Rockwell Boulevard. Mr. Flamm provided an overview

           a. Howland Partners Proposal Regarding Four Acres of Property Behind

Dana Howland and Tina Franco from Howland Partners, Inc. introduced themselves and noted that
they are the developers of Saddleback Pointe located on the comer of 2700 West and Bangerter
Highway. Mr. Howland stated that they have the property behind Mid valley Bible Church under
contract and met with neighbors to propose expanding Saddleback Pointe. Due to opposition from
the community, other options were presented including a 55 and over active adult community to
be called Country Villas. The neighbors responded very positively to the idea and approved the
idea of eight units per acre there.

Mr. Howland presented the preliminary site plan, which consists of slab on grade, main floor
access, ADA compliant units with two-car garages, and enough space to park a car in front of the
garage. Traci Crockett asked about the size of the units. Mr. Howland stated that they will range
between 1,500 and 1,800 square feet with the option to build a loft above the garage.

in the area with the same type of finishes. Ms. Franco added that a clubhouse is also planned.

approve of the plan. The neighbors indicated that they are in favor of the plan. Mayor Timothy
asked City Attorney, Vaughn Pickell, what authority the City has to ensure that the units only
house residents who are 55 years of age and over. Mr. Pickell stated that the age guidelines will
be enforced by the HOA.

Dave Kallas raised concerns about breaking the barrier of one-acre lots and stated that neighbors
in surrounding areas do not want anything smaller than one acre lots. Mr. Howland stated that
residents within 1,000 feet of the property were invited to the discussion. Members of the church
were asked what they think of the plan and they were very excited about the idea. Dave Kallas
was open to the idea but preferred to see an assisted living facility that provides senior care.

City Planner and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell noted that the General Plan will

6.      PUBLIC HEARING - Consideration and Vote on an Amendment to the City of
        Bluffdale General Plan, Specifically an Update to the City-Wide Transportation Plan,
        City of Bluffdale, Applicant, Staff Presenter, Trevor Andra.

Assistant City Engineer, Trevor Andra explained that in the work session DAI asked that the
Transportation Plan be tabled. He and noted that he would still like to go through the plan. He
reported that the Transportation Master Plan, Drinking Water Master Plan, and Storm Water
Master Plan have all been updated. Mr. Andra explained that the plans were developed based on
the Master Plans including areas of development, projected growth rates, and what existing units
will look like and cover the next IO and some build-out plans.

Mr. Andra displayed maps with existing development and unit counts as well as a map of areas
that could be developed by 2030 based on the projected growth rate. He added that the plans
project approximately 2,400 new units, which would be mostly residential.

Jeremy Searle from Hales Engineering addressed the Transportation Plan and stated that the
considerations include existing and future traffic demand as well as capacity deficiencies and cost
estimates to address the deficiencies. He also provided the most recent and updated Functional
Classification Map, which outlines the road DAI has concerns about.

Mr. Searle stated that the Wasatch Front Regional Council ("WFRC") Demand Model was used
to run 2030 projections for Bluffdale's no-build model to identify locations with capacity
deficiencies and provided a list of projects that that would address those deficiencies. Mayor
Timothy asked Mr. Searle to keep in mind the high expense of transportation projects.

Mr. Searle stated that a similar process was used for the 2050 build-out and includes additional
capacity deficiencies that involve widening roads, intersection improvements, and the Bringhurst
extension. Mayor Timothy raised concerns about Loumis Parkway being widened and asked if
there are studies supporting the need for three lanes. Mr. Searle stated that it is based on future
projections and there are no studies at this time. He noted that tum pockets at intersections could
serve as an alternative in addition to other options.

          and RDA Open and Public Meeting Laws Training Held in Coniunction).

Mr. Pickell stated that public bodies are required to follow Open and Public Meeting Laws and
outlined the following rules:

      •   Meetings consisting of a quorum of ~ouncil Members must be designated as public
          meetings, except in certain situations such as a Christmas Party or an unintentional meeting.
          Mr. Pickell cautioned the Council against communicating via email or other electronic
          communication as it could rise to the level of a public meeting.

      •   All public meetings require 24 hours' advance notice to the public, except in an emergency
          such as a flood or other unexpected event. Notices are posted at City Hall, on the Utah
          Public Notice website, and provided to newspapers. Notice must include the date, time,

         meetings such as web conferences and a physical site must be made available for the public
         to participate.

     •   Minutes must be recorded for all open and public meetings and must include the date, time,
         location, attendees, action taken, and votes as they serve as the official record of the
         meeting. Meetings also must be recorded and the audio recordings made available within
         three days of the meeting. Meeting minutes must be approved by the City Council.

     •   The Council may have closed meetings, but only for specific purposes. The Council must
         be in an open meeting and have a two-thirds majority vote to hold a closed meeting. The

         collective bargaining strategies, litigation, real property acquisitions, security issues, or any
         allegations or investigations of criminal misconduct. The Mayor must sign off indicating
         that the meeting was held for one of those reasons.
