SUMMARY & BACKGROUND
This application is a request for approval of a Plat Amendment for approximately 0.47 acres located at
14812 S Oak Bluff Place (Exhibit B). The property is currently zoned RM, as established through the
1999 Draper City Municipal Code (1999 DCMC) and the Suncrest Master Development Agreement. The
applicant is requesting that a Plat Amendment and deviation from 1999 DCMC Section 9-15-040(a) be
approved to allow the placement of a new single-family dwelling outside the approved buildable area by
moving the limit of disturbance and allowing construction within a portion of the slope that is steeper than
30%.

The subject property was platted in 1999 as part of the Oak Vista No. 1 at Suncrest subdivision
(application 98-037). As part of that subdivision approval, a Limit of Disturbance (LOD) area was
approved on many of the lots due to the natural grade and slope on the site. The Suncrest Development
Guidelines set specific criteria in determining where disturbances should be allowed. Section 4.2.3.4 sets
this criteria as follows:

      1. Avoidance of (30%+) slopes and natural drainages.
      2. Preservation of significant trees and native vegetation.
      3. Fire prevention and safety including, but not limited to, location of vegetation near structures.

The LOD does not allow for permanent structures to be placed within that area, which on the subject lot
has a slope steeper than 30%. This requirement was due to the “Hillside Overlay District” that was in
effect at that time, and currently still exists as the “Hillside Sensitive Lands Overlay District.”


ANALYSIS
General Plan and Zoning. The Land Use Map of the General Plan calls for the Residential Medium
Density land use designation for the subject property (Exhibit C). This category is characterized as
follows:




The property has been assigned the RM zoning classification, supporting approximately twelve dwelling
units per acre (Exhibit D). The purpose of the RM zone, as established in the 1999 DCMC, is to “provide
areas for low-to-medium residential density, with the opportunity for varied housing styles and character,
providing for a maximum density of up to twelve (12) units per acre for medium to high density
residential unit projects subject to conditional-use permit procedures and conditions for this type of use
and based on minimum development guidelines adopted by the City.”

The RM Zone zoning designation is not identified by the General Plan as a preferred zoning classification
for the Residential Medium Density land use designation as it is no longer a zoning designation used by
the City. RM zoning abuts the subject property on all sides.

Subdivision Layout. The applicant is proposing to modify the area for the LOD. The Suncrest
Development Guidelines call for no grading or vegetation removal taking place within the LOD area,
except for selective vegetation removal in order to meet minimum fire break requirements. The existing
LOD area on Lot 27 occupies 12,675 square feet of the 20,500 square foot lot (Exhibit E). The LOD area
is located primarily along the rear half of the property, and can be measured as a distance from the
property line of roughly 106-feet to 59-feet deep.

The proposed LOD area is 5,178 square feet (Exhibit F), a reduction of 7,497 square feet. The new LOD
area would be located along the rear of the lot, and would range in depth from the property line of roughly
42-feet to 27-feet. According to the Slope Map for the property (Exhibit I) slopes 30% or greater are
found on 50.5% of the property, or 10,360 square feet. The proposed modification would allow 4,854
square feet of the 30% or greater slope area to be impacted by new buildable area.

The applicant is not proposing to modify any property lines, public utility easements, or access points.

Geologic Review. The applicant has provided Geo-technical and Geo-hazard reports for review and
approval by the City’s consultants, Taylor Geotechnical and Simon Associates LLC. Both reports were
approved on December 7, 2020. The reviews can be found as Exhibit M and the reports can be found as
Exhibit N of this staff report. The reports found the plat designated buildable area contains a volcanic ash
layer. This area has been identified in the reports as a Quaternary Landslide Block Failure Deposit. This
means that the ground has been subject to landslides. A landslide can be defined as a mass of soil and
rock that is moved downslope by gravity. Block slides occur where blocks or masses of intact soil or rock
move downslope along a failure surface without rotation or internal deformation of the landslide mass. A
block slide has straight failure surfaces, and the motion is analogous to a box (the landslide mass) sliding
down a ramp (the failure surface).

In order to build within the plat designated buildable area, the applicant would have to remove unsuitable
soils. This would require an excavation of 30-feet or more. The applicant is proposing to move the home
to the southwest of the plat designated buildable area and into the 30% slope hillside (Exhibit H). This
would result in a required excavation of 2-feet to 15-feet in order to remove the unsuitable soils. A map
showing the location of the bedrock has been provided demonstrating where the excavation will have to
occur in order to reach bedrock (Exhibit L).

If the deviation is approved, the applicant would be required to comply with all of the standards and
findings in the geo-technical and geo-hazards reports. The applicant would also be required to ensure the
slope stability of the site during construction so that there is not detrimental effects on adjacent properties.

The applicant has submitted a letter outlining the request in Exhibit G. Images of the property can be
found in Exhibit K. Comments received from neighbors can be found in Exhibit L of this report.

Criteria For Approval. The 1999 DCMC did not contain criteria for review and potential approval of a
Plat Amendment.

The criteria for review and potential approval of a deviation request is found in Section 9-15-040 of the
1999 DCMC. This section depicts the standard of review for such requests as:

     9-15-040 Development Requirements
     (a) Development in General. Slope areas in excess of 30% may not be developed, and no more than
     thirty percent (30%) of a development's slope areas in excess of 30% may be included in the area
     calculation to determine density. The Planning Commission may modify this requirement upon
     finding that:
     (1) No significant harm will result; and
     (2) The proposed modification will result in a more functional and improved plan; and
     (3) The developer/builder agrees to comply with any conditions or requirements imposed by the
     Planning Commission to mitigate any adverse effects which may result from the proposed
     modification.

The Suncrest Development Guidelines prohibit development within the 30% slope area, set the limits of
disturbance requirement, and provide a process for deviation to construct within 30% slope areas in
sections 4.2.3 and 4.7.

     4.2.3 Natural Lots
     1. Buildings may not be constructed in areas exceeding 30% slope except as provided for in Section
     4.7.
     3. Limits of disturbance shall be designated for each Natural Lot at the time of final plat approval.
     The final plat shall designate on each Natural Lot the area on which construction, grading, filling
     and vegetation removal may occur. No grading or vegetation removal shall take place outside the
     established limits of disturbance except as may be provided in Paragraph 4. Limits of disturbance
     shall be marked by construction fencing, colored tape, or other means acceptable to the City on
     Natural Lots prior to starting construction to prevent incidental incursion into areas to be protected.
     Building footprints of principal and accessory structures, walls, decks, patios, access driveways,
     gardens, lawns and excavation spoils shall be included within the designated limits of disturbance.

     4.7 Special Exceptions
     Notwithstanding any language contained in the SunCrest Development Agreement, Reimbursement
     Agreement, or Master Plan, including these Development Guidelines or the Architectural Design
     Guidelines, Special Exceptions to City Laws or these Development Guidelines may be granted by the
     City upon finding that no significant harm will result from granting the Special Exception. Special
     Exceptions are distinct from variances allowed by City ordinances, and are subject to the following
     standards of review and findings:
     a. The Special Exception complies with the purposes and intent of the Sun Crest Master Plan, is
     compatible with the overall SunCrest Development Concept and Planning Objectives and is based
     upon their site-specific application and the circumstances and constraints unique to the lot or
     involved site.
     b. The Special Exception constitutes an appropriate balancing of health, safety, aesthetic and general
     welfare objectives and the specific facts and conditions that justify the Special Exception.

If the Planning Commission decides to approve the request, staff recommends they include the following
conditions of approval:

1.        That all requirements of the Draper City Engineering, Public Works, Building, Planning, and Fire
          Divisions are satisfied throughout the development of the site and the construction of all buildings
          on the site, including permitting.

          development of the site and the construction of all buildings on the site.
3.        The applicant work with their contractor and geo-technical/geo-hazards consultants during site
          construction to ensure the property maintains slope stability for adjacent properties.

          modification.

5.        The deviation constitutes an appropriate balancing of health, safety, aesthetic and general welfare

          modification.

5.        The deviation does not constitute an appropriate balance of health, safety, aesthetic and general
          welfare objectives and the specific facts and conditions do not justify the deviation.

Enforcement of Zoning Ordinance:
Currently the lot is subdivided as a single family lot and therefore deemed “Buildable”. The constraints
of the platted buildable envelope and the discovered ash fill would require significant expense for the
property owner to remove, pier and/or support a structure within the current buildable area.

Special Circumstances:
The lot in discussion is unique as many of the lots East appear to be sitting more on the volcanic rock
whereas this lot appears to have been at the base of a small slide where the ash fill settled.

Granting of deviation from DCMC 9-16-040(A)
Granting of the deviation is not about enjoyment of the property, it is about safety of the design of the
structure and crucial to proper connection between the foundation of the house and sustainable natural
soils for required weight loads.

General Plan and Public Interest:
We are aware applications and requests to build higher on a slope are a regular occurrence. Many
applications use reasons of evidence to attempt to support the goal for a better view. This individual
occurrence has nothing to do with view however I can see a negative impact of other property owners
using reason of “If they are allowed, why am I not” in future presentations.

Spirit of Zoning:
I do believe the sprit of zoning and the intent of the general plan will remain intact with approval of this
special occurrence. As Draper city planning commission requires review of all applications when asking
for a plat amendment and/or variance I believe each application is subject to fair consideration and will
be determined based of evidence of each request and reasons behind the application. Safety is of
upmost importance.
No significant Harm by approving application:
We believe the approval of this application will not deter or create harm among the existing homes nor
the existing neighborhood. The currently approved limit of disturbance already required grading and
removal of natural vegetation on the land. Further, the approval of the application allows a much more
safely built structure on the land. This would not have been determined when the land was approved in
subdivide as the geological explorations were not explored to the depth or detail of what would be
needed to determine the ash issues on this land. One might display concern that this application would
block a view from the directly neighboring lot/existing home however the existing buildable envelope on
this lot already allows for a structure to be built that could block the same view.

More functional and improved plan:
The approval of application will allow the structure to be secured to bedrock on the hillside that will
support required structural engineering to safely build a home suitable for occupancy. This application is
not about view, cost or design; it is based of safety.

 Response: While performing a soils investigation the geotechnical engineer found a slip plane running north and
