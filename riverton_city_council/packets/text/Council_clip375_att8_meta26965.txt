30       b. Accessory Dwelling Unit Ordinance Update
31
32   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that the City’s contract consultant Zions Public Finance,
33   Inc. would be presenting information and recommendations in a Moderate Income Housing
34   Report related to the General Plan.
35
36   Mr. Carter explained that, as a result of the rising statewide housing prices, the Utah State
37   Legislature passed SB34 in the 2019 Legislative Session stipulating actions that municipalities
38   shall take to encourage and facilitate affordable housing, requiring three or more from a list. He
39   said the legislation did not include funding for this year but they may in future years; He
40   recommended that the Council Members be thinking about what types of options the City should
41   pursue in order to meet the legislature’s expectations and providing progress in facilitating
42   modern income housing within Riverton City. He said that staff has recommended the adoption
43   of an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) Ordinance as one of the stipulating actions. He said the
44   adoption of an ADU ordinance would produce the most realistic and immediate results.
45
46   Mr. Carter then presented information regarding Moderate Income Housing, which is defined by
47   HUD as “housing occupied or reserved for occupancy by households with a gross household
48   income equal to or less than 80% of the median gross income for households of the same size
49   in the county which the City is located.

 1   Jonathan Ward explained that in June of this year, the State of Utah, Department of Natural
 2   Resources awarded a loan to Riverton City in the amount of $11,985,000 to install secondary
 3   water meters on all customer connections (the “Project”). The interest payable is set at the
 4   annual rate of 1%. As a condition of receiving this loan, he said that Riverton City must secure
 5   the loan by issuing bonds secured by revenues collected as water rate fees. The Department of
 6   Natural Resources also expects Riverton City to provide $2,115,000.00 to the Project.
 7   Additional conditions of receiving the loan were enclosed with a notification letter that Riverton
 8   City received from the Department of Natural Resources.
 9
10   Mr. Ward said the purpose of the proposed resolution was to begin the process of issuing a
11   series of bonds that shall be repaid from revenues generated from water utility fees (“revenue
12   bonds”). In addition to the terms required by the Department of Natural Resources, Riverton
13   must comply with the Local Government Bonding Act, Title 11, Chapter 14, Utah Code. Before
14   closing on a transaction to issue revenue bonds the Act requires: 1) Riverton City give notice of
15   its intent to issue such bonds; 2) hold a public hearing on the date of December 3, 2019 to
16   receive input from the public. The public hearing must receive information, if any, regarding the
17   issuance of the bonds and “what the potential economic impact that the improvement, facility or
18   property for which the bonds pay all or part of the cost will have on the private sector.

46       c. Discussion Regarding Compensation for City Council Members Who Serve on
47          Governmental Boards, Committees, Districts, or Interlocal Entities
48
49   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that members of the City Council individually serve on
50   various governing boards. While each governing board may require roughly the same
51   commitment in terms of the number of board meetings to attend each year, he said that the
     Page | 9 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     October 15, 2019



 1   stipends offered by the governing entities for attending board meetings could vary widely from
 2   one entity to the next. Some offer no compensation, while others pay upwards of $5,000 per
 3   year.
 4
 5   Mr. Carter continued and said that, stated generally, elected officials who attend board meetings
 6   of other governing entities because they were elected to serve Riverton City ought to be
 7   compensated for this service. Providing such compensation advances the cause of enabling
 8   qualified individuals to participate in government without sacrificing personal income in order to
 9   do so. However, he said that the disparity in pay offered by the various governing entities should
10   not influence decisions made by the City’s elected officials as to which committees they seek to
11   attend.
12
13   Mr. Carter said the purpose of the agenda item was to discuss whether the stipends offered by
14   the different governing entities ought to be equalized in some fashion with use of City funds. A
15   summary of the stipends offered by the different governmental entities which need the
16   participation of the City’s elected officials was presented and discussion was held whether a
17   program needed to be implemented that facilitates equal pay for equal participation in different
18   governing boards.
19
20   Each Council Member spoke in favor of equalizing board meeting stipends due to the amount of
21   time spent each month representing Riverton City. They all concurred with holding more
22   discussion at the November 12th City Council Meeting.
