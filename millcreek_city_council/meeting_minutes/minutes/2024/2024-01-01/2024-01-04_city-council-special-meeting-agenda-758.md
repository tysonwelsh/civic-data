Mayor Silvestrini called the work meeting to order at 8:10 a.m.

1. Millcreek Report
   Mayor Silvestrini spoke about Millcreek concerns. Millcreek is especially concerned with
   overturning local zoning control of mining operations. This is something to keep eyes on. It is
   very easy to start a small mining operation and then convert it into a larger mining operation.
   Given the air quality for air challenges that the state faces it is bad for the health of all valley
   residents so adding more mining operations is very problematic. Utah can only control a
   percentage of Utah air quality, adding mining operations that add particulates to the air could
   even threaten Federal Highway funding if Utah does not meet Federal air quality standards.
   Senator Blouin said that the Olympics coming to Utah is helpful because people will not
   want to see a mine in Parley's Canyon as they drive through it. Mayor Silvestrini said that
   Parley's Canyon is largely neglected and should be protected more. There are a lot of
   recreation opportunities there. Representative Moss said the canyon is also historic. Council
   Member DeSirant said that state legislators complain about the federal government coming to
   Utah when they don't understand Utah. It is the same at the local level. Local government
   governs best.

  Mayor Silvestrini spoke about housing problems in Utah. The Governor is supportive of the
  construction of new stai1er homes so the legislature will be working on that issue. There is
  still the problem of local control here. Millcreek is supportive of affordable housing, and
  missing middle housing. A lot of Millcreek development is infill, there is not space for 200 lot
  subdivisions. The concern that Millcreek has is that development will sometimes tear down
  affordable housing to create new development. Millcreek has reduced regulations regarding
  parking where it makes sense to do that. Mayor Silvestrini asked the legislators to work on
  solutions for affordable housing that work with a minimum of unintended consequences.
  Representative Moss spoke with a Millcreek resident who asked why not remodel older
  housing stock instead of building more homes. Mayor Silvestrini said that he spoke with the
  Governor on this, and the justification of the Governor said he would like to put dollars
  towards new housing because Utah is growing so fast. New construction does help the
  housing stock deficit. People ask Mayor Silvestrini why Millcreek allows higher density
  housing. It is because the market is moving that way and Millcreek does not have space to
  expand into empty fields. Land is more expensive and the room to grow is up. Phoenix and
  Los Angeles are sprawling. Millcreek itself does not build housing but they allow higher
  density to address infrastructure needs. Council Member Jackson keeps seeing that schools in
  the center of the valley are closing because people move to the edges of the valley where
  homes are more affordable. Affordable housing in the center of the valley would help.

  Representative Hayes asked about affordable housing mandates for cities. Planning Director,
  Francis Lilly said that he has heard from his Planning colleagues that developers do not want
  to build Condos due to the risk of liability issues if there are problems with the building later.
  Lenders do not like to finance condos for a variety of reasons. Mr. Lilly says the affordability
  issue raises questions that some in the legislature are not comfortable worth having. Rules are
  in place for affordable housing, but these rules are not taken advantage of. Mr. Winder said
  that one issue that influences affordable housing is that low-income deals for housing are
  running out. Those people are then being displaced and the apartments are being turned into
  higher cost housing. Mr. Winder spoke about making some RDA tweaks that encourage more
  affordable housing. Mayor Silvestrini spoke about housing and unsheltered populations, there
  needs to be more subsidization, but cities do not have the money to do this. Representative
  Moss spoke about one of her bills that will tie funds received from the sale of alcohol to
  subsidizing housing costs. Mayor Silvestrini then spoke about entitling smaller lots, this
  would help affordability. Representative Hayes spoke about problems in Salt Lake City with
  hedge funds buying housing stock. Mayor Silvestrini said this is a problem all over Utah.
  Institutional investors are building homes to only rent them, not sell them. The more transient
  a population is the less involved they will be in civic affairs. Mr. Lilly spoke about how Short­
  Term Rentals negatively influence the housing market. Millcreek is losing 130 units at
  Holladay Hills but is gaining 150 units in west Millcreek but that project costs $50 million. It
  is far cheaper to keep housing stock than build new homes. Representative Moss spoke about
  her constituents in Murray who live in a condo development where so many of the units are
  owned by investors that there are not enough people to vote to make improvements to the
  building. Senator Blouin said that Murray City is redeveloping their old city hall building
  and will turn those into units for ownership.

  Mr. Spatafore spoke about important bills to watch out for. HB 13 is a financing infrastructure
  bill that would help developers build infrastructure like sewer lines in a development and then
  get their money back. This would incentivize new home builds where infrastructure does not

2. Capstone Strategies Report
   Mr. Spatafore spoke about HB 84. This deals with school security. This is a good bill but has
   issues that need to be dealt with. One issue is a school security officer that is a law
   enforcement officer, but they cannot maintain their certification without being part of a law
   enforcement agency. Other options include other armed security or armed volunteers. The bill
   does not address what entity would pay for this. Administrative control of school security
   would also go to the county not the city that the school is in. This is incredibly problematic.
   There are not enough sworn police officers to have one in every school. This bill needs
   changes, consequently law enforcement, and city officials will be proposing changes.
   Representative Moss said that school resource officers in Holladay have been very successful.
   Council Member Uipi arrived at 9:17 a.m. Representative Hayes asked if this bill is a sure
   thing. Mr. Spatafore said this bill is very important for many people, but changes need to be
   made for the next steps for school security. Millcreek wants an opportunity for engagement
   between UPD officers and the community. In schools in Millcreek SRO officers are very
   important, these officers are a great resource. Mr. Spatafore believes that they can help get the
   bill to a better place.
