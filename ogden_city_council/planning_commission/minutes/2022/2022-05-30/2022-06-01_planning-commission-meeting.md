Mr. Brierley stated Staff has been working with community members in an effort to update of the East Central
Community Plan over the past few years. While not a comprehensive update, the focus was to include specific
regulations for Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) which will soon run through the community, to include regulations to
address recommendations of the 9-Rails Creative District Master Plan and to determine whether accessory
dwelling units are appropriate in the single-family zones within the community. A steering committee of residents
has made some recommendations, surveys were conducted, and Staff held some pop-up meetings at existing
transit stops, and then a public open house was held at the Library in March to obtain comments from the
community.

BRT
The Bus Rapid Transit is to travel along 25th Street between Washington and Harrison with four transit stops within
the East Central Community. The BRT will provide more travel options, reduce traffic and promote health by
providing cleaner air and make the community more walkable. He stated comments relative to uses along the
transit line include allowing mixed uses, live/work options, with consideration of providing benches and secure
bicycle parking as well as lighting and art. Residents were concerned with its usability, that they can get to where
they want to go in a timely manner. In terms of land use, consideration is to allow small cottage homes on small
lots (minimum 1,500 square ft.) within a two block radius of each of the transit stops. Addressing the option of
live/work within the community, Staff is considering allowing up to 5 employees within one block from each stop.
Commuters also suggested sidewalk vendors would be desirable within the community, and Staff is suggesting
they be allowed at Monroe and 25th Street.

9-Rails Creative District
The Nine-Rails Creative District extends into the East Central Community from the CBD, with an effort to provide an
epicenter for the creative community. The 9-Rails Plan identifies streetscape improvements, activities and the
allowance of mixed uses in an effort to support the creative community. Staff is suggesting the section of the
community identified as 9-Rail be rezoned from R-3EC to R-9 with ordinance amendments being considered which
would identify specific uses such as galleries, offices and other art-related uses. As a trade-off for increased uses,
Staff also is suggesting a requirement that as development occurs public art be included at the expense of the
development.

Accessory Dwelling Units (ADU)
The City had adopted the option to allow accessory dwelling units throughout Ogden several years ago, but as
language in the East Central Plan prohibited them in the R-1 areas, they have been restricted. An accessory
dwelling unit is an owner-occupied single-family home which is allowed to rent either a portion of the main building
(basement apartments) or a separate structure to a second family, limited to two adults and any minor children. He
stated housing demographics have changed and many homes have now become multi-generational, and as
families evolve living space becomes empty. Accessory units allow older residents to remain in their homes and
have assistance with their home upkeep as well as allowing new home buyers to provide adequate income so they
can become homeowners. He stated some concern has been identified relating to the ability to create units above
garage space due to the limited height and the requirement that the pitch of the roof be matched. Staff is
suggesting the ratio of roof pitch be reduced so that if an existing home has a 6:12 pitch, a garage or accessory
structure may have a 4:12 pitch.

He stated the first consideration of the Commission is to determine whether it is appropriate to amend the East
Central Plan to include these items; then to determine whether regulations should be amended, and then whether
areas should be rezoned as recommended by Staff. He suggested as each of these items are interrelated,\ but
each is a public hearing, the Commission take comments at one time and then vote on first the Plan Amendment,
then the Ordinance regulations, and finally the rezonings.

11. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment to consider amendment to 15-13-33.C. to allow metal siding for
   accessory structures when over 400 square feet and on less than ½ acre. (Attachment 11)

   Mr. Mike Miller, 169 W. Downs Drive, stated he desires to construct a new garage with a metal exterior as a wood
   garage needs to be repainted every few years while the enamel paint on metal typically requires no upkeep. He
   stated this is an older neighborhood but has no continuity of building style, and there is a mixture of building
   materials, particularly for accessory buildings. He stated wind blows off vinyl siding and the proposed metal is
   more durable and cost effective. He also understands a firewall will be required.

   Mr. Montgomery stated this request is to allow metal as a finish material for accessory buildings over 400 square
   feet. He reviewed the existing standards as well as revisions to these over time. He stated current standards now
   limit the size of accessory buildings to 80% of the size of the home, which prohibits large metal buildings in
   neighborhoods. He reviewed types of metal, indicating with new technology, there is little visual difference between
   architectural metal and prefabricated metal. He stated in order to consider the amendment, the Commission
   should determine whether the provisions of the General Plan to strengthen neighborhoods through property design
   are being complied with. If it is felt the size and scale is a better determining factor to the neighborhood than is
   building material, the amendment would be appropriate. Based on that finding, the Commission action could be to:
   1. Make no changes to the code;
   2. Amend the code to consider metal on a case-by-case basis to review the individual context of the structure; or
   3. Set standards to require minimum design standards.

   He presented particular amendment language as presented options, stating in any case there should be some
   standards for metal structures, including requirement of an eave overhang, lines be horizontal and gable end
   variations, as well as a minimum of a 5’ setback from any property line. Option A would require the Planning
   Commission to review applications for metal buildings and determine whether the proposal fits with the context of
   the particular neighborhood subject to the standards being satisfied. Option B would adopt particular standards for
   metal accessory buildings which would then be a permitted use in all residential areas. Staff recommendation is
   that standards be adopted and each request be reviewed on a case-by-case basis to assure the proposed building
   fits with the context of the area.

   Commissioner Safsten noted some traditional wood outbuildings become dilapidated over time and metal might
   retain a better appearance. Commissioner Southwick agreed the architectural style is as important as the building
   material, stating the requirement for eave overhang and roof pitch are important standards to be included,

12. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment for revision of multi-family development standards in 15-13-27
   and 15-38. (Attachment 12)

   Mr. Montgomery explained while the City had adopted new standards for multi-family uses in commercial zones,
   these were not considered for residential zones at that time. He stated as the demand for multi-family structures
   continues Staff feels some of these standards should also be applied to residential zones. This also is an effort to
   provide consistency between different zones. He reviewed current standards, indicating the particular residential
   zone dictate the number of units as well as its height. Building types are also factors into how construction can
   occur, particularly in having main access doors facing the street. He stated building material standards were
   adopted or multi-family residential in commercial zones but also should be applicable to projects in residential
   zones. Again, the building type would regulate the number of building materials allowed. The ordinance also would
   clarify that while different finishes are available on fiber cement finishes, only one can count as a building material.
   The Ordinance also would require windows be symmetrical on multi-story buildings. The mass of buildings also is
   addressed, and entryway features defined to fit the architecture of the building., It also would prohibit open-air
   corridor entries and require and require a main door facing the street for multi-family structures and require
   amenities for projects housing more than 16 units. Increased setbacks are also proposed for projects in C-3 zones
   as many of these are not in walkable neighborhoods.

As there were no public comments a motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to close the public hearing. Motion
   was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously.
