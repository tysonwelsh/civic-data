                               SPECIAL CITY COUNCIL
                              WORK MEETING MINUTES
                                  Wednesday, September 30, 2020
                                   Approved November 18, 2020

The following are the minutes of the City Council Electronic Meeting of the Herriman City Council. The
meeting was held on Wednesday, September 30, 2020 at 8:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Hall Council
Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required
by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and
interested citizens.

Presiding: Mayor David Watts

Council Members Present: Jared Henderson, Sherrie Ohrn, Steve Shields, and Clint Smith.

Staff Present: City Manager Brett Wood, Assistant City Manager Tami Moody, Police Chief Troy Carr, City
Recorder Jackie Nostrom, City Attorney Chase Andrizzi, Communications Manager Jonathan LaFollette, and
Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas.

8:00 PM (or as soon as possible thereafter) - SPECIAL MEETING:
This meeting will also be conducted electronically at www.herriman.org/agendas-and-minutes

Mayor David Watts called the meeting to order at 9:38 p.m.

1. Administrative Reports
     1.1. Discussion of the Creek Ridge, Mountain Ridge and Automall Developments - Chase Andrizzi,
          City Attorney and Brett Wood, City Manager
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi offered an extensive timeline and summary of the Automall project commencing
in 2015 to present. He highlighted the Butterfield Property Purchase discrepancy due to an inaccuracy in the
legal description in which the closing documents had been based upon. To close on the property and satisfy the
discrepancy, the City paid $131,075.10 which had already been budgeted, and amended the reimbursement
agreement to include the Promissory Note which needed to be paid back. Councilmember Shields asked for
clarification on the role of the Promissory Note. Attorney Andrizzi explained that developer funds had been
used to complete this property transaction in the amount specified in the closing documents. When the City
September 30, 2020 – City Council Minutes                                           Page 2 of 6
received the survey documentation which outlined the legal description error, the developer was unable to pay
the additional amount. To help bring the Automall to fruition, the City paid the remainder. Attorney Andrizzi
acknowledged there were several options available to secure repayment. The property was purchased and titled
to Herriman City utilizing the funds from a developer. Councilmember Shields indicated this approach seemed
appropriate. Councilmember Smith reiterated the City contributed funds were previously approved in the budget
for the purchase of property. Attorney Andrizzi confirmed and noted the repayment of the debt has an
outstanding balance of approximately $29,000. The developer has received an invoice for the balance due.

Attorney Andrizzi presented a map outlining the requirements of how far a competing automall must be. He
touched on the Tax Increment Fund, Public Infrastructure District Policy, the Participation Agreement, and
rezones approved within this project area.

Councilmember Shields asked if the borrower on the $131,075 Promissory Note was the same entity as the
extension of an agreement granted May 13th for $200,000. Attorney Andrizzi indicated the entity which received
an extension is Herriman 73 Partners, LLC and the Promissory Note was with Riverside Development.

Councilmember Shields relayed his concern of the specificity offered to the Council of who the legal
representatives of the developers and partners for projects. He explained of a situation where the City had been
working with a developer who was believed to be representing the property owners; however, the property
owners stated after the agreements had been reached, the developer did not have the ability to legally bind the
property owners in any type of agreement. He continued with the lack of specificity had created some concerning
situations. Attorney Andrizzi questioned if this scenario was a direct concern relating to the Automall project
to which Councilmember Shields answered in the affirmative due to the previously discussed agreements.
Attorney Andrizzi informed the Council the representative of Riverside Development was James Horsley, and
was also a partner associated the Herriman 73 Partners, LLC. Councilmember Shields relayed his concern of a
partner being eleven months delinquent on a loan and then was granted a year reprieve on another loan.

Attorney Andrizzi recognized the concern offered by the Council. Assistant City Manager Moody explained to
the Council the documents put together for the Council to review had links embedded into the document so
research and explanation would be easier. She encouraged each member of the Council to come into the office
to review the appropriate documentation and noted these could not be presented during an open meeting as this
would be a direction violation of the closed meeting discussions. Councilmember Shields acknowledged the
plethora of documents Attorney Andrizzi was going through, and stated it was more difficult for a member of
Council to review those documents especially when they weren’t in office when those discussions and decisions
had occurred. Councilmember Henderson asked if the links in the document were hand selected to specific
parts. Attorney Andrizzi explained the entire recording was embedded; however, timestamps were offered as a
courtesy.

At 9:32 p.m. Councilmember Ohrn moved temporarily recess the City Council work meeting to convene in a closed session to
discuss the character, professional competency, or physical or mental health of an individual and the purchase, exchange or lease of
real property, as provided by Utah Code Annotated §52-4-205. Councilmember Henderson seconded the motion.

Councilmember Smith asked for discussion on the motion offered. Mayor Watts granted the request.
Councilmember Smith relayed his confusion as to the reasoning behind the calling of a closed session. He
notated he was unaware if there was an outstanding property items which would warrant a closed session. City
Manager Brett Wood clarified the City does not have any open items which would warrant the calling of the
closed meeting. City Attorney Andrizzi concurred.

Councilmember Smith indicated he was unaware if there was a current personnel issues which needed to be
discussed as a Council. He expressed concern about holding a closed session over the last week which had
been notated in emails to the Council. He acknowledged the item had been agendized as outlined in the
Council’s procedures; however, was unable to get information from the requesting members of Council as to
what this conversation would entail. He recalled the previous City Council meeting where Councilmember
Shields had requested to know what items would be discussed in closed session prior to the meeting and relayed
his understanding the Council agreed to the request. Councilmember Smith reiterated his request to know prior
to voting to enter into a closed session, the nature of the topic which are to be discussed. He acknowledged
one member of the Council who was supportive of conducting the closed session reached out to relay their
position, to which Councilmember Smith relayed his appreciation of the dialogue. Councilmember Smith
opined the discussion described to him did not rise to the level warranting a closed session.

Councilmember Smith offered an excerpt of an email response from a requesting Councilmember indicating
‘I don’t know what anyone is waiting for, three Councilmembers have requested it’. He indicated he respected
the ability for how the item was placed on the agenda; however, requested to know the purpose of the
discussion. Referring back to the request of the Council knowing what items are to be discussed in closed
session prior to the meeting, Councilmember Smith questioned was it unanimously agreed upon last week to
have the Council be aware of what topics would be discussed, but not now. He did not believe there was any
topic which met the threshold of needing a closed session and could not be supportive.

Councilmember Henderson indicated the original request was for a special meeting to conduct a closed session
to discuss the competency of an individual, and two other Councilmembers expressed support of the item. He
noted several days had passed and nothing had happened. Councilmember Smith indicated several things did
occur during that specified time including legal counsel offering their opinion. Councilmember Henderson
continued with an excerpt from his email ‘I don’t know what anyone is waiting for. Three Councilmembers
(which I’m pretty sure is a majority) requested that a special meeting be scheduled following the joint Planning
Commission and City Council meeting on Wednesday, September 30, 2020. If a specific time needs to be
declared, I will suggest 7:00 p.m. As to the reason, I thought I was pretty clear in my desire to discuss the
competency of an individual or individuals regarding accountability for the way SLR/Mountain Ridge was or
has been handled, Automall as well as other recent issues which now includes directly failing to carry out the

The motion failed with the lack of a super majority vote as required by Utah Code Annotated 52-4-204.

Councilmember Henderson asked what happened to only needing one or two members of Council to have an
item discussed. Attorney Andrizzi explained the two members of the Council are needed to place an item on
the Agenda; however, under Utah Code Annotated 52-4-204 it indicates the Council is required to have a 2/3
majority vote to enter into closed session.

Councilmember Shields indicated he would like to continue the discussion in open meeting. Councilmember
Ohrn agreed, unless other members of the Council would like to protect the names of the individuals being
discussed. Councilmember Smith expressed concern of the intimidation being offered. Mayor Watts interjected
this item was not noticed on the agenda to be discussed in open meeting and would not be conducted. He added
he had not been briefed on this matter by any member of the Council, and acknowledged he had not reached
out to anyone either.

Councilmember Shields relayed his frustration of the purpose of having this meeting was to have a discussion
which could not occur in open meeting, and to not enter into the closed meeting was absurd. The meeting was
to discuss the professional competence of an individual. He opined for Council should not go into the closed
meeting and talk about the individual was illogical. He said the Council has entered into closed sessions without
a clue of what items would be discussed.
