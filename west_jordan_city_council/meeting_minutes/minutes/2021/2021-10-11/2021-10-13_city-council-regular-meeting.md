A.     Amendments to Redistricting Code 1-15-2 (D)(2)
Council Office Director Alan R. Anderson reviewed West Jordan Code 1-15-2(D)(2) regarding
redistricting with the Council. He suggested replacing the opening phrase “In 2021, 2023, and in
each succeeding fourth year thereafter, or” with “Within six months after the Legislature completes
its decennial redistricting process, or”. Responding to a question from Vice Chair Green, staff said
they would look into whether language related to Salt Lake County should be changed.

Chair Jacob asked if the Council wanted to have input in district mapping. Council Member
McConnehey said he felt the Council should rely on the Recorder’s judgement and stay away from
the process. The Council discussed current district boundaries and the number of district and at-
large Council positions.

Council Member Pack stated the philosophy behind electing representatives by district was that
citizens would elect representatives who lived in their general vicinity and shared in the same
geographic concerns. He expressed the opinion that broadly lengthening the district boundaries
would result in a “watered down” effect.

Council Member Whitelock said she did not want the City to become east and west quadrants. She
pointed out that she may not necessarily agree with someone just because they lived near her.
Council Member McConnehey said he was interested in staff exploring different options and making
recommendations.

Mr. Anderson said staff would bring back a revised Code 1-15-2(D)(2) before the end of the year.
The Council discussed voting by district versus voting at-large. Vice Chair Green said he would want
districts to be aligned with major geographic points as much as possible.

B.     Modification of Land Use Table for PF Zone
At the August 25, 2021 Council meeting, responding to a question from Council Member Pack, a
majority of the Council expressed willingness to discuss the possibility of rezoning Veterans Park
to allow farm animals for a petting zoo.

Council Member Pack explained that the petting zoo currently located at Gardner Village under the
direction of Farmer Gill would be leaving Gardner Village. He said he believed the petting zoo was a
benefit to the community, and he would not want the City to lose the 501(c)(3). He expressed the
opinion that the petting zoo with basic farm animals and feeding opportunities fit with the historic
nature of the community and suggested it should be part of the City Center. Council Member Pack
suggested land at Veterans Park near the West Jordan Historical Museum as a possible location.

Council Member Whitelock asked about City liability, the type of structures in which animals would
be housed, and how animals would be protected at night. Council Member Pack suggested Council
Members visit Gardner Village to see how the petting zoo was set up. Council Member Whitelock
said she was overall in favor but needed answers to questions.

Council Member McConnehey asked how the animals would be affected by public events and
activities like fireworks and asked if the City would pay for the housing.

MOTION:       Council Member Pack moved to suspend the rules to allow Farmer Gill to answer
              questions. Council Member McConnehey seconded the motion, which passed by
              unanimous vote.

Farmer Gill explained that grants were available from Salt Lake County that would provide funding
for nicer facilities, and said he believed the attraction would be a draw for the City. He spoke of the
possibility of a general store and a fun western aesthetic. Farmer Gill said he only had the minimum
space for a petting zoo at Gardner Village and expressed a desire to be able to provide space for the
animals to roam and stretch their legs. He described security efforts on-site around the clock.
Farmer Gill said he had wanted to be able to visit the Historical Museum for quite some time, but it
had not been open. He expressed willingness to fund a tour guide at the Museum so it could be open
during business hours.

A representative of the West Jordan Historical Museum said the Museum had been open for 22
years, except during COVID-19. She explained the Museum was a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization
with volunteer tour guides. She stated the Museum would probably not open to the public until next
Spring on the advice of the Health Department. The Museum representative pointed out that the
sidewalk around the space in question was used every year by the Museum during the Pumpkin
Walk event, and for other events with permission of the City. Another representative of the Museum
commented that parking in that area, especially during baseball season, was an issue.

Council Member Lamb brought up the fact that the planned art center could not be at the subject
location because of an existing easement. He suggested the same conflict would exist for a petting
zoo. The Council discussed the location of the easement.

Assistant City Attorney Duncan Murray spoke of the many policies for use of public land that would
need to be followed. He stated many issues would need to be analyzed before the Council could
make a fully informed decision. Deputy Public Works Director Isaac Astill mentioned potential
odors and rodents. He added that the subject location was a popular digital online game area.

Senior Planner Ray McCandless presented a Preliminary Development Plan and Development
Agreement for a proposed Pura Vita Living Senior Housing development at 1992 West 9400 South.
The property was zoned R-3-20 – Senior Housing Overlay (SHO) in 2013; no rezone was requested
with the current application. The proposed development would include 23 dwelling units on 2.98
acres, with a total of six single-story buildings with basements. Occupants would be 55 years and
older (SHO), with the age requirement administered through the HOA. Mr. McCandless showed

the Planning Commission reviewed the application and recommended approval of the request as
submitted.
Responding to a question from Vice Chair Green, Mr. McCandless explained the application was
presented to the Council because it was within the Senior Housing Overlay. Tina Franco,

 7.    BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Annexation petition from Third Cadence, LLC and G & N Wood Properties, LLC
City Recorder Tangee Sloan reviewed with the Council the process involved with an annexation
petition. Assistant City Attorney Duncan Murray showed on a map the 170 acres for potential
annexation and inclusion in the Wood Ranch Development. He explained that an island or peninsula
of unincorporated land was not desired under current annexation law, and pointed out that it was
generally understood if West Jordan approved the annexation of 170 acres, the City would be
committing to approving annexation of the remaining island of unincorporated land in the long-
term. Mr. Murray emphasized that acceptance of the petition however would not mandate approval
of annexation in the future.

Council Member Lamb asked why the City was not considering annexation of the portion of
unincorporated land west of the proposed annexation area. Mr. Murray responded it would be
problematic for additional property owners to be involved with the already complicated alignment
of annexation and current development agreements. City Administrator Korban Lee added that the
owner of unincorporated property west of the proposed annexation area was currently in the
middle of a development process through Salt Lake County.

Vice Chair Green pointed out a discrepancy between acreage listed on the petition and the proposed
ordinance.

MOTION:       Council Member McConnehey moved to approve Resolution No. 21-046 initially
