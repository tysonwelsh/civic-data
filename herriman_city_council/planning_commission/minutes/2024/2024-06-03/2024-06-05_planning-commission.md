Planning Director Maloy briefed during the joint meeting the amendments to Chapter 10-10 were
talked about. There are a few high-level questions on the project staff is asking the commission
where to go with it. Commissioner Rypien encouraged being cognizant to get small houses on
small lots. lf they are giving up square footage what are the builders compensating factors to get
a presentable option. Planning Director Maloy expressed his concern is if there are no design
guidelines, what the end product will be. He wants obtainable housing that is desirable.
Commissioner Rypien presented the question how you get a developer to build small lots, small
homes instead of the town homes. Commissioner Fenn pressed we don’t have anything for a
2,000 square foot house with design codes incorporated. Planning Director Maloy relayed the
state has said the most you can require is 1,000 square foot and you can’t require a standard
unless they ask for a modification from the city. lf a modification is requested, then we can
require standards. He wants to layer in more restrictions where the state has not limited the city
to have restrictions. In the draft RA-1 we have added a single dive approach. Also, we have
added minimum and maximum setbacks. Staff want to keep it tight enough to make them want
to enter a discussion about negotiations. We can require a dry utility plan to learn where all the
utility boxes are being placed. Some of our partners have learned if they work with utilities up
front, they can control where the boxes pop in.
Commissioner Oberg probed if you could create two options, one that is a standard option and
the other would allow them to choose from options and allow design standards. Planning
Director Maloy informed him he has had similar tools in other cities. His experience if you’re not
giving a bonus or density they won’t do it. Here Planned Unit Developments (PUDs) are overlays
that go to the City Council for approval. lt involves another body of approval and more time
involved. We are trying to keep conversation more fluid with site plan reviews. Commissioner
Fenn advised asking the developers to give their advice in this. Chair Powell expressed looking at
minimum and maximum setbacks along with driveway width and length restrictions, he
considers these would encourage developers to start conversations into negotiations. Planning
Director Maloy voiced he doesn’t recommend R-15 or R-16 with typical setbacks he thinks the
marketplace would very easily abuse them. He urged them to write the code so tightly that the
builder must build a better product or start negotiations. Planning Director Maloy disclosed one
option is to allow a net and gross density based on minimum lot size. Gross density calculations
could allow for lot averaging as an incentive. We allow gross density in Master Development
Agreements (MDA’s) all the time. Chair Powell advised if they went to this they wouldn’t need to
go to council for approval.
