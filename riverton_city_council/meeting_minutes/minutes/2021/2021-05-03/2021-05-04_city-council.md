       Budget Discussion – Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director

Mayor Staggs said that all Councilmembers should have received a copy of his budget and that
the budget discussion was the only item on the work session agenda.

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained how the budget process works. He
said that each department submits their budget first and they insert the numbers into the
budget. He said they do not cut anything at that point. He said that after this, he and David
Brickey meet with each department head and talk about their budget needs and talk about
balancing the budget. He said that he sent an email to the Councilmembers highlighting the
difference in the Mayor’s budget versus the City Manager’s budget. He said that they should
remember the long range plan for the City. He said the strategic planning meeting was very
helpful but the budget is still a work in progress.

He explained that the electronic budget was sent to each Councilmember. He said that every
column is required by law to produce, for example, audit, current year budget, current year to
date, projected finish. He said the actual fiscal 2022 budget includes the City Manager’s column

Presentations/Recognitions

         Mayor’s Budget Presentation

Mayor Staggs said that he had served on the Council for four years and as mayor for 3 years
and that he appreciates the staff and all of the time they have put into this budget. He said that
there have been a lot of challenges in the last year because of the pandemic. He said the
Council directed that staff have some rate studies done across the various enterprise funds.
He said that HDR was one of the firms conducting the rate studies and they recommended
doubling the culinary and secondary rates over the next five years. He said this was a difficult
budget to try to put together. He said that in spite of the challenges, the City has
demonstrated a lot of resilience and been able to maintain AAA credit rating. He said that
sales tax revenues have doubled in the last couple of years. He said that eight years ago, the
sales tax revenue was $4.5 million and today is $9.2 million.

Mayor Staggs said that the City offered retirements for staff and were able to restructure
departments which resulted in a savings of 6.5 employees. He said that Riverton has 3.49
employees per 1000 residents. He said that Riverton is the lowest of surrounding cities and
believes that the City can do more with fewer employees.

Mayor Staggs said that given the savings in the general fund, there is an opportunity to move
Stormwater operations into the general fund. He said for residents this means that instead of
a $6 ERU charge, they will be charged $1 for Stormwater. He said that the $5 savings will be
moved to sanitation and water funds.

Mayor Staggs said that the America Rescue Plan will give $350 billion to state and local
governments. He said that Riverton will receive about $5 million which can be spent for
COVID expenses along with broadband, water and sewer infrastructure investments. He said
that he has been a big proponent of improving broadband. He said the City could spend about
$1 million to build the backbone infrastructure for broadband and this would save the City
about $100,000 in annual expenditures. He said this backbone infrastructure would improve
connectivity to City assets and provide the opportunity for broadband throughout the City.

Mayor Staggs said that House Bill 244 will give Riverton $700,000 every year for 15 years for
roadway infrastructure. He said the Riverton Economic Development Infrastructure Fund will
have $6 million at year end even after paying off $4 million in water bonds which will help
reduce the costs on the secondary water fund. He said he would like to see the $5 savings
from Stormwater fees applied $1 to culinary and $4 to sanitation. He said that the average
annual utility fees per household is $756 which is below any surrounding cities.

Mayor Staggs said that the secondary water fund has fallen short because of stalled
development and new legislation in SB199 which requires all secondary water lines to be
metered. He said he has proposed a $2.50 increase to each residential connection for
secondary water which is lower than staff recommended. He said that ARP funds can be
