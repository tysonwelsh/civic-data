than normal.

     Daria Evans, living in The Villas subdivision, expressed concern that minutes from a previous
meeting had mischaracterized her statements and wanted the record to reflect that her request that
the council reconsider their decision to change the form of government was because she felt the city
did not have the money to afford another councilmember at that time.

     Karen Cornelious, living in The Villas subdivision, expressed her disappointment regarding an
email exchange with City Manager Eric Ellis, where she was advised that the matters she wanted to
discuss would be best handled during city council meetings and not in-person meetings. She stated
that the two minutes given for public comment was insufficient to address the issues she had.

     Russell Evans, living in The Villas subdivision, was concerned that questions asked during
public comment were not being answered in real time and that it hindered discussion because
follow-up questions could not be asked.

     Mr. Ellis offered clarification on the email that was mentioned by Mrs. Cornelious.

    Councilmember Holdaway asked for legal clarification from City Attorney Jayme Blakesley on
whether Mr. Ellis should be able to meet with the citizens’ group he formed to address budget
concerns. There was also a legal discussion over Councilmember Holdaway’s ability or authority to
“deputize” citizens.


5.  MAYOR AND COUNCILMEMBERS' REPORTS/DISCLOSURES/RECUSALS
    Councilmember Cameron shared her support and appreciation for Finance Director Kristie
Bayles, as well as the overall transparency and answers provided by staff.

    Councilmember Sifuentes gave an update on development meetings she had with Flagborough
regarding the Huntsman Cancer Institute. There was additional discussion on a future drive thru
pharmacy and the zoning code that would need to be amended to allow for that. She also gave an
update on a Truth in Taxation meeting that had occurred the night before with Alpine School
District, but that she would need more time to study it to present a more detailed report on that
meeting.

     Councilmember Rasmussen gave an update on the Bike Commission as well as the library and
their progress in putting together their fall programs. She also encouraged residents to visit the
community garden.

    Councilmember Holdaway expressed his gratitude to Councilmember Sifuentes for her
invocation and stated that he too hoped the meeting would be productive.

9.  BUSINESS ITEMS
   9.1. Public Hearing - Truth in Taxation
   Motion: COUNCILMEMBER RASMUSSEN MOVED TO ENTER A PUBLIC HEARING
AT 6:00 PM. COUNCILMEMBER SIFUENTES SECONDED THE MOTION. MAYOR
FULLMER, COUNCILMEMBERS CAMERON, SIFUENTES, RASMUSSEN AND
HOLDAWAY VOTED YES. THE MOTION CARRIED UNANIMOUSLY.

     Finance Director Kristie Bayles gave a presentation on the budget and the tax rate.

    Mr. Cornelius stated that he felt there had been several misguided decisions made by the
council. Specifically, he felt that the city hall was not needed at this time and that the money set
aside for it could be used instead of a tax increase.

    Brent Gray, living in The Villas subdivision, expressed concerns that any tax increase would
negatively impact those on a fixed income.

    Terry Ewing, living in The Villas subdivision, expressed concerns over what she viewed as a
lack of transparency from the city and overspending on planning.

     Ms. Evans explained that she was not a member of any political group. She presented 137
signatures from residents in her area that were against the tax increase. She also expressed concerns
that an increase would create a burden for those on a fixed income.

    Justin Jones, living in The Meadows subdivision, also expressed concerns about the impact on
those living with fixed incomes.

     Sonia McGrath, living in the Le Cheminant subdivision, thanked the city council and city staff
for their hard work. She stated that has had very positive interactions and that she found the staff to
be very knowledgeable and open.
 Page 3 of 6; August 14, 2024, City Council Minutes
    Ms. Cornelius expressed concern about past planning for city growth and asked about future
plans specifically impact fees for public safety.

     Mr. Evans also wanted to express his concern over the impact on widows and those on fixed
incomes and that he believed there was more that could be cut from the budget before considering a
tax increase.

    Natalie Harbin, living in the James Bay subdivision, stated that she was aware that increased
taxes would be challenging for those on a fixed income and that she hoped those residents would be
able to take advantage of state programs. She also thanked the council and staff for working hard to
plan for future growth.

    David Lauret, living on Holdaway Road, spoke against the increase and asked the council to
continue to find ways to cut the spending, he recommended shifting funds from the planning of a
new city hall.

    Keith Holdaway, living on Holdaway Road, felt there was unaccounted for spending by the city
and a general lack of transparency.

    Randy Gray, living in The Villas subdivision, expressed concerns over the impact of an
increase tax on elderly and disabled residents and those on fixed incomes.

    Tim Blackburn, living in the Sleepy Ridge subdivision, expressed concerns over travel costs
and requested reports detailing the benefits of those trips. He asked why the city had a lobbyist and
what had been the benefit of that position.

    Caden Rhoton, living in The Cottonwoods subdivision, encouraged the council to follow
through with building the fire station and to continue to work hard for increased growth and focus
on economic development.

     Jordan Christensen, living in The Preserve subdivision, expressed concerns regarding building
restrictions placed on townhomes, specifically basements and parking structures. He stated that he
would be comfortable paying more in taxes if he could further develop his land.

      Ms. Cornelius wanted to clarify that no one was against funding for public safety but that she
felt there could be additional cuts made before voting on a budget.

    Cory Teuscher, living in The Villas subdivision, advised the council to be cautious with
increases.

   Motion: COUNCILMEMBER RASMUSSEN MOVED TO CLOSE THE PUBLIC
HEARING AT 6:54 PM. COUNCILMEBER CAMERON SECONDED THE MOTION. MAYOR
FULLMER, COUNCILMEMBERS CAMERON, SIFUENTES, RASMUSSEN, AND
HOLDAWAY VOTED YES. THE MOTION CARRIED UNANIMOUSLY.

    Councilmember Rasmussen asked if meetings similar in style to public hearings, regarding
taxes could be made more routine. Additionally, she expressed her support for the increase.

    Mayor Fullmer asked Finance Director Kristie Bayles to give a short explanation of the term
“revenue neutral.” A discussion ensued.

     Councilmember Holdaway gave a presentation on what he felt was the impact of the Vineyard
Redevelopment Agency (RDA) on current taxes being collected. A discussion ensued.
Councilmember Sifuentes wanted the record to show that her property value had dropped and asked
for clarification on the point of the presentation. Councilmember Holdaway felt some of what was
presented by staff was deceiving or misleading. There was a discussion about projects, planning,
and impact fees.

    Councilmember Rasmussen commented on statements made by Councilmember Holdaway
during his presentation and stated she felt it was inappropriate for councilmembers to name
individual residents and to imply they have a full understanding of that resident’s viewpoints.

    Councilmember Holdway concluded his presentation by thanking members of the public that
helped advise him throughout the budget process. He also asked that RDA funds be used to make
up the shortfall, instead of a tax increase.

    Councilmember Holdaway initiated a discussion about the leasing of vehicles for city use.

     Councilmember Sifuentes gave a breakdown of her work during the creation of the budget and
her thoughts on the process.

    Councilmember Holdaway initiated a discussion about the benefits of a lobbyist for the city.

    Mayor Fullmer followed up on what was done during the legislative session and with the
lobbyist. She also offered clarification on the RDA, the budget process as a whole, and taxes.
Additionally, she spoke on the process of approving projects and planning in general.
