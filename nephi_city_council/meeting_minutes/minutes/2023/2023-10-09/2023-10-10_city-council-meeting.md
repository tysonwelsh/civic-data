1.    Colin Gibbs, a representative from Smart Rain, talked to the council about a sprinkler
     controller system his company offers. Parks and Recreation Director Craig Oswald and
     City Arborist Gerratt Bethers answered questions from the council about the need for a
     smart sprinkler system. Mr. Oswald informed the council that the cost estimate of a mini
     excavator came in at $94,000. He said that if we purchased the new sprinkler control
     system the problem with the ruts at the cemetery would be less because it would be
     easier to control the moisture of the ground. The smart controllers would be much more
     cost effective than purchasing a mini excavator that the council had wanted to purchase
     to alleviate the problem of ruts caused by wet soil and heavy equipment used to dig
     graves. The council directed staff to get a cost estimate from Smart Rain for the
     controller system.

2. City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council that Nephi City uses the
   International Building Code (IBC) to deal with many safety issues on residential,
   commercial, and industrial developments. There are some issues that are not included
   in the IBC that some cities include in their land use code. Examples of some of these
   things are building on steep slopes, radon mitigation, requiring engineering on retaining
   walls between properties, stormwater mitigation, and geologic and soil mitigation. He
   stated that the City Councils must make the determination on how involved the
   government should be on these issues or if it will be left to individual home and business
   owners to appropriately address them.

     One of these issues that is currently affecting several homeowners in Nephi is
     collapsible soils. Currently, the city requires developments to complete a geotechnical
     report on the whole site of their development. The report wouldl include multiple test
     holes but generally not a test hole on each lot of the subdivision. If collapsible soils are
     found, the typical recommendations from the geotechnical report require over-excavation
     of the lot with fill material brought back in and compacted. Some homeowners also pour
     wider footings as a precaution. Builders often attach pipes to each gutter outlet on the
     home that will help drain stormwater away from the home. Despite these efforts,
     sometimes homes still experience settling issues. The cost to repair a settling home can
     be quite expensive.

     Recently the Council has heard from a concerned resident who would like more to be
     done by the government to prevent homeowners from experiencing settling in their
     homes. Actions by the City Council could include:
        ● Maintain the current code
        ● Provide a warning with each building permit to indicate that collapsible soils are
           an issue in Nephi and that it is recommended to get a geotechnical report with
           your individual lot and to follow the recommendations from that report. If this
           warning is included, the Council may want to explore including other warnings as
            well (e.g. radon mitigation, retaining soils from neighboring lots with higher
            elevations using engineered retaining walls, etc.)
        ●   Require each lot in a subdivision to obtain a geotechnical report and encourage
            the homeowner or business owner to follow the recommendations in that report.
            A geotechnical report may cost an additional $800-$900 to obtain. This would be
            required of each building lot, regardless of whether or not they were found to be
            in an area suspected of having collapsible soils.
        ●   Require each lot in a subdivision to obtain a geotechnical report and require the
            owner of the lot to follow the recommendations. Estimated costs to the
            homeowner would be the cost of a geotechnical report, mitigation efforts that
            may be anywhere from $2,000 to $10,000 or more. Additional inspection fees
            would also apply and the city would need to ensure that it had the appropriate
            amount of inspection services to add this for each building lot in the city. The
            cost of a geotechnical report would apply to each lot regardless of whether or not
            the lot was in an area suspected of having collapsible soils. The other costs
            would apply if recommendations from the geotechnical report would require other
            mitigation efforts.

     After some discussion the council thought it would be best to require a geo technical
     report on every lot and encourage the builder to follow the recommendations of the
     geotech report.
