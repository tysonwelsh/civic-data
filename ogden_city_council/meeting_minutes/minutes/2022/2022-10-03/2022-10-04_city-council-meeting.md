Minutes of Regular Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah, October 4, 2022

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 539 22™ Street
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the City’s Planning Division has proposed the rezone of the southern
portion of property located at 539 22" Street from the current Central Business District/Conditional Overlay (CBD/CO) zoning
classification to the Residential Multi-family East Central (R-3EC) zoning classification. The property is being rezoned to allow
the owner to comply with the requirements of the R-3EC zoning with regard to the renovation of the four-plex on the property.
The portion being rezoned was previously owned by Midtown Clinic and zoned CBD/CO to comply with their development plan;
however, those plans were not completed, and the property was lost in a tax sale. The proposed rezone would allow consistent
zoning on the subject property.

Deputy Planning Manager Simpson summarized the memo and stated the Planning Commission reviewed the proposal at
its July 6, 2022, meeting and forwarded a recommendation of approval. The Planning Commission’s recommendation was made
with the finding that the proposed rezone will provide a definite edge to development and buffering between types of uses, will
reflect the prevailing character of the overall district, will not cut across lots or developments, and is supported in the East Central
Community Plan.

Acting Chair Lopez introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2022-43, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City of Ogden City, Utah, amending the zoning map of Ogden City, as adopted by
Section 15-3-3- of the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as multiple-family residential zone — east central
(R-3EC) property heretofore classified as both multiple family residential zone — east central and central
business district/conditional overlay zone (R-3EC and CBD/CO), generally located at 539 22" Street, and
providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”

A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Chief Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder
have at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.

Acting Chair Lopez then called for public input regarding the proposed ordinance.

No persons came forward to be heard regarding this matter.

ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA,
ORDINANCE WAS PASSED AND ADOPTED AS OGDEN CITY ORDINANCE 2022-43 AND ORDERED POSTED AS
REQUIRED BY LAW UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE — COUNCIL MEMBERS
BLAIR, CHOBERKA, HYER, RICHEY, WHITE, AND VICE CHAIR LOPEZ. VOTING NO-— NONE. Chair Nadolski
was not present when this vote was taken.

The Chair signed the ordinance as passed and adopted and the Chair’s signature was attested by the City Recorder. The
authenticated ordinance was then filed with the City Recorder for transmittal to the Mayor for review and consideration.

Public Comments

Steven Van Wagoner stated he is present this evening to ask if any Council member, Administration, or City staff is
aware of any intent to develop properties located at the Grandview Elementary, Lynn Elementary, or North Jackson Elementary
sites that have been designated as surplus parcels by Ogden School District. On behalf of the community’s youngest children, he
implored the City to take immediate action to secure the properties in accordance with State Code; if the City acquires the land, it
cannot be used for any other purposes other than a town hall, park and open space, cultural center, or community center. The
‘clock is ticking’ for the City to purchase the land as it received the notice of the opportunity to purchase the land on September
15, 2022; the community has less than 72 days to provide written intent to Ogden School District before the property/open space
is lost forever to a developer and vertical structures. Last week during a meeting with Chief Administrative Officer Johnson,
Public Services Director Lowder, and Recreation Manager Bridge, they proclaimed that recreational fields are maxed out and the
City is out of usable/programmable space and that next year they may need to limit participation in youth sports programs in the
City. Outdoor activities and lifestyle are important to the community and the City has an opportunity to increase the amount of
open space that can be used for programming.

Sarge Morialli stated she has been talking with the City Engineer about some traffic safety concerns she has; there are
several streets in Ogden with solid lines that prevent a left turn, but many people are crossing those solid lines, which could result
in a traffic ticket if a police officer is present. She stated that the City needs to identify funding to correct these issues; there are
many areas where solid lines are not appropriate because it is necessary for traffic to make a left turn into a business. She added
that speeding is a problem in the City and last week a neighbor of hers was nearly killed while walking in the bike lane on the side
of the road. Something needs to be done to slow traffic down. Additionally, the City needs to hire more employees that can take
care of aging trees in the City cemetery before they fall and kill someone. These issues could result in a situation where the City is
sued. She identified other traffic safety issues she has noticed in the community and thanked the Council for their attention.

Kelly Crozier stated that he owns hangar 2808 at the Ogden Airport and he referenced commitments that have been made
to the community regarding the Ogden Airport that have never come to fruition. He noted the Airport Master Plan has a great deal
of data and charts that are inaccurate and could lead to improvements that are not sustainable. Such improvements will not be
supported by private carriers locating at the Airport and this will be a very negative situation for the residents of the community.
He stated Ogden’s answers to these problems has been to get rid of its Airport Advisory Committee, which had over one hundred
years of experience; old members have been replaced by new members who have no experience. He encouraged the Council to
reach out to other communities that have successful airports to see what they are doing differently from Ogden City.

David Willis thanked the Mayor, Council, and City Administration for the work they do for the community; he
recognized that elected officials are often criticized and rarely get recognition for the work they do. He stated when he came to
Ogden in 2006, the trajectory of the City was incredible and much of the great plans for the community have come to fruition
under Mayor Caldwell’s leadership and support from the City Council. Improvements were made much quicker than he thought
possible and in ways he never thought possible. He has lived in other large cities, such as Chicago, and has found their quality of
life unsustainable; crime has increased, and public safety officials were not supported in those communities. He thanked Ogden
City officials for recognizing the need to prioritize public safety in order to provide a great quality of life for the residents here.

Teresa Holmes stated she is present tonight on behalf of the Union Station museums; she does not want to see the
museums gutted and moved out of the building. Most of the people who arrive in Ogden via the Front Runner stop at the museums
at the Union Station. She has been volunteering in the Union Station for over 21 years and was forced to sign a non-disclosure
agreement about this issue, but she chose to speak. She has every right to speak, and she will make her voice loud when it comes
to saving the museums. She stated the volunteers have been treated horribly under new management and they are ruining the
facility; people come to Ogden from all different countries and states, but their access to the many great things at the Union
Station has been limited. The new management of the Station is misrepresenting the number of visitors who come to the facility,
and she encouraged the Council members to visit the Station on their own to gather their own information. She will be at the
meeting on October 19 to talk about saving the museums and many of her friends will be there with her. She has been watching
the dismantling of the Union Station for the last four years and she is very upset.

Angel Castillo stated that there will be a Dia de los Muertos festival this week at Ben Lomond High School; there will be
many activities at the festival, and she encouraged all to participate. She then noted that she has contacted the City’s Planning
Division about the negative impact that the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) construction has been having on businesses in the
community. She stated it is her understanding that UTA has funds to help support businesses, but the businesses she has spoken
with have not heard of those benefits. She suggested that Council members reach out to the businesses in their district to help
guide them in the right direction. She then congratulated the City’s housing authority on receiving a LIHTC project award; she
understands the LIHTC program is very complicated and suggested the City Council familiarize themselves with the program
because it can be a source of free money for vital projects in the City.

Heath Satow stated that the Union Stock Exchange Building was discussed during tonight’s work session meeting; he
likes the developer’s vision for the building, and he feels that it will provide a great boost for the surrounding area. He hopes the
project goes forward as shown, but he is still concerned about the developer selection process employed by the City’s
Redevelopment Agency; the developer of this project will be receiving very large perks funded by Ogden taxpayers and the City
will essentially be buying the same piece of land twice. He referenced another project in Ogden’s downtown; an acre of property
was given to a developer free of charge. There is a great deal of unknown information about the developer in that scenario and this
is why it is important to demand increased transparency in the developer selection process.

Mayor Comments

Chief Administrative Officer Johnson referenced Mr. Van Wagoner’s comments regarding surplus property owned by the
Ogden School District; they did declare three parcels as surplus, and the City has 90 days to execute its first right of refusal on the
properties. Administration is currently evaluating the matter at this time and once the evaluation is completed, they will meet with
the Council to discuss the issue. He then thanked Sarge for her comments and indicated that he will look into the issues that she
raised; he noted that many major streets in the City are controlled by the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) and the City
does not have the authority to change road striping or traffic configuration. However, the trees in the cemetery do need attention
and the City needs increased staff to respond to those matters. He then stated that he is unsure the source of the rumor regarding
the potential closure of museums at the Union Station; the City has no intention of closing any museums and the focus of the
October 19 meeting will actually be about opportunities to expand the museums. He has visited the Browning Gun museum two
times in recent months and is interested in expanding the displays and increasing the signage about the artifacts contained there.
He stated the museums are part of Ogden and he hopes they will always be. He stated there is one museum at the Station that is
controlled by the State of Utah, and it may be that Ms. Holmes was discussing that museum.

Council member Comments

Council member Choberka invited any resident to contact the Council via email to provide follow up information
regarding the topics that were raised tonight. She then addressed Ms. Castillo’s comments about the impact that UTA construction
is having on businesses; she has reached out to the businesses in her district and informed them of opportunities to secure coupons
for their customers. She stated that UTA has an outreach employee who was assigned to discuss these issues with all businesses,
and she is unsure why the businesses referenced by Ms. Castillo did not hear from that individual. Mr. Johnson stated that he does
not believe the coupons are working very well.

Acting Chair Lopez stated he would like for the Council to hear from Administration regarding their evaluation of the
Ogden School District surplus properties. Mr. Johnson stated that Administration may be able to provide a report in the next few
weeks.
