      2. Administrative Reports
          2.1. Discussion Regarding Cost Sharing of School Safety Shuttles at Oak Leaf
                Elementary – Bryce Terry, City Engineer
      Community Development Director Blake Thomas informed the Council this was a follow-up
      discussion for the Oak Leaf Elementary shuttle request proposed by Jordan School District.
      The current issue was the lack of sidewalk being installed to complete the safe walking route
      prior to the commencement of school. The delay was due to the City not being able to acquire
      access to the property in a timely manner to complete the road improvements for a large
      section of the project. A temporary safe walking route had been identified; however, it would
      be out of the way for students in the walking area.

      Jordan School District proposed to supply safety shuttles for hose students for the 2023-
      2024 school year but requested that Herriman City be responsible for half of the cost to
      operate the temporary shuttle service. The proposal included two buses being dedicated
      costing $734.22 daily. Director Thomas provided a letter dated December 10, 2019, to Jordan
      School District outlining the timeline for access and utilities to the site and requested a
      prepayment of impact fees. He highlighted the impact fee summary of fee waivers and
      deductions. It was noted Jordan School District paid just over $600,000. Councilmember
      Jared Henderson asked if it was standard practice to waive impact fees for the school district.
      Director Thomas shared the typical differences in communities when it would come to
      development, subdivision, and building permits would be approved and issued.
      Councilmember Steven Shields asked if waiving those fees was considered an administrative
      or legislative duty. City Manager Nathan Cherpeski clarified it was a legislative decision.

      Director Thomas continued with background information of an updated letter sent June 23,
      2020, providing the construction schedule and reimbursement agreement information with
      Ivory Homes and the request for prepayment of impact fees. Additionally, on January 10,
      2022, another letter was provided to Jodan School District providing dates of when utilities
August 9, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                                   Page 3 of 8


      were expected to be provided. The school district was again notified in June 2023 as to the
      delay of the project due to unforeseen circumstances. He noted the contractor was provided
      access on July 31, 2023 which resulted in a 16 week delay of the project. City Engineer Bryce
      Terry offered an update of the construction contract after the eminent domain proceedings
      were finalized. The contractor was asked to prioritize the installation of the sidewalk which
      was anticipated to be completed in December. The Council was given three options to
      consider:

          •   Option 1 – Do not participate with Jordan School District in the cost of school shuttles.
              It would be a possibility that the district may not provide the shuttles without the
              City’s financial participation. The result would be that students walking to school
              would need to walk the route as described in Exhibit 2, which was the only safe route
              to school based on current conditions.
          •   Option 2 – Participate in the cost of school shuttles at half of the cost. This would
              require a budget amendment to accommodate the cost. This option would ensure that
              safety shuttles continue for this school year (2023-24). 7300 West crosswalk crossing
              guard would be stationed at the location.
          •   Option 3 - Participate in the cost of school shuttles at a lower portion than half. This
              would still require a budget amendment to accommodate sharing this cost and has not
              been discussed with Jordan School District to determine if they would accept a lower
              portion from the City for the cost of the shuttles.

      City Engineer Terry highlighted how school crossings were audited. Police Chief Troy Carr
      relayed concerns for the safety of the students as well as the crossing guards stationed in
      the area.

      The Council discussed options and concluded a future conversation was necessary to discuss
      solutions. Council consensus determined to allocate $10,000 to allow the temporary shuttle
      service to operate through September and allow some time for further discussion.

          2.4. Discussion Regarding Police Structure, Guiding Policies, and Potential HCSEA
                Tax Increase – Nathan Cherpeski, City Manager
      City Manager Cherpeski recalled the notice for a Truth in Taxation hearing regarding the
      Herriman City Safety Enforcement Service Area proposed property tax rate and explained
      staffing for the police department was reviewed. The Herriman Police Department currently
      handles more calls per officer than all but three of the surrounding cities. He highlighted there
      were no significant changes in crime rate data over the past five years and calls for service
      have increased approximately 20-percent every year since incorporation. Herriman Police
      Department internally tracks several metrics to measure the call load of the officers.

      City Manager Cherpeski reviewed the 60/60/60 matrix guidelines which had at least 60
      percent of the total number of sworn officers interacting with the public. Secondly, the
      average workload for patrol staff should not exceed 60 percent. To allow for time to be
      available to proactively address community problems and be available for serious
      emergencies. Next, the total service time should not exceed a factor of 60. In essence, this
      would mean the total time per call for an officer should not exceed 60 minutes. Herriman,
      specifically, adds a fourth category of priority one calls response times of six minutes to
      address the unique geography.

      City Manager Cherpeski highlighted the five-year Strategic Plan being developed to identify
      key metric based on unit-specific needs. The plan identified targeted specifics such as total
      committed time in patrol, number of cases assigned to each detective, and number of hours
      spent on administrative and support tasks, with data-driven trigger points to identify when
      new allocations should be discussed or considered.

      City Manager Cherpeski continued with the internal pay range for officers which was based
      on an 11-step merited pay scale. He explained Herriman City tries to stay competitive within
      the market; however, not at the top. Steps within the scale were based on years of police
      experience. Officers within the step plan and not at the top would be those with fewer than
      11 years’ experience. City Manager Cherpeski recognized the lack of desire of individuals
      wanting to become police officers and the struggle in recruitment efforts. Councilmember
      Henderson acknowledged the labor shortage.

      City Manager Cherpeski wanted to better understand the specific needs in the police fleet as
      being a unique community, it had been determined the F-150 vehicles were appropriate for
      the Herriman Police Department operations. The Council discussed evaluating vehicles to
      extend the life of the vehicles. Deputy Police Chief Cody Stromberg agreed staff would
      continue to evaluate industry changes to ensure the highest and best use of equipment.

      City Manager Cherpeski offered an overview of the long-range financial plan for the
      Herriman City Safety Enforcement Area and noted the priority was to maintain the fleet
      replacement as needed to sustain the level of investment. The department also noted
      numerous calls were received each week which are mental health related and currently, the
      department has been utilizing officers in a way which they aren’t best suited. Due to this
      need, a Mental Health Specialist position was warranted. Currently, officers are required to
      cover demand. Based on the growth plan, this was likely a few years out. And lastly, the city
      needed a reasonable balance in the police fund to account for unexpected costs and to curtail
      inflationary demands.
