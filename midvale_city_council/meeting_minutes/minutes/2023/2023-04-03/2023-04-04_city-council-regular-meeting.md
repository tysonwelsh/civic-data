      A. BINGHAM JUNCTION STATION AREA PLAN PRESENTATION
Adam Olsen said House Bill 462 was passed during the 2022legislative session. The bill
requires municipalities with fixed rail guideways (commuter and light rail) to complete
Station Area Plans (SAP's) covering a half-mile radius of each fixed rail station. SAP's
are unique to their jurisdiction and setting; however, key aspects include strategies to
increase the availability of affordable housing, promotion of sustainable environmental
conditions, enhancement of access to employment opportunities and increase of
transportation choices and connections.
Midvale has three stations within its boundaries: (1) Bingham Junction, (2) Midvale Fort
Union, and (3) Midvale Center. Staff applied for and received Technical Assistance grant
funding for all three stations and their requisite SAP's. A joint application was submitted
with West Jordan for the Bingham Junction and Historic Gardner (West Jordan) stations.
As such, Midvale is among the first cities to undertake the station area planning as
required by HB 462.

On December 14, 2022. the Council passed Resolution No. 2O22-R-51 authorizing the
Mayor to execute an agreement with Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC) for the
Bingham Junction Station Area Plan (SAP). The SAP is underway and the consulting firm
leading the study (Design Workshop) will update the Council on its progress and next
steps. Jordan Swain with Design Workshop will present initial findings, progress, and next
steps.

Jordan Swain, Design Workshop discussed the West Jordan and Midvale Station Area
Plan.

Project Statement:
The West Jordan and Midvale stations are situated at a particularly beneficial location,
where they are far enough from downtown Salt Lake City to be distinct from the capital,
yet close enough to be convenient regional destinations. The three stations are also each
individually unique in their surrounding land uses that they have a built-in identity to work
from.

These Station Area Plans provide an opportunity to build community support for transit
adjacent development that appeals to the commuters that use them now and imagines a
vibrant future for those who don't yet view them as part of daily life.

to the opioid epidemic. The final settlement amount is dependent on Midvale and other
cities' participation. lf it wishes to participate, Midvale must join the settlement no later

Resolution No. 2023-R-14 would authorize the City to join the national settlement and
permit the Mayor to submit the required participation forms on the City's behalf.

BACKGROUND AND OVERVIEW
Midvale City has been approached by the Utah Attorney General's Office about
participating in another national opioid settlement. This proposed settlement would
resolve opioid litigation betvveen the State of Utah and 27 ol 29 of Utah's counties with
Teva and Allergan-manufacturers of opioids-and CVS, Walgreen's, and Walmart-
operators of pharmacy chains. Any settlement amount will be split 50/50 between the
State and the counties. The State and the counties will receive these funds because they
provide health services related to the opioid epidemic. Although municipalities will not be
receiving any of the settlement funds, they stand to benefit from the additional services
and resources provided by the counties and the State from the settlement funds.

The final settlement amount will be determined based on the amount of participation by
political subdivisions with populations greater than 30,000. Under the formula, Utah and
its counties would split the following:

   . With 100% subdivision participation: $210 million
   . With 95% subdivision participation: $185 million
   o With 75% subdivision participation: $123 million
ln order to maximize the potential settlement amount, Midvale must participate in the
settlement. Midvale may participate in the settlement by executing and submitting a
provided participation form no later than April 18,2023. By participating in the settlement,
the City will waive all of its rights to individually sue any of the involved companies for
opioid epidemic-related issues. Additional information about the settlement can be found
at: https://nationalopioidsettlement.com/.
