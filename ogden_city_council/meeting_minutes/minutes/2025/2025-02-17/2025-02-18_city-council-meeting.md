Proposed Weber Housing Authority Project. Representatives from the City
Administration will provide an update on efforts to relocate Weber Housing
Authority’s proposed permanent supportive housing project
         Mayor Nadolski provided a progress report on discussions with Weber Housing Authority (WHA) and other key
stakeholders, and ongoing efforts to find an alternative site for WHA’s proposed permanent supportive housing project. Other
options that have been discussed include obtaining an appraisal for the property and building located at 2325 Madison Avenue,
which was purchased by WHA in 2022 and is the preferred site for the project. This appraisal would be used to determine if the
City could purchase the property from WHA to make it financially whole. City Administration also favors relocating the project to
a more acceptable location, preferably in another city in Weber County.
         Chair White thanked Mayor Nadolski for the information provided; she suggested the next step would be for the Council
and Administration to meet in a work session setting to discuss these options in greater detail. Mayor Nadolski expressed a
willingness to engage in continued discussion with the Council. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith suggested scheduling a
follow-up meeting tonight in order for the WHA and the public to be informed of the timeline.
         Discussion among the Council, Council staff, and City Administration centered on the types of actions relative to the
matter that would require Council approval; the desires and preferences of WHA; they determined to schedule a work session
discussion to discuss the issues raised this evening and to receive any updates on March 11, with possible action scheduled for the
March 18 meeting.

The Lotus Company – Citywalk Fire Recognitions. The Lotus Company will
be thanking Ogden Police, Ogden Fire, and the Bureau of Alcohol,
Tobacco, Firearms, and Explosives (ATF) for their swift and skilled
responses to contain and manage the fire at the Lotus Citywalk project, and
for a quick resolution to the case
          Jim McQueen, representing The Lotus Company, expressed appreciation to the City Fire and Police Departments, City
Administration, and other entities for their professional and calming response to the January 25 fire at the Lotus Citywalk project
site. The project has been underway for seven months and it will take some time to get the project up and running again to proceed
with providing affordable housing for the community. He presented plaques to Mayor Nadolski, Fire Chief Mathieu, Police Chief
Sube, and Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms, and Explosives (ATF) Special Agent Boles. He acknowledged the difficulty the
fire has caused, but communicated that The Lotus Company is committed to getting back on track and working with the City of
Ogden for many more projects in the community.
          Mayor Nadolski stated this was the first structure fire he was on site for as Mayor; he echoed the support for the Police
and Fire Departments for their efforts and stated he is grateful they were able to contain the fire and provide needed support. He
also acknowledged the Public Services Department of the City, namely Director Anderson, for their response and assistance to the
public safety officials at the scene.

Recognizing Cathy Blaisdell for her more than two decades of services on

         A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of Cathy Blaisdell for more than two decades of service on the Ogden
Planning Commission came before the Council for consideration. Vice Chair Hyer read the certificate of recognition issued by
the Ogden City Council and Mayor Nadolski and presented Ms. Blaisdell with the certificate.

Proposed Ordinance 2025–7 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to revise
provisions regarding engineering regulations, design standards,
construction specifications, traffic control regulations, variances, by making
technical changes, and by amending the code index accordingly
         A memo from the Engineering Division regarding proposed amendments to Engineering Standards for public
improvements came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Engineering Standards establish minimum
requirements for the design and construction of public improvements such as streets and water/sewer utilities. On November 6,
2024, the Planning Commission considered the proposal and unanimously recommended approval of the updates. On February 4,
2025, the Council discussed the proposal during a work session.
         City Engineer Nielsen summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide a summary of the
proposed amendments:
              • Updated variance request process:
                      o Variances may address Ogden City Engineering Standards and Storm Water Design Manual.
                      o Review period for variance applications: 15 business days
                      o Criteria for approval explicitly defined:
                                Avoidance of undue hardship
                                No conflict with safety, functionality, or fire protection requirements
                                Does not modify development approval conditions
              • When traffic impact studies are required:
                      o Generate 100 or more vehicle trips per day
                      o Include 50 or more dwelling units, or
                      o Add 30,000 square feet of building area
                      o Requirement can be waived if not needed
              • Traffic impact studies
              • Sidewalk standards
              • Sewer standards
                      o New sewer main shall require an air test
                      o Clarified details for sewer cleanouts
                      o Updated easement language for easement width
                                 1 foot depth = 2 feet of easement width, minimum 20 feet
              •   Storm drain standards
                      o Created a storm water design manual
                      o Added language about State requirements for a dewatering permit
                      o Updated storm water construction requirements
              • Low Impact Design (LID) standards
              • Water standards
                      o Added a maximum depth requirement (5.5’)
                      o Clarified pressure/bacteria testing requirements (minimum of 20 feet in length of mainline).
                      o Updated lateral installation alignment
              • Utility boring and exploratory potholing.
         For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

Proposed Resolution 2025–4 authorizing and approving the form of an
interlocal sales tax pledge agreement and the issuance of the city obligation
           A memo from the Management Services Department regarding a proposed Sales Tax Pledge Agreement for the purchase
of the U.S. Forest Service Building came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated on December 10, 2024, the
Ogden City Redevelopment Agency Board approved several actions to authorize the purchase of the U.S. Forest Service Building
located at 507 25th Street. The Redevelopment Agency Board approved a resolution authorizing the Redevelopment Agency
Executive Director to purchase the Forest Service building for $3.6 million, a resolution declaring the intent of the City to
reimburse the Redevelopment Agency for expenditures incurred in connection with the building, and a budget amendment
appropriating $600,000 for earnest money and maintenance costs associated with the purchase of the building. The Agency put the
earnest money down as required by the purchase contract in December 2024.
           Financial Advisor Laura Lewis, representing Lewis Robertson Burningham, summarized the memo and stated the
proposal for the Council in this action is to approve a resolution pledging sales tax as a backstop to the Redevelopment Agency
tax increment financing for the purchase of the Forest Service building and setting parameters for issuing bonds. Also included in
the proposed action is for the Council to set a public hearing on the bond parameters for March 18, 2025. Ms. Lewis concluded
that this item is directly related to the action item included on the agenda for tonight’s Special Redevelopment Agency meeting.
            Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2025-4, entitled:
                     “A resolution of the City Council of Ogden City, Utah (the “City”), authorizing and approving the form of an
                     Interlocal Sales Tax Pledge Agreement and the issuance of the City obligation (as defined herein) created
                     thereunder; fixing the maximum aggregate principal amount of the City obligation, the maximum number of
                     years over which the City obligation may mature, and the maximum interest rate which the City obligation may
                     bear and the maximum discount from par at which the City obligation may be sold; providing for the
                     publication of a notice of public hearing and bonds to be issued; providing for the running of a contest period
                     and setting of a public hearing date; authorizing a supplemental indenture and other documents required in
                     connection therewith; acknowledging the issuance and sale by the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency, Utah of
                     Sales Tax and Tax Increment Revenue Bonds, Series 2025; and authorizing the taking of all other actions
                     necessary to the consummation of the transactions contemplated by this resolution; and related matters..”
           ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER RICHEY AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA,
RESOLUTION 2025-4 WAS ADOPTED, AND A PUBLIC HEARING SET FOR MARCH 18, 2025 AT 6:00 P.M. IN THE
CITY COUNCIL CHAMBERS REGARDING THE PROPOSAL, UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE:
VOTING AYE – COUNCIL MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, MYERS, RICHEY, VICE CHAIR HYER, AND
CHAIR WHITE. VOTING NO – NONE.

Public Comments
          Sarge Morielli thanked the Mayor and Council for their service. She then addressed conditions on 29th Street and Polk
Avenue; two weeks ago, there was a major accident at an intersection involving two vehicles. There is not a 4-way stop sign at the
intersection and the vehicles landed in front yards of residences on Polk Avenue. Conditions are very dangerous, and a 4-way stop
is needed. She recommended two 4-way stop signs within two blocks of every school in the community to improve safety for
residents, students, and motorists. She has already purchased two stop signs and poles and needs permission to have them
installed. Many residents are very upset and need to see something done. She also suggested reducing speed limits on roads
throughout the community; there are too many people that do not care about the safety of others. She concluded by urging the City
to increase wages and benefits for Police and Fire Department employees in the City.
           Laura Lewis discussed water supply lines; her street is constantly dealing with dirty water coming out of their lines and a
main line broke earlier this week and that resulted in the water in her home looking like mud. She spoke with workers in the area
and asked them to flush the lines, which they agreed to do, but there is still wastewater in her water lines and that is building up in
her water heater, which will cause it to fail prematurely. She inquired as to the City’s plans for replacing older water main lines in
the City. Much is being done for new construction, but those that have lived in the City for a long time deserve the same quality of
water as new residents and businesses.
           Karece Thompson stated he is from the Utah Office of the State Treasurer; he encouraged the Council to consider passing
a resolution recognizing April as Financial Literacy Awareness Month. He discussed the amount of household debt in Ogden and
in the State of Utah and stated that one way to help to eradicate poverty is to educate people on financial literacy initiatives.
           Jaime Wadman with PATH Forward Utah echoed Mr. Thompson’s comments and talked about the tools available to the
public from the Utah Office of the State Treasurer. She thanked the Council for their attention and for consideration of adopting
the proposed resolution.
           Robert Hunter stated the timeline that the Mayor and Council discussed earlier this evening regarding the Weber Housing
Authority (WHA) permanent housing project was very concerning; there is not enough time to wait, and WHA is at risk of losing
grant funding that was awarded for this project. The Ogden City Council’s job is to enhance the economic viability of Ogden, and
the goal of the WHA is to find housing for the homeless population in the community as expeditiously as possible. They will
move forward with that goal as quickly as they can.
           Anne Dunaway stated the people of Ogden City are so lucky to have so much to celebrate; tonight, the Council and the
public celebrated Ms. Blaisdell’s commitment to the community and that seemed like a perfect gateway for her to hone in on her
purpose for attending tonight’s meeting. Ogden City’s pattern of ignorance over community and social development project
components leads to eventual lambasting of projects like the WHA project. That established pattern of broadsiding community
development projects leads to those projects being sunk into the murky waters of the City’s economic opportunities. She urged the
City to take a long look at its obligations to let community development leaders do the leading, and for government to stick to
what it does best, which is to protect the community’s ability in those pursuits.
           Kevin Lundell commented on the update provided by Mayor Nadolski regarding the WHA project; he asked if the plan
truly is for Ogden City to spend millions of dollars of taxpayer money to purchase the WHA property simply to prevent disabled
homeless individuals from being housed in permanent supportive housing that the neighborhood actually supports. He stated that
it does not seem like a very good plan. City Administration has had an entire month since the last time the topic was discussed to
submit a thorough report to the Council, but basically no information was provided. The information that was presented could
have easily been sent to the Council in an email so that further action could have been taken tonight. He referenced the funding
sources for the project and the possibility of securing additional funding during the 2025 Legislative Session, noting that there are
a record number of legislative bills pending and he is doubtful that additional funding bills will be considered this year. He stated
that none of the citizens of Ogden know the Council’s position on this project because they have never actually voted on it. He
concluded by referencing comments made in a recent meeting regarding the City’s Good Landlord Program and the fact that the
City has a policy that a landlord will receive a discount on their business license if they do not rent to anyone with a drug offense.
He stated he wants to know if that is indeed a policy of the City. During that same meeting, Council member Hyer and Mayor
Nadolski commented that the WHA project flies in the face of the Good Landlord program based on housing those with a past
drug offense. He stated he wants to know if the City truly wants to prohibit the housing of recovering addicts either privately or
publicly. He has family members who have been impacted by the opioid crisis in the United States; it is not an option to express
opposition to opioids but then abandon those that have fallen victim to the drug. A great deal of time and effort has been poured
into the WHA project and the residents of the neighborhood surrounding the property deserves to know where the Council stands.
           Heath Satow also expressed disappointment about the lack of information regarding the WHA project, as well as a lack
of leadership on the matter. He noted Mayor Nadolski’s presentation felt like a ‘kid showing up to do a book report’ and asking
those he is presenting to to read the book on their own and share their thoughts. He is disappointed about the delay in the project
and the lack of communication between the Mayor and Council. He stated the City government has failed on this issue, and it is
time to step aside and let the WHA move forward.
           Brooke Barragan also referenced the information provided tonight and in the Council packet regarding the WHA project;
in the January 14 meeting, the Mayor indicated Administrative staff had made some progress in their research of the project but
needed additional time. Again, on January 21, Administration agreed to provide a progress report on efforts to find an alternative
site for the permanent supportive housing project. The public came to tonight’s meeting expecting a progress report and they got
nothing. She cannot believe the public has waited this long for the Mayor to not say anything at all. She asked why the offers that
have been made to buy the property from WHA were not discussed tonight in the progress report. She stated that the City is
failing; inaction is an action, and the City is actively leaving disabled homeless people out in the cold.
           Chair White asked Mayor Nadolski to respond to Ms. Morielli’s comments about the need for a 4-way sign on Polk
Avenue. Mayor Nadolski stated he and the Public Services Director are aware of the matter and will continue discussions with her
after this meeting.
           Chair White then acknowledged there are a number of old water lines throughout the City and there are capital
improvement plan (CIP) projects in place to eventually address those issues. Council member Richey stated that he also discussed
Ms. Lewis’s concerns with Public Services Director Anderson who informed him that the City is currently not drawing any water
from its wells; all water being supplied comes from the treatment plant and due to temperatures and mineral components, the
water may look a bit different, but it is safe and drinkable.
         Chair White then asked the representatives of the Utah Office of the State Treasurer to submit a sample resolution or
proclamation for the Council to consider at a future meeting. Council member Graf stated he also supports action to bring
awareness of personal finances to the public. Council member Richey agreed.
         Chair White then thanked everyone who commented on the WHA project; she acknowledged the concern that the project
has been underway for some time but noted that the details of the project were only made available to the Council in recent
months. She stated that the Council heard tonight from those in support of the project, but there are just as many people on the
other side of the issue that have expressed their concerns and opposition to the project and the Council must listen to both sides
and weigh what is best for the community.

Mayor Comments
          Mayor Nadolski addressed the public comments regarding the Weber Housing Authority (WHA) project; if the City were
to purchase the property from the WHA, it would be done using the policies and approach from adopted neighborhood
stabilization efforts. The property and building could still be used to provide housing opportunities if that is something that the
Council wishes to do. He addressed Ms. Barragan and stated that the information he provided during his report tonight was based
upon the information that was provided to him by the WHA, and he was trying to be deferential to the Council’s authority to
control the City’s budget. He stated that he has also heard from many people in the community who are afraid to comment
publicly about the issue because they do not want to be chastised in the way that he and the Council have been chastised for
stating their opinions. Many residents have great concerns about the project and are grateful to City Administration for exploring
alternatives. He stated he is doing his best to reach the best conclusion as quickly as possible. He stated that he wished he and the
Council would have known about the law that requires City approval of a county housing authority project sooner; if that had been
the case, these issues would have been addressed much sooner. However, he does not believe the City should bear all the blame
for the misunderstanding of that law.
