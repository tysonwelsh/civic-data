Proposed Ordinance 2021-45 reclassifying property at 551 East 900 North
from Single-family Residential Zone (R-1-6} to Multiple-family Residential
Zone (R-3) (Continued from November 9, 2021)
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the rezone of property at 551 East 900
North came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a developer has petitioned to rezone a vacant church property
from R-1-6 (single-family residential) to R-3 (multi-family residential). The petitioner is requesting to rezone the property so he
can build a townhome development. The Planning Commission determined that this rezone request does comply with the General
Plan, and townhomes would create an effective transition from higher density housing at Washington Boulevard to single-family
housing to the east of the property in question. It was also noted that a new development would be preferable to a vacant church
lot, which could fall into disrepair and negatively impact the neighborhood. The Planning Commission unanimously
recommended rezoning the property from R-1-6 to R-3. The Council voted to extend this item during their October 5, 2021
Council meeting; the matter was discussed again on November 9, 2021, and the Council extended consideration of the ordinance
and directed staff to negotiate a conditional overlay agreement regulating certain components of the project.
         Deputy Planning Manager Brierly summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
adjustments that have been made to the plan since the Council's last review of the application; Planning staffhas also worked with
the applicant to negotiate a development agreement, which provides for a maximum of32 units, two-story maximum height, eight
to 10 guest parking spaces, screening and shielding oflights on the site, a livable square footage of 1,400 square feet per area, and
defmed architectural standards. He concluded the updated plan meets the City's group dwelling standards. The options before the
Council this evening are to either deny the petition, accept the Planning Commission's recommendation, or approve the R-3/CO
zoning with the development agreement.
         Chair Blair invited input from the applicant.
         Steve Diamond reported on efforts underway to demolish the church building on the site; many of the materials inside the
building have been removed and repurposed. He stated he is now seeking Council support of the adjustments to his development
proposal and indicated he feels he can provide a quality project that will benefit the community.
         Council member Hyer stated he has spoken with many of the residents who previously expressed concern about the
project and he believes that Mr. Diamond has gained their trust; it was admirable that he attended the community meeting that was
scheduled to discuss the proposal. Mr. Diamond stated he understands and appreciates the concerns of the neighborhood; they are
sad to see their church disappear and replaced by a housing project.
         Council member Stephens asked if the project will be managed by a homeowner's association (HOA). Mr. Diamond
answered no; there will be a property management company hired to oversee property management, but they will be offsite. He
then indicated that he does feel the safety of the neighborhood would be increased if the City were able to limit or restrict on-street
parking on 900 North.
         Chair Blair introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2021-45, entitled:
                   "An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Zoning Map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
                   the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as multiple-family residential zone/conditional overlay zone (R-3/CO)
                   property heretofore classified as single-family residential zone (R-1-6), generally located at 5 51 East 900 North,
                   and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after fmal passage."
         A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Chief Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder
have at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.
         Chair Blair then called for public input regardirig the proposed ordinance.
         Angel Castillo thanked the City Council for working through the process of suggesting a conditional overlay zoning
mechanism for this project; she supports the project as it has been amended by the applicant.

        Joint Resolution 2021-7 establishing Voter Participation Areas
                  A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a joint resolution establishing voter participation areas.
        The memo stated the City Council is required to adopt new Voter Participation Areas (VPA) before the end of the year, but the
        boundaries can be amended in early 2022 to match the new Council district boundaries. The goal of this meeting is to adopt VPA
        boundaries that match the current Council district boundaries, then update the VPA boundaries in early 2022 when new Council
        district boundaries are adopted.
                  Council Policy Analyst Watkins summarized the memo and presented the proposed VPA Map, which was created by the
        Weber County Clerk's Office.
                  Chair Blair introduced in writing proposed Joint Resolution 2021-7, entitled:
                           "A joint resolution of the Ogden City Mayor and the Ogden City Council establishing Voter Participation

        Public Comments
                 The following individuals spoke to their disappointment about the postponement of action on the resolution supporting
        the replacement of the Marshall N. White Community Center (MWC): Betty Sawyer, David Timmerman, Angel Castillo, Heath
        Satow and Sebastian Benitez. They indicated they are suspicious of the timing of communication from the Young Men's
        Christian Association (YMCA) regarding the possibility of submitting a proposal to construct a YMCA facility in Ogden. They
        indicated the community has waited too long for this action and the decision made by the Council tonight was to 'kick the can
        further down the road'.
                 Angel Castillo also referenced a funding opportunity from the State of Utah that can be used to compensate employees
        who worked throughout the COVID-19 pandemic; for Ogden City, this would include Police and Fire Department employees. The
        City could pay up to an additional $13 per hour per employee, with a cap of$25,000 per eligible worker and she asked why the
        City is not applying for those funds. She indicated public safety employees have worked long and hard enough while being
        underpaid and the City should make the decision to fairly compensate them by accessing these funds.

        Mayor Comments
                Mayor Caldwell stated that he will honor the promises that have been made to work through all the issues surrounding
        the Marshall White Center (MWC); he appreciates the partnership and support of the City Council in that endeavor. He concluded
        by wishing all present a happy holiday season.
