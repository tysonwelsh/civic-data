         Code Enforcement Ordinance Discussion

Mr. Brickey explained that he met with a group of directors and discussed the ongoing code
enforcement issues. He asked Ryan Carter, City Attorney, and Jason Lethbridge, Development
Services Director, to report to the Council.

Mr. Carter explained that staff and Council have had an ongoing discussion about what
ordinances could be added to improve code enforcement. He said in state code, it is illegal to
drive a vehicle that is unregistered, but legal to park an unregistered vehicle on the road. In
state law, parking a vehicle for more than 48 hours on the street is illegal. However, it is hard
for law enforcement to prove the vehicle was parked in the same location for more than 48
hours.

Mr. Carter explained that a draft ordinance was sent to Councilmembers in an email. The draft
ordinance would require a vehicle to be moved more than 400 feet every 48 hours. The draft
ordinance would also make parking an unregistered vehicle on the street illegal.

Mayor Staggs asked the Council how they would like to move forward.

Councilmember Wells asked for more time to review the changes.

Mr. Lethbridge explained he would implement a policy that if a dog bite results in an injury, the
dog will be impounded. The City will hold the dog until vaccination status can be obtained. The
resident will be able to appeal this through a process.

Councilmember McDougal would like to move forward with the changes to the parking
ordinances. He asked if the City has a policy for multiple offenders related to dog bites. In
response, Mr. Carter said the current ordinance states the license will be revoked if a dog bites
three times. Mr. Carter said that staff is discussing whether this should be reduced from three
times to two times. Mr. Lethbridge said impounding the dog allows staff time to evaluate the
dog while the animal is in the care and custody of the City.

Councilmember Buroker asked if the ordinance would outline when the dog is returned to the
owner. Mr. Lethbridge said the ordinance will specify when the dog would be returned to the
owner.

Mayor Staggs asked staff to clarify the policy versus the ordinance. In response, Mr. Lethbridge
said the current ordinance allows the code enforcement officer to determine what to do with the
dog. In the future, the ordinance language will state that the dog will be impounded, however,
until the ordinance is adopted, he has directed staff to impound the dog in the event of a dog
bite that causes injury.

Councilmember McDougal asked about the City’s liability in releasing a dog that may bite again.
Mr. Carter said there is not a liability to the City.

Councilmember Wells asked about City code regarding cargo containers. In response, Mr.
Lethbridge said the City’s ordinance prohibits cargo containers which raises the question about
whether they should be prohibited. Councilmember Wells commented that he has noticed
containers in his district and he would like the Council to consider if these should be allowed.
Councilmember McCay agreed and said she would like to discuss this topic in a future work
session.

Councilmember Buroker expressed appreciation for staff’s work on the dog bite ordinance.

Councilmember McDougal said he would also like a discussion on storage containers and
asked staff to research what other cities are doing in regards to these containers.

Mayor Staggs asked staff to bring these ordinances back on October 4 or October 18 for
approval.

Mr. Lethbridge said the property is rectangular and roughly 466-ft wide and 750-ft deep. The

         Ordinance No. 22-22 – The Master Development Agreement for Riverton Ranch
         Estates, establishing requirements, standards, and parameters for development of
         approximately 3.2 acres located at 13040 S Redwood, and including approval of an
         eight (8) lot private lane subdivision on that property

Mr. Lethbridge explained this was a public hearing for a Development Agreement. Riverton
City is proposing approval of a Development Agreement establishing requirements, standards,
and parameters for development of approximately 3.2-acres located at 13040 S Redwood
Road, including approval of an eight (8) lot subdivision of that property, with all lots accessing a
private lane.

Mr. Lethbridge said the property is currently zoned R-3-SD, a single-family residential zone, with
1/3 minimum lot sizes. The zoning and general plan designations were previously approved by
Riverton City. The subdivision accesses Riverton Ranch Road via a private drive, and the
applicant has proposed a gate as part of the development. The lane and configuration and
location of the gate has been reviewed by the Riverton City’s Unified Fire Authority
representative.

Mr. Lethbridge recommends that this item be continued to the next meeting so they can
complete the final redline comments and have the complete and final plans attached to the
master development plan.

Councilmember Buroker asked if any area would be between the sidewalk and the solid
masonry fence. Mr. Lethbridge said the fence would go right up to the sidewalk.
