23 Kearns Improvement District Communications Pole; 5590 West 7000 South; Conditional Use
Permit for a 50-foot-tall Communications Pole; R-1-6A Zone; Kearns Improvement
District/Greg Anderson (applicant) [#30312; parcel 20-24-355-019]
James Woodruff, Kearns Improvement District, stated that they provide 3200 water and sewer connections
in West Jordan and are replacing all meters with those with technology to communicate with the system to
provide 24-hour reading that will allow residents to monitor their water usage at all times. They will have
seven strategically placed poles to maximize coverage. The subject site also has a water well. For
comparison, the closest power pole is 50 feet tall. There are large trees on the residential property behind
the pole, which will hopefully make the pole less intrusive.
Catherine Richardson said this is good for easier meter reading and that residents will have better data. She
asked about the protocol when a leak is detected with this new technology.
James Woodruff said the protocol! will remain the same. When an increase in water usage is detected, they
contact the resident at their home or through other communications. Meter reading is currently done once
per month. There will be an option for the homeowner to set notifications within parameters that they
identify. This system is similar to the one that West Jordan has implemented. He reviewed the site that
includes a small well building. There are power poles and streetlights in the vicinity as well.
There was a discussion regarding the difference between a well and a reservoir and how water is distributed
by Kearns Improvement District and West Jordan’s system.
After additional questions about water capacity and needs, Ammon Allen suggested that staff could present
an overview in a future training.

3. Kum & Go; 8554 South 5600 West; Preliminary Subdivision Plat (2 lots on 2.45 acres) and

Preliminary Site Plan; SC-1 Cone; Galloway & Company/Taylor Smith (applicant) [#29228,
29229; parcel 20-35-479-017] :

Taylor Smith, Galloway & Company civil engineers, stated that the property will be divided into two

parcels with Kum & Go on the south lot. The north lot will be sold, and any future development will also

come to the city for approvals.

Tayler Jensen gave an overview of the subject site and surrounding properties. The property was zoned SC-

1 about 10 years ago and is also on the future land use map as neighborhood commercial. The 8600 South

flyover is under construction. The lots meet the minimum size requirements of at least one acre. Because

5600 West and 8600 South are collector roads, Kum & Go only has right-in and right-out access, and there

will be a shared easement on Lot | from 5600 West. It was important to get the access points as far away

from the intersection as possible. The traffic impact study showed that the proposed design will operate at
an acceptable level of service once constructed. The Design Review Committee recommended approval of
the building design and layout.

Based on the analysis and findings contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning

Commission approve the Preliminary Major Subdivision Plat for 5600 West Krause Crossing, and the

Preliminary Site Plan for Kum & Go, located on approximately 2.408 acres at 8554 South 5600 West, with

all requirements for approval.

It was pointed out that there is a traffic signal at the intersection with crosswalks for pedestrian crossing.
There are no current applications for Lot 1 or for the high-density housing or SC-2 property to the south.
Any future approvals will come to the planning commission.

Ammon Allen opened the public hearing.

Lori Shenefelt, West Jordan resident, stated that although she knew the property was commercial, she did
not think a gas station would be built, especially so close to the high school. Her concerns included
lighting, illuminated signs, the need for a taller wall for privacy and security, and hours of operations.
Shelly and John Reed, West Jordan resident, speaking remotely stated that they had emailed some
comments to the commission. Her concerns were with lighting, headlights from vehicles coming in from
5600 will shine into their home, placement of light posts along the property line, noise that is generated at
the gas pumps, and placement of fuel tanks against the residential lots. They asked for a buffer from the
noise and light with a higher fence, a fence on the north side of the Kum & Go, and for more landscaping
next to the residential.

Amanda Phelps, West Jordan resident and business owner, was concerned with the additional traffic that
this will create at the intersection of Old Bingham Highway and 5600 West. There have already been
several accidents due to traffic back up, especially with student drivers. She did not trust the traffic impact
study. The site is directly accessible to the high school, which makes accessibility to alcohol too easy. She
did not see the need for another gas station in this community based on the number already in the area, and
she did not want it.

John Phelps, West Jordan resident, agreed with the previous comments. There are significant traffic issues
and a blind spot from 5600 West due to trees. Kids walk to and from schools in the area. The amount of
light was a big concern. He said that 5600 West is being used as a drag strip now and these people will now
congregate at the gas station. This project is a bad idea

Jeremy Draidfort, West Jordan resident, speaking remotely, agreed with the comments. This is duplicitous,
there is an abundance of gas stations already, the lighting, increased traffic and noise, and view of a gas
station will be detrimental to the residents.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Ammon Allen clarified for the public the role of the Planning Commission compared to the City Council.
The Commission has to review each project based on certain criteria, which if met, they are obligated to
approve. Part of the review is to make sure the code is addressed appropriately with conditions to mitigate
impacts where allowed.

Tayler Jensen addressed the concerns. The photometric study shows 29 lumens next to the pump island, but
the lumens are zero at the property line. The lights will be directionally facing down, and the plans show
that the light trespass will not be over the property lines.

Ammon Allen pointed out that you can still look over the fence and see the lights, but the light won’t shine
over the property line.

Trish Hatch asked about height limits for the illuminated sign.

Taylor Smith said they will take all concerns into consideration. He pointed out the proposed locations for
the 6-foot-tall monument signs. Gas prices will be LED readouts. No pole signs are proposed. The highly
lit Maverik that was referred to by a resident does not have the same standards as this location since it is
next to residential.

Catherine Richardson was sympathetic with the lighting concerns with no landscaping to protect the
residents. She was concerned that with proximity to the high school and future high density to the south,
that this will create a place for kids to congregate and cause trouble and noise. She liked the design and
thought it would be a good idea, until she heard from the residents. She did not think it was fair to the
residents.

Scott Langford said the Planning Commission’s authority is well defined when it comes to site plan
approval. A lot of the concerns stated dealt with the use of the property. The property owner has a right to
use the property under the current zoning, and the proposed use is permitted. The Planning Commission
should focus on mitigating impacts and not the use itself.

Duncan Murray explained that if this were a conditional use then they could look at ways to mitigate, but
there is no ability to impose conditions in this situation as a permitted use. The focus of the discussion on a
site plan and subdivision plat review should be if there is anything that does not meet the city code
requirements.

Scott Langford asked staff to explain to those in attendance requirements for items such as landscaping and
fencing.

Tayler Jensen said the code requires a 6-foot-tall masonry wall between commercial and residential uses.
This will be installed along the Kum & Go site, and along Lot 1 when it develops in the future. The finish
grade at the access from 5600 West generally slopes up toward the west and is almost seven feet higher
than the entrance point. Along with the six-foot fence, that slope will do a good job in blocking headlights
from the residential properties. If the commission is still concerned with that, staff can work with the
applicant to see about getting more landscaping along the property lines. The applicant has been working
with staff in providing a lot of landscaping and they could potentially move some next to the residential
properties. All noise regulations fall with the Salt Lake County noise ordinance and if levels exceed the
limits, appropriate action should be taken through code enforcement. He clarified that the required fuel tank
locations are a minimum of 20 feet the property line, which they exceed. This plan was reviewed by the
utility engineer, public works engineer, and the fire department and will have to be built to code. The city
traffic engineer also reviewed the traffic impact study and did not have any concerns with the findings in
the report. Alcohol sales are regulated through the State of Utah and will have to get approvals before West
Jordan issues a business license. He thought that the minimum distance for alcohol sales from a school is
500 feet, and this is approximately 900 feet as a pedestrian would walk from door to door.

Ammon Allen said the issues with trees creating a blind spot and drag racing and noise in general would be
to contact a city council member. He reiterated that the noise ordinance falls under Salt Lake County
regulations. The best thing to do would be to contact the police department when the noise is occurring.

Larry Gardner asked Commissioner Richardson what her opinion was based upon, because the traffic study

he didn’t want that to be reflected upon the rest of the Planning Commission.

Commissioner Richardson said she did not want to be against the applicant. She suggested flipping the site
plan to be on the side instead of in the middle of the subdivision.

Larry Gardner asked how that would mitigate her traffic concerns.

Catherine Richardson said the traffic issue exists, but her suggestion with the site plan would be a different
thing. Mostly she was saying there is not enough access to the pedestrians or to the vehicular traffic. It will
be a horrible intersection for the residents, and it will decrease property values especially of those homes
next to the site.

Larry Gardner said that was not true.

Catherine Richardson said she stood by her statements that property values will be reduced and that the
Planning Commissioners should be concerned about the residents.

George Winn asked staff to address site plan criteria B regarding negative impacts to adjacent properties or
neighborhoods.

Tayler Jensen said the code regulating the SC-1 zone is a decision that has already been made deeming it
an appropriate use at that location. Staff reviews the plan for compliance with the standards that were
adopted by the City Council through a legislative action. So, when staff indicates there is not a conflict
with the criteria it is because it has been found to meet all of the development standards for things such as
setbacks, heights, parking, building orientation, building appearance, etc. Staff does not have discretion in
those regulations.

George Winn understood Mr. Jensen to say that criteria B is not a subjective statement about the proposal,
but it is a reasonably objective statement about meeting the criteria.

Tayler Jensen said yes, that is his belief.

4. Wood Ranch Plat 2 Subdivision; 7400 South and SR-111 Preliminary Subdivision Plat (154

residential and 2 commercial lots on 35.352 acres) and Sub Area Development Plan;
PCH/MDA #29628; parcel 20-27-100-005; 101-001; 176-001]

Gary Langston, Third Cadence, master developer of Wood Ranch, said the plat will consist of

approximately 150 lots and including roads and open space on 35 acres. This plat will include residential

amenities and their Base Camp that includes a food and beverage service Bistro and welcome center.

Kent Shelton commented on a discussion with the Design Review Committee about the proximity of the

trail to the Bistro roof. The committee wondered if there would be a fence and if that might obstruct any

views.

Gary Langston said the trail is several feet above the roof. They have considered some type of barrier or

handrail on the top of the wall along with some plantings that will prohibit people from getting on the roof.

Catherine Richardson said the project was well thought out and she liked the idea of different types of

housing.

Ray McCandless familiarized the commission with the project layout. Staff supported the proposal to move

the swimming pool to the south, which would help to distribute traffic and parking. This plat has 50

townhome lots, and the rest are single-family. There will also be some open space dedicated to the City as

part of the approval.

Based on the analysis and findings contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning

Commission approve the Preliminary Major Subdivision Plat for Wood Ranch Plat 2 Subdivision and

Preliminary Sub Area Plan located on approximately 35.352 acres at 7400 South and SR-111, with the

conditions and requirements of approval listed.

5. Villas at Stone Creek Subdivision; 4977 West 7800 South; Preliminary Subdivision Plat,
Preliminary Site Plan, Final Development Plan, and Road Vacation; P-C Zone; Peterson
Development (applicant) [#29284, 29286, 29283, 29281; parcel 20-36-226-004, 005; 227-001,
008, 002, 004, 006, 009, 010, 011]

Justin Peterson, Peterson Development, said this is the last substantive phase of the overall Stone Creek

development, which was one of West Jordan’s first planned communities. They are excited to add this

senior component to the Stone Creek development. They have done this product in South Jordan and

Heber, and when this property became available, they thought it made the most sense for a location in West

Jordan. They are excited about the demographic and less impact on the roads and schools that the

demographic represents. He explained that when the commercial plat was recorded in approximately 2001,

it included a road to be dedicated to the city. With this new product and layout, there is no need for the road

at that location.

Duncan Murray explained that the road was dedicated to the city and the vacation will undo that

dedication. There were no public utilities installed in the area that was going to be a road. The Commission

will make a recommendation to the City Council on this part of the application.

Ray McCandless said there have been no changes to the layout since the last time they saw this item when

the land use map was amended. The road exists on the plat only and has never been developed. Of the 116

1. Per City Code, Section 14-3-8: Expiration of Subdivision Approval: An approved preliminary

2; Per City Code, Section 13-7B-5: Expiration of Approved Site Plans: An approved preliminary site
plan shall remain valid for one year following the date of the approval.

3. The preliminary subdivision, site plan, development plan and road vacation shall comply with all
applicable city code requirements.
