Peggy Kelsey of the Utah County Community Development Office came forward with Utah County Deputy Attorney
Robert J. Moore to answer questions. Commissioner Ellertson summarized Regular Agenda Item Nos. 2, 3, 4, and 5 all
addressing a request for an agriculture protection area, submitted by Kenyon Farley. Payson City has requested the right-
of-way for a future power line be excluded from the protected area, and Utah County has made a similar request to
exclude a planned road from the agriculture protection area. All parties have been attempting to reach a compromise,
which is being presented as a restrictive covenant agreement. Mr. Moore explained the restrictive covenant agreement
would need to be signed by Mr. Farley as well as the BNH Properties LLC representative in order to receive all
protections associated with land contained within an agriculture protection area, with two exceptions: eminent domain
and state development projects.

Robert Moore went on to clarify that Kenyon Farley and the other property owners within the agriculture protection area
would benefit from protection against nuisance complaints, annexation petitions, and other issues. Commissioner
Ellertson added that the eminent domain exception would only pertain to the specific portions identified through the
requests made by Payson City and Utah County. Mr. Moore explained how the restrictive covenant is binding for future
property owners, and will be included on the deed to notify buyers. He detailed protections for appraisal related to
eminent domain, and Commissioner Ellertson reiterated that condemnation would be a final effort after all negotiations
have been attempted. Mr. Moore confirmed, and noted the entire protection area would be forfeited if the restrictive
covenant were to be declared null and void. He also mentioned that the bank or mortgage company for the property in
question would need to sign the contract if applicable.

Commissioner Lee asked whether similar measures have previously been taken for agriculture protection areas. Robert
Moore stated not to his knowledge, repeating this restrictive covenant is the middle ground between granting the full
protection area and accepting the requested exemptions. When Commissioner Lee asked whether this agreement would
be upheld in a court of law, Mr. Moore responded yes, but also reminded the Board that a court can challenge the
protection area even without the covenant. Commissioner Lee expressed this contract seems to negate previous
discussions regarding “good faith” negotiations, and Mr. Moore countered that it more appropriately ensures both sides
will negotiate fairly. Without the restrictive covenant, there is no motivation for the property owners to seek a solution
with Payson City or Utah County.

Peggy Kelsey noted agriculture protection areas are reviewed every twenty years for viability. Robert Moore spoke about
the potential for a road within the next thirty years, mentioning a previous case which underwent a similar process with
the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) for Interstate-15 reconstruction. He pointed out that needs are changing,
and Commissioner Lee responded that is subjective. Commissioner Lee asked about the ratio between development
actively being stopped by agriculture protection areas compared to property owners willing to work with government
entities, mentioning the restrictive covenant is unnecessary. Mr. Moore responded the simplest solution would be to deny
the agriculture protection area altogether, reiterating the contract is a compromise for all parties.

Robert Moore detailed the process for acquiring easements if an agriculture protection area is granted with no exceptions.
Commissioner Graves noted the benefit of a contract tied to the property in case future owners are not as willing to
negotiate. Commissioner Lee pointed out the Board has repeatedly stated they are friendly towards agriculture, but this
restrictive covenant is stepping in the opposite direction of that statement. Commissioner Ellertson disagreed, explaining
this is the middle ground. Mr. Moore added Utah County is putting itself at a disadvantage if the protection area is
approved without this covenant. When Commissioner Lee asked how often this situation arises, Mr. Moore reminded
the Board this is unique because of the road already planned for this section before the protection area application was
submitted. When Commissioner Lee stated he would rather the property owner have an advantage in negotiations,
Commissioner Ellertson reminded the Board that they have a duty to the general public as well as individuals.

Utah County Civil Division Chief Deputy Attorney David Shawcroft commented on the process for agriculture protection
areas and involvement of the Utah County Planning Commission and the Utah County Agriculture Protection Advisory
Board. Commissioner Graves noted again that the restrictive covenant serves the applicant, since without it the Board
may choose to deny the agriculture protection area or approve it with the exemptions requested by Payson City and the
county. Robert Moore commented on the court process in cases where the property owner refused to compromise.

Kenyon Farley came forward and thanked the commissioners for taking the time to review and consider all angles of the
issue. Mr. Farley clarified that his agriculture protection area application was first submitted in September 2014, but
needed revision and was re-submitted in December 2014 - a letter from Payson City notifying residents about the
potential power line was distributed at the end of October 2014. Mr. Farley stated he is not trying to use agriculture
protection as a way to “beat” or hoodwink Payson City. Based on growth, cities will expand and agriculture will shrink.
Payson and Santaquin City have been going back and forth over city plans for years, and in this case, Mr. Farley has
ended up right in the middle of their tug-of-war. Mr. Farley suggested the proposed power line is an informal fence by
Payson to prevent the expansion of Santaquin. Mr. Farley reviewed the qualifying factors for agricultural protection and
asked the Board to follow policy since he meets all requirements.

Kenyon Farley commented on the argument of agriculture versus development, stating there is no huge conflict as
suspected by many. Mr. Farley also disagrees with several points of the restrictive covenant, such as the signature from
his mortgage company and the implication that he can bind future owners to certain actions regarding their property.
Mr. Farley expressed this agreement infringes on his rights, and asked the Board to consider how important open area
is to the community.

Commissioner Lee noted that Payson City and Santaquin City have overlapping annexation plans, and Kenyon Farley’s
property is included in both. They discussed the incoming power line, and Mr. Farley expressed willingness to negotiate
with the entities. Commissioner Ellertson acknowledged Mr. Farley’s optimism but reiterated that the agreement must
follow the land, not just the current owner. The Board and Mr. Farley continued to discuss the purpose of the restrictive
covenant.

Brett Herbst owns property north of the Kenyon Farley property; he approached the bench to concur with Mr. Farley.
Mr. Herbst spoke about the right-of-way for 12000 South compared to 12400 South, stating it would not be as
encumbering if placed on 12000 South. Mr. Herbst commented that no one can predict the future, but it is important to
remember the definition of progress is not to remove agriculture. Mr. Herbst does not object to signing the agreement
because he considers it a compromise for all parties.

Utah County resident Larry Ballard approached the bench to comment on issues with the Mountainland Association of
Governments (MAG) and Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO). He applauds the Utah County Community
Development Office staff as well as the Board for addressing property and community rights for a population which is
expected to double by the year 2040. Mr. Ballard expressed that issues like pollution which come with increased
population are overcome in the agricultural areas; widening roads and placing power lines in rural communities is
counterproductive - the emphasis should be on agriculture. It is good planning and zoning to be able to produce food
where we live.

Garrick Hall of the Utah Farm Bureau is in favor of agricultural protection areas, which are designed to preserve open
space, and not to create division between landowners and counties or cities. If the easements will be needed when growth
occurs, the matter should be addressed at that time. Since review of the area will take place in twenty years, it can be
determined at that time that the land is needed more for development. Commissioner Ellertson clarified that any action
which takes place today is to help agriculture. Mr. Hall stated he understands, but also pointed out this application is
not receiving the same treatment as other protection areas in that same neighborhood.

Peggy Kelsey confirmed the timeline of Kenyon Farley’s application compared to notices from Payson City regarding
the proposed power line. Robert Moore explained the legal action which would need to take place for these agenda
items, and they discussed with the Board the various possible findings and variations of the proposed restrictive covenant.
