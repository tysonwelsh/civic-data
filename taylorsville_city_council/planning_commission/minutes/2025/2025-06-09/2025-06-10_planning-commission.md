OTHER MATTERS

         2.   Planning Commission           Discussion     Regarding    External Accessory          Dwelling
              Units ( ADUs),      Accessory     Buildings,     and   Minimum    Parking    Standards,      in

              Response to the City' s Moderate Income Housing Plan Strategies- Jim Spung,
              Senior Planner




e    Mr. Spung led the discussion           on External   Accessory Dwelling Units ( ADUs),    accessory

buildings,    and    minimum      parking   standards.    He   explained   that this   discussion    was   in

response to the city' s Moderate Income Housing Plan strategies and was intended to gather
input from the commission before bringing a formal item to amend the code through a public
hearing.

Spung provided       background    on   Utah Code § 13- 9a- 403, which requires cities to incorporate

a moderate- income housing element in their general plan and include strategies to achieve
moderate income housing. He mentioned that Taylorsville had selected five strategies,
including creating or allowing for and reducing regulations related to internal or detached
accessory     dwelling units in residential     zones.

The discussion focused on several key points:

       Lot Size Requirements: Mr. Spung proposed a minimum lot size of 8, 000 square feet
       for ADUs, with an exception for new construction.                         This sparked further discussion

       with Commissioner Quigley about whether to start with a more conservative 10, 000
       square         foot    requirement     or to eliminate        the   minimum      lot size      altogether      in the
       beginning.

       Owner Occupancy: The proposed standards required the property owner to live on
       the property, either in the ADU or the main house.

       Parking: The discussion touched on parking requirements, with Mr. Spung noting that
       state legislation          might impact future regulations.

       Size and Design             Standards:        Proposed   standards       included     a minimum         size of 200
       square feet and a maximum                 of 1, 500 square feet for detached ADUs, with a limit of
       two bedrooms.             Design standards were discussed                to ensure ADUs would match the

       primary dwelling' s appearance.
       Utility        Connections:
                             The proposed standards required ADUs to share utility
       connections with the primary dwelling( to help avoid subdivision of property).
       Short- term Rentals: The commission discussed prohibiting the use of ADUs as short-
       term rentals so as to reserve any housing for Utah residents in need of housing
       instead of investors.

       Height         Restrictions:      A   maximum       height    of    16   feet   was       proposed,     with   some

       discussion about allowing two- story ADUs in certain circumstances.
Commissioners           expressed various concerns and opinions throughout the discussion:

       Commissioner Quigley suggested a more conservative approach, starting with larger
       lot sizes and gradually expanding if successful.
       Commissioner Munoz proposed gathering more data on inquiries about ADUs and
       suggested limiting the number of permits rather than lot sizes.

       Chair McElreath advocated for moving forward with allowing external ADUs sooner
       rather         than    later,   potentially     positioning    Taylorsville     as    a    model      city for ADU
       regulations. He liked Commissioner Munoz' suggestion of limiting the number of
       permits issued instead of increasing the allowable square footage. But if it came
       down to choosing a minimum lot size, he preferred 8, 000 sq ft over 10, 000 sq ft.
The commission agreed to expedite the process for external ADUs and requested more GIS
analysis to better understand the potential impact on different neighborhoods. They also
discussed the possibility of requiring a public hearing for any two- story ADU proposals.

Mr. Spung agreed to send the PowerPoint presentation to the commissioners and prepare a
more formal review for the next meeting, potentially leading to a public hearing for a text
amendment        in   July.
