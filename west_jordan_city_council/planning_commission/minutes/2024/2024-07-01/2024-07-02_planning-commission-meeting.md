2. Wendy’s; 7914 South 5600 West; Preliminary Site Plan and Administrative Conditional

Use Permit for Restaurant, Fast Food; SC-2 Zone; Dominion Engineering/E. Farley
Eskelson (applicant) [#32633, 32634; parcel 20-35-226-021]

Andrew Horne, Dominion Engineering, was in attendance.

Tayler Jensen said the application is for a preliminary site plan and administrative conditional use permit

for a fast-food restaurant in the SC-2 zoning district. The drive-through lane goes around the building, and

parking exceeds the required standards. The building has four-sided architecture and roof top equipment is

screened by a parapet wall to meet design standards.

approve the Conditional Use Permit for Restaurant, Fast Food (General) and the Preliminary Site Plan

for Wendy’s at Highlands located on approximately 1.11 acres at 7914 South 5600 West.

There are no conditions of approval suggested by staff because no reasonably anticipated detrimental

effects of this use were identified. Any subsequent decision of the Planning Commission relative to the

application will supersede any conflicting analysis or opinions of staff stated in the report or otherwise

presented to the Planning Commission.

3. Terraine West Bench Plat 5; 7400 South U-111 Highway; Preliminary Subdivision Plat (112
lots on 23.382 acres) and Sub Area Development Plan; PCH/MDA Zone; Third
Cadence/Gary Langston (applicant) [#32637; parcel 20-28-200-009]

Gary Langston, Third Cadence representing Wood Ranch Development, stated that this plat will

contain 112 lots with a mix of single-family and townhome units on 23 acres with almost four acres of

open space. This plat is an extension of plat 3, which is under construction, and plat 4, which will
begin mass grading in the next few weeks. Plat 5 will start construction early next year. They agree
with the statements made in the staff report that it is compliant with all city ordinances and
requirements and is a logical extension of city infrastructure and meets all requirements under the

Master Development Agreement. Mr. Langston clarified some information regarding statements in the

staff report. This area is served by Kearns Improvement Districts with no constraints for off-site

improvements at this point. In fact, the existing infrastructure from KID could support all 2,000 units.

The Green Court lot is a definition within the PCH zone and is a lot that fronts onto open space. About

half of the lots will front a road and the other half will front on open space.

Based on the analysis and findings contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning

Commission approve the Preliminary Major Subdivision Plat for Terraine Plat 5 Subdivision and

Preliminary Sub Area Plan located on approximately 23.382 acres at 7400 South and SR-111, with the

conditions and requirements of approval listed.

Conditions and Requirements of Approval:

4. Transportation Master Plan — Amend the West Jordan City Transportation Master Plan;

City-wide applicability; City of West Jordan (applicant) :

Nathan Nelson, City Engineer, said several months ago the Commission was given a progress report on

the update. Jeremy Searle with WCG will be presenting the final version of the plan and receiving

comments from the Commission and the public.

Jeremy Searle, WCG, presented the story map that includes all of the information for the Transportation

Master Plan. The document is also available and includes the details and analysis to back up the plan.

Questions were answered as the information was presented:

Safety improvements recommended in the plan focus on infrastructure and mechanics rather than

recommendations for additional personnel. Safety Comparisons are based on miles traveled and not road

miles, so the rates can be accurately compared to other cities. Improvements in safety are reviewed for

each intersection, which will help the crash numbers go down, but there isn’t a percentage projection for
the entire city overall.

Regarding Transit, the city does not have direct influence over other agencies, but participation in studies
allows West Jordan to make recommendations. West Jordan is ahead of the game with regards to
adoption of its Station Area Plans.

The Active Transportation Plan was recently completed, and those recommendations were incorporated
to make sure they align.

The Travel Demand Model Roadway shows the current levels of service and how it will change in the
future. These projections are determined by complex analysis by specialized programmers using
socioeconomic data.

Roadway and Intersection Projects are recommended to address the roadway demand issues identified by
the model.

Regarding the airport gap at 7000 South, they met with the Airport and suggested ideas such as
connecting a roadway, or going underground, transit routes, or going around the edge of the airport, but
the Airport was hesitant to agree to any of them. There is a recommendation in the plan for a study on
7000 South to see if there is a way to make some kind of connection.

Regarding the planned roundabout at 6400 West 7800 South, there is an ordinance that states the City
Council has to approve any roundabout on an arterial road, and this location was one that was

approved years ago. Property has been acquired for three of the corners. This was in the previous plan
as well.

An explanation of the property acquisition process was given, including local and regional planning,
funding, feasibility and environmental studies to identify the preferred alternative, and property
purchase.

The planned pedestrian overpass on 7000 South has been designed to accommodate seven lanes of
traffic.

George Winn opened the public hearing.

Lanese Hendrickson, West Jordan resident speaking remotely, said she and her husband are
specifically concerned about the area near 9000 South, New Bingham Highway, and 6400 West. When
they purchased their home, the West Jordan Transportation Plan showed that New Bingham would be
closed, and that 9000 South would remain open. They are disappointed that the current plan shows that
both roads will remain open. They understand growth happens, but there are significant concerns with
the livability and safety of their neighborhood. She asked what changes had been made after public
input was given on the plan, and what provisions are being made to ensure that their neighborhood will
remain safe as 9000 South goes through. For example, controlling speed and truck traffic, what kinds
of traffic calming provisions will be installed and if there are crosswalks that will make it safe for
members of the community to be able to cross 9000 South.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Jeremy Searle said that the extension of 9000 South has been on the city and regional plan for a couple

of decades, so that plan is moving forward. They have met with the neighborhood multiple times and

are aware of and understand the concerns. As part of the plan a new signal at New Bingham Highway
and 6400 West is currently being designed. A new signal is planned at 6400 West and 9000 South that
will provide a signalized crosswalk for safe crossing between the neighborhoods and to the elementary
school. There are bike lanes. Another signalized crossing will be at New Bingham and 9000 South to
the east. Trucks are a concern and the industrial area to the south is heavily utilized by trucks. New

Bingham Highway is the primary roadway for trucks, which is why that road will stay open. They can

designate and encourage the use of truck routes, but they cannot legally prohibit trucks on arterial

highways. They did an in-depth analysis on whether they could de-emphasize New Bingham Highway,
but with the growth that is planned they need the capacity of both roads to serve the areas. Changes
that were made after receiving public input include looking more closely at Duck Ridge Road with

additional studies, feedback on the intersection at New Bingham Highway and 6400 West initiated a

signal warrant study, which found that a signal was warranted and UDOT is building one there. The

public wanted them to look at other alternatives, including a ‘no build’ scenario that sent all traffic to

New Bingham and around. There was an additional in-depth study completed that found that there is

enough demand that they need both roads, otherwise the queuing and delay went back thousands of

feet with a significant delay, and it was not feasible in order to have the transportation network in that
area function.

MOTION: _ Trish Hatch moved, based upon the information and findings set forth in the staff
report and upon the evidence and explanations received today, to forward a
positive recommendation to the City Council concerning the proposed updates to
the West Jordan City Transportation Master Plan. The motion was seconded by
Ammon Allen and passed 7-0 in favor.

6. Text Amendment — [OZ Development Standards; Amend the 2009 West Jordan Municipal
Code Section 13-6K-5: Development And Design Requirements; 1. Buildings; 5.
Commercial Buildings; City of West Jordan (applicant)
Larry Gardner said it was found that some of the adopted IOZ ordinance development standards are
inconsistent with some proposed uses for the area. For example, an office building can be six stories, but
a commercial building, which includes hotels, can only be three stories. He reviewed the proposed
changes that deal with architectural features including removing the requirement for 60% glass on the
first floor to help with functional building design. The Planning Commission can still weigh in on the
design during their review. The amendment would allow for parking on all sides of the building to allow
maximum flexibility. Setbacks were adjusted to have a minimum of 15 feet in the front and rear in order
to allow for utility easements. Other changes dealt with building height and setbacks, architecture, and
site design. The reference to Bangerter Highway was removed.
Based on the analysis and findings contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning
Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City Council for this application.

positive recommendation to the City Council for the Text Amendment regarding
IOZ Development Standards. The motion was seconded by Emily Gonzalez and
passed 7-0 in favor.
