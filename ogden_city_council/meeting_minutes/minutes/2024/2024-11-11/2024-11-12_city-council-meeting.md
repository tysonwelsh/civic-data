Proposed Ordinance 2024-31 to rezone property at 139 North Wall Avenue
from R-1-6 (Single-family Residential) to R-3/CO (Multiple-family
Residential with Conditional Overlay) in order to allow two single-family
dwellings and tenant parking garages
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed rezone of 139 North Wall
Avenue came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Craig Butters has submitted a petition to rezone 0.56 acres
from R-1-6 (Single-family residential) to R-3/CO (Multiple-family Residential with a conditional overlay) with a development
agreement. The subject property has recently been added to the Fenway Fields Subdivision so that two single-family lots can
access Fenway Fields Drive, as the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) will not allow access to Wall Avenue at this
location. The applicant has sufficient acreage in the R-1-6 zone to develop two single-family lots, however, the rezone is
necessary to ensure that the zone boundary follows the property lines. In addition to the single-family lots, the applicant proposes
tenant garages for the Fenway Fields townhome residents on the subject property.
          Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and briefly reviewed the history of the Fenway Fields development
and surrounding properties. He then explained on August 7, 2024, the Planning Commission held a public hearing to consider the
proposal. At this meeting, public comment was made requesting that the decision on this item be postponed to the September
meeting to allow the community to review the proposal. On September 4, 2024, there were no public comments made and the
Planning Commission recommended that the proposed zone change be approved with a development agreement. Planning staff
feels the purpose of the development agreement is to provide more restrictive site development standards than in the required
underlying R-3 zone, add additional protection to surrounding property, provide for general traffic circulation needs or other
required public facilities within and for the benefit of the affected property. He also specified that each unit/lot in the proposed
project can be independently owned. He then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide an overview of the concept plan
for the property, garage architecture and locations on each unit, and to review the Planning Commission’s recommended terms of
the development agreement as follows:
              1. The completion of a site plan review if the rezoning is approved.
              2. Any single-family dwelling shall have a single-story appearance by having a pitched roof with the roofline
                   extending down to the top of the main level. A second level can be created but must be incorporated into the
                   roof of the buildings.
              3. Any single-family dwelling shall have a building wall exterior that is 80 percent brick matching the attached
                   elevation plans, and the space around the front doors be just brick. The color matching as close as practical to
                   that in the Fenway Fields subdivision.
              4. Any garage shall be rear-loaded, 2-car garage and have brick veneer covering the wall areas of the ground floor
                   exterior walls shall match as close as practical to that in the Fenway Fields subdivision.
              5. A wood, masonry, or vinyl fence a minimum of six feet high shall be installed, if not already in place, and
                   maintained along the interior side or rear property lines abutting any property outside of Fenway Fields
                   Subdivision.
          Council member Hyer asked if there is sufficient space on the site to allow access for rear-loaded garages on the units.
Mr. Brierley answered yes; Planning staff has reviewed the setbacks and concept plan and believes access can be achieved.
          Vice Chair White inquired as to who will be responsible for the garages and who will have any liability for damages to
the garage spaces. Mr. Brierley stated the homeowners association (HOA) for the site will be responsible for and liable for any
damages to the garages. This is one of the reasons that Planning staff required the lots in the project area be part of the HOA for
the Fenway Fields development.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-31, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Zoning Map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
                   the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as multiple family residential zone /conditional overlay zone (R-3/CO)
                   property heretofore classified as single-family residential zone (R-1-6), generally located at 139 North Wall
                   Avenue, and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
                   passage.”
          Chair Richey then called for public input regarding the proposal.
          Bob Giboney stated he is representing the Lynn Irrigation Company; the Company has a ditch that runs alongside the
subject property; the original owner of this property removed a fence that went between the property and the ditch, and that fence
has never been reinstalled. He referenced the concept plan presented by Mr. Brierley and stated the ditch is located directly below
the area labeled as ‘road’; he also identified the location of the headgate for the ditch and the location where the fence should be
erected. He stated that these matters are very important to the Lynn Irrigation Company and the users of the water in that ditch.
Access to the ditch and headgate must be preserved and the fence is needed to keep people and vehicles out of the ditch.
           Laura Lewis also addressed the site plan and asked if there are height restrictions for the garages to be built as part of the
project.
           No additional persons came forward to be heard.
           Chair Richey invited input from the applicant. Craig Buttars approached and discussed the history of the ownership of the
property; his intention was to buy the property as a long-term investment opportunity. He owns a good portion of the area
included in the Fenway Fields development and does not intent to sell it anytime soon. He has worked to evaluate many different
development options for the subject property, and it has taken him a great deal of time and effort to get to this point and he has
focused heavily on improving the parking accommodations for the current residences in Fenway Fields. Changing the zoning of
the property will help him to improve the current parking conditions. Additionally, moving the location of the garages was done to
make the project more aesthetically pleasing. He acknowledged Mr. Giboney’s comments and understands his concerns about
preserving access to the ditch and headgate; however, it appears to him that the ditch is located on the other side of the fence from
his property. He plans to install a six-foot vinyl fence along the property line, and he will work with Mr. Gibony to identify the lot
lines in the area to determine where the fence should be installed and where a gate can be installed – if needed – to provide access
to the headgate. He cares deeply about the property and the surrounding area and wants to pursue development that will have a
positive impact on the area. He is willing to work with other property owners in the area and looks forward to favorable approval
from the Council so that he can proceed with his project.
           Chair Richey asked Mr. Brierley to address Ms. Lewis’s question. Mr. Brierley stated in this case, a pitched roof is
required for the homes to be built and building heights can be no taller than those in the Fenway Fields project, which is
approximately 25 feet. The garage height cannot be more than 80 percent of the height of the single-family home.

Proposed Resolution 2024-13 approving an interlocal agreement between
Ogden City and West Haven City for ongoing monitoring and maintenance
of the property at 910 West 21st Street
          A memo from the Public Services Department regarding a proposed Post Closure Care Permit for 910 West 21st Street
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Ogden City and West Haven City are intending to enter into an
interlocal agreement for the purpose of a Post-Closure Care Permit for the ongoing monitoring and maintenance of the Moulding
and Sons Landfill located at 910 West 21st Street. The landfill was closed in 2008 and the property donated to Ogden City and
West Haven City shortly thereafter. The closure of a landfill requires a Post-Closure Care Permit which was not submitted to the
Utah Department of Environmental Quality when the landfill was closed.
          Public Services Executive Director Anderson summarized the memo and stated as the current owners, Ogden City and
West Haven City are responsible for the post-closure care and for applying for the permit. Ogden City owns approximately 80
percent of the landfill property with 20 percent owned by West Haven City. The permit application is being proposed as part of an
interlocal agreement between Ogden City and West Haven City with Ogden City being the lead agency. The agreement sets forth
the responsibilities of each party with regard to post-closure care. The agreement and the permit provide background on what the
property may be used for and what is required with the post-closure care. The post-closure maintenance is typically required for a
period of 30 years; however, the transmittal documents indicate that the City will apply for early termination after one year. The
post-closure plan provides additional background on the property and what is required with regard to inspections and reporting.
This includes quarterly inspections, annual reporting, facility maintenance, quarterly settlement measurements to ensure
stabilization, and adequate record keeping. The plan also sets forth uses for the site. The site is currently being used for storage of
rock, gravel, road base and other fill materials. No excavation is taking place on the property. Moving forward, the property may
be used for a limited number of uses including recreational and green spaces for public use, municipal uses, and specialized
structures and facilities. Any modification of the site, including new vegetation and trees, require review and approval to ensure
there is no disturbance of the materials under the cap. The permit also provides estimates for post-closure care costs. The plan
provides an estimate of approximately $11,500 of annual cost between the two entities. Mr. Anderson concluded that within one
year, Administration intends to petition the State of Utah for termination of the post-closure permit.
          Chair Richey asked if Administration expects the State to authorize termination. Mr. Anderson stated given the site was
properly capped and maintained, he believes the State will authorize termination within one to two years. Council member Graf
asked that Mr. Anderson inform the Council if and when termination is approved by the State. Mr. Anderson answered yes and
indicated that upon termination, there will be ongoing maintenance requirements for the property.
          Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2024-13, entitled:
                    “A resolution of the Ogden City Council approving an Interlocal Agreement between Ogden City and West
                    Haven City for ongoing monitoring and maintenance of the property at 910 West 21st Street.”
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF,
RESOLUTION 2024-13 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, HYER, MYERS, VICE CHAIR WHITE, AND CHAIR RICHEY. VOTING

Public Comments
          Heath Satow addressed the discussions during tonight’s work session; the Land Exchange Agreement (LEA) involving
Lotus is a positive improvement for the City, but he is disappointed there is a rush to proceed to approval of the transaction even
when there is some hesitation on the part of the Council. He asked who performed the appraisal for the land involved in that LEA.
In 2018, a Standard-Examiner article included comments from the City’s former Community and Economic Development
Director regarding the plans to relocate the Beehive Storage facility to the property. He asked if there has been any movement on
that matter since 2018. He then noted that he has emailed the Mayor and Council in recent weeks regarding a push to increase
donation limits for those seeking election to a public office; in his research he found that higher donation limits only benefit the
incumbent in a race and not the general welfare of a community. He added that the developer that is party to the Lotus LEA
donated the maximum amount to the Mayor’s campaign last year, though he is not saying there is a direct connection between that
donation and the current proposal. However, large donations do erode trust in government, and he hopes the Council will maintain
current donation limits.
          Laura Lewis referenced the transfer of property near 18th Street and Washington Boulevard near the river bridge; the
appraisal amount cited for the property was near $200,000. Several months ago, she asked who performed the appraisal and if
more than one appraisal was performed; if the property were appraised when the building was still located on it, it would have
been much higher. She feels the appraised value is low. She understands the City took payment of $250,000 for the property but
used $190,000 to purchased Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) property. She stated the City’s appraisal process is
undefined and vague and she is interested in a documented process the City must follow for future transactions. She then noted
that about a year ago there was a lot of discussion about the Wildlife Rehabilitation Center of Northern Utah and there was a push
to get them to move out of a City-owned building they occupied near Dinosaur Park. As of today, nothing has happened with that
property, and she asked why nothing is moving forward. She inquired as to plans and a timeline for the reuse of the property. Most
of the public felt the non-profit entity should have been allowed to stay in the building.
          Adam Jensen also expressed concern about the speed at which the Lotus LEA is moving; he asked why there is a rush
and asked that the matter we slowed down. He encouraged the Council to solicit input from the public regarding the matter.
          Scott Jensen echoed Adam Jensen’s comments and asked that the City respond to the concerns from the public. He stated
ever since a decision was made to proceed with the LEA with Lotus, his business has dealt with harassment from the City. He has
received notices of violations, though no violation exists. His business license was reclassified without his knowledge and when
he approached the Business Licensing Division of the City, they could not tell him why. They also do not have the history of his
business license past 2019. The reclassification has resulted in the code enforcement actions because City staff believes that he
cannot use his property as he is using it due to the change in his business license. He stated this activity is highly coincidental
given the acceleration of the Lotus development and he asked if the City is helping the developer to push him out of his property.
He has tried to make a deal for his property in the past and the City has never entertained them; now, even if he wanted to move,
he could not. Chair Richey asked Mr. Jensen the name if his business, to which Mr. Jensen answered Creative Welding.
          Teresa Bramwell stated she also shares concerns about the Lotus project. A few months ago, she attempted to research
the properties along the river and could not find anything owned by a company called Lotus; she found some properties were
owned by a company that has not paid their property taxes, LLC renewals fees, and they seem to be defunct. They purchased the
property from Ogden City in 2014 for a very low price, something close to $2.6 million. She would like to know the history of the
property, how Lotus acquired it, and the price they acquired it for because she would like to learn how much it has appreciated,
and the exact number of years Lotus has owned it. Her concern about them trading for property on Wall Avenue is that they will
build apartment units rather than housing for sale. She referenced the rezone of the rezone of the property near 2nd Street and Wall
Avenue near the Fenway Fields project and stated the public was initially informed that the units built there would be for sale
rather than rent, but the applicant tonight expressed his intent to maintain ownership of the properties and rent the units. If Ogden
City is going to have any say in development of property, they must be sure that the units being built are for sale so that young
people can invest in their community and ensure their own future wealth. She stated that she is also concerned about building
housing for homeless people in the community and she would like to know the developer that is pursuing that type of development
and if they have some sort of a land swap agreement with the City to give them access to cheap property, but rights to other
property where they can build high priced units.
          Chair Richey stated the majority of the questions raised during the public comment period were Administrative in nature
and he asked Chief Administrative Officer Brown to respond. Ms. Brown stated that relative to the Wildlife Rehabilitation Center
matter, she is willing to perform research and report back to the Council in a future work session. She offered the same response
for the questions regarding the Lotus LEA. Chair Richey addressed the public and stated the Council shares some of the concerns
that were communicated tonight; the Council is trying to gather additional information and will evaluate it before taking action on
the current proposals.
