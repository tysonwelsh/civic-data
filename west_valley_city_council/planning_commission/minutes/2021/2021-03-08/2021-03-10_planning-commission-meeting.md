Z-1-2021
Emanuel and Letica Moreno
7041 and 7055 W Gates Avenue
Zone change from A to M
0.88 acres

Emanuel and Leticia Moreno have requested a zone change on two parcels totaling 0.88
acres at 7041 and 7055 W Gates Avenue from A (agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre) to
M (manufacturing). Surrounding zones include A to the west, south and east and M to the
north. Neighboring uses include single family homes to the west, south and east and car
sales to the north. The subject property is designated as light manufacturing in the West
Valley City General Plan.

Back in October of last year, the City initiated an enforcement case on these properties for
several violations. Two of the violations were first, the properties were being used for a
stucco business without a license and second the business was operating in a zone where the
business was not allowed. A copy of the courtesy notice and notice of violation are included
with this report. If this zone change application is approved, the owners would then need to
apply for a conditional use permit and business license for the stucco business to bring it
into compliance. As part of the conditional use permit, a 6’ tall masonry wall will be
required along the west, south, and east sides of the property. If this zone change application
is denied, the stucco business will need to be removed from the site.

The home on the property at 7055 W Gates Avenue is being used exclusively as an office
for the stucco business. The home on the property at 7041 W Gates Avenue was recently
demolished and the owners are using this parcel as storage for the stucco business.

Over the last 10 years or so, the south side of Gates Avenue has gradually begun to
transition from residential or vacant property to commercial or industrial uses. Along 7200
W, 7-11 was constructed in 2012 and a shop/storage building at 7079 W Gates Avenue was
built in 2018. All of the property on the north side of Gates Avenue is zoned M. There are
three homes remaining on the south side of Gates Avenue.

When the property at 7079 W Gates Avenue was rezoned from A to M, the City entered into
a development agreement largely to protect adjoining residences. Some of the standards
were proposed by the applicant and others were proposed by staff. A copy of this
development agreement is included with this report. In summary, the development
agreement limited the hours of operations from 8 AM to 6 PM (proposed by the applicant),
required an entry feature on the north side of the building and limited the potential uses to
those allowed in the LI (light industrial) zone.


Since this development agreement has been in place, staff has received a number of calls
from potential tenants inquiring about the hours of operation limitation. If the Commission
believes that a similar limitation on the hours of operation is appropriate here, staff would
suggest 7 AM to 10 PM which aligns with noise regulations.

The proposed outside storage for the stucco business would only be allowed within the M
zone. In addition, the two properties together do not meet the minimum LI zone lot size of 3
acres. So, the intended use and size of the property would not work in the LI zone.
However, the Commission could include use limitations to minimize impacts on adjoining
residential uses.

The M zone requires a 20’ setback on sides adjacent to a residential use. Both parcels
include garages/sheds toward the south end of the property. Based on measurements from
aerial photos, neither of these structures meet the required setbacks. As part of the
development agreement, staff recommends that these structures be demolished or
reconstructed to meet the setback requirements.

Staff Alternatives:

      Approval. This zone change application should be approved subject to a
       development agreement that:
          o limits the hours of operation from 7 AM to 10 PM,
          o with the exception of outside storage, limits the uses to those allowed in the
              LI zone, and
          o requires the demolition or reconstruction of the two garages/sheds so that
              they meet the required setbacks.

ZT-1-2021
West Valley City
A zone text change to amend residential landscaping and recreation space standards in
Titles 7 and 9.

This application was continued during the Planning Commission meeting on February 24th,
2021.

At the request of the City Council, City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to the
residential landscaping and recreation space standards in Titles 7 (Zoning Ordinance) and 9
(Condominium Ordinance). After reviewing a rezone application for a townhome
development and then holding several discussions in study meetings, the Council asked staff
to revisit landscaping and recreation space standards and to clearly define what is included
in recreation space.

In recent years the City has seen more residential projects where space for recreation is
being provided in ways not previously seen including higher amounts of indoor space.
Accordingly, the change central to this application is the removal of the term “open space”
and its replacement with the term “recreation space”.

For reference the term “open space” is currently defined as: “landscaped areas that are not
occupied by buildings, structures, parking areas, streets or alleys and are devoted to
recreation use or preservation of natural features.” The proposed definition for the term
“recreation space” is:

a. outdoor space shared by residents of a residential Development for passive and active
recreation. Examples include terraces, Courtyards, plazas, shared roof decks, sports courts,
sports fields, open lawn areas, private parks, swimming pools, hot tubs, tot lots, picnic areas,
fire pits and trails.
b. indoor space shared by residents of a residential Development for passive and active
recreation. Examples include fitness rooms, libraries, computer labs, swimming pools, hot
tubs, sports courts, lobbies, clubhouses, social rooms, classroom space, community centers,
recreation centers, community game rooms and theater rooms.
c. private outdoor space provided only for the use of the residents of the Dwelling Unit to
which it is attached. Examples include private Yards, front porches, patios, balconies, decks
and roof decks.
Recreation Space shall not include: public Right-of-way; private curb, gutter, and roadway
improvements; Parking Spaces and maneuvering areas to Parking Spaces; all Building roofs
that do not cover a porch or that do not have a roof deck that is accessible for recreation use;
storm water retention areas; and storm water detention areas where the slope exceeds 30%
and the depth exceeds 2’ from surrounding areas.


Allowing indoor recreation features to count toward minimum recreation space percentages
will encourage interior features that have year round utility and are more costly than a
similar sized outdoor feature (example indoor sport court versus outdoor sport court). If
indoor recreation features don’t count toward minimum required percentages, there is less
incentive to a developer to include them.

In addition to this overarching change, the proposed ordinance also includes the following
changes:

      establishes minimum landscaping percentages for multi-family residential
       development that vary based on the project density, not the zoning,
      establishes minimum percentages for the combination of landscaping and recreation
       space for multi-family residential development that vary based on the project
       density, not the zoning,
      sets a tree requirement for landscaped areas outside of the minimum required
       setbacks and outside of parking areas,
      updates the definition of “Planned Unit Development” to reflect current practice,
      removes the standards for allowing small condominium developments as these are
       already addressed elsewhere in the code, and
      removes provisions from the Condominium Ordinance that are unenforceable.

At the Planning Commission meeting on February 24th, the Commission asked how the
proposed standards compare with other cities and asked if an on-site daycare could be one
of the amenities a developer could choose from. Included with this report is a table that
describes standards from other cities. The Commission could recommend to the Council that
an on-site daycare be included in the list of amenities in Section 7-11-412.


Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. The ordinance should be approved as proposed by staff.

   2. Approval. The ordinance should be approved as proposed by staff plus the addition
      on an on-site daycare as one of the amenities listed in Section 7-11-412.

   3. Continuance.

   4. Denial.

Lee Creek Subdivision
5993 West 2100 South
M Zone
2 Lots
32 Acres

BACKGROUND

Scott Thorsen, representing the property owner is requesting preliminary plat approval for
the Lee Creek Subdivision. The subject property is located at approximately 5993 West
2100 South. It is bordered on all sides with property zoned for manufacturing uses.


ISSUES:

The proposed subdivision is being requested in order to create 2 lots on 32 acres. Lot 1 will
be approximately 20 acres in size with lot 2 being approximately 12 acres in size. The land
use proposed for both lots is office/warehouse.

The name Lee Creek is derived from a creek that extends north from the Riter Canal. This
creek actually crosses 2 other properties, one being the Godfrey West Subdivision to the
south. The creek will remain in its current location and matters associated with wetlands or
flood plain do not appear to affect future building placement on either lot within the
subdivision.

Access to the subdivision will be gained by 5900 West. This right-of-way was constructed
by the Utah Department of Transportation and replaces the old SR-201 South Frontage
Road that now terminates just east of Godfrey Trucking. This new right-of-way curves to
the south in front of the proposed subdivision and then intersects at 2400 South.

At 2400 South, vehicles could go east over the Mountain View Corridor or south on 5900
West which provides access to vacant property and the Godfrey West Subdivision reviewed
by the Planning Commission in February. At the south boundary of the Godfrey West
Subdivision, 5900 West will make a connection with Beagley Road which then heads west
providing access to a number of large office/warehouse developments and eventually ends
up at 7200 West.

The road that UDOT built does not have formal improvements on either side. As a
condition of approval for the subdivision, the City will require curb, gutter and sidewalk
along the west right-of-way of said road. The City does not anticipate this road being
dedicated at the present time.

As mentioned earlier, the subdivision is traversed by Lee Creek. Future buildings within the
subdivision will be subject to FEMA Flood Zone requirements. The developer is working
with the Engineering Division and Salt Lake County to resolve and establish guidelines
related to those issues. In addition, portions of the subdivision are located in a perceived
wetland area. Studies will need to be done with the Army Corps of Engineers to determine
if these wetlands are jurisdictional. Based on the site plan that will be reviewed during this
meeting, the wetland or Lee Creek does not appear to affect building placement as
proposed.

Vacating all of 6655 West Orchard Hills Phase 2 Subdivision
6655 West Fuelner Drive (3635 South)

BACKGROUND

Mr. Ken Milne, developer of Newton Farms Subdivision, is requesting a street vacation for
all of 6655 West at Fuelner Drive.

The right-of-way to be vacated was platted as part of the Orchard Hills Phase 2 Subdivision.
This subdivision was recorded in 1996. The stub street was platted to provide access to the
Newton property to the south.

During the preliminary review of the Newton property – to be known as the Newton Farm
Subdivision, Mr. Milne inquired as to the City’s willingness to support the eventual vacation
of this stub road when the last phase of Newton Farms was submitted. After reviewing the
overall subdivision design for Newton Farms, the City agreed that vacating this right-of-way
would not be detrimental to the adjacent subdivision to the north and would provide for an
exclusive design with two cul-de-sacs. The City is currently reviewing the last phase of
Newton Farm, thus a recommendation from the Planning Commission is needed to vacate this
right-of-way.

The vacation is being requested for two reasons: First, due to the grade difference between
the Newton Farm Subdivision and the Orchard Hills Subdivision, there will be approximately
5-6 feet of grade difference at property line, as it is all along the north side of the Newton
Farm development. The second reason is that this phase will provide larger lots in two cul-
de-sacs, thus providing an opportunity for larger and more expensive homes.

As part of the vacation, Mr. Milne will be responsible to remove all existing improvements
in the right-of-way and will install formal improvements along the south side of Fuelner
Drive. The property owner to the west would like landscaping installed, while the property
owner to the east would like a drive approach in order to help access a future driveway to a
future detached garage. Mr. Milne will be installing the landscaping and drive approach as
mentioned above.

According to City Ordinance, streets and/or alley vacations shall be reviewed by the Planning
Commission with a recommendation to the City Council.

RECOMMENDATION

   1. Approval of the street vacation as requested.
   2. Continue the application due to issues raised during the Planning Commission
      hearing.

M Zone (32.7 Acres)

The applicant, CIR Engineering, is requesting a conditional use for an office/warehouse
facility at 5993 West 2100 South. This property is zoned M, Manufacturing, and the West
Valley City General Plan designates this area as Light Manufacturing. The surrounding
zones are Manufacturing (M). The surrounding uses are Godfrey Trucking to the west and
office/warehouse (Hines 201 MVP, Sansone) to the south. The new 201 South Frontage
Road and Mountain View Corridor roadway are to the north and east.

The following request is for approval of a 32.7 acre two-lot office/warehouse park. The
development will consist of two (2) buildings. The sizes are proposed to be 211,229 sqft for
building A/ lot 1 and 178,909 sqft for building B/ lot 2. The buildings will be constructed of
tilt up concrete panels that will be painted with a variety of brown/tan tones. The office
portions of the buildings are enhanced with large windows and relief in the façade and roof
line. These features will be repeated along the office portions of the building as well as on
both ends of the building. This facility is not required to meet the Commercial Design
Standards due to the use being limited to a manufacturing zone.

The general breakdown of the uses is proposed to have approximately 20,000 sqft of office
in each building (~10%). The uses/tenant mix will be monitored as building permits and
business licenses are submitted and the parking will be evaluated periodically to ensure that
the site is in compliance.

The required landscaping for this site is a minimum of 5%. The plan currently indicates
there is approximately 18% (lot 2) - 30% (lot 1) of the sites that is landscaped. This includes
a natural wetland area on the west side of the overall site, as well as more formal irrigated
landscaping. So those values will be further defined on the final plan set to verify
compliance. Landscaping will be provided along the east street frontage, the perimeter of
the buildings as well as enhanced areas by the office portions and parking areas. The north
boundary of the site is primarily wetlands so the formal landscaping is concentrated around
the developed areas and not the roadway frontage. As discussed in the study meeting, staff
also recommends additional landscaping be added to the perimeter of the site along all of
the improved drive aisles and parking as required in 7-9 of the WVC Municipal Code. The
wetland area will remain natural vegetation and will be maintained as part of the overall
association for the center.

The Mountain View Corridor frontage road pavement was installed by UDOT but the final
improvements including curb, gutter, park strip and sidewalk will be installed and
coordinated along with the subdivision.

Wall signage is proposed for the buildings. They may possibly provide monument style
directory signs throughout the site and a development sign on the frontage. However, the
details of signage have not been discussed at this stage in the development. All signage shall
comply with the requirements set forth in the Sign Ordinance and require a building permit
prior to installation.

SA-2-2021
Cabco 5600 West Subdivision – 3rd Amendment
2530 South Anna Caroline Drive
M Zone


BACKGROUND

The West Valley City Engineering Division is requesting preliminary approval for the
Cabco 5600 West Subdivision 3rd Amendment. This application will amend a portion of lot
1A, lot 2 and a portion of Anna Caroline Drive recorded as part of the Cabco 5600 West
Subdivision Lot 1 Amended.

ISSUES:

The application is being submitted at the request of the Engineering Division on behalf of
the Utah Department of Transportation. The purpose for the amended subdivision is to
revise the alignment of the unbuilt portion of Anna Caroline Drive within the Cabco 5600
West Subdivision Lot 1 Amended.

The Cabco 5600 West Subdivision was recorded with the Salt Lake County Recorder’s
Office in 2014. At that time, the construction of Anna Caroline Drive was postponed until
such time that a bridge was installed over the Riter Canal. Once the bridge was installed,
the owner of the Cabco 5600 West Subdivision would be required to build his portion of
Anna Caroline Drive.

In 2019, the Utah Department of Transportation recorded the Anna Caroline Drive
Subdivision which is located immediately to the north of the Cabco 5600 West Subdivision.
The UDOT application created a large lot for UDOT to build an operations center and
building for the storage of material. As part of the Anna Caroline Drive subdivision, the
north end of Anna Caroline Drive was constructed from the north property line of the Cabco
5600 West Subdivision and extended north to 2400 South.
Shortly after the approval of the Anna Caroline Drive plat, the City began preparations to
build the bridge over the Riter Canal. During the initial phases of the bridge project and as
survey work began, the City noticed that the portion of Anna Caroline Drive installed as
part of UDOT’s Subdivision was not built according to plans. The portion of Anna Caroline
Drive constructed by UDOT is approximately 3 feet to the east of where it should be.

The Engineering Division contacted UDOT and the owner of the Cabco 5600 West
Subdivision to discuss the problem. Since the owner of Cabco 5600 West was preparing to
build his portion of Anna Caroline Drive, a resolution to the problem needed to be worked
out sooner than later. The solution that everyone agreed upon, is to re-align the road
slightly to the east in order to make the connection of this road work. In order to do this,
lots 1A, 2 and a portion of Anna Caroline Drive will be amended.

The subdivision plat identifies the area of the existing Anna Caroline Drive that will be
vacated and identifies a portion of the Cabco 5600 West Subdivision that will accommodate
an easement for the new road alignment. Thus, the amendment of Lots 1A, 2 and a portion
of Anna Caroline Drive.
