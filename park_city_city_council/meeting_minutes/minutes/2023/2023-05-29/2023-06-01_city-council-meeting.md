Deer Valley Development Company Petition for the City to Vacate Portions of
Right-Of -Way on Deer Valley Drive West and South, and to Dedicate Doe Pass
Road to the City, as Part of the Snow Park Village Base Area MPD and Subdivision
Application. No Action Will Be Taken:
Mayor Worel noted Council Members Dickey and Rubell were excused from the
meeting. Deer Valley (DV) President Todd Bennett introduced the goal of creating a
base area that would reflect what was expected of a destination resort such as DV. He
indicated that to accomplish this, DV would need a right-of-way vacation across DV-
owned land and a transfer of 2.5 acres of the roadway from Park City Municipal. He
furthered that the three plans to be presented tonight would aim to improve
transportation and circulation, increase housing, and preserve public space.

Bennett identified a modern transit mobility hub that would increase transit options for
electric buses and provide efficient traffic flow to and from the resort during peak
periods; the development of a housing plan to help address the ongoing housing crisis
in Park City; and an improved Snow Park base area public park, fully connected bike
and walking trail, and other family-friendly amenities and gathering areas.

Jake Romney, Alterra Mountain Company Director of Development, reviewed the Deer
Valley Master Planned Development (MPD) and stated the special exception permit was
issued in 1978, and there had been 12 amendments to date. The total number of MPD
unit equivalents was 2,110 Units of Entitlements (UOEs), and the remaining Snow Park
Unit Equivalents were 209.75 UOEs. Romney furthered that the Planning Commission
reviewed traffic mitigation and recommended the City Council review the request for a
right-of-way (ROW) vacation in January 2023. As such, DV proposed improvements to
the area, including but not limited to the overall site plan and new roadway
configurations, as part of the conditional use permit (CUP) submittal. He felt the ROW

vacation was critical to the success of this plan and would include new drop-offs and
multiple arrival points, a world-class transit hub, and significantly elevated facilities for
all modes of transportation.

Romney displayed the previous plans. These plans allowed for 2000+ parking stalls and
condominium development or real-estate sales, but they were not ideal for many
reasons: longer walking distance; no tram/shuttle; no transit hub; did not remove
vehicular and pedestrian conflicts; did not align with Park City transportation goals; no
après ski experience; and no gondola from Snow Park to Silver Lake. Romney
presented the proposed plan, which would improve safety by removing pedestrian and
vehicular conflicts at the drop-off area, create a transit hub for six buses in a covered
garage with lockers, restrooms, a waiting area, and driver facilities, create a multi-use
path that would be safer and a better experience around the loop and across the new
plaza; align with Park City transportation goals; align with the Lower DV Neighborhood
General Plan; include roadway improvements for bikes, pedestrians, and buses, create
a world-class resort experience; après ski experience during the wintertime and après
bike in the summertime; a gondola from Snow Park to Silver Lake; and utility upgrades
along Deer Valley Drive West.

Mayor Worel asked what the intent of the original MPD was. Bennett responded that
there were no parameters in the likely event the property might be developed two to
three decades later than presumed. Previous plans solely focused on creating HOAs
and wealth from entitlements. Council Member Doilney inquired as to when the most
recent amendment occurred, to which Romney estimated it was in 2012. Council
Member Doilney inquired as to the history and scope of the amendments and how many
entitlements remained to be developed. Romney stated that initially, there were 2,110
entitlements, most of which had been used to develop HOAs. The remaining 209.75
UOEs were in the lower parking lots of DV and represented the last of the developable
land.

Romney introduced the traffic simulation for Deer Valley West. The simulator was set for
morning traffic on a peak ski day with a fully parked lot at 2,262 stalls. Council Member
Toly inquired about a school bus drop-off lane. Romney responded it would be up to the
City if school buses would drop within the transportation hub or drop off at the cited
location on DV Drive, and noted either location would work within this simulation.
Romney added that DV had been listening to the community and addressing where they
might be negatively impacted. He stated that In The Trees would have an additional
lane to merge into traffic as they entered and exited their property. Additionally, plans
also addressed another concern surrounding Doe Pass. At the juncture of the Y on
Solamere and Queen Esther, there would be a turn lane in the proposed plan to get up
to speed and into traffic. Council Member Doilney asked if the simulation considered
paid, reserved, or ICON parking. Romney stated that it simulated the year 2040 and
was set to register 2,262 stalls, the different uses around the property (hotel and
residential), day skiers, and projected background growth.

Council Member Toly inquired about the workforce’s need regarding projected growth
and if it was factored into the simulation and parking plans. Romney stated that both
were included in the design. Council Member Doilney noticed that the road was
significantly broader in the simulation and asked where the land to widen it would come
from. Romney identified that for most of the widening, there was enough land in the
ROW already owned by the City. Areas without land in the ROW would use DV property
to widen; however, in certain sections, the path would only gain two feet versus four feet
when it neighbored private property. Additionally, Doe Pass widening would occur on the
DV side of the property and would not infringe on the adjacent community.

Mayor Worel asked if the drop-off location would have three lanes of traffic. Romney
responded it would be three lanes, with DV employees helping to mitigate traffic flow.
Council Member Gerber asked if there would be a lane for parents to drop off their
children. Romney said that parent drop-offs were integrated into the current plan. Mayor
Worel asked if the City controlled whether property owners could build a road on their
property. Romney indicated DV would like to maintain ownership of the Doe Pass land
but would provide an access easement and ROW to the City so the public and City
could use it. However, DV would be responsible for maintaining the land. John
Robertson, City Engineer, said the road must be dedicated as an easement and meet
all City standards. Regarding maintenance, Doe Pass would need a level of service and
maintenance that would be challenging for the City to maintain. A dedication, rather than
an easement, would mean the City would be responsible for everything; however, if it
was an easement, DV would own the land and assume the responsibility of maintaining
it. Romney noted that DV was indifferent regarding land ownership of Doe Pass and
would support whatever decision was best for the collaboration.

Romney illustrated a one-acre park that would be built for the community to enjoy but
also would be maintained by DV. Mayor Worel noted that the next closest park was City
Park. Council Member Doilney asked if there were other resorts DV used as inspiration
when creating the proposed development plan. Romney stated that easy access and
proximity to amenities were crucial for a world-class experience. In addition, operating,
transit, and après experiences were all hallmarks of resorts providing the best guest
experience possible. Bennett added that there needed to be a sense of place and
vibrancy to maintain a world-class experience. Connecting the ski area to the village
would allow restaurants and retail shops to have a vibrant year-round business, instead
of seasonal. Bennett cited an example of another development created for year-round
guest experiences.

Council Member Toly asked how DV’s proposed restaurant and retail shops would
complement versus compete with those on Main Street. Bennett responded that it would
be an opportunity to create a mountain-adjacent, après experience complementary to
Main Street businesses and shops.

Council Member Gerber asked how the prospective development would meet the needs
of residents for years to come. Romney stated previous plans were created with a focus
on real estate. The proposed plan would allow DV to maintain ownership and rent
spaces, creating flexibility in the village atmosphere. Council Member Gerber asked for
clarification from staff regarding the previous questions concerning the City vacating
portions of the ROW as it applied to the whole, particularly if questioning needed to be
more specific toward the ROW. City Attorney Margaret Plane stated the Council’s
authority was on the ROW question; the conversation covered the project’s scope in
that they are inextricably intertwined. There were no concerns with the broader
questioning.

Council Member Doilney asked DV to address the “Protect the Loop” group’s concerns.
Bennett believed the proposed plan was an improvement. DV was incentivized to
provide the best possible plan because it would be a part of the community for a long
time. DV’s idea of great was operable and open for debate and discussion with Council.
Romney stated the ROW vacation would allow all features and amenities discussed to
occur. He identified that the two most impacted communities, Trails End and Powder
Run, issued letters of support for the project. They had access to their properties,
favored the proposal, and viewed the amenities as a positive living experience.

Council Member Doilney stated all the proposed plans to Council illustrated 2,250
parking spaces; however, present-day parking could accommodate 1,300 cars and
1,700 cars on peak days. He inquired how DV would account for the 550-car difference
in parking illustrated in the proposed plans. Bennett stated that using 10% of street days
for parking, DV could park 1,700 cars. Council Member Doilney inquired if DV discussed
paid or reserved parking to help curb the traffic flow of the extra 500 built parking
spaces. Bennett stated that the carrying capacity of the road from the Y to the traffic
circle in fifteen minutes would remain the same. DV had a peak load problem. For much
of the day, there was no traffic issue entering or exiting the end of the DV canyon. The
proposed plan would spread out peak load times. Regarding the number of parking
spaces, at the 90% percentile, or peak day, hotel and residential locations would
account for 150 units, 100 hotels, and 50 residential; by code, DV was required to build
parking for those spaces, and they did not affect peak load time traffic. DV requested
more parking, but they were open to discussing a plan where the parking,
transportation, housing, and public spaces would meet the goals of DV, the City, and the
community. Council Member Doilney furthered that the DV proposed plan, at best,
would mitigate or improve traffic impacts at some level. Romney stated that the plan
would mitigate impacts within the boundaries of their MPD.

Council Member Toly asked how many employees currently parked at the resort.
Bennett stated around 10% of employees parked on-site, equaling 300-400 employees
during peak season. Most employees did not park onsite, which would continue with the
proposed plan.

Mayor Worel asked whether studies had indicated if the two proposed traffic lights
would impact the roundabout and Y intersection. Romney said that with the addition of a
traffic light, there would be a metered element of control regarding the flow of traffic.
Mayor Worel inquired if there would be emergency access with the proposed plan.
Romney stated that DV received an approval letter from the Fire Marshall agreeing and
acknowledging that they favored the shared management lane and the emergency
access across the plaza.

Council Member Toly asked if the proposed parking plan would be 100% paid and
reserved parking. Bennett stated that it would be paid to park; however, DV was still
determining the plans for reserved parking. Reservations helped when there was a
significant peak load issue, but DV found that bi-parking with a shuttle and a variable
message sign (VMS) board at the Y had been helpful in mitigating traffic, as opposed to
reservations. Council Member Toly asked if the Traffic Monitoring Management System
(TMMS) would factor in the additional 500 parked cars based on the 2,250 parking
spaces available. Romney stated they were considering full parking and would not
consider TMMS; however, DV was open to continued discussions on this topic.

Council Member Toly asked if other HOAs had come to DV with concerns. Romney
illustrated that most concerns surrounded property access and the loop as they
experienced it today. In The Trees inquired about access, and Lakeside brought up the
road’s proximity to their property. Council Member Toly inquired if DV would be
interested in a shuttle or microtransit to the DV neighborhoods that did not currently
have bus systems. Bennett stated systems were already in place for their DV HOA and
rental management programs. DV was open to exploring the idea further. Council
Member Toly asked how many additional vehicle trips per day, based on the simulation,
would occur. Romney stated that during the morning peak time there were
approximately 100 additional trips, and in the evening, just over 200 additional trips.

Council Member Toly asked if the state code regarding material injury concerning the
proposed ROW vacation was foreseen as an issue. Romney indicated he would like
legal representation to address that question entirely, but DV felt their plan would uphold
the state code concerning the ROW vacation.

Mayor Worel asked if DV could bring more information regarding the state code and the
ROW vacation to the next meeting on June 15th, to which Romney agreed. Mayor Worel
asked if the fully developed proposed plan would use the remaining 209.75 UOEs.
Romney stated the proposed plan would only partially exhaust the remaining UOEs; 60-
70 UOEs would remain and could be used in Lot Five to develop 60 residences or
townhomes if DV chose to do so. However, the traffic studies had been created as if the
209.75 UOEs had already been exhausted.

Council Member Doilney asked for follow-up information on when the 12 MPD
amendments happened and what the details included. Mayor Worel reminded the public
