Ms. Sherri Ohrn , Herriman City Council, stated on the Council agenda today is a 
resolution relating to the annexation of the Olympia Hills development into Herriman City .  
The resolution associated with this has language that is concerning to her.  The language 
encourages Herriman City to adopt all the provisions included in the current Master 
Development Agreement (MDA) between Salt Lake County and the developers of the 
Olympia Hills development.  Herriman City has the option to create its own MDA and will 
probably seek advice from the County relating to this.  The language sends the wrong 
message to the city.  She asked the paragraph relating to this be stricken before the 
resolution is adopted.

3.3 Update on Salt Lake County ’s Coordinated Response to 
COVID-19
21-0889
Attachments: Staff Report
Presenters: Dr. Angela Dunn, Executive Director, Health Department and 
David Schuld, COVID Coordinating Officer (Approx. 30 mins). 
Informational
Ms. Angela Dunn , Director, Salt Lake County Health Department, stated 
she has not put forward a mandate for children to wear masks.  She is 
looking into finding the best way to protect children under the age of 12.   
She then delivered a PowerPoint presentation regarding the Pandemic of the 
Unvaccinated. The PowerPoint showed that the 7-day case rate increased by 
133 percent in two weeks; the case rates and vaccination coverage by 
Council district; there are 235,064 children under 12 who are not eligible to 
be vaccinated; more than 60 children will be infected per day if something is 
not done; the percentage of cases hospitalized; that medical systems are 
stressed as the peak surges; the comparison between COVID -19 and 
Influenza; the mask mandates decrease COVID -19 transmission; that school 
infections were driven by high community case rates; with preventative 
measures, children do not spread to each other; that 31,198 middle and 
elementary children were infected during the 2020-21 school year; that over 
11,500 children less than 12 years old were infected; that 58 children less 
than 12 years old have been hospitalized; and 27 children have developed 
Multisystem Inflammatory Syndrome (MIS-C) due to COVID -19 infection.  
She stated the goal of the Salt Lake County Health Department is to provide 
in-person learning safely.

3.4 Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD) 
Presentation Regarding WFWRD ’s Desire to Reorganize from 
a Special Service District to a Local District
21-0895
Attachments: Staff Report
WFWRD Board Letter to SLCo Council and Exec 
Summary-Re-organize to a Local District
Local District Reorg UT Code 17D-1-604
Sponsors: Council Member Jim Bradley and Council Member Aimee Winder 
Newton
Presenters: Pam Roberts, Executive Director; Councilman Scott Bracken, 
Board Chair; and Rachel Anderson, Legal Counsel (Approx. 30 mins). 
Informational
Some members of the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District 
Administrative Control Board who were present or online introduced 
themselves. These included: Scott Bracken, Sherrie Ohrn, Brint Peel, Diane 
Turner, Dwight Marchant, Daniel Gibbons, Kris Nicholl, Anna Barbieri, and 
Keith Zuspan. The attorney for Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District, 
Rachel Anderson, also introduced herself. 
Ms. Pam Roberts , General Manager, Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling 
District (WFWRD), stated she was here on behalf of WFWRD ’s 
Administrative Control Board to request the Council reorganize the WFWRD 
from a special service district to a local district.  This reorganization was 
recently allowed under new state statute. With this proposal, nothing would 
change as far as service to over 86,000 homes, which are part of the district .  
All municipalities and townships have signed off on this proposal showing 
they support and accept this change. 
Mr. Scott Bracken , Chair, Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District, 
stated the change in the state statute allowing WFWRD to become a local 
district would allow the board to make all decisions without having an extra 
step of coming before the Council.  
Council Member Snelgrove stated he wanted to go on record of 
supporting this change.  Taxpayers deserve an efficient and well -run 
organization.  By being independent, WFWRD can shop for the best deals 
for its customers.
