59   Jacob Curtis presented the item. The way the lot is zoned would only allow seven lots to be
60   created. Due to road connections, an additional lot has been allowed as density in lieu, but the
61   developer is asking for one more additional lot. Symphony Homes developed an adjacent
62   subdivision in 2024 in which two lots were combined into one, and they would like to move the
63   additional lot over to this development.
64
65   Commissioner Everett asked if the developer or the purchaser combined the lots in the previous
66   development. Jared Schmitt, representing the applicant, clarified it was the developer that
67   combined the lots.

71   reimbursed for these.

74   would include sidewalks, curb, and right-of-way, and that the current plans are to assign them to
75   the HOA (of the existing neighborhood, which they hope to include this new development in) for
76   maintenance. He displayed the subdivision adjacent to the property and explained that they are
77   planning on matching the style to integrate into the current neighborhood. The previous
78   subdivision was approved for 98 lots but reduced to 97 on behest of a client. He showed
79   examples of the homes built there as well as the amenities in the area. They are asking for
80   additional lots in order to make them a more traditional shape. Commissioner Eyre asked if there

204   3.4) Public hearing and consideration of Steven Kelly’s request for approval of exceptions
205   to the building elevations for the Ember Hotel site plan located at 26 East Main Street.
206
207   Jacob Curtis presented the item. Two exceptions are being requested: to allow siding with a
208   historic-style mural where the wall is visible, rather than having it be brick, and lowering the
209   glass requirement to 36% rather than 50%, as there are concerns with fire and power equipment.
210
211   The applicant, Steven Kelly, was present. He explained that the power boxes are located close to
212   the front of the building on one side, and thus that glass needs to be fire grade, and can’t match
213   the initial design. He can’t make the current windows larger as they are located where the shower
214   units are in the hotel. He also showed how most of the side of the building is obscured by another
215   building. He showed an example of what the wall would look like without the brick exterior of
216   the rest of the building.
217
218   Commissioner Lockhart clarified that the side of the building that is mostly obscured is due to a
219   previous Planning Commission decision.
220
221   Commissioner Everett asked if it was possible to put any more glass on the street-facing side of
222   the building. Kelly explained that due to the location of walls in the hotel, and code requirements
223   for awnings, there is currently no way to get more glass on the first floor of the building.
224   Commissioner Everett asked if there could be more glass on upper floors of the building. Glass
225   requirements only apply to first floors.
226
227   Kim Struthers explained that getting power to the building was difficult. The next door neighbors
228   wouldn’t grant an easement, so the power has to come from across the street.
229
230   Commissioner Kunze argued that she interprets the glass requirement as a way to break up the
231   front façade of a building, and she feels that the parking lot entrance arch does that well enough
232   without adding more glass.

234   The architect, Mr. Gilford, was present. He asked commission to consider the use of the building
235   as a hotel, and to understand that larger windows in the bathrooms will become a privacy issue.

237   Commissioner Lockhart asked about the signage of the building. In addition to the mural, there

375   3.8) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for a review of a
376   Development Code amendment to Table 05.040-A Residential Bulk and Intensity, reducing
377   the setback for remodels and additions in the RA-1 and R-1-15 zones.
378
379   Jacob Curtis presented the item. Very minor changes have been made. Superscripts have been
380   added to indicate allowances to certain zones. This is on the behest of City Council.
381
382   Commissioner Lockhart asked why setbacks can be changed or ignored after certain periods of
383   time. Kim Struthers explained that it encourages people to age in place by providing flexibility
      Lehi City Planning Commission                     8                                      July 31, 2025
384   for people who may otherwise need to move. Commissioner Lockhart asked if this change was
385   being made for a single applicant. Struthers explained that it is not; the goal was to make
386   setbacks more equitable across zones. He clarified that this part of code doesn’t get utilized very
387   often.
388
389   The commission discussed if the determined waiting periods made sense in relation to the stated
390   goals of the change. Commissioner Eyre asked if the setbacks are dependent on the type of
391   addition. They are not. Commissioner Lockhart doesn’t think the argument of aging in place is
392   valid if the setbacks change after two years. Commissioner Everett clarified that the change is to
393   adjust all zones to the same requirements. Commissioner Jackson suggested more research and a
394   work session overview.
395
396   Commissioner Lockhart recommended changing setbacks so that they are consistent. The council
397   discussed the merits of changing the code versus completely overhauling it. City staff mentioned
398   that changing setbacks may encourage the construction of detached ADUs. Commissioner
399   Lockhart argued that arbitrary setbacks are a violation of landowner rights. She thinks that
400   setbacks should have firm reasoning behind them. The commission discussed the origin of
401   setbacks (New York City, where they were implemented to ensure sunlight still made it to the
402   street with the advent of skyscrapers), and their current purpose.
403
404   Commissioner Jackson opened and closed public comment.
405
406                   Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to give a negative recommendation to the
407                   Lehi City Council with the findings that while the intent to conform with other
408                   areas of the code is appreciated, this update is not needed, doesn’t solve problems,
409                   and creates more questions than it answers. She recommends staff and City
410                   Council to look at setbacks overall, with the intent to keep them the same from the
411                   beginning in order to be consistent in what rights are allotted to property owners.
412                   Commissioner Lockhart seconded the motion.
413
414                   Vote: Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner

419   3.9) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a Development
420   Code amendment to Chapter 12, to add requirements for retaining walls.
421
422   Jacob Curtis gave a brief overview of the item before turning it over to Gary Ellis. The goal is to
423   increase safety. The biggest change is that rock and boulder walls are no longer allowed if they are
424   over four feet tall. Up until now, the city has had no specifications or guides for developers to
425   consult before building.
426
427   Commissioner Lockhart asked if the code applies to retaining walls under four feet. It does not.
428   Walls are measured from the base to the height of the retained earth.
429



      Lehi City Planning Commission                    9                                      July 31, 2025
430   Commissioner Kunze asked if there had been any issues with retaining walls. There have been in
431   Traverse Mountain. Gary Ellis explained that there is worry over improperly constructed walls,
432   especially when it comes to earthquakes, landslides, and erosion.
433
434   Commissioner Everett asked why landscaping walls were not allowed to be more than four feet.
435   Ellis explained that there needed to be a line drawn somewhere, and it ended up at four feet.
436   Anything taller will need to be approved for a building permit. Commissioner Everett didn’t like
437   adding restrictions to walls built for aesthetic purposes, but understood regulating walls built for
438   structural stability. Ellis clarified that four feet is the standard code for surrounding cities.
439
440   Commissioner Jackson opened and closed public comment.
441
442                   Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to give a positive recommendation to the
443                   Lehi City Council with the findings that the proposed development code
444                   amendment will increase the safety of development on and near hillsides by
445                   requiring higher levels of engineering with retaining walls, the proposed
446                   development code amendment is in conformance with the purposes intent and
447                   provisions of the General Plan and its various elements. He included all DRC
448                   comments. Commissioner Kunze seconded.
449
450                   Vote: Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner Everett, no. Commissioner
451                   Eyre, yes. Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes.
