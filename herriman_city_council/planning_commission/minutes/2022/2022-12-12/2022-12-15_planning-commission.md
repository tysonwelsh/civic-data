City Engineer Bryce Terry discussed the draft master transportation plan update, process, and timeline,
which were based on the new General Plan. Jeremy Searle from WCG presented roadway project phases
to maintain acceptable traffic operations. He reviewed the recent annexation and demographic
projections from the Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC). The travel demand model had been
updated to reflect Herriman projections (which exceeded the WFRC projections). The baseline Level of
Service (LOS) capacities and roadway cross sections for minor collector, minor arterial and principal
arterial roads were compared to future conditions models. Possible U-111 realignments and extensions
were being studied by UDOT.

Commissioners cautioned against funneling too much traffic onto Rosecrest Road, 6400 West and 13400
South. Commissioners also expressed 12600 South was already a high traffic road. They recommended
expanding 11800 South. Community Development Director Blake Thomas informed the Commission
traffic studies would be conducted to pursue roadway expansions and improve traffic flow. Mr. Searle
acknowledged the lack of east/west traffic roadways were an issue. Minimum build projects to
accommodate traffic for the Olympia development, and proposed 2030 and 2050 Level of Service build
projections were provided. Impact fees would be utilized to fund some roadway construction in the next
six to ten years. A light rail extension to 13200 South area was identified as corridor preservation.
Commissioners recommended the reduction of zoning densities, if possible. Commissioners were
encouraged to forward additional comments to Planning Manager Spencer.

1.4. Olympia MDA Amendment Discussion — Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
Community Development Director Blake Thomas reviewed the proposed MDA amendments. The items
being considered included a reduction in secondary water in specific zones. Configuration of the
proposed intersection at Ulll and 12600 South to be partially within Herriman City. The PID modification
regarding fees included a request to discuss options with Council. MDA amendments currently required
a signature from a Jordan School District representative. This proposal requested removal of that
requirement if they were not impacted. Director of Public Works Justun Edwards provided secondary
and culinary water requirements based on lot size and reviewed water requirements for zones in the
development.

2. Adjournment

The Commission adjourned the work meeting by consensus at 6:52 p.m.

Herriman Springs at 15100 Spring Canyon Drive in the FR-1 (Forest Recreation) zone
Applicant: Jake Hone with Toll Brothers (authorized agent)
Acres: +209.85
File Number: C2022-013
Planning Manager Spencer reviewed the request for consideration of a Conditional Use to amend the
Planned Development of the Cove at Herriman Springs. The approved conditional use permit and
preliminary subdivision plat vested the development of 645 residential lots. According to City records,
423 of those lots have been recorded, which left 222 lots to be developed. Within the undeveloped
portion of the PUD, the applicant proposed amending the lot configuration, lot frontages, road layout,
open space, architecture, and entry monumentation. The intent of the proposal was to reconfigure the
lots into a more efficient layout, and no additional lots have been proposed. The new layout would
preserve an additional fifty acres of open space, improve road configuration, and a more efficient
layout of public utilities. The applicant also added additional architectural design elevations, extra trail
connections and a trailhead parking lot. The city had no traffic concerns. This development required
additional services, including a water tank, pump station and pipelines prior to construction. Per the
applicant’s proposal, nearly eighty percent of the proposed lots were over one-third of an acre. In
response to feedback on the proposal, the applicant agreed to include at least three larger lots
adjacent to the existing development as a transition area. The applicant also proposed three additional
architectural designs—Mountain Modern, Prairie, and Farmhouse—and new fencing requirements for
the remaining 222 lots in the PUD. The proposed amendments would require high-quality
architectural elements that would complement the existing homes in The Cove. Two monument signs
were proposed, and the developer agreed to limit the lighting to the hours of 6 a.m. to 10 p.m.

4.4 Discussion and consideration of a conditional use amendment for signage on the Lone
Peak Hospital Emergency Center located at 15306 S Fort Herriman Parkway in the
MU-2 (Mixed Use) zone
Applicant: Kevin Anderson, R&O Construction (agent)
Acres: +3.52
File Number: C2022-154
Planner Howa reviewed the request. On August 4, 2022, the applicant received conditional use
approval to install two internally lit roof signs and one internally lit wall sign adjacent to residential
development. The wall sign which was proposed and approved was to be constructed of channeled
letters and graphics. Due to the design and small size of the channeled letters and graphics, the sign
manufacturer could not fabricate the sign. The applicant requested to amend their original approval to
modify the design of the wall sign to an individual flat-cut lettering and graphic design. The lettering
and graphics would be constructed of three-eighth-inch aluminum, and would be externally lit. The
lighting fixture would be surface mounted to the building and positioned to face-light the signage,
directing all the light toward the building and away from adjacent residential properties.
Applicant Representatives J.D. Drozbeck and Jeff Krantz expressed appreciation for the City
partnership and offered to answer questions.
Commissioner Powell confirmed additional signs would be considered at a later meeting.

amendment for signage on the Lone Peak Hospital Emergency Center located at 13506 S Fort
Herriman Parkway in the MU-2 (Mixed Use) zone with the following six staff recommendations:
1. Receive and agree with the recommendations from other agencies.
2. No additional wall signage shall be installed on the north facade.
3. Signage is approved as proposed, and any modifications shall be submitted to the City for
review to ensure compliance with applicable standards.
4, Lighting fixtures shall be shielded and project all light toward the sign copy.
5. Lighting fixtures shall be mounted above the sign and directed down and towards the
building and ground.
