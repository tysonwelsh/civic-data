Open and Public Meetings Act Annual Training — Attorney Lee Edwards indicated
Utah law requires this training. Edwards said a meeting is defined by Utah law as a

meeting of a public body convened with a quorum present. There are exceptions for
social or chance gatherings. Edwards would place parades in that category. What if
UAC is doing training and there is a quorum present? Worthen responded the Council
should not try to make decisions, etc. at the training. Edwards also said, for instance,
they should not group together after the meeting to discuss options presented at a
UDOT meeting and decide on the best option. Edwards recommends scheduling the
next training after the legislative session because the Office of Legislative Research and
General Counsel publishes an update with any changes to the Open and Public
Meetings Act made during the session. The county is doing a good job of noticing public
meetings with specificity. Edwards told Council members they may send any questions
in an email to him.

MEMORANDUM 25 September 2018

To: County Council
Subject: Design Exception request for a future amendment of the Tom Pitcher Lot Split Subdivision

This is the second request made by Brad and Joni Schumann for a Design Exception for an extension
to 960 West, a dead end private road accessed from SR 218. This second request is to allow a
private 400-foot driveway that would serve three additional single family dwellings. The first
request was included as part of a multi-subdivision request for a private road serving 14 single
family dwellings. That request was denied by the Council and the developer that requested the
exception has since redesigned their lot layout for their subdivision to comply with the standards of
the Road Manual. The Schumann’s have submitted this request specific to their property.

With the addition of a 400-foot driveway, the total length of 960 West would be approximately 900
feet. The purpose of the 400-foot extension is to provide access to three additional buildable lots
(Exhibit A) for a total of seven dwellings on 960 West. The Cache County Manual of Roadway
Design and Construction Standards (Road Manual) does not allow dead end roads longer than 500
feet nor does it allow private roads to provide access for more than three (3) single family dwellings.

The Road Manual section 1.8 Authority and Design Exceptions, specifies that:
“Full justification and documentation must be provided explaining the reasoning as to why the
roadway standards cannot be met, why an alternative design or construction method can meet the
intent of the roadway standards, and including any other relevant information.”

The submitted request for Design Exception provides the following reasoning:
1. The applicant's preferred lot layout may pose a safety hazard if access is from 800 West.
2. The existing private road, 960 West, has been built to a Local Road standard and can
accommodate additional traffic without improvement.
3. UDOT has approved 960 West as an access to SR 218 for up to 15 dwellings.

The Development Services staff and Director have completed a review of the Design Exception
request and have identified the following:

1. The Road Manual requires that roadways serving 4 or more single family dwellings must be
located on a Local Road, a County facility. Private roads are permitted to serve up to 30
average daily trips, which equates to three residences.

The applicant is requesting the addition of a driveway that would serve 3 additional single
family residences. Exceptions have been allowed for previous requests to allow private road
that serve more than three single-family dwellings in a subdivision, including the Birch
Hollow Subdivision which has four lots on a 400 foot long private access easement across
Lot |. However, the County Road Department has discouraged the continuance of this
practice, and roads serving more than 3 single-family dwellings must be on public roads.
The County does not provide maintenance on private roads and is not accepting any new
county roads under Resolution 2015-20. Logan City Environmental also no longer provides

3. The Manual does not permit dead-end roads longer than 500 feet unless it is a temporary
dead-end road that is intended to become a through road in the future and meets other
requirements. Further, per the definition in the Manual, private roads are not intended to be

this location, and the proposed dead-end private road does not follow a typical county road
grid pattern.

4, Per the Manual, county developments that take access off of a roadway under a municipal
jurisdiction must obtain express written approval from the municipality for access and the
development must meet all road standards and requirements of the municipality.

5. As to the applicant provided justifications:

a. The claim that the possible subdivision amendment layout is unsafe does not limit the
land owner in identifying a different lot configuration with access from 800 West that
considers the safety of persons in the area. This layout appears to be the most convenient
for the developer. It is not good practice for the County to issue a Design Exception
strictly for the convenience of a developer.

b. The claim that the existing private road, 960 West, has been built to a Local Road
standard and can therefore accommodate additional traffic without improvement does not
address the additional requirements of the Road Manual.

i. The Road Manual allows for a temporary dead-end road longer than 500 feet if
that road is intended to become a through road in the future. Private roads or
driveways are not intended to be through roads.

ii. Roads serving more than 3 single-family dwellings must be on public roads. The
County does not provide maintenance on private roads and is not accepting any
new county roads under Resolution 2015-20.

iii. Logan City Environmental also no longer provides collection services on new
private roads due to safety and efficiency concerns as well as the fact that private
roads tend to be more easily damaged by the large collection trucks.

c. The claim that UDOT has approved 960 West as an access to SR 218 for up to 15
dwellings is accurate, however, UDOT highway standards and review are specific to
UDOT highways, and in this case do not direct or alleviate the requirements of the Road
Manual.

Therefore, the Director recommends that the Council deny this request for a Design Exception, as:
1, The request for a Design Exception from the standards of the Road Manual failed to provide
sufficient justification, analysis, or documentation in order to allow access for three
additional single family dwellings from the private road 960 West.

implications of his proposed 15-home subdivision located at 960 West, and Hwy 218, as required by
the Utah Department of Transportation as a condition of their granting a private driveway access to
this state highway.

driveways. The highway itself has approximately 2,000 average vehicles per day (ADT). The proposed
subdivision of 15 homes would generate between 150 and 200 trips per day, which would be about 15-
20 peak-hour trips (assuming 10% of daily trips are in the peak, which is very typical).

SECTION 2.24.040 RELATING TO OFFICERS’ BONDS

WHEREAS, Utah Code Section 17-16-11 provides that the legislative body of each county
shall prescribe the amount of each general fidelity bond or of theft or crime insurance to be acquired
for county officials, except the county treasurer, which is governed by the State Money Management
Act and rules promulgated thereunder, as the same may be amended; and,

WHEREAS, Cache County has an interest in updating the County Code to conform to the
current Utah Code; and,

Cache County and its citizens,

If the County elects to have County officials execute bonds, the bonds shall meet the

A RESOLUTION OF THE COUNTY COUNCIL OF CACHE COUNTY
UTAH ELECTING TO PROVIDE OFFICIALS WITH CRIME
INSURANCE PROVIDED BY UTAH LOCAL GOVERNMENTS TRUST

WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code Section 17-16-11 the Cache County Council has
prescribed the amount of each general fidelity bond or of theft or crime insurance to be acquired for

Cache County and its citizens,

1. Cache County elects to meet the requirements of Utah Code Section 17-16-11 with
Crime Insurance provided by Utah Local Governments Trust, an interlocal
cooperative agency acting as a public agency insurance mutual.
2. This Resolution shall be effective immediately upon its adoption.
