4.     CONSIDERATION AND VOTE on a Conditional Use Application for More than One
       Home Occupation per Residence in Compliance with the Bluffdale City Code Title 11-
       23-2 for a Beauty/Salon Services Located at 15282 South 2200 West. Sheena
       Schwemmer, Applicant. (Application 2020-10).

Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison presented the staff report and stated that a public hearing was
held on March 4, 2020. The matter was then tabled to allow for additional information to be
provided by the applicant, who was not present. Mrs. Robison reported that a home occupation
requires a business license. City Code specifies the requirements including a Conditional Use
Permit, which is required if there are two home occupations operating from the same address. The
existing home occupation, Natures Hardwood Flooring, is owned and operated by the husband of
the applicant, Sheena Schwemmer. Mrs. Schwemmer was proposing to operate a tooth whitening
business called Luminescent Smiles. Mrs. Robison provided an aerial overview of the subject
property. Both businesses will operate out of a building that is detached from the home.

The matter was tabled because there were questions from the neighbors about parking.
Mrs. Robison stated that there would be adequate parking on the property that would preclude on-
street parking. The application was determined to meet all City's requirements to allow for a
second home occupation at the same address.

Ms. Schwemmer reported that the majority of her business will take place outside of her husband's
business hours. If there is overlap, she will have only one or two clients at a time. At most there
would be only one or two additional cars parked on the property. Mrs. Schwemmer stated that her
clients will be asked not to park on the street.

It was confirmed that a maximum of two home occupations are allowed at the same address.
Concerns were raised regarding whether the parking issues had been adequately addressed.
Mrs. Schwemmer confirmed that business hours will be 4:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m., Monday through
Friday, and 8:00 a.m. to noon on Saturday. The intent was for the business to open as soon as social
distancing requirements are lifted and salons are allowed to operate.

Mrs. Robison reported that no emails were received on the application. City Attorney, Todd
Sheeran indicated that it was not necessary to conduct a second public hearing tonight.

6.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on a
       Proposed General Plan Amendment Application for 4.50 Acres from Very Low
       Density Residential to Multi-Family Residential Located at Approximately 13975
       South 2700 West. Howland Partners, Applicants. (Application 2020-11).

Mrs. Robison presented the staff report and identified the location of the subject property, which is
east of the Midvalley Bible Church. She next reviewed the City's Land Use Map, which had not
been modified in many years. The subject property is designated as Very Low-Density Residential,

which is typical on the west side of the City. The intent was to maintain the rural character of
Bluffdale and provide animal rights and buffers for surrounding property.

The request was to change the land use designation from Very Low-Density Residential to Multi-
Family Residential. Property in Multi-Family Residential areas can have densities of 4 to 20
detached or attach~d multi-family dwelling units per acre. It is a broad designation in the General
Plan to allow the City to look at areas to in which to allow additional types of land uses in the
future. The General Plan was intended to be a forward-looking document based on anticipated
growth and development.

Mrs. Robison reported that a determination should be made as to whether the Planning Commission
feels the City needs multi-family opportunities in different areas of the City. She clarified that the
present discussion was not about zoning but about policy and direction for the future.

City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, reported that there is a multi-step
process involved in moving forward with development. The City currently does not have a zoning
category for senior housing or stand-alone townhomes. The next step would be to develop specific
approaches to the type of use designated in the General Plan.

Mrs. Robison reported that the Midvalley Bible Church approached the City Council about two
years ago asking for a similar type of development. It was determined by the City Council to not be
the right time. The Midvalley Bible Church was very supportive of the proposed action.

In response to a question raised, Mrs. Robison stated that the closest multi-family residential zoning
is The Bluffs. Commissioner Walston asked if UDOT has a tentative timeline for completing the
interchange mentioned earlier. Mr. Crowell responded that it has been the focus of a lot of
discussion but no date had been set. UDOT was currently working on several interchanges on
Bangerter Highway.

Commissioner Brown stated that Commissioner Luker indicated that his primary concern was
ensuring that the property is protected for senior living. Since the City currently does not have a
zone for that type of development, he would like to see a designation for senior living. In response
to a clarifying question from Mr. Crowell, Commissioner Brown stated that Commissioner Luker
was in favor of senior living in this area. If the current project fails, there should be no changes to
the proposed use on the subject property. Mr. Crowell stated that if the City Council agrees, the
City Attorney would need to draft a document to that effect.

Mrs. Robison explained that the City currently does not have a zone dedicated to senior housing. A
zone would need to be created or the applicant would have to apply for a Special District Zone. If
the City were to create a zone for senior housing, it could be applied City-wide. Mr. Crowell
commented that it would be erroneous to assume that just because the General Plan is changed, the
zone change would be automatic. The process is much more complex. The applicants were seeking
direction.

Commissioner Loumis noted that in the past it has been difficult to designate senior housing and
living centers. He emphasized that housing built for older residents needs to be designed for an

maximum density of eight units per acre.

Mr. Sheeran explained that the applicants are trying to change the Land Use Map. When they apply

Dana Howland made a PowerPoint presentation and stated that when Howland Partners purchased
the property from the Midvalley Bible Church, the intent was to expand their commercial property.
They were advised by the City Council to meet with the neighbors and get input. The neighbors
were not interested in more commercial development and suggested the concept of patio homes for
those over 55. Patio homes are all single-level buildings that require minimal maintenance. The
belief was that they would be a good transition from commercial to residential one-acre lots. The
property is landlocked, so it would be extremely difficult to develop as one-acre lots.

Mr. Howland provided renderings of the proposed single level homes. Potentially, there could be a
bonus room above the garage to accommodate visitors. The amenities would be geared toward an
older population. The proposed development would provide a better transition to residential than
the back of a flex-office facility. Another benefit of development geared at the 55-and-over
population is that it would add to the tax base without placing an additional burden on schools.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Howland stated that 75 to 80 percent of those who attended the
neighborhood meeting supported the proposal. Their priority was to enable the church to
accomplish its goals, expand the parking area, and make good use of the land. They also want the
neighbors to approve of the development. The developers desired to move forward as soon as
possible.

Commissioner Loumis, a lifetime Bluffdale resident, stated that his primary goal has been the health
and welfare of the City. The youth and elderly were also of concern to him. He expressed support
for the proposed senior living project. Mr. Howland reiterated that their priority is to build a
product that will appeal to the senior population. Commissioner Loumis urged Howland Partners to
concentrate on a product they would be proud of and that would serve the population well.

Commissioner Brown expressed her strong support for the proposed project.

Commissioner Flynn asked about the average size of each unit. Mr. Howland stated that they will
range in size from 1,450 to 1,550 square feet on the main floor. Their goal was to develop 30 units
on the 4.5-acre property.

Commissioner Walston asked about the interface between the subject property and the neighboring
business development. Mr. Howland stated that they anticipate constructing a concrete precast wall
around the subdivision, including a gate. Commissioner Walston considered the subject property to

7.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on a
       Proposed General Plan Amendment Application for 1.96 Acres from Commercial to
       Multi-Family Residential Located at Approximately 14643 Camp Williams Road.
       Quail Ridge Bluffdale Development LLC, Applicants. (Application 2020-08).

Mrs. Robison presented the staff report and stated that about 20 years ago, the City Council felt that
Redwood Road would become a significant commercial corridor. When the Planning Commission
and City Council revised the General Plan in 2014, they did not feel it was necessary to change the
Land Use Plan. The City Council addressed how they would like to see commercial development
take place throughout the City. The City Council identified five commercial nodes and were
adamant that they serve as the locations for commercial development. The nodes were identified as
follows:

       1.       The Gateway at Redwood;

       2.       2700 West;

       3.       The interchange at 14600 South;

       4.       The area where there was already a Maverik; and

       5.       Porter Rockwell Corridor in the distant future.

In 2014, the City did not foresee some of the major changes that have occurred. Of note was the
imminent completion of Porter Rockwell Boulevard and the impact on 2700 West. The Gateway
Zone was developed for large commercial development. That development was just now starting to
materialize. It was noted that sometimes general plans take a long time to manifest themselves as
anticipated, and unexpected changes occur. Within that context, the Planning Department met with
the City Council over the past year to monitor economic development and growth in the City to
create a Strategic Plan to realize some of the goals identified in the 2014 General Plan.

The City Council developed priorities in light of the changes that have taken place. The first was
the result of the significant development that has taken place at the 14600 South interchange. The
City Council made commercial development and economic growth a priority around that

                                                  10

interchange. The Gateway at Redwood is also high on the City Council's priority list for
commercial development. A close third and fourth priority was development at 2700 West and
Bangerter Highway and the type of commercial development that might take place on the Porter
Rockwell Boulevard, which will be a high-traffic corridor.

Mrs. Robison explained that the subject property was originally identified as Commercial. Below
the site is property that is still zoned R-1-10 Residential. The applicants were seeking to apply the
zoning to the subject property for residential lots. The subject property was approved for
subdivision as R-1-10 (I-0) Infill Overlay. The applicants were moving forward when the question
came up about the idea of letting the City have the option of putting a road through the subject
property on 14600 South to Redwood Road. That road connection was determined to be an
important part of the City's Transportation Plan.

There was some question as to whether the property would be viable for multi-family development.
The current zoning structure does not account for that type of development. The City Council
intended to place commercial where it can make a difference. The subject property does not appear
to meet that objective. The applicant was seeking to change 1.96 acres to allow for multi-family
development.

Mrs. Robison explained that Infill-Overlay allows for property to have different standards than the
stand-alone zone. It is only permitted in the R-1-10 zone and allows for potentially different
setbacks, design standards, or road cross-sections. The R-1-10 zone retains a minimum lot size of
10,000 square feet. The Infill-Overlay Zone has been applied in other areas of Bluffdale. The
applicants asked to amend the General Plan to a multi-family type of land use. If approved, an
ordinance would have to be crafted to allow a smaller type of multi-family zone. It was noted that
the subject property is too small to qualify for a Special District Zone.

Chair Cragun asked if the 1.96-acre property would be viable for commercial use. She confirmed
that its topography is problematic. Mrs. Robison responded that it would not be a particularly
desirable location for commercial development. Mr. Crowell stated that developers ask about other
places in Bluffdale for commercial development. No interest had been shown in the subject
property for commercial development. It was noted that COVID-19 has had a significant impact on
commercial development.

Mr. Crowell stated that the City was awarded a $75,000 matching grant from the Transportation and
Land Use Connections Program. The intent was to use the funds to examine the viability of various
development priorities.

Andrade Christensen from Quail Ridge Bluffdale Development, LLC reported that in 2017 they
presented the Quail Ridge project to the City. The development would come off of 14600 South
and include 10 lots that would be flat and never touch Redwood Road. The City then asked if a
partnership could take place to construct a road from 14600 South to Redwood Road. They
consequently sold the City the needed property at cost. That sale of property impacted the project
by reducing it from 10 single-home lots to six. The six lots have numerous topography issues,
which makes developing it as commercial difficult.

She displayed the current General Plan map and noted that Spring View Farms was approved in

to allege spot zoning. He did not believe that the proposal involves spot zoning.
