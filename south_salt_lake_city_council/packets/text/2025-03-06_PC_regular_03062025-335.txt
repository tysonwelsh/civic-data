VARIANCE PROCEDURE AND STANDARD OF EVIDENCE TO CONSIDER:

17.12.010 Variances.

   A. Where strict compliance with the provisions of this Title would cause an unusual and unnecessary
      hardship on the Applicant because of peculiarities regarding the size of the tract to be Developed,
      its topography, the condition or nature of adjoining areas or the existence of other unusual physical
      conditions, the Applicant may petition the Community Development Director for a variance from
      such provisions.

   B. Granting variances. The Community Development Director may grant a variance only if:

      1. Literal enforcement of the Land Use Regulations would cause an unreasonable hardship for the
         Applicant that is not necessary to carry out the general purpose of the Land Use Regulations;
      2. There are special circumstances attached to the property that do not generally apply to other
         properties in the same district;
      3. Granting the variance is essential to the enjoyment of a substantial property right possessed by
         other property in the same zoning district;
      4. The variance will not substantially affect the general plan and will not be contrary to the public
         interest; and

                                                     4
       5. The spirit of the land use ordinance is observed, and substantial justice done.

   C. Variance determinations. In determining whether or not enforcement of the land use ordinance
      would cause unreasonable hardship under Subsection (B)(1), the appeal authority may not find an
      unreasonable hardship unless the alleged hardship:

       1.     Is located on or associated with the property for which the variance is sought; and
       2.     Comes from circumstances peculiar to the property, not from conditions that are general to
              the neighborhood.
       3.     In determining whether or not enforcement of the land use ordinance would cause
              unreasonable hardship under Subsection (B)(1), the appeal authority may not find an
              unreasonable hardship if the hardship is self-imposed or economic.
       4.     In determining whether or not there are special circumstances attached to the property
              under Subsection (B)(1), the appeal authority may find that special circumstances exist only if
              the special circumstances:
              a.      Relate to the hardship complained of; and
              b.      Deprive the property of privileges granted to other properties in the same zoning
                      district.

   D. The Applicant shall bear the burden of proving that all of the conditions justifying a variance have
      been met.

   E. Variances run with the land.

   F. The Community Development Director may not grant a Use variance.

   G. In granting a variance, the appeal authority may impose additional requirements on the Applicant
      that will:

       1.     Mitigate any harmful effects of the variance; or

       2.     Serve the purpose of the standard or requirement that is waived or modified.

Conclusions of Law:

   1. Without a variance, the applicant’s use as proposed, does not meet the required minimum lot size
      requirements in the Commercial Corridor Land Use District. The minimum requirement is 1 acre and,
      the proposed lot size is 0.8 acre.

   2. The applicant has not provided sufficient evidence to show an unreasonable hardship associated with
      this property. An unreasonable hardship refers to difficulty in complying with a zoning ordinance
      because of circumstances unique to the property and not to conditions general to the area. A
      hardship may not be self-imposed or solely economic. Smaller parcel sizes are not a unique
      circumstance or characteristic of property in South Salt Lake or that particular area. The proposed
      newly combined property is a self-imposed, economic hardship where compliance with the Code
      may be achieved through acquiring more properties.

   3. The applicant has not provided sufficient evidence to show any special circumstances attached to the
      property. Special circumstances refer to physical conditions unique to the property, which relates to
      the hardship, and make compliance difficult or impossible. The adjacent lots are developable, there
      are not any circumstances that additional acreage, site planning and development cannot overcome,
      therefore circumstances are not of difficulty or impossibility, but rather economic.



                                                      7
   4. The applicant has not shown that granting a variance for the lot is essential to the enjoyment of a
      substantial property right possessed by other property in the same zoning district. The properties
      have existing, permitted uses as an auto repair and service and a restaurant that may continue, similar
      to nearby properties.

   5. The applicant has shown that the variance would not violate the goals of the General Plan, however,
      the variance would be contrary to the public’s interest as other future new developments within the
      Commercial Corridor that have sufficient property or have acquired sufficient property to meet the
      minimum 1 acre lot size requirements. If this variance is granted, the minimum lot size requirement
      may become an unfair burden on other future developments that are combining lots.

   minimum lot size requirements in the Commerical Corridor Land Use District. The

   hardship associated with this property. An unreasonable hardship refers to
   difficulty in complying with a zoning ordinance because of circumstances unique

   self-imposed or soley economic. Smaller parcel sizes are not a unique
   circumstance or characteristic of property in South Salt Lake or that particular
   area. The proposed newly combined property is a self-imposed, economic
   hardship where compliance with the Code may be achieved through acquiring
   more properties.

3. The applicant has not provided sufficient evidence to show any special
   cirumstances attached to the property. Special circumstances refer to physical
   conditions unique to the property which relates to the hardship, and make
   compliance difficult or impossible. The adjacent lots are developable, There are
   not any circumstances that additional acreage, site planning and development
   cannot overcome, therefore circumstances are not of difficulty or impossibility,
   but rather economic.

4. The applicant has not shown that granting a variance for the lot is essential to the
   enjoyment of a substantial property right possessed by other property in the same

RE:    Variance Application

Mr. Weidenhamer,

Wendy’s Restaurant has the NE corner of 3300 South and 200 East which includes multiple
parcels Parcel 1, 16-30-327-012, Parcel 1, 16-30-327-032-2000 & 16-30-327-032-2001, Parcel
2, 16-30-327-015, and Parcel 2 16-30-327-016 under contract to remove the existing buildings
and build a new Wendy’s Restaurant building. All of the parcels on the site are owned by the
same owner and the area totals 30,858 sq ft (0.708 acres). See Wendy’s South Salt Lake City
Property Exhibit, there is enough property (0.708 acres) to build the Wendy’s. There will be
access onto the existing 50’ existing driveway on the east side and a driveway from 200 East.
Using those two (2) accesses points there will be no new access made on 3300 South.

In your ordinance 17.03.050 Commercial Corridor (CC) District, B-1 states “The minimum area
for a new non-residential Lot in the district is one (1) acre.” The owner of the parcels does not
have 1.0 acre. There are two buildings on the property, one has been vacant for years and the
other is an auto shop. The approval of the Wendy’s would clean up the corner and remove two
old buildings one with hazardous materials. The owner of the parcels has asked Syufy
Properties Inc. if they would be willing to sell the driveway easement to the east or sell 58.98 ft
of property on the north side. There is no interest from Syufy Properties in selling property on
either side.

The request for Variance is because the Commercial Corridor zoning (1) acre of property cannot
be accomplished. The South Salt Lake requirements for Granting Variances require that the
following five (5) questions be answered:

    1. Literal enforcement of the Land Use Regulations would cause an unreasonable hardship
       for the Applicant that is not necessary to carry out the general purpose of the Land Use
       Regulations.

       The property owner does not have one (1) acre of land (0.708 acre). The owner has
       asked to purchase more from neighbor and has been turned down. The Wendy’s can be
       built on 0.708 acres. Since there is an existing ingress/egress access on the east side of
       the property approximately 50 ff x 136 ft – 0.156 acre makes the site look like there is
       more than one (1) acre associated with the property. The ordinance creates an
       unreasonable hardship with the owner not owning one (1) acre.

2      There are special circumstances attached to the property that do not generally apply to
       other properties in the same district;

         5684 S. Green Street ● Murray, Utah 84123 ● (801)713-3000 ● Fax: (801) 713-3030
       The special circumstances are that there are currently two (2) old buildings that would
       be removed with one having hazardous (auto shop) materials and old cars leaking fluid
       on the pavement. There is an existing open easement 50 ft wide on the east side, of the
       property, which will continue to be an existing ingress/egress driveway. That driveway
       has been in operation for over 20 years making it a prescriptive easement. The site is
       also on a corner with good exposure for Wendy’s.

3.     Granting the variance is essential to the enjoyment of the substantial property right
       possessed by other property in the same zoning district.

       Granting the variance would clean up the corner and enhance and bring a fast-food
       restaurant to the stagnant corner. It would be beautified with a new landscape and new
       building. It would bring customers to the area and potentially more traffic flow for
       other neighbor commercial stores.

4.     The variance will not substantially affect the general plan and will not be contrary to the
       public interest; and

       There will be no impact on the surrounding area. It will only be a plus. It will bring
       jobs to the area along with patrons that will also see the area. There will be no impact
       to the general plan or the Commercial Corridor. The area consists of commercial
       buildings that are on small parcels of property. It is very hard for owners of the
       property to do much when they must have one (1) acre to revitalize.

5.     The spirit of the land use ordinance is observed, and substantial justice done.

       The spirit of the land use ordinance will have no impact. The property is very close to
       one (1) acre. With open land to the north, which will be developed at some time, and the
       existing ingress/egress on the east, there will be no notice that there was ever a variance
       requested. If the request is denied, then there will be no improvement of the corner by
       Wendy’s.

The property owner and Flynn Corporation, who is the franchise for Wendy’s respectfully,
requests the approval of the Variance for the property owner.

CONDITIONAL USE REVIEW STANDARD:
Utah Code Ann. § 10-9a-507(2) sets forth the following standard of review for conditional uses by stating:

    (a)     (i)       A land use authority shall approve a conditional use if reasonable conditions are proposed,
                      or can be imposed, to mitigate the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of the
                      proposed use in accordance with applicable standards.
            (ii)       The requirement described in Subsection (2)(a)(i) to reasonably mitigate anticipated
                       detrimental effects of the proposed conditional use does not require elimination of the
                       detrimental effects.
    (b)            If a land use authority proposes reasonable conditions on a proposed conditional use, the land
                   use authority shall ensure that the conditions are stated on the record and reasonably relate to
                   mitigating the anticipated detrimental effects of the proposed use.
    (c)     If the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of a proposed conditional use cannot be
            substantially mitigated by the proposal or the imposition of reasonable conditions to achieve
            compliance with applicable standards, the land use authority may deny the conditional use.

South Salt Lake Municipal Code §17.05 sets forth the following standard for review for conditional uses by
stating:

   A. A person seeking approval of a Conditional Use must file a Complete Application, using the forms
      established by the Community Development Department, and include payment of all fees. For any
      Application to construct a Building or other improvement to property for a Use that is defined by this
      Code as allowed in the zoning district in which the Building is proposed, the Community Development
      Department and the City Engineer must review the Application to determine whether the proposal:
          1. Is allowed within the district where it is proposed;
          2. Is proposed for Development on a legally subdivided Lot;
          3. Can be adequately serviced by Dedicated roads, improved to City Standards and existing or
              proposed utility systems or lines;
          4. Complies with all applicable Development requirements of that district, including Building
              Height, Setbacks, and Lot Coverage;
          5. Meets the applicable Development Standards requirements;
          6. Conforms to the Design Guidelines and the design review process established for that district;
          7. Requires additional conditions of approval;
          8. Complies with the Construction Codes; and
          9. Pertains to land for which all tax assessments have been paid

   B. The Community Development Department staff shall notify the Applicant of any specific deficiencies in
      the proposal.

Staff recommends the Planning Commission approve a Conditional Use Permit, for Car City, an Auto Dealership,

Findings of Fact:

   1. On October 7, 2022, Edgardo Martinez on behalf of Car City submitted a Conditional Use Permit
      application to amend the existing Conditional Use Permit (“CUP”)

          a. The existing CUP is C-16-033.

          b. It was approved on August 4, 2016.

   2. The subject property is located at 3165 South State Street.

   3. The applicant applied for a subdivision application on October 7, 2022, to consolidate the subject
      properties by plat to comply with South Salt Lake Municipal Code. The preliminary subdivision plat
      went before the Planning Commission for approval on March 6, 2025;

   4. The site is approximately 1.4 acres.

   5. The parcel is located in the Commercial Corridor land use district.

   6. The land is not currently part of subdivision lot, but the applicant has submitted an application to
      consolidate the parcel into a lot.

   7. The business will have access from State Street and 3185 South.

   8. Section 17.03.010 of the City Code classifies Auto, Light Truck, RV, Boat, Trailer Dealerships (Sale, Lease,
      or Rent) as Conditional Uses subject to Planning Commission approval.

   9. Section 17.05.040(E) of the City Code requires all Auto Dealership businesses adjacent to residential
      districts to have design review approval from the Planning Commission.

Conclusions of Law:

    1.   The use, as specifically conditioned below, is consistent with the South Salt Lake City General Plan.

    2.   The use, as specifically conditioned below, is compatible in use, scale, and design with allowed uses
         in the Commercial Corridor land use district.

    3.   The use, as specifically conditioned below, does not compromise the health, safety, or welfare of:
         Persons employed within or using the proposed development; Those residing or working in the
         vicinity of the proposed use or development; or Property or improvements in the vicinity of the
         proposed use or development.

    4.   As there are no environmentally sensitive lands on site, no impact to environmentally sensitive lands
         is expected to occur as part of the business.

    5.   The use, as specifically conditioned below does not impose disproportionate burdens on the citizens
         of the city.

    6.   The use, as specifically conditioned below, meets the standards found in Section 17.05.010,
         17.05.020, 17.05.030, 17.05.040, 17.05.050, and 17.05.060, as outlined above.

    7.   No specific hazards or adverse impacts are expected due to the proposed use.

Conditions of Approval:

The Following Conditions Shall Be Met Prior to Any Business Operations Can Take Place:

    1. The applicant shall comply with all of the conditions of approval below and within the Conditional Use
       Permit prior to the release of a South Salt Lake building permit;

    2. The Auto Dealership shall meet all requirements of the South Salt Lake Community Development
       Department, South Salt Lake Fire Marshal, the South Salt Lake Building Official, and the South Salt
       Lake Engineering department;

    3. The applicant shall comply with all federal, state, and local laws, including the development standards.

    4. The applicant shall maintain a minimum of 23 off-street parking stalls on-site, excluding the display
       stalls;

    5. The applicant shall maintain a minimum of 2 off-street parking stalls on-site, for display;

    6. The applicant shall maintain the 8-foot fencing along the residential property line boundaries;

    7. The applicant shall maintain the 10’ landscape buffers along the single-family property line boundaries;

    8. The applicant shall maintain the 8’ landscape buffers along the non-single-family residential use
       property line boundaries;

    9. The applicant shall remove any trash and solid waste that builds up on the property;

    10. The applicant shall not use the panic button to find cars on the lot;

RECOMMENDATION:
Staff recommends the Planning Commission forward a recommendation of approval to the City Council for
the ordinance amending section 17.10.120 of the South Salt Lake City Municipal Code (“Title 17”), creating
a standard road profile for Haven Avenue from State Street to Main Street in the Downtown District.

BACKGROUND:
In 2020, as part of the comprehensive Land Use and Development Code rewrite, “requirements for
improvements and design” were adopted in Title 17. 1 Specifically, standard road profiles were established
to ensure minimum road profiles are met as part of a Subdivision or Condominium Plat.
On April 14, 2022, the City Council adopted
Ordinance No. 2022-08, establishing standard road
profiles for Rights-of-Way (ROW) in the Downtown
District. The image to the right is taken from section
17.10.120 and shows the road profiles for the
following streets:

      •   State Street (2100 S to I-80)
      •   300 West (2100 S to I-80)
      •   Downtown Local Street
      •   Main Street (2100 S to I-80)
      •   West Temple (2100 S to I-80)
      •   Utopia (Main to Trax (200 W))
With the redevelopment in Downtown South Salt
Lake, property platting will occur. Requiring ROW
dedication in this process is essential as it enables
the City Engineer to design and uphold ROW
improvements with uniformity and quality.
This Ordinance Amendment aims to establish an
additional Standard Road Profile in the Downtown
District for Haven Avenue between State Street and
Main Street (see image on the following page).




1
    Ordinance No. 2020-02
                                                          1
The proposed standard road profile for Haven Avenue between State Street and Main Street will establish a
“right-sized” local street with the following elements:

       •    58-foot ROW
       •    11-foot sidewalks with trees
       •    Dedicated public street parking on the north
       •    Two 11-foot travel lanes




In the next six months, the Market Center project will start to take shape at 2280 S. State Street, adjacent to
Haven Avenue. The project developer plans to establish a four-lot subdivision as part of the entitlement
process. Adopting the Haven Avenue standard road profile will ensure that proper right-of-way dedications
are in place for all future public improvements.
ANALYSIS: GENERAL PLAN CONSIDERATIONS
The South Salt Lake General Plan 2040 was adopted in 2021 and directs the development and growth of
the city through goals and strategies that support the city’s community and economic values. Additionally,
the General Plan identifies the neighborhoods of South Salt Lake, including Downtown. The General Plan
identifies the Downtown Neighborhood as a regional mixed-use center. 2 The following goals and strategies
align with the purpose of establishing a standard road profile for Haven Avenue:

       •   The General Plan states: “Public streets and sidewalks should be well maintained and enhanced for
           public use.” Community Facilities Goal 1, Strategy 2 advocates for development that provides
           services in a logical, orderly manner such that adequate streets can be provided efficiently and
           economically.
       •   The General Plan also promotes enhanced connectivity within neighborhoods. Specifically, it
           establishes an implementation strategy that mandates sidewalks in and between new developments
           along well-connected streets.


2
    South Salt Lake General Plan 2040, p. 18-19.
                                                           2
   •   The General Plan further directs support for creating planning and zoning tools that promote quality,
       neighborhood-oriented development and revitalization. Implementing standard road profiles is a
       method that ensures roadways meet the city’s standards.

Downtown South Salt Lake is a key area for redevelopment and reinvestment. As this neighborhood
evolves, the General Plan supports efforts to enhance its health, vitality, and overall integrity. This
ordinance ensures that public improvements to the ROW along Haven Avenue in the Downtown
Neighborhood are high-quality and consistent with the City’s development goals in the area.
