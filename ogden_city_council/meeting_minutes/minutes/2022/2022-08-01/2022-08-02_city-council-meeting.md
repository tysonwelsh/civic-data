A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023 budget came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated the Council will consider adoption of the final FY2022-2023 Budget which includes Schedule A-
1 changes and a proposal to increase the certified tax rate by 18.83 percent. The tax rate increase will generate just under $3
million in additional property tax revenue that will be used for increases to employee salaries and benefits and to fund additional
positions, including five police officers, a police grant manager, two homeless outreach coordinators, six fire fighters, a recreation
supervisor, and a sustainability coordinator. After discussions, the Council requested that an option to reduce the proposed tax
increase by two percent, to 16.82 percent, be placed on the agenda for consideration. The Council stipulated that any adjustments
to the budget could not impact employee salary and benefit increases which are a priority for the Council.
An additional memo from Council staff regarding employee salary schedules came before the Council for consideration.
The memo stated the Council will consider an ordinance adopting the proposed FY2022- 2023 Salary Schedules for all non-
elected employees. The proposed schedules shift current ranges to accommodate the proposed cost of living increases as follows:
e Sworn Fire 14% Cost of Living Adjustment (COLA).
e General Employees and Officers 13% COLA.
e Sworn Police 8% COLA.
The total cost of the wage and benefit changes for all funds, including proposed new positions and elected officials, is
$13,874,975.

A final staff memo regarding salary schedules for elected officials came before the Council for consideration. The memo
stated the Council will consider an ordinance adopting the proposed FY2022- 2023 Salary Schedule for all elected officials. The
Administration proposed a cost of living increase consistent with the COLA proposed for the general employees—13%. This
increase met the requirement of Ogden Municipal Codes §2-2-3 and §2-3-6 in place at the time. On July 12, 2022, the Council
amended these sections to allow the Council to consider other options. The Council has requested two options for consideration.
Option A adopts the salaries for elected officials with no increases over the current rate. Option B adopts the salaries for elected
officials with a 4% increase over the current rate.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
the proposed budget; she offered a comparison of the FY2022 adopted budget with the FY2023 proposed budget, indicating there
ina 16.58 percent change, or a $35.7 million increase. The originally proposed Schedule A-1 to the budget would have increased
revenues and expenditures by $1,455,600 for the following:

e Added Sustainability and Recreation Positions ($201,900)

e Funded Matching Funds for Water Conservation Program ($400,000)
e Increased Art Program Funding ($15,000)

e Adjustments to Other Funds ($838,700)

During the last public hearing held regarding the budget in July, many residents suggested the Council consider utilizing
or generating other revenue rather than approve a property tax increase; she addressed other revenue sources currently utilized by
the City, including property taxes, sales/use tax, franchise taxes, fees in lieu, licensing permits and fees, intergovernmental
revenues, charges for service, fines, and miscellaneous. The subtotal of tax revenue available to the City is $55 million and
property tax is just 25 percent of that amount. The sub total of other revenue sources is $23.7 million. Total general fund revenues
are $78.9 million. In FY2021, the City received $15 million in COVID related grant funds. She added that some residents also
asked for more details about certain items in the budget; she noted it is important to understand that the Council has spent the last
six weeks studying the specifics of each Departmental budget and it is difficult to reduce all of that information into a short budget
presentation, but she touched on a few budget highlights. First deals with employee costs, including cost of living increases for
employees and the creation of 26 new employment positions. Second deals with acceptance and utilization of American Rescue
Plan Act (ARPA) funds, and thirds is funding for strategic plan projects. She expounded briefly on each of these three priorities,
after which she moved to focus on the proposal to increase property taxes and the relationship between taxes and property values.
The average home in Ogden increased in value by 34.43 percent from 2021 to 2022; residents can dispute or appeal their property
valuation with the Weber County Assessor and Weber County Clerk Auditor. The State Law governing property taxes is designed
for revenue neutrality, plus ‘new growth’; Ogden City’s current rate is 0.002397, and accounts for approximately 17.5 percent ofa
resident’s total property tax bill. She presented a chart illustrating the history of the City’s property tax rate over the last 20 years;
the City’s rate has actually declined steadily as it was being adjusted downward to ensure the City received the same amount of
property tax revenue from year to year. The County recommended a certified tax rate of 0.001940, which would generate $16.3
million in revenue. The current rate is 0.002397 and the Mayor has proposed a certified tax rate of 0.002306; this is actually lower
than the current rate, but higher than the recommended rate from the County, which means it must be advertised as an increase.
The proposed rate would generate $19.3 million in property tax revenue. Administration and the Council have considered
opportunities for cutting costs rather than raising taxes; this includes opportunities such as performing certain duties in house,
using technology to automate programs and processes, pursuing energy savings, and leasing vehicles/equipment rather than
purchasing. However, inflation has wreaked havoc on the City’s budget; the cumulative inflation rate from 2002 to 2022 is 62.5
percent, yet tax revenues have only grown by 16.91 percent in that same time frame. She then discussed the alternative property
tax proposal requested by the Council; Administration has agreed to adjust the tax rate to 0.002267, or 16.82 percent, which will
generate an additional $2.6 million in revenue. This will reduce the burden on taxpayers by approximately $10 per year. If the
Council wishes to accept this proposal, it is necessary to adopt an alternative Schedule A-1, which would accomplish the
following:

e Increased Revenues and Expenditures By $1,343,100 (-$112,500 difference from Original A-1)

e Reduces Property Tax Revenue ($330,805)

e Reduces Expenditures — Elected Official Salaries and Various Accounts Within Each Department ($112,500)
e Uses Fund Balance for one-time expenses ($306,250)

Ms. Eller-Smith concluded by summarizing the options available to the Council tonight; they can approve option one,
which includes the original proposed budget and Schedule A-1, or option two which includes the original proposed budget with a
substitute Schedule A-1. Alternatively, the Council could also amend the proposed budget and consider a different tax rate or
continue the public hearing to a future date.

annual salary shall be adjusted by the percentage approved by the City Council as a Cost-of-Living Adjustment (COLA) for non-
merit special employees in the annual pay plan adoption. The Council amended the ordinance in July to indicate that “beginning in
