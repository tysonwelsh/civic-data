PUBLIC HEARINGS
1.      Residential Site Grading Permit – 4932 South Waimea Way – (R-1-10 Zone).
Community Development Director, Paul Allred presented the staff report and stated that the
request is for a retaining wall to be topped by a fence. The proposal is similar to other
applications that have been approved in the same neighborhood. Retaining walls are allowed
taller than two feet but must be reviewed by the Planning Commission. In this case, the
applicants are proposing a six-foot wall topped by a six-foot fence that is very similar to the
property to the south. The proposed wall is essentially an extension of the wall next door. The
intent is to flatten out the backyard. The staff report recommended approval.

The applicant, Nick Tanga expressed his desire to level out his backyard. He explained that his
wife is an avid gardener and making the backyard flat will make it safer for her to get around.
His neighbor to the south recently did something similar, which he would like to do as well. The
only difference is that his wall would only extend two-thirds across the back of his property.

Because one abutting other neighbor to the west was not aware of what is proposed, there was
hesitation to make a ruling. It was suggested that Mr. Allred contact them, (Wright) and inform
them of the request, get input from them and then bring the matter back for a decision at a future
meeting.

Chair Ricks opened the public hearing.

Rick Jensen lives directly west of the subject property. He was present at the last meeting and in
an effort to be a good neighbor, expressed his backyard approval for his neighbors, the Winkles,
to do something similar. He stated that in his backyard his view is now a six-foot concrete wall,

Mr. Jensen stated that the Winkles have been very accommodating. They apologized and
indicated that they did not know what the end result would be so tall and he believed them.
Another similar request has now come forward that would extend across the rest of Mr. Jensen’s
backyard and result in yet another wall of concrete topped by a fence.

Mr. Jensen stated that the one advantage is that it will at least be uniform. He wanted assurance
that his trees are not going to continue to be compromised as they have been through the years.
He explained that they have been chain-sawed off on the backside. The inside of the trees has
also been removed and they will die. In addition, the project will require his fence to come
down. Mr. Jensen wanted assurance that if the request is approved, his fence will be put back in
the same fashion as it is currently. He would not want the integrity of his fence to be
compromised. He stated that several things have happened that have been of concern. For
example, wet concrete flowed under the fence onto his property and is now in his garden area.

Mr. Allred explained that walls and fences are supposed to be measured from the grade that
existed before the land was manipulated. The rules are very specific that a fence from the
natural grade shall not be taller than six feet. By choosing to construct a retaining wall, it may be
topped but cannot exceed a total height of eight feet for both fence and wall combined and any
such wall/fence directly abutting neighbor must agree to the additional height. In this case, if a
retaining wall were not proposed on the property to the south, the maximum allowed under the
current ordinance is eight feet and only if any abutting property owner agrees to it in writing.
Mr. Allred was concerned that they have already exceeded the maximum. If the matter is
continued, he recommended that staff be given an opportunity to discuss the matter internally
and investigate the situation further. Mr. Jensen stressed that the situation was not done
maliciously and the Winkles have been very good neighbors. He did not object to what is
proposed with stipulations. Procedural issues were discussed.

Commissioner Layton moved to continue the public hearing and the Residential Site Grading
permit for property located at 4932 South Waimea Way to allow staff and the Planning
Commission can get better information and feedback from the adjoining neighbor to the west
who was not present at the meeting. Commissioner Mackin seconded the motion. Vote on
motion: Troy Holbrook-Aye, Jan Bradshaw-Aye, Ann Mackin-Aye, Chris Layton-Aye, and
Chair Marianne Ricks-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.

2.      Zone Map Amendment – From Public (P) to Residential Multi-Family (RM) – 1306
        East Spring Lane.
Mr. Allred presented the staff report and stated that the above matter was properly noticed with
notices mailed to all property owners within 500 feet of the subject property. The request is to
change the zoning of a 1.2-acre property at 1306 Spring Lane from Public or “P” zone to RM
(multi-family). The surrounding zoning and land uses are high-density multi-family. The RM
zoning that has been requested is the same as to the south, east and west. The property is

currently zoned Public (P). There are two accesses to the property but one is for emergencies
only. The General Plan designation for the area is Medium-Density Residential-Stable (MDR-S)
which allows for the zone requested. Staff recommended approval of the rezone based on the
findings set forth in the staff report.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Layton, Mr. Allred stated that the maximum
density allowed in the RM zone is 16 units per acre. He clarified that regardless of whether the
Planning Commission recommends approval or denial of the rezone, the issue will still go to the
City Council for a public hearing, which will again be properly noticed to the neighborhood.

The applicant, Jeff Kruckenberg from Alan Kruckenberg Construction, was present to request a
zone change from P to RM. It is consistent with the General Plan and the surrounding
neighborhood. If approved, the applicant will work with staff on the subsequent public meetings
and City Council on the subdivision application to ensure a quality project.

Chair Ricks opened the public hearing.

Peggy Sulzbach a Tobermory Ridge resident reported that she has lived in Holladay for over 30
years. She was concerned that the applicant is proposing to rezone the property for multi-family
development rather than single-family dwellings. It is not conducive to Spring Lane or
Holladay. Her understanding was that the developer has developed seven single-family one-
level homes on 1300 East 6400 South, which are beautiful and conducive to the area. Ms.
Sulzbach’s opinion was that Holladay has a specific aesthetic that will be lost if multi-family
housing is pursued.

Doug Beck, a Tobermory resident, described problems pulling into and out of his property. He
was concerned that the developer is proposing 16 additional homes and parking stalls with the
project. If approved, the traffic situation will be exacerbated. Mr. Beck was very upset about the
proposal and the situation that exists.

Christine Goddard, a Tobermory resident and member of the HOA board, commented that she
realizes the property will be developed but was concerned about the number of units that are
proposed and the impact the project will have on the corner of Spring Lane and 1300 East, which
is already very heavily traveled. If the request is to be approved for multi-family development,
she asked that the Commission consider the number of units. Currently, the developer is
proposing 16. She was concerned about the density and the impact on that particular corner that
will impact the traffic in the immediate area.

A Tobermory resident, Gaddesh Kocut, asked about the location of the entrance to the property.
Chair Ricks explained that it is part of the site plan. The request tonight deals with the zone.
The resident stated that because of the slope, his own home sank by almost one foot and there
has been a significant amount of cracking. In addition, the proposed development will block the
view to the main road and create a traffic hazard. He was also concerned about the potential of
the neighboring homes sinking.

Another Tobermory resident, whose name was not audible, addressed the issue of density and
traffic. He believed that adding more properties in that location will make it not only more
difficult for the people who live there, but for the public in general. He was concerned that there
will be problems in the future if the rezone is granted.

Adam Haver gave his address as 1323 East Tobermory Way and expressed concerns with density
and safety as a result of the rezone.

Fred Dickerson, a Tobermory resident, wanted to have a better understanding of the criteria the
Commission uses to go from P to RM. Chair Ricks stated that they look at the General Plan, the
vision for the future of the area, and what is in the surrounding areas. She agreed that what is
proposed seems to be a lot of density in an area that already has heavy traffic. The Commission
prompted Mr. Allred to respond to his concerns in detail.

Mr. Allred reported that he has been with the City since 2005 and is the City’s fourth Planning
Director. From a land use perspective, the first step is to look at the General Plan, which was
updated in 2016. The General Plan is not the law but is a document that guides decision for
making future land use decisions The primary reason the property was zoned P is that it was for
a church which is what all churches, schools and other similar uses are zoned in Holladay
Appropriate potential uses for the site were considered during the General Plan update. The
intent was to provide compatibility with the surrounding uses. Mr. Allred stated that all land use
decisions should be made based on the future. He acknowledged that there is a major housing
shortage and the State has required every city to provide more affordable housing. One of the
ways of improving affordability is to create less scarcity. Affordable units are currently not
available in the City because the land is so expensive.

Mr. Allred’s opinion was that the proposal is a good use although the issues raised are legitimate.
Issues raised regarding this particular development such as groundwater, soil settling, quality of
construction, and traffic safety will be addressed at the subdivision development stage. In terms
of the basic use of the land, what is proposed is an ideal use if developed properly. He explained
that a traffic study is required for every development that would produce 100 car trips during the
peak hour. The average number of car trips per day from a residence is 6 to 10. While the
proposed project will not require a traffic study per se under the code, the city can certainly look
at Tobermory and its capacity. Mr. Allred pointed out that there is a slope coming up Spring
Lane from 1300 East that is troubling. Because of the slope, it would have been very dangerous
for Tobermory to have an access directly onto 1300 East. As a result, access had to come
through the church property. The subject property is on a corner with the question being whether
there can be a logical separate access from the one they have currently. Mr. Allred stated that in
terms of the land use, what is proposed fits the area and what the General Plan says is
appropriate for the future. The General Plan is updated every five years with a major update
done every 10 years.

Chair Ricks asked if in the future density could be limited on the basis of traffic. Mr. Allred
stated that it could be done but the question would be how to fashion such findings. Other
potential uses were identified. Mr. Allred pointed out that institutional uses will also generally
produce more traffic than residential ones.

Craig Hyland, a Tobermory resident, asked if a traffic study was conducted for Tobermory and if
the existing construction problems can be addressed in a traffic study. Chair Ricks stated that it
normally would not be because it is a temporary situation. Chris Layton commented that clearly

Mr. Kruckenberg thanked the Tobermory residents for coming out and expressing their opinions.
He pointed out that there are a variety of ways to mitigate traffic and construction issues within

he lives in Holladay and wants to build a good quality project. He requested that the zoning be

Margaret Beck commented that the existing emergency exit is impossible to drive through. The

There were no further public comments.

Chair Ricks moved to close the public hearing. The motion passed with the unanimous
consent of the Commission.

Troy Holbrook asked about the 1.19 acres and stated that it appears that the lot line includes the
private drive back to Tobermory as well as to the middle of Spring Lane.

Dale Bennett from Benchmark Engineering reported that Tobermory Lane is owned by the
owner of the subject property. Mr. Allred commented that if the rezone is granted, all of the
property in question that will be rezoned including the portion within the right-of-way. Any
subsequent action to subdivide will require the roadways be removed from the area eligible to
use for dwellings. There is a gross density and the developer will be held to whatever the
calculation is minus the road area. It was noted that access into Tobermory is not an issue and is
not germane to whether the land should be rezoned. Procedural issues were discussed.

Troy Holbrook asked if there are issues with the right turn off of 1300 East onto Spring Lane and
if there will there be a turn lane added as part of the rezone. Mr. Allred stated that that would be
a development issue addressed later. If a recommendation is made on the zoning, it should be
after information has been presented. Chris Layton’s position was that the rezone does not have
much to do with existing problems on the site. The issue pertains to the use and what it can
potentially become. Mr. Holbrook stressed that the issues need to be resolved. His goal was to
determine whether what is proposed is the best and highest use of the property and if this is a
legitimate reason to rezone it. He saw no reason for it to be rezoned RM rather than P. He
further commented that there will be impacts associated with the rezone. He suggested that if
approval of the rezone is recommended to the City Council that it include contingencies, one of
which is additional traffic on the corner.

Mr. Allred disagreed with Commissioner Holbrook and was concerned that he was delving into
the development before those issues have been determined. He noted that that is done by TRC
after a rezone. Any concerns will be addressed in a later staff report. He suggested the Council
be allowed to address the zoning issues during their hearing. Commissioner Holbrook clarified
that he believes the greater density is acceptable as long as safety concerns are addressed.
Commissioner Layton’s opinion was that it should not be connected to the rezone and should
instead be connected to the design of the site regardless of what zone it is in.

Commissioner Holbrook wanted the City Council to know that thought has gone into the
Planning Commission’s recommendation and that they understand there are additional impacts.
Mr. Allred commented that the staff report will reflect outstanding concerns. He did not
recommend getting into the site design at the rezone stage and stated that it is premature.

Ann Mackin’s position was formed primarily by the General Plan and how they have looked at
this particular property over the years and into the future. She was confident that as site plans
come forward, they will have every opportunity to address the issues that arise.

Chair Ricks reminded the public that the Planning Commission is a recommending body and the
final decision will come from the City Council. She encouraged the public to voice their
concerns at the City Council level.

Commissioner Layton moved forward a positive recommendation to the City Council of the
zone map amendment from Public (P) to Residential Multi-Family (RM) at 1306 East Spring
Lane based on the following:

Findings:
      1.      The 2016 General Plan is the envisioning document, guiding future
              redevelopment of Holladay.

       2.     The General Plan recommends RM zoning at this location. The request is for
              RM zoning.

       3.     The General Plan notes that only 2.1% of land use in Holladay is Duplex/Twin
              Home type of development. This would generally indicate that this type of
              development is not overrepresented in the community.

       4.     The request proposes housing that is not incompatible and is generally
              consistent with the character of existing development in the vicinity and
              surrounding housing developments such as Tobermory Ridge (south),
              Sandpiper (west) and Wolf Hollow, (west) to name a few.

       5.     Single-family zoning at this location at the confluence of Spring Lane and 1300
              East is less viable and less desirable than the proposal for twin-home
              development.

       6.     Staff is not aware of any good reason that the Public zone needs to stay in place
              at this location.

       7.     The low-profile nature, (two-story max) of the proposed dwellings should not
              have a negative impact on abutting property to the north (institutional) to the
              west (much taller multiple family) and to the south (two-story multi-family).

       8.     The zoning and attendant density proposed may allow for more affordable

       9.     Commercial development/zoning is not called for at this location and would

Commissioner Bradshaw seconded the motion. Vote on motion: Chris Layton-Aye, Ann
Mackin-Aye, Jan Bradshaw-Aye, Troy Holbrook-Aye, and Chair Marianne Ricks-Aye. The
motion passed unanimously.
