14   DISCUSSION – Southwest Area Planning
15
16   Mr. Bench stated this area had been discussed previously but with the new information of the
17   Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints (LDS) Temple site there has been some further
18   discussion and direction. He inquired if the city wanted to look at other alternative than what had
19   previously been approved. Mr. Bench reviewed the current area and what it is zoned, then
20   reviewed some options to see if there was any interest to make some changes. Mr. Bench reviewed
21   some possible concept options.
22
23   Discussion ensued regarding the need to widen Geneva Road and other issues that may need to be
24   addressed due to the LDS Temple and other sites possibly being built in the area.
25
26   Mr. Bench stated that in making changes they would like to look at having a possible trail network
27   in that area. He continued to give an overview and considerations of the concept plans presented
28   and stated they would like some direction as to what the Council would like to do with this area if
29   anything.
30
31   General discussion continued regarding infrastructure and what would be needed and the fees it
32   would take to update the infrastructure.
33
34   Mr. Davidson stated if they are going to change the density and ERU’s in this area the council
35   needs to be mindful there is a formal process they would need to go through in making those
36   changes. They would need to go back to the engineering studies and master plans, update the
37   model and guarantee they can do what they want to do. After that they would also have to amend
38   their Impact Fees Facilities Plan and based on that plan amend the Impact Fees. This is a lengthy
39   process and can only be done after amending the General Plan. He doesn’t want the Council to
40   think they can instantly go from one zoning to another, it may be well over a year to accommodate
41   what direction they may decide tonight.
42
43   General discussion continued regarding the LDS temple land, road access and current discussions
44   happening with the Church.
45
46   Mayor Brunst indicated he feels they need to continue this discussion until they hear back from
47   the LDS Church and receive confirmation from them regarding how the temple building would
48   proceed.

23   Murray Low, Orem resident stated he read a newspaper article regarding the absolute necessity for
24   higher density housing in Utah and would like to make a few comments. What the article didn’t
25   say was what percentage of the suburb of large cities was zoned for single family density. It seems
26   to imply there is an unstoppable movement towards higher density housing across the country
27   which he does not believe is happening. He sees a significant difference between cities and states
28   based on demographics, politics, the availability of land, economic growth and other factors.
29   Despite the increase in house prices he feels the dream of owning a home in Utah County is still
30   achievable to most people. He feels the issue they are facing is space not affordability. If they
31   mandate high density housing in Orem through legislation, those that want a single-family home
32   will go to a community or suburb where they can get what they want. He feels this is a fundamental
33   question of who they are in Orem, what’s our identity today and in the future and if they move
34   down that path as the newspaper article suggests, Orem’s Family City USA will go by the way
35   side and he feels it matters to residents deeply and they need to do everything they can to make
36   those choices.

 8             PUBLIC HEARING
 9             Amending Section 14-3-3 (Wall Signs) of the Orem City Code by adding the CM Zone as
10             a permitted zone for wall signs

11   Mr. Bench stated the current zoning code does not list commercial manufacturing as a zone
12   where wall signs are permitted while wall signs are permitted in all other commercial and
13   manufacturing zones throughout the city. A recent applicant had requested for approval of a
14   wall sign in the CM zone, and those in Development Services noticed the absence of language
15   allowing for wall signs in the zone. In the past, the City has approved wall signs within the CM
16   zone, and this amendment would clean up the language within the code.

17   Mayor Brunst opened the public hearing at 6:07 pm. Hearing no comments, the Mayor closed the
18   public hearing and brought the item back to the City Council for further discussion.
19
20   Mr. Macdonald moved, by ordinance, to amend Orem City Code by amending Section 14-3-3
21   (Wall Signs) of the Orem City Code by adding the CM Zone as a permitted zone for wall signs.
22   Mr. Spencer seconded the motion. Those voting aye: Richard F. Brunst, Jeff Lambson, Debby
23   Lauret, Tom Macdonald, David Spencer, and Brent Sumner. Those voting nay: Terry Peterson.
24   The motion passed.

27             PUBLIC HEARING
28             Amending Section 22-5-3(A) and the zoning map of the City of Orem by changing the
29             zoning on approximately 0.28 acres for property located generally at 166 North 400 West
30             from the R7.5 zone to the R6.5 zone.
31

32   Mr. Bench stated the existing property is approximately 12,196 square feet (0.28 acres). It
33   currently has one single family dwelling. It is a corner lot with large yard space. The applicant has
34   submitted a concept plan to divide the lot into two single family lots--one being 6,939 square feet,
35   the other being 6,540 square feet. The current R7.5 zone requires a lot size minimum of 7,500
36   square feet meaning that the applicant’s proposed lots will not meet current zoning requirements.
37   The proposed R6.5 zone would allow for the proposed two lots and their sizes with the minimum
38   size requirement of 6,500 square feet being met. The applicant has also submitted concept plans
39   for the homes to be constructed on the proposed parcels. The homes would be identical in design
40   and would be mirrored over the property line. Each home would be approximately 2,150 square
41   feet separately in size and would have a 750 square foot one-bedroom, one-bathroom accessory
42   apartment. The primary units would be approximately 1,400 square feet. The applicant meets the
43   required parking for both the primary and accessory units. The primary units have a one (1) car
44   garage and an additional parking stall on the side of the home. The accessory apartments each have
45   a one (1) car garage with doors that lead into the units. The Development Review Committee has
     City Council Minutes – January 28, 2020(p.6)     A complete video of the meeting can be found at www.orem.org/meetings
 1   stressed to the applicant that the primary units must be owner occupied in order to have accessory
 2   apartments. The applicant held a neighborhood meeting on Friday, September 13th at 6:15pm.
 3   After the applicant assured the neighbors that these homes would not be used for multifamily uses,
 4   the neighbors appeared to be in support of the project.

 5   Mr. Sumner inquired as to adequate parking to accommodate the single-family home with an
