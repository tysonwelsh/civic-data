       Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Presentation

Sherrie Ohrn, Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District Board of Trustees, presented
information about the water supply and other water issues. This presentation is included in the
packet.

Ms. Ohrn explained that to recover from the drought, Utah needs another year like this year.
She talked about the importance of water in the Great Salt Lake. She urged citizens to continue
conservation efforts.

Ms. Ohrn said there was potential legislation that would take away taxing from the Jordan Valley
Water Conservancy District and said this would have a large financial impact on the district.
This legislation would affect the bond rate for the District and this would in turn affect
conservation grants and efforts. Instead of passing the bill, the legislature decided to do a study
for the next couple of years to analyze all of the data and how the bill would affect different
agencies.

Ms. Ohrn said the rate increase at the district is about 5% but Riverton will only absorb about
1% of this cost. She asked Councilmembers and staff to continue to share success stories and
encourage residents to conserve water.

Councilmember Wells asked how much water is too much water. Ms. Ohrn said even with all of
the water this year, reservoirs will not exceed capacity.

Councilmember Pierucci asked Ms. Ohrn to share effective ways that cities are communicating
water conservation. Ms. Ohrn said that most of the cities are doing a good job because
consumption has decreased. She asked Riverton to analyze what messaging or programs
caused the decrease and continue to promote these areas.

Councilmember Pierucci asked if the water districts are impacted by ESG metrics in bonding.
Ms. Ohrn said she does not know but will find out.


          Ranked Choice Voting Presentation

Kelleen Potter, Executive Director of Utah Ranked Choice Voting, gave a presentation to the
Council. This presentation is included in the packet.

Ms. Potter said some grants can be utilized to cover the increased cost of Ranked Choice
Voting in Salt Lake County.

Councilmember Pierucci expressed concern about the increased cost for ranked choice voting
in Salt Lake County and asked where the grant funding would come from and what the
qualifications to receive the grant would be. Ms. Potter said three sources have promised
funding but she does not know which specific funding source they will use.

Councilmember Pierucci asked if education costs are included in the grant. Ms. Potter said she
may have some sources that will help with education costs but that most cities did not spend
money on education in previous years. She said there is free candidate training, however.

Councilmember Pierucci said this is a pilot program that sunsets in 2026 and currently the
legislature does not plan to extend the pilot program. He asked if it is worth the effort if the pilot
program does not continue. Ms. Potter said she believes it is worth the effort and her argument
is to let cities decide.

Councilmember Pierucci said there are many concerns from cities that participated in ranked
choice voting, including Sandy and Genola. Ms. Potter said it was not the system but the voter
who made the choice. Councilmember Pierucci disagreed.

Councilmember Wells asked what grant funding was available in past elections. Ms. Potter said
funding was provided if cities wanted to send out a postcard to voters. Councilmember Wells
said his biggest concern is educating voters.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal said the Council needs to communicate with Mr. Brickey if they
would like this to be on the April 18 agenda.

          Discussion of Lot Coverage Standards

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said there is a current ordinance that states
that the maximum coverage is 30%. The maximum coverage refers to “buildings,” meaning it
has a roof supported by columns and walls. The 30% lot coverage maximum has been a
concern for residents because of the reduction in lot sizes. The reduction in lot sizes has led to
a trend towards larger homes and additions to homes which causes concerns for residents
when they aren’t able to build what they would like to build. He said that swimming pools are
considered part of the 30%. He asked the Council if they would like to change the maximum
coverage to 40% and exclude swimming pools and pavement.

Councilmember McCay said she errors on the side of personal property rights and would like to
increase it. She suggested a maximum lot coverage of 45%.

Councilmember Pierucci said that he also supports personal property rights. He asked what
percentage of the City is part of a special district that would not be included in this standard. Mr.
Lethbridge estimated that about 25% of the City is in a special district, mostly on the western
end of the City. Councilmember Pierucci supports at least 40% on the maximum lot coverage.

          Electric Motorized Bike Discussion

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal said there is no ordinance that prohibits electric motorized bikes in

Public Hearing(s)/Action Items

          Ordinance No. 23-09 – Dissolving the “Redwood South Neighborhood
          Redevelopment Project Area”

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the Riverton City
Redevelopment Agency in 1997 voted to create the "Redwood Road South Neighborhood
Redevelopment Project Area", which encompassed areas of the Riverton City downtown from
12600 South along Redwood Road. The area's purpose was to support and facilitate
development and redevelopment within that project area. All such project areas have a defined
timespan, and this project area expired in 2017, though the project area itself remains in place
until formally dissolved. There are no remaining contractual obligations associated with the
project area, nor is there any outstanding indebtedness connected with it. The Riverton City
Redevelopment Agency adopted a resolution dissolving the project area, which is required by
State Code to be implemented by ordinance by the Riverton City Council.

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
Hearing.

Councilmember Pierucci MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 23-09
– Dissolving the “Redwood South Neighborhood Redevelopment Project Area.”
Councilmember McCay SECONDED the motion. Mayor Pro Tem McDougal called for
discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as
follows: McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, Pierucci-yes and Wells-yes. The motion passed
unanimously.

          Ordinance No. 23-07 – Residential Chicken Ordinance, Adopting Amendments to
          the Residential Chicken Ordinance
Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the Riverton City ordinance currently allows up to 6 residential
chickens to be kept on residential property of 10,000-square feet or larger. 10,000 square feet is
¼ acre. Based on recent requests by residents and inquiries by the City Council. Staff has
reviewed various approaches to allowing chickens on lots below 10,000 square feet. One
method was a ‘sliding scale”, used in Herriman and other communities. The sliding scale allows
for keeping a progressively smaller number of residential chickens in lots of different sizes. The
zoning results in residents of a 5,000-7,999 square foot lot being allowed to keep four (4)
chickens, and residents of an 8,000-10,000 square foot lot are allowed to keep six (6).

Mayor Pro Tem McDougal called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
Hearing.

Councilmember Pierucci MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 23-07
– Amending 18.135.090 Animal and Fowl created a sliding scale regulating the number of
allowed residential chickens by lot size and amending the required minimum distance for
keeping chickens as described in Exhibit A. Councilmember Wells SECONDED the motion.
Mayor Pro Tem McDougal called for discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for
a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, Pierucci-yes and Wells-
yes. The motion passed unanimously.
