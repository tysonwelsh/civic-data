          day on UVX which ran the same length as the 830 bus. Very quickly, the ridership was
          increased by five times of the previous line. She said that she believed that resulted
          because it was fast, frequent, and free.

2.0    Presentation: Draper Police Department 2020 Year in Review - Chief John Eining

2.1    Chief Eining said that he wanted to provide the Council with an update on what happened
       in 2020. He said that he wished that there was better news to share. 2020 brought some
       interesting challenges with COVID. He said that city trends matched those across the
       valley and nationally. First, the Police Department saw a small increase in calls for service.
       However, the department saw a significant drop in citations from 4,035 in 2019 to just
       1,802 in 2020. He said that this, to some extent, was self-created. In the early months of
       the pandemic and into the summer, police officers were encouraged not to make traffic
       stops to limit interactions to maintain the health of workers and ensure that they had a
       viable workforce. As the Police Departments moves through 2021 and restrictions are
       lifted, citations would be an area that the department was going to pay special attention

to. Chief Eining recognized that there was a need and that every neighborhood meeting
he attends wants more traffic enforcement. He said that the Police Department’s main
goal for traffic enforcement was to focus on the areas identified by the Traffic Committee.
When the committee identifies a problem that could not be solved through engineering,
or signage making law enforcement the only means, that is where the department
responds. He said that generally, officers don’t like just going out and running radar in
their spare time.

Arrests were also down significantly from 719 in 2019 to 446 in 2020. When speaking
about use-of-force, a topic that was heavily addressed in the previous year’s update, he
noted that they had similar success in 2020 as in 2019. The only force used was arrest
control tactics (ACT). These were situations where someone had to be taken to the
ground as a result of non-compliant handcuffing.

Property crimes across the board were up. The biggest increases were in vehicle
burglaries and vehicle thefts. Concerning person crimes, the biggest decrease was in
drug-related crimes. This was largely a result of fewer traffic stops which meant that
fewer drugs were found inside of cars. Driving Under the Influence (DUI) was also down
because of the lack of traffic stops. Domestic assaults were not included. Chief Eining
noted that there was almost a 35% increase in criminal domestic assaults. If verbal
domestic assaults where the police were called, but no violence occurred, were included,
the number of domestic assaults was up significantly.

When the number of property and person crimes were compared, Draper has significantly
more property crimes. For Chief Eining, this is a good thing as it indicates that violent
crimes are not something that Draper has to worry about.

Mayor Walker asked about the 10 robberies. He wanted to know if that was 10 burglaries.

when Five Guys was robbed by a suspect with a knife. The suspect was tracked down.

special assignment patrols. An example of how volunteers served the city was their
response to the uptick in burglaries. Volunteers had circulated flyers to apartment

3.0   Presentation: Draper Fire Department 2020 Year in Review - Chief Clint Smith

3.1   Chief Smith provided a call volume statistical update for 2020. He said that one of the
      goals of the department since being in existence in Draper was to compile an annual
      report. He invited the Council’s feedback on how to improve the utility of the annual
      report in future years.

      The total call volume for 2020 was 3,600 calls, a slight decrease from 2019. COVID played
      a challenging role regarding emergency response over the past year. In the early months
      of the pandemic, the department saw a decrease in call volume as people stayed home.
      As was typical, 80% of the calls were medical and 20% fire.

      One of the areas of focus for Chief Smith was reducing the response time to medical calls.
      He noted that response times were limited by the location of stations and the areas of
      growth in the city. It was because of this that he wanted good data to be able to inform
      future decisions where stations needed to be placed. In 2020, it took 1.8 minutes from
      the time a unit was notified before it was in route, 5.5 minutes before that vehicle arrived
      on the scene, and 14.5 minutes between when the unit arrived on the scene and left the
      scene. When responding to significant calls like a heart attack or stroke, beyond six or
      seven minutes can cause significant tissue damage.

      Concerning fire response times, the average time from dispatch to arrival was eight
      minutes. There was some variation between shifts. This is significant because fire tends
      to double in size every minute.

Despite mutual aid agreements with jurisdictions in the surrounding area, 94% of the Fire

5.1   Todd Draper, planner, explained that this ordinance was a bulk update on the queuing
      definition in Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC) so that the language referred to queuing
      as opposed to staking. Also, there were some references to the Utah Fire Authority (UFA)
      that were outdated and needed to be replaced with the Draper City Fire Department. A
      similar update needed to be made to references to remove references to high-density
      residential commercial zones that were no longer permitted. The design guideline manual
      referenced in the code was no longer utilized as a result of changes in state law. Thus,
      replaced design guidelines in the ordinance with design standards would reduce
      confusion with applicants and increase compliance. Lastly, there were some minor
      typographical and grammatical changes needed in Titles 9 and 17 of DCMC.

      Mr. Draper quickly reviewed those changes.
