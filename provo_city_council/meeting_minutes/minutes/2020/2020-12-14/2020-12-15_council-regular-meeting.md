 1.       A presentation of the Comprehensive Annual Financial Report (CAFR) by the Provo City
          Finance Division and HBME. (20-251) (0:12:50)

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, presented the 2020 CAFR. He read from the report that
can be found online at https://www.provo.org/departments/finance/financial-reports. Mr. Borget
formally thanked the finance staff for their hard work, as well as the departments for their support. An
increase in revenues was contributed to $6 million the City received from the CARES Act relief fund. The
amount of money in the unassigned fund balance had increased. Mr. Borget explained this was due to
careful planning and scrutinization of any carryover funds.

Dan Follet, Finance Director, reviewed parts of the report and explained that the city was in a strong
financial position and well diversified.

Jeff Miles of HBME, LLC reviewed the audit firm’s reports that confirmed the City was in compliance with
General Accounting Principles (GAP). There were no significant deficiencies in internal controls. There
was a single instance of non-compliance with HOME grants, this was self-reported by the City. The City
was in the process of working with HUD to remediate this issue. Overall, Provo had a good year,
especially considering the challenges that came with COVID.

 2.       Resolution 2020-45 appropriating a combined $134,000 in the General Fund for a wildland
          mitigation program, a behavioral-health services provider, and a continuity of operations
          plan. (20-252) (0:51:26)

       Motion:           An implied motion to adopt the Resolution 2020-45, as currently constituted, had been
                         made by council rule.

Fire Chief Jim Miguel presented to the Council. The Provo City Fire Department requested that an
appropriation to the General Fund be approved for a combined $134,000. The appropriation included
$45,000 for a wildland mitigation program, $79,000 for public safety behavioral-health services, and
$10,000 for a continuity of operations plan. The appropriations would be funded using fiscal year 2021
wildland fire response revenues.

Chair Handley opened public comment.

Mary Howell, Provo, asked if there were funds in the education program that would help with
enforcement to protect property from fire. Chief Miguel said there would be enforcement included with
this program. Their goal was to prevent fires, and if they do get started, stop them quickly. (1:13:25)

There were no other comments from the public. Chair Handley called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:             The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                         Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.

 3.       Resolution 2020-46 transferring $235,000 from the General Fund to the Golf Fund. (20-245)
          (1:16:58)

       Motion:           An implied motion to adopt Resolution 2020-46, as currently constituted, had been made
                         by council rule.

David Mortensen, Budget Officer, presented. The Golf Fund suffered operating losses as a result of a
combination of events that had a negative impact on operations, namely, the golf course hole
realignment construction project and the COVID-19 pandemic. State code required the fund to have an
unrestricted fund balance of at least 5% of revenues. A transfer of $235,000 from the General Fund was
required to bring the Golf Fund unrestricted fund balance into compliance. A more detailed
presentation had been given during work session on December 1, 2020.

Chair Handley stated there had been an email from a citizen questioning why the course was not self-
sustaining. Mr. Mortensen explained the issues that had caused this were temporary and he did not
think this would be an issue going forward.

Chair Handley opened public comment.

Jared Curtis, Provo, had concerns about the golf course. He said there had been many promises they
would be solvent, but they were not. Mr. Curtis was also opposed to the golf course being open on
Sundays. He thought it was time to sell the course.

There were no other comments from the public.

Councilor Shipley clarified this money was not earmarked for anything specific, it was simply to satisfy a
state requirement and would not fund their budget going forward and it was not going to be used to
expand services. Mr. Mortensen agreed, this would not be spent on operations or projects. It just
rebuilds the fund balance to comply with state code. Any use of the funds would require an
appropriation, public hearing and approval by the Council.

Chair Handley called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:             The motion passed 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding, Hoban,
                         Sewell, and Shipley in favor.

 4.       Ordinance 2020-63 amending Provo City Code to allow more options for conducting
          Neighborhood Chair elections. (20-253) (1:28:32)

       Motion:           An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2020-63, as currently constituted, had been made
                         by council rule.

Karen Tapahe, Community Relations Coordinator, presented. Provo City Code 2.29.030 required that
those voting in the election of a Neighborhood Chair and Vice Chair(s) be present in person at the
neighborhood meeting where the election is being held. The pandemic conditions had restricted in-
person gatherings in the interest of safety and health. There were some neighborhoods with
Neighborhood Chair vacancies and others where the Chair's term has expired. Neighborhood meetings
had moved entirely online because it was too cold for an outdoor meeting option. An adjustment to the


Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - December 15, 2020                                                 Page 3 of 8
City Code was needed to allow the elections to be held in an online meeting. This was also an
opportunity to establish a consistent set of rules to guide the elections. The Neighborhood Advisory
Board met November 23, 2020, to review the proposed amendment and gave a unanimous
recommendation to bring it to the Municipal Council for approval.

Chair Handley opened public comment.

Jared Curtis, Provo, wanted to know why there was a concern about voting in person. He did not think
that in person neighborhood meetings had been the source of any mass COVID transmission. He
thought the election should take place in person.

Mary Howell, Provo, was supportive of this measure. One reason there was little involvement in the
neighborhood program was because people tend to have families and other responsibilities that prevent
them from attending in person. Having the ability to vote on a virtual platform could increase the
involvement, said Ms. Howell.

Ms. Tapahe responded to Mr. Curtis and said the few events held since the pandemic had been outdoor
meetings that allowed for physical distancing, but now it was too cold. Neighborhood Chair Elections
required someone from the Council Staff to be present, and they preferred to meet virtually during the
pandemic for their own safety.

Chair Handley noted that COVID-19 had created many new opportunities for flexibility in the way
meetings are held and it helped in accommodating specific needs with technology.

Councilor Ellsworth was supportive of these updates. It provided more access to these meetings. She did
not feel these were serious elections, they were more informal selections of Chairs. Ms. Tapahe agreed
and said the Neighborhood Chair did not have power in being able to vote on projects, they were only
responsible for the transfer of information, hence the casual nature of these elections.

Chair Handley called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:             The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                         Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.

 5.       An ordinance amending the Zone Map classification of approximately 8.5 acres, generally
          located at 1724 South State Street, from CM, RA, R1.6, R1.10, and A1.5 to the MDR Zone.
          Spring Creek Neighborhood. (PLRZ20190356) (1:42:49)

       Motion:           An implied motion to adopt the ordinance, as currently constituted, had been made by
                         council rule.

Brandon Larsen, City Planner, presented. There were 14 parcels with various zones in the proposed
location, the applicant proposed rezoning approximately 8.5 acres to MDR. Mr. Larsen described 1640
South State Street as the northern boundary, and the access point was at 1724 South State Street, which
was the current access for Treeside Charter School (TCS). The project was bisected by the entrance to
TCS. This request was compliant with the area plan and General Plan recommendations of mixed use
and compliant with the Southeast Area Plan recommendation of MDR zoning designation.




Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - December 15, 2020                                                 Page 4 of 8
There would be 30 townhomes, 30 studio apartments, 64 two-bedroom apartments, and 80 one-
bedroom apartments. Each building would be three or four floors. There were two different amenity
spaces located within the development. The building materials would be a hardy board and stone
exterior. If this were approved, there would still need to be project plan approval before beginning
construction.

Mr. Larsen displayed the various elevations and renderings. He also spoke the other developments that
had recently been approved in the area and the mix of zoning and demographics in the Spring Creek
Neighborhood. (1:48:24)

Chair Handley invited Mary Millar, Neighborhood Chair, to speak. She felt the applicant had worked well
with staff and the neighbors had learned a lot about the economic development of this area. The subject
property had been vacant for 44 years prior to the school going in. The neighbors liked having Treeside
Charter School in the area and wanted additional development. The neighborhood was supportive and
thought this would be a good thing for the City.

Chair Handley invited Erin Preston, representing Treeside Charter School (TCS), to speak to the Council.
TCS had a 20-year lease with a purchase option. They had not been included in the planning process,
most of the information obtained had been received through GRAMA requests. Traffic was discussed in
the neighborhood meeting, but there was not a recent traffic study available. Later, the project was
denied for multiple reasons, including the need for a traffic study. In December, the application was
approved but she felt the project had been misrepresented. One issue for TCS was the traffic analysis,
the traffic study being used was outdated. The parking in front of the school was dedicated and already
short seven required spaces. They worried overflow parking would end up in the school’s parking lot.
The primary access point was currently used for the school pickup and drop-off, it was utilized heavily.
The available parking study for State Street traffic shows accidents and issues. Ms. Preston requested a
continuance for an updated traffic study. She felt approval of this project would create a dangerous
situation for the students and all those involved. (2:12:25)

BJ Taylor, Salem, was a school board member and volunteer teacher at TCS. He said there was already
inadequate parking at the school. They would welcome the homes, it is likely they would feed into the
school, but they were concerned about the safety of the school. Charter schools did not have bus
programs, so there were even more cars than a typical school. Using the school’s lanes did not seem
reasonable for entering and exiting the portion of the development south of the school.

Todd Hepworth, Santaquin, was the Director of TCS. He said TCS brough value to the community by
providing employment for 45 employees. Students came from all over the valley and their parents
supported the local economy. The school also brought in millions of dollars in grants. He said any rumors
of the school closing were very untrue, they had a 20-year lease, and they were only five years into it.

Chet Barber, Bountiful, owned land adjacent to this project. They support the rezone but said the exhibit
shown was showing his property as retail, which was not what they intended, they would be applying
for an MDR rezone too. He also noted that the property to the south was also shown as retail, but they
did not see this as being developable, it was a large culvert. He was a commercial real estate broker and
thought the reason commercial does not work in this area was a lack of rooftops. He thought proper
MDR developments would help to eventually bring in quality commercial developments.

Mary Howell, Provo, had three children attending school at TCS. There had been rumors of what would
be done with the land. Originally, she was okay with the development because it was supposed to be a
preschool. She was not in favor of adding 1200 additional cars to her kids’ pickup lane. She was fearful a
child would be harmed; it was already concerning as it was today.

Lindy Townsend, TCS Consultant, said Ms. Millar was a great Neighborhood Chair. She explained that
Chett Barber, who spoke earlier, was planning to develop high-end townhomes and never made any
assumptions about using the school’s property for access. The process had taken a year because the
glaring issue of child safety was being ignored. Collaboration and friendliness were needed. She had
development experience across the world and thought TCS should not be compelled to allow this
developer to profit from TCS, a non-profit entity. She thought the item should be continued.

April Clausen was the school board chair one of the founders of TCS. She thought the school needed to
be involved in the process.

Chair Handley allowed David Gardner to address the Council, he was the developer of this project. He
explained the units were condominiums that would be sold, not rented. He felt there was a strong
demand for this type of housing. The townhome units were 3 bedrooms with a studio unit on top that
could also be purchased with the townhome or sold separately. The one- and two-bedroom units were
in high demand for owner occupancy. A total of 14 parcels were acquired for this project with the intent
to remove any blight. They had not requested any parking waivers and had 140 percent of what was
required for open space. The building materials were superior to other projects. He said their traffic
study showed they were not significantly increasing the traffic on State Street.

Councilor Hoban asked Mr. Gardner if he shared the same concerns with cross pollination of traffic
during drop-off times and the apartments to the south having to use the same driveways as the school.
Mr. Gardner was not aware of another option for accessing that parcel. It was possible a signal might be
installed so there were breaks between traffic pods. He thought this development could be helpful to
the school.

Councilor Harding desired for this item to be heard for a second time.

Councilor Ellsworth was passionate about this area, she worked in the area and was very familiar with it.
She wanted to preserve the quality of the neighborhood. Ms. Ellsworth was not sure this met the
requirements of the 2015 Southeast Area Plan. She had received emails about having more of this type
of development, these constituents preferred commercial development. She thought everything south
of 1640 South should be primarily commercial. Her preference would be for this to be a commercial
node. The area plan said this was compatible with MDR, but she thought it was her job to course correct
and continuing with this trend would create more problems. Only 15 percent of the project would be
townhomes. She was concerned about a balance of commercial and residential. She was willing to wait
for the right type of development. She wanted to make decision on what was best for Provo over the
next 40 years.

Chair Handley thought the presentation helpful and he appreciated the effort that went into it. There
were some differences of opinions with the school and developer. He thought there needed to be more
dialogue between the developer and TCS. Mr. Handley did not think the issue of safety had to do with
State Street, rather the road into the school where the children were.

Councilor Harding wanted to understand who owned the road going into the school. Whenever they
review these greenfield developments there have been too many times when a development goes in
and blocks good use of adjacent land. One step was to create a concept plan for a property adjacent to it
to ensure there was still a viable way to develop the other land. He wondered if this was taken into
consideration when the school went in.

Councilor Sewell was sympathetic to the long process the developer had gone through. He appreciated

Residential use seemed more compatible to Mr. Sewell. He hoped they could figure out a way for a

Councilor Ellsworth thought there were commercial developments that would be compatible, banks,

Councilor Shipley asked what the developer would be interested in terms of rescheduling. Mr. Gardner
said he did not know how fast they could work through this, but tentatively wanted to be on the next
meeting agenda.
