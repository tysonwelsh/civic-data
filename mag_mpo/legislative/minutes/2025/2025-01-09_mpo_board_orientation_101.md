LaNiece Davenport began the session with introductions, facilitating a round of introductions from one side of the room to the other. She then provided an overview of the Association of Governments (AOG) in Utah, which were established in the early 1970s by Governor Rampton to promote regional planning in areas such as transportation and land use. The Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG) is one of seven AOGs in the state, serving Utah, Summit, and Wasatch Counties. MAG’s mission is to empower communities in these regions by fostering collaboration, providing access to funding and services, and advocating for local issues. The organization is governed by an Executive Council composed of representatives from the chief elected oﬃcials of the three counties it serves. LaNiece Davenport elaborated on MAG’s structure, which comprises three departments: Planning, Community and Economic Development, and Aging and Family Services. Within the Planning department, there are three governing bodies—the MPO Board, the Wasatch Back RPO, and the Station Area Planning Policy Committee. She also detailed the Planning department’s divisions, including Transportation, Community Planning, and Analytics. She outlined the Department’s goals which are achieving effective results, maintaining open communication with members and partners, and advancing the Wasatch Choice Vision. The Wasatch Choice Vision, introduced by LaNiece Davenport, is a regional planning framework that spans from southern Box Elder County to southern Utah County. It focuses on four key strategies: transportation choices, housing options, parks and open spaces, and the development of city and town centers. This Vision, which aggregates local planning efforts, is updated regularly, with current efforts concentrating on Utah County. Dan Wayne emphasized the importance of creativity in planning beyond current general plans. LaNiece Davenport also explained the composition and role of the MPO Board, which includes all elected oﬃcials in Utah County, three county commissioners, a transportation commissioner, a UTA representative, and non-voting members. Federally designated, the MPO ensures a continuous, comprehensive, and coordinated planning process, focusing on developing a regional transportation plan (RTP) and a Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) in collaboration with UDOT and UTA. The MPO considers multimodal transportation systems, broader land use and economic opportunities, and sustainability factors. A discussion led by Mayor Bill Wright and other participants highlighted the challenges of long-range planning, particularly in predicting population growth and job trends. Tim Hereth and Shawn Eliot offered insights into build-out scenarios and shifting population dynamics, while LaNiece Davenport suggested forming a subcommittee on growth to address these challenges. The group emphasized the need for accurate projections and continuous feedback from local governments. LaNiece Davenport explained the TIP as the RTP’s implementation arm, funded with approximately $60 million annually. Bob Allen outlined eligible TIP projects, such as active transportation, transit, road projects, and Page 2 of 3 studies, and detailed the biennial selection process that aligns projects with RTP goals and fiscal constraints. Discussions also addressed local option sales taxes and the impact of legislative changes on revenue. She introduced Utah’s Unified Transportation Plan, which fosters collaboration among all transportation agencies and MPOs in the state. LaNiece Davenport highlighted the Wasatch Back RPO, managed by Bob Allen, which serves Summit and Wasatch Counties and operates similarly to the MPO. Other programs discussed included the Corridor Preservation program, managed by Calvin Clark, and the TAG and LAA programs, which support integration of transportation and land use, and support smaller city projects and activities. In addressing disaster mitigation, Mayor Dan Olson raised concerns about flood risks. LaNiece Davenport introduced Shauna Mecham, the pre-disaster mitigation planner, who explained the availability of federal grants for such planning and encouraged collaboration on project submissions. LaNiece Davenport concluded by thanking participants for their efforts and inviting feedback to improve future sessions. The meeting ended with an announcement that the MPO Board meeting would commence at 5:30 PM, focusing on specific agenda items. Adjournment (01:13:54) Mayor Frost moved to adjourn the meeting. Commissioner Gordon seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Page 3 of 3
