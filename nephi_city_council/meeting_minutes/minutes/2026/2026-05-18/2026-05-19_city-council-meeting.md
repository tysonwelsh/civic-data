PLANNING ITEMS
      TRANSPORTATION MASTER PLAN PRESENTATION
      Seth Atkinson, City Administrator, introduced the Transportation Master Plan prepared by Jones
      and DeMille highlighting its scope and purpose. He then introduced Matthew Lenhart, who has
      been working on the project, to present the plan to the council.
      Mr. Lenhart displayed the interactive website (LINK) and detailed what was on it. He explained
      the different road classifications and the proposed roadway network. The plan includes future
      roadways, traffic counts, and crash data to guide future development and funding. The grid
      system was extended out past the airport and south to Four Mile Road. During planning, they
      prioritized roads that already have existing railroad crossings. Including this in a framework that
      is beneficial to the city will be helpful when planning future developments. Having the plan will
      be beneficial in securing funding for projects.
      Mr. Atkinson described the $1.5 million grant received for upgrade and upkeep of roads. The
      council discussed specific roads needing repair including 400 E. The 400 E project will be phased
      beginning in 2028.
      Once adopted, the Transportation Master Plan will be available on the Nephi City Website
      (nephi.utah.gov). The council was impressed with the extensiveness of the plan and the detailed
      information available on the transportation plan website. The council will explore the
      transportation plan and make any recommendations before the plan is adopted.

ADMINISTRATIVE AND COUNCIL UPDATES
    Mr. Atkinson discussed the property tax impact schedule which shows the current rate and the
      proposed rate He stated it is required to be presented at every meeting that the budget is on
      the agenda. He explained the purpose of the proposed tax increase this year is to construct a

    Mr. Atkinson announced there will be a public hearing regarding the amended budget for Fiscal
      year 2026 on June 2. The hearing about the proposed tax increase will be August 11.
      Mr. Atkinson talked about the noise ordinance public hearing, pertaining to the data center,
       held May 13 at the planning commission meeting. It will be reviewed again at the June 10
       meeting before being brought to the council.
      Mr. Atkinson discussed the land requested to be annexed for the data center. He stated a public
       hearing would be held in the future before anything is approved. He encouraged anyone with
       questions to ask, and he would be happy to address them.
      Ms. Cowan announced the Just Serve community service project to plant flowers on Main Street
       Saturday, May 23 from 9-11 am.
      Mr. Worwood wondered whether studies have been done on industrial businesses concerning
       their water usage. Working with city billing in the past, he recalled some industrial customers
       using more water than the proposed data center usage. Mr. Atkinson confirmed the first phase
       of data center would use less water than some established businesses are already using.
      Mayor Seely reported, after talking with different mayors, the reason work sessions will not be
       recorded. It would create confusion. Not all work session discussion items make it to a regular
       meeting for action—they are just discussion items.
      Mayor Seely addressed the armory base near the airport after hearing misinformation that it
       was there for data center security. He stated the military wanted to move away from the fault
       line on the Wasatch front. Initially the armory was going to be an air wing, but with
       administrational changes, plans changed. He declared that this armory is bringing back
       something that used to be a proud part of our community. He reminisced about NRP coming to
       during World War II to build hoses and different things. Those workers had to have routine lead
       tests because they worked in a toxic environment. Technology has improved since the 1950s.
       The same is true for data centers. Technology has improved and the problems that some studies
       show are no longer issues because of those enhancements. He encouraged citizens to contact
       him with questions or concerns. He emphasized the importance of community engagement and
       the council’s commitment to making informed decisions.
      Mayor would like the tractor show at the fairgrounds in July to celebrate America 250 to be
       amazing and would like the community’s help for any contact information.

Work Session 8:59 pm
   1. City Attorney Contract
             a. Last year the city agreed with the Juab School District and the Central Valley Medical
                 Center to continue jointly using attorney services. The tentative arrangement has been
                 to contract with the Juab School District for 40% of their attorney’s time and that the
                 attorney would be a school district employee.
             b. Mr. Atkinson explained the challenges faced due to state legislation not covering city
                 attorneys under governmental immunity. Brittany is employed by the school district and
                 thus is covered.
             c. The council reviewed the drafted agreement between the three entities and Brittany
                 Tellez for attorney services. The agreement outlines scope of duties, cost sharing,
                 dispute resolution, wages, benefits, termination, and other standard legal subjects.
             d. Maternity leave was discussed. Brittany is currently taking 12 weeks and has that in the
                 draft agreement. The school district currently offers 5 weeks. The council said that as a
                 district employee, the contract should follow the 5 weeks. The city currently does not
                 have a maternity leave policy, and Sara Samuelson discussed the need to adopt its own
                 policy.
    2.   Capstone program
             a. The school district applied for a $20 million grant to build a catalyst center.
             b. Capstone students have been involved in various community projects and have seen
                 success in different competitions.
             c. The school district has requested from the city a letter of support and participation on
                 the advisory board for the grant.
    3.   Groundbreaking for Nephi Western Credit Union will be held May 20 at noon.
    4.   Seth will be giving the State of the City address at the Neph Chamber Luncheon on June 4.
    5.   Accessibility Concerns at Gun Range
             a. The mayor brings up the need to make the gun range wheelchair accessible.
             b. Discussion ensued about how to make this happen and the potential involvement of the
                 National Guard.
             c. The accessibility would include bathroom upgrades and getting road mill for smoother,
                 firmer ground.
