        1.3      Discussion of a Proposed Development at 6400 West and 13400 South, including
                 Commercial Property and Single Family Lots
Applicant Jeff Neal presented in person. High Country Homes Craig Bonham, Josh and Jake Thorne for
JCraft Homes, and Dave Price with Elevation Homes were also present. Applicant Neal reported every lot
would be large, would have 100 feet of frontage, and a three car garage. The concept plan was displayed, which
included 69 lots, and a commercial corner. Current zoning was A-1 (Agricultural), but the applicant would
request a rezone to A-.25. The surrounding landowners have access to 13100 South, 13400 South or Kokomo
Court, all of which were major or residential roads. Commissioners asked about access to properties on
Kokomo Court. Engineer Josh Petersen answered they had platted access easements to Kokomo Court, a
private road. Engineer Josh Petersen offered to confirm land ownership and access rights, for the adjacent
area, before the next discussion. Applicant Neal would purchase one acre to make the property line less jagged.
Commissioners requested identification of bordering zones, when the item returns. Property to the north, west
and east were A-1, property to the south was A-.5, per City Planner Maloy. Commissioners inquired if fee in
lieu funds could be used to improve the roadways. Community Development Director Blake Thomas clarified
13400 South and 6400 South were both capital facility roads, so any improvements would be reimbursed with
impact fees. Development of them was planned to take place in phases over the next couple years. The
Commissioners emphasized development should be completed as soon as possible, and requested the range of
lot sizes. Applicant Neal replied the smallest lot was around .256 and the largest lot was over a half acre. Their
goal was to keep 100 feet of frontage and have deep enough lots for a large backyard. Commissioners Berbert,
Garcia, and Jacobson agreed the zones should be buffered gradually, and half acres lots should border on the
side with the one acre sized lots. Commissioner Rypien agreed somewhat but emphasized the demand for
more quarter acre lots. Commissioner Jackson noticed the neighborhood had only one entrance for thirty plus
lots, and more would be needed. Applicant Neal agreed to work with Engineering to determine adequate road
locations.

         3.1    Request:         Text Change to Create a Village Mixed Use Overlay Zone (Public
                Hearing)
                Applicant:       Academy Village Land Holdings, LLC
                File Number: Z2020-044
City Planner Maloy stated this would be a recommendation to the City Council, and they will make the final
decision. The location was adjacent to Mountain View Corridor (MVC). The purpose of the Village Mixed Use
(VMU) Overlay Zone was to facilitate development of a mixed use master planned area for retail, restaurant,
grocery, professional services, office, multi-family residential, and hotel uses. The provisions would apply only
to land larger than 40 acres, located within the Village Mixed Use (VMU) overlay zone. New signage rules
would apply in the VMU overlay zone.

John Lindsley spoke on behalf of the applicant. He said they felt it was a solid plan, and would provide tax
revenue for Herriman City.

Chair Berbert opened the public hearing.

No comments were offered.

Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.

Commissioners asked about adjusting the height of signs due to elevations along separate sides of MVC.
City Planner Maloy clarified contiguous property may include intervening public roads. Commissioner Jackson
stated he thought the tall, verticle entrance and directional signs worked well. He offered feedback that the
monument sign was chaotic and difficult to read. He proposed a design standard modification to limit it to the
name of the shopping center. City Planner Maloy proposed finding a middle ground with the applicant.
Applicant Lindsley recognized Commissioner Jackson’s comment and respectfully disagreed. He stated
individual tenants wanted and requested this type of signage, and it was industry standard for leasing ability.
He stated each monument sign was very specific to draw customers into each area. Smaller directional signs
were necessary for storefront identification. Commissioners debated the appropriate number of businesses on
each sign. Applicant stated on average six or seven businesses would request to be on the monument sign.
Commissioner Rypien thought the monochramatic color scheme was easy to read. If desired the
Commissioners could include language that encouraged readability. Commissioner Powell said the signs would
be easily readable for those stopped at the intersection. Applicant Lindsley stated that input was received from
sign companies regarding the demographics, size, speed of road. Commissioner Powell emphasized the signs
themselves would come back for approval, and just the text change was being approved today.

Commissioner Andy Palmer MOVED to recommend approval to City Council for the item 3.1 Text Change to Create a Village
Mixed Use Overlay Zone (Public Hearing). Commissioner Brody Rypien SECONDED the motion.

         3.2     Request:        Text Change to the Land Development Code Regarding Electronic
         Signs (Public Hearing)
                 Applicant:      Herriman City
                 File Number: Z2020-059
City Planner Maloy explained the City proposed a text change to the Land Development Code regarding
electronic signs. The current ordinance has several requirements for electronic signs, including the restriction
that they shall not be permitted within 500 feet of residential use. This restriction was added as part of the
2017 update to the Land Development Code. There are areas of the City where commercial property was
located across the street from residential use, which eliminated the ability for commercial users to have an
electronic sign. Several new businesses interested in locating in the City have expressed the desire to have an
electronic sign. Amending this ordinance will allow the City to continue to attract commercial development
and businesses. Staff recommend removing the location restriction, and rely on the brightness, and animation
restriction to mitigate potential nuisances for nearby residences.

Chair Berbert thought electronic signs should be allowed within 500 feet of residential, if they are on MVC.
Commissioners were also concerned with the height of signs. The Commissioners debated appropriate
distances between homes and electronic signs. Chair Berbert offered the opinion they tend to deteriorate the
feel of the neighborhood. City Planner Maloy confirmed that if the Commission continued the item then staff
would continue to study options and issues.It was mentioned that schools now have flashing signs and they are
very close to homes. The Commissioners offered signs near residential areas should be turned off at night.

Chair Berbert opened the public hearing.

No comments were offered.

Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Adam Jacobson MOVED to continue item 3.2 Text Change to the Land Development Code Regarding Electronic
Signs (Public Hearing). Commissioner Heather Garcia SECONDED the motion.
