0 National Association of Development Organization (NADO) Report — Executive

Buttars reported he was in Washington D.C. at a NADO conference. The US Economic
Development Administration works through this agency and the Cache County
Economic Development Alliance (CCEDA) has applied for an Economic Development
Administration loan through the Federal government to develop their economic
development plan. While Executive Buttars was in D.C., he visited in Senator Hatch’s
office and Diane Browning will help with securing the funding for the CCEDA.

Opioid Crisis — Executive Buttars also attended a breakout session on the national
opioid crisis where possible solutions to the problem were discussed such as limiting the
amount of opioid that can be prescribed after surgery.

OO Events Center Update — Executive Buttars indicated stormy weather has slowed down

the roof insulation work, but the contractor is still planning to be on schedule. The Fair
Board has challenged them to have the work completed two weeks prior to the Fair so
an inaugural event can be held. The air conditioning unit is in and they will start to pour
some of the flooring around the outside of the building so some of the conduit can be
installed. Council member White asked if there will still be community booths at the Fair.
Council member Worthen replied there will be, but they will be a standard 8x8 size to be
compatible with the State Fair.

ITEMS OF SPECIAL INTEREST

°

2018 Legislative Session Summary Report — Representative Val Potter stated there
was surplus and most of it - $292,000,000.00 — went into education as a compromise
with the Our Schools Now promoters. HJR20 will be on the ballot in November which
will include a 10 cents per gallon increase with transportation receiving part and a
portion going toward education. If approved, this will be a nonbinding vote of the people
— the legislature will decide whether to implement it in the next session. Potter
sponsored HB106, an Education Grant Program created by Terryl Warner, where
someone convicted of a crime is given the opportunity to spend time in a technical
college instead of jail. The Justice Reinvestment Amendment which helps fund training
for inmates in county jails with an opportunity for GED and behavioral training.

Higher education funding issues were a disappointment. The League of Cities does a
better job of lobbying the Legislature than UAC does. There is a push in the legislature
to ignore county wishes because they are a “subdivision of the state.” Potter urged
Council members to push UAC to help county voices be heard. He had six bills pass
with a seventh at the last that was swept.

Council member Zilles said the 20 cents gas tax for education seems odd and asked
how it was received. Representative Potter responded it was not well received by some.
It is an unusual compromise, but the legislature felt it was better than the options. There
is a huge need for more transportation dollars which the legislature recognizes.

PUBLIC HEARING: MARCH 27, 2018-5:45 P.M.-PETERSBORO HEIGHTS REZONE —
Request for a rezone of 5.27 acres from the Agricultural (A10) Zone to the Rural 2 (RU2) Zone
located at 7000 West Center Street, Petersboro — Chris Harrild referred to the staff review and
said the average parcel size within a one-mile radius with a home is about 11 acres. The
Planning Commission’s recommended denial (4-2) is based on the five conclusions:

1. Location is not compatible with the purpose of the proposed RU-2 Zone - the average size of the
subdivided lots, if approved, would not be consistent with the average size of the surrounding
properties.

It is not consistent with the Cache Countywide Comprehensive Plan's vision.

Rezone does not advance the general welfare of the citizens of Cache County.

Approving a rezone for this single lot would set a precedent for adjacent properties.

The burden to serve and maintain this type of development pattern is not in the best interest of
Cache County or its citizens.

MAWN

Chairman Erickson opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Carol Bailey, applicant, said she believes she has a more accurate count of the size of each parcel with a
home on it with the results being — 63 total parcels with homes and the total number of parcels with
homes on 1-2 acres is 15; 2-3 acres is 8; 3-4 acres is 9 and 5 acres is 7. Thus 51%, or a majority, of the
parcels with homes are 1-3 acres. This request would result in splitting a 5-acre lot so the two parcels
would fall in the 1-3 acre size. She asks for approval.

Mike Bailey, applicant, said they only want to make legal what has existed since 2002. The former
owners told him it was zoned for two homes, but further investigation disproved that. There is an
abandoned house trailer and a burned out foundation. It will be very costly to remove the trailer and clean
up the area. He hopes to build a home to sell and recoup the cost of the cleanup and asks for approval.

Betty Hammond questioned the reasoning that this is agricultural property because the wind removes the
soil and another portion of it is in a floodplain. The best and highest purpose of the land is for a home
and she urges approval. Hammond provided letters of support from neighbors.

There was no other public comment.

PUBLIC HEARING: MARCH 27, 2018-6:00 P.M.-SPRING RIDGE REZONE — Request for a
rezone of 63.04 acres from the Agricultural (A10) Zone to the Rural 2 (RU2) Zone located at
7800 West 2300 North, Petersboro — Chris Harrild said this is another request for a rezone from
A-10 to RU-2. There is an RU-2 zone nearby with 34-35 lots in High Country Estates. If the
rezone is approved, there is a potential for thirty-one lots. The Planning Commission
recommends denial based on the following:

1. The location of the subject property is not compatible with the purpose of the proposed
Rural 2 (RU2) Zone. The average size of the subdivided lots, if approved, would not be
consistent with the average size of the surrounding properties.

2. The subject property is not adequately serviced by utilities or public service provision.

3. The rezone is contrary to the stated purpose of locating the RU2 zone adjacent to
municipalities.

PROCLAMATION
2018

WHEREAS, service to others is a hallmark of the American character, and central to how we meet our challenges; and

WHEREAS, the nation’s counties and cities are increasingly turning to national service and volunteerism as a cost-
effective strategy to meet their needs; and

WHEREAS, AmeriCorps and Senior Corps participants address the most pressing challenges facing our communities,
from educating students for the jobs of the 21st century, to fighting the opioid epidemic, to responding to natural disas-
ters, to supporting veterans and military families; and

WHEREAS, national service expands economic opportunity by creating more sustainable, resilient communities and
providing education, career skills, and leadership abilities for those who serve; and

WHEREAS, AmeriCorps and Senior Corps participants serve in more than 50,000 locations across the country, bolster-
‘ng the civic, neighborhood, and faith-based organizations that are so vital to our economic and social well-being; and

WHEREAS, national service participants increase the impact of the organizations they serve, both through their direct
service and by managing millions of additional volunteers; and

WHEREAS, national service represents a unique public-private partnership that invests in community solutions and
leverages non-federal resources to strengthen community impact and increase the return on taxpayer dollars; and

WHEREAS, national service participants demonstrate commitment, dedication, and patriotism by making an intensive
commitment to service, a commitment that remains with them in their future endeavors; and

WHEREAS, the Corporation for National and Community Service shares a priority with local leaders nationwide to en-
gage citizens, improve lives, and strengthen communities; and is joining with the National League of Cities, the National
Association of Counties, Cities of Service, and local leaders across the country for National Service Recognition Day on
April 10, 2018.

THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that I, Craig Buttars, Cache County Executive, do hereby proclaim April 10, 2018, as
National Service Recognition Day, and encourage residents to recognize the positive impact of national service in our
county; to thank those who serve; and to find ways to give back to their communities.

Lait) Bille

Craig Buttars, Cache County Executive VA

17.01.010: Title
A. This title shall be known as the Cache County Land Use Ordinance. hereinafter "this title”.

17.01.020: Authority and Purpose of Title 17

A. The Cache County Council adopts this title and associated maps and appendices pursuant to
the County Land Use Development and Management Act, Utah Code Annotated section 17-
27a as amended.

B. The primary purpose of this title is to promote the health. safety. and welfare of the present
and future inhabitants of the county. This includes but is not limited to securing safe routes of
travel. safety from fire and other natural and manmade dangers, the classification and
distribution of land use and development. protection of both residential and nonresidential
development. and the implementation of the goals and policies of the Countywide
Comprehensive Plan.

17.01.030: Applicability
This title applies to all lands within the unincorporated area of the county.

17.01.040: Permits Required

A. All development must obtain the review and approval of the specified land use authority.

B. The development, alteration, or use of all buildings and property must follow the
requirements of this title.

17.01.050: Exemptions

A. Properties owned and operated by the State of Utah or the federal government are exempt
from the requirements this title.

B. This exemption does not apply where state or federal law requires that the agency of federal
or state government take steps to comply with all applicable local regulations

17.01.060: Severability of Provisions

A. If any section, provision, sentence, or clause of this title is declared unconstitutional by a
court of competent jurisdiction, such determination does not impair the validity of the
remainder of this title: the remainder of this title remains in full force and effect.
