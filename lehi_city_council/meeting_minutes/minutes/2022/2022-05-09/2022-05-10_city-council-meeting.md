2. Joint Work Session with the Planning Commission
2.1 Discussion of Moderate-Income Housing Element goals and new State requirements.
Brittney Harris, City Planner, lead a discussion on Moderate-Income Housing and the goals
required by the State. She stated that they would like to be more innovative with their goals and
try to think outside of the box. She stated that some of the causes for high housing prices have
been the lack of supply for the demand, lack of skilled labor, inflation, individual consumer and
student debt, high paying jobs increasing income disparity, and investment buying. She reported
that the median household income in 2020 in Lehi was $101,429. In Utah County it was $77,057.

Ms. Harris reported on the survey results from residents and developers. She stated that things to
consider that may help reach the goals include regulations that aren’t over burdensome,
consistency between cities, and different living situations and family types.

2.2 Discussion of Bike and Pedestrian Master Plan Update
Mike West, Planning Department, gave an update on the Bike and Pedestrian Master Plan. He said
that the Council should consider its priorities and determine if they want to have places that invite
more bike and pedestrian activity.

3. Discussion of Fencing Along City Trails.
Steve Marchbanks, Parks Director, stated that generally they haven’t split costs with residents
that want to install a fence along city trails. He said that if they begin doing this for everyone
then it would quickly consume the budget. He suggested that they could look at it on a case-by-
case basis. The Council also brought up concerns that residents may seek reimbursement from
the city for a fence that they paid for.

18. Consideration of Ordinance #29-2022, amendments to the Lehi City Development Code,
    revising areas where accessory dwelling units are not allowed.
     Kim Struthers stated that they are trying to meet the State’s requirements for accessory
    dwelling units (ADUs) allowed in the city. He said that they are allowing them in areas
    where they know they have more sewer capacity and restrict them in other areas where it
    would have the largest impact to the system.

    Brenden Ethington inquired as to how the restricted areas were determined. He asked that his
    area be restricted.

    Erin Ethington agreed with the previous comments and was also concerned about how
    residents were notified.

    There was discussion of tabling the item and directing staff to look at The Crossings
    neighborhood and determine if it’s possible to include it in the restrictions, and also look
    more closely at other areas that could be restricted.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to table this item and directed staff to look at including
                ADU restrictions in the crossings neighborhood and other areas. Councilor
                Southwick seconded the motion.
