Recognizing the Boots and Bunker program for over 30 years of service to
schools in the Ogden area
          A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of the Boots and Bunker program for over 30 years of service to
schools in the Ogden area came before the Council for consideration. Council member Stephens read the certificate of recognition
issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Caldwell and presented representatives of the Ogden Fire Department with the
certificate.
          Chief Mathieu thanked the Mayor and Council for this recognition of the Boots and Bunker program and he introduced
the present and past members of his Department who have kept the program going since 1990; there are 15 individuals who have
filled the shoes Boots and Bunker over that time period, but one individual has been with the program since its inception in a
volunteer capacity: Marie Tyron. She has made significant contributions to the program and the Department is very grateful for
her service.
          Chair Blair then encouraged comments from the Firefighters who have served in the Boots and Bunker roles. Joe
Anderson discussed the initial creation of the Boots and Bunker program; he initially refused to serve as a clown in the program,
but after seeing a similar program in another community, he understood the significance and importance of it. It has been
successful because of the many great employees who also saw the significance of the program and because of the support from
Department administration. It is now the longest running Boots and Bunker program in the State of Utah.
          Another Firefighter also spoke about his participation in the program and the opportunity the Ogden employees have had
to learn from other clowns in other communities. It his belief that the Ogden Boots and Bunker program is better than any in the
western United States. He relayed a story about why the program matters; Boots and Bunker visited a school in Morgan County to
provide lifesaving training. They later received a postcard from the principal of the school who told them about children being
approached by a stranger in a car who told them to get in. The children followed what they had been taught and they ran to fmd a
trusted adult. When the principal interviewed them, they stated that they just did what Boots and Bunker told them. He noted that
if even just one life has been saved by the program, it is worthwhile. It is not possible to quantify the value of the program; up to
15,000 kids receive the Boots and Bunker training each year and over the last 30 years that equates to over 400,000 kids that have
received the message of life and fire safety. He stated he is grateful to have been a part of it.
          Chair Blair stated that his own children have had the opportunity to attend Boots and Bunker training and it was very
impactful to them. He is grateful for the impact the program has had on the community.

      Consideration of modified Resolution 2021-13 adding Weber County as an
      entity for the Northern Utah Venture Fund
               A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider ratification of modified Resolution 2021-13 adding
      Weber County as an entity for the Northern Utah Venture Fund The memo stated the modified resolution authorizes an interlocal
      cooperative agreement with Weber State University, Ogden Weber Technical College, Davis County Council of Governments,
      Weber County, and Davis Technical College to create the Northern Utah Development Foundation which will manage the
      Northern Utah Venture Fund. The Fund will seek investors to fund startup costs for promising new businesses. The ratification is
      required to provide a clear record for including Weber County as a party to the interlocal agreement.
               Chair Blair introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2021-13, entitled:
                        "A resolution of the Ogden City Council approving an Interlocal Agreement between Weber State University,
                        Ogden Weber Technical College, Davis Technical College, the Davis County Council of Governments, Weber
                        County, and Ogden City to create a Venture Capital Fund to encourage and support the economic development
                        in Northern Utah."
              COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE RESOLUTION BE ADOPTED, AND THE CHAIR BE
      AUTHORIZED TO SIGN THE SAME. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY VICE CHAIR WHITE WITH THE
      FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE- COUNCIL MEMBERS CHOBERKA, HYER, STEPHENS, VICE
      CHAIR WHITE, AND CHAIR BLAIR. VOTING NO- NONE. Council members Lopez and Nadolski were not present
      when this vote was taken.

      Public Comments
                Ed McKinney stated he is past member of the Ogden Airport Advisory Committee; he referenced the agenda item dealing
      with the appointments and reappointments of the Ogden Airport Advisory Committee and stated the purpose ofhis comments are
      to suggest that the Council not approve that list of candidates with the exception of Robert Dandoy. Having sat on several Airport
      Advisory Boards in many states, he can say the role of Airport Manager requires some of the most in-depth and diverse
      knowledge of any municipal job; with this in mind, it is equally important that the Advisory Committee have similar in-depth
      knowledge on topics such as grant assurances, FAA regulations, liability insurance, and airport safety. The candidates that have
      been recmmnended for appointment have little to no aviation knowledge, skills, or experience and no in-depth relationships or
      understanding of the businesses or stakeholders at the Ogden Airport and they lack the skills and experience to advise the Airport
      Manager in Ogden City as is required by Ordinance2021-6, Section 2(c)(1)(a). As a result, he appealed to the Council to fill these
      critical roles with candidates who can speak with authority on airport and aviation issues and opportunities.
                 Angel Castillo referenced the work session agenda item relating to ordinance text amendments regarding conditional use
      permits (CUPs). She is most concerned about an exterior favade change not requiring Planning Commission input and approval.
      She supports the other adjustments as she feels they will help Planning staff process applications more efficiently, but the
      Planning staff is not as representative of the community as the Planning Commission is and, therefore, the Planning Commission
      is better equipped to handle request for exterior favade changes. Until the City adopts a very specific list of exterior building
      materials to govern decisions of Planning staff, she suggests that type of CUP amendment should be referred to the Planning
      Commission. She referenced another work session item dealing with a zone change that would allow development of a housing
      project on a cul-de-sac; she does not believe cul-de-sacs are a wise use of public investment in tenns offacilities. Additionally, the
      Council needs to consider the smallest residential lot size they are comfmtable with and how many single-family homes can be
      built on a smaller lot; the City needs high density housing, single family homes, and townhomes because there is a demand for all
      types of housing right now. However, developers in the cmmnunity are stymied by development regulations that are too restrictive
      for the current market.
                Priscilla Martinez simply stated she would echo Ms. Castillo's input.
