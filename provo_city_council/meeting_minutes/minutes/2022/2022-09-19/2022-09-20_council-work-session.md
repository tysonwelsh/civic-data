Bill Peperone, Development Services Director, presented these three related items together.
These recently went to the Planning Commission, along with the concept plan. This proposal
includes a rezone from research and business park to medium-density residential; the developer
already owns other property zoned as MDR across the river next to University Avenue; they are
essentially relocating the MDR designation so the portion along University Avenue can be
changed to commercial. The residential portion includes 198 units and exceeds the needed open
space and parking requirements. Mr. Peperone noted neighboring property owners have raised
many questions; they plan to address these questions in detail at the Council Meeting that
evening. Mr. Peperone addressed the plans for the street through the development, as well as
how the design facilitates future installation of a signaled intersection at 5600 North and
University Avenue (which will eventually tie in with River Run Road and Canyon Road). The
Council will consider two general plan amendments and a zone change.

Councilor Bill Fillmore asked about the vision for this project and how the parcels were
connected. Mr. Peperone explained that the property owner wants to take the maker spaces and
innovation spaces Provo has become known for and take that to the next level. This project
would expand the availability of commercial office space, as many companies end up leaving
Provo as they expand and outgrow the office space available. This would also locate housing
units nearby for those who want to utilize the makerspaces. The townhome units will be owned
and rented out by the developer. Although this would not expand owner-occupancy opportunities
in the City, it also prevents the townhomes from being bought by investors and potentially over-
occupied. With local ownership by one entity, the maintenance will also be better coordinated.
Ryan Smith, founder of Qualtrics, is the project owner. Mr. Fillmore shared comments about
innovation and incubating technology companies in Provo. Mr. Peperone also addressed several
questions about the building height requirements and maximums allowed. Mr. Fillmore asked
about several of the surrounding properties; Mr. Peperone related some of the issues with those
properties, including one located in Orem.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked about the relation between this area and the Community
Development project area the Redevelopment Agency previously approved. Keith Morey,
Economic Development Director, explained that the Community Development Project Area was
adjacent to this parcel, on the Qualtrics campus, and it was approved but never activated.
Councilor George Handley shared concerns he received from Ron Woolley about potential coal
ash contamination. Mr. Peperone indicated the developer would do soil testing to make sure
there are no problems with the site. Mr. Peperone reiterated that the project exceeds the
requirement for 10% open space in the MDR zone, as it has even more open space. The
developer has also met and exceeded the amount of required parking, which is rare. Presentation
only. These three items were scheduled for the Council Meeting on September 20, 2022.

4.      A resolution adopting the Provo Moderate-Income Housing Supply and Strategies
        Report. (22-069) (0:32:02)

Brandon Larsen, Planning Supervisor, presented. He referenced several changes to the report,
including several corrections and updated references. The most notable change is that the report
now recognizes 440 accessory dwelling units (ADUs). Mr. Larsen invited questions and
feedback on the updated version, which had previously been distributed to the Council. The City
is required by State law to adopt the report by October 1.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth commented on the approval timeline for the state. Councilor
Rachel Whipple asked about several of the definitions in the report and whether a single-family
home with an ADU is still designated as such; Hannah Salzl and Brandon Larsen indicated that
such a residence is still considered a single-family home. Ms. Whipple noted that 2.4% of single-
family homes having a registered or permitted ADU was extremely low. Mr. Larsen agreed with
Ms. Whipple’s suggestion that if there were more ADUs, that would qualify more housing units
as moderate-income housing. Councilor Travis Hoban commented on the ratio of ADUs and
rental housing in the City, and whether more ADUs were needed since Provo has many rentals.

Councilor Bill Fillmore thought this needed additional public input. He also asked without the
housing audit, how confident Development Services was about the ADU figure. Mr. Larsen
explained that 440 is the number of licensed or registered ADUs in the City. In response to
additional questions from Mr. Fillmore, Mr. Larsen explained that this report did not establish
specific quotas for ADUs. Hannah Salzl, Planner, added to this response and clarified that the
numbers in this report become Provo’s benchmark and generally the report requires some
improvement, so in future years Provo’s improvement will be measured against this initial
report. Mr. Larsen added that the State uses this report to evaluate the City’s compliance with
state code and there are some funding opportunities contingent on the report. He explained that
every city has to adopt at least three strategies identified by the State; Provo has to adopt a fourth
since Provo has a fixed rail system (Frontrunner).

Ms. Ellsworth asked where the strategies originated. Mr. Larsen explained that the state provides
a list of which the City must choose three strategies, with the additional fourth strategy identified
based on the rail system. Ms. Salzl indicated that the implementation matrix for housing goals in
general is part of the General Plan. She indicated that if the Council has concerns about goals in
this table, as long as they aren’t required by the state, they can remove them. Staff offered
clarification on the purpose and scope of the report.

Councilor Katrice MacKay shared comments about ADUs and her concern that allowing more
ADUs would result in less availability of larger single-family homes. She noted that Provo has a
high percentage of low- and moderate-income housing compared to surrounding cities.

Councilor George Handley shared comments. He noted that the student population is not
distinguished in the report. He felt that it was misleading to consider Provo as leading the state in
low- and moderate-income housing because much of the housing in those categories is student
housing; he suspected that without the students, Provo’s ratio was much closer to the state
