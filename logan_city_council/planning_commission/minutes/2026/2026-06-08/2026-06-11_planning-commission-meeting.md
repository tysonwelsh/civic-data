PUBLIC HEARING:

PC 26-022 Honey Rezone [Zone Change] George Honey, authorized agent/owner is requesting the
rezone of a 0.21-acre property located at 165 North 200 East from Traditional Neighborhood
Residential (NR-6) to Town Center Two (TC-2); TIN 06-066-0024, in the Adams Neighborhood.

Staff: Planner, Aimee Egbert presented a request to rezone a 0.21-acre property located at 165
North 200 East from Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) to Town Center Two (TC-2). The
property contains a single-family dwelling in the front and an apartment above the detached garage
in the rear. A studio apartment was constructed on the ground floor of the garage without a building
permit. A previous owner sought recognition of the property as containing three legal dwelling units;
however, the request was denied due to insufficient evidence that the third unit had been legally
established. The property is currently recognized as containing two legal units.

A. Egbert explained that the TC-2 zone permits residential density of up to 30 units per acre, which
could allow up to six dwelling units on the site, subject to compliance with building permits and
applicable development standards. A previous rezone request for the property was denied by the
City Council in September 2025 due to uncertainty surrounding updates to the Future Land Use Plan
(FLUP). The recently adopted FLUP designates the area Downtown, which is generally consistent
with the proposed TC-2 zoning designation.

Proponent: George Honey, property owner, stated that the request is intended to legalize the
existing residential units on the property. He also discussed the need for affordable housing and
stated that the proposed units would be compatible with the surrounding area.

Public: No public comments were made.

Commission: Commission Doutre stated that the proposed zoning is consistent with the recently
adopted FLUP. Commissioner Lewis expressed concern that the TC-2 zone could allow up to six
dwelling units on this property. Community Development Director, Russ Holley explained that any


redevelopment would be required to comply with applicable height limits, setbacks, open space
requirements, parking standards, and other development regulations.

Commissioner Lewis asked about the maximum building height permitted in the TC-2 zone. R. Holley
stated that the maximum height is 45 feet; however, because the property is adjacent to a
neighborhood residential zone across 200 East, building height would be limited to 35 feet along the
street frontage and could increase to 45 feet at a ratio of two feet horizontal for every one foot of
additional vertical height.

MOTION: Commissioner Lucero made a motion to forward a recommendation of approval to the
Municipal Council on a zone change for PC 26-022 with the findings for approval as listed below.
Commissioner Duncan seconded the motion. The motion was approved 4 to 1.

FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. The surrounding zoning to the west is TC-2 and a rezone of this property will result in a contiguous
   zoning pattern on the block.
2. The TC-2 zone is appropriate because it aligns with the adopted FLUP and fits into the existing
   land use patterns of the block.
3. TC-2 zoning and land uses are in close proximity to transportation choices, downtown commercial
   goods and services, and employment centers.
4. The majority of the surrounding existing land uses to the west are commercial in nature.
5. The LDC development standards will ensure a quality project that will be compatible with the
   surrounding neighborhood.

PC 26-023 LDC Amendment - Wetlands [Code Amendment] Russ Holley/Logan City are proposing
amendments to the Land Development Code Chapter 17.24 Development Standards for Wetlands.

Staff/Proponent: Community Development Director Russ Holley presented proposed amendments
to Chapter 17.24 of the Land Development Code regarding wetland regulations within the Critical
Lands Overlay Zone. He explained that the current wetland standards were adopted in 2023 and that
while riparian area regulations were updated in 2025, the wetland requirements remained
unchanged. Staff found that the existing 50-foot wetland setback can create significant development
constraints, particularly on smaller properties.

The proposed amendments would require City staff to determine when a wetland delineation is
necessary based on National Wetlands Inventory mapping, aerial photography, site inspections, or
other evidence indicating potential wetlands. Development applications would not proceed to a
decision-making body until a wetland delineation has been approved in writing by the U.S. Army
Corps of Engineers (USACE) or the USACE has provided documentation confirming that wetlands
are not present on the subject property.

The amendments establish a 10-foot no-disturbance buffer adjacent to delineated wetlands for all
public and private projects, except for approved native vegetation restoration activities. Additional
setback requirements vary based on project type and size. Development projects of 10 acres or more
would require a 50-foot setback for structures, paved parking areas, and driveways, and a 15-foot
setback for stormwater facilities, sidewalks, trails, patios, and other site improvements. Development


projects of less than 10 acres would require a 20-foot setback for structures, paved parking areas,
and driveways, and a 15-foot setback for other site improvements.

For public and recreational projects, a 20-foot setback would apply to structures, paved parking
areas, and driveways, with a 15-foot setback for stormwater facilities, streets, roads, sidewalks, trails,
and similar improvements. Boardwalk trails may be permitted within wetlands and setback areas with
USACE approval. For residential subdivisions, no new building lot line may be established within 10
feet of a delineated wetland. Wetlands and no-disturbance areas would be placed on separate
parcels and maintained in accordance with project approvals and City regulations.

Public: No public comments were made

Commission: The Commission discussed the proposed distinction between projects larger and
smaller than 10 acres and whether setback requirements should decrease incrementally based on
project size rather than applying a single threshold. Commissioner Lucero expressed concern that
developers could potentially subdivide larger properties into parcels smaller than 10 acres to qualify
for reduced setbacks and questioned the basis for selecting the 10-acre threshold. R. Holley
responded that 10 acres threshold was chosen because it is the size of a city block. Commissioner
Doutre requested data showing the acreage of vacant properties containing potential wetlands to
better evaluate the proposed threshold.

R. Holley stated that staff could prepare examples of undeveloped properties illustrating parcel
acreage and the resulting wetland setback impacts. He indicated that this information could assist
the Commission in determining whether a different acreage threshold would be more appropriate.

MOTION: Commissioner Lucero made a motion to continue PC 26-023 to the July 9, 2026, meeting.
Commissioner Heare seconded the motion. The motion was approved unanimously.

WORKSHOP:

Maple View Subdivision – Planned Development Overlay

Staff: Planner, Aaron Smith reviewed the Planned Development Overlay (PDO) process, which
allows for flexibility of zoning regulations for projects larger than three acres in size. The PDO is a
two-part process, the first step is a concept plan review, where a preliminary layout is presented to
Planning Commission and City Council for feedback; no formal action is taken at this stage. The
second step is the formal PD project review, which includes a zoning map amendment to apply the
Overlay Zone, a Land Development Code amendment identifying specific zoning exceptions, and a
preliminary subdivision plat. Similar projects that have been approved through this process include
Willow Lakes and Cache Valley Marketplace.

The parcel came through Planning Commission in February 2026 as a rezone request to MR-9. At
that time the Planning Commission recommended denial of the rezone and eventually the application
was withdrawn before the hearing with City Council. During the rezone application review process,
the proponent expressed interest in a development agreement to restrict overall density but allow
additional housing types and lot sizes. Direction was given that the PD Overlay is the preferred
process to bring forward custom development options.

The proposed Maple View Subdivision is a 10-acre development located at approximately 350 South
and 1400 West. It includes 51 single-family building lots ranging in size from 4,000 to 10,000 square
feet. The subdivision is designed with street connections to the existing 350 South Street to the east
and 1400 West Street to the west, along with proposed street stubs to the north and the south to


support future connectivity. Staff is recommending a street connection to 290 South through City-
owned property located north of the subdivision. This connection would leave a remnant parcel of
approximately 10,000 square feet, which could potentially be sold or conveyed to the developer and
incorporated as an additional residential lot if traded for additional public street construction, and
subject to future city approval.

The property is currently zoned NR-4, and the Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) designates the area as
Low Density Residential, which allows for single- and two-family dwellings at a density of 1-9 units
per acre. This designation is consistent with the scale and character of surrounding neighborhoods.
The proposed gross density of the project is approximately 5 units per acre.

Under existing NR-4 zoning, the site could accommodate up to approximately 40 units; however, due
to constraints including a non-developable landfill overlay in the northwest corner and required
streets, the realistic yield under the current zone is approximately 28 lots.

The proposed subdivision includes a mix of lot sizes to provide variety within the development. Seven
lots (approximately 25%) are approximately 10,000 square feet, consistent with NR-4 standards.
Seven additional lots (approximately 16%) are approximately 6,000 square feet, similar to NR-6
zoning. The majority of the development—37 lots—are approximately 4,000 square feet, similar to
MR-9 zoning standards.

The proposed development fronts 1400 West, which is designated as a future minor arterial in the
Transportation Master Plan and ultimately requires a 102-foot right-of-way. As part of this project, the
applicant will be required to dedicate one-half of the ultimate right-of-way width (51 feet) along the
property's frontage.

Because projected traffic generated by the development, combined with existing traffic volumes on
1400 West, does not warrant construction of the full minor arterial cross section at this time, the
applicant will only be required to construct improvements to the City's collector street standard. This
includes constructing one-half of the 80-foot collector street cross section from the centerline of the
existing 1400 West right-of-way to the property line, while preserving sufficient right-of-way for the
future expansion of 1400 West to its ultimate minor arterial width.

PROPONENT: Brad Brown stated that the applicant's goal is to create a mix of residential densities
that are compatible with the surrounding neighborhood while helping the City meet its housing
objectives.

Commissioner Lucero commented that she would like to see greater residential density and asked
where townhomes would be located if they were incorporated into the project. Commissioner Heare
said he was surprised the revised proposal did not include multifamily housing and expressed support
for including twin homes. Mr. Brown responded that townhomes would most likely be located near
the park area, but feedback received on the previous concept had discouraged that approach.
Commissioner Doutre stated that if the applicant is requesting a Planned Development Overlay, the
project should include a greater diversity of housing types. Commissioner Duncan agreed that adding
multifamily housing would strengthen the proposal.

Commissioner Doutre asked about the proposed open space adjacent to the existing City park. R.
Holley stated that staff would need to discuss with Russ Akina, Parks and Recreation Director,
whether the open space should ultimately become part of the public park. Additional discussions
would also be needed regarding the proposed street connection to 290 South and the disposition of
the remaining parcel adjacent to the subdivision. Aaron Smith added that a portion of the open space
would also serve as stormwater detention.


Commissioner Doutre commented that, in the long term, it would make sense for the open space to
become part of the public park rather than remain under homeowners’ association maintenance. Mr.
Holley agreed, noting that the area would visually appear to be part of the park, and without fencing,
the public would likely assume it was public property.

Commissioner Heare asked whether the private drive located in the center of the subdivision would
require an emergency vehicle turnaround. A. Smith stated that the applicant would be required to
provide a hammerhead turnaround to accommodate fire apparatus at the end of the private drive.

Commissioner Lucero reiterated that increased residential density should be encouraged, noting that
the City lacks sufficient developments that provide a true mix of housing types.

The Commission discussed the possibility of constructing accessory dwelling units (ADUs) on the
larger lots. Mr. Brown explained that the applicant's role is limited to developing the subdivision and
selling the lots rather than constructing the homes. R. Holley clarified that this Planned Development
applies only to the subdivision layout and that the individual single-family lots may ultimately be
developed by different builders and property owners without Design Review Permits.

In summary, the Commissioners indicated they would like the applicant to further explore
incorporating a greater mix of housing types, such as townhomes or twin homes, and to work with
City staff to evaluate whether the proposed open space should become part of the adjacent public
park.

Aaron Smith reviewed the upcoming projects for the July 9, 2026, meeting.


Meeting adjourned at 7:04 p.m.
