Fiscal Year 2021 Budget Amendment- River Restoration CIP Grants
         Comptroller Stout explained the proposed budget amendment recognizes revenues from two grant awards. A grant from
the Utah Department ofNatural Resources ($68,500) was awarded for completion of the Ogden River Marriot Ditch Diversion
Reconstruction Phase 1. A grant from the U.S. Department of Agriculture ($389,9500) is for debris removal and erosion
prevention along the Ogden River. These funds will be appropriated to the Storm Sewer Utility Fund and expended as part of the
River Restoration CIP project. A grant match of$111,916 for the USDA grant will come from previously appropriated funds in
the River Restoration CIP project. Administration requests the Council consider setting a public hearing to consider the proposed
budget amendment.

Fiscal Year 2022 Budget
          General Fund Revenues: Comptroller Stout used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide an overview of General
Fund revenues in the Fiscal Year (FY) 2022 Tentative Budget. She provided a comparison of tax revenues included in the adopted
FY21 budget and the projected revenues in the FY22 tentative budget; sales tax revenues are expected to increase by $5.8 million,
or 37.52 percent and property tax revenues are expected to increase by 0.97 percent or $157,675. Franchise and fee in lieu oftaxes
are both projected to decrease by $25,000. All other miscellaneous revenue sources in the General Fund are expected to increase
by $1.3 million, or 6.49 percent. Other governmental fund revenue sources, including revenues like special assessment funds and
grant funds, are expected to increase by $7 million, or 39.71 percent. Proprietary funds, or enterprise funds, are expected to
increase by $17 million, or 19.94 percent. This includes all utility funds, the Airport and Golf Course funds, property management
funds from Business Depot 0 gden (BDO), and the medical services fund. Internal service funds are expected to decrease by $1.4
million, or 7.30 percent. The grand total of all revenue sources in the City budget are expected to increase by $30 million, or 16.30
percent when compared to FY21.
          Vice Chair White stated she is concerned about the sales tax revenue projections, and she asked if Administration is
aware of the current amount of sales tax revenue the City has collected this year to date. Ms. Stout stated that City Administration
has created an internal committee that meets monthly to closely monitor sales tax trends and revenues. The City receives sales tax
revenues around the 20th of every month and each month the revenues have exceed projections by nearly $500,000. Based upon
these actual revenue collections, Administration estimates the FY21 revenue project will be exceeded by $1.8 million. This
information was used to formulate the FY22 sales tax revenue projection.
          Business Depot Ogden Lease Revenues: Comptroller Stout then provided an overview of the tentative budget for BDO
Lease Revenue appropriations. The proposed appropriations are directed by the BDO Lease Revenue ordinance that has been
adopted by the City Council, including funding for the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative, Marshall White Center Reserve Fund,
and Central Business District (CBD) Mall District debt service payments. Additional appropriations will be made to public arts,
Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Projects, and miscellaneous non-CIP projects and expenditures. The total use of BDO Lease
Revenue in the FY22 budget is $12,787,300.
          Compensation Study Changes: Management Services Director Brown then discussed the proposed budget for employee
compensation in the FY22 Tentative Budget; the budget contemplates 69 job classification title changes, the adoption of new pay
ranges for merit and non-merit employees, and adoption of new step ranges for sworn fire and police employees. Phase one of
implementing the compensation study recommendations was a four percent range adjustment in FY21; phase two is bringing all
non-sworn employees to the minimum level for their wage range and, if that is less than a two percent increase, their pay will be
further increased for a total of four percent. Sworn employees will be moved to their closest step and date of service step in their
wage scale. The total fiscal impact of these two actions is $4.25 million. She then presented a chart illustrating all staffmg changes
proposed in FY22 as identified in the Compensation and Benefit Study staffmg document. She indicated she will review this
information in greater detail in the future review of the overall budget for her Department. She concluded by summarizing
changes to employee benefits; there are no retirement contribution rate adjustments proposed in FY22; the City's contribution to
the two-party health savings account (HSA) will be increased to the former family HSA level; and the City's insurance plan will
transition to a self-funded plan which will help employees control the way they shop for health services and flatten the annual
increase for employee health insurance.

           As a result of the ordinance amendments, the CIP and budget will now be reviewed together to allow a better alignment
of CIP planning and proposed funding in year one of the CIP. The CIP will still stand as a long-term planning document for the
City and CIP projects will be reviewed with each of the Departments' proposed budgets through the budget review process. The
CIP will ultimately be adopted with the annual budget. Review of the CIP commences with tonight's discussion and culminates
with a public hearing on June 22, 2021. There will be several opportunities for detailed review of various Department CIPs
between those two dates. The Planning Commission's role is to review the proposed CIP for consistency with the City's General
Plan; they reviewed the CIP at their April 7, 2021 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of approval. The Ogden Archway
Project (GCOO 1) was added to the CIP after submittal to the Planning Commission but reviewed by the Commission with the full
plan; it is proposed for early funding as part ofthe FY21 budget and CIP amendments item.
