2. Presentations and Reports
2.1) Ranked Choice Voting Discussion
Mayor Johnson stated that this is also on the agenda for a vote. Mr. Walker stated that there was
discussion of this at the last meeting. Tonight’s vote just allows the conversation to continue. It
won’t lock them into having an RCV election. Shayne Ruitenbeek, Management Analyst, gave a
presentation on Ranked Choice Voting and explained how the votes are tallied. She also noted that
a voter doesn’t have to rank each candidate. A voter can decide how many candidates they want
to rank. Mrs. Ruitenbeek explained that in the first round, the first ranked votes are tallied. If no
one reaches 50% of the total vote, then the person with the least amount of votes is eliminated and
then that continues until someone is declared the winner with 50% of the vote. It’s a little more
complicated with a multiple seat election, because there are multiple cycles with multiple rounds.
She also said that if a candidate is never eliminated (either by being the winner or loser) in each
cycle, then the voters that raked that person first, would never have their other votes redistributed
and therefore not counted. Councilor Koivisto stated that she wants to understand this more and
has not made a decision.

Ron Smith expressed his support for Ranked Choice Voting.

7. Consideration of Resolution #2020-79, authorizing the Steel Solar Project Transaction
   Schedule under the Master Firm Power Supply Agreement with Utah Associated
   Municipal Power Systems (UAMPS); and related matters
   Joel Eves, Power Director, and Crystal Robinson presented on the Steel Solar Project. Mr.
   Eves noted that the location is in Box Elder County, the pricing is $31.45 / MWh and the terms
   of the agreement will be for 25 years. He noted that the purchase agreement includes a
   scholarship program for seniors that live in communities serviced by UAMPS. Ms. Robinson
   then reported on current resource projects and upcoming projects. She stated that the peaking
   resources are a concern as the city growth, so they continue to evaluate and study opportunities.
   Councilor Condie inquired about the internal power generators because residents have inquired
   about why they were needed if they aren’t running. Mr. Eves stated that it’s a difficult concept
   to understand. He explained that it’s a good thing not to have them running because that means
   that the market isn’t high enough that they’re in trouble. He said the generators easily pay for
   themselves during those peak times because the purchase price is so high. He said that this
   summer they saw market prices go to $1,000 for a KWh, and they can internally generate for
   $35 per KWh. He said that’s the volatility in the market that they are preparing for. He also
   explained that the internal generation has non-monetary benefits in an emergency.

    Motion:     Councilor Koivisto moved to approve Resolution #2020-79, authorizing the Steel
                Solar Project Transaction Schedule under the Master Firm Power Supply
                Agreement with Utah Associated Municipal Power Systems (UAMPS); and related
                matters. Councilor Southwick seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Southwick, Yes;
    Councilor Hancock, Yes; and Councilor Koivisto, Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
