1.     PUBLIC HEARING – An Application to Approve a Conditional Use Permit for a
       Billboard Relocation and Modification located at 155 West 3300 South.

Action Item

Applicant:     Reagan Outdoor Advertising
Address:       155 West 3300 South

City Planner, Jed Shum presented the Staff Report and stated that the request is from Reagan
Outdoor Advertising for a Conditional Use Permit at 155 West 3300 South. The intent is to modify
the existing billboard in the Transit-Oriented Development Zone. The existing billboard will be
removed and the square footage combined with the previously removed billboard located at 202
East 3300 South. The billboard at 125 West 3300 South will also be removed and the square
footage of the two billboards combined. A photo showing the current condition of the billboards
was shown. The new proposed billboard will combine the square footage from the green box
billboard and the previous one that was removed previously at another location. The proposed
elevation and size will comply with the Code. Ms. Ungricht reported that the elevation of the
proposed billboard will change slightly. The applicant made Staff aware that instead of being
stacked they will be side by side. The square footage and height will not change.

Mr. Shum stated that staff found that the proposed project will meet the Land Use &
Neighborhoods Goal 1 as they continue to welcome new residents and businesses into South Salt
Lake City. Reagan Outdoor Advertising has been a long-term business with assets in the City. It
also meets the Land Use and Neighborhoods Goal 3, which is to enhance urban and streetscape
design in the City to support South Salt Lake City’s distinct image and enhance community pride.
As part of the proposed relocation and modification, Reagan outdoor advertising has proposed to
upgrade the new billboard and include upgraded lighting

Staff recommended the Planning Commission approve the request based on the Findings of Fact,
Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of Approval set forth in the Staff Report. The Planning
Commission can approve, deny, or continue the request.

Mr. Shum reported that the square footage of the proposed billboard will only use the square
footage from 202 East 3300 South and the billboard at 125 West 3300 South. The State no longer
allows new square footage to be added to billboards. As a result, Reagan can only use what exists.

For the In-N-Out Burger billboard, they will use square footages from the boxes shown in white
and red. For the proposed billboard, they will only use the square footage from the green billboard.
The total square footage from the four panels will be applied toward the relocated sign to make
space for the In-N-Out Burger.

Ms. Ungricht stated that the sign in green is being incorporated as well as the sign that was
demolished further east on 3300 South. The sign for In-N-Out was brought up because the square
footage from the red sign was allocated toward In-N-Out and has not yet been demolished. The
new sign will be roughly in the same location as the smaller white one. The billboard in yellow is
no longer standing. The structure was not owned by Reagan so the square footage does not count.

A question was raised as to whether the Commission can deny the side-by-side layout. A question
was also raised about height. Ms. Ungricht stated that the images provided by staff were not to
scale. With regard to whether the billboards will be extended into the street, Ms. Ungricht stated
that the Code requires that the edge of the sign be in the setback but not the pole. Depending on
how it is laid out, it cannot go over the sidewalk or the public right-of-way. The sign can be pushed
in further to meet the current setbacks standards.
Guy Larson from Reagan Outdoor Advertising reported that Reagan Outdoor Advertising owned
the property years ago and they have an easement in perpetuity there now on the entire parcel.
About two years ago they were approached by Block 33 who planned to develop housing there.
They reached out to Reagan because they have an easement and questioned whether they could do
anything about the location of the sign. Mr. Larson informed them that their future goal was to
move the sign from the east to the proposed location and combine some square footage.
Mr. Larson stated that they originally spoke to them about the stacked unit design. Because the
bottom panel is a visual eyesore, they asked if they could put it side-by-side, which would benefit
their development. Because development had not yet occurred, they proposed to change the
configuration to make it more developable. Mr. Larson’s preference was to place it side-by-side
in the event the property is ever developed. He stated that they will not be overhanging the
sidewalk and will have a setback.

Mr. Larson stated that they were recently before the Commission regarding the In-N-Out Burger
application and are starting construction. They took the previous stacked sign down and the new
pole is coming next week at which time they will begin construction. The smaller sign will be
demolished this coming week since that square footage is allocated to the In-N-Out Burger. The
benefit to the City is that it will have two fewer poles but the same square footage and height.

Chair Carter commented that he exits the freeway at State Street frequently and can see the
apartments going in on Main Street, which was designed around a billboard. It serves as a reminder
to him of the rights that everyone has. His feeling was that the stacked signs will impede less on
future development since it will likely exceed two stories. It may be less of an impediment being
closer to the sidewalk instead of being pulled back 48 feet off the street. He asked that Mr. Larson
consider stacking the billboards rather than placing them side-by-side. Mr. Larson stated that the
last developer requested to build underneath. They decided to request the same height and square
footage with a different configuration.

Commissioner Southey found it to be less of an eyesore because when driving or walking by, less
of the mountain is blocked. As proposed, it will only block out other buildings and billboards.

Commissioner Slifka asked if the billboard could be taken down if there is a problem in the future.
Mr. Larson stated that it would be very costly. He noted that they have an easement there, which
controls the property because the sign cannot be blocked. For that reason, having a panel
underneath it is better. He stated that a three-story building could not be built there because it
would block the billboard.

Chair Carter opened the public hearing. There was no public comment. The public hearing was
closed.

Commissioner Southey agreed with Chair Carter that the reality of any future development is that
it is more likely to go up than out. Chair Carter stated, however, that that would require the owners
of the easement to authorize that, which he did not see happening. Ms. Ungricht stated that there
are currently no pending applications. There was a previous request for a proposed townhome
development, which has since expired. Mr. Ungricht clarified that there are no similar design
standards in the Code that specify that they have to be stacked versus vertical. The Commission
can impose conditions on Conditional Use Permits to mitigate adverse impacts. Unless the adverse
impact can be articulated, she suggested it not be imposed.
