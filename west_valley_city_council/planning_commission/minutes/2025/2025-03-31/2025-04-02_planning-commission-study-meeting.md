PRESENTATIONS:

1) ZT-1-2025 Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting an amendment to Chapter 7- 10 of
   the zoning ordinance which addresses standards for landscaping along high image arterial
   streets. This change would provide landscape alternatives in addition to lawn and artificial
   turf. Staff – Jody Knapp at 801-963-3497 or jody.knapp@wvc-ut.gov.

Jody presented an application from West Valley City to amend Chapter 7-10 of the city ordinance, which
pertains to high image arterial streets. The proposed amendment aims to provide an additional
alternative to the current lawn and turf requirements. Jody discussed the history of the ordinance, its
current requirements, and the proposed changes. The changes include clarifying the gross requirement,
adding an alternative to lawn or turf for plant material, and increasing the percentage of live plant
material. Jody also highlighted the importance of landscaping and the negative impacts of removing all
plant material. The proposed changes were well received, with no concerns or questions.

   3.02 acres and is zoned CC (City Center) and R-2- 8 (Two Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum

Brock presented a conditional use application, which is a renewal of a previously approved application
from April 2024 and remains unchanged. The site, located just west of the County Library and down the
road from City Hall, is primarily zoned as City Center with a small portion zoned R-2-8. Although the
applicant intends to build, they are uncertain about the timeline for submitting permits or starting
construction due to market changes and financing issues.

The project involves constructing a 3- to 7-story building along 3650 South, with a total of 42 studio
units, 201 one-bedroom units, and 87 two-bedroom units. The building will have two levels of parking
and three surface stalls, totaling 460 parking spaces. The project meets the city's zoning requirements,
including a 10% reduction in parking due to its proximity to transit. The development will also include
various amenities, such as a pool deck, fitness room, and community room. The project's landscape plan
includes 15% landscaping, with streetscape improvements along 3650 South and perimeter landscaping
around the building. The project's materials will include brick, fiber cement siding, and metal siding.
The development will also include a cul-de-sac and a road termination at 3000 West.

Planning Commission raised concerns about over-saturating the area with similar projects, but Brandon
Hill clarified that the zoning decision has already been made, and the focus is on the conditional use
framework. Brock emphasized the importance of high-density residential development to support transit,
aligning with the City Center zone's goals.
