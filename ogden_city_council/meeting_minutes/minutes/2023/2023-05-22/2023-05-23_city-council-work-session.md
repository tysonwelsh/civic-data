Traffic Citations Police Discussion: Chief Young used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss a recent media
article claiming a traffic citation quota for officers in the Ogden Police Department (OPD). He noted that the journalist who is
responsible for the recent article was going for ‘shock factor’ and did not report actual facts that were provided by the City. He
stated that the article claims that an OPD officer’s performance evaluation is based solely on whether that Officer is meeting a
quota; that is not the case, but State law does not prohibit a city from including a peace officer’s engagement with the community
or enforcement activity as part of an overall determination of the peace officer’s performance. In the City of Ogden, traffic
weighted criteria is one of just nine evaluation criteria and an officer who is underperforming in the traffic area can easily make up
—* the performance score in other areas. He strongly asserted that he has never discussed traffic quotas with the Chief Administrative
Officer or Mayor; prior to being appointed to the position of Chief, he was never informed of a citation quota as a means of
increasing revenue for the City. He then presented data regarding the number of roadway deaths in Utah over the past 20 years,
accidents per 100,000 residents, and traffic enforcement strategies. The number of citations issued in 2022 in Ogden City
nt
increased by 25 percent when compared to 2021 data, but the number of serious accidents in the community decreased by 11
percent and the number of accident-related fatalities decreased by 42 percent. Several peer reviewed case studies show that | }

citations have been demonstrated effective in reducing motor vehicle accidents. Additionally, traffic stops lead to warrant arrests,
drug seizures, illegal weapon seizures, and disruption of crimes in progress. Key takeaways from his analysis of this issue include
the following:

e No officer has missed a pay raise, been negatively impacted for promotion, or been terminated based on their
performance in traffic criteria.

e There are no traffic criteria for Gang’s, CP, Investigations, ATAC. Only for patrol, CRU and traffic bureaus.

e National Highway Safety Institute has shown through peer reviewed research time and time again that traffic
enforcement with citations is the MOST effective way to reduce accidents and injuries from accidents.

e For officers who have faced discipline for significant policy violations, underperformance is the first clear
indicator of a struggling employee. It is the warning sign for supervisors and administrators to ask questions
about how they are doing and what is interfering with their work performance before they travel down the road
to policy violations that may cost them a career and a black eye or lawsuit for the City and Police Department.

e The Ogden Police Department is firmly committed to holding our employees accountable at the lowest possible

levels of discipline to try and ensure officers do not reach a higher level of discipline. The terrible things seen
from officers across the country at times are typically due to departments that have lost sight of their
responsibility to hold employees accountable and create a culture of accountability.

e Itshould cause any reasonable person to pause if the complaints about being required to work are coming from
officers and not the public. This case and the anonymous letter the City Council received within the last couple
years are from officers who do not want to write tickets. The National Highway Safety Institute has shown

; through peer reviewed research time and time again that traffic enforcement with citations is the MOST
effective way to reduce accidents and injuries from accidents.

e Since the story aired on Fox, Chief Young has responded to one complaint from a citizen.

e The Utah State Code addressed citation quotas; Ogden City’s legal department has reviewed this code and our
evaluation system and finds no concerns with our compliance with State law. The highlighted language was
included by the Legislature at the urging of several municipalities and law enforcement agencies to ensure that
evaluations such as Ogden City’s would be acceptable. | }

e Chief Young’s interview with the reporter from Fox lasted more than 45 minutes and less than a minute of it
was included in the story. Chief Young explained to him that a high majority of officers do not like to write
traffic tickets. It is an uncomfortable interaction with an upset citizen for officers who are dealing with
significant workplace stress and would rather avoid. The reporter told Chief Young that he talked to several
people in Ogden who wanted more traffic enforcement not less, but he chose not to include those interviews.
He also chose not to include that if the Attorney General found the City’s practice was in violation of the code,
the City would change it.

¢ There is no effective way to address complaints such as loud vehicles, reckless driving, neighborhood speeding
and the variety of others that the Council receives, and we receive on a regular basis, other than to do proactive
traffic enforcement. What is not checked is not important and if not checked traffic enforcement will reduce
significantly in Ogden.

e Chief Young spoke with a leader from a prevalent Utah agency before doing this interview. They handle their
evaluations by setting goals for their areas and whether the officer is participating in reaching the goals. Part of
the goals are the number of citations issued. (9) other police agencies responded to Fox that they had a system
of this nature in some form.

e The Ogden City Police Department is one of very few in Utah that is fully staffed with a waiting list of new
hires. High performing officers like working at an agency that they know acknowledges them for working hard
for 40 hours a week.

e Anyone who drives regularly in Ogden knows that there is no shortage of dangerous driving habits on our
streets. Chief Young has personally stopped three drivers in the last two weeks for very dangerous driving in
Ogden. Ogden City has recently been included in a study by UDOT based on its high level of fatal accidents
and injury accidents on state roads.

e  Ogden’s evaluation system is about safety, crime reduction and officer accountability.

Police Department Budget: Chief Young used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed FY24
budget for his Department. His presentation included the organizational chart for the Department; information about the values of
the Ogden Police Department (OPD); use of the Axies Balanced Organization model; significant changes in the Department in
FY23; reductions in dispatches; use of the SPIDR project management tool; audits completed in FY22 and underway in FY23;
employee recruiting and hiring efforts; positive media highlights on the OPD; crime reduction statistics; improvements in the
patrol bureau; average caseloads for detectives in the OPD; Geotab data; vehicle safety compliance; training goals for the :
department; and new position requests. He concluded by discussing the manner in which a five or 10 percent budget reduction
would impact his department; five percent equates to $1.3 million, or 11 full-time employee (FTE) positions and 10 percent
equates to $2.6 million, or 21 FTEs. Programs that would have to be considered for elimination include:
