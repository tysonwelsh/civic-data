PC 23-048 LDC Amendment, Chapters 17.7, 17.10, 17.34, 17.35, 17.62 (Moderate Income

Housing/Affordable Housing Provisions) -Continued from the July 27, 2023 meeting.[Code
Amendment] Mike DeSimone/Logan City is requesting amendments to the Land Development Code
to allow Moderate Income Housing density bonuses in the MR-9, MR-12, MR-20, MR-30, TC-2,


and COM zones, add efficiency living units in table 17.08.040 and table 17.11.030, create chapter
17.35 for efficiency living housing, and amend definitions to modify occupancy thresholds for
residential dwelling units.

STAFF/PROPONENT: Mike DeSimone reviewed the proposed amendment to the Land
Development Code to add Moderate Income Housing (MIH) Provisions, including density bonuses
for MIH, efficiency living units with enhanced densities and reduced parking, and a change in the
residential occupancy standard. The purpose of the proposed changes are to implement regulatory
changes to encourage more MIH.

M. DeSimone reviewed what constitutes Moderate Income Housing, affordable housing, the City’s
2020 Median Household Income, and the countywide Adjusted Median Income (AMI). M DeSimone
the differences between household income and income calculated under the AMI.

M. DeSimone reviewed the 2022 MIH Plan, the 8 goals identified in the plan, the 19 State strategies,
and the City’s approach to complying with the State’s MIH requirements. The proposed LDC
amendments fall under goal 5 in the MIH Plan and meet 4 different State MIH strategies. The timeline
established in the MIH Plan is to complete these amendments in 2023.

M. DeSimone reviewed Logan’s average growth rate, the predicted number of new residents and
demand for housing by 2040, Logan’s Median Household Income, and the percentage of Logan
households that are at or below 80% of the Area Median Income (AMI). The conclusion is that
Logan’s population is relatively young and a large percentage of renters and homeowners are cost
burdened for housing related expenses.

Commissioner Lucero asked what data source the AMI numbers are from. M. DeSimone said that
the AMI is from HUD’s 2020 data. The median listing prices and monthly rent prices are from 2023.

The proposed LDC amendment addresses goal five of the 2022 MIH Plan adopted November 14,
2022. The purpose is to evaluate the City’s Land Development Code to determine if there are short-
term regulatory changes that the City could make to encourage more Moderate Income Housing.
Goal 5 includes four specific objectives that reflect a number of State strategies fram which we are
required to implement. This specific LDC amendment targets objectives 2 — 4 of goal 5.

The first proposed change includes a proposed MIH density bonus in the MR-9, MR-12, MR-20, MR-
30, TC-1, TC-2, and COM zones, is patterned after the existing MIH density bonus in the MU zone,
and satisfies objective 2.

Commissioner Doutre asked where the numbers came from for the 25% density bonus if at least 5%
percent of the total units are affordable. M. DeSimone said that if 5% of units within a project are
deed-restricted/income-restricted the cost of those units can be absorbed into the overall project with
the rest of the units making the project economically feasible.

Commissioner Peterson asked how deed restrictions are enforced. M. DeSimone said that a deed
restriction will be required upfront during the permitting process. The Commission discussed renters
continuing to stay in an income-restricted unit after their income exceeds the limits. Mohammed
Abdullahi, City Attorney said that would need to be addressed in the lease agreement and enforced
by the landlord.

The second proposed change includes a proposal for Efficiency Living Unit Housing in the MR-20,.
MR-30, TC-1, TC-2, COM, and MU zones and satisfies objective 4. Efficiency Living Units are
smaller living units ranging in size from 150 square feet to 400 square feet that will provide an

affordable residential space for no more than two lower-income occupants and will be located near
a high-traffic volume corridor (Main Street) or large employers with access to transit. The proposed
code language includes a requirement to restrict occupancy based on income levels and includes a
density bonus of 2 ELUs/1 DU and has a reduced parking requirement of .75 stalls per unit.

Commissioner Doutre asked if the requirement to be located within a % mile of a CVTD transit stop
or an identified bike/ped route is necessary. Commissioner Lucero asked if there has been any
mapping done to show what properties would meet these requirements. M. DeSimone said that this
type of housing or project that provides housing for low-income residents should be located near a
transit or bike/ped route as some of the residents may not be able to afford a car.

The third proposed change includes a proposal to increase the maximum residential occupancy for
a residential dwelling unit from three to four individuals and allow for a “family” to have one additional
person in the household. This satisfies objective 3 of the MIH Plan.

PUBLIC: Matthew Fatuesi talked about the proposed code being geared toward investors by not
requiring 25% affordable units within a development and said that the proposed code is not from the
perspective of low-income housing.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Lucero asked what the time frame is regarding the State Legislature
on the eight goals listed in the Moderate Income Housing Plan.

M. DeSimone said the city reports annually to the State Legislature in August. The first report in 2023
was for a shorter reporting period and included the changed fee structure for moderate-income
housing. By next year, more items need to be completed and removed from the list. Next year we
will start updating the General Plan, Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), and Zoning, which will be a two-
year plus process and that will also include updating the Moderate Income Housing (MIH) Plan. We
are currently working with Neighborhood Non-Profit on potentially building a small moderate-income
housing project which has a longer timeframe for completion.

Commissioner Doutre asked if 17.35.030 needs to require the efficiency living units to be located
near both Main Street and CVTD Transit stops or along an identified bike/pedestrian route. If the
units are within % mile of Main Street it is guaranteed that it will be within % mile of a bus stop or
identified bike/pedestrian route.

Commissioner Doutre asked if 17.35.040. is too restrictive requiring 25% for each AMI percentage
and asked if there is a better way to split up the AMI percentages. The commission discussed
different ways to split up the required AMI percentages within the efficiency living units and
determined that the code should be amended to say no more than 25% of efficiency living units shall
be at market value and at least 25% shall be available for 30% AMI.

The Commission discussed updating the definition of “Family” in the Land Development Code and
needing more time to research a more current definition to be used.
