GPZ-5-2022
Scenic Development, Inc.
3672 and 3690 S 5600 W
General plan change from Low Density Residential and Large Lot Residential to General
Commercial and zone change from R-1-8, A, and A-2 to C-2
5.79 acres


Braiden Rindlisbacher with Scenic Development, Inc. has submitted a General Plan/zone change
application for three parcels totaling 5.79 acres located at 3672 and 3690 S 5600 W. Each of the
three properties is zoned differently – R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size
8,000 square feet), A (Agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre), and A-2 (Agriculture, minimum lot
size 2 acres). The properties zoned A and A-2 have a General Plan designation of Large Lot
Residential (2 to 3 units/acre) and the property zoned R-1-8 has a General Plan designation of
Low Density Residential (3 to 4 units/acre). The proposed zone is C-2 (General Commercial)
and the proposed General Plan designation is General Commercial.

Surrounding zones include R-1-8 to the north, west, east, and a portion of the south and A for the
remaining south portion. The property is surrounded by homes except for some undeveloped
land to the south. Two of the three parcels include homes and the third parcel is vacant.

If this application is approved, the applicant plans to build two, two-story medical office
buildings on the subject property. The existing structures on the property would be demolished.
Included with this report is a concept plan for the property along with a letter from the applicant
that supports this application.

As pointed out by the applicant, the University of Utah is planning to construct a hospital on the
property directly to the south. To this end, the University of Utah has acquired 22.3 acres of land
to the south. According to the University, construction on the hospital is anticipated to start in
late 2023 or early 2024. Completion is anticipated in late 2026.

Traffic
During the Planning Commission’s study meeting, a question was raised about the traffic impact
of the proposed development. According to the Institute of Transportation Engineers (ITE), a
medical office building generates 34.8 weekday trips per 1,000 square feet of building. At 99,000
square feet, the proposed medical office buildings would generate an estimated 3,445 weekday
trips.

According to UDOT, AADT (average annual daily trips) on this section of 5600 West was
32,000 in 2019 and 28,000 in 2020. Mountain View Corridor opened in June of 2021 between
4100 South and SR-201. As a result, the Wasatch Front Regional Council projects AADT on this
section of 5600 West to be 18,000 in 2024 as many commuters using 5600 W have moved to the
Mountain View Corridor. To put these numbers in perspective, a 5-lane arterial street can handle
26,500 trips at a level of service (LOS) C and 30,500 trips at a LOS D.
Development Agreement
If the Commission believes the changes proposed in this application are appropriate at this
location, staff recommends a development agreement for several reasons. These reasons are
outlined below:

      While the applicant’s intended use is medical office, the C-2 zone allows for many other
       uses, some of which would be more impactful on neighboring homes. For this reason,
       staff recommends that the development agreement limit the uses allowed on the property
       to those uses allowed in the C-1 zone which is more restrictive than the C-2 zone. A copy
       of the commercial and manufacturing zone use table with this report for reference.
      No parkstrip exists along the frontage of the property. When UDOT widened the
       intersection at 3500 S and 5600 W as part of the Mountain View Corridor project,
       parkstrips were added along 5600 W north of the subject property. Parkstrips create a
       safer and more comfortable environment for pedestrians, improve the aesthetics of streets
       by softening the edges of streets with landscaping, create a place for snow storage in the
       winter, and make space available for things like streetlights and utility boxes so that they
       don’t encroach into the sidewalk. Staff recommends that the development agreement
       require a parkstrip to be included along the frontage of this property by shifting the
       sidewalk 5’ to the west. Staff is also working with the University of Utah to include a
       parkstrip along their frontage.
      Overhead power exists along 5600 W. The City has worked to bury power lines wherever
       possible to reduce the risk of power disruptions and to reduce clutter along streets. Where
       this project involves redevelopment and a zone change that allows for more development
       intensity, staff recommends that the development agreement require the burial of
       overhead power along 5600 W. Here again, staff is also working with the University of
       Utah to bury the powerlines along their frontage to the south.
      The City’s ordinance requires a 6’ masonry wall to be installed when new commercial
       development occurs adjacent to residential uses. To ensure consistency between this site
       and the proposed hospital to the south, staff recommends that the development agreement
       state that the masonry wall type and color be established by the City in cooperation with
       the University of Utah.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This application should be approved subject to a development agreement that
      incorporates the standards recommended in this report.

   2. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the
      public hearing.

   3. Denial.

Z-9-2022
Shane Keyvani
3940 South 5200 West
A to RE
10.6 acres

This application was continued by the Planning Commission at the applicant’s request during the
public hearing on June 22nd. The applicant requested continuance to allow more time to “get a
more objective and detailed development agreement in place”. At the time this staff report was
prepared, the applicant had hired someone to help him draft a revised development agreement,
but a revision had yet to be submitted. The report that follows is the same report from June 22nd
as no additional information is available.

Shane Keyvani has requested a zone change on four parcels totaling 10.6 acres at 3940 South
5200 West from A (Agriculture, minimum lot size of ½ acre) to RE (Residential Estate).
Surrounding zones include R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 sq.
ft.) to the north, west, and south and R-1-10 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size
10,000 sq. ft.), A, and R-1-8 to the east. The property is surrounded by homes to the north, east,
and west and a church and homes to the south. The subject property is designated as large lot
residential, which anticipates 2 to 3 units/acre, in the West Valley City General Plan.

Development Proposal

The applicant has submitted a concept plan, which is attached, that shows the subject property
being subdivided into 25 lots yielding a density of 2.4 units/acre. Also included is a letter from
the applicant supporting this application. The concept plan includes 10 lots that do not meet the
minimum lot width for the RE zone of 100’. After reviewing the applicant’s concept plan, staff’s
opinion is that the plan can be modified so that no width exceptions would be necessary.

Development Agreement

A development agreement is required for this project. Section 7-11-213 of the West Valley City
Zoning Ordinance states: “All new subdivisions involving a rezone of property, or a PUD, shall
participate in a Development Agreement that addresses housing size, quality, exterior finish
materials, Streetscapes, Landscaping, etc.” The applicant’s proposed development agreement is
attached.

Staff has two concerns with the proposed development agreement. First, a few of the proposed
standards are, in staff’s view, too subjective to be enforced. For example, for the architectural
standards, the proposal simply states: “The architecture of the homes will be comparable to the
following illustrations”. The proposal then includes three renderings of home plans used in the
Newton Farm Subdivision. To remedy this issue, staff recommends one of two approaches –
either include specific renderings/elevations that will be used in this development or include
specific standards that are objective and measurable. An example of an objective and measurable
standard is all homes shall have a minimum roof pitch of 6:12.

Second, when compared with other recently approved development agreements, the standards in
the proposed development agreement are lower. Below are specific examples:
     House Size: The proposed size for multi-level homes is 500 square feet smaller and there
       is no commitment for basements.
     Exterior Materials: There is no minimum amount of brick or stone and thin brick, brick
       veneer panels, and stucco are included in the list of allowed materials.
     Architecture: As pointed out earlier, there are no specific architectural standards listed.
     Entry Feature: The applicant is requesting an exception to the entry feature requirement
       in the RE zone.

Given the relatively little land remaining in the City, the City Council has emphasized larger,
high quality homes on larger lots to provide a more balanced mix of housing within the City.

Staff Alternatives:

      Continuance. This application should be continued to allow the applicant time to modify
       the proposed development agreement.
      Denial.

Z-10-2022
John Baird
3909 South 2200 West
Zone change from A to R-1-8
0.52 acres

John Baird has requested a zone change on a 0.52-acre parcel at 3909 South 2200 West from A
(Agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre) to R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot
size 8,000 square feet). Surrounding zones include R-1-8 to the west, R-1-10 (Single Unit
Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 10,000 square feet) to the north and east and A to the
south. The property is surrounded by single unit dwellings. The subject property is Low Density
Residential (3 to 4 units/acre) in the General Plan.

Development Proposal

The subject property includes a home that was built in 1924. According to the applicant, he
would like to subdivide the property into two lots, keep the existing home on the west lot, and
build a new home on the east flag lot. While the existing lot is large enough in area to be
subdivided, there is not sufficient lot width to allow for two lots without a development
agreement exception. The applicant is requesting that the lot width be 79’ for the west lot. For
reference, the minimum lot width in the R-1-8 zone is 80’.


Development Agreement

A development agreement is required for this project. Section 7-11-213 of the West Valley City
Zoning Ordinance states: “All new Subdivisions involving a rezone of property, or a PUD, shall
participate in a Development Agreement that addresses housing size, quality, exterior finish
materials, Streetscapes, Landscaping, etc.” For the development agreement, the applicant is
proposing to use the same standards that were in place up until July of last year. These standards
are attached for reference.

After a site visit, staff identified some items that we would recommend addressing in the
development agreement. These items are described below:
    Curb, gutter, and sidewalk improvements extend up to the northern boundary of the
        subject property but not across the frontage of the subject property.
    The driveway for the existing home is in poor condition and in need of repairs.
    The exterior of the existing home has some peeling paint and is in need of maintenance.
    The landscaping on the property is in poor condition (large dead tree, weedy/dead lawn)
        and in need of maintenance and/or replacement.
    The existing detached garage serving the existing home would fall on the new parcel and
        would likely be demolished. While the ordinance does not require a garage for a home of
        this age, the Commission may want to consider requiring a new detached garage to serve
        the existing home since it currently has one.

Staff Alternatives:

      Approval. The application should be approved. The recommendation for approval is
       made subject to a development agreement including the following standards:
          o The lot width of the west lot may be reduced to 79’.
          o The home built on the new lot shall follow the standards proposed by the
             applicant.
          o Curb, gutter, and sidewalk improvements shall be installed across the entire
             frontage of the property.
          o The cracks in the existing driveway shall be repaired.
          o The wood portions of the exterior of the existing home shall be repainted.
          o All dead and weedy landscaping shall be replaced with landscaping that complies
             with City standards.
          o A new, two-car garage shall be built on the same lot as the existing home.

      Continuance.

      Denial.
