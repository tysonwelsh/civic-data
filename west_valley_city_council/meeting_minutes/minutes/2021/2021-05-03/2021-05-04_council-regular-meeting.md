COMMENT PERIOD
A.  PUBLIC COMMENTS
    Jim Vesock stated that the Council will vote on the tentative budget and questioned
    how much the budget will increase throughout the coming year due to vehicle
    purchases, employee hires, etc. Jim stated that there is a lot of misinformation about
    the new parking ordinance related to 5 feet separation from driveways. He stated
    that this is a public safety concern and people need to be able to safely back out of
    their homes.

       Natalie Holt stated that she understands the safety perspective of the new parking
       ordinance that requires 5-foot separation from driveways. Ms. Holt stated that this
       does not work in all neighborhood situations and a blanket approval is not effective
       for the City as a whole. Ms. Holt expressed concern about family gatherings,
       delivery personnel, service calls to homes, vehicle safety from theft, etc. She also
       stated that some parents park their cars in front of their own driveways to allow
       children a safe place to play. Ms. Holt indicated that parking in her neighborhood
       is very limited already due to it being a cul-de-sac and the presence of fire hydrants
       and mailboxes. She stated that overcrowded streets are not a problem in every
       neighborhood and expressed concern about multi-generational families needing on-
       street parking for their vehicles. Ms. Holt encouraged the Council to revisit the
       ordinance to find a better solution for all residents in the City.

       Renee Layton indicated that she lives in Diamond Summit and has actively worked
       with West Valley Police Department in finding street calming solutions, additional
       lighting, and other options to ensure neighborhood safety. She indicated that she
       has worked with some amazing officers who go above and beyond and is impressed
       with Chief Jacobs’ leadership. Ms. Layton stated that West Valley City is one of
       the busiest agencies and noted that she has participated in ride-a-longs and has
       witnessed firsthand the challenges the officers face. She expressed concern that
       West Valley City officers are on the low end of the pay scale and the department is
       understaffed which results in longer response times. She added that more and more
       officers are moving to neighboring Cities that are less busy and pay higher. Ms.
       Layton indicated that losing these qualified, hardworking officers is a detriment to
       the community. She encouraged the Council to increase pay and incentives so that
       the City may retain veteran, qualified officers for the benefit of the residents.

C.     CITY COUNCIL COMMENTS
       Mayor Bigelow stated it is the responsibility of Government to build, maintain, and
       provide safe roads and streets. He indicated that all levels of Government institute
       laws and ordinances related to parking and traffic that are meant to keep residents
       and citizens safe. Mayor Bigelow indicated that ordinances are meant to benefit the
       public and are rarely changed without a concern being expressed by many. He
       indicated that changes to the ordinance apply to all people equally. Mayor Bigelow
       stated that there are a few times a year where families in his neighborhood hold
       gatherings and cars line his street but there are other places in the City where this
       is a very real problem every day. He stated that just because a neighborhood hasn’t
       experienced this safety concern yet, doesn’t mean that it won’t become an issue in
       the future. Mayor Bigelow stated that he feels requiring a 5-foot parking separation
       from a personal driveway is a reasonable and necessary ordinance but noted that he
       is willing to discuss other options if the Council decides to pursue any potential
       changes. Mayor Bigelow also suggested sharing the new parking ordinance
       regulation via a utility bill.

       Councilmember Fitisemanu thanked Ms. Layton for her comments related to Police
       Officer pay. He indicated that he would like to review public safety pay scales at a
       future date.

RESOLUTION 21-63: ADOPT A TENTATIVE BUDGET FOR THE FISCAL
YEAR COMMENCING JULY 1, 2021 AND ENDING JUNE 30, 2022 AND SET
AUGUST 10, 2021 AS THE DATE FOR A PUBLIC BUDGET HEARING
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 21-63 that would adopt a Tentative Budget
for the Fiscal Year commencing July 1, 2021 and ending June 30, 2022 and set August 10,
2021 as the date for a Public Budget Hearing.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       This resolution adopts the tentative budget for West Valley City for the 2021-2022
       fiscal year and sets a date for public comment at a hearing to be held on August 10,
       2021.

       West Valley City will adopt a tentative budget that will be made available for public
       inspection during regular office hours in the City’s Recorder’s office and gave
       notice of a hearing to receive public comment, before the final adoption of this
       tentative budget for FY 2021-2022.

RESOLUTION 21-64: AUTHORIZE THE EXECUTION OF AN AGREEMENT TO
REIMBURSE PHIL CONDIE FOR CERTAIN STORMWATER FACILITIES
EXPENSES
Mayor Bigelow discussed proposed Resolution 21-64 that would authorize the execution of
an Agreement to reimburse Phil Condie for certain stormwater facilities expenses.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Phil Condie is building the “Phil’s Farm” subdivision at approximately 4195 South
       4625 West. The drainage from the existing neighborhoods to the south currently
       discharge onto the property that is being developed.

       The developer is required to build a drainage system out to 4100 South to handle
       runoff from the developing property, which could be handled with a 15-inch pipe.
       The city requested that the developer upsize the system to an 18 and 24-inch pipe
       system to convey additional water from the neighborhoods to the south.

       This reimbursement agreement pays the developer for the difference in cost
       between the 15-inch pipe and the 18 and 24-inch pipe.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and he called for a motion.

RESOLUTION 21-65: APPROVE A LEASE AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITY
AND THE UTAH DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION TO FACILITATE
CONSTRUCTION ON 2700 WEST
Mayor Bigelow proposed Resolution 21-65 that would approve a Lease Agreement between
the City and the Utah Department of Transportation to facilitate construction on 2700 West.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       This portion of the Utah Department of Transportation parcel located at 2780 West
       4700 South includes 1,098 feet of street frontage on 2700 West which will be
       affected by the 2700 West Improvement Project Phase 2, scheduled to be
       constructed in 2021. This project will include construction of sidewalk, textured
       colored concrete parkstrip, street trees, and decorative street lighting along the west
       side of Constitution Boulevard (2700 West) from 4100 South to 4700 South. The
       project will also include an eight-foot tall decorative concrete post and panel wall
       along those properties with backyards facing Constitution Boulevard. UDOT, as
       Landlord, has agreed to enter into a Lease Agreement with West Valley City, as
       Tenant, to allow for construction of improvements. UDOT has agreed to execute
       the Lease Agreement in exchange for the improvements to be constructed along
       2700 West by the City. While we had originally a Temporary Construction
       Easement from UDOT, it was their preference to do a Lease Agreement for no
       charge.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and he called for a motion.
