20.    APPROVE R STEVENS SUBDIVISION, PLAT ‘E,’ SUBJECT TO THE CONDITIONS IN THE
       STAFF REPORT
(Track A, 11:35)
County resident Robert Stevens talked about monument markers. One-lot subdivisions currently require two
monument markers, and Mr. Stevens would like the Board to waive that requirement for his plat. The costs would
be high due to an irrigation ditch and shared asphalt roadway. Commissioner Lee commented on the importance of
the monument markers to prevent overlapping lines, especially on private property. Brandon Larsen of the Utah
County Community Development Office also added that the county ordinance states plural “monuments” are
required, but not specifically the number two – if Mr. Stevens had submitted all eighteen of his lots at once, two
monuments would still meet the requirements. As is, two monuments will be needed on each individual lot. Utah
County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore and Commissioner Ivie discussed the ordinance wording and potential
text amendments to address the issue. Mr. Stevens pointed out because surveyors use global positioning systems
(GPS), property owners are less likely to hire surveyors when there are multiple monuments. He summarized the
increase in monument markers decreases accuracy.

1.      APPROVE REDUCING THE PENALTY IN THE AMOUNTS LISTED FOR THE ATTACHED
        PERSONAL PROPERTY ACCOUNTS, HAVING BEEN IDENTIFIED AS MEETING THE
        QUALIFICATIONS UNDER ORDINANCE NO. 2010-4 AND RESOLUTION NO. 2011-64 OF THE
        UTAH COUNTY CODE
(Track A, 24:20)
Commissioner Lee pointed out a company on the list owes taxes of exactly one cent. The original fee for missing
payment of the one penny was twenty-five dollars, and this is a request to abate the amount down to five dollars.
The final bill sent would be for a total of $5.01. Commissioner Lee asked why the penalty could not be completely
removed, and Utah County Deputy Attorney Paul Jones came forward with Jeanne Bowen of the Utah County
Treasurer’s Office to respond. Mr. Jones explained county ordinance sets the penalty amounts. Commissioner Lee
stated he is bothered by the additional charge of five dollars to warrant sending a bill for one penny, and then
demanding the recompense of that same five dollars. Mr. Jones suggested a group discussion to find satisfactory
and reasonable measures for this issue. Ms. Bowen explained these bills are being sent to individuals who have not
filed taxes; if the penalties are waived entirely, the Board would need to consider alternate ways to motivate filing.

6.      APPROVE AND AUTHORIZE THE COMMISSION CHAIR TO SIGN A MEMORANDUM OF
        AGREEMENT WITH THE DEPARTMENT OF HERITAGE AND ARTS, UTAH STATE LIBRARY
        DIVISION FOR BOOKMOBILE SERVICE IN UTAH COUNTY (Continued from the July 18, 2017
        Commission meeting)
(Track A, 33:50)
Commissioner Lee mentioned Commissioner Graves had provided a breakdown of hourly rates for services based
on information provided during previous meetings regarding this issue. Commissioner Lee reviewed the dollar
figures assigned per Bookmobile stop, coming to a final total of $71,616. Britton Lund, Bookmobile Program
Manager for the Utah State Library, talked about ‘on-the-road’ costs being only a portion of the total expense. She
also explained Summer hours are the least operational, and summarized the updated figure of $92,178 is based on
reduced hours and fewer county stops. Commissioner Ivie asked about the option to suspend and continue services
at will, and Ms. Lund noted it is a possibility but advised against constant unpredictability. The commissioners and
Utah County Clerk/Auditor Bryan E. Thompson reviewed the unincorporated county locations. Commissioner Lee
stated he is comfortable with the proposed $92,178 rate with reduced services, and Commissioner Ivie agreed,
pointing out cities can choose to ‘buy in’ later.
