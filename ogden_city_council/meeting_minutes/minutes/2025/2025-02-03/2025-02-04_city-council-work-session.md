2019-2024 Strategic Plan Report – Community Safety
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported that the purpose of this agenda item is for the Council to review the
final report prepared to highlight the City’s progress and success in accomplishing the strategic initiatives for the Community
Safety Strategic Directive. The focus tonight will be public safety and future work sessions will be held to review the same
information for Community and Economic Development, Parks and Recreation, City Image and Reputation, and Fiscal
Sustainability Strategic directives. She provided background regarding the development of the Five-Year Strategic Plan for the
City. The City contracted with Tanner, LLC and created a Strategic Planning Advisory Committee. The consultant and Committee
conducted community surveys, held community dialogue sessions, and met with community organizations and stakeholders to
develop key directives. The strategic directives are considered each year by each Department during the annual budget process.
The Community Safety Directives include the following:
               • Improved Part 1 Crime Rate relative to other comparable cities in Utah and surrounding states
               • Feet of sidewalk maintained or improved, and sidewalk added near schools
               • Improvement in Pavement Condition Index
               • Improved funding system to maintain sidewalks and roads
               • Increased number of streetlights in operation
               • Decrease in aggressive panhandling and disruptive behavior in public places
               • Improved results of Ogden Policing Survey, including awareness of services
          Police Department Accomplishments: Police Chief Sube used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
efforts of the Police Department responsive to the directives listed by Ms. Eller-Smith. He compared Ogden City’s Part One crime
rates with other cities in the surrounding area as well as the State of Utah and highlighted reduction in different types of Part One
crime. He also discussed the use of new software that assists the Department in tracking crime rates and community feedback
regarding interactions with Police Department staff. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for
the meeting. There was brief discussion among the Council and Chief Sube regarding efforts to improve interactions between
police officers and the community and the handling of panhandling and disruptive behavior in public places.
          Fire Department Accomplishments; Fire Chief Mathieu used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
efforts of the Fire Department responsive to the directives applicable to his Department. Most notable is that the Fire Department
achieved the first-ever Insurance Services Officer (ISO) “2” rating, placing Ogden among the top 6.5 percent nationwide. He also
reported on recent structure fire events in the community and the ability of the Fire Department to respond to those events much
quicker than in the past due to improvements in communications, equipment, and personnel. For a copy of the presentation in its
entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Public Services Department Accomplishments: Public Services Executive Director Anderson used the aid of a
PowerPoint presentation to expound on the efforts of the Fire Department responsive to the directives applicable to his
Department. He provided information about various projects included in the City’s Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) aimed at
sidewalk improvements, street construction, road improvements, mapping City-owned electrical infrastructure, and the
construction of new sidewalks near schools. He also discussed key water infrastructure and water operation projects in the
community. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Anderson centered on criteria used to evaluate certain public infrastructure to
determine if it can be repaired or must be replaced.

Proposed Amendments to Engineering Standards for Public Improvements
        Public Services Assistant Executive Director/City Engineer Nielsen explained the Engineering Division is requesting that
the Council review and approve proposed updates to the City’s Engineering Standards for Public Improvements. The City
Engineering Standards establish minimum requirements for the design and construction of public improvements such as streets
and water/sewer utilities. He used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide a summary of the proposed amendments:
              • Updated variance request process:
                       o Variances may address Ogden City Engineering Standards and Storm Water Design Manual.
                       o Review period for variance applications: 15 business days
                       o Criteria for approval explicitly defined:
                                ▪ Avoidance of undue hardship
                                ▪ No conflict with safety, functionality, or fire protection requirements
                                ▪ Does not modify development approval conditions
              • When traffic impact studies are required:
                       o Generate 100 or more vehicle trips per day
                       o Include 50 or more dwelling units, or
                       o Add 30,000 square feet of building area
                       o Requirement can be waived if not needed
              • Traffic impact studies
              • Sidewalk standards
              • Sewer standards
                       o New sewer main shall require an air test
                       o Clarified details for sewer cleanouts
                       o Updated easement language for easement width
                                ▪ 1 foot depth = 2 feet of easement width, minimum 20 feet
              • Storm drain standards
                       o Created a storm water design manual
                       o Added language about State requirements for a dewatering permit
                       o Updated storm water construction requirements
              • Low Impact Design (LID) standards
              • Water standards
                       o Added a maximum depth requirement (5.5’)
                       o Clarified pressure/bacteria testing requirements (minimum of 20 feet in length of mainline).
                       o Updated lateral installation alignment
              • Utility boring and exploratory potholing.
        Mr. Nielsen concluded on November 6, 2024, the Planning Commission considered the proposal and recommended
approval of the updates. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

Canyon Waterline Public Involvement Review
         Public Services Assistant Executive Director/City Engineer Nielsen used the aid of PowerPoint presentation to provide a
brief overview of the status of the 36” Canyon Water Line replacement project and to familiarize the Council with the
OgdenCityPipeline.com website, which provides public information regarding the project. He also reviewed the construction
schedule and photographs of the project thus far. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the
meeting.
         Brief discussion among the Council and Public Services staff centered on the project alignment.
Administrative Update
          Chief Administrative Officer Brown deferred to Chiefs Sube and Mathieu to discuss recent public safety events in the
community. Chief Mathieu reported on the recent structure fire in downtown Ogden, which resulted in the total loss of a housing
project that was under construction. He also referenced the recent catastrophic wildfires in California and touched on the efforts of
Ogden City’s Public Services and Fire Departments to ensure preparedness for wildfire events in the Ogden community.
          Discussion among the Council and Chief Mathieu centered on communication between the City and Rocky Mountain
Power regarding control of their infrastructure on the hillsides of the City during a fire event and fire mitigation efforts in wildland
areas.

Council Business
         Council Executive Director Eller-Smith provided information about upcoming Idle-Free Month presentations,
information needed from the Council to continue planning the Hof, Germany visit, and an opening on the City’s Audit Committee.


         The meeting adjourned at 5:55 p.m.
