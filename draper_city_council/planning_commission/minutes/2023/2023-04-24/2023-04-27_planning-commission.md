5.     Public Hearing: Towne Storage Draper Land Use and Zoning Map Amendments
       (Legislative Action).
       On the request of Gary Free, representing Via Digital Solutions, a request to amend the
       land use designation from Neighborhood Commercial to Community Commercial and
       rezone from CN (Neighborhood Commercial) to CG (General Commercial) on
       approximately 2.38 acres for the properties located at approximately 11761 and 11757 S.
       700 East. Application numbers: TEXTMAP-008-2023 and TEXTMAP-001-2023. Staff
       contact is Maryann Pickering at 801-576-6391 or maryann.pickering@draperutah.gov.

Due to a conflict of interest, Commissioner Nixon recused herself from the discussion and vote on
the above item.

City Planner, Maryann Pickering, presented a project vicinity map and identified various points of
interest. The current land use designation is Neighborhood Commercial and the current zoning is
CN. The request is to change the land use designation from Neighborhood Commercial to
Community Commercial, which would go along with the zoning map change from Neighborhood
Commercial Zoning to General Commercial Zoning. If approved by City Council, the applicant
has expressed a desire to construct a self-storage facility on the property. For public comment,
Ms. Pickering received about 15 emails, all of which were in opposition to the project. The emails
were forwarded to the Commissioners.

Ms. Pickering reported that currently, self-storage facilities are not permitted in the CN zone. This
application is for a zoning and land use change that goes to the City Council for a final decision.
A list was provided of any potential uses that could occur if the self-storage was not to be built.
This site is the former location of the Home Sweet Home Kennel property, which was a dog
boarding facility. It has been gone for a few years. The property borders the Sunset Ponds
neighborhood with residential properties on the north and east side of the property.

Ms. Pickering identified the differences between the two zones. If rezoned, the proposed self-
storage facility would require a Conditional Use Permit. In addition, if the area is rezoned vehicle
sales, which are currently not permitted, would be permitted. Tattoo establishments, hotels, and
Draper City Planning Commission Meeting
April 27, 2023

medical and dental would also be permitted. The Commission reviewed the types of uses that
would be permitted if this area was rezoned.

David Peterson, representing Towne Storage, was present on behalf of the applicant, Gary Free.
Mr. Peterson stated that he has responded to all of the emails received by staff. The hope was to
address many of the concerns raised. Mr. Peterson reported that Towne Storage has been in
business since 1994 and they have 32 facilities in three states. Six are currently in development.
Their corporate headquarters are in Draper. Both the CEO and COO live in Draper. This site will
be the first facility they have in Draper. They are unique because they integrate their storage
facilities into the residential community. Mr. Peterson displayed photos of other facilities that
show homes surrounding the facility. They are very cognizant that they build in and around
communities. They make sure to landscape and design with that in mind. They intend to build a
two-story design, a single building that will be fully landscaped. They know what they need to do
to blend in with the community.

Of the 15 emails received, traffic and parking were major concerns. Traffic Studies were
conducted for their Bluffdale and Phoenix facilities. Bluffdale has 362 units and is a traditional
storage facility. Mr. Peterson reported that storage units are low generators of traffic. People drop
their items off and then do not return every day. In the former study, the AM peak traffic was
about seven visits and the PM peak was about 11 visits. In Phoenix, the peak was 11 visits in the
AM and 15 in the PM. What that shows for Bluffdale is that it commiserates with a subdivision
of three to 10 single-family homes. That is the traffic impact that a storage facility has. A retail
facility in Phoenix on the same three-acre parcel would have 10 times the amount of traffic. He
noted that that is universal across all the facilities. Their facilities typically generate the same
amount of traffic as eight single-family homes.

Parking issues were also raised. Mr. Peterson stated that Towne Storage typically over parks its
units. Most people pull up to the door closest to their unit, take care of business, and then move
out. He commented that parking has never been an issue. 24/7 access was also a concern expressed
in the emails. Towne Storage does not have 24/7 access except with very limited exceptions. Most
of their units operate from 6 AM – 9 PM. A few operate from 7 AM – 10 PM. Most of the units
are inside the building and there are no windows on the second story except for one. No one will
be able to see into anyone's backyard. They also keep the facilities very well-lit but contain the
lighting to the footprint of the building. Safety was of concern and security cameras are used.
Each unit is individually alarmed with a unique code. The facility design results in minimum
crime. Safety is of paramount importance and there are no areas where vagrants can hide out.
Standing water was raised as another concern. Towne Storage will eliminate the standing water
and they will landscape. He noted that their St. George facility received a Beautification Award.

Mr. Peterson reported that their projects fall more within NC Zone, however, because they are a
self-service storage unit, they have to ask for the Community Commercial and a Conditional Use
Permit under General Commercial zoning.

Chair Adams commented that these are not the typical structures that people drive into at all hours

Andrew Morgan was present representing the Sunset Ponds HOA and stated that they are opposed
to the proposed zone change. In 2020, the two parcels were rezoned from Residential to
Neighborhood Commercial. The Sunset Pond HOA supported this change because it would
provide commercial use that supports the immediate residential area and aligns with the type of
commercial properties in other CN designations along the mile stretch between 11400 South and
12300 South. The HOA acknowledges and supports maintaining the current CN designation
because they have established the need for these types of businesses to support the immediate area.
According to Draper City, the CN zone is intended to have a small trade area and meet nearby the
day-to-day needs of nearby residents. The CG designation does not fit within the mile stretch zone
and there are no CG designations anywhere within that mile stretch. He noted that there are very
few CG zones in all of Draper. The few that exist do not fall within a predominantly Neighborhood
and Light Commercial Zone. According to Draper City Code, the CG designation is intended to
apply to existing commercial developments and should not be used for new commercial
development unless specific topographical criteria have been met. Therefore, the designation does
not seem appropriate for this location.

Mr. Morgan stated that the residents of the HOA are also concerned about the consistency of
zoning and land use changes in the City. They are proud of their community but consistent and
appropriate land use are of major concern. Towne Storage has requested a zone change to CG as
well as a land use change from Neighborhood Commercial to Community Commercial. He noted
that the next parcel immediately to the north of these two, owned by Federal Heights Holding, is
looking for a decrease in the land use from Community Commercial to Neighborhood
Commercial. Mr. Morgan stated that from a land use perspective, Federal Heights Holdings is
doing the exact opposite of Towne Storage. The HOA residents asked for consistency and
appropriate use in our area. Building size was also of concern. Mr. Morgan questioned how an
80,000-square-foot facility will look next to 2,000-square-foot homes.

Assistant City Attorney, Spencer DuShane, reported that Northern Monticello Alliance (“NMA”)
is a group of landowners that sued and are currently in litigation with San Juan County. The
applicant planned to develop a wind farm. The initial application was shy on facts so the County
Planning Commission asked for more information. They came with more data about what could
be done to mitigate the harmful effects. Tonight’s Conditional Use Permit application included
someone asking about noise issues and other impacts. Conditional Use Permits or any type of
deviation that is granted in the Code should be carefully considered in terms of impacts and how
they can be mitigated. San Juan County Code permitted the use as a conditional use. The wind
farm surrounded several property owners and the applicants claimed to be able to mitigate the
harmful effects. They presented data on sounds and distance from residences. The wind farm
described how they can mitigate the harmful effects of the use by buying out the property owners.
They also claimed to have ways to mitigate the light flicker from the blades and sound. The
Planning Commission agreed to grant approval if they did what they said they would do or if they
mitigate the negative effects of light flicker and sound to the extent possible.

Mr. DuShane was confused since the wind farm presented substantial data yet the County Planning
