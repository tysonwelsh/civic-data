                          REIMBURSEMENT AGREEMENT

       This Reimbursement Agreement (“Agreement”) is entered into by Lloyd’s TLC
Limited, a Utah limited partnership (“Developer”), and the City of Draper, a
municipality and political subdivision of the State of Utah (“City”). Developer and City
are collectively referred to herein as “Parties,” and each may be referred to
individually as “Party.”

                                      RECITALS

       A. Developer intends to develop the real property located at 13782 South 300
East in Draper, Utah (“Property”) into a commercial shopping center (“Development”).
To facilitate safe traffic flow for the Development and surrounding public roads, this
Agreement is contingent upon Developer entering into a separate Shared Access
Agreement with the owner of adjacent property to the South of the Property; and

       B. The Development will generate additional traffic impacts on surrounding
public roads, including 13800 South, Bangerter Parkway, and 150 East, necessitating
certain infrastructure improvements attributable to the Development (“Project
Improvements”), which would ordinarily be the Developer’s responsibility to fund
and construct; and

       C. Independent of the Development, the City has identified preexisting
deficiencies in its transportation system on the same surrounding public roads
(“System Deficiencies”), which are the City’s responsibility to remedy through
infrastructure improvements (“System Improvements”); and

       D. The City is eligible for an appropriation of state funds in the amount of
$3,450,000 (“State Funds”) pursuant to Utah Code § 72-2-121(4)(k)(v), for
improvements to Bangerter Highway between 13800 South and I-15, subject to
revenue availability after distributions under § 72-2-121(4)(j), and anticipates
receiving such funds upon completion of the Improvements contemplated in this
Agreement. In the interim, the City has equivalent funds available to advance the
System Improvements, with the intention of replenishing its general fund upon
receipt of State Funds; and

     E. In the ordinary course of business, the City would publicly bid the System
Improvements and select the lowest responsible bidder. However, the Parties agree

                                           1
that it would be more efficient for the Developer to engage a contractor to construct
both the Project Improvements and the System Improvements concurrently as part
of the Development, with the City reimbursing the Developer solely for the actual
costs of the System Improvements. This coordinated approach allows the Parties to
share the costs of their respective obligations in a manner that benefits the public
and enhances the appearance, function, and long-term durability of the
improvements; and

       F. Typically, developers are solely responsible for funding all Project
Improvements that are necessary for their proposed development, unless such
improvements are necessary to mitigate an existing deficiency. In this Agreement,
as an exception to standard practice and as a benefit to the Developer and the City,
the City may, at its sole discretion, reimburse certain costs of Project Improvements,
but only if excess State Funds remain after full reimbursement of Actual System
Costs, as detailed in Section 2; and

       G. The parties believe that coordinating the Project Improvements and System
Improvements in this manner will benefit the City, its residents, and the public by
minimizing disruptions, accelerating completion, reducing overall costs, improving
quality, and enhancing the appearance, function, and long-term life of the project;
and

       H. The Developer shall dedicate all rights-of-way that it owns and that are
necessary for the construction of the Improvements at no cost to the City. In the
event that condemnation is required to acquire property from third parties,
additional costs above the value of the property may be incurred, and if tied to the
Bangerter Highway improvements, may be included in the Actual System Costs
eligible for reimbursement under Utah Code § 72-2-121(4)(b); and

      I. The Parties acknowledge that the Developer contributed to efforts to secure
State Funds, and that the Project Improvements, in conjunction with the System
Improvements, will enhance the safety and functionality of Bangerter Highway,
providing significant public benefits to the City and its residents.

      THEREFORE, in consideration of the foregoing recitals (which are incorporated
herein by reference) and the mutual promises set forth herein, the Parties agree as
follows:




                                          2
1) Project Scope.

   The project scope encompasses all activities and costs necessary to complete the
   Project Improvements and System Improvements (collectively, the
   “Improvements”) as illustrated in Exhibit A of this Agreement. This includes, but is
   not limited to, design, engineering, surveying, geotechnical analysis, property
   acquisition, land transfers with adjacent landowners, right-of-way dedications or
   acquisitions, construction, construction management, quality control and testing,
   landscaping, traffic control measures, bonding, insurance, permitting fees,
   development fees, inspection fees, and all other direct or incidental costs
   associated with the Improvements. The Improvements shall be constructed in
   accordance with applicable federal, state, and local laws, regulations and
   standards, as well as the specifications and plans approved by the City and
   detailed in all approved Engineering Designs. The Developer shall coordinate and
   cooperate with the City to ensure that any land transfers required for the
   Improvements are properly documented and executed to support the
   Development and public access needs.

2) Prioritization of Funds.

   a) The City shall reimburse the Developer solely for the actual, verified costs of
   the System Improvements (“Actual System Costs”), up to a maximum of
   $3,450,000 (“Maximum Reimbursement”), upon submission and City approval of
   Draw Requests in accordance with Section 6 of this Agreement. The City shall
   prioritize the use of State Funds, once received, or equivalent available funds (with
   replenishment upon receipt of State Funds) for reimbursement of Actual System
   Costs. Reimbursement shall not occur for any Project Improvements unless and
   until all Actual System Costs have been fully reimbursed and State Funds remain
   unexpended, in which case any remaining State Funds may be applied, at the
   City’s sole discretion, to reimburse Project Improvements the City determines
   provides a direct public benefit (defined as shared infrastructure that enhances
   the functionality, safety, or capacity of Bangerter Highway between 13800 South
   and I-15), with the City and Developer mutually agreeing on eligible costs within
   30 days of reimbursement eligibility, in accordance with Utah Code § 72-2-121(4).
   In no event shall reimbursement for any Project Improvements exceed the lesser
   of (i) the remaining State Funds after full reimbursement of Actual System Costs
   or (ii) the verified costs of such public-benefit portions of the Project
   Improvements, as determined by the City in collaboration with the Developer.



                                           3
   b) Notwithstanding the foregoing, if the State Funds are not received by the City
   due to insufficient revenue balances for any reason, the City’s reimbursement
   obligation shall be limited to the Actual System Costs, using available City funds,
   and no reimbursement shall be provided for any Project Improvements, provided
   that the City shall make reasonable efforts to secure alternative funding sources
   within 90 days to cover Actual System Costs, failing which the City and Developer
   shall negotiate an equitable cost-sharing arrangement for System Improvements.
   The Developer acknowledges and agrees that it remains solely responsible for all
   costs of Project Improvements, except as expressly provided in Section (a) for
   potential limited reimbursement of public-benefit portions using any excess State
   Funds. The Parties further acknowledge that this Agreement supersedes any
   prior discussions or representations regarding reimbursement terms. The City’s
   obligations are subject to its legal duty to ensure all funds comply with applicable
   laws and grant conditions, including Utah Code § 72-2-121 and verification by the
   Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”).

3) Developer Obligations.

   a) The Developer shall be responsible for procuring and funding all designs,
   engineering, and related professional services necessary for the completion of
   the System Improvements and Project Improvements (collectively, the
   Improvements) as illustrated in Exhibit A. The Developer shall bear all costs
   associated with the Project Improvements and, subject to reimbursement as
   provided in Section 2, the System Improvements. The City shall provide written
   feedback on Engineering Designs within 30 days of submission, or the designs
   shall be deemed approved unless the City demonstrates good cause for delay. All
   Engineering Designs must be pre-approved by the City in writing to be eligible for
   reimbursement of Actual System Costs.

   b) The Developer shall obtain and maintain, at its sole expense, all necessary
   bonds, including a warranty bond for a period of one year following completion
   of the Improvements, as required by the City to guarantee the Improvements. The
   City, in its sole discretion, may accept a retainer in lieu of a warranty bond, to be
   held for the one-year warranty period, provided the terms of such retainer are
   mutually agreed upon in writing. No permitting fees or inspection fees shall be
   charged to the Developer for the Improvements, and the City will treat the
   Improvements as a City project for purposes of such fees.




                                           4
   c) The Developer shall ensure that all work performed under this Agreement
   strictly adheres to any approved Engineering Designs and to the City’s adopted
   standards and specifications, unless otherwise agreed in writing by the Parties.
   Any deviation from the approved Engineering Designs without prior written City
   approval may, at the City's sole reasonable discretion, result in the denial of
   reimbursement for the affected portion of the Actual System Costs, provided that
   the City shall consult with the Developer to assess the impact and propose
   corrective measures before denying reimbursement, unless the deviation poses
   an immediate safety hazard.

   d) Reimbursement under this Agreement shall be limited to the Actual System
   Costs, as defined in Section 2, and only for work within the Project Scope. No
   reimbursement shall be provided for any work or costs outside the Project Scope
   or for any Project Improvements except as expressly permitted under Section 2(a)
   for Project Improvements if excess State Funds remain.

   e) To receive reimbursement for Actual System Costs, the Developer shall submit
   Draw Requests to the City, accompanied by written records of Actual System
   Costs, including but not limited to receipts, invoices, contractor cost sheets, and
   other documentation verifying costs incurred. For any real property acquisition
   or land transfers necessary for the Improvements, the Developer shall provide
   closing documents or other commercially reasonable evidence of those costs. The
   City shall review and approve Draw Requests in accordance with Section 6, the
   approval of which by the City shall not be unreasonably withheld.

4) City Obligations.

   a) The City shall review and approve the Engineering Designs submitted by the
   Developer, and provided the submissions are complete and comply with
   applicable regulatory standards, return any redlines or complete the review
   within the timeframe required by the Utah Municipal Land Use, Development,
   and Management Act (Utah Code 10-9a, et seq., as amended) and applicable
   Draper City ordinances.

   b) The City shall review Draw Requests submitted by the Developer pursuant to
   Section 6 and approve or deny without unreasonable delay and in accordance
   with the terms of this Agreement. Approved reimbursements for Actual System
   Costs shall be paid to the Developer in accordance with Section 6 and subject to
   the availability of funds as described in Section 2.


                                          5
   c) The City shall reimburse the Developer for eligible Actual System Costs, and for
   Project Improvements if excess State Funds remain, as set forth in Section 2 and
   up to the Maximum Reimbursement.

   d) The City shall waive all permitting fees and inspection fees that would
   otherwise apply to the Developer for the Improvements.

   e) The Parties shall mutually agree upon a reasonable construction schedule for
   the Improvements, to be attached hereto as Exhibit B upon agreement. Any
   amendments to said schedule must be approved in writing by both Parties.

5) Property Acquisition.

   The Parties shall cooperate in good faith to facilitate any property acquisitions,
   transfers, or dedications necessary to complete the Improvements set forth in
   Exhibit A, including potential transfers from UDOT to the City or from the City to
   Developer as needed to support the Development and public infrastructure
   needs. All conveyances of real property from the Developer to the City required
   under this Agreement shall be made by warranty deed, free and clear of any liens
   or encumbrances, at no cost to the City. Upon approval of this agreement,
   Developer agrees to convey to the City all rights of way over the Property for the
   purpose of providing access and infrastructure improvements. The City shall be
   responsible for acquiring any third-party property via condemnation necessary
   for the Improvements, and such costs, if tied to the System Improvements, may
   be included in the Actual System Costs eligible for reimbursement. Construction
   of the Improvements shall not commence until all necessary rights of way have
   been conveyed to the City or acquired by the City. If excess State Funds remain
   after full reimbursement of Actual System Costs, the City may, at its sole
   discretion, reimburse the Developer for property acquisition costs associated
   with Project Improvements, up to the limits set forth in Section 2(a).

6) Reimbursement Payments.

   a) The City shall make reimbursement payments to Developer for eligible Actual
   System Costs, and for Project Improvements as provided in Sections 2 and 7, only
   after the Developer submits a completed Draw Request Form as provided in
   Exhibit C, accompanied by all required documentation verifying the costs
   incurred. The City shall review and either approve or deny each Draw Request
   within 14 business days of receipt, provided all documentation is complete. If the

                                          6
  City denies a Draw Request, it shall provide written notice to the Developer stating
  the reasons for denial. Failure to provide such notice within 14 business days shall
  constitute approval of the Draw Request.

  b)   Prior to reimbursement, the City shall inspect and verify that the
  Improvements or portion thereof for which reimbursement is sought have been
  completed in accordance with the City-approved Engineering Designs and
  specifications. Inspections shall be conducted as part of the Draw Request review
  process within the 14-business-day period specified in subsection (a).

  c) Upon approval of a Draw Request and verification of the Improvements, the
  City shall issue payment to the Developer within 14 business days, subject to the
  availability of funds as described in Section 2.

  d) Reimbursement for stored materials purchased in advance by the Developer
  or its subcontractors shall be permitted only for materials intended for the
  Improvements, provided the Developer submits documentation verifying
  purchase, delivery, and storage at the project site or a City-approved offsite
  location. Such materials shall be subject to City inspection and approval to
  confirm compliance with the Project Scope. Ownership of stored materials shall
  transfer to the City only upon reimbursement to Developer for such materials, or
  upon their incorporation into the Improvements and City acceptance, whichever
  occurs first.

7) Reimbursement Amounts.

  a) Maximum Reimbursement. The maximum reimbursement for the Actual
  System Costs shall not exceed three million four hundred fifty thousand dollars
  ($3,450,000). If excess State Funds remain after full reimbursement of Actual
  System Costs, the City, at its sole discretion, shall, in collaboration with the
  Developer, reimburse the Developer for verified costs of Project Improvements,
  as described in Section 2(a), up to the lesser of the remaining State Funds or the
  verified costs of such public-benefit portions, as mutually determined by the City
  and Developer.

  b) “Actual System Costs” means the costs actually incurred or expended by the
  Developer to acquire property, design, engineer, install, and construct the System
  Improvements, excluding any costs related to bonding, insurance, permitting
  fees, development fees, or inspection fees, as these are either the Developer’s
  responsibility or waived by the City per Sections 3 and 4. Costs arising from any

                                         7
  City-initiated condemnation or threat of condemnation for property acquisitions
  shall be borne by the funding source for the specific improvement for which the
  property is acquired, as determined by the City.

  c)   Documentation.       The Developer shall provide to the City detailed
  documentation, acceptable to the City, demonstrating the Actual System Costs
  incurred for the acquisition, design, engineering, construction, and installation of
  the System Improvements, and if applicable, for Project Improvements that may
  be eligible for reimbursement under Section 2(a). Acceptable documentation
  includes, but is not limited to, receipts, checks, vouchers, bills, statements,
  contractor cost sheets, bid documents, change orders, real property closing
  documents, and any other information necessary for the City to verify the costs
  incurred. The Developer’s failure to submit complete and acceptable
  documentation with a Draw Request shall result in the rejection of the
  undocumented claimed amount.

  d) No Interest. No interest shall be included in the reimbursement amount, and
  no interest shall be paid by the City on any amounts due to the Developer under
  this Agreement, regardless of the timing of reimbursement payments.

8) Ownership of System Improvements and Warranty Period.

  a) Transfer of Ownership. The Developer shall convey fee title absolute to the
  City for the System Improvements, pursuant to the terms of Section 5, including
  associated property and rights-of-way, via warranty deed, free of liens or
  encumbrances.

  b) Inspection and Acceptance. Within 14 business days of the Developer’s
  completion notice, the City shall inspect the System Improvements for
  compliance. Within 7 business days post-inspection, the City shall issue a written
  acceptance or specify deficiencies in writing. The Developer shall promptly
  remedy deficiencies at its expense until compliant.

  c) Warranty. The System Improvements are subject to a one-year warranty from
  the date of the City’s acceptance. The Developer shall repair or replace any defects
  in workmanship or materials at its expense, secured by a warranty bond or City-
  approved retainer.




                                         8
   d) Warranty Release. The City may inspect for deficiencies before the warranty
   period ends and the Developer shall remedy deficiencies within 30 days’ notice. If
   the City waives its inspection rights, the warranty period ends after one year from
   the date of the City’s acceptance.

   e) Project Improvements. The City shall not own Project Improvements unless
   dedicated to the City or otherwise agreed to in writing, subject to the same
   ownership and warranty terms.

9) Term of Agreement

   This Agreement commences on the date of execution by both Parties and shall
   terminate upon the later of (i) the expiration of the one-year warranty period for
   the System Improvements, or (ii) the City’s full reimbursement to the Developer
   of eligible Actual System Costs, up to the Maximum Reimbursement.

10) Effect of Agreement. This Agreement shall not relieve the Developer of any
   obligations imposed by federal, state, or local laws, ordinances, regulations, or
   standards applicable to the Development, System Improvements, or Project
   Improvements. The terms and conditions of this Agreement are in addition to,
   and do not supersede, any obligations under other development agreements,
   improvement construction and guarantee agreements, or other agreements
   related to the Project Scope.

11) Assignment. Neither the Reimbursement Agreement nor any of its provisions,
   terms or conditions may be assigned to any other party, individual, or entity
   without the prior written consent of City. However, the Developer may assign this
   Agreement to an affiliate or successor with City consent, not unreasonably
   withheld, provided the assignee assumes all obligations, and such consent shall
   be deemed granted if not denied with written reasons within 30 days of request.

12) Entire Agreement. This Agreement constitutes the entire understanding
   between the parties regarding reimbursement to Developer for the System
   Improvements, and if applicable, Project Improvements as described in Section
   2(a), and supersedes all prior written or oral agreements, representations,
   promises, or understandings between the parties concerning such
   reimbursement. This Agreement does not affect or supersede other agreements
   between the Parties related to the Development or Project Scope that do not
   pertain to reimbursement.


                                          9
13) Binding Effect. This Agreement shall be binding upon and inure to the benefit
   of the Parties and their respective officers, employees, representatives, agents,
   members, successors, and permitted assigns.

14) Severability. If any provision of this Agreement is held invalid or unenforceable,
   such invalidity or unenforceability shall not affect the remaining provisions, which
   shall remain in full force and effect. This Agreement shall be construed as if the
   invalid or unenforceable provision were omitted, provided that the modified
   Agreement preserves the basic intent of the Parties.

15) Amendment. This Agreement may be amended only by a written document
   signed by both Parties.

16) Construction of Agreement. This Agreement shall be construed as a whole
   according to its fair meaning and governed by the laws of the State of Utah. The
   Parties acknowledge that they have reviewed this Agreement, understand its
   contents and legal effect, and have had the opportunity to consult with legal
   counsel. The terms of this Agreement shall not be construed in favor of or against
   any Party due to the Parties’ mutual negotiation. Headings are for reference only
   and shall not affect the interpretation of this Agreement.

17) Venue. Any action arising out of or related to this Agreement shall be filed in a
   state or federal court located in Salt Lake County, Utah. The Parties consent to
   the jurisdiction of such courts and waive any objection to venue in Salt Lake
   County, Utah, including claims of inconvenient forum.

18) Counterparts. This Agreement may be executed in multiple counterparts, each
   of which shall constitute one and the same instrument. A facsimile, electronic, or
   other non-original copy of a signature shall be deemed an original for purposes
   of enforcing or establishing of the validity of this Agreement.

19) Dispute Resolution and Attorneys’ Fees. Any disputes arising out of or related
   to this Agreement shall first be submitted to mediation under AAA mediation
   rules within 30 days of written notice. If unresolved, disputes shall proceed to
   binding arbitration under the AAA Construction Industry Arbitration Rules,
   conducted in Salt Lake County, Utah. The prevailing party in any dispute
   regardless of forum is entitled to recover reasonable attorneys’ fees and costs.
