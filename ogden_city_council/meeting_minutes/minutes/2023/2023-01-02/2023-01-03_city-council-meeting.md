consideration. The ACFR is prepared by the City Administration each year as part of the fiscal year-end budget process. The
ACER provides an overall picture of the City’s fimds, investments, assets, and debt information over and above the regular annual
budget. The City’s budget provides a plan for revenues and expenditures, while the ACFR provides an overview of the prior
year’s financial activity.

City Comptroller Stout recognized the efforts of City staff, especially the staffmembers of her office, in facilitating the
audit process and development of ACFR. She noted that the document will be posted on the City’s website within 24 hours ofthis | /
meeting and indicated she looks forward to continued discussion and review of the content of both the audit and ACFR during a
February work session scheduled for that purpose. She suggested that the Council review the management analysis, which
summarizes key points of the document and the City’s fund balance position.

internal collections group to serve as a repository for the City’s outstanding debts that could be managed and worked by a
centralized team. The collections group currently manages debts for general billing, utility billing, code enforcement, parking, and

whether notification of collection fees is required and found that is not the case; however, the City does include verbiage on

attempt to recover approximately 90 percent of the costs associated with debt collection.

A memo from the Management Services Department regarding proposed mid-year fee adjustments came before the

Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and stated the most previous proposed fee increases were effective on January
1, 2019, for green fees, season passes, and certain punch tickets and 2021, with a $1.00 increase in motorized golf cart rental per
nine holes. Historically, green fees generally increase in the marketplace on an every-other-year basis. As a result of the increased
amounts of rounds being played, Administration has found that a number of customers are supportive of periodic fee adjustments,
if the quality of the experience continues to improve at both courses. Sixty-two (62) new golf carts will debut in 2023, and
Administration will also move forward with continued irrigation projects and course enhancements. The business of golf is very
competitive, and the consumer has many choices in our local market. Consequently, Ogden courses must continue to provide a
superior product, which comes at a cost. As appropriate, at times, it is necessary to pass a cost increase on to the consumer. He
presented a chart illustrating the proposed rate adjustments of $1 per nine holes and $2 per 18 holes, between $13 and $20 for
various types of punch passes, and between $50 and $200 for season passes, with a $600 fee increase for the annual high school
team fee. There will also be minor adjustments to discount rates, cart fees, and driving range fees. The proposed golf fee
amendments are estimated to increase golf revenue by approximately $235K annually. Administration will propose a budget
increase of $115,000 for the period of January to June of FY23 in the golf fund to recognize the additional golf revenue
anticipated.

courses, including the purchase of new carts and irrigation system improvements.

Proposed Ordinance 2023-4 amending Ogden Municipal Code Chapter 6 of
Title 5 to revise provisions relative to burglary and robbery alarms and
alarm businesses
A memo from the Police Department regarding proposed amendments to the City’s alarm ordinance came before the :
Council for consideration. The memo stated Administration proposes amendments that modify Ogden City Code Chapter 6, :
Section 5-6-1 through 5-6-14 as follows:
e Citizens and Businesses will no longer be required to file an alarm permit application or pay a fee.
e The Police Department, Business Licensing, and Treasurers office will spend less time monitoring, billing, and
collecting for false alarms charges.
e Ogden City will retain the ability to bill reoccurring and unresponsive alarm owners.
Police Chief Young summarized the memo and stated pursuant to current Ogden City Code of Ordinances Chapter 6, 5-
6-3, every alarm user who is also engaged in any "business" within the city, shall obtain from the Police Department an alarm
user's permit for each system prior to any installation of an alarm system. Users of alarm systems having both a robbery alarm
system and a burglary or intrusion alarm system shall obtain separate permits for each system if they are distinct alarm systems.
\ An application for a burglary or robbery alarm user's permit and an annual twenty-five-dollar ($25.00) fee shall be filed with the
. Business License Division prior to installation of the system and thereafter on or before the business license renewal due date,
with a copy of the application to the Police Department. Also under Section 5-6-8, alarm permits may be subject to revocation
requiring a written notice from the Police Department and a meeting with the Chief of Police or their designee may be requested.
The use of third-party business and residential burglary alarm systems (Vivint, Ring, ADT, etc.) has increased based on low cost
and available technologies. Under the current ordinance, it is unlawful to report an alarm at a location that does not have a
documented permit. Management of the alarm permits, false alarm billing, and revocations utilizes a full-time employee at the
Ogden Police Department and a significant amount of an employee’s time in the Treasurer’s Office. Ogden Police and the Ogden
City Legal Department have reviewed the current alarm ordinance with the goal of removing the permit requirement, removing
language making it illegal to report an alarm at an unpermitted residence or business, and doing away with the revocation and
appeal process. Through research or best practices in law enforcement, Ogden Police Department believes alarm permits and
management are an unnecessary expense to the community, and do not accomplish the goal of reducing false alarm responses by
police officers. Chief Young concluded that under the proposed changes, Ogden City will retain the ability to fine owners of
alarms that have more than three false alarms in one calendar year. The Police Department staff has thoroughly researched current
practices in similar sized cities in the intermountain west and have found the proposed change to be consistent with what cities of
similar size are doing regarding false alarms.
Council member White asked if the City has reached out to alarm companies in the City to communicate the proposed.
changes. Chief Young answered yes and added that he has also made contact with the president of the Utah Alarm Association
who has indicated he will also make contact with all alarm companies regarding the changes. He stated most alarm companies
were not responsive to contact from the City. He noted he has created a spreadsheet cataloguing the contact and feedback from the
alarm companies and he is happy to share that with Council staff.
