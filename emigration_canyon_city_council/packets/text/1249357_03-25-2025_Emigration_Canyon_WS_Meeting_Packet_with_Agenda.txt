1. CALL TO ORDER

Mayor Joe Smolka, presiding, called the meeting to order at 6:00 PM.

2. COUNCIL DISCUSSION TOPICS
   A. Identifying Specific Topics for Work Sessions Including Road Projects, Capital
      Improvement Projects, and Finances.

Mayor Smolka opened the discussion by inviting council members to propose ideas for topics
they would like to address in future work sessions. He noted that discussions could take place
during the current meeting and also be added to future agendas.

Council Member Pinon expressed a specific interest in following up on the canyon survey
conducted the previous year, emphasizing its importance as it related to community wellness.

Council Member Hawkes suggested several topics for consideration:

   1. Collaborative Decision-Making in Meetings: She proposed that two council members
      be present at meetings with entities such as Rocky Mountain Power to ensure that

      transparency and accountability in the decision-making process.
   2. Quality Control in Utility Work: Council Member Hawkes raised concerns about the
      quality control for utility work conducted during the road moratorium. She suggested
      establishing a mechanism to ensure the proper completion of projects to the expected
      standards. This concern was echoed by other council members who engaged in a
      discussion on the possibility of requiring a bond for such work, as a guarantee of quality.
   3. Traffic Data Collection: She recommended installing traffic counters to gather data on
      vehicle numbers during road closures or emergencies, which could prove useful for
      obtaining grants and making informed decisions regarding infrastructure and traffic
      management.
   4. Speed Limit Signage in Pinecrest: Noting recent accidents, Council Member Hawkes
      highlighted the need for additional speed limit signage in Pinecrest. She detailed the
      current placement of signs and areas lacking adequate signage, suggesting that
      additional signs could enhance driver awareness and safety.

Cameron Platt provided clarification regarding the appointment of committees. He explained
that while the mayor could initiate committees, the council collectively, or the mayor
individually, could assign individuals to serve on these committees. This clarification helped
outline the framework for collaboration and delegation within the council.

Council Member Brems suggested collaboration with the Unified Police Department (UPD) to
develop a summer noise and speeding strategy. This strategy could include deploying a
mobile radar unit to monitor and address speeding issues, thereby enhancing community
safety during the busy summer months.

   B. City Budget.

Council Member Harris inquired about the city's revenue sources and the allocation of sales
tax, particularly for online purchases. This led to a broader discussion among council members
about the challenges of ensuring accurate tax allocation given the shared zip code with other
areas like St. Mary's and Indian Hills.

Council Member Pinon suggested that residents use the correct four-digit zip code extension
for online purchases to ensure that sales tax revenue is properly allocated to the city.

Mayor Smolka elaborated that the city's primary sources of income include Class B and C road
funds and sales tax, emphasizing the importance of verifying that the city is receiving its fair
share.

Council members debated the intricacies of tracking sales taxes from online sales, with
Council Member Harris raising concerns about the allocation process and the mechanisms that
ensure accuracy. It was noted that while the zone's population might be lower relative to the
larger metro area, the online purchasing habits might differ, potentially impacting the actual tax
revenues generated.

Mayor Smolka proposed the idea of inviting someone from the state or possibly involving the
MSD in a project to ascertain these allocations and ensure the city's interests align with
logistical realities.

Council Member Pinon pointed out that most significant vendors now collect sales tax, and
these are distributed by the state, with a portion being shared based on population and point of
sale. The council acknowledged the need for outreach to educate residents about the
significance of including the four-digit zip code in online orders to support fair allocation.

Council Member Harris highlighted that despite differing population densities between
municipalities, the purchasing patterns could heavily influence where the taxable actions occur,
suggesting a need for detailed investigation and ongoing monitoring.

 Canyon      Sales Tax Information
             • Annual Revenue. The City generates about
               $300,000 per year in sales tax, and about $30,000
               per year in County Highway and Public Transit tax
               (SB136).
             • Distribution Formula. Sales tax distribution is
               based on a combination of the following (less
               some nominal State-level administrative fees and
               deductions):
                 o 50% of the sales tax on direct point of sale
                    (POS) plus motor vehicle.
                 o Share of the 50% Statewide POS pool, which
                    is based on the City's population as a
                    percentage of the total state population.

             • Annual Revenue. The City generates about $105,000
               per year in class B&C Road Funds revenues.
             • Distribution Formula. Class B&C Road Funds
               distribution is based on a combination of the
               following:
                 o Weighted Mileage Detail (paved road miles and
                    unpaved road miles).
                 o A share based upon the population of the
                    municipality.
             o Use: Road maintenance or road-based capital
                improvements.
