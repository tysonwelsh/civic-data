                             AMENDMENT TO SECTION 13. 11. 040 OF THE TAYLORSVILLE MUNICIPAL CODE,
           AGENDA ITEM•
                             RELATED TO REGULATIONS FOR VEHICLES AND TRAILERS ASSOCIATED WITH A
                                                               HOME OCCUPATION.

                  FILE#:   3Z24/DCA-000476- 2024

    ORDINANCE REQUIRED:    0 Yes     0 No

                AUTHOR:    Jim Spung, AICP, Senior Planner
             PRESENTER:    Jim Spung, AICP, Senior Planner

                           The use of vehicles and trailers associated with home occupations has become
                           more common in recent years. In an effort to support business- owners and

                           entrepreneurs within the city, the city' s administration requested that staff research
                           potential amendments to the zoning code to allow for added flexibility regarding
                           the parking of business vehicles and trailers while also ensuring proper safeguards
                           are in place to minimize potentially negative impacts on surrounding uses.

                           The proposed amendments are intended to accommodate the parking of additional
                           commercial vehicles and trailers associated with a home occupations in areas

                           where such uses may be deemed acceptable. The primary changes proposed as part
                           of this Ordinance include:
              SUMMARY:
                               New modified standards to allow more vehicles and trailers for residential lots

                               that satisfy the following criteria:
                               o
                                    Within the R- 1- 20 or R- 1- 40 zoning district.
                                o
                                    Primary access via collector or arterial street.
                                o   Vehicles and trailers parked behind primary residence and screened from
                                    public view.

                                o   Parking and maneuvering area limited to 35 percent of the rear yard area
                                    and located at least 50 feet from neighboring dwellings.
                                o   Maximum of 5 vehicles and 10 trailers at any given time.
                                o   Registered to the business owner or current resident.
                                o   Storage of equipment, materials, etc. not allowed.




                                                1
                                       New modified standard allowing a gravel surface for parking and
                                       maneuvering areas for residential lots that meet the above criteria may be used
                                       when:

                                       o   Parking and maneuvering area has been design and constructed to support
                                           weight of vehicles.

                                       o   Gravel is clean, compacted, free of weeds, dust, etc.
                                       o   Minimum 30- foot paved track-out pad is installed between the gravel
                                           surface and public street.

                                       o   Area is maintained to remain compliant with standards.


                               Staff performed GIS analysis and identified 23 residential properties that satisfy
                               the location criteria proposed( Exhibit B). An individual review of each property
                               would be required during business license review to ensure proposed operations
                               satisfy the standards.


                               On July 9, 2024, the Planning Commission reviewed the proposed draft and
                               expressed the following concerns:

                                      The potential creation of nonconforming conditions if adjacent lots were
                                      subdivided( e. g., flag lots);
                                      Compromising residential character and future residential development; and
                                      Potential noise, dust, and visual impacts to adjacent neighbors.


                               The Planning Commission discussed increasing the minimum 50- foot setback
                               proposed between parking and maneuvering areas and adjacent dwellings as well
                               as limiting the hours larger vehicles could operate; however, the Planning
                               Commission did not feel comfortable with the merits of the proposed draft and
                               forwarded a negative recommendation to the City Council.

                               Staff recommends approval of Ordinance No. 24- 08, based on the findings

                        ASSOCIATED WITH A HOME OCCUPATION.

       WHEREAS, the Taylorsville        City Council ( the " Council") met in regular session on
August 7, 2024, to consider, among other things, approving a text amendment to Section 13. 11. 040
of the Taylorsville Municipal Code, related to regulations for vehicles and trailers associated with
a home occupation; and


       WHEREAS, pursuant to TAYLORSVILLE MUNICIPAL CODE Section 13. 35. 050, notice of a
public hearing before the City' s Planning Commission was published on the city' s official website
on June 27, 2024; and


       WHEREAS, the notice of a public hearing before the City' s Planning Commission was
also published on the Utah Public Notice Website on June 27, 2024; and


        WHEREAS, the City' s Planning Commission held a properly noticed public hearing on
the proposed amendments on July 9, 2024, during which the Planning Commission voted 5- 2 to
forward a negative recommend to the Council regarding the proposed text amendment; and

       WHEREAS, although not required by City ordinances, the City Council heard courtesy
public comment on the proposed amendments during its regularly scheduled meeting on August
7, 2024; and


       WHEREAS, after careful consideration, the Council has determined that it is in the best
interest of the health, safety, and welfare of the citizens of Taylorsville to approve the proposed
amendments.




       NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED by the Taylorsville City Council that the
proposed text amendment to Section 13. 11. 040 of the Taylorsville Municipal Code, related to
regulations for vehicles and trailers associated with a home occupation, are hereby approved, as
articulated in Exhibit" A."


       This ordinance, assigned Ordinance No. 24- 08, shall take effect as soon as it shall be
published or posted as required by law, deposited, and recorded in the office of the City Recorder,
and accepted as required herein.

                                                   EXHIBIT A

 1   13. 11. 040: HOME OCCUPATIONS:

 2   A.   Purpose:
 3        1.   Provide an opportunity for home occupations as an accessory use when they are compatible with
 4             the neighborhoods in which they are located.
 5        2.   Provide an opportunity for a home occupation to engage in the business of childcare and other
 6             group child activities and encourage this type of home occupation to draw clients/customers from
 7             their immediate neighborhood.
 8        3.   Guide business activities which are not compatible with neighborhoods to appropriate
 9             nonresidential zoning districts.
10        4.   Safeguard peace, quiet, and domestic tranquility within all residential neighborhoods. Protect
11
               residents from the adverse effects of business uses being conducted in residential areas from
12             noise, nuisance, traffic, fire hazards, and other possible business uses that create significant
13             impacts on a neighborhood.
14        5.   Provide a means to enforce and regulate the businesses that are licensable through the authority of
15             the business license regulations of the applicable ordinances, and if necessary, terminate home
16             occupations if violations of the ordinances regulating home occupations occur.
17   B.   Review Procedure.
18        1.   The Director is the approval authority for a home occupation; however, if the Director determines
19             the application is unusually complex, requires greater scrutiny or public involvement, or raises
20             potentially unique or serious impacts on the City or the surrounding development and/ or
21             neighborhood( s), the Director may refer the application to the Planning Commission for decision
22             in accordance with the nonadministrative conditional use procedure established in Chapter 13. 33.
23        2.   If the Director refers the application to the Planning Commission. the Director shall notify the
24             applicant in writing and include an explanation of why the application has been referred to the
25             Planning Commission.
26        3.   If the Director refers the application to the Planning Commission, a notice for a public hearing
27             shall be provided in accordance with Section 13. 35. 090.

28   C.   General Standards. All Home occupations shall operate in accordance with the following
29        requirements:

30        1.   Licensing and Number.
31             a. Home occupations shall only operate after receiving a home occupation business license from
32                the City, in accordance with Title 5: Business Licenses and Regulations.
33             b. No more than two home occupations shall be allowed per dwelling unit.
34             c. No more than one residential child day care, residential preschool, high- impact residential
35                instruction, or home occupation including a business vehicle or trailer shall be allowed per lot
36                  or parcel.

37             d.   Home occupations shall remain compliant with all applicable statutes, rules and regulations.
38             e.   Home occupations shall not be licensed, approved, or conducted without the written consent
39                  of the property owner.
40             f. A home occupation license granted by the City shall not be assignable or transferable to
41                  another person or another location.
42             g.   Home occupations are subject to review upon complaint. If a complaint is received by the
43                  City, the home occupation will be reviewed for compliance with applicable ordinances and
44                  conditions of approval.
45   2.   Location and Proximity. Residential child day cares, residential preschools, and high-impact
46        residential instruction home occupations are subject to the following standards:
47        a.   Shall only be allowed in an attached or detached single-family dwelling that:
48              1) Has a privately fenced rear yard area for the sole and exclusive use of the residents of the
49                   lot or parcel on which the home occupation is situated; and
50              2) Fronts on a public or private street with a minimum pavement width of 25 feet that has
51                   curb, gutter, and sidewalk; and

52              3)   The minimum 25- foot pavement width and curb, gutter, and sidewalk extends from the
53                   home occupation lot or parcel to the nearest through street with those same
54                   improvements.
55        b.   Shall not be permitted on a flag lot.
56        c.   Shall not be permitted on a lot or parcel with an accessory dwelling unit( ADU).
57        d.   Shall not be located within 300 feet of another actively licensed:
58              1) Residential child day care;
59              2) Residential preschool; or
60              3) High-impact residential instruction.
61             This distance shall be measured in a straight line from the nearest property line. This standard
62             does not apply if the properties are bisected by a freeway or principal arterial street, as
63             identified in the Taylorsville General Plan.
64   3.   Operational.

65        a. Home occupation activity shall be clearly incidental to the residential dwelling. No more than
66           50 percent of the total floor area of any dwelling unit shall be used for the home occupation.
67        b. The outside appearance and architecture of the dwelling shall not be modified to
68             accommodate or promote a home occupation.
69        c.   Home occupations shall be conducted entirely within the dwelling. Garages and accessory
70             buildings shall not be used for home occupations except for the parking of approved business
71             vehicles or trailers.
72        d.   No storage of merchandise, equipment, supplies, or materials shall occur outside of the home
73             and shall not be visible from outside of the dwelling. Explosive or combustible materials shall
74             not be stored or used in association with a home occupation.
75        e.   Delivery of commodities used in connection with the home occupation shall be provided by
76             parcel or letter carrier service only.
77        f. Activities associated with the home occupation shall not produce traffic, light, glare, noise,
78             fumes, odor, or vibration that will diminish the residential character of the neighborhood.
79        g.   Unless otherwise authorized by the decision-making authority, home occupations shall not be
80             conducted after 08: 00 P.M. or before 6: 00 A.M.
81        h.   Employees that do not reside on-site are prohibited, except for residential child day cares
82             which may have one nonresident employee. Off-site employees may visit the residence in
83             accordance with§        13. 11. 040( C)( 4): On-Site Visits.
84        i.   Property address numbers a minimum of four inches in height that are a contrasting color
85
               from the primary dwelling shall be maintained on the dwelling unit.
86   4.   Vehicles and Trailers.

87        a.   Only one vehicle may be used in association with a home occupation.
88        b.   Vehicles and trailers used in association with a home occupation must be parked in an
89             approved off-street parking location and shall not encroach or overhang on any sidewalk or
90             pedestrian way.
 91        C.   The number of off-street parking spaces must be sufficient for both the residents of the home
 92             and the vehicle and/ or trailer used in association with a home occupation.
 93        d.   The size of a vehicle used in association with a home occupation shall not exceed a 10, 000-
 94             pound gross vehicle weight rating and have no more than two axles.
 95        e.
                Tow trucks, tanker trucks, box vans, delivery vans, and similar vehicles are prohibited and
 96             shall not be parked on-site. Such vehicles may be located off-site in an approved storage
 97             location.
 98        f.   Only one trailer with no more than two axles may be used in association with a home
 99             occupation.

100        g.   The maximum body length of an enclosed trailer is 20 feet. The maximum body length of an
101             open trailer is 16 feet. For purposes of this standard, trailer length does not include the trailer
102             tongue.

103        h.   Auto transport trailers designed to transport more than one vehicle or that exceed the body
104             length requirements are prohibited and shall not be parked on-site. Such trailers may be
105             located off-site in an approved storage location.

106   5.   Modified Vehicle and Trailer Standards

107        a.   Vehicles and trailers used in association with a home occupation that do not satisfy the
108             standards in paragraph( 4) above may be approved when the following criteria are satisfied:
109              1)   The home occupation associated with the vehicle( s) and/ or trailer( s) receives an
110                   administrative conditional use permit approval;

111             2) The lot or parcel with the home occupation is in an R- 1- 20 or R- 1- 40 zoning district and
112                   is one acre or larger in area;
113             3) The primary means of ingress and egress to the lot or parcel with the home occupation is
114                   via a collector or arterial street, as identified in the General Plan;
115             4) All vehicles and trailers related to the home occupation are parked behind the primary
116                dwelling and screened from public view;
117             5) The parking and maneuvering area for vehicles and trailers is limited to 35 percent of the
118                   rear yard area;

119             6) The designated parking and maneuvering area is located a minimum of 50 feet from the
120                nearest dwelling;
121             7) No more than five vehicles and 10 trailers may be parked or stored within the approved
122                   on- site parking and maneuvering area at any given time;
123             8) The vehicles and trailers related to the home occupation must be registered to the home
124                   occupation business or the current resident; and
125             9) The outdoor storage of equipment, materials, or other items related to the home
126                   occupation is prohibited;
127        b.   Gravel may be used for parking and maneuvering areas for vehicles and trailers associated
128             with a home occupation when:
129              1)   The criteria in paragraph( 5)( a) have been satisfied;
130             2) The parking and maneuvering area has been designed and constructed to support the
131                   weight
                         g   of the vehicles and trailers usingthe area;
132             3) The gravel used is clean, a minimum of two inches deep, compacted, free of dust, grass,
133                   and weeds, and contained with durable borders;
134             4) A minimum 30- foot paved track- out pad is provided between the public right-of-way and
135                   the gravel surface. Gravel surfacing is not permitted within 30 feet of the public right-of-
136                   way; and
137             5) Gravel parking and maneuvering areas are maintained to remain compliant with these
138                   standards.
139        376. On- Site Visits. Clients, customers, and/or patrons may visit the home occupation in accordance
140             with the following requirements:
141             a.   Direct retail sales from the residence is prohibited. Products purchased online may be picked
142                  up on- site.
143             b.   The number of off-street parking spaces at the residence must be sufficient for the residents
144                  of the home and any clients, customers, patrons, or others visiting the home occupation.
145             C.   Except for residential child day care, residential preschools, and residential instruction, home
146                  occupations shall have no more than two business- related visitors per hour.
147             d.   For residential child day care, residential preschools, and residential instruction, drop-off and
148                  pick-up shall not occur in the public right-of-way.
149   D. Residential Child Day Care. Residential child day care may be offered as a home occupation in
150      accordance with the standards in this Section 13. 11. 040, and the following:
151        1.   Residential child day care means a home occupation that provides care for between five and 12
152             qualified children at any given time, as   defined in Utah Code § 26- 39- 102. The designated
153             number of children includes the caregiver' s own children that meet the state' s definition of
154              qualified children,"   at the time the home occupation is conducted.
155        2.   A secure outdoor play area is required for all residential child day care home occupations and
156             shall comply with the following:
157             a.   The play area shall be located in the rear and/ or side yard of the dwelling.
158             b. The play area shall be secured by a well-maintained fence that is at least six feet in height
159                with a self-latching gate.
160             c. The outdoor play area shall be maintained in good condition and be finished and landscaped
161                to include ground covers, paving, or other surfaces that do not pose a risk to children.
162             d. The outdoor play area shall not be used by the residential child day care before 8: 00 A.M. or
163                  after 8: 00 P.M.

164   E.   Residential Preschools. Residential preschools may be offered as a home occupation in accordance
165        with the standards in this Section 13. 11. 040, and the following:
166        1.  A residential preschool is a home occupation providing care and instruction, whether or not for
167            compensation, of 12 or fewer children including the caregiver' s own children under the age of six
168            and not in full day school, prior to entrance into kindergarten.
169        2.   A maximum of 12 children per session is allowed. A single session shall not exceed four
170             consecutive hours per day and individuals shall not attend more than one session per day.
171        3.   Instructor licensing and curriculum requirements shall comply with the requirements of the State
172             of Utah, as applicable.

173   F.   Residential Instruction. Residential instruction may be offered as a home occupation in accordance
174        with the standards in this Section 13. 11. 040, and the following:
175        1.  Residential instruction is a home occupation providing instruction( e. g., dance lessons, music
176             lessons, martial arts lessons, or other instruction for children that is artistic in nature), whether or
177             not for compensation, of children under the age of 18 years old. Residential instruction does not
178             include residential child day care or residential preschools as defined in this Title.
179        2.   Low-Impact Residential Instruction. Low-impact residential instruction is the instruction of four
180             or fewer children at any given time.
181        3.   High-Impact Residential Instruction.

182             a.
                     High-impact residential instruction is the instruction of five or more children at any given
183                  time.
184             b.   A maximum of 12 children per session is allowed. A single session shall not exceed four
185                  consecutive hours per   day and individuals shall not attend more than one session per day.
186   G.        Home Occupations Not Allowed: The following uses are not allowed as home occupations:
187        1.   Any use that requires or utilizes chemicals or hazardous materials in excess of volumes allowed
188             by the International Fire Code in a residential setting.
189        2.   Auto body or fender work.
190        3.   Auto, truck, diesel, boat, trailer, or motorcycle repair or services at the residence.
191        4.   Furniture or cabinet making.
192        5.   Junkyards, storage or recycling yards.
193        6.   Lawn mower, small engine or motor repair or services.
194        7.   Major appliance repair( washers, dryers, refrigerators, etc.).
195        8.   Manufacture or sale of explosives or similar products.
196        9.   Medical clinics and laboratories.
197        10. Mortuaries or crematoriums.
198        11. Reiki type therapy.
199        12. Sexually oriented businesses.
200        13. Short term rental.
201        14. Vehicle sales or rental exceeding one on- site vehicle at any given time.
202        15. Vehicle towing or impound operations.
203        16. Welding or ironworks.
204        17. Any other use that would produce traffic, light, glare, noise, fumes, odor or vibration that will
205             diminish the residential character of the neighborhood.

206   H.   Exceptions: Notwithstanding anything herein to the contrary, a home occupation is not required for
207        the following activities:
208        1.   Garage and yard sales. Refer to section 13. 11. 130 of this chapter.
209        2.   Temporary social gathering sales that do not exceed one day, such as Tupperware parties, book
210             parties, candle parties, etc., not to exceed four( 4) occurrences per year.
