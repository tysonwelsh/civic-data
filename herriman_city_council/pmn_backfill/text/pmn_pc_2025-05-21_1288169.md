   1.2. Presentation and discussion of the Herriman City Status Report and other demographic data
        for Herriman City – Trevor Ram, City Analyst

City Analyst Ram introduced himself as a management analyst for Herriman City, having been with
the city for two years. He explained that he prepares a monthly status report for the city council,
providing updates on metrics such as the number of new businesses, housing stats, and sales tax
information.

He presented a long-term trends analysis of housing and population within the city. He explained that
the analysis uses certificates of occupancy data, multiplied by an average household size, to estimate
the city's population at any given point. He also shared projections for the city's population at build-
out, estimated to occur around 2065. He noted that the projections showed a range of potential
population sizes, from the low 90,000s to just over 110,000 residents.

City Analyst Ram discussed the current estimated average household size of 3.39 persons per
household, based on the 2020 census. He mentioned that demographic projections suggest this
number may decrease to 3 or lower in the future.

In response to questions from commissioners, He explained that he has conducted comparisons of
staffing levels and budgets for various public works departments with neighboring cities. He also
mentioned that the Wasatch Front Regional Council provides good regional population trend data.

City Analyst Ram noted that multifamily housing was growing over time, with the current distribution
being roughly 60% single-family and 40% other housing types. He acknowledged some challenges in
data integrity and governance, particularly in defining and categorizing different housing types
consistently across city departments.

Commissioners expressed interest in obtaining data on owner-occupied residences, moves within the
city, and the volume of properties for sale. Director Maloy mentioned that some real estate market-
driven reports were being received by the administration, but they had not been extensively utilized
or fact-checked.

   1.3. Review of Agenda Items – Planning Staff
Planning Director Maloy introduced the agenda item 4.1, where a resident constructed a building that
did not comply with the approved permit, leading to the proposal of a text amendment for the city
code. This particular case arose from a misunderstanding by the resident, resulting in the construction
of a structure that exceeded the permitted specifications, specifically related to height and setback
requirements.
The planning commission engaged in a thorough discussion regarding the need for amendments to
ensure compliance and ease of ordinance administration. Key points of the discussion included the
importance of clear communication and education to prevent future noncompliance issues. This
highlighted the need for additional resources or educational materials to assist residents and
contractors in understanding the complexities of building codes.
The commission also considered the possibility of adjusting the language in the code to reduce
confusion among residents. It was acknowledged that the current code might be overly complex, with
various nuances that are not easily understood by the general public, leading to inadvertent violations.

The discussion underscored the importance of balancing property owners' rights with the need to
maintain neighborhood consistency and safety standards. Commissioners explored how these
proposed amendments might affect property owners, particularly in terms of their ability to use their
property as desired, while also protecting neighboring properties from potential adverse effects such
as excessively large or improperly placed structures.
Throughout the conversation, there was recognition of the challenges faced by staff in administering
the existing ordinance, particularly in communicating its requirements to residents. This led to a
consensus on the need to simplify the ordinance to facilitate better understanding and compliance,
along with a consideration of additional flexibility for different lot sizes and configurations.

Planning Director Maloy presented the proposed text amendment to the accessory structures
ordinance, initiated by a resident who experienced compliance issues with a building permit. He
emphasized that while the amendment was prompted by a specific incident, the commission should
consider it as a broader issue affecting the entire city.

He outlined the proposed amendments, which included:
   1. Clarifying language and intent
   2. Ensuring consistency with the recently recodified city code.
May 21, 2025 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                                         Page 5 of 7

   3. Potentially increasing the maximum height for small accessory structures (200 square feet
      or less) from 16 feet to 20 feet with building permit for a 2nd story loft space.
   4. Standardizing maximum building heights based on lot size rather than structure size.
   5. Establishing consistent minimum rear yard and side yard setbacks of 3 feet.
   6. Simplifying the front setback requirement for accessory structures.
   7. Adding a 10% height deviation allowance, similar to the existing setback deviation option.

Planning Director Maloy explained that these changes aimed at addressing issues such as complaints
about structures appearing too tall (despite being compliant) and simplifying the administration of
the ordinance.

The applicant, Cyle Jones, addressed the commission, expressing appreciation for the consideration
of the amendments. He explained that his property backs up to city-owned drainage land and
suggested that different setback standards could be appropriate for such situations.

Vice Chair Ferguson opened the public hearing.

No comments were offered.

Commissioner Garcia moved to close the public hearing, Seconded by Commissioner Jacobson and
all voted Aye

During the discussion, commissioners raised several points and concerns regarding the proposed text
amendments to the accessory structures ordinance. Among the topics discussed was the potential
impact of increasing maximum building heights on neighboring properties, especially in situations
where taller structures might cast shadows or otherwise affect the adjacent landowners.
Commissioners expressed the need to thoughtfully balance private property rights with the potential
adverse effects these changes could have on adjoining properties.

The complexity of the existing ordinance was another point of concern. Commissioners noted the
ordinance's intricacy and the desire for simplification, highlighting that the current multitude of
exceptions and conditions might confuse property owners and complicate the enforcement process
for city staff. The discussion also touched upon the relationship between the proposed amendments
and future conversations regarding Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs). Some commissioners
suggested that these changes might need to align with or anticipate the eventual integration of ADU
regulations.

The importance of maintaining flexibility for different lot sizes and configurations was also
emphasized. The commissioners recognized that Herriman City's diverse property sizes and shapes
require ordinances that can adapt to various scenarios while remaining equitable and clear. After an
extensive discussion covering these issues, the commission narrowed their focus to two specific
amendments. They decided to apply a 3-foot minimum setback for rear and side yards for structures
over 200 square feet, addressing concerns about spacing and potential intrusions into neighboring
properties' privacy or light. Additionally, they agreed to allow a 10% height deviation to account for
potential measurement errors during construction, acknowledging that minor discrepancies in
