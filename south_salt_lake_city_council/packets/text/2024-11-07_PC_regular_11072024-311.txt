 SYNOPSIS: On February 5, 2024, Tuan Tran, the
 owner of the lot at 159 E 3900 S, submitted a Plat
 Application to plat the subject property. The
 structure on the lot is primarily used as office space,
 and the parcel is located in the Commercial Corridor
 district. The purpose of the plat application is to
 legally plat the property, allowing for the
 submission of application for building permits to
 reconfigure parking spaces and adjust the internal
 layout of the building in order to bring it into
 compliance with parking requirements.

 On August 15, 2023, Tuan Tran applied for a
 Variance to request an exception to the minimum
 lot size standard for a proposed office development.
 This request was approved on October 20, 2023,
 allowing for a lot size of 0.11 acres, which matches the size of the applicant's property. The zoning code requires four
 parking stalls per 1,000 square feet of space, but the parcel only had two stalls for its 1,800 square feet. To meet
 parking requirements, the applicant reconfigured the building, creating six parking stalls on the first floor and reducing
 the upstairs office space to 1,400 square feet, now meeting the requirement of six parking spaces. Additionally, there
 will be a 10-foot public utility easement (PUE) around the lot, a cross-access easement for the shared drive approach,
 and a Public Right of Way dedication to South Salt Lake City on 3900 S. The existing roadways comply with current
 Public Right of Way standards.

 The Planning Commission is the land use authority over all Plat Applications.

 STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
 Staff recommends approval for a preliminary one-lot subdivision plat located at 159 East 3900 South.

 STAFF RECOMMENDATION:


Based upon the information submitted in the application, supporting documentation, and analysis of the relevant
code sections and general plan goals, Staff recommends the Planning Commission move to approve the preliminary
one-lot subdivision plat located at 159 E 3900 S, based on the findings of fact, conclusions of law and conditions of
approval for the subdivision in the staff report.


Findings of Fact:

     1. The applicant has an office building located at 159 East 3900 South.

     2. The lot is in the Commercial Corridor land use district.

     3. The “Office, Professional” land use is an approved use in the Commercial Corridor land use district.

     4. On August 15, 2023, the applicant Tuan Tran applied for a Variance requesting an exception to the minimum
         lot size standard as it is set forth in the Commercial District land use district for a proposed development of
         an office space.

     5. On October 20, 2023, the applicant’s request for an exception to the minimum lot area was approved for a
         lot size of 0.11 acres.

     6. The applicant’s property is .11 acres

     7. The code requires a minimum of four (4) parking stalls for every 1,000 SF of space. Currently the parcel has
         only two parking stalls for 1,800 SF.

     8. The applicant has now created 6 (six) parking stalls inside the first floor of the building and they have
         reconfigured the upstairs to be 1,400 SF of office space, which then only requires 6 (six) parking spaces.


     9. On February 05, 2024, the applicant submitted a Plat Application for 159 East 3900 South to consolidate a
         one-lot plat for parcel 16-31-303-021-0000

     10. The purpose of the plat application is to legally subdivide the lot, allowing for the submission of building
         permits to reconfigure parking spaces and adjust the internal layout of the building in order to bring it into
         compliance with parking requirements.



                                                           8
     11. No impact is expected as part of the business on environmentally sensitive lands. There is no environmentally
          sensitive land on site.

     12. No specific hazards or adverse impacts are expected due to the proposed use.

     13. The “Office Space” use is permitted in the Commercial Corridor land use district.

     14. The proposed development will comply with all requirements of the South Salt Lake Municipal Code.

     15. Staff has reviewed this subdivision application for compliance with South Salt Lake Municipal Code Sections
          17.10.120, 17.10.130, 17.10.140, 17.10.150, 17.10.160, 17.10.170, 17.10.180, 17.10.190 and 17.10.200, and
          found that this subdivision application is compliant with this section, as outlined above.

Conclusions of Law:

     1.    The use, as specifically conditioned below, is consistent with the South Salt Lake City General Plan.

     2.    The use, as specifically conditioned below, is compatible in use, scale, and design with allowed uses in the
           Commercial Corridor.

     3.    The use, as specifically conditioned below, does not compromise the health, safety, or welfare of: Persons
           employed within or using the proposed development; Those residing or working in the vicinity of the
           proposed use or development; or Property or improvements in the vicinity of the proposed use or
           development.

     4.    The use, as specifically conditioned below, does not impose disproportionate burdens on the citizens of the
           city.

CONDITIONAL USE REVIEW STANDARD:


Utah Code Ann. § 10-9a-507(2) sets forth the following standard of review for conditional uses by stating:

    (a)     (i)    A land use authority shall approve a conditional use if reasonable conditions are
                   proposed, or can be imposed, to mitigate the reasonably anticipated detrimental
                   effects of the proposed use in accordance with applicable standards.

            (ii)    The requirement described in Subsection (2)(a)(i) to reasonably mitigate anticipated
                    detrimental effects of the proposed conditional use does not require elimination of
                    the detrimental effects.
    (b)     If a land use authority proposes reasonable conditions on a proposed conditional use, the
            land use authority shall ensure that the conditions are stated on the record and reasonably
            relate to mitigating the anticipated detrimental effects of the proposed use.
    (c)     If the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of a proposed conditional use cannot be
            substantially mitigated by the proposal or the imposition of reasonable conditions to achieve
            compliance with applicable standards, the land use authority may deny the conditional use.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION:
Staff recommends approval for a Conditional Use Permit, C24-00005, to place a new EMC located at 93 W
3300 S based upon the information submitted by the applicant, the general plan considerations, and
ordinance analysis and proposes the following Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of
Approval.

Findings of Fact:

    1. On September 26, 2024, Nikki Christensen applied for a Conditional Use Permit (“CUP”) on behalf
         of Brighton Bank to build an Electronic Message Center located at 93 W 3300 S.

    2. The subject location, 93 W 3300 S, is in the TOD core Land Use District.

    3. The proposed new EMC sign will be about 20 sq ft, the new EMC will be added to the existing sign
         structure replacing the existing time and temperature display.

    4. The proposed EMC will be controlled via cell phone and software.

    5. The proposed EMC will have programmable brightness, with maximum 8000NITs daytime,
         dimmable via software by 1% increments to desired level.

    6. Will display full-color advertising content that are non-flashing and non-scintillating, with 2 plus
         second intervals.

    7. The new EMC is within 500 ft from residential area and will not operate between the hours of ten
         p.m. and six a.m. of the following day.

    8. A minimum of 5% of screen time will be devoted to public service messages.

    9. No impact is expected as part of the use on environmentally sensitive lands. There is no
         environmentally sensitive land on site.

    10. No specific hazards or adverse impacts are expected due to the proposed use.

    11. All utilities are currently on-site, and there is no proposal at this time to upgrade the existing utilities.

Conclusions of Law:

    1.    The use, as specifically conditioned below, is consistent with the South Salt Lake City General Plan.

    2.    The use, as specifically conditioned below, is compatible in use, scale, and design with allowed
          uses in the Commercial Corridor.

    3.    The use, as specifically conditioned below, does not compromise the health, safety, or welfare of:
          Persons employed within or using the proposed development; Those residing or working in the
          vicinity of the proposed use or development; or Property or improvements in the vicinity of the
          proposed use or development.

    4.    The use, as specifically conditioned below, does not impose disproportionate burdens on the
          citizens of the city.

                                                        10
Conditions of Approval:

    1. The applicant shall comply with all of the conditions of approval below and within the Conditional
       Use Permit prior to the release of a South Salt Lake building permit.

    2. The applicant shall comply with all applicable International Building Codes and International Fire
       Codes and must obtain and complete a South Salt Lake building permit.

    3. The occupancy shall never exceed the maximum allowed, as determined by the South Salt Lake
       Building Official and South Salt Lake Fire Marchal’s Office. The maximum allowed occupancy of the
       building shall be posted on the interior of the building.

    4. The applicant shall obtain a South Salt Lake City sign permit prior to any sign installation.

    5. All Electronic Message Centers must have an automatic dimmer to reduce Sign intensity after dark.

    6. Light intensity may not exceed that produced by fifty-four (54) watts of incandescent lighting for
       daytime usage. An automatic dimmer must be installed to reduce nighttime intensity to that
       produced by thirty (30) watts of incandescent lighting. Light emitting diodes, magnetic discs and
       other lighting types may be used if the light intensity is not greater than that produced by
       incandescent lighting.

    7. An Electronic Message Center may not flash or scintillate except to change the displayed wording
       to different wording.

    8. Any display on the Electronic Message Center must remain lighted for at least two seconds.

    9. An Electronic Message Center located within five hundred (500) feet of a residential area, or as
       otherwise determined by the Planning Commission, may not operate between the hours of ten p.m.
       and six a.m. of the following day.

    10. For a minimum of five percent of the time the Sign is in use, the Electronic Message Center shall be
        devoted to public service messages.

    11. The applicant shall comply with the other provisions in Title 17.

PLANNING COMMISSION STAFF REPORT
Meeting Date:         November 7, 2024
Request:              Consider the pending ordinance and forward a positive recommendation to the City
                      Council to establish uniform regulations for all auto-related uses regarding outdoor
                      storage, increasing the minimum lot size, minimum lot frontage, and minimum interior
                      display requirements for car dealerships, add a definition for Luxury Vehicle, and
                      update the Land Use Matrix.
Applicant:            South Salt Lake City
Type of item:         Legislative – Code Amendment

RECOMMENDATION:
Staff recommends the Planning Commission forward a recommendation of approval to the City Council for
the ordinance amending chapters 17.01, 17.03, 17.04, and 17.07 of the South Salt Lake City Municipal
Code (“Title 17”) that clarify the uniformity of existing regulations across all auto-related uses regarding
outdoor storage, the minimum lot size and minimum interior display requirements for car dealerships, add a
new definition for Luxury Vehicle, and update the Land Use Matrix.

SUMMARY OF CHANGES:
Since the October 17, 2024, Planning Commission meeting, the following updates have been made to the
proposed ordinance:
   • Expanded definition of Luxury Vehicle.
   • Inclusion of metal materials in fencing requirements
   • Reduced lot and site requirements for new or expanding Automotive Dealerships.
   • Commercial Repair Services to remain in the Commercial General District.
   • Addition of Automotive Restoration in the Business Park District.
   • Shielded storage for Automotive Dealerships that provide secondary services.

BACKGROUND:
On September 5, 2024, the Community and Economic Development Director issued public notice on the
State Public Notice Website of a Pending Ordinance establishing uniform regulations for all auto-related
uses, amending land use definitions, and increasing minimum lot and site requirements for car dealerships.
Planning Staff informed the Planning Commission of this intent during their Regular Meeting on September
5, 2024. On October 3, 2024, as required by State Law and City Ordinance, staff mailed public notices to
affected property owners in the following Zoning Districts:

   •   Commercial Corridor
   •   Commercial Neighborhood
   •   Commercial General
   •   TOD
   •   TOD-Core
   •   Mixed-Use
   •   Business Park
   •   Downtown and Subdistricts
   •   East Streetcar Neighborhood and Subdistricts


                                                      1
On October 17, 2024, the Planning Commission conducted a public hearing and directed staff to further
evaluate the proposed recommendations in more isolated sections, including an independent analysis of
each auto-related use, and provide a comprehensive review of the minimum lot and site requirements for
dealerships. The Planning Commission continued the public hearing to November 7, 2024.

In general, this proposed ordinance clarifies a number of existing zoning standards in a uniform manner
across all auto-related uses:
    • Outdoor Storage, including vehicle parts, scrap metals, waste, etc., is currently prohibited. The
       proposed update clarifies:
            o All vehicle parts or accessories must be stored indoors at all times.
            o All wrecked or inoperable vehicles must be stored within the site’s primary or accessory
                structure. Carports and fencing do not mitigate this requirement.
            o Only operable vehicles used by the business, customers, and employees may be parked
                outdoors. No wrecked or inoperable vehicle may be stored on-site for more than seventy-two
                (72) hours.
            o Exceptions to outdoor storage include operable and/or inventory vehicles being serviced or
                used tires in the rear yard only and shall be shielded from neighboring properties and the
                public Right-of-Way. Front Yards, Side Yards, and all required Setbacks must always be free
                from storage.
    • Parking Standards:
            o Operable and/or inventory vehicles shall be organized in a unified manner and inside
                designated, marked 9-foot by 18-foot parking stalls (no double stacking).
            o All vehicles must be parked on paved, hard surfaces and never on gravel, dirt, or other
                porous/soft surfaces.
Standards for new auto dealerships, or those expanding their business operations, must have a minimum
lot size of 1.5 acres, a minimum of 6,000 square feet of internal showroom area, and a minimum lot frontage
of 200 feet.
ANALYSIS:
       I.     GENERAL PLAN CONSIDERATIONS
The South Salt Lake General Plan 2040 was adopted in 2021 and directs the development and growth of
the city through goals and strategies that support the city’s community and economic values. The following
goals and strategies directed staff to recommend these code amendments:

   •   Community Values Goal 4 is to enhance community pride and identity. This is achieved by
       strengthening neighborhood livability through proactive and coordinated code enforcement to
       address health, safety, and welfare concerns (Strategy 9). By prioritizing the enforcement of uniform
       codes that maintain the aesthetic and functional quality of existing neighborhoods, a sense of
       ownership and pride is fostered among residents and business owners. This not only helps preserve
       the city's unique character but also encourages active participation in local initiatives, ultimately
       leading to a stronger, more connected neighborhood.
   •   Economic Goal 1, Strategy 3 encourages developing and preserving neighborhood-based shops
       and services in strategic locations. The focus on building up local businesses strengthens the
       community's economic backbone and encourages vibrant place-making.
   •   The General Plan further directs support to create planning and zoning tools that encourage quality
       neighborhood-oriented development and revitalization. Implementing uniform zoning regulations
       ensures that the right type of business is located in the locations most appropriate for that type of
       Use. This approach promotes a balanced mix of commercial uses while enhancing the overall
       quality of life for residents of South Salt Lake.

                                                     2
   •   The General Plan also guides staff to the ongoing implementation and updating of land use
       regulations based on compatibility with surrounding uses, the health of residential areas, and
       economic feasibility (Land Use & Neighborhoods Strategy 6). However, the Land Use and
       Development Code lacks uniformity in the standards for each auto-related use, which can lead to
       incompatibility with surrounding uses.
   •   Downtown South Salt Lake is a vital area for redevelopment and reinvestment. As this neighborhood
       continues to progress, the General Plan advocates for the enhancement of its health, vitality, and
       overall integrity. To realize this development objective, Strategy 5 of Economic Development Goal 1
       promotes development and revitalization while fostering the incremental development of
       underutilized parcels.
This ordinance aims to standardize regulations for Auto Body Repair, Automotive Restoration, and
Automotive Service and Repair by considering the vitality of neighborhoods and their compatibility with
surrounding uses. The proposed amendments also establish updated standards for new dealerships or
those expanding their current operations. Non-conforming (grandfathered) businesses can continue to
operate without disruption.

office, and retail west of I-15 and along major corridors, including State Street, 3300 South, and 3900 South.

         III.     NONCONFORMING DEALERSHIPS

During the Planning Commission public hearing on October 17, 2024, numerous attendees expressed
concerns that the amended regulations will render their dealerships inoperable, potentially driving them out
of the city. On average, dealerships in the city contain approximately 1.3 acres (58,286 square feet). Any
new dealership is required to meet the minimum lot and site requirements. However, active dealerships that
do not meet the minimum lot and site standards are classified as a “Nonconforming Use”. The Land Use
and Development Code defines Nonconforming Use as:

                  A Use of land that (a.) legally existed before its current land use designation; (b.) has been
                  maintained continuously since the time the Land Use Regulations governing the land
                  changes; and (c.) because of one or more subsequent Land Use Regulations changes, does
                  not conform to the regulations that now govern the Use of land.

Based on this definition, under the amended regulations, dealerships can continue operating as long as
they maintain an active business license and adhere to the state, county, and city's health, safety, and
welfare standards.

It is important to emphasize that existing dealerships are not only allowed to enhance their properties but
are also encouraged to make improvements as needed. For instance, dealership owners looking to improve
their facilities – whether through paving, minor structural upgrades, or combining adjacent properties – have
the right to collaborate with the owner of the property and the City to obtain the proper permits to implement
these site improvements. This ensures that businesses can adapt and thrive, ultimately contributing to a
vibrant and economically viable community. By fostering an environment that supports small businesses
through both operational continuity and property enhancements, the amended regulations aim to promote
the long-term success of existing dealerships within the city and not displace them.

17.04.010 Review Process and Requirements.
A.   A person seeking approval of a Permitted Use must file a Complete Application, using the forms established
     by the Community Development Department, and include payment of all fees. For any Application to
     construct a Building or other Improvement to Property for a Use that is defined by this Code as allowed in
     the zoning district in which the Building is proposed, the Community Development Department and the City
     Engineer must review the Application to determine whether the proposal:
     1.     Is allowed within the district where it is proposed;
     2.     Is proposed for Development on a legally subdivided Lot;
     3.     Can be adequately serviced by Dedicated roads, improved to City Standards and existing or proposed
            utility systems or lines;
     4.     Complies with all applicable Development requirements of that district, including Building Height,
            Setbacks, and Lot Coverage;

17.05.020 General Conditional Use Standards.
      In reviewing an Application for a Conditional Use Permit, the land use authority shall consider whether the
Application:
A.   Identifies the maximum intensity of the proposed Development and Use;
B.   Complies with all provisions of the Code; and
C.   Compared to Permitted Uses and Development within the district, substantially mitigates the adverse
     impacts that are reasonably anticipated from the magnitude and intensity of the Development and Use, as
     proposed, considering:
     1.    The size and location of the site;
     2.    Traffic generation, timing, and nature of traffic impacts and the existing condition and capacity of the
           Streets in the area;
     3.    Utility demand and available capacity, including storm water retention;
     4.    Emergency vehicle access and anticipated average and peak day demand;
     5.    Location and amount of off-Street parking;
     6.    Internal vehicular and pedestrian circulation system, including delivery vehicles, loading and unloading;
     7.    Fencing, Screening, and Landscaping to separate the Conditional Use from adjoining property and
           Uses;
     8.    Building mass, bulk, design, and orientation, and the location of Buildings on the site including
           orientation to Buildings on adjoining Lots or Parcels;
     9.    Usable open space;
     10.   Signs and lighting;
     11.   Physical design and Compatibility with surrounding structures in terms of mass, Scale, style, design,
           and architectural detailing;
     12.   Noise, vibration, odors, steam, or other factors that might adversely affect people and property off-
           site;
     13.   Control of delivery and service vehicles, loading and unloading zones;
     14.   Generation and Screening of waste;
     15.   Recycling program and pickup areas;
     16.   The potential adverse impacts arising from the conduct of patrons, guests, employees, occupants, or
           their affiliates;

     18.   Hours of operation, delivery, and activity;

17.05.050 Conditional Use Permit Standards.
     Upon review and consideration of the criteria identified in this Chapter, as compared to the impacts of

17.05.060 Conditional Use Permit Determination.
A.   The land use authority shall issue a Conditional Use Permit, describing the scope of the permit and all
     reasonable conditions of approval if the Application complies with Code and the Applicant has proposed, or
     the land use authority has required, conditions to substantially mitigate the reasonably anticipated
     detrimental effects of the proposed Use.
B.   If the land use authority determines that the Application does not comply with the standards and criteria of
     this Chapter and the Applicant has not proposed or the land use authority cannot impose additional,
     reasonable conditions of approval to that would bring the proposal within the standards and criteria of this
     Code, the land use authority may deny the Conditional Use Permit Application.

17.05.070 Modification of Conditional Use Permit.
A.   The land use authority may reasonably modify the conditions of a Conditional Use Permit if the actual
     detrimental effects of previously identified adverse impacts are greater than anticipated, or the proposed
     mitigation has been unsuccessful at mitigating those actual detrimental effects to comply with the standards
     of this Code.
B.   Modification proceedings may be initiated by the Applicant, the City, or an injured party with standing. The
     party seeking the modification must pay the costs associated with the modification proceedings and file a
     petition for modification with the City. Modification proceedings are conducted in the same manner as an
     initial review.
C.   The Conditional Use permit holder is a necessary party to these proceedings and shall be afforded due
     process.

17.05.080 Expansion or Growth of Conditional Use.
A.   A Conditional Use may not be expanded without first undergoing modification proceedings, as provided in
     Section 17.05.060, and obtaining a Conditional Use Permit that addresses and allows the proposed
     expansion of the Conditional Use.
B.   Expansion of a Conditional Use occurs if the square footage of a Structure on a property subject to a
     Conditional Use Permit or associated with a Use listed as "Conditional" in this Title's Land Use Matrix will be
     increased by greater than ten (10) percent of the square footage existing at the time of the initial Conditional
     Use Application.
C.   A Conditional Use has grown if any of the following occurs:




                                                   Page 31 of 34
     1.     The pedestrian or vehicle traffic has increased by greater than twenty (20) percent than was
            anticipated at the time of the initial Conditional Use Application, and the increased traffic is a result or
            impact of the Use;
     2.     Off-Street parking has become inadequate due to the number of customers, employees, or occupants
            associated with the Conditional Use; or
     3.     Other detrimental effects, such as noise, odor, or light pollution, have increased beyond what was
            reasonably anticipated at the time of the initial Conditional Use Application.
D.   The City may initiate modification proceedings if a Conditional Use has grown to the extent provided in this
     Section.

17.05.090 Revocation of Conditional Use Permit.
A.   A Conditional Use permit may be revoked or suspended if any of the following occur or are found to have
     occurred:
     1.     The Conditional Use Permit was obtained by fraud or misrepresentation;
     2.     One or more of the conditions of the Permit have not been met;
     3.     The holder or user of the permit has failed to comply with any local, state, or federal laws governing
            the conduct of the Conditional Use;
     4.     The holder or user of the Permit has failed to construct or maintain the site as shown on the approved
            plans; or
     5.     A Conditional Use has been expanded or grown and cannot mitigate the detrimental effects of that
            expansion or growth.
B.   Revocation is appropriate when the Applicant has knowingly engaged in conduct that violates the
     Conditional Use Permit, or when the holder or user has previously had the Permit suspended. Notice shall be
     given of a pending revocation and the Property Owner will be given a reasonable opportunity to cure the
     violation in the same manner as provided for other violations of ordinances in this Title.

17.05.100 Building Permits.
      The issuance of a Conditional Use Permit does not excuse an Applicant from applying for and obtaining
Building Permits for the location, unless Building Permits are not required for the approved Conditional Use.

17.05.110 Conditional Use Permit Expiration.
A.   Conditional Use Not Implemented. A Conditional Use Permit expires if the Permit has not been implemented
     within two (2) years from the date of approval. The Permit is considered implemented if the holder of the
     Permit engages in the Conditional Use on the site or maintains an active building permit (excluding
     demolition permits) on the site for which the Conditional Use Permit was granted.
B.   Conditional Use Abandoned. If the approved Conditional Use or activity ceases for any reason or does not
     maintain an active building permit for a continuous period of two (2) years or more, the Conditional Use
     Permit shall automatically terminate without further notice, as having been abandoned. A person may only
     reinstate the Conditional Use after applying for and receiving a new Conditional Use Permit.
