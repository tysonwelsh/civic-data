REGULAR MEETING

Chair Edwards called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

      PUBLIC HEARINGS


1.    Dean Cardinale requests a zoning code text amendment to Table 17-7-12.10 of the
      Regional Commercial (RC) Zone to increase the allowed percentage of changeable copy
      signs.

      The application proposes a “request to modify the monument sign regulations to allow up to
      85% changeable copy” see Attachment 2. Exhibits B-G shown in Attachment 2 along with
      Union Park Avenue and in the surrounding area, identify signs that are not within Midvale
      City’s limits (Cottonwood Heights for the billboard-Exhibit B, and Sandy for the remaining
      signs-Exhibits C-G) and thus not applicable as they are regulated by different jurisdictions.

      The current language in Table 17-7-12.10 permits 25% of a sign’s area for wall or monument
      sign types as changeable copy, which is reasonable to keep signs from being a nuisance or
      distraction for vehicular traffic.

      Public Notice has been sent to affected entities as required in 17-3-9(B) of the Midvale
      Municipal Code. No comments have been received as of the writing of this report.

-ZONING MAP AMENDMENT CRITERIA-

Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) outlines the criteria necessary for the amendments to the zoning
code.

F. Zoning Code Text Amendment Criteria. To establish and maintain a sound, stable, and
desirable city, a zoning code amendment application may only be approved if the reviewing
body determines, in written findings, that the proposed amendment demonstrates on or more of
the following:

       1. The proposed amendment promotes the objectives of the general plan and purposes of
          this title;
       2. The proposed amendment promotes the purposes outlines in Utah State Code 10-9a-
          102;
       3. The proposed amendment more clearly explains the intent of the original language or
          has been amended to make interpretation more straightforward; or
       4. Existing zoning code was the result of a clerical error or mistake of fact.

Staff finds that this proposal does not meet the required criteria, see the responses below for
each related above-criteria.

       1. The current sign standards permit reasonable signage of various types to be
          constructed to facilitate commercial business activity and already promote the
          objectives of the general plan.
       2. The current sign standards permit reasonable signage of various types to be
          constructed to facilitate commercial business activity and already promote the
          purposes of UCA 10-20-101 (renumbered from 10-9a-102).
       3. The existing language of “25%” is already clear and does not need to be amended to
          be interpreted more straightforward.
       4. The existing language of “25%” is not a clerical error or a mistake of fact.


                                             ***


STAFF RECOMMENDATION:

Staff recommends the Planning Commission recommend denial of the zoning code text
amendment with the following finding:

Findings:

   1. The amendment does not comply with Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F).

APPLICANT:

Dean Cardinale, owner of Worldwide Trekking, stated that the current 25% sign limitation does
not reflect existing signage within the area, including signs located within Midvale City limits and
along the Union Park corridor in both Midvale and Sandy. Mr. Cardinale explained that the request
is intended to address inconsistencies between the current regulations and existing conditions,
noting that several areas within Midvale do not comply with current sign standards. He stated that
the proposed amendment meets the City’s criteria for text amendments by promoting consistency,
supporting business activity, and aligning with existing development patterns.

DISCUSSION:

Daniel Van Buege, Deputy City Attorney, informed the commissioners that this is a legislative
decision and that the Planning Commission will provide a recommendation to City Council for
final approval and/or denial.

Commissioner Anderson requested clarification regarding whether the sign in question conformed
to current sign regulations.

Dean Cardinale stated that when the sign was installed, the plans submitted with the building
permit application matched the sign currently installed outside his business and met all setback
requirements for the zone. He explained that the only revision requested during permit review was
to update the plans to include a professional engineering stamp.

Commissioner Anderson requested clarification regarding the building permit requirements for the
sign and whether a permit had been obtained prior to installation.

Jonathan Anderson, Planner II, and Wendelin Knobloch, Planning Director, stated that plans were
reviewed and a building permit application was received and approved; however, the electrical
components of the sign were not included in the original permit submittal.

Mr. Cardinale stated he is willing to modify the sign and work with Midvale City due to his
substantial investment. He further stated that, based on receiving approved plan sets bearing a
Midvale City stamp and an active building permit, he operated under the assumption the installed
sign, including electrical components, had been reviewed and was compliant with applicable
regulations. He noted that after concerns were raised, the sign was turned off after approximately
four months of operation.

Chair Edwards stated that the Planning Commission must evaluate the proposed text amendment
in relation to the current zoning regulations, which were drafted in compliance with the General
Plan. Chair Edwards added that the Commission must also consider the long-term vision for the
RC Zone and whether that vision supports signs with 85% illumination or changeable copy.

Mr. Cardinale stated he was unable to identify where the Historic Midvale Zoning Code allows
the sign located outside Midvale City Hall to operate at 85% illumination and requested
clarification regarding how that sign had previously been allowed while his sign is considered
nonconforming.

Commissioner Brauchie stated that the primary distinction between the signs is that the sign at
Midvale City Hall serves a public information purpose, while the sign on Mr. Cardinale’s property
is used for private advertising.

Chair Edwards and Vice Chair Liedtke further noted that Midvale City Hall and Mr. Cardinale’s
property are located in different zoning districts and are therefore subject to different code
requirements.

Commissioner Anderson asked whether the building permit would have been approved if the
electrical components had been included in the original plans.

Wendelin Knobloch stated that if the electrical components had been included, the sign would
have been identified as noncompliant under current sign regulations during the permit review
process, and approval would not have been granted as submitted.

Commissioner Erickson stated that questions regarding municipal boundaries or other potentially
noncompliant signs within Midvale do not alter the requirements applicable to the sign at issue.
Commissioner Erickson added that the existence of other nonconforming signs would not justify
amending current sign regulations and noted that if additional noncompliant signs exist, separate
enforcement actions may be necessary.

Commissioner Erickson further stated that other mitigating approaches, such as illumination
limitations or hours of operation, may be options for future consideration; however, increasing
allowable illumination from 25% to 85% does not appear to be the most feasible option at this
time.
