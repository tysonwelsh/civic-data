SV-1-2026
2300 South Vacation
2300 South 5700 West

BACKGROUND:
West Valley City is requesting consideration to vacate a portion of 2300 South which is located
east of the Mountain View Corridor and west of Lester Street (5700 West). 2300 South,
originally named Second Street, was platted as part of the Hayne’s Addition Plat E Subdivision
and was approved in May 1889.

The section of right-of-way in question, approximately 14,155 square feet in area, currently
dead ends into the Mountain View Corridor. The Mountain View Corridor opened for traffic in
this section in June of 2021. Prior to that, UDOT purchased many parcels west of Lester Street
to construct the freeway. The area that encompasses the Hayne’s Addition Plat E Subdivision
has historically been occupied by salvage yard businesses. As the salvage yards have
become fewer, a result of the Mountain View Corridor, and with the subsequent adoption of the
Light Industrial Zone in 2016, land uses are changing in this area.
Wadsworth Development Group recently petitioned the City and rezoned a large area of
property on the north and south sides of 2300 South, west of 5700 West, for a new building
and storage yard development (Z-8-2025). They purchased approximately 5 acres of property
from UDOT and are in the process of acquiring a 7,000 square foot parcel from the City on the
north side of 2300 South, which will all be a part of the project area. They intend to also include
the vacated right-of-way into their project.

When right-of-way is vacated, typically half of the vacated portion goes to the adjacent
property owners on either side of the street. In this instance, 33.75 feet of 2300 South was
already vacated by the City in 2005 (Ord. 05-38). That year, the Economic Development
Department requested the vacation of the southern portion of 2300 South in conjunction with a
special improvement district that was created. Since the property owners on the south side of
2300 were given the vacated right-of-way with that ordinance, the vacation of the remaining
right-of-way as outlined in this application will go to the property owner on the north side.

According to City Ordinance, streets and/or alley vacations shall be reviewed by the Planning
Commission with a recommendation to the City Council. Upon approval of the street vacation,
Wadsworth Development Group will follow up with a subdivision application and a conditional
use application.

RECOMMENDATION

       1. Approval of the street vacation subject to the staff report.
       2. Continue the application due to issues raised at the public hearing.

BACKGROUND:
The Planning Commission last approved a conditional use permit for this project on April 9,
2025. Since conditional use permits automatically terminate within 12 months of Planning
Commission approval when a building permit has not been obtained and construction of at
least the foundation has not been completed, the applicant has reapplied for a new conditional
use permit. Due to market conditions, they are uncertain exactly when they will submit for
permits and begin construction on the project but would like to keep their conditional use
approval current.

Castlewood Development’s application is essentially identical to the project that was approved
previously. They are requesting conditional use approval to redevelop 3.02 acres at 2940 West
3650 South into a 330-unit, 2 to 7 story, Class A apartment development. The proposed unit
mix for the project is 42 studio units, 201 one-bedroom units, and 87 two-bedroom units. The
majority of the property (2.94 acres) is zoned CC (City Center) and the balance (0.08 acres) is
zoned R-2-8 (Two Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet). The entire
building is within the CC zone. The property is designated as very high density residential
(over 20 units/acre) in the Fairbourne Station Vision, which is part of the City’s General Plan.
The property is currently vacant as the office development previously on the property was
demolished in 2014.

The Public Works Department recently completed the 3650 South reconstruction project. They
used the subject property as a staging area during construction. The street frontage now has
new 5’ sidewalk, 5’ stamped concrete park strip with trees, new curb & gutter and a striped
bike lane. No on-street parking is allowed.

Project Review
The first section below outlines the purposes in the CC zone and how this development
accomplishes these purposes. The second section addresses the main ordinance standards (it
is not a comprehensive list) applicable to this development.

City Center Zone Purposes

description of how the apartment development meets the stated purpose:

•   Create a recognizable center or downtown for West Valley City. At 7 stories, this
    apartment development will add to the unique, urban environment in Fairbourne Station.
•   Encourage and direct development that supports transit. High density residential
    development is one of the best land uses for supporting transit.

Parking
The parking ordinance allows the Planning Commission to reduce the parking standard for
multi-family residential from 2 spaces per unit to 1.3 spaces per unit for studio units, 1.5
spaces per unit for one-bedroom units, and 1.75 spaces per unit for two-bedroom units if the
Commission finds that the reduction is not detrimental to the surrounding area and will
enhance the proposed development. The CC Zone also allows a 10% reduction to parking
requirements. The applicant has submitted the attached parking study in the form of a memo
from Hales Engineering to support the proposed amount of parking.

Amenities
For a project of this size, the ordinance requires a minimum of five amenities from the list in
the ordinance with at least three of the five being major amenities. This project has six major
amenities (outdoor swimming pool, courtyards, clubhouse/community room, rooftop patio,
fitness room, and community game room/recreation room) and seven minor amenities
(outdoor fire pit/fireplace, hot tub, building lobby, package locker system, indoor bicycle
storage, electric vehicle charging stations, and outdoor covered pavilion with tables and a
grill).

Unit Sizes
The ordinance requires a minimum of 400 square feet for studio units, 600 square feet for 1-
bedroom units, and 850 square feet for 2-bedroom units. The proposed minimum unit sizes
are 520 square feet for studio units, 604 square feet for 1-bedroom units, and 930 square feet
for 2-bedroom units. Given the mix of unit layouts, many units will exceed these proposed
minimums.

Landscaping and Recreation Space
The ordinance requires a minimum of 10% of the site area as landscaping and a minimum
of 40% of the site area as landscaping and recreation space. The landscaping percentage
for this project is 15.0% and the combined landscaping and recreation space percentage is
54.8%.

Architecture
After reviewing the elevations and renderings, staff noted to the applicant that the parapet
along the south elevation needs more variation. The applicant has acknowledged this
deficiency and will have the architect make the correction. Once this comment is addressed,
the elevations and renderings will meet or exceed the City’s design standards.

General Commercial. The surrounding zone is MXD (Mixed Use) to the east, C-2 (General
Commercial) to the north and west, and RMH (Residential Mobile Home) to the south. The
surrounding uses include the Village at Rivers Edge residential to the east, the UP&L power
corridor to the west, a mobile home park to the south and vacant land to the north. This

This site was historically occupied by a business called Hubcap Heaven. They were in
