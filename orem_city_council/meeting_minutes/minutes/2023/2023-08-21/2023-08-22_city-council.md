Economic State of the City: Presenter: Kathi Lewis, Economic Development Division Manager

Ms. Lewis is presenting on the Economic Development of the City of Orem. Economic
Development promotes the economic well being and quality of life for communities, by creating,
retaining and expanding jobs that facilitate growth, enhance wealth and provide a stable tax base.
Ms. Lewis spoke of the recently completed projects, such as the Orem Tech Center, the Call
Commercial Center, La-Z-Boy Furniture, Petco, Big Blue Swim School, and 2nd & Charles.

Ms. Lewis went over the Economic Development Data. In Commercial Building
Permits, commercial addition or remodel permits had a high in 2015 at 262 and the low was in
2022 at 149. The commercial / industrial building permits reached a high in 2018 at 22 and a low
in 2021 at 7. In Residential Building Permits, single family permits had a high in 2016 at 108
and the low in 2022 at 47. Condos or townhomes permits were high in 2018 at 239 and low in
2019 at 36. Mixed use - multifamily combined high in 2014 at 843 and the low at 2020 with 8.
Commercial business licenses had a low of 2,505 in 2013 and it is now closer to 3,000 licenses in
2022. The number of employees reported has consistently stayed around 30,000 since 2014.

The Utah Department of workforce did a comparison of Provo, Orem, Lehi and
American Fork. The results for employee count are: 3,442 Provo, 3,756 Orem, 2,380 Lehi and
1,479 American Fork. Employment numbers results are 71,149 Provo, 53,295 Orem, 43,392
Lehi and 26,054 American Fork. Monthly wage results are Lehi is over $6,000, Provo is over
$4,000. Orem is just under $4,000 and American Fork is over $3,500. Ms. Lewis explained that
although Orem has the largest average number of firms of the four cities, Provo has the largest
average employment and Lehi has the highest average monthly wage. The high tech industry in
Lehi drives up the average wage while the hospital and medical industry drives up the
employment numbers in Provo.

Ms. Lewis moved on to Office and Retail Vacancy Rates. Central Utah County consists
of Orem and Provo. Office buildings have 3 classes with Class A as the best, Class B is Better
and Class C is good. Central Utah County has had low vacancy rates in Class A offices. Class B
vacancy rates have been between 15 and 20%. Class C vacancy rates have increased since 2019
in Central Utah County.

The City of Orem New Property Tax Increase was under $100,000 for 2018-2020. In
2021 it jumped to over $300,000 and then went down to $200,000 in 2022. The General Fund
Sales Tax has been increasing since FY 2018 with the highest being over $30,000,000 in FY
2022.

Ms. Lewis continued on to talk about the Orem Business Alliance (OBA). It was created
in March of 2020. The aim of the OBA is to provide monthly business education, opportunities

Freedom Plaza Concept: Presenter: Ryan Clark, Assistant City Manager and Rob Beishline,
Method Studios

Mayor Young read a proclamation that will establish September as Constitution Month in

Passing of City Manager Daryl Berlin:
Ms. Lauret spoke about the passing of Former City Manager Daryl Berlin. Mr. Berlin

served as the Orem City Manager for 14 years (1980-1994) and stepped down after a serious
heart attack. He was named City Manager of the Year twice and served under Mayors Jim
Mangum, DeLance Squire, Blaine Willis, Joyce Johnson, and Stella Welsh. He was instrumental
in making sure women received equal pay; established master plans for sewer, water, streets,
parks, and land use; and developed an employee retirement system. Mr. Berlin made the deal to
purchase SCERA Park so it would remain a park in perpetuity and not become commercially
developed. He helped create WordPerfect Business Park, CEDO, Orem Redevelopment Agency,
and many other projects. He accomplished great things for Orem and left a wonderful legacy.

Public Hearing - Amending Article 22-5-3(A) and the zoning map of the City of Orem by
rezoning a portion of the property that fronts Geneva road from the OS5 Zone to the PO
zone (approximately 4.83 acres) and the remaining property from the OS5 zone to the PRD
zone (approximately 4.79 acres) and amending Appendix KK of the Orem City code for the
property located generally at 1875 South Geneva Road_ Presenter: Jason Bench, Assistant
Development Services Director

Mr. Bench explained that this property is where the Autistic Academy resides. These
homes could be purchased by the families of the students that attend the facility. They want to
convert the property into housing lots. The homes will be single family homes. Ms. Lauret
asked if the homes would be owned by the school. Mr. Washburn, who is representing the
Autistic School, gave a brief update of the school and what it does. He reported that there is a
demand for family housing for Autistic adults and their families. The original plan was for
townhomes but the plan was changed to single family homes to address concerns from
neighbors. Mr. Washburn confirmed these homes will be privately owned.

Ms. Trish Nelson who is the executive director and president of the Utah Autism
Academy. She started this organization 10 years ago. She wants to create a community that is
autism friendly. She explained some of the modifications that may be found in homes for
autistic adults and families.

Mr. Bench showed the four house facades. These homes will mostly not have basements
and they will not be allowed to have accessory apartments. The counsel asked if the PRD is the
right zone choice. There was no recommendation from the planning commission. Mr. Washburn
explained they are trying to create a community that will be friendly to their needs. Ms. Nelson
explained that this is a new concept and that they will sell the homes and the proceeds will go
back to the school. The HOA will be set up to be autistic friendly. This would be 23 homes.

homes larger.
