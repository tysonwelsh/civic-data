PUBLIC HEARING:

PC 24-034 Old Station Remodel & Addition [Design Review] Justin Campbell/Old Station Partners,
LLC, authorized agent/owner is requesting a design review permit to remodel the exterior of the
former Logan City Fire Department Station 70, adding a 2nd level over the truck bays and a stair tower
to the southwest corner of the building located at 76 East 200 North in the Town Center-One (TC-1)
zone; TIN 06-115-0002 (Adams Neighborhood).

Staff: Aimee Egbert, City Planner, reviewed the request for a design review permit to remodel the
exterior of the former Logan City Fire Station, add a 6,020 square foot second story above the truck
bays, a 720 square foot stair tower to the southwest corner, where the old hose drying room was
located, add pergolas, outdoor plaza space, and an outdoor seating area along the north side of the
building. The proposed use of the building will be for restaurant and office space. The project meets
all Land Development Code (LDC) requirements for lot coverage, building frontage, and setbacks in
the TC-1 zone. The rear and east parking setbacks can be waived due to the shared public parking
lot however, the southwest corner will have to meet the required 5-foot parking setback. The
proposed rear entrance to the main level of the building needs a walkway connection to the sidewalk.
The door on the southwest side of the building will need an awning above the door and a walkway
connection to the sidewalk.

A. Egbert reviewed the building design and said that the existing building does not meet the current
building design standards, therefore any upgrades to the existing facades would bring the building
closer into compliance with current articulation and fenestration standards in the LDC. As proposed
the new second-story addition meets the design standards in the LDC, except for the west side
fenestration, which is at approximately 24% of the 30% fenestration requirement. Presently, there is
a row of mature trees in the park strip along the west side of the building that the proponent is planning
on keeping. These trees screen most of the west façade and can be considered as an option for
meeting the remainder of the 30% fenestration requirement.


Proponent: Justin Campbell, the project architect, said they are willing to add additional pedestrian
connections to the south and west entrances. Mr. Campbell also stated that they are planning to keep
the trees along the west of the building and asked the commission to count the trees towards the
fenestration requirement.

Public: There were no public comments.

Commission: Commissioner Lucero said she is fine with the existing trees on the west side making
up for the additional fenestration needed, however, would like a condition added stating that any
trees removed along the west side of the building during construction will have to be replaced.

Motion: Commissioner Heare made a motion to conditionally approve a design review permit for
PC 24-034 Old Station Remodel & Addition with the amended conditions of approval and findings
for approval as listed below. Commissioner Lewis seconded the motion.

PC 24-035 BRMH Receiving Center [Design Review & Conditional Use Permit] Justin
Campbell/Bear River Mental Health is requesting a design review and conditional use permit for a
new 12,300 SF Receiving Center and a 15,450 SF, 20-unit temporary housing building to support
the operations of the Receiving Center and Bear River Mental Health located at the corner of 200
West and 1980 North in the Public (PUB) zone; TIN 04-080-0044 (Bridger Neighborhood).

Staff: Russ Holley, City Planner, reviewed the request for a Design Review and Conditional Use
Permit for a new 12,300 square foot, two-story, Bear River Mental Health (BRMH) Receiving Center
and a proposed two-story, 20-unit housing complex located on a 2.09-acre property at the northeast
corner of the 1950 North 200 West intersection, in the Public (PUB) zone. The Land Development
Code (LDC) conditionally permits Mental Health Receiving Centers in the PUB zone. Receiving
Centers, as defined by the LDC, do not allow residential land use and states that care received at
the center is capped at 23 hours. The LDC does not permit housing in the PUB zone unless state or
federal laws supersede local zoning and the proposed housing is state-recognized as a “Residential
Treatment Program where treatment is provided on-site”. If the applicant receives proper state
authorization the project would meet the requirements in the LDC.

The LDC requires parking lots to be located to the side and rear of buildings in the PUB zone. As
proposed with the parking lot located between the building and the street, the site layout does not
comply with the LDC. As conditioned in number 5, with the south parking lot moved to the
west/east/north of the building, the project would comply with the requirements in the LDC.

The LDC requires four-sided architecture when a building does not orient to the adjacent street so
that facades are inviting and add character to the streetscape. The south, street-facing façade is
proposed with minimal transparency and does not meet the four-sided architecture requirement. As
conditioned in number 4, with either rotating the building to orient to the street or making the south
façade match the east/west façade, the project would meet the building design standards in the LDC.

With the proposed use, the required parking would be approximately 40 parking stalls. As proposed
the project has 90 parking stalls for both buildings, possibly causing some over-parking issues.

Commissioner Lucero asked about greenspace requirements for the housing units. R. Holley said
that the project will have approximately 34% landscaped area, which is over the minimum
requirement in the PUB zone.

Commissioner Peterson asked if housing is allowed in the public zone. R, Holley said only if the
proposed housing meets the state definition of a residential treatment center where care and
treatment are provided on-site. Commissioner Lucero said it is common to have a receiving center
located in a campus environment with associated housing on site.

Proponent: Beth Smith, CEO of Bear River Mental Health, said that the receiving center will be fully
funded with State dollars. The housing will be for mental health patients who receive services from
BRMH. There will be treatment provided within the Receiving Center building, but not in the housing
units.

Justin Campbell, the Architect on the project, spoke about the housing proximity to the Receiving
Center being very beneficial to the individuals living there who receive care. The Receiving Center
will be the licensed facility, the housing will not be licensed as a treatment facility. Mr. Campbell
spoke about the proposed orientation of the building, having the large windows face the east to


provide calming mountain views that will enhance the care within. It also allows for more privacy
along the south street facing façade, and north side, police drop off.

Tim Frost, Clinical Director at Bear River Mental Health, said they are trying to establish housing for
their mental health patients who need a supportive environment where the services they need are
located on the same property, where they can recover and integrate back into society.

Commissioner McNamara asked Mr. Frost to clarify that the Receiving Center will be where the
treatment will take place and the housing is just for living. And asked if the Receiving Center would
be staffed 24 hours a day and if there will be any live-in staff in the apartments.

Mr. Frost said that Commissioner McNamara is correct, the Receiving Center is where the treatment
will take place, not in the housing. There will not be any staff living in the apartments. The Receiving
Center will be staffed 24 hours a day with licensed therapists to provide treatment. The Mobile Crisis
Outreach Team (MCOT), located on the second floor of the Receiving Center will also be available
24 hours a day to help anyone who needs it.

Guth asked if the housing is limited to the individuals who are receiving treatment with BRMH. Tim
Frost said it is considered on a case-by-case basis. Some individuals may lose their funding for
treatment, but they are not moved out of BRMH housing until the individual can find alternate housing.

Beth Smith said that BRMH provides housing because it helps them provide better care. They are
not mandated to provide housing.

Public: Michael Jewell read the definition of a Residential Treatment Facility in the State Code “a
24-hour group living environment for four or more individuals unrelated to the owner or provider that
offers room or board and specialized treatment, behavior modification, rehabilitation, discipline,
emotional growth, or habilitation services for persons with emotional, psychological, developmental,
or behavioral dysfunctions, impairments, or chemical dependencies”, and stated that the proposed
project meets the State definition of a treatment facility.

Jeannie Simmonds asked if BRMH would own the housing units.

Brent Dahle, who owns property to the south, asked if there are any safety concerns for the residents
who live close to the Receiving Center regarding the individuals being dropped off at the Receiving
Center by the police.

Raylene Gnehm, who lives to the north of the project, brought up a concern for the safety of the
children who live in her neighborhood.

Adam Boman who works at BRMH and will be supervising the facility said that he understands the
safety concerns and said that individuals who are not considered safe for a public setting are not
brought to the Receiving Center, but instead are taken to either the hospital or jail. If there are any
issues with individuals at the Receiving Center they work to quickly get the issue resolved and the
individual removed.

Commission: Commissioner Peterson said that where there is no treatment being provided in the
residential space it would not apply to the state definition of a treatment facility. Commissioner
Peterson suggested that maybe the appropriate path would be to downzone the housing portion of
the site from Public to NR-6.


Commissioner Lucero said that the State definition does not require treatment to take place in the
apartment unit. There is room for interpretation of the definition regarding a site versus a building
itself, especially if viewing this as a campus approach rather than individual, unrelated buildings.
Commissioner Lucero also stated that in her involvement with the local homeless Coalition, every
month there are at least 50 households on a coordinate entry waiting list with at least 60% of those
households able to qualify based on some cognitive disability or need. Commissioner Lucero believes
that stepping back through a rezone process is wasted time and the proposal is consistent with State
requirements for supportive housing, especially in a campus based approach.

Commissioner Heare stated that BRHM has said they have no plans to get the housing established
through the State as a treatment facility. Commissioner Heare stated that he doesn’t believe the
City’s code doesn’t allow for the housing.

Commissioner Lewis said that if the buildings were connected then they would have a Receiving
Center, treatment, and housing in one building. Commissioner Heare said this proposal feels like it
is two separate things, a Receiving Center and housing.

The Commission discussed the proposed housing units and whether they can be considered directly
related to the Receiving Center or not, or whether they were just housing. The Commission stated
that it appeared that the applicant was giving them inconsistent information about the purpose or
intent of the housing.

Mohammed Abdullahi, City Attorney, re-read the definition of “Residential Treatment Facility” in Utah
Code 26B-2-101(41)(A) and said that if it is not a 24-hour group living environment, then it does not
meet the State’s definition of a residential treatment facility.

Commissioner Lucero asked what a 24-hour group living environment is.

Commissioner Heare asked if a group living environment would have staff on site and all residents
who live there attending treatment.

Commissioner Lucero asked the proponent to provide additional information.

Tim Frost with BRMH, explained their application process for housing and said that individuals who
live in their apartments may or may not receive treatment from BRMH. The purpose of this housing
component is to provide a housing environment that stabilizes an individual to a point where they
move forward with their life and function in society. Tenants may or may not receive treatment here
but may receive treatment elsewhere. BRMH views this as temporary housing where individuals can
gain some level of stability and support in helping them move forward with their care and eventual
integration into society and traditional housing.

Mike DeSimone said that the public zone allows for homeless resource centers and supportive
housing, which is similar to what BRMH is proposing. When the LDC was amended to allow for a
Receiving Center in the public zone, it was not anticipated that supportive housing would be part of
the Receiving Center, however, the current definition of a Receiving Center can be amended to
include Supportive Housing.

Tim Frost, BRMH, said that the individuals staying in the BRMH apartments are housing burdened
in one way or another, whether it is due to mental conditions, economic hardships, changes in marital
status, or transition from the State’s hospital or group living arrangements. Individuals who are
already living in the BRMH group home or other transitional housing may qualify to live in one of the


BRMH apartment units until they are ready to integrate into traditional housing. Mr. Frost explained
that this facility is not being considered as a Homeless Resource Center.

Commissioner Heare stated that he does not doubt there is a need, but stated that the Planning
Commission can not approve something that is not permitted.

M. Abdullahi asked if the proposed uses are permitted in the public zone and read the definition of a
Homeless Resource Center from the LDC. “Homeless Resource Center means an establishment in
which co-located supportive services such as sleeping, bath, eating, laundry facilities, and house
case management is provided on an emergency basis for individuals experiencing homelessness.
Additional services may include preparation and distribution of food; medical care and treatment;
behavioral and mental health counseling; education instruction; and vocational training”.

M. DeSimone said there is a nexus that can be made between a Homeless Resource Center, which
is allowed in the Public zone, and the range of services, including supportive housing, proposed by
the Receiving Center.

Commissioner Lucero said that if it is as simple as adding “Supportive Housing” to the LDC as a
permitted use associated with a Receiving Center, then this project can be conditionally approved
pending a code amendment approval.

Trevor Cook, BRMH, asked if it would be a simpler process to request a rezone for the portion of
property where the housing will be because they want to avoid having to meet the State requirements
of a Treatment Facility.

R. Holley said to rezone a portion of the property, the property would first have to be subdivided.
Once subdivided the housing would be on a separate property and not considered on-site with the
Receiving Center. There was discussion about rezoning and subdividing the site.

There was discussion about process, and whether the housing element should be pulled from the
Conditional Use Permit application, and then reviewed under a separate process, whether it was
through a rezone or code amendment process.

Tim Frost, BRMH, said that the Receiving Center is for individuals who live in the community who
are having a mental health crisis and need immediate assistance other than going to the emergency
room. Mr. Frost discussed the general State licensing requirements and explained that they are not
trying to build housing that fits into a specific state license, but rather to provide broader, but
supportive, housing for individuals needing it.

Commissioner Guth asked about the ownership and landscaping questions brought up during public
comment. R. Holley said that both buildings are currently on one property that is owned by one
owner, BRMH. The site is required to have 30% landscaping, trees and shrubs are not specified.

Commissioner Peterson spoke about the orientation of the building and asked if rotating the site plan
clockwise and putting the apartments on the north side of the property would solve some of the
fenestration and 4-sided architecture issues.

Commissioner Lucero said that having the drop-off areas on the east and north sides allows for more
privacy and screening of the individuals in crisis who are being dropped off. Having the drop-off right
in the front of the building would not serve the larger purpose or in a trauma-informed way.


Commissioner Heare said he was fine with the building orientation as proposed because it is set
back from the street and there is room to add additional landscaping along 1950 North.

Commissioner Peterson asked if the second row of parking on the south side of the building could
be removed.

R. Holley explained that the number of housing units would require approximately 20 parking stalls,
leaving 70 parking stalls for the Receiving Center. BRMH needs to provide an alternate parking plan
that shows how many employees and anticipated clients they will have in order to determine the
number of parking stalls needed. However, the proposed 90 parking stalls seems higher than what
they will need.

The Commission discussed how to proceed with the motion on this project and determined that a
conditional approval pending the approval of a code amendment adding supportive housing to the
definition of a Receiving Center, striking condition numbers 4 and 5, and adding additional landscape
screening along 1950 North to condition 8c.
