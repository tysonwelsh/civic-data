WORKSHOP ITEMS: Mike DeSimone reviewed the purpose of the workshop is to prepare for the
public hearing on August 12, 2021 where the Plannign Commission will make a recommendation
to the City Council on adopting local standards on ADU’s (Accessory Dwelling Units). An
accessory dwelling unit is a small second dwelling unit on the same grounds as a single-family
home. In 2021 Utah State Legislature adopted House Bill (HB) 82 that mandated Internal
Accessory Dwelling Units Effective October 1, 2021. This means that ADU’s must be within the
footprint of the primary dwelling, not in an accessory or separate building. The home must be
owner-occupied and the ADU cannot be used as a short-term rental. A municipality may prohibit a
second utility meter; prohibit any change of appearance of the primary dwelling for the ADU;
require 1 additional parking space; require parking replacement if a garage is converted to ADU;
prohibit ADU’s in mobile homes and homes on failing septic; require a permit or license to establish
an ADU; require a minimum lot size of 6,000 square feet; prohibit the use of ADU for rentals less
than 30 days (short term rentals); and prohibit the use of an ADU in a non-owner occupied single-
family residential dwelling. ADU’s must be allowed on at least 33% of land area that is zoned for
residential.

M. DeSimone presented six maps that tentatively identified six varied approaches for locating
ADU’s. The benefits of an ADU include providing people flexibility or options in where or how they
live during their life cycle, e.g., rent out ADU, parents or adult children live in ADU, use it as extra


living space, etc., making ownership affordable, urban renewal, property rights, better use of
existing infrastructure, or providing social assistance at the neighborhood level. We also discussed
the negatives of ADU’s, namely potential impacts of additional occupancy and parking problems in
existing residential neighborhoods. The commission willneed to determine the strategy on ADU
applicability in their recommendation to City Council.

Commissioner Goodlander asked the ratio of owner-occupied dwellings to rental units in Logan,
and if the ADU’s would change occupancy laws. M. DeSimone said it is close to 41% owner, 59%
rental, while each neighborhood is slightly different, with Hillcrest and Cliffside around 90% owner-
occupied. ADU’s will not change occupancy, it will remain 3 individuals or 1 family per legal unit.

Commissioner Goodlander said that if the owner is required to occupy the home, renting out an
accessory unity would be less offensive than renting out the entire home.

Commissioner Heare asked if there would be fines imposed and to what extent if the home was not
owner-occupied. M. DeSimone said it would follow standard code enforcement procedures.

R. Holley said that there is flexibility in the maps, the commissioners can change the areas and
take what they like from each option.

PUBLIC: Paul Davis, BRAG/Homeless Coordination Council, said he is very familiar with the
housing need in Logan. People are not able to find affordable housing, and even with the available
housing assistance, costs are well above the fair market rate. He would like to see housing that is
not primarily for students, students will figure out housing. This bill is to create affordable housing
for people in the market.

Commissioner Heare asked what the average rent for a two-bedroom is in logan. P. Davis said
$850 - $900 for a 1 bedroom and $1100 or more for a two-bedroom.

Jack Nixon talked about 45 years ago when several homes were opened to create living spaces for
college students. When the city started to clean up the parking issue, several of these living
spaces were shut down, this took out many inexpensive apartments and left the homeowners
without that extra income they were depending on. If these spaces could be opened back up and
legally rented for an affordable amount, this serious need for affordable housing could be fixed.

Riley Duke spoke about being pro ADU and neighborhood preservation. He is in support of
allowing ADU’s city-wide. If ADU’s are in an owner-occupied residence, the property is more likely
to be well maintained. This is a great opportunity to create affordable housing.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Goodlander spoke about looking at the neighborhood plans, what
has been approved and what the goals are. Finding a happy medium to create affordable housing
and keep the neighborhoods family-oriented.

Commissioner Croshaw said that he thinks the biggest issue will be with the parking. He
mentioned the homes that had rental units were taken off the market most likely due to code
violations and how there would have to be updating done to bring the homes up to code to be
rented.

Mike said that per state standards we are not allowed to require more than one parking spot. He
also said that the people that created these second units in their homes did have the option to get
a building permit and do it legally but choose not to.


Commissioner Dickinson said that ADU’s should be city-wide to allow more housing options for
families that are low income. She asked why the restriction on a second utility meter. M.
Desimone said it’s a way to make sure that the home stays as a single-family home.

There was further discussion about the ADU’s being allowed city-wide and being more stringent on
the requirements such as lot size, home being owner-occupied, only in single-family homes, no
second utility meter, and strict licensing requirements.

Paul Davis spoke about House Bill 82 and how there have been resources put in place to help
people create an ADU.

Jack Nixon spoke about the parking issue and the winter parking restriction creating more of an
issue than it does help. There is a lot of streets to park on and not a lot of snow in the winter M.
DeSimone said that the city is not obligated to provide parking for businesses. Commissioner
Dickinson said that this can be addressed in the future if it does become a bigger issue with more
housing opening up.


Meeting Adjourned at 6:43 PM
