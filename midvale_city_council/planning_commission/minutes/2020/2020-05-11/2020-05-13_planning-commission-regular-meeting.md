REZONE
Rezones are legislative decisions required whenever a desire to change the designation on the
City’s Zoning Map is requested. Rezones are heard in a public hearing by the Planning
Commission who make a recommendation to the City Council to consider. The Council will then
hear the request in a second public hearing and make a decision. The Zoning Ordinance includes
requirements for findings and process necessary to consider and adopt a rezone. The General Plan
provides direction on the City’s future land use goals and should be considered whenever a rezone
request is made.
ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS:
Section 17-3-1 of the Midvale City Municipal Code requires the Planning Commission hold a
public hearing and adopt a written recommendation to the City Council, advising the City Council
to approve, disapprove, or modify a request on all zoning map amendments. The Planning
Commission may only recommend approval of a rezone application if it determines, in written
findings, that the proposed rezoning is consistent with the policies and goals of the general plan
and that the applicant has demonstrated the following:

lL, The proposed rezoning is necessary either to comply with the general plan
proposed land use map or to provide land for a community need that was not
anticipated at the time of adoption of the general plan;

2. The existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or
that it failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the
natural characteristics of the land including, but not limited to, steep slopes,
floodplain, unstable soils, and inadequate drainage; or

3. The land or its surrounding environs has changed or is changing to such a degree
that it is in the public interest to encourage redevelopment of the area or to
recognize the changed character of the area.

The discussion below in the General Plan Considerations and the Large Scale Master Plan sections
address how the proposed rezoning is consistent with the 2016 Midvale City General Plan.
Additionally, the existing lots were created and originally developed to be used for agricultural
purposes and, since then, the area transitioned away from an agricultural use to a higher density
residential use piece by piece and even the subject properties no longer see regular agricultural
use. With the other projects that have been developed along 8000 South in the decades since, the
surrounding environs have changed and continue to change. As a result, rezoning from SF-1/AO
to RM-12 will make the zoning designation consistent with other properties in the area and
encourage redevelopment of underutilized property. Tying this rezone request to the Large Scale
Master Plan and Development Agreement guarantees the rezone can respond to concerns raised
during the public process.

GENERAL PLAN CONSIDERATIONS:
The 2016 Midvale City General Plan locates the subject properties in a Stability Area, near the
border of the Middle State Street and South State Street Opportunity Areas. The proposal to rezone
the subject properties from SF-1/AO to RM-12 is generally supported by the General Plan because
it allows for redevelopment in a manner consistent with the surrounding neighborhood and satisfies
the land use goals for Stability Areas in general.
The General Plan states that:
“In Stability Areas, the current overall land use mix is desirable, and preservation of these areas’
character and function is the desired future condition. These areas are nearly fully developed and
have little foreseeable change in the types or intensity of land uses in the future.
In Opportunity Areas, minor-to-major changes in current land uses are likely to occur due to
market forces, increasing land values, and opportunities to optimize land uses to take advantage of
transit and other public investments. Projects in Opportunity Areas are anticipated to be at higher
levels of density than current land uses and should be carefully planned and designed to integrate
into the fabric of the area and minimize impacts on adjacent and nearby existing land uses.” (p.
46).
The subject property is in a neighborhood that has been transitioning over the past two decades
from single family detached residential to medium density residential uses in the form of single
family attached and duplex style developments. 8000 South is lined with projects using this
medium density style of development, including White Pines, Creekside Townhomes, Auburn
Townhomes, Pinstripe Cove, and others. The broader area also has seen this shift to medium
density developments; nearby projects not located on 8000 South include the Candlestick
Apartments complex and the Station Place Condominium project. A few single-family dwellings
remain scattered throughout the area but are generally located on property that is challenging to
redevelop individually, requiring consolidation or coordination between multiple property owners,
and are outnumbered by single-family attached and duplex developments. Rezoning the subject
properties to RM-12 would allow them to be redeveloped in a manner consistent with the changes
in the surrounding overall land use mix.

The single-family portion of the proposal has generally demonstrated compliance with the

Wetlands
The wetland/drainage area at the south end of the project is required to remain under the ownership
and control of the Master Developer but is considered a public asset for the Jordan Bluffs area.
The proposal currently identifies this as an area to be planted using native grasses and vegetation,
with existing vegetation preserved to the maximum extent possible. The quality of the wetland and
surrounding vegetation has declined over time and, to properly address the actions necessary to
protect and enhance the wetland area, Staff recommends requiring the applicant to work with the
City and Master Developer to retain a qualified wetland restoration specialist tasked with
redesigning and enhancing the overall wetland area, including the adjacent areas that are part of
this project. The boundary of this design area should include the delineated wetland and the space
between the existing Jordan River Parkway Trail extension and the Jordan River and should also
consider future impacts from changes proposed by West Jordan for the Big Bend Project on the
opposite side of the Jordan River. Staff will address this process in the Development Agreement.

GENERAL PLAN CONSIDERATIONS:
The 2016 Midvale City General Plan locates the subject properties in a Stability Area, on the
border of both the North State Street and 7200 South Opportunity Areas. The proposed residential
development is supported by the General Plan because it allows for redevelopment in a manner
consistent with the surrounding neighborhood and satisfies the land use goals for Stability Areas
in general, while also buffering the effects of higher intensity uses in the neighboring Opportunity
Areas.
The General Plan states that:
“In Stability Areas, the current overall land use mix is desirable, and preservation of these
areas’ character and function is the desired future condition. These areas are nearly fully
developed and have little foreseeable change in the types or intensity of land uses in the
future.
In Opportunity Areas, minor-to-major changes in current land uses are likely to occur due
to market forces, increasing land values, and opportunities to optimize land uses to take
advantage of transit and other public investments. Projects in Opportunity Areas are
anticipated to be at higher levels of density than current land uses and should be carefully
planned and designed to integrate into the fabric of the area and minimize impacts on
adjacent and nearby existing land uses.” (p. 46).
The subject property is in a neighborhood that has been gradually transitioning from single family
detached residential to medium density residential uses in the form of single-family attached and
duplex style developments, including the Granstone 2 MPD directly east of the subject property.
Regarding future land use goals for Stability Areas, the General Plan states:
“The land use goals for Stability Areas include:
1. Support property maintenance and neighborhood stability.
2. Buffer uses in Stability Areas from more intensive land uses nearby, including
adjacent Opportunity Areas.
3. Provide for better pedestrian/bicycle connections through and between
neighborhoods.
4. Provide for access to parks, trails, and recreation facilities.

12. CUP-21-24-451-002; VINTAGE SPIRITS; 6844 S 300 W; CONDITIONAL USE
PERMIT FOR MANUFACTURING USE AND PACKAGE AGENCY; CI ZONE;
DEREK YODER (APPLICANT).

Ms. Jana Ward stated the applicant, Vintage Spirits LLC, is requesting a conditional use permit

approval to establish and operate a manufacturing business and a package agency in an existing

42,782 SF industrial building located at 6844 S 300 W. The building contains multiple tenant

spaces, with the applicant proposing to occupy a 2,500 SF tenant space. The property is located in

the Clean Industrial zone (CI).

The proposed use is for the manufacturing of spirits and to function as a package agency.

Manufacturing of spirits will take place 24 hours a day 7 days a week. The package agency will

operate during the DABC permitted package agency hours.

The subject property is surrounded by other Clean Industrial (CI) zoned properties.

ORDINANCE CONSIDERATIONS:

The Clean Industrial (CI) zone allows manufacturing and package agency uses under a conditional

use permit, provided the Planning Commission concludes that the application fully mitigates all

identified adverse impacts and complies with the following general standards:

1. The application complies with all applicable provisions of this title, State and Federal Law;

2. The structures associated with the use are compatible with surrounding structures in terms
of use, scale, mass and circulation;

3 The use is not detrimental to the public health, safety and welfare;

4. The use is consistent with the Midvale City General Plan, as amended;
