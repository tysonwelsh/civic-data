1.    Austin Cooper, representing JDH Development, requests rezone approval for parcels
      located at 52, 30, & 24 W Center St and 7710 S State St from Transit-Oriented
      Development Zone to Transit-Oriented Development Overlay Zone. Information
      regarding the proposed regulations, prohibitions, and permitted uses that the property
      will be subject to if the rezone is adopted can be found in Midvale City Code 17-7-17.

      The project proposal involves a rezone of 4 parcels totaling approximately 3.46 acres. The
      applicant’s justification for the request and supporting documents are included in the
      attachments.

      Midvale City Code 17-3-1(E.) outlines the criteria necessary for granting a rezone as follows:

      17-3-1 Criteria/Required Findings. …rezoning of land is to be discouraged and allowed only
      under the limited circumstances herein described. Therefore, the planning commission may
      recommend, and the city council may grant, a rezoning application only if it determines, in

written findings, that the proposed rezoning is consistent with the policies and goals of the
general plan and that the applicant has demonstrated that the:

      1. Proposed rezoning is necessary either to comply with the general plan proposed land
         use map or to provide land for a community need that was not anticipated at the time
         of adoption of the general plan;
      2. Existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or that it
         failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the natural
         characteristics of the land, including but not limited to, steep slopes, flood plain,
         unstable soils, and inadequate drainage; or
      3. Land or its surrounding environs has changed or is changing to such a degree that it is
         in the public interest to encourage redevelopment of the area or to recognize the
         changed character of the area.

Staff has reviewed the proposal against the criteria listed above and finds that the rezone request
satisfies the third criterion. The surrounding area has experienced incremental redevelopment
and increased intensity of land uses. These changes indicate a shift toward higher-density and
mixed-use development, and support the conclusion that it is in the public interest to recognize
the evolving character of the area and encourage continued redevelopment.

The Midvale City General Plan identifies nine Opportunity Areas. Opportunity Areas are where
changes in the types and/or intensities of current land uses are anticipated. In these areas, denser
residential uses, mixed residential and commercial uses, and larger, taller buildings are
anticipated to take advantage of the transportation infrastructure and to generate more tax
revenue for the City.

The 2016 Midvale City General Plan identifies the subject property as being in the Middle State
Street Opportunity Area and lists eight goals for the area:
        1. Support development of higher-value commercial and business uses through the
           development of mixed-use and higher density residential uses
        2. Develop a small area plan for the development near the TRAX station
        3. Support and encourage development to take advantage of transit and transportation
           opportunities
        4. Focus commercial and mixed-use development at the nodes of 7500 South and
           7720/7800 South
        5. Support redevelopment of the underutilized commercial properties between the
           commercial nodes into mixed-use residential development
        6. Explore the potential for unifying urban-design elements and beautification of the
           area
        7. Employ design elements to buffer the effects of higher intensity uses in the core
           areas on adjacent existing residential uses
        8. Strengthen pedestrian connections to the Center Street TRAX Station

The proposed rezone is consistent with several goals for the Middle State Street Opportunity
Area. Specifically, the proposed rezone would facilitate higher-density and mixed-use
development (Goal 1), leverage the site’s proximity to transit infrastructure (Goal 3), support

DISCUSSION:

Commissioner Anderson requested and received clarification regarding the location of the parcels
along State Street.

Daniel Van Buege, Deputy City Attorney, informed the commissioners that this is a legislative
decision and that the Planning Commission will provide a recommendation to City Council for
final approval and/or denial.

Vice Chair Liedtke requested clarification regarding the differences between the Transit-Oriented
Development (TOD) Zone and the Transit-Oriented Development Overlay (TODO) Zone.

Elizabeth Arnold, Senior Planner, explained that one of the primary differences is the higher
density permitted within the Transit-Oriented Development Overlay (TODO) Zone. The Transit-
Oriented Development (TOD) Zone allows up to 25 units per acre, whereas the Transit-Oriented
Development Overlay (TODO) Zone allows up to 85 units per acre. She further stated that the
Transit-Oriented Development Overlay (TODO) Zone permits taller buildings and allows for
mixed-use development.

Commissioner Anderson stated that, due to the location of the parcels along State Street and their
distance from other residential areas and single-family dwellings, the higher density permitted
within the Transit-Oriented Development Overlay (TODO) Zone may be beneficial and preferable.

Commissioner Brauchie requested and received clarification regarding whether the required 65%
street frontage parking within the Transit-Oriented Development Overlay (TODO) Zone could be
utilized for office spaces or if it applied solely to retail and restaurant uses.

Chair Edwards requested clarification regarding parking requirements within the Transit-Oriented
Development (TOD) and Transit-Oriented Development Overlay (TODO) Zones, specifically
whether the requirements pertained to employee and public parking allocations or were based
solely on net leasable area without additional parking calculations.

Wendelin Knobloch, Planning Director, and Elizabeth Arnold clarified that the parking regulations
within these zones are based on net leasable area with no additional parking requirements applied.
They further stated that all future development applications for the parcels would be reviewed in
accordance with the applicable zoning requirements and regulations in effect at the time of
submittal and review.

2.   Midvale City initiated zoning code text amendment modifying setback exceptions in
     various sections of Title 17 based on changes in the Utah State Code from HB476 in the
     2024 General Session.

     The proposed amendment modifies every applicable Section of Title 17 that relates to residential
     rear setback exceptions. House Bill 476 of the 2024 Utah Legislature enacted UCA 10-9a-540
     (renumbered to 10-20-623 on 11/6/2025) which permits “allowable features” as defined within
     the rear setback area of a residential dwelling, see Attachment 1.

     Amendments are also proposed to further clarify how the International Residential and Building
     Codes are applicable for window wells for setbacks on single family homes, and amends
     language to be more clearly understood for International Building Code applicability for building
     setbacks.

     Public notice has been sent to affected entities as required in 17-3-9.B of the Municipal Code. No
     comments have been received as of the writing of this report.

     -ZONING CODE AMENDMENT CRITERIA-

     Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) outlines the criteria necessary for amendments to the zoning code.
     A proposal may only be approved if it demonstrates one or more of the following:
            1. The proposed amendment promotes the objectives of the general plan and purposes
                of this title;

     PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION MAY 13, 2026.
                                                                                      7|Page

        2. The proposed amendment promotes the purposes outlined in Utah State Code 10-9a-
           102;
        3. The proposed amendment more clearly explains the intent of the original language or
           has been amended to make interpretation more straightforward; or
        4. Existing zoning code was the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact.

Staff finds that this proposal meets criteria 1, 2 and 3 listed above. The proposed amendment
promotes the general plan, purposes of this title, and purposes of Utah State Code 10-9a-102
(renumbered to 10-20-101) by conforming Title 17 to the governing Utah State Code as amended
by the State Legislature. The proposed amendment also amends language to make interpretation
more straightforward for setbacks in relation to the International Residential and Building Codes
as applicable.

3.    Midvale City initiated text amendment to Title 17 of the Midvale Municipal Code relating
      to home-based microschools and micro-education entities.

      This text amendment implements S.B. 13 from the 2024 Utah Legislative General Session. The
      amendment adds definitions for “Home-based microschool” and “Micro education entity”,
      establishes new chapters within Supplementary Regulations to govern these uses, and updates
      the use tables to permit both uses in all zones.

DISCUSSION:

Commissioner Snow requested clarification regarding the potential traffic impacts associated with
the allowable number of students for both Home-Based Microschools and Micro-Education
Entities. He expressed concern regarding the potential for 16 to 100 students requiring daily pick-
up and drop-off, which could result in an excess number of vehicles parking along residential
streets.

Elizabeth Arnold stated that, based on the legislation adopted by the State of Utah, both Home-
Based Microschools and Micro-Education Entities would be permitted within residential zones,
provided they meet all additional applicable criteria. She explained that these concerns are the
reason the proposed regulations include a requirement for traffic plan review and approval by the
City Engineer, ensuring that traffic impacts on residential streets are monitored and considered.

Elizabeth Arnold further explained that the proposed text amendment is intended to ensure that
Midvale City Code remains compliant with superseding state code. She also noted that, to date,
there has been no expressed interest in establishing a Home-Based Microschool or Micro-
Education Entity within the city.

Commissioner Snow requested and received additional clarification regarding the square footage
requirements for a Micro-Education Entity.

Wendelin Knobloch provided additional clarification, stating that any proposed Micro-Education
Entity would also be subject to other applicable regulatory review processes and would require
review and approval from additional staff members, including the Building Official, Building
Department, Fire Marshal, and Planning Department as part of the Building Permit and Business
License review process.

Wendelin Knobloch further explained that numerous additional code requirements would need to
be satisfied in conjunction with this section of the Midvale Municipal Code, including, but not
limited to, the State of Utah Building Code and Fire Code requirements. He also noted that other
planning mechanisms, such as the Transportation Master Plan and Functional Classification Maps,
are utilized to classify roads as arterial, minor arterial, collector streets, and similar roadway
designations.

Elizabeth Arnold stated that the provisions previously presented and approved by the State of Utah
are optional for the City to regulate, and that the proposed optional regulations have been included
within the text amendment. She further explained that larger Micro-Education Entities would still
be subject to occupancy limitations and other applicable requirements, which would limit the
number of students permitted within the buildings.
