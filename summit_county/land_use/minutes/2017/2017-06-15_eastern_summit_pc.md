Findings of Fact:  
1. The proposed project is located at 3158 North 5000 West, Peoa.  
2. The property is located in the Agriculture Protection (AP) zone.  
3. Accessory buildings and uses to the principal residential dwelling unit or subdivision, 
exceeding 2,000 square feet are a conditional use in the AP zone.  
4. The lot is 17.2 acres in size.  
5. Access to the property is from 5000 West Road.  
6. The building meets the required setbacks and height in the AP zone.  
7. Height in the AP zone is 32 feet above grade. The proposed building will be 32 feet above 
grade.  
8. There are wetlands and natural streams on the property.  
9. The applicant has demonstrated compliance with all required setbacks from the Weber 
River and the delineated wetlands.  
10. The application was routed to affected county departments, state agencies and service 
providers, no issues were raised.  
11. The proposed building location is approximately 1000 feet from the nearest single 
family home.

area.

4.   Public hearing and possible action for Amendments to the Eastern Summit County 
Development Code to incorporate Engineering regulations. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner  
 
Planner Milliner stated that the State passed a Bill that all Land Use Regulations be tied into 
one document, which would be the Eastern Summit County Development Code. Currently, 
the Engineering regulations are sorted into a number of Ordinances that were adopted by 
the County Council but not organized into the Land Use Document. The goal is to put these 
Ordinances into the Development Code as Chapter 8. The language in the Staff Report is 
taken straight from the Ordinances, with very minimal changes. The definitions and the 
pipeline regulations still need to be included. Planner Milliner asked that the Planning 
Commission conduct a public hearing and give input to Staff and continue the public 
hearing to July 20, 2017 to allow the definitions and pipeline regulations to be added. 
Tonight, there is no request for action but the public hearing serves to have a pending 
Ordinance, which effectively creates Chapter 8 so the requirements of the law can be met 
and Chapter 8 can still be worked on.  
Commissioner Sonntag asked if there are any changes to the wording of the Ordinances. 
Engineer Mike Kendell answered there are some changes, mostly just language changes 
and updated UDOT (Utah Department of Transportation) regulations. The County has been 
designated an MS-4 (Municipal separate storm sewer system) so the conditions required to 
be followed are current in the Code. The ADA access guidelines were also included. Most of 
the changes were made to bring the Ordinance current with State statutes.

As part of the County Council’s adoption of Chapter 4, the definition of wetlands sounded as 
though it is only a wetland if it has been delineated, according to Commissioner Sonntag. 
Director Putt read the existing definition and stated that the language that was adopted is 
what the Planning Commission forwarded to the County Council. “An area of land can only 
be determined to be wetlands through the designation by the US Army Corps of Engineers” 
was included. Ultimately, to be wetlands there had to be a delineation and that delineation 
had to be made by the Army Corps of Engineers. This does not mean that an environmental 
engineer or a hydrologist cannot prepare that study and have it reviewed by the Army 
Corps of Engineers. Commissioner Sonntag stated there are two ways to play that. Either 
“hands off,” this is a delineated wetland and cannot be touched, or the Planning 
Department is saying in order to process an application, any wetlands must be delineated 
before the plan is processed. This leaves people at the mercy of the rate at which the Corps 
moves. 
Commissioner Wharton asked if wetlands are not delineated, but Staff determines it is a 
wetland area at the site inspection, then can a person still build in that area. Director Putt 
stated he cannot think of a specific instance where that happened. If that were the case, 
then Staff would ask for a delineation. The process is when a permit is reviewed, typically 
by the Planning Department, as part of the process, Staff will go to the GIS system, which 
has a fairly broad mapping of wetland and riparian areas. In the event that a property has 
wetlands delineated on the GIS, there will be a site inspection. If it appears to be wet, then 
Staff will request a delineation from the Corps. Commissioner Sonntag asked if there can 
be a safe harbor process where a landowner can agree on a line and not waste a year going
