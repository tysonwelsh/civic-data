DISCUSSION WIH THE DAUGHTERS OF THE UTAH PIONEERS REGARDING THE USE
OF THE PIONEER COURTHOUSE:
     Jeanine Vander Bruggen with the Daughters of the Utah Pioneers addressed the
     Council regarding the use of the Pioneer Courthouse. She provided a handout
     background outlining the uses at the Pioneer Courthouse and how their organization
     will benefit from staying in the building.

DISCUSSION REGARDING SUICIDE PREVENTION AND AWARENESS:
     Theresa Willie with Southwest Behavior Health Center and Roger Nelson with Utah
     Behavioral Services. Together they presented a PowerPoint presentation covering
     the following topics: Death ruled as suicide in 12-month periods (August 1 – July 31)
     from 2015-2016 to 2019-2020; Suspected suicide deaths by sex; Suspected suicide
     deaths by age; Percentage of suspected suicide deaths by ethnicity; Percentage of
     suspected suicide deaths by race; Percentage of deaths by lethal means; Suicide
     deaths by methods 2019-2020; Reach4Hope – Training and resources; How can you
     help?; Lifetime suicide attempts by highly rejected LGBT young people; and
     Language matters/safe reporting.

      Councilmember Randall spoke about the ULCT Mental Health Class she attended this
      afternoon. Spanish Fork has a website in which they post a lot of information and
      resources; she asked that the City do something similar. Additionally, they
      addressed CALM training in the class she attended.

      Ms. Willie explained that they incorporate that training in the QPR training they offer.
      She mentioned that there are grief support groups in town and spoke about other
      resources that are available.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard left the meeting.

      Mayor Pike stated he would like to put together a small group to takes suggestions
      and help Ms. Willie and Mr. Nelson with prevention and awareness.

      Mr. Nelson outlined what his team does to help with suicide prevention and
      awareness. His entire team is certified to perform the Columbia Suicide Prevention
      Scale. He provided statistics on the phone calls his team has received after which he
      explained white, pink, and blue sheets. In Iron County their organization received
      direct referrals from the dispatch center; they sometimes respond rather than a
      police officer on mental health or suicide calls.

      Chief Whitehead explained Sgt. Gilman has been working on a plan to offer this in
      Washington County. He noted that the SGPD is not equipped to deal with people
      dealing with suicidal thoughts. He is interested to see what they can do to make this
      work and benefit the community.

      Councilmember Smethurst spoke about suicide among veterans. This is something
      that needs to be done to help individuals.

      Mayor Pike noted the City needs to help build awareness.

      Ms. Willie explained the community model they use. They are required to do a
      community readiness survey every couple of years. They have been preparing the
      community for the services they offer. The community is aware of the problem, but
      they may not be aware there is an active coalition in the community that has
      resources.

      A discussion took place regarding QPR training.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman noted Ms. Willie and Mr. Nelson has a fantastic with
      their limited resources. Suicide is a hard topic due to the impact it has.
      Intermountain has done well with providing resources as well. He spoke about
      genetic testing for anti-depressants and how it helps which will be most effective for
      the patients. He then asked if the hospital can make the tests available for those
      that do not have insurance.

      Housing and Economic Development Director Shirlayne Quayle asked if they are
      connected with Switchpoint. She invited Mr. Nelson and Ms. Willie to attend one of
      their board meetings.

      Ms. Willie noted they have done QPR training with the SwitchPoint staff.

      Mr. Nelson noted they get a number of calls regarding SwitchPoint residents.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman mentioned Encircle will open on October 17th. The
      house is 190 South 100 East. It caters to youth who are LGBTQ+; it is a connective,
      social place for them to feel safe and welcome. Families may struggle when they
      learn their loved one is LGBTQ. There is no agenda, the goal is to help people. Their
      tagline is No Sides, Only Love.
