36          Water Rate Study Conversation with Shawn Koorn (HDR)
37
38   Shawn Koorn, HDR, reviewed the water rate study process, explaining that the goal is to determine
39   the total revenue needed to fund operating and capital expenses over a 10-year period, with a focus
40   on the next five years. He clarified that current revenues are not sufficient to cover expenses and
41   future capital needs, meaning rate adjustments are necessary. He reviewed the secondary water
42   revenue needs, noting it is in slightly better financial condition than culinary water but still requires
43   rate increases to cover operating costs and capital improvements.
44
45   Mr. Koorn outlined projected monthly bill increases over several years and explained that,
46   regardless of how rates are structured, the total revenue target remains the same. He also
47   recommended moving toward differentiated rate structures by customer class to better reflect
48   system usage and future needs. Council discussion highlighted concern that postponing funding
49   increases risk, especially with ongoing inflation driving up construction and infrastructure costs,
50   while others noted that borrowing also carries uncertainty due to fluctuating interest rates.

 7   City staff and Councilmembers discussed the proposed water rate adjustments and focused on
 8   option three/ Councilmembers emphasized balancing a reasonable base rate with consumption-
 9   based charges to incentivize conservation while avoiding undue hardship on residents with fixed
10   incomes. They debated whether to include bonding or other financing methods, ultimately agreeing
11   that cash-funding projects and minimizing subsidies would be fiscally responsible.
