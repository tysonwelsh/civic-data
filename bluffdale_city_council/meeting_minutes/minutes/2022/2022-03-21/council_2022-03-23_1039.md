Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner overviewed the proposed Utility Assistance
Program . He explained that the program had been around for a little while, but the govenm1ent was
ramping up its promotion of it and wanted to make it more accessible. To do that, there was a
requirement that cities sign a n agreement w ith the agency distributing the fund s. Mr. Kmichner
repo1ied that the Federal Govenunent included monies as part of the last American Rescue Plan Act
("ARPA") allotment to help those having financial difficulties. It was determined that the monies
would be moved through the Department o f Wo rkforce Services. The department was responsible
for admini stering and tracking the funds. However, they were not in a position to evaluate indi vidual

needs. As a result, the Department of Workforce Services was working with the same organization
that handles the HEAT program. Mr. Kaitchner reported that the Utility Assistance Program would
aid low-income individuals and cover the following:

     •   Drinking water;
     •   Wastewater;
     •   Stormwater; and
     •   Groundwater.

Mr. Kaitchner reported that someone could apply for the program tlu·ough the HEAT program. The

There were limitations on how much an individual family could benefit from the program.
Additionally, this was not an open-ended program. Mr. Kartchner explained that it was broken into

City. For instance, if someone received funds for water reimbursement, the City could not shut its
water off for delinquency for a certain number of days. This could potentially cause collection issues.
However, he believed those sorts of issues would be relatively minimal, and overall , the program
would be beneficial to those in need.
