III.    Special Recognition for Paul Allred.
City Manager, Gina Chamness, recognized the work of former Community Development Director
and Senior Planning Advisor, Paul Allred who recently transitioned into retirement. She thanked him
for his commitment, kindness, and vision for the City. Council Member Fotheringham described
Mr. Allred as a charming, warm, and helpful individual. He praised him for his strength of character
and assistance over the years. Mr. Allred was thanked for his service on behalf of the City of Holladay
and all of the elected officials.

Mr. Allred thanked everyone for their kind words and wonderful gifts. He made note of a painting
that hung in his office. It was done by a local artist named Dawna Barton and depicted Spring Creek.
Mr. Allred noted that Spring Creek is a critical part of the community. One day, Ms. Barton called
the City and was upset that her painting could not be seen in the City Hall hallways. Mr. Allred asked
Ms. Barton to meet with him and she immediately saw the painting in his office. The artwork meant
a lot to him and was a reminder of Spring Creek. Mr. Allred explained that the special moment with
Ms. Barton was one of many special moments he had had with residents over the years. Mr. Allred
was grateful for the department and the wonderful employees. He specifically thanked City Recorder,
Stephanie Carlson, and Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink.

Mr. Allred extended an apology to his family for the countless evenings he was not home due to
Planning Commission and City Council Meetings for various employers. He expressed gratitude to
his wife and noted that family meant everything to him.

Mayor Dahle thanked everyone who had come to celebrate with Mr. Allred and his family.

V.      Public Hearing on Proposed City-Wide Fireworks Restrictions.
Mayor Dahle reported that he UFA Board met on June 15, 2021, and Fire Marshal, Brad Larson,
provided a briefing about the existing environmental conditions. The drought across the State coupled
with the extreme heatwave created a unique environment for fire danger. Mayor Dahle noted that
there had been discussions in the past about the State Legislation and what could and could not be
done. He felt that the City of Holladay had gone as far as they could when it came to restricting
certain areas within the City as part of the map that was submitted to the County. Those areas largely
revolved around waterways, the mature tree canopy around Walker Lane, and Cottonwood Lane as
well as everything East of I-215.

UFA Chief Legal Officer, Brian Roberts, felt strongly that the existing environmental conditions gave
municipalities the latitude to expand those boundaries. Mayor Dahle discussed the map that was
submitted. Salt Lake City restricted personal fireworks on everything east of 900 East. Millcreek
was currently looking into a line that would be east of 900 East as well. Sandy City restricted the
area east of 1300 East with some exceptions as did Draper City. A line existed from 1300 East to
Millcreek and 900 East, Millcreek, and Salt Lake City eastbound to the foothills. Mayor Dahle
wondered what the City Council thought about restrictions in the City of Holladay.

Mayor Dahle noted that the item was added to the current City Council Meeting agenda to avoid
holding an emergency meeting. Staff had done their best to inform the public about the item and the

public hearing. The intention was to determine whether the City of Holladay wanted to restrict
personal fireworks from 1300 East to the foothills. The enactment would prohibit the discharge of
personal fireworks in the City of Holladay until October 15, 2021. Mayor Dahle noted that the City-
run fireworks show was still planned because it is licensed, controlled, and monitored.

Mr. Godfrey was comfortable that in the past the City had gone as far as they legally could with the
restrictions. The current year was different as conditions were different. He was satisfied that the
information from the Fire Marshal was clear enough to warrant the restriction proposed in the
ordinance. Mayor Dahle noted that this was a unique situation and the map would likely return to
something similar and was voted on previously unless the extreme weather conditions remained. He
felt that restricting the use of personal fireworks would be consistent with what the other East Bench
municipalities were planning to do.

Council Member Durham joined by phone.

Mayor Dahle noted that there may be issues with enforcement. UFA Captain Dan Brown reported
that there would be two additional brush trucks on the east side as well as a Fire Prevention Specialist
for enforcement. Additionally, there would be a Communications Chief to coordinate responses. Ms.
Chamness reported that the UPD would have two additional officers and the Code Enforcement
Officer circulating.

Mayor Dahle expected 90% of residents to comply and understand the reason behind the decision to
restrict the use of personal fireworks. However, there would likely be some people who would use
fireworks anyway. He hoped that additional patrolling would remind residents that fireworks were
not legal that year due to safety concerns. Council Member Quinn wondered how the fireworks
decision would be publicized. Ms. Chamness explained that last year, 28 signs and banners were
distributed to neighborhoods where fireworks were prohibited. The number of signs would likely
need to increase for the current year. Additionally, social media would spread the word as much as
possible. The information would also be shared in The Holladay Journal.

Council Member Fotheringham noted that high schools did not report to the City and were not subject
to City restrictions related to fireworks. Though graduations were done, the ordinance went until
October 15, 2021. He believed there would likely be events with fireworks in the fall and wondered
how communication could be increased between the schools and the City Council. Ms. Chamness
noted that there had been recent discussions about that. For instance, at the Olympus High School
graduation, there had been fireworks. UPD was notified but the City was not. This additional step
of communication was needed in order for the City to notify residents. Ms. Chamness believed that
there were two fireworks displays that could be impacted by the October 15, 2021 date included in
the ordinance. It would be necessary to stay in contact about the environmental conditions.

Mayor Dahle opened the public hearing at 7:19 pm. He reported that 19 written comments were
received via email. Those comments would be added to the record. Mayor Dahle noted that all of
the written comments, with the exception of one, were supportive of the City taking these cautionary
measures. The one comment that was not fully in support had suggested that there be a two-hour
block on July 4, 2021, to allow for some kind of personal fireworks use.

VIII. Consideration of Ordinance 2021-15 Determining the Rate of Tax for 2021 Tax Year and
       Levying Taxes Upon All Real and Personal Property within the City of Holladay.
Mayor Dahle noted that both the Certified Tax Rate and the increase that was in the budget were
included as part of the approval. Council Member Gibbons asked that someone read the number from
the proposed ordinance. Ms. Chamness reported that the number was .001605. She explained that
the number was the calculated rate that exceeded the certified rate for that 50% increase.

Mayor Dahle explained that once the City Council approved the number, it would be passed on to the
County Assessor who would go through the noticing process. If the City Council decided that the
number should change after that process takes place, a budget amendment would need to be done.
Ms. Chamness noted that other Councils she had worked with had chosen to reaffirm the budget once
the rate was approved. That was something the City Council could consider in the future.

X.       Consideration of Ordinance 2021-17 Restricting the Use of Personal Fireworks within All
         Portions of the City of Holladay Lying East of 1300 East Street.

concerns related to fireworks. His district is heavily wooded and there had been some scary incidents

XVIII. City Manager Report – Gina Chamness.
Ms. Chamness presented construction updates and reported that on June 22, 2021, for approximately
one week, eastbound traffic from 2700 East to 2820 East will need to be detoured from 7:00 a.m. to
5:00 p.m. Traffic headed eastbound on 3900 South before 2700 East would be diverted along 2700
East to 4500 South to access Wasatch Boulevard and SR-215. That information had been
communicated to residents in the area.

The Highland Drive Intersection Project was originally estimated to take four months. It was intended
to start in April and end in August. However, due to shortages in construction supplies, the project
was expected to take an additional month to complete. Crews were nearly finished on the south end
of the project and there would be minimal construction in that area. Construction would continue
along the remainder of the project area through September. Ms. Chamness also reported that the
pickleball court was being upgraded. The project would be finished within the next couple of days.

Assistant to the City Manager, Holly Smith, discussed the Truth in Taxation process. She reported
that Salt Lake County suggested August 17, 2021, as their preferred date for the Truth in Taxation
hearing. Based on the dates that City Council Members sent to Ms. Carlson previously, it appeared
that all Council Members would be available on that date. The suggested date for the City Council
Public Open House was August 11, 2021.

Council Member Gibbons stated that it would be important to hold a City Council Meeting shortly
after the Truth in Taxation Hearing to discuss the events of the hearing and decide whether to make
a change to the proposed tax increase. Discussions were had about availability. It was suggested that
the meeting be held on August 19, 2021.

Ms. Smith overviewed next steps with the City Council, which included the following:

   •   Early July: Residents receive a newsletter with the property tax update as well as the hearing
       and open house dates;
   •   July 8, 2021: City Council Meeting update and Open House details;
   •   6 Weeks: Ongoing messaging across different communication platforms (website, social
       media, emails, and banners);
   •   Mid-to-Late-July: Residents receive postcard mailers from the City, followed by the Salt Lake
       County Property Valuation Notice; and
   •   Early August: Truth in Taxation official noticing would begin.

Council Member Fotheringham mentioned the property tax relief programs that were available. He
wondered if any of the informational materials would include references to those programs.
Ms. Smith reported that those details had been published in the past and would be published again.
Additionally, links could be added to the project website. She made a note to emphasize those
programs more to inform residents who may not be aware of them.

XIX. District Issues and Board Reports.
Council Member Fotheringham reported that the Summer Concert Series would begin shortly.
Holladay Music Fest would begin on June 26, 2021, at 7:00 p.m. After July 10, 2021, the concerts
would take place every Saturday for eight weeks. Council Member Fotheringham encouraged City
Council Members to support the concerts and share information about them with their constituents.

had been a meeting with the contractor and they would begin construction after the Fourth of July.
The underground work would be done first. Ms. Smith explained that a substantial portion of the
Skate Park would be done by the end of September and the skatepark would be completed by early
October. This was approximately four weeks behind schedule. Council Member Petersen also asked
about the park service. Ms. Chamness noted that there would need to be a Request for Proposals
(“RFP”). The RFP would likely be issued mid-October and someone would most likely be under
contract by the end of the year. This would allow the work to begin in the spring.

Council Member Gibbons noted that he had a discussion with Mr. Teerlink recently and an application
had been filed in the new Holladay Crossroads Zone. The application was not for the full entitlement
as far as height. It would be a three-story structure, with retail on the bottom and two residential
floors above. Council Member Gibbons noted that it would be interesting to see that unfold.

Mayor Dahle shared updates related to the Fourth of July celebrations. There would be a playlist with
music and he would say a few words before 10:00 p.m. They were still looking into someone to sing
the National Anthem before the fireworks display takes place.

Mayor Dahle also reported that the Granite Education Foundation, a non-profit that supported the
district, was hosting the Backpack and School Supplies Drive. The drive would ensure that all
students in the district can have their basic needs met when they started in-person school. The City
volunteered to get behind that effort as a City. Backpacks could be put together at the City and there
would be a community collection at the concert scheduled for August 7, 2021. Granite Education
Foundation volunteers would help staff that. Mayor Dahle reported that there were marketing pieces
put together for release on July 1, 2021. He asked that Council Members share the information with
constituents on social media.
