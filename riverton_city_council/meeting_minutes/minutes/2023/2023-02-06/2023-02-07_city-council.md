Call to Order

Mayor Staggs called the Work Session to order at 5:07 PM and conducted a roll call.

Wells was excused.

Discussion Items

       Community Center Discussion

The Council met at the Community Center to discuss first-floor renovations and changes to the
auditorium on the second floor.

Sheril Garn, Director of Events and Operations, presented the plan for the first-floor renovations
that was originally presented to the TRCC Board to obtain funding for the project.

Councilmember Buroker said she opposed the metal awning over the front entrance of the
building because it would hide the historic features of the building. She supports the courtyard
design but would like it to be rectangular to reflect the shapes of the windows, instead of
circular. Councilmember McCay disagreed and said she liked the circular design.
Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting
February 7, 2023




Councilmember Buroker expressed concern about moving the historical cases to the main level.
She worried there would be too much light exposure and they could be damaged since they
would be open to the public. Mayor Staggs said the Historic Preservation Commission would
like the historical cases to be more accessible and he would like them to be moved to the first
floor.

Councilmember Buroker said she would like the north side of the entrance to be open space for
the public and not offices for the facilities department. Ms. Garn said this area could not be
open space because of the access needed for the elevator and other utilities.

Councilmember Buroker reiterated the historic value of the vestibule door frame and the need to
preserve it.

Mayor Staggs suggested that once the Council makes recommendations, they allow staff to
carry out the plan without further changes. The first-floor renovations will need to be completed
by December 31, 2023 to get the TRCC grant funding.

Councilmembers agreed to move forward with Phase 1 of the first floor renovations and asked
that the architect design the north courtyard area for future presentation to the Council.

Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the City move forward with planned renovations of
the Sandra Lloyd Community Center taking into consideration the historical nature of the
building and preserving those historical elements as much as possible as the City
renovates the center and moves forward with the plaza. Councilmember Pierucci
SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs
called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes,
and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed unanimously.

Public Hearing(s)/Action Items

         Ordinance No. 23-03 - Wright-Parkside Rezone, Proposed Rezone Of
         Approximately 6.1 Acres, 12995 S. Sand Hollow Drive, from RM-14-SD-EHOV to
         RM-14-SD, removing the Elderly Housing Overlay, Â Applicant- Wright Homes

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained this was a public hearing for a
Rezone application brought by Wright Homes seeking to rezone an existing development
consisting of approximately 6.1-acres located at 12995 South Sand Hollow Drive. The subject
property is a triangular parcel located south of 12600 South near 4245 West and is also known
as “Parkside Phase 3”. It is located south of the Rocky Mountain Power Corridor and just east
of the Welby-Jacob Canal.

Mr. Lethbridge said the subject property is currently zoned RM-14-SD-EHOV which means
Residential Multi-Family, 14-units per acre. SD is an abbreviation for “Specific Development”
Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting
February 7, 2023




meaning the Rezone Ordinance had additional zoning requirements which are specific to the
property. EHOV is an abbreviation for “Elderly Housing Overlay” which gives some allowances
and some additional requirements related to providing an age-restricted (55 and older) housing
development. The applicant is requesting the Elderly Housing Overlay Zone be removed.

Derek Wright, Wright Homes, explained that they are asking for the elderly housing overlay to
be removed. They are not trying to change the structures or the plat.

Councilmember Pierucci asked what the analysis is on traffic if they remove the elderly housing
overlay. Mr. Wright presented the traffic study to the Council.

Councilmember Buroker asked if the elderly housing overlay remained, would the developer be
required to make changes to the current construction. Mr. Lethbridge stated they would not.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;

Robert Woods said he was opposed to the removal of the overlay. He was disappointed that
Doug Young was able to convince the City Council that the elderly overlay would be okay for
high density.

Sarah Nelson said she lives in the Parkside community where the streets area very narrow.
The probability of more traffic is enormous. This development would likely delay the Parkside
community HOA. She believes the higher density will drain public resources including schools.

Justen Smith said he also lives in Parkside and believes it makes sense to remove the elderly
housing overlay. He would like the application to be approved.

Manuel Palacios said he lives in Parkside. He does not have an issue with removing the elderly
overlay and would like more families to have the opportunity to move into this community.

Shirley Van Vliet said she was opposed to removing the elderly housing overlay because the
streets are too narrow and there would also be a water supply issue.

Daniel Rudd said he is in the middle with his feelings about removing or keeping the elderly
housing overlay. He said that removing the elderly housing overlay could cause more traffic
and would like the City to install speed bumps. However, he is in favor of affordable housing.
He would like the developer to invest in the community by adding additional amenities.

Daniel Horne said he lives west of the zoning area. He believes there is value in leaving the
elderly housing overlay and his biggest concern is the number of vehicles.

Alex Reese said he’s been to several meetings and is glad that the City Council listens to
citizens. He believes traffic will increase if the elderly housing overlay is removed.

Mr. Wright explained that Dutchman Lane will connect to the east according to the City’s master
plan. This connection to Dutchman would greatly benefit this community as a whole. He
explained this project is over-parked meaning each unit has a two-car garage and a driveway.
They implemented a parking ordinance that had to do with these traffic challenges.

Mayor Staggs noted there were multiple email comments from residents that were received by
the Council and by staff. These comments are included in the packet and listed below
Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting
February 7, 2023




Aubrey Worthen opposed the removal of the elderly housing overlay and said Wright Homes
should not be allowed to change the zoning.

Chelsey Necaise expressed concern over removing the elderly housing overlay and the impact
it would have on schools and school buses.

Shirley Van Vliet-Galarza opposed the high density building by Wright Homes.

Jenna Duff opposed the removal of the elderly housing overlay because of traffic concerns and
concerns about the impact on schools.

Sarah Henderson said she was against the proposed rezone because of traffic and delay in
finishing the project, as well as a drain on public utilities.
Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
Hearing.

Mayor Staggs said he doesn’t understand how the plat was approved in 2017 or how buildings
were constructed that are not conducive to elderly housing living. In response, Mr. Lethbridge
said there was a lot of discussion when this was presented for rezone in 2017. The text of the
ordinance that was adopted stated “assisted living congregate care” for this area. This
language was not ignored when the site plan came forward. The Planning Commission
discussed this and struggled with this language needing to be more specific. These units were
approved, allowing for a living area for elderly and their caretakers. In retrospect, staff should
have been more specific in the language approved in this ordinance.

Councilmember Buroker asked about the potential connection to the main road. Mr. Lethbridge
said there could potentially be connections however these are private right-of-way areas. This
would require agreements with these property owners.

Councilmember McCay asked if the site plan approval included three-story buildings. Mr.
Lethbridge said the site plan approval included building type and height. If there are any
changes to the site plan, they would need to ask for an amended site plan approval.

Councilmember McCay expressed frustration that a three-story building was approved for an
elderly housing overlay. She said she would only agree to the removal of the elderly housing
overlay if the height of the buildings was decreased.

Councilmember Wells said he opposed having an elderly overlay on a three-story building and
was also frustrated that the site plan was approved with this building height.

Councilmember McDougal explained that he was part of the group that opposed this
development in 2017. When it was approved, the code needed to be more to exclude building a
three-story unit. He said it was embarrassing that Councilmembers want to go back on
decisions that were made in the past. He believes the environment has changed in the City.
Since 2017, zoning plans have been put in place that provides a good plan for the City. There
are numerous elderly housing developments, however there is a shortfall in affordable housing
and the state is pressuring cities to provide affordable housing. He can see the advantage of
pulling the elderly housing overlay and he does not think the Council can go back on a past
agreement with the developer.

Councilmember Pierucci said he lives in a community similar to this area. He shares frustration
with the discrepancies in the approved site plan and also shares Councilmember McDougal’s
concerns with honoring legal agreements currently in place. He worries about the impact of
partially finished townhomes if the elderly housing overlay is not removed.

Councilmember McCay said she was concerned about the Council’s reputation to the residents
because residents in this area bought their homes expecting there would not be three-story
buildings behind them. She doesn’t want one party to be a loser in this situation and would like
to compromise.

Councilmember Buroker said she would not support asking a developer to tear something
down.

Councilmember McDougal asked verifying questions about amending the plat. Mr. Carter said
they could amend the plat and if they decide on a solution that is outside of the parameters of
the plat then he recommended tabling this item. Councilmember McDougal responded that the
majority of the project is not affected by the elderly housing overlay and he was still supportive
of removing it.

Councilmember Pierucci said in government there needs to be consistency. He does not think
there was consistency for the residents or the developer on this project. He committed to not
support something like this in the future.
