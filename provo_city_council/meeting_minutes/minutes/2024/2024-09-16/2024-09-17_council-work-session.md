Item 1: A presentation regarding the Fiscal Year 2024 Budget Carryover Report
(24-080) 0:06:46

Substance of the Presentation: Andrea Wright, Budget Analyst, presented the Fiscal Year 2024
Budget Carryover Report. She explained the process of reviewing remaining operating budgets
and purchase orders after expenses are paid out for the year. Departments submit requests to
carry over funding to the next fiscal year, and the compiled list is reviewed by the mayor for
approval.

Wright highlighted different types of carryovers, which were color-coded in the report:

       Blue: Encumbered on purchase orders for ongoing projects.
       Green: Historically agreed upon carryovers such as capital accounts or grant balances.
       Yellow: Unspent FY24 budget requests.
       Purple: Automatic carryovers per budget ordinance.

She noted that carryovers were lower this year due to expected revenue shortfalls. Departments
were alerted early, and payroll savings measures were implemented.

John Borgett, Administrative Services Director, provided additional context on the revenue
shortfall and measures taken:

       Revenue was down about $2.5 million.
       Directors were notified starting in October of the anticipated shortfall.
       Payroll turnover savings of $1.7 million were not allowed to be transferred for operating
        expenses.
       Departments proactively found $300,000 in budget savings.
       Total budget savings of about $3.2 million against the $2.5 million shortfall left a
        $735,000 surplus.

Borgett explained that they typically set aside 25% of the available balance for the motion fund
and employee recognition. However, due to the shortfall, they recommended not adding to
employee recognition this year. He also noted that the local government disaster fund currently
has a balance of $1,780,841.

Council Discussion: There was discussion about potentially adding to the disaster fund. Borgett
clarified that it can only be used for state-declared disasters. Some councilors expressed interest
in maintaining flexibility in the general fund balance instead.

Motion: Councilor Hoban made a motion not to contribute to the local government disaster
fund, which was seconded by Councilor Christensen. The motion passed 6-1, with Councilor
Whipple opposed.

Item 2: A discussion regarding an ordinance amending Provo City Code
regarding signs permitted in the downtown zones. Downtown Neighborhood.
(PLOTA20240191) 0:33:56

Aaron Ardmore presented proposed amendments to allow marquee signs in downtown zones.
Key points included:

       New sign type: Restricted to three per block face.
       Location: Limited to Center Street between 500 West and 200 East.
       Neon signs: Would not be allowed.
       Purpose: Intended to support businesses and create a nightlife atmosphere.

Some councilors expressed support for maintaining the historical look and character of the area.
Questions were raised regarding how to determine which businesses would be permitted to
install marquee signs, given the restrictions. Ardmore clarified that the permits would be issued
on a first-come, first-served basis. However, he noted they do not anticipate high demand due to
the expense involved.

Item 3: A discussion regarding an ordinance amending Provo City Code
regarding exceptions to signs and outdoor advertising regulations. Citywide
Application. (PLOTA20240187) 0:48:55

Scott Johnson presented proposed amendments to address electronic campaign signs in
residential areas where other electronic signs are not allowed. He explained that a recent
complaint highlighted a gap in the current code. The amendment would prohibit electronic or
animated campaign signs in residential neighborhoods.

Brian Jones reformatted the language in the amendment to clarify that the restrictions apply to
public rights-of-way, animated signs, and electronic display signs.

Item 4: A discussion regarding an ordinance amending the Zone Map
classification of real property, generally located at 5544 N River Run Drive, from
the OSPR Zone to the Medium Density Residential Zone. Riverbottoms
Neighborhood. (PLRZ20230328) 0:55:19

Aaron Ardmore presented a request for a zone change for 5544 N River Run Drive, from the
Open Space, Parks, and Recreation (OSPR) Zone to the Medium Density Residential Zone. This
request is part of the River District residential project. Ardmore noted that the project plan had
been approved by the Planning Commission and includes proffers for trail easements.

Some councilors expressed disappointment about the inclusion of rental units rather than
homeownership opportunities. Ardmore clarified that the trail easement and landscaping would
be built and maintained by the developers or the homeowners' association, with public access
ensured.

Item 5: A discussion regarding a resolution approving the appropriation of
$13,743 in the General Fund for a contractual sales tax increment post-
performance payment to Day’s Market. (24-011) 1:14:45

Keith Morey presented a resolution to appropriate $13,743 for a contractual sales tax increment
payment to Day's Market. He explained that this was part of an agreement made several years
ago to share back a portion of increased sales tax revenue from improvements made to the
shopping center. This is the fifth year of payments, bringing the total to $64,902 to date.

Councilors expressed support for the program and its benefits to the neighborhood, though they
noted that the state has since eliminated the ability for cities to make such agreements.

Item 6: A discussion regarding a resolution approving and adopting the
Amended Project Area Plan for the Freedom Plaza Community Development
Project Area. (24-078) 1:20:25

Keith Morey presented an amended project area plan for the Freedom Plaza Community
Development Area. He explained this amendment was necessary to correct an oversight from
when the project area boundaries were originally defined, ensuring they align with the
development's actual location. As an RDA function, this allows the city to inform other taxing
entities of the intent; however, further conversations will be necessary to finalize interlocal
agreements within the area.

During the council discussion, Council members sought clarification on why the project area
boundaries had not been updated earlier and what steps would be taken moving forward. Morey
elaborated that the boundaries initially included the 4th District Court building and required
adjustment due to subsequent development changes. He also emphasized that while the area
designation is discretionary to the RDA, conversations with taxing entities, such as the school
district and county, will be critical to outline and update tax agreements based on the corrected
boundaries.

Morey clarified that this amendment was an RDA function, and the city retains discretion to
notify other taxing entities of the intent. Further conversations with taxing entities would be
needed to finalize interlocal agreements within the area.

Item 7: A discussion regarding a resolution approving the appropriation of
$214,600 in the tax increment affordable housing set aside fund received from
redevelopment projects last fiscal year. (24-081) 1:25:25

Keith Morey presented a resolution to appropriate $214,600 in tax increment funds for affordable
housing. He explained that the funds came from redevelopment projects, such as The Mix at
River's Edge, which chose to contribute to an affordable housing fund instead of including
affordable units on-site.

The funds would be used to purchase property at Center Street and 2000 West for an affordable
housing project with the Housing Authority. Morey noted that they anticipate receiving similar
funds annually from The Mix for approximately 20 years.

Item 8: A discussion regarding a resolution of the Redevelopment Agency of
Provo City designating a Survey Area for the proposed City Center Community
Reinvestment Project Area. (24-082) 1:31:55

Keith Morey presented a proposal to designate a survey area for a potential City Center
Community Reinvestment Project Area. He explained this was a preliminary step to create
flexibility for future redevelopment options, particularly around the old city hall block.
