2. Planning Matters
First Reading:
a. ZT-19-002, Amendments to Title 19 Regarding Noticing and Hearing

Requirements for Land Use Hearings

Francis Lilly said he would like to make changes to the processes for the Land Use
Hearing Officer. That office is the official appeal authority designated by State statute
and Millcreek code. The Hearing Officer works in a quasi-judicial manner on variances
or on decisions Millcreek officials/staff make. Millcreek has established the position as a
single person rather than a Board of Adjustments. There are a range of decisions that can
be appealed, and the Hearing Officer can make decisions on: conditional uses; variances;
decisions made by the Planning Director with single family developments and on parking
requirements; certain Planning Commission decisions; certain approvals of lots and
buildings on private rights-of-way; clarification on zone boundaries and decisions on the
Geologic Hazards Ordinance; and alterations, enlargements, relocations, or
reconstructions of a noncomplying structure or a structure occupying a nonconforming
use.

Lilly said there have been recent projects that have come before the Hearing Officer that
have had implications on surrounding neighbors. He said the hearing is not subject to
public clamor, but the neighbors should be aware of the appeal. There are certain
determinations that need to be made or be done by the Hearing Officer that does not
create an unreasonable burden on surrounding property owners and is the intent of the
land use code. In the proposed ordinance, staff had recommended mailing notice of the

hearing to property owners within 150 feet of properties with land use appeals though the
Millcreek Community Council and Planning Commission recommended that the notice
be to properties within 300 feet. The proposed code allows the Hearing Officer to make
two additional findings; that enlargements meet the standards of the Residential
Compatibility Overlay Zone (RCOZ) special exception building envelope requirements,
and to empower the Officer to impose limitations on the issuance of a permit to minimize
adverse effects to the neighborhood. He said making the power explicit will result in
better applications and stronger grounds for approval/denial. The code also adds the
language that a person seeking to participate in a land use hearing must file an application
to intervene within 5 days of the hearing. Lilly said he worked with the City Attorney on
the ordinance. He said he tried to strike a balance between the quasi-judicial nature of the
hearings and allow potentially affected parties to communicate on an application that
goes to the Hearing Officer.

Mayor Silvestrini said there was previously an issue with a nonconforming structure and
this ordinance amendment aims to fix that loophole. There was no mechanism to provide
notification to the neighbors without an ordinance. He said in that specific circumstance
the Hearing Officer approved the application because there was no reason in code to deny
it. State law does not allow the public to make comment on the appeal, the decision is to
be made on strict legal requirements. The public can legally intervene, but this code
requires them to provide notice and their basis for standing which then allows the Officer
to be ready for their argument. The RCOZ standards would also be applicable with the
ordinance. The Council said they were okay with the 300-foot notice boundary. Mayor
Silvestrini said the public is limited on what they can raise in the quasi-judicial
proceeding. Council Member Marchant asked about the cost of the noticing the public.
Lilly said the applicant would pay for it. John Brems said the ordinance is a good solution
to a problem but creating more rules could create new problems.

3. Business Matters
3.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-28, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement with Salt Lake County for the Second Amended Agreement
for Addressing
Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek contracts with Salt Lake County for addressing services
for new developments. The resolution is a renewal of the agreement at the same cost.

Council Member Jackson moved to adopt Resolution 19-28, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement with Salt Lake County for the Second Amended Agreement for
Addressing. Council Member Catten seconded. The City Recorder called for the vote. The
motion passed unanimously.

3.2 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 19-38, Amending the Financial
Administrative Ordinance Creating a Director of Finance

Mayor Silvestrini said the ordinance creates the position of Director of Finance, which
Laurie Johnson currently holds. The ordinance also allows the position to sign for certain
things, do minor purchasing, and adds the position to the procurement code.

Council Member Uipi moved to approve item 3.2. Council Member Marchant seconded.
The City Recorder called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.
