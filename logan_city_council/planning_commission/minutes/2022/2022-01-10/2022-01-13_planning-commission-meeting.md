 PC 21-069 Lloyd Estates -Continued from the December 9th meeting [Subdivision Permit]
 Riley Duke/Cache Cal Holdings, authorized agent/owner, request a subdivision permit to
 subdivide 7.23 acres into 44 single family home building lots located at approximately 200 West
 600 North in the Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) zone; TIN 05-045-0016. (Bridger
 Neighborhood)


STAFF: T. Rice reviewed the request for a 44 lot single family residential subdivision on
approximately 8.5 acres, in the NR-6 zone. The Land Development Code (LDC) permits
subdivisions within the NR-6 zone at a density of six (6) units to the acre and minimum lots sizes of
6,000 SF. The LDC sets an average minimum lot width of 50 feet. The proposal shows lots sizes
vary from 6,000 SF to 15,162 SF, are 50’ in width and contain lot depths that are between 82’ and
120’. The proposed density of the subdivision is 5.17 units per acre
The proposal includes two new city streets connecting to 200 West. The Land Development Code
(LDC) 17.30.170 requires subdivisions with greater than 20 lots to have at least three (3) street
connections. As proposed with only two street connection and no clear third connection, the
project does not meet the minimum LDC code requirements for street connectivity. As conditioned
with full street stubs, better street grid conformity and street connectivity, the project meets the
LDC requirements.

PROPONENT: Riley Duke, proponent, indicated that their surveyor was redrawing the
preliminary plat to meet the City’s right of requirements.

PUBLIC: Paul Sanders asked that the existing irrigation lines and irrigation flows be protected, and
that the stormwater draining into the ponds be accounted for. M. DeSimone responded that the


irrigation company will need to agree to the preliminary plat and sign off on it, the proponent will need
to complete a wetland delineation of the site and will need to design for all stormwater on the site.

COMMISSION: There was discussion about the requirement for a third access into the subdivision.
The preliminary plat identifies a stub on the west side of the property, but it is unclear as to where it
leads. T. Rice stated that the proponent said one owner agreed with a road while the other property
owners of the Cottonwood Park Subdivision were proceeding with their recently approved
subdivision and did not want to accommodate the road. There was discussion about the LDC
permitting a stub for a future roadway as a third point of access. A cul-de-sac or turn-around will
need to be installed to accommodate emergency vehicles. Mr. Duke stated that they could tie their
third access into Crystal Avenue. Staff pointed out that Crystal Avenue was not a public roadway,
but a driveway. Staff also suggested to the Commission that they request written confirmation from
the adjoining owners on whether they will agree or not on the road through their property.

The Commission directed the applicant to come back with a redesigned plat that addresses the
right of way requirements and the third point of access.

MOTION: Commissioner Guth moved to continue PC 21-069 to the January 27, 2022 meeting so
the proponent can address the access issue, irrigation, and road right of requirements.
Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion.

Moved: J. Guth                 Seconded: R. Croshaw                    Approved: 5-0

Yea: Dickinson, Croshaw, Guth, Heare, Lewis                    Nay:            Abstain:


 PC 22-003 2200 South [Zone Change] Joshua Low/David Nielsen Trust, authorized agent/owner
 are requesting a zone change of 11.13-acres from Resource Conservation (RC) to Commercial
 (COM) located at 1262 West 2200 South; TIN: 03-007-0010; -0011; -0023. (Woodruff
 Neighborhood)

STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request to rezone approximately 11.62-acres (three parcels) from
RC to COM along the south side of 2200 South near the intersection of Highway 89/91. The land
is being used for agricultural and residential uses with the existing home being built in 1955. Other
than two agricultural accessory buildings and two ponds, the property is currently vacant. The
Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), adopted in 2008, identifies this property as Commercial (COM).
and considering its proximity to highway, the current low-density residential land use is not a viable
long-term solution. As development on these properties is proposed, the South Highway Overlay,
sensitive lands and other constraints will need to be addressed during the design review phase.
Being surrounding on two sides by the City of Nibley and existing development, the site layout and
circulation will need to account for these issues. With the 2200 South corridor developing rapidly,
this property will likely be developed in this near future. Staff is comfortable with the rezone request
as this is consistent with the Logan City FLUP and is an appropriate land use considering the
context of the area. The rezone is consistent with the existing city pattern of aligning the bulk of
commercial zoning along Main Street and Highway 89/91

R. Holley indicated the only comments received were from Nibley City outlining their concerns
about a shared sewer line running through the site and the existing ponds collecting stormwater
run-off. R. Holley stated these were technical issues and would be addressed during the design
review process, but at this time, there is not a pending project.


Commissioner Guth asked about whether the site was in a floodplain. R. Holley stated the site is
not located in a floodplain, and there is adequate land on the site outside of the wetland and
drainage areas that can be developed.

PROPONENT: Josh Low, proponent, confirmed what R. Holley presented.

PUBLIC: There were no public comments made.

COMMISSION: The Commission members agreed that the proposal was consistent with the FLUP.

MOTION: Commissioner Heare moved to forward a recommendation of approval to the municipal
council for a zone change on PC 22-003 with the presented findings for approval. Commissioner
Croshaw seconded the motion.

RECOMMENDED FINDINGS FOR APPROVAL
1. The Logan City FLUP identifies the area as COM.
2. This area is positioned along Highway 89/91 with commercial uses to the north.
3. Infrastructure and access is located nearby and has the ability to adequately serve the future
   development of this property.
4. Design incompatibles with surrounding areas can be mitigated through Design Review
   processes.

Moved: K. Heare               Seconded: R. Croshaw                  Approved: 5-0

Yea: Dickinson, Croshaw, Guth, Heare, Lewis                 Nay:            Abstain:


 PC 22-004 Costco Wholesale Warehouse and Fuel Facility [Design Review & Conditional Use
 Permit] Sean Anderson, Barghausen Consulting Engineers/Jason Larsen, Blue Springs Business
 Park, authorized agent/owner, request a design review and conditional use permit to construct a
 new 160,526 SF Warehouse/Sales and Service (Big Box) building for Costco Wholesale and a new
 8,648 SF fuel facility. This project is located at 1160 North 1000 West in the Industrial Park (IP)
 zone; TIN: 05-050-0006. (Bridger Neighborhood)


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a new 160,500 SF Costco Wholesale building, fuel
station, surface parking lots, stormwater retention and landscaping. The big-box building is
positioned on the east side of the 17.65-acre property with the parking areas and the fueling
station adjacent to 1000 West.

R. Holley presented the site design, covered access, landscaping, etc. Listed two outstanding
issues: first is pedestrian connection north to 1200 North. Second, the proposed building does
not meet the design requirement for 30% transparency along the street facing façade. R. Holley
explained to the Commission that LDC 17.43.080 gives the Commission discretion in evaluation
projects, especially as it relates to the design of industrial buildings. R. Holley explained the
differences in industrial uses and buildings, explained that this project is a warehouse and is not
a restaurant or office space on Main Street. R. Holley went through the landscaping plan and
how it will buffer the building from the road, explained the proposed landscape buffer adjacent to
the building, and explained the building is proposed to be setback almost 600’ from 10th West.
These are mitigating factors to help the Commission in adjusting the design requirements for
certain types of projects.


PROPONENT: John Ellingsen, Costco Real Estate, spoke about the number of Logan residents with
Costco memberships, the number of Costco locations throughout the state, and the fact that they
will create approximately 250 well-paying jobs in the area. Mr. Ellingsen stated they were in
agreement with the recommendations of staff and will provide a pedestrian connection to the north.
Steve Bullock, Architect for Costco, said that their goal is too keep the building looking like a
warehouse, just an attractive warehouse, and consistent with the Costco brand. He indicated that
they had been meeting with staff for several months on site layout, design, and conditions. He stated
that the landscaping proposed for use as a buffer against the building as shown on the plan sets
represents approximately 10 years of growth and would consist of evergreens in order to soften the
buildings appearance. He also stated they are willing to comply with Commission requirements on
landscaping installation and size of trees to be planted.

PUBLIC: David Zook stated his concerns about traffic patterns on 1000 West, the impacts to future
traffic volumes, and the proposed entrance.

Layne Beck talked about 1000 West and it being a bypass for residents north and south of Logan.
He stated that Logan should eliminate the proposed access onto 1000 West.

Janet Beck spoke about her concern that this project will disturb the traffic flow on 1000 West,
concerns about the location of the gas stations, and suggested rotating the building 180 degrees to
face east instead of west.

Jason Larsen, landowner, said that they made a conscious decision to eliminate existing and
potential accesses onto 1000 West at the new FedEx building located south of Costco & 1000 North,
limit access to 1000 West from their proposed retail pads, and only install one entrance into Costco
off of 1000 West. They will also be constructing 1200 North on the north side of Costco. They met
with UDOT several times to design and approve the entrance location. Mr. Larsen read a letter of
support from UDOT indicating their willingness to permit the location of the proposed entrance with
the proposed median & porkchop limiting vehicular access to right in/right out only. He also
mentioned that they have heard nothing of UDOT decreasing the speed limit on 1000 West.

COMMISSION: Regina asked Bill Young to address the comments made regarding 1000 West. Bill
Young, Logan City Engineer, stated that they do have a Corridor Access Management Agreement
with UDOT that states there will be traffic lights at 1000 North, 1400 North and 1800 North. B. Young
also stated that the construction of the entrance lines up with 1100 North and the new roadway at
1200 North are consistent with the grid patterns required of new development. B. Young stated that
improvements will be made to the intersection of 1000 W/1000 N to facilitate the flow of traffic. B.
Young stated the project and proposed improvements are consistent with UDOT requirements, City
requirements, and the Access Management Agreement between all parties.

Commissioner Heare asked about whether the Commission could prohibit the proposed entrance.
Staff stated that access control onto 1000 West, which is a State Highway, is controlled by UDOT
and is not within their purview as a Commission.

There was discussion regarding the 30% transparency. Commissioner Dickenson stated she wasn’t
comfortable with a deviation, as that would set a precedent, and was not something they normally
do. R. Holley explained their discretion does allow for a deviation to design requirements based on
the project type, site context, surrounding land uses, etc. Commissioner Guth asked about the
landscaping and whether it was, as shown, actually getting them to 30%. The Commission agreed
on changing the condition of approval #2 regarding transparency from 30% to 20%. Staff suggested
that they require the proponent utilize a combination of three specific elements: landscaping


screening, architectural detailing, and building fenestration to get to the 20% requirement. The
commission agreed and directed the proponent to work with staff on meeting this requirement.

MOTION: Commissioner Guth moved to conditionally approve a design review & conditional use
permit for PC 22-004 with the amended conditions of approval and findings for approval as listed
below. Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion.

4. The project shall provide at least 321 parking stalls and a bike rack.

    submitted for approval to the Community Development Department prior to the issuance of

    a. Open Space and Useable Outdoor areas shall total a minimum of 153,766 SF (20% of

    b. If parking stalls counts exceed 125%, 36 SF of interior parking lot landscaping shall be
         provided per parking stall.
    c. A total of 353 trees and 882 shrubs, perennials and ornamental grasses shall be

     aesthetical issues and requirements if they are consistent with surrounding areas and do not
     compromise future approvals.
3.   The proposed project provides required off-street parking.
4.   The proposed project complies with maximum height, density and building design, open
     space standards and is in conformance with Title 17.
5.   The project met the minimum public noticing requirements of the Land Development Code and
     the Municipal Code.
6.   1000 North and 1000 West provides access and is adequate in size and design to sufficiently
     handle all traffic modes and infrastructure related to the land use.
