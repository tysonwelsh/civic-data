6:00 p.m. – WORKSHOP
   • DISCUSS DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENTS.

Wendelin Knobloch said changes in state law during the most recent legislative session
have made development agreements once again available as a tool cities can use to
guide development.

In a recent internal meeting that included the City Manager, City Attorney, and other
senior staff the group discussed how to utilize development agreements most effectively
in the context of a zone change.

Considering the resource intensity development agreements bring with them due to
review and coordination time between the developer and several city departments, city
staff would like to explore how the City Council feels about the following process for zone
changes, which could eliminate unnecessary development agreements:

   1. Hold a City Council Workshop in which the developer presents a concept plan that
      illustrates the development proposal a zone change would facilitate.

   2. The City Council requests a development agreement during the City Council
      Workshop if they think they may be interested in the zone change and require an
      agreement.

Adam Olsen said Midvale City was awarded financial assistance through a Transportation
and Land Use Connection (TLC) grant for a Parks and Open Space Master Plan. This will
be the first of its kind for Midvale, providing comprehensive analysis and
recommendations for the City’s parks and open space network, including preparation of

The scope of the project includes, among other things, an analysis of existing conditions,
future demands analysis, park and open space classification system, an implementation
plan, and an Impact Fee Facilities Plan (IFFP). Opportunity for public input and project
updates to City Council are included in both the scope and proposed timeline.

The Transportation and Land Use Connection Consultant Agreement outlines services to
be provided with the Midvale Parks and Open Space Master Plan. A page detailing the
budget and the parties responsible is included. As the City was awarded a TLC grant
through WFRC, WFRC will contribute a large portion of the budget, with the City
contributing roughly a third ($40,000).

The consultant chosen for the project (MHTN) will work under the guidance of both WFRC
and the City; WFRC disbursing payment as benchmarks in the project are accomplished.
The Mayor’s signature on the attached agreement is recognition that the City understands
the parameters of the project and will work with both WFRC and the consultant in
preparation of the Parks and Open
Space Master Plan.

As noted above, the City’s required match is $40,000. This amount has been included in
the FY 24 budget and carried over to the FY 25 budget in anticipation of the plan.

The Parks and Open Space Master Plan and associated IFFP will ultimately be approved
by the City Council.

Erinn Summers said the State Homeless Shelter Cities Mitigation (HSCM) Fund was
established in 2018 through legislation (HB 499) with the objective to alleviate the impact
of year-round and emergency shelters on the host cities throughout the State. The HSCM
Restricted Account is funded through sales tax revenue from cities that do not host eligible
shelters and direct funding from the state. Host cities must reapply for HSCM funds each
year, and funds awarded must be used within the fiscal year in which they are awarded.
The Office of Homeless Services (OHS) is responsible for reviewing the applications from
eligible municipalities to ensure funding requests are directed to eligible services (public
safety, direct services, or emergency services which mitigate the impacts of the eligible
shelter on the municipality) and disbursing funds to eligible entities (municipalities and
designated service providers, like shelter operators) from the Homeless Shelter Cities
Mitigation Fund, following the guidelines and formula outlined in Utah State Code 35A-
16-4.

This fiscal year, Midvale City (City) received $3,597,026.71, which was approved to fund
the FTE for 9 Shelter Resource Officers, 0.5 victim advocate, insurance and supplies
needed for these FTEs, a weekly clean-up of the 7200 S Corridor, underpass
improvements on Center Street, and $200,000 to pilot a business and property
improvements reimbursement program (Reimbursement Program). The Reimbursement
Program is intended to support businesses, within a ½ mile radius of the homeless
shelter, mitigate the impacts of homelessness or transient activity on their businesses
through the use of reimbursement for the costs associated with improvements to the
property.

The City has been working to connect businesses along the 7200 S Corridor with
resources which help address issues they are facing, especially around crime and
transient activity. Through discussions with various businesses along the corridor, staff
identified a gap in support needed for businesses: mitigation improvements and damage
repair are costly and many of these businesses are looking for financial support to pursue
these improvements. There are no existing grant programs available in Utah to support

homeless and transient mitigation efforts for businesses. The Office of Homeless
Services and the Utah Homeless Services Board have approved this grant program for
Midvale City with the FY 2025 Mitigation Funds.

The grants will be awarded to eligible projects on a first-come, first-serve basis, until June
1, 2025. Each awarded grant will cover improvements up to $20,000, while funding lasts.
Midvale City expects to authorize up to 10 grant awards. Businesses will submit an
application with proposed project(s) – demonstrating how the project(s) will deter
unwanted behavior specifically related to homelessness or transient activity, or
demonstrating how the project(s) will repair property damage that is transient related.
Once a complete application is submitted, City staff will review the application within five
business days.

The following improvements will be eligible under the Midvale City Mitigation Grant for
Businesses:
       -     Security cameras (expansions, updates, etc.)
       -     Fencing (type of fencing should be able to withstand damage)
       -     Landscaping (no rocks, no areas of overgrowth, etc)
       -     Add Lighting
       -     Adapting or removal exterior spigots and electrical outlets
       -     Security guards
       -     Bathroom alarms or updated locks (such as Halo, Brave Sensors, which
             detect drug use and alert the business)
       -     No Loitering or Trespassing signs
       -     Damages to the property caused by transient individuals (businesses must
             be able to reasonably demonstrate that the damage is homeless or transient
             related, through police reports, camera footage, or other means).

Applicants are able to request reimbursement for other improvements but will be expected
to show how the improvement will mitigate the impact of homelessness or transient
activity on their business.

Grantees must prove that they are legally able to make the improvements (they are the
property owners, or they have permission from the property owner to do so). Businesses
will have to follow all permitting and zoning requirements for their property. Grants will
only be awarded to applicants who are able to use licensed contractors and professionals
for the project(s), when applicable.

Staff will review the application for completeness and eligibility. For businesses
determined to be eligible, staff will make a written recommendation to the City Manager
for final approval. The business will be notified of the final decision. If the application is
approved, the City will reserve the grant awardee up to $20,000, and the business will
sign a grant agreement with the City. Grantees must have an active business license and
be in good standing with the City throughout the term of the agreement. Additionally,
grantees agree to enforce Midvale and State code and regulations on trespassing,
loitering, camping, and traffic obstruction. Businesses will then implement or complete the

    • Approve the process for addressing tie votes.
Matt Dahl said state law allows for up to two rounds of voting by the City Council to select
the mayor. If no candidate receives a majority in the initial vote, a coin toss may be
required to narrow the field to two candidates. If three candidates receive votes, the one
with the fewest votes can be eliminated. The second vote then selects the mayor from
the two remaining candidates.

State law requires that the municipal clerk to use a coin toss to reduce the field of
candidates to two candidates in the event of an initial vote which generates three or more
tied candidates (e.g., vote distributions like 2-1-1-1 or 1-1-1-1-1). However, state law does
not clearly address how to structure the coin toss for three-way or greater ties. To address
this, staff proposes a round-robin coin toss tournament among the tied candidates. After
each round, the candidate(s) with the fewest wins would be eliminated, continuing until
only two candidates remain for the final vote. This method aims to be equitable while
aligning with the intent of state law.

        A. DISCUSSION OF AMENDING VETO AUTHORITY IN THE HARVEST
           FESTIVAL COMMITTEE BYLAWS.
Erinn Summers said Harvest Days, a longstanding Midvale tradition, was originally
started by the city's farming community as a celebration of the fall harvest. The local
Midvale Kiwanis Club sponsored the first Harvest Days in 1938. Supported by other civic
organizations and the cooperation of surrounding towns, the Midvale Kiwanis Club
organized a parade, queen contest, car raffle and fireworks show. More than 85 years
later the Midvale Harvest Days celebration is enjoyed by thousands of residents from
Midvale and surrounding communities.

For many years prior to the creation of the Harvest Days Festival Committee (Committee)
in March of 2022, the Harvest Days Festival (Festival) has been an administrative
responsibility of the City, under the direction and management of the City Manager. The
event was a huge undertaking for City staff each year. The City Council established the
Harvest Days Festival Committee (Committee) through the Harvest Days Festival
Committee Bylaws (Bylaws) to support the City in planning and executing the festival.

Under the Bylaws, the Committee is made up of individuals who live or work in Midvale.
It consists of up to 4 members and 2 alternate members, appointed by the Mayor, and a
Committee Chair who is appointed by the Mayor and confirmed by the Council. The
Committee members and Chair work with ex officio members, including the City Manager,
City staff, up to 2 City Council members, and an event planner. Only Committee members
are able to vote on Committee actions, with alternates voting the in absence of other
Committee members. The City Manager has the authority to veto any, and all, decisions
made by the Committee, if deemed that the decision(s) are against the policies or best
interest of the City. Under the current Bylaws, vetoes are at the sole discretion of the City
Manager.

On October 15th, City Council instructed City staff to investigate options for a formalized
appeal process for vetoes to be added to the Bylaws. City staff has prepared three options
for City Council to consider and to facilitate discussion on this topic.
