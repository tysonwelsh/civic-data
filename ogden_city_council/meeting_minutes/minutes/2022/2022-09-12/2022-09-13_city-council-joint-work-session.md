Agency (RDA) meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; review the Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Fourth Quarter Capital Improvement

Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Fourth Quarter Capital Improvement Plan Report

Comptroller Stout used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to briefly review the Fiscal Year (FY) 2021-2022 fourth
quarter Capital Improvement Plan Report. The report identifies the amount funded in the current year and shows the total amount
available for expenditures for all General Fund and Enterprise Fund projects. Capital Projects at Business Depot Ogden (BDO) are
completed by the City’s partner, The Boyer Company, and are included on a separate report. Throughout the presentation, there
was brief input from various City employees that were responsible for managing certain CIP projects.

Proposed Rezone of Property at 539 22"4 Street

Deputy Planning Manager Simpson explained the City’s Planning Division has proposed the rezone of the southern
portion of property located at 539 22" Street from the current Central Business District/Conditional Overlay (CBD/CO) zoning
classification to the Residential Multi-family East Central (R-3EC) zoning classification. The property is being rezoned to allow
the owner to comply with the requirements of the R-3EC zoning with regard to the renovation of the four-plex on the property.
The portion being rezoned was previously owned by Midtown Clinic and zoned CBD/CO to comply with their development plan;
however, those plans were not completed, and the property was lost in a tax sale. The proposed rezone would allow consistent
zoning on the subject property.

Proposed Zone Text Amendment — Business Exchange Historic (BEH)
Zone

Planning Manager Brierley explained a petition has been filed by the development firm Q Factor to amend the City’s
Business Exchange Historic (BEH) zone to allow the development of the area around and connected to the historic Stock
Exchange Building in the Trackline Economic Development Area. The proposed amendments would reduce side and rear yard
setbacks, increase the maximum height, amend requirements for several architectural elements, and propose an option for reduced
parking requirements. The City’s Business Exchange Historical (BEH) and Business Exchange Industrial (BEI) zones were
created in 2015 as part of the redevelopment of the historic stockyards in west Ogden. As development has taken place since that
time, a number of amendments have been made to the BEH and BEI zones to accommodate changing uses and needs. Mr.
Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the applicant’s redevelopment plans for the Stock Exchange
Building within the BEH zone, noting that much of the proposed redesign is harmonious with existing development in the area.
He concluded the Planning Commission reviewed the proposal at its August 3, 2022 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of
approval. Commissioner Schade voted against the recommendation indicating that he was uncomfortable with the proposed
height.

Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered upon the precedent set by the requirements that were placed on
other new developments in the area and whether it is problematic to waive such requirements for this new petitioner; namely,
height restrictions were placed on previous developments, but this new application would be allowed to build a taller building.
The Council expressed concern for previous developers given that they complied with design and development standards, but
those standards will be waived for anew developer if the application is approved. They discussed the fact that the Stock Exchange
Building is considered the ‘gem’ of the entire area and Council member Hyer stated that it would be helpful to hear input from the
City’s Landmarks Commission regarding this proposal. Council Deputy Director Symes stated that before an action item is placed
on a Council meeting agenda, Council staff will solicit a recommendation from the Landmarks Commission. Council member
Hyer stated it will be difficult for the Landmarks Commission to provide a recommendation without having a rendering of the
proposed redevelopment project. Mr. Symes stated that a rendering would need to be created to submit to Landmarks
Commission; that rendering would then also be made available to the Council. Council member Hyer and Acting Chair Lopez
stated that their greatest concern relates to the proposed amendments regarding building height; they indicated they feel they do
not have enough information to make a decision on the application.

Council member White stated she is also concerned about the building height as well as parking accommodations in the
area. She added it would be helpful to have information about the financial contribution to the City has made to the project thus
far.

Mr. Brierley thanked the Council for their feedback and assured them that the developer is very interested in preserving
the historic character of the building and the surrounding area; he will provide the Council’s input to the developer for
consideration.
