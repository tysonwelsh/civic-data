2.     ADMINISTRATIVE ITEMS
       a. Council Leadership
Council Office Director Alan R. Anderson oriented the Council with the process for choosing a new
Council Chair and Vice Chair. He outlined powers and duties of Council Chair and Vice Chair as stated
in City Code. Each Council Member successfully logged into the voting machines provided and
participated in a practice voting round, which resulted in a three-way tie. Assistant City Attorney Duncan
Murray reviewed rules and procedures for a tie vote. The three options were reduced to two with the roll
of a die, and the Council participated in a practice tie-breaking voting round. The Council and Staff worked
through a few technical difficulties. Mr. Anderson further clarified the Council leadership process.

Chair McConnehey asked Council Members to declare if they did or did not want to be considered for the
position of Council Chair. Council Members Green, Worthen, Pack, Lamb, and Whitelock stated they did
not want to be considered for the position of Council Chair. Council Members Jacob and McConnehey
declared a desire to be considered.

Staff electronically delivered a ballot, and Council Members voted for the position of Council Chair. Mr.
Anderson reported that Zach Jacob received five votes and Chris McConnehey received two votes.
Council Member McConnehey congratulated Chair-Elect Jacob.

Citizen Comments:
Alexandra Eframo, a West Jordan resident, spoke of an incident in which she personally called Council
Member Worthen on the phone. She said she was disappointed that Council Member Worthen hung up
on her and did not call her back. Ms. Eframo insisted she did not call Council Member Worthen a name
as Council Member Worthen claimed and stated Council Member Worthen was a liar.

Council Member Lamb called point of order. He said he did not believe the citizen comment period was
the appropriate time to make accusations, and asked that Ms. Eframo keep comments short. City Attorney
Rob Wall advised the Council to allow Ms. Eframo to continue her comments. Council Member
McConnehey stated Ms. Eframo had two minutes remaining to comment.

Ms. Eframo repeated that Council Member Worthen hung up on her and would not return her phone call.
Ms. Eframo repeated her insistence that she did not call Council Member Worthen a name, and again
called her a liar.

Council Member Worthen called point of order. She said she did not believe accusatory comments that
attacked any Council Member should be allowed. Mr. Wall advised the Council to allow Ms. Eframo to
continue, stating she had a right to speak irrespective of the truth of the comments. Based on advice from
legal counsel, Council Member McConnehey allowed Ms. Eframo to proceed for the remaining minute
thirty-seconds.

Ms. Eframo said she needed help against the illegal accusations. She stated Council Member Worthen
hung up on her, claiming Ms. Eframo called her a “pig”. She stated Council Members needed to listen to
negative remarks and should return phone calls and asked for help with the matter.

Council Member McConnehey closed the citizen comment period at 7:17 p.m.

Council Member Whitelock said she believed Council Member Worthen should be given the opportunity to
respond if she desired. She expressed the opinion that behavior taking away from the decorum of Council
meetings should be not tolerated, and suggested appropriate steps should be added to Council Rules.

Council Member Worthen said she knew she was not the only individual who had dealt with such an attack
from Ms. Eframo. She said she had recorded proof that what Ms. Eframo said was not true, and said she had
tried to work with Ms. Eframo. Council Member Worthen said she appreciated that Ms. Eframo attended
Council meetings but did not appreciate disrespectful behavior. She implored Ms. Eframo to speak civilly and
graciously.

       c. Religious Freedom Day
Council Office Director Alan R. Anderson said an individual contacted the Council with information
regarding National Religious Freedom Day (established in 1992). Utah established a State Freedom of
Religion Day in 2015. Mr. Anderson said the City of South Jordan established its own annual Religious
Freedom Day in 2020. He said the request was for West Jordan to establish an annual Religious Freedom
Day on January 16th. Mr. Anderson presented proposed Resolution No. 21-002 to proclaim January 16,
2021 as Religious Freedom Day in West Jordan.

Chair Jacob suggested altering the proposed Resolution to proclaim January 16 of each year as Religious
Freedom Day in West Jordan. He expressed support for the proposed Resolution. Council Member
Whitelock said she believed it was important to recognize that citizens could be different and still get
along and be peaceable.

Council Member Green said he spent 39 years in the United States Army defending the Constitution,
which includes more than one civil right. He expressed concern that the proposed Resolution would
distract from the City and involve the City in things they could not control. He listed many civil rights,
and asked if the City was going to establish special days recognizing all of them. He said he would
probably not vote against the proposed Resolution, but questioned whether it would open a Pandora’s box.
Council Member Green said he would rather establish a West Jordan Read-the-Bill-of-Rights-and-
Understand-the-Constitution Day.

Council Member Lamb said he had been unaware of the many days like National Donut Day until the
advent of social media. He said he was not against freedom of religion, but was not sure creating an annual
holiday was appropriate. He asked how the proposed Religious Freedom Day would be celebrated.
