22            EntryPoint Networks Discussion – Bruce Patterson
23
24   Mayor Staggs explained that EntryPoint Networks had been awarded the RFP to install the
25   backbone fiber network in the City and introduced Bruce Patterson who presented on behalf of
26   EntryPoint Networks.
27
28   Bruce Patterson said the initial contract was divided into phases. Phase One revolved around
29   design and costs to enable City Council to move forward and make decisions. He explained
30   that they had designed the path, contacted UDOT about sharing assets and met with staff. As
31   part of the design process, he said they created a city wide design of what it would look like to
32   provide fiber to every address.
33
34   Mr. Patterson explained that the market is very volatile for supplies and labor. Mayor Staggs
35   commented that the first quote for fiber cable and ducts was $0.40 per foot and is now at $0.75
36   to $0.80 per foot. Mr. Patterson said that supplies are getting very scarce and it is hard to get
37   labor lined up.
38
39   Mr. Patterson presented a google earth map showing the backbone fiber network plan. The two
40   slides he presented are included in the packet. The backbone fiber network would go down
41   12600 South and make use of existing UDOT conduit which is a great savings to the City, $20
42   per foot. The backbone fiber network ties all of City assets together. There would be a savings
43   between the sports complex and the Public Works building because the City installed an empty
44   conduit when they built the road.
45
46   Mr. Patterson explained that 90% of the costs are for fiber duct and cable. He explained each
47   of the line items in the project summary page. He said this a private network and the speeds
48   should be exponentially better than what the City currently has.
49
50   Councilmember Stewart said that one of the goals for this project was to have a connection in
51   every district and his district is completely cutoff. Mr. Patterson explained that this is a
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   backbone fiber network, however, they could make adjustments which would affect the budget
 2   for this project. Councilmember Stewart said that he believes there is UDOT conduit that goes
 3   right out to Mountain View. Mr. Patterson said that because there is no agreement with UDOT,
 4   these plans are based on best effort assumptions and reliance on a future agreement with
 5   UDOT.
 6
 7   Mayor Staggs explained that the eight locations that were part of the RFP did not include a City
 8   asset west of Bangerter. There is only one conduit on 12600 South left and the City needs to
 9   secure this line which may provide access to Councilmember Stewart’s district.
10
11   Mr. Patterson explained the difficulties in getting an agreement with UDOT. He also
12   recommended only using UDOT’s conduit, not their fiber. The City should own their own fiber to
13   maintain future control.
14
15   Mayor Staggs apologized to Councilmember Stewart for the RFP not including a City asset west
16   of Bangerter. Councilmember Stewart expressed frustration with the backbone fiber network
17   plan and said that the City has failed to deliver to his district on numerous projects. Mr.
18   Patterson responded that the west side is represented and will be serviced.
19
20   Mayor Staggs said the next step would be to issue an RFP to build the network. He
21   commended Mr. Patterson and EntryPoint for designing this network and is excited that the City
22   will have better connection speeds, privacy, and will have the opportunity to go out and service
23   others down the road.
24
25   Mr. Patterson said the total budget comes in lower than what was expected and includes a 14
26   home pilot behind the Public Works building. He explained that building this network allows the
27   City to discontinue paying $90,000 for the outsource connectivity which brings this in-house with
28   a bandwidth that is much more robust and secure.
29
30   Councilmember McCay asked if the backbone network followed along the canal. Mr. Patterson

32   able to use the canal roads for fiber.

 8            Ordinance No. 21-32 – Amendment to the “Standards and Specifications Plan
 9            Manual” amending and adopting a standards for Narrow Trench Excavation –
10            Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director
11
12   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the City maintains a Manual of
13   Standards and Specifications which has details, installation instructions and designs for public
14   infrastructure improvements. The subdivision and site plans that the Commission reviews are
15   required to meet these Standards. When utility work is done in City streets, the work must be
16   permitted and inspected by the City according to the Standards. The standards are adopted by
17   City Council and are part of City Ordinance.
18
19   Mr. Lethbridge said that this proposed change to the standards formalizes City practices for
20   digging and repairing narrow trenches, these narrow trenches are used by utility providers and
21   are often used in city streets or in the parkstrips.
22
23   Mayor Staggs opened the Public Hearing and called for public comments; there being none, he
24   closed the Public Hearing.
25
26   Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 21-32
27   – Amendment to the “Standards and Specifications Plan Manual” amending and
28   adopting a standards for Narrow Trench Excavation. Councilmember Wells SECONDED
29   the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for
30   a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: McCay-yes, Stewart-yes, McDougal-yes, and Wells-
31   yes. The motion passed unanimously.
