 application. Minutes from that meeting are included below. The Planning Commission
 recommended approval of the rezone at Commercial Gateway, and recommended the following
 motion:
I move the City Council adopt Ordinance #22-06, rezoning approximately 0.51-acres of land located at
3479 West 12600 South from Commercial Neighborhood to Commercial Gateway (C-G).

Background

This is a public hearing for an application to Rezone 0.51-acres of land located at 3479 West 12600
South.

Kim Williford, representing Scooters Coffee, is requesting the rezone to accommodate a potential drive-
through coffee shop on the property. The property is currently zoned C-N, Commercial Neighborhood.
The applicant had initially requested a rezone to C-PO, Commercial Professional Office. However, during
the Planning Commission hearing, the Commission discussed the appropriateness of that zone, and
recommended that the Commercial Gateway zone is also consistent with the General Plan and more
compatible with the proposed use of the property. The Commission made that formal recommendation to
the City Council, and the applicant made the change in their requested zoning, as well.

The property is adjacent to 12600 South on the south side. It is 123-ft wide and about 181-ft deep.
The subject property is vacant, it does not have access rights to the private lane to the east.

Nearby properties are a mix of zoning with C-PO to the west and east, and C-PO with Elderly Housing
Overlay to the east. There is also RR-22 to the South, and the area has M-1 and C-R.

As mentioned, the applicant intends to put a drive-through coffee shop on the property. The table of
commercial uses lists “Drive-through windows for food services as NP, or Not Permitted in the C-N zone,
and Conditional in the requested Commercial Gateway Zone.

                                MEETING MINUTES

       E.     REZONE, OF 0.51-ACRES LOCATED AT 3479 WEST 12600 SOUTH.
              APPLICANT – KIM WILLIFORD.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the applicant was participating
remotely with a representative present. He described the location of the proposed rezone
at 12600 South and 3500 West on the south side of the street. The property is on the corner
of a major intersection and has remained vacant because the size and location make it
difficult to develop. The current zoning is Commercial Neighborhood. There is residential
zoning in the back of the property and is zoned RR-22 but the General Plan for the area is
C-PO (Professional Office). Various zoning surrounds the parcel.

The applicant has requested a C-PO zoning change, (Commercial Professional Office), but
C-G (Commercial Gateway) is more applicable to the proposal. Mr. Prestwich discussed
the differences in the zoning and the requirements that are permissible with each. The
applicant is interested in a drive-thru window, which requires a Conditional Use Permit in
both of the proposed zones. The signage is the biggest difference to consider between the
zones. C-PO allows for only the name of the business with no logo or specific font while
C-G allows for the company’s logo to appear on the signage.

The three sample motions included in the packet were described. One was to approve the
application as written, the second is to approve the application with the C-G zoning, and the
third was to deny the motion and keep the zoning as is. The exterior structure would be
similar to what currently surrounds the property but the different zoning would allow the
applicant to achieve the desired outcome.

Notices were sent to property owners within 1,000 feet of the property and staff received no
comments or questions.

Chair Rushton asked about the difference between a drive-thru window for food service and
a drive-in for fast food. Mr. Prestwich explained that a drive-in would be similar to a Sonic.
There are no specific requirements or exclusions set forth in the Code.

Commissioner Russell proposed that a C-G zone would keep future possibilities open. If
the applicant or different owner wanted to make slight changes in the use, they would not
be required to obtain another rezone.

The applicant’s representative addressed the Commission and stated that ownership of the
property is contingent on the rezone. Scooter’s Coffee will consist of a kiosk with no seating
and just a drive-thru. After discussing the proposal with Staff, they were leaning toward the
C-G zone.

The applicant, Kim Williford, identified herself as the Director of Pre-Development for
Scooter’s Coffee. She agreed that the C-G zone would be the best fit for the proposed site.

There were no public comments. Chair Rushton closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Russell agreed that in his opinion the C-G zoning would be the best fit. A
question was raised regarding access. Staff stated that it will be a Utah Department of
Transportation (“UDOT”) controlled access. Ms. Williford stated that they have submitted a
request with UDOT to obtain a permit to access the site. It was noted that the fence on the
south side will be at the discretion of the Planning Commission and addressed at the site
plan stage.

Commissioner Lefgren moved that the Planning Commission recommend
APPROVAL of Application PLZ-21-4013 rezone of approximately 0.51-acres of land
located at 3479 West 12600 South and recommend changing the zone to C-G,
Commercial Gateway. Commissioner Brown seconded the motion. Vote on motion:
Commission Russell-Aye, Commissioner Brown-Aye, Commissioner Cluff-Aye,
Commissioner Lefgren-Aye, Chair Rushton-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
