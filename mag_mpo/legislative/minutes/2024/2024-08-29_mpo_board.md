Wasatch Choice Vision - Utah County Update (00:03:39) LaNiece Davenport introduced Ryan Beck, Cody Lutz, and Sara Beck with Envision Utah. Ryan Beck stated the Wasatch Choice Vision is the start of a vision for the future of Utah County. This is a community shared vision for coordinated transportation investments, development patterns, and economic opportunities. This vision is created by those that live, work, and travel through Utah County. This vision helps identify centers, land uses, and transportation choices. The vision process is broken into four phases with the expectation of the full process to be completed by March 2025. Ryan Beck asked the committee the following questions:● How familiar are you with the existing Wasatch Choice Vision?● How familiar are you with Valley Visioning?● What should the benefits of the vision be?● What ideas would you like to explore as we updated the Wasatch Choice Vision? UTA’s Long Range Plan and Five-Year Service Plan Updates (00:24:49) Bob Allen introduced Dede Murray with UTA. Dede Murray presented the 30 year Long Range Transit Plan, Utah Moves 2050. This responds to the community needs into the future, looking beyond what we have today in budgets. The study started about 2 years ago through public outreach, virtual tours, stakeholder meetings. The plan is broken into three 10-year phases. There is the vision network that is not bound by funding, for when funding is available those projects will be explored. The plan network is financially constrained and is planned within the next 10 years. Dede Murray presented the draft 5-year Service Plan. These updates are driven by improving operator workload and environment, implementing long range transit plans, responding to community feedback, optimizing current systems, and preparing for future services. Page 2 of 5 Dede Murray presented the draft concepts for Utah County, broken up by regions, including routes added, new park and rides, innovative mobility sones, paratransit coverage expanded, and UTA on demand service. The Committee raised the question related to Utah County passing a tax to go to UTA. How are those funds being spent? Are all the funds being applied to projects within Utah County? Of the UTA projects within the state, what percent of those are in Utah County. There was concern raised that little to no planning is shown past Spanish Fork. It was determined it would be beneficial to have UTA back on an MPO Board agenda to address the concerns raised related to funding. Action: Corridor Preservation Project List Update (01:00:12) Calvin Clark presented the updated Corridor Preservation Project map that aligns with the most recent RTP. The Corridor Preservation Project List can only be updated once a year. At this time, and based on the funds in the account, there is little need to prioritize requests for corridor preservation. Action: TIP Modification | Geneva RD UDOT Funding Request (01:04:03) Bob Allen explained that Orem requested money in 2016 and 2018 for the purchase of ROW for 1600 North to five lanes. The project never started due to ownership of the road changing and other outside factors. In 2022 Orem requested funds to connect Lakeview Parkway through the border up to Geneva Road. The scope of that project and UDOTs project raised the cost significantly for UDOT. UDOT is asking the Transportation Commission for $12M and requesting Orem/MAG pay the remaining $6M. Mayor David Young moved that $6,067,000 be transferred to the UDOT Geneva Rd project. Mayor Carolyn Lundberg seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Action: Draft Public Engagement Plan and Title VI Plan Approval (01:14:58) LaNiece Davenport stated as a finding of the Federal Certification Review the Public Engagement Plan (PEP) and Title VI Plan needed to be updated. Both plans have been drafted and the action request of the Committee tonight is to approve the draft to go to public comment. There is a 45 day public comment period for the PEP. The PEP outlines the efforts the MPO will do to make sure to give the public opportunities to participate in the development of various programs. The Title VI plan outlines how MAG MPO will not discriminate on the basis of race, color, or national origin. An analysis was completed within Utah County identifying where the concentrations of seniors, persons with limited English proficiency, and other minority groups to help MAG best serve those areas with their needs. Commissioner Brandon Gordon moved to approve the Draft Public Engagement Plan and Title VI Plan. Mayor Denise Andersen seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Page 4 of 5 The South Valley Transit Study started in summer of 2024 with Locally Preferred Alternative (LPA) refinement and early scoping. This first step is drawing to a close and the following steps include the environmental review in fall/winter 2024-2025 with the preliminary design during 2025-2026.
