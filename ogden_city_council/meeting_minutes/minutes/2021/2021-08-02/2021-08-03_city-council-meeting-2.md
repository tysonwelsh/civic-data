         Division of Unclaimed Property - Efforts to Return Assets to Ogden
         Residents
                    A representative from the Division of Unclaimed Property, State Treasurer's Office, will report on efforts to reunite
         Ogden residents with unclaimed property being held in trust by the State of Utah.
                    Utah State Treasurer Marlo Oaks and Administrator Dennis Johnston approached the Council and stated the goal of this
         agenda item is to inform residents about their efforts to return unclaimed property to Ogden residents. Each year, millions of
         dollars in lost or unclaimed money is turned over to the State of Utah. This includes unclaimed wages, savings and checking
         accounts, insurance proceeds, customer deposits or overpayments, credit balances, refunds, money orders, and even safe deposit
         box contents. Once property is in the custody of the state, an aggressive outreach effort begins through mailings, social media,
         advertisements, and local media coverage. In March of 2021 the Unclaimed Property Division developed a mapping tool to
         visualize unclaimed property data. This allowed the Division to identify lower income areas throughout the state that also had high
         concentrations of unclaimed property. Using this software, the Division identified several census tracts in Ogden that had an
)        abnonnally high concentration of unclaimed property-more than $3 Million dollars. The City agreed to partner with the Division
         in its efforts to reunite this property with rightful owners. In June of2021, the Division sent out letters and started a social media
         campaign to encourage residents to contact the Division regarding their unclaimed property. The City's newsletter and magazine
         were also used to encourage residents to reach out to the Division for assistance in claiming their property. Through those efforts,
         the Treasurer's Office has now paid out over $349,000 to residents in Ogden through 669 claims. There are stilll,700 active

Ogden Trails Network Committee, Presentation of Sunset Report; and
Proposed Ordinance 2021-40 amending the Ogden Municipal Code by
amending Section 3-22-2 to reorganize the Trails Network Committee; and
amending Section 3-22-5 to extend the sunset date to June 30, 2024; and
Committee Appointments/Reappointments
         Brian Waters, Chair of the Ogden Trails Network (OTN) Committee, presented the sunset report for the Ogden Trails
Network Committee. The purpose of the OTN Committee is to advise the City Council and Mayor in all matters pertaining to non-
motorized trails. Their mission is to provide a world-class trail system for the betterment of Ogden. Significant contributions of
the Committee from 2018 to 2021 include improved trail user information; trail planning and development; and extensive
volunteer work. Ongoing work of the Committee includes trail system assessment and completion of Phase Two of the Trackline
Bike Park. The Committee recommends the Committee ordinance be amended to remove the requirement for specific
neighborhoods to be represented on the OTN Board and to extend the sunset date for the Committee to June of2024. Additionally,
the Committee recommends the creation of an Ogden City Trails Manager position within the next two years; volunteers are
currently performing project management and fundraising duties and it is appropriate to create a permanent position that will
handle these responsibilities. The OTN currently performs volunteer trail work equivalent to two full-time employees and a
dedicated Trails Manager can plan more involved trail projects and direct volunteers in their endeavors.
         Vice Chair White asked what the OTN needs to be able to move forward with the Trackline Bike Park project. Mr.
Waters stated the Committee needs conceptual drawings for the overall Trackline project so that they have a clear understanding
of how and where development can occur within the project area.
         Council member Nadolski asked if the Committee works with the Trails Foundation ofNorthem Utah. Mr. Waters
answered yes; the two groups have a good working relationship and have participated in joint training opportunities.
         Public Services Director Lowder then noted the OTN Committee does a great job for the community and he reiterated the
proposed ordinance amendments mentioned by Mr. Waters. He added he feels that the amendments regarding membership of the
Committee are consistent with the membership language for other Committees in the City.

Redistricting - Daniel Wade, Candidate ServicesNoter Registration
          Every ten years the Council is required to engage in adjusting and approving new voting boundaries in Ogden City. The
City is required to approve boundaries for City Council districts, Ogden School Board districts, and Voter Participation Areas.
Population data is provided by Weber County, but due to issues with the Census Bureau, data was not transmitted in a timely
manner, and the County/City have limited time to compile information and establish voting districts.
          Mr. Wade discussed the role Weber County will play in the redistricting process; before the City can start drawing new
voting boundaries the County must provide preliminary data from the state. The County analyzes this data and creates voting
precincts. Voting precincts are the foundation for building voting districts. The county will create 28 voting precincts in Ogden
City, and then the City will use these precincts to decide boundaries for City Council districts, Ogden School Board districts, and
Voter Participation Areas (which are used for referendums, initiatives, and petitions) as follows:
              •    7 precincts will be used in the formation of each council district.
              •    4 precincts will be used in the formation of each school district.
              •    7 precincts will also be used to create Voter Participation Areas; however, it is recommended that these borders
                   follow the same borders as the council districts.
          The County will fmalize voting precincts in December 2021, at which time the City can start putting together Council
and school board districts.
          Mr. Wade then participated in discussion with the Council regarding the overall process and their role; he also discussed
opportunities for streamlining the process. He invited Council members to provide feedback regarding their ideas for adjusting
district boundaries, noting that the County will use that input to formalize their fmal redistricting recommendation for the Council
and Ogden School District.

Consideration of certifying the August 10, 2021, Municipal Primary Election
results
          A memo from the City Recorder came before the Council to consider certifying the August 10,2021, Municipal General
Election results. The memo stated Section 20A, Chapter 4 of the Utah State Code sets forth the election review process, designates
the legislative body- the City Council- and the Mayor as the Board of Canvassers and outlines the Board's duties for municipal
elections. The City Recorder acts as the election official. The Board of Canvassers is responsible for certifying the official election
results of municipal elections. Canvassing of returns for a Primary Election must occur no sooner than seven (7) days after the
election and no later than fourteen (14) days after the election. After reviewing the returns, the Board must certify the results of
the election and file a report that includes the following information:                          ·
               1. The total number of votes cast in the Board's jurisdiction;
               2. The name of each candidate whose name appeared on the ballot;
               3. Each office that appears on the ballot;
               4. The number of votes for each candidate;
               5. The total number ofvotes given in the Board's jurisdiction to each candidate;
               6. The number of ballots that were rejected; and
               7. A statement certifying that the information contained in the report is accurate.
          City Recorder Hansen summarized the memo and reviewed the report of unofficial election results dated August 12,
2021. The seats to be filled in the 2021 Municipal Election are At Large Seat A and District 3. She indicated the candidates for the
At Large A position were: MarciaL. White, Daniel J. Gladwell, John H. Thompson, Jase Reyneveld, John Ogden, and Jack
Barnes. MarciaL. White and Daniel J. Gladwell received the highest number of votes, 3,734 and 869, respectively and will
advance to the General Election. The candidates for the District 3 position were Priscilla Martinez, Ken Richey, and Mary Khalaf.
Pricilla Martinez and Ken Richey received the highest number of votes, 804 and 801, respectively, and will advance to the
General Election. She indicated an important item of note is that the number of registered voters in Ogden increased by nearly
6,000 voters; this is likely due to increased voter registration for the 2020 Presidential Election. The overall turnout for the Ogden
City election was 18.9 percent. She briefly reviewed the report regarding the number of ballots that were disqualified and cited the
reasons for disqualification, noting that voters are notified of the potential for their ballot to be disqualified and given time to
rectify the issue from which the disqualification would result. She concluded that it is her recommendation that the Board certify
the results of the August 10, 2021, Municipal Primary Election.
