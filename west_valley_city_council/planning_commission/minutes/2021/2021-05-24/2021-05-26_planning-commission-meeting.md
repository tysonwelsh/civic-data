GPZ-10-2021
Adam Maher
3540 West 4700 South
General Plan change from Non-Retail Commercial to High Density
Residential and a zone change from C-2 to RM
1.08 acres

Adam Maher has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for a 1.08-acre parcel
located at 3540 West 4700 South. The property is currently zoned RB (Residential Business)
with a General Plan designation of Non-Retail Commercial. The proposed zone is RM
(Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential) and the proposed General Plan designation is High
Density Residential (12 to 20 units/acre).

Surrounding zones include RB to the west, R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential,
minimum lot size 8,000 square feet) to the north and east, and LC (Limited Commercial) and
R-1-6 to the south in Taylorsville. Surrounding land uses include single family homes to the
north and east, Cyprus Credit Union to the west, and an office building and single family
homes to the south in Taylorsville. The subject property includes a home that, according to
the County Assessor, was built in 1898.

The applicant’s original proposal was for a 14-unit, 2-story townhome development with a
density of 13 units/acre, hence the request for High Density Residential. After receiving
feedback from staff, the applicant has reduced the proposed number of units to 12, yielding a
density of 11.1 units/acre. Each unit would have a two-car garage with a driveway large
enough to park two vehicles in. The proposed unit sizes range from 1,822 to 2,127 sq. ft. and
all units would include rooftop decks. The attached concept plan and application materials
from the applicant provide details on and support for the proposed project.

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

   1. Is this the right location for a townhome development?

   2. Assuming the location is appropriate, does the quality of the project further City
      goals?

These two questions are explored below.

Is this the right location for a townhome development?

According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be
considered by the City if the following four conditions are met:

   1. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the
      property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per
      acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property
      must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units
      per acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of
      the West Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the
      Redwood Junction light rail station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;

   2. The property shall meet any one of the following four requirements: (i) have access
      and Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for
      properties under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as indicated
      on the Major Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential
      development on two sides; or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-
      half mile of the West Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail
      station, or the Redwood Junction light rail station; or (iv) if the property is vacant, was
      vacant as of January 19, 2021, will be an exempt housing development
      under 24 CFR 100.304 or successor regulation with a density no greater than seven
      units per acre, and have access and Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way
      width of at least 80 feet;

   3. A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application that
      addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping, and
      project Amenities; and

   4. A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for projects
      with 100 or more Dwelling Units.
On the first condition, the property includes structures that would be demolished and is over
20,000 square feet in area. The proposed density is under 20 units/acre. For the second
condition, the subject property has access and frontage on 4700 South, which has a planned
right-of-way width of 106’. On the third condition, the applicant’s development agreement
standards are addressed later in this report. The fourth condition does not apply because the
project is under 100 units.

The points below may help the Commission answer the first question.

      The subject property was rezoned from R-1-8 to RB in 2004. The RB zone allows
       assisted living facilities, day cares, financial institutions, nursing homes, professional
       offices, medical/dental offices, and veterinary hospitals. The RB zone also allows
       single unit dwellings on 8,000 square foot lots.
      4700 South is a 5-lane arterial street that is serviced by UTA bus route 47.
      Average annual daily traffic (AADT) on this segment of 4700 South was 18,000 in
       2019. A 5-lane arterial can handle a traffic volume of 26,500 at a level of service
       (LOS) C and 30,500 at a LOS D. According to the Institute of Transportation
       Engineers, townhomes generate 5.81 trips per day.
      There are commercial services like a convenience store, credit union, and strip
       commercial center within walking distance of the site.



Does the quality of the project further City goals?

If the Commission believes that townhomes are appropriate here, the quality of the proposal
should then be considered.

Dwelling Unit Sizes
The proposed unit sizes for the four unit types are 1,822, 1,893, 1,922, and 2,127 square feet.
All units will have three bedrooms. The ordinance requires a minimum area of 1,400 square
feet for a three-bedroom townhome.

Exterior Materials
The ordinance states the following concerning exterior materials: “All multi-unit residential
Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding. With
the exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire Building exterior, no single
material shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s exterior. Where stucco, tile,
metal panels or fiber cement siding are used, at least 20 percent of the Building’s exterior
shall be brick or stone. Wood and vinyl may be used as trim, soffits and/or accent materials
only.”

The proposed materials are stucco, fiber cement siding, and brick. It appears that the stucco
may exceed 60% of the exterior.

Architecture
Staff performed a preliminary review of the proposed building architecture. To comply with
the City’s design standards, the rear elevation would need to be modified to include at least
one relief treatment such as a building offset and at least one building design treatment such
as a change of material.
Landscaping/Recreation Space
Townhome developments require at least 50% of the site as landscaping and recreation space.
The concept plan shows 46% of the site as landscaped and an additional 5% in rooftop decks.
As pointed out in study meeting, staff believes an alternative layout with less roads would
yield more landscaped space.

Project Amenities
For a project of this size, the ordinance requires one amenity. The proposed project includes
two amenities from the ordinance – a sport court and an outdoor fire pit.

Other Considerations

The Planning Commission expressed concerns about the long-term viability of a
homeowner’s association with only 12 units. The City’s PUD ordinance does allow projects
as small as one acre. At the request of the Commission, staff will be researching this issue for
a future discussion.



Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. A General Plan change to Medium Density Residential and a zone change
      to RM should be approved subject to a development agreement that incorporates the
      standards proposed by the applicant with the following additions recommended by
      staff:
          a. The maximum number of units shall be 12.
          b. The existing masonry wall along part of the north side of the property shall be
             extended to the east boundary of the property.
          c. A 6’ tall, vinyl fence shall be installed along the east property line.
          d. All standards in the City’s Multi-Unit Residential Design Standards shall be
             met.
          e. Upon completion of all of the on-site improvements, the developer shall
             provide a letter to the City from an independent engineering firm which
             certifies that all of the on-site improvements are completed as shown on the
             plans approved by the City. For the purposes of this provision, on-site
             improvements shall include landscaping, irrigation, private streets, sidewalks,
             parking, lighting, entrance features, fencing, and any other outdoor
             recreational amenity.

   2. Continuance.

   3. Denial. This property should remain RB as it could be developed as single family
      residential or one of the non-residential uses allowed in the RB zone.


Discussion:    Steve Pastorik presented the application. Adam Maher, the applicant, said he
               likes the concept plan recommended by staff. He asked if there could be
               further discussion about the roof-top garden. Mr. Maher feels the roof-top
               green space is unnecessary and will increase cost without adding any value to
               the building.
          Commissioner Winters is concerned that an HOA would not be effective
          enough to maintain a twelve-unit project. Mr. Maher said that he understands
          the burden an HOA can impose. He said the City’s code states that projects
          less than twenty units are required to have fifty percent landscaping, which
          causes more cost and HOA maintenance. Mr. Maher explained the roof-top
          garden would increase the landscaping percentage. However, it would not add
          any aesthetic value to the project.

          Commissioner Lovato feels the City would like to see future developments
          become more sustainable, and projects of this size are not. Mr. Maher said he
          understands the concern with maintaining a duplex with one owner. He said
          this project would have twelve different owners and an HOA, keeping the
          building well maintained.

          Commissioner Winters asked Steve if he feels a roof-top garden is an effective
          way to meet the landscaping requirement. Steve said we have some more
          modern projects with roof-top decks, and they have turned out to be a valuable
          amenity. He stated the intent of the roof-top patios for this project is to be
          used as recreation space. Steve pointed out this would not be a green roof but
          could contain some potted plants.

          Commissioner Winters, Fuller, and Woodruff do not feel the roof-top deck
          would be a good addition, and they would like to consider a landscape
          reduction.

          Commissioner Woodruff likes the concept plan that Commissioner Fuller
          explained in the study meeting, with two six-unit buildings on the north and
          south of the subject property. He realizes that the back of the building will be
          facing the street and feels the applicant can work with staff to make it
          aesthetically pleasing.

          Commissioner Lovato seconded the motion.

C-10-2021
West Valley Retail
4936 W 3500 S
C-2 Zone (2.47 acres)


The applicant, Ezequiel Martinez Flores, is requesting conditional use approval for a new
shopping center, which will be located on a vacant parcel at 4936 W 3500 S. The property is
zoned C-2, General Commercial, and the West Valley City General Plan designates this area
as General Commercial. Zoning to the north, east, and west is C-2, while parcels to the south
are zoned R-1-8. The surrounding uses include a credit union to the west, charter school to
the north, and a vacant commercial building, as well as the former Shopko building, parking
lot, and restaurant pad sites to the east.

The applicant is proposing to construct an 11,695 sq. ft. multi-tenant retail building. Access
to the site will be from an existing private road on the west side of the property that is
currently utilized by the adjacent credit union and charter school. A sidewalk is not required
running north to south on the west side of the site next to the road since it is not a public
street, but a pedestrian connection needs to be included from the site to the adjacent credit
union parcel. 46 parking stalls are required on the property and 56 stalls are proposed. The
tenant space on the northern end of the building will include a drive-up window. At the time
of the study meeting the applicant was still revising the plans for that area of the site. The
drive-thru shall comply with all applicable requirements in Title 7.

3500 South is one of the City’s high image arterial streets, so the first 20’ of landscaping
adjacent to that road must meet the standards in 7-10-103. Fifteen percent landscaping is
required throughout the site, and approximately 34% is proposed. There are some existing
parking lot landscape islands that are on the east end of the parcel that need to be cleaned up
and maintained.

The building shall meet all the Commercial Design Standards outlined in 7-11-300P.
Exterior materials will be EIFS, brick, metal siding, and wood siding. Some Planning
Commission members expressed concern during the study meeting about the east building
elevation, which will be visible from 3500 South. That elevation is considered a secondary
building façade and needs to meet all the building relief, design and roof requirements
outlined in the code.

All signage on the property shall comply with the regulations in Title 11.


Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as
the following:

            1. The site shall be constructed per the approved plan.
            2. The drive-thru shall comply with all Title 7 regulations.
            3. There shall be 15% landscaping on the site, and the frontage along 3500
               South shall meet the Standards for Landscaping Along High-Image Arterial
               Streets found in 7-10-103. All landscaped areas shall include 50% live plant
               material.
              4. The existing parking lot landscape islands on the east side of the property
                 shall be cleaned up and maintained.
              5. The building elevations shall meet all the Commercial Design Standards
                 outlined in 7-11-300P.
              6. Signage shall comply with the West Valley City Code.
              7. Shall meet the requirements of all affected departments and agencies.
              8. Subject to review upon valid complaint.

Discussion:     Brock Anderson presented the application. He requested the Planning
                Commission add a sentence to the third staff alternative stating a three-foot
                berm shall be added to the landscaped area adjacent to the western side
                parking.
                Commissioner Winters asked if the berm needs to be a hill or if it could be
                shrubs or a masonry wall.
                Brock explained since it is a private street and not a public street, they would
                have the option of a three-foot masonry wall with the landscaping sloping up
                to the wall. Commissioner Winters asked if both the wall and berm are needed.
                Brock said both are typically done; however, if the Commission feels just the
                wall is sufficient, that could be added to the staff alternatives.
                Commissioner Woodruff said when driving west, the east elevation of the
                building would be the first thing you would see. He felt having a solid wall
                will open up a canvas for graffiti. Commissioner Woodruff said there needs to
                be more relief added to the east elevation. He also suggested that something
                like spandrel glass be used to break up the plain façade. Brock said the
                requirement is for one relief treatment and two design treatments, and they are
                currently meeting one design treatment with the 20% materials change. They
                still need to add a relief treatment such as awnings, as well as an additional
                design treatment.
                Commissioner Woodruff said he is unsure why the east elevation is considered
                a secondary façade where it is the most prominent portion of the building.
                Brock explained that it fits the definition of a secondary façade because it does
                not have a customer entrance and is not fronting a road. Brock mentioned an
                option for the Commission is to add additional requirements for the secondary
                facade.
                Commissioner Wood agreed with Commissioner Woodruff and was also
                concerned about graffiti. She was also bothered by the possible traffic issues
                with the drive-thru option. She also agreed that spandrel glass should be added
                to the east elevation.
                Cory Middleton, the applicant, addressed the Commission. He explained that
                he wanted to offer a drive-thru since businesses have been relying on them
                during the pandemic. He explained that wrapping the drive-thru around the
              building would help to prevent traffic issues. Mr. Middleton said he would be
              open to looking at some options for the secondary façade.
              Commissioner Wood asked Mr. Middleton what type of business would be
              operating the drive thru. Mr. Middleton was not sure what the company will be
              at this time. He just wanted to have that option available in the building.
              Commissioner Winters asked Brock if there are any limitations to the type of
              business that can offer a drive thru. Brock said there is not a limitation. Any
              use allowed in the C-2 zone can have that option.
              Commissioner Lovato liked Commissioner Wood's suggestion of the spandrel
              glass.

              alternatives, with the addition of a three-foot berm be added to the landscaped

              Commissioner Woodruff seconded the motion.
