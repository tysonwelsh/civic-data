ZONE TEXT APPLICATIONS
ZT-3-2022 Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting an ordinance amendment to
          change the Zoning Ordinance (Title 7) and the Condominium Ordinance (Title 9).
          The purpose of these changes is to prohibit private streets, allow parking spaces
          for townhomes and condominiums attached to the side of each other to back onto
          a street, and create two new street classifications for use in planned unit
          developments. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-
          ut.gov). (Continued from the May 11, 2022 public hearing)

Discussion:   Steve Pastorik explained that this application was continued from the last public
              hearing. He stated that if this proposal were passed, three things would happen.
              First, private streets would be prohibited. Second, allow parking spaces for
              townhomes and condominiums attached to the side of each other to back onto a
              road, and third, create two new street classifications for use in planned unit
              developments.
Commissioner Winters was not present at the last meeting and asked if Steve
could recap any questions or concerns. Steve said that Commissioner Woodruff
had expressed concern with a development containing shorter private streets,
around 100 to 150 feet long, without a turnaround. He explained that a road 150
feet long or longer is required to have a turnaround by fire code. Steve said he
was wondering how this would affect developments in the future.

Steve mentioned that an architect who has worked with Scott Godfrey on some
industrial developments asked if this would only apply to residential properties or
if it would also apply to industrial projects. Steve answered that the Council had
not made a distinction. He noted that there are some existing dead-end streets
within the City that would need to be modified.

Coby Wilson – said it would not be safe for snowplows or dump trucks to turn
around on a dead-end street. He stated that right now, our code and engineering
standards require that all dead-end roads end in a cul-de-sac, so if this passed, we
would recommend continuing with that requirement.

Commissioner Woodruff said creating a thru street within some of the proposed
developments would lose several units and might not make it profitable enough to
develop.

Commissioner Winters asked if the options would be to end the street in a cul-de-
sac or a hammerhead. Steve said that a hammerhead was not allowed on a public
street, so the two options would be a roundabout or cul-de-sac.

Commissioner Wood is concerned about developing land and having it be
economical. She asked what will be required of the developments that have been
approved but not yet constructed. Steve said those developments can still have
private streets because the application was approved before this ordinance was
passed.

Commissioner McEwen asked if Public Works would be able to handle the
increase in the number of streets they need to maintain. Steve stated that a map of
all the roads they would need to maintain was presented to the Council, and they
still wanted to eliminate private streets.

Commissioner Wood asked if this would apply to industrial developments and
PUDs. Steve explained as it is proposed, yes. However, if the Commission would
like to recommend a carveout for those, they can.

Commissioner Lovato asked if there has been a history of concerns regarding
private streets within an industrial development or has it mainly been residential.
Steve said the concerns that the Council mentioned were residential. The residents
feel that they should not pay an HOA fee and property tax when the City doesn't
maintain their streets. Steve explained that another concern is situations where the
              HOA is underfunded and cannot maintain the private roads.

              Commissioner Fuller asked if some of the existing private streets fit the
              specifications of a public road can if they ask the City to make that change. Steve
              explained that the Council would need to approve the street dedication.
              Commissioner McEwen asked if the Council would receive the recommendation
              from the Commission. Steve said they would receive the meeting minutes, and
              when the proposal is added to the Council's agenda, the planner would add the
              recommendation as part of the presentation.

              Commissioner Woodruff asked if the applicant would ask for a variance from the
              Board of Adjustment. Brandon Hill, City Attorney, said that would not be a
              permitted variance. However, they could ask for a variance regarding frontage, for
              example, to accommodate the needed space.
