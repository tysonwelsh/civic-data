GPZ-2-2022
Chris Clifford
5143 W 3500 S
General Plan change from mixed use and large lot residential to small lot residential and a
zone change from A to RS
5.9 acres

Chris Clifford has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for four parcels totaling 5.9
acres located at 5143 W 3500 S. The property is currently zoned A (agriculture, minimum lot
size ½ acre) with a General Plan designation of mixed use for approximately the north 230’ and
large lot residential (2 to 3 units/acre) for the rest of the property. At this location, the mixed use
designation includes office and high density residential (12 to 20 units/acre). The proposed zone
is RS (residential sustainability) and the proposed General Plan designation is small lot
residential (4 to 7 units/acre).

Surrounding zones include A to the south, west, and a portion of the east and R-1-8 (single unit
dwelling residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet) to the north and the remaining east
portion. Surrounding land uses include single family homes to the north, south, and east and
single family homes, a six-plex, and two duplexes to the west. The north parcel includes a home
that, according to the Salt Lake County Assessor, was built in 1900. The three parcels to the
south are vacant.

As shown in the attached concept plan, the applicant is proposing to demolish the existing home
and develop a 36-unit planned unit development (PUD) of single unit dwellings. The original
proposal included 38 units; however, the applicant chose to remove 2 units. The proposed
density is 6.1 units/acre. For reference, 6.5 units/acre is the maximum density allowed for a
planned unit development in the RS zone. The RS zone allows for smaller lots in exchange for a
development agreement commitment to build homes with high energy efficiency and low water
use. Included with this report are the minimum standards for the RS zone. The applicant’s
proposed development agreement incorporates all of the RS zone standards as written in the
ordinance along with additional commitments.

In reviewing this application, staff suggests that there are two primary questions to answer:

    1. Is this the right location for a RS zone neighborhood?

    2. Will the increase in density be compensated by increased amenities and improved design
       that are proportional to the density increase?

These two questions are explored below.

Question 1: Is this the right location for a RS zone neighborhood?

Surrounding Zoning
If this application is approved, this project would be the second RS zone subdivision. The first
subdivision, which is called the Han Subdivision, is located at 3730 South 3200 West on 4.42
acres. The subject property is similar to the Han Subdivision in that much of the surrounding
areas are zoned R-1-8. The RS zone standards for lot size and setbacks are the same as those for
the R-1-8 zone.

Proximity to Transit and Commercial Services
When the RS zone was first established in 2020, the whereas statements from the ordinance that
created the zone included the following: “Whereas, the City desires to create additional options
for development of environmentally friendly, sustainable, and energy efficient housing…”
Clearly one way that an RS zone subdivision can be environmentally friendly, sustainable, and
energy efficient is through the design of the homes and landscapes in the subdivision. Another
way is to locate RS zone subdivisions near transit and commercial services to minimize the
number and distance of vehicle trips. The property has access and frontage on 3500 South which
is serviced by UTA bus route 35. There are also commercial services roughly 1,000’ to the east
centered around 4800 W. There are also commercial services to the west scattered between 5200
West and 5450 West and then centered around 5600 West less than one half of a mile from the
subject property.

Maximum District Size
The RS zone includes a maximum district size of 10 acres. When the RS zone was first created
in the ordinance, the City Council wanted to see how a smaller RS zone development would
work before considering a larger development. This property is well below the 10-acre cap.

Current General Plan Designation
In the General Plan, the north 1.5 acres of the subject property includes the option of high
density residential, which has a density range of 12 to 20 units/acre. The remaining 4.4 acres of
the subject property is designated as large lot residential, which has a density range of 2 to 3
units/acre. The table below summarizes the number of units anticipated in the General Plan at the
low and high ends of the density ranges for the subject property.

           Density Range                             End of the Density Range   End of the Density Range

In summary, for a project to comply with the existing General Plan designation, the number of
units would range from 27 to 43. At 36 units, the proposed project falls within this range;

however, the density is spread out over the entire site instead of being mostly concentrated along
3500 South as the existing General Plan recommends.




Question 2: Will the increase in density be compensated by increased amenities and
improved design that are proportional to the density increase?

For PUDs, the City’s ordinance allows for a density increase; however, any increase in density
must be compensated by increased amenities and improved design which are proportional to the
density increase. As pointed out earlier, the maximum density possible for a PUD in the RS zone
is 6.5 units/acre, and the proposed density for this application is 6.1 units/acre. The sections that
follow address the amenities and design of the proposal.
Amenities
   Parking: The Planning Commission recently recommended approval on an ordinance that
      would require a minimum 18’ x 18’ driveway for single unit dwellings, twin homes, and
      townhomes. The same ordinance also required 0.75 stalls per unit for the same housing
      types whenever such units were not adjacent to a public street with no more than two
      lanes. The proposed project includes the driveways for each unit. If this application is
      approved, the final plan would need to include the 0.75 stalls per unit, which yields 27
      spaces that should be spread out over the site.
   Perimeter fencing: The applicant is proposing perimeter fencing. The north end would be
      a 60-inch pre-cast concrete wall. The material for the other sides is not defined.
   Recreational amenities: The applicant lists walking paths, a sports court, tot lot, and
      pavilion with BBQ area.
   Landscaping and recreation space: City ordinance requires a minimum of 50% of a PUD
      to be landscaping and recreation space. While no percentage is called out on the concept
      plan, the 50% would still apply.

Design
    Unit sizes: The minimum square footage of finished, above-ground, habitable floor space
       for homes within the RS zone is 1,400 square feet. The proposed minimum unit sizes for
       the proposed project are 1,560 square feet for ramblers and 2,400 square feet for two
       story homes. All homes would have basements unless soil conditions preclude them.
    Architecture: The applicant has submitted renderings for a rambler plan and a two-story
       plan. The RS zone requires a minimum of 80% brick on the exterior of homes. The
       applicant is proposing brick, stone, or equivalent for 80% of exterior surfaces.
    Interior features: One of the requirements of the RS zone is: “The development
       agreement shall include commitments on the quality of the interior finishes of all homes.”
       To address this requirement, the applicant has proposed the following interior features:
       range hood vented to the outside, granite kitchen countertops, full height Travertine tile
       backsplash in kitchens, undermount kitchen sink, garbage disposal, walk-in kitchen
       pantry, vaulted ceilings, cabinets with full extension soft-close drawers, air-sealed
       recessed can lighting in ceilings, unfinished basements, elongated toilets with soft close
       seats, solid core painted interior doors, bullnose (rounded) drywall corners, walk-in
       closets in master bedrooms, 5” baseboards on floors and 3” door casing trim, engineered
       hardwood floors (or similar) in main level common areas, oil-rubbed bronze or chrome
       faucets, mud-set shower pan with built-in bench seat and porcelain tile with accent band
       in master shower, separate wall, trim and ceiling colors with two additional wall colors,
       and painted wood window sills with rounded bullnose edge on three sides.
    Sustainable features: The applicant is proposing to follow the energy efficiency and water
       conservation standards outlined in the RS zone.
    Subdivision layout: There are many things that could influence the final design of this
       development including where UDOT allows access onto 3500 S, grading and drainage,
       and whether the type of streets proposed will be allowed. Realizing that there will likely
       be changes, staff is still concerned about three items. First, the rear setback along the
       south side is only 10’. For PUDs, the smallest rear setback the City has done has been
       15’. Second, the space between some of the backs of units is only 20’ which seems pretty
       tight. Third, The City’s Major Street Plan calls for a 115’ right-of-way along 3500 South.
       The current right-of-way width is 80’. Staff recommends that the development agreement
       require 17.5’ of right-of-way dedication.
      Road connection: The concept plan includes a public cul-de-sac at the end of Kiowa
       Way. All other streets are private streets. The concept plan also includes a private street
       connection to Kiowa Way. Since fire code requires a second point of access for more
       than 30 homes, a connection to Kiowa Way would be required. However, this connection
       could be gated for emergency access only if cut through traffic is not desired.

Staff Suggestions

In addition to the standards proposed by the applicant, staff recommends these additional
standards along with a few revisions to the applicant’s standards.

      Number of units: The number of units shall be no more than 36.
      Parking: In addition to the parking provided in garages and driveways, at least 27 surface
       stalls shall be provided throughout the development.
      Fencing: Perimeter fencing along the west, south, and east sides shall be 6’ tall vinyl. The
       perimeter fence along the north side shall be 6’ tall, pre-cast concrete in the same color
       and pattern used by the City along 4100 South. An entry feature shall also be included
       along 3500 South.
      Landscaping and recreation space: A minimum of 50% of the site shall be landscaping
       and recreation space. A 5’ wide area of landscaping shall be included between the
       sidewalk and precast concrete wall along 3500 South.
      Architecture: An alternative front elevation shall be developed for both the rambler plan
       and two-story plan. In addition, a third house plan shall also be developed for this
       development. The alternative front elevations and third house plan shall be reviewed and
       approved by the Planning Commission before a building permit is issued. Brick or stone
       shall be used on at least 80% of the exterior of all homes.
      Setbacks: The minimum rear yard setback along the perimeter of the property shall be
       15’. The minimum distance between the backs of units shall be 25’.
      Right-of-way dedication: 17.5’ shall be dedicated along the entire frontage of 3500
       South.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This application should be approved subject to a development agreement that
      includes the standards proposed by the applicant along with the additions and revisions
      recommended by staff in this report.

   2. Continuance.

   3. Denial. The RE zone is more appropriate for most of the property at this location.

C-1-2022
Verizon (J5 Infrastructure Partners)
3540 S. 4000 W.
C2 Zone (1.94 Acres)

J5 Infrastructure Partners, on behalf of Verizon, is requesting a conditional use for roof mounted
antennas on the Harmon Building at 3540 South 4000 West. This property is zoned C2, General
Commercial, and the West Valley City General Plan designates this area as General Commercial.
The surrounding zone is C2 on all sides.

There are currently roof and wall mounted antennas at this location and Verizon would like to
replace and relocate those antennas as part of their 5G upgrade. They would consolidate the
antennas and convert the facility to roof mounted and install the equipment on top of the existing
mechanical penthouse. The height would comply with the WVC Code which allows for a
maximum height of 8’ per 7-7-405(1).

Verizon has not proposed to add any screen type structure around the antennas and would paint
them brown to match the building.


Staff Alternatives:

      Approval, subject to the resolution of any issues raised at the public hearing and the
       following conditions:

           1. The antennas shall be installed per the approved plans and in accordance with
              Chapter 7-7-400p of the WVC Municipal Code.

           2. The antennas shall be painted to match the colors of the existing building and no
              additional screening shall be required.

           3. Must comply with all other relevant requirements set forth from applicable
              department and agencies.

      Continuance, to allow for the resolution of any issues raised at the public hearing.


Discussion:    Jody Knapp presented the application. Keleigh Glass, the applicant, explained
               that they are requesting to raise the antennas because the parapet wall is currently
               obstructing the signal. She said by raising the antennas, and they would be able to
               provide better 5G service to everyone.

Motion:        Commissioner Lovato motioned to approve C-2-2022 subject to any issues raised
               at the public hearing and the three conditions outlined in the staff report.
