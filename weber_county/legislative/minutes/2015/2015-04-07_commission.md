    2. RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING EMINENT DOMAIN PROCEEDINGS RELATED TO THE LITTLE WEBER
       RIVER CUTOFF CHANNEL PROJECT – RESOLUTION 25-2015
         Lance Petersen, with County Emergency Management, gave a presentation reviewing the history of
         flooding of the Weber River since 1952. The 1952 flood occurred at approximately 7000 cfs., and
         flooding continued on the lower Weber in 1983, 2006, and 2011. The 2006 flooding was caught
         early and stopped. The county has worked extensively to clear debris from the channel, repair banks,
         enlarge gates on the Ogden Bay bird refuge, install an automatic trash rack on the Willard Bay
         Diversion Canal (the trash rack cost about $1 million), and is now ready to complete the Little Weber
         Diversion channel that the county and the farmers in western Weber County began in 1952. He said
         that flooding is a hazard that needs to be addressed. It will help sustain the economic viability of the
         area, whether it is used for farming, residential or commercial. The county received some grants
         from NRCS (Natural Resources Conservation Service) in 2013 for the severe 2011 flooding, and the
         county has been working extensively to mitigate the flood threat for the past four years. He said that
         the county’s repair strategies developed in 2011 are sound and the projects that Mr. Andersen will
         address next are prudent projects.

         Jared Andersen, County Engineer, stated that this project has been a main focus of the county for the
         past few years. In the spring of 2012 NRCS hired the McMillan Group
         (engineers/biologists/environmentalists), which conducted the damage assessment and the
         environmental assessment.

    County staff went along on the boat rides with NRCS, reviewed the damage survey reports and
    informed them of damaged areas. The county began hiring design firms (mainly Bowen Collins &
    Associates, which technically surveyed the entire area in 2012 to help with design work in western
    Weber County and also hired a public information firm for public notification process (including the
    newspaper, mailers, emails, postings) on the projects and the first open house to obtain public input
    was in 9/2012. Ogden Bay and the Little Weber Cut Off channel projects needed an environmental
    assessment (EA) and subsequently there was another open house on 8/2013. Subsequently, the
    McMillan Group made its recommendations, and there is a good possibility that the county would
    not receive the federal funding if it did not follow those. The final assessment report was completed
    in March 2014. The county could not finalize the projects until the EA report was finalized.

    The county immediately submitted application for an Army Corp permit for the Little Weber Cut Off
    channel. Bowen Collins designed the Little Weber Cut Off channel project. Mr. Andersen stressed
    that timing is of the essence and the federal emergency watershed funds must be expended during a
    certain period of time. The fourth and final federal extension expires at the end of summer. He
    noted that early settlers in 1896 had also diverted water in close proximity to the current channel.
    The current concrete channel has been completed from the river to 5500 W. and the county has a
    permanent easement on that first section of property since 2011. The next section from 5500 to 5900
    W., is the Hansen property/O&O Farm, and the flooding in this area has the potential for the most
    impact, according to the McMillan Group. The channel is being done in concrete because an open
    channel would necessitate yearly maintenance and a lot of work for the county into the future. The
    Hansen property will be the most impacted by the channel but it will best mitigate flooding for the
    Hansen’s and the surrounding area. The last section is the Gary Nelson property, the county has a
    right to occupy agreement with him, and the county’s contractor has started work there. The
    Hansens have been before the Commission over the last few months opposing this channel on their
    property. However, the other two sections are about to be completed and the middle section/Hansen
    property needs to connect. County staff has spent a lot of time with the Hansens, including visits on
    their property to ensure they can continue to operate as close as they currently operate and are close
    to addressing their last couple of items. He wishes this issue had not come to this point and
    appreciates the Hansen’s time in the last two weeks to try resolving the issue.

    Douglas Hansen, of West Weber, said that this channel critically impacts their farming operation and
    bisects their pasture and they do not want the canal. He said that there were additional floods in 1936
    and 1948 and their families are used to the flooding. His family attended the public meetings and
    made comments (which are in the EA), sent a letter to the State conservationist and Mr. Andersen in
    March 2014, and he has also spoken with past commissioners and worked with county staff since
    1997 proposing alternatives, which he feels are more effective and allow the river to heal itself and
    carry more water, but their recommendations were not heeded, and the 2011 flood ensued.

    Mr. Hansen has been before the Commission this year several times to define the alternatives. Now
    they are dealing with the large cement canal through their property and have been working with Mr.
    Anderson to try to mitigate the related problems. The original design did not have an irrigation
    system, had very little drainage and did not have the sufficient access they needed to operate their
    farm. Mr. Hansen said that he spoke about this a lot but nothing seemed to occur and he wrote a list
    and Mr. Andersen has acknowledged most of those items, which have been made a part of the current
    design for that area and satisfies the Hansens a little more that they can continue to farm, though it
    will be limited. Mr. Hansen had the addendum to the contract that is nearly complete, and which he
    sent to Mr. Andersen this morning. There are about 4-5 items to be resolved because details need to
    be added to the agreement, but only a couple of those items require agreement. The Hansens prefer
    not proceeding with eminent domain procedures and would like to reach agreement and be able to
    sign the right to occupancy.

       the lawsuit. There are 30 days from the date of adoption before the condemnation action can be
       filed. He recommends adopting the resolution.

       Chair Gibson said that the 2011 flooding had been a major challenge. Some people had put their
       lives in peril and he expressed thanks to all who assisted—citizens and entities. The county
       approached the State, which previously had been unwilling to address the concerns, and they became
       a partner to help correct the problem. He said that this portion of the project is the most expensive
       and is critical to the health and safety of the area citizens. The county has been trying to get the
       Hansens to sign on for four years and have exhausted options. He stated that construction has been
       completed on one end and construction has started at the other end and the middle section (the
       Hansen’s property) must be done to have the project function properly. Other property owners with
       similar impacts have worked very hard to work with the project. A permanent easement was signed
       in 2011 over 2/3 of this right-of-way for this project. The Hansens have been treated as the other
       property owners, have been notified and given opportunity to comment, and the county believed it
       was dealing with the person who had authority to represent the LLC. The LLC recently informed the
       county that the person who they were dealing with did not have authority to sign the LLC. The
       county is trying to follow the correct procedures and does not want to have to de-mobilize a
       contractor again. The county is committed to minimize the impacts.

       Karen Tucker, Doug and Larry Hansen’s sister, stated that she had been living out of state for some
       time and was not being informed of what was going on and had not receive documents. Ms. Tucker
       said that this is not a one-owner LLC and the person who had signed the documents did not have the
       authority. She prefers not to go forward with eminent domain procedures and requested making the
       resolution effective one week out (April 14). Mr. Andersen feels comfortable with making the
       resolution effective April 14, 2015.
