Proposed Ordinance 2024-24 amending Ogden Municipal Code to extend
the sunset provision for the Ogden Trails Network Committee to April 30,
2026
          A memo from Council staff regarding proposed Ogden Trails Network (OTN) Committee ordinance amendments came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the OTN Committee gave their sunset report to the City Council during
the work session. City advisory committees have sunset provisions in their enabling ordinances which terminate their existence on
a certain date, unless reauthorized by the City Council. The Ogden Trails Network Advisory Committee will sunset on June 30,
2024. If extended, the new sunset date for the committee will be extended two years to April 30, 2026.
          Acting Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-24, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City Utah, amending Section 3-22-5 of the Ogden Municipal Code to extend the
                   sunset provision for the Ogden Trails Network Committee to April 30, 2026; and by providing that this
                   ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS,
ORDINANCE 2024-24 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, HYER, MYERS, AND ACTING CHAIR WHITE. VOTING NO – NONE. Council
member Graf and Chair Richey were not present when this vote was taken.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-25 amending Ogden Municipal Code to revise
mixed use zone project standards; to revise Appendix A regarding signs;
and to make technical changes
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed Ogden Bend mixed-use zone
text amendments came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposal includes a request to make the
following changes to the code, to conform to the Ogden Bend Master Plan updates:
              1. Modify building types allowed;
              2. Modify maximum building lengths fronting the street; and
              3. Establish sign standards.
         Planning Director Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to orient the Council to
the area encompassed in the Ogden Bend Master Plan area; summarize the mixed-use zoning procedures; review proposed
amendments to the concept plan section of the Master Plan; and to expound on the implications of the proposed code amendments.
Mr. Brierley concluded the Planning Commission recommended the following change pertaining to mixed-residential areas: at
least one building type comprising at least 25 percent of the buildings or dwelling units shall be platted or condominiumized to
allow ownership of individual dwelling units. Lotus Development, a property owner in the Plan area, requested the following:
              • Allow surface parking;
              • Allow six-story building heights at 18th Street and Wall Avenue;
              • Allow 10-foot-high ground floor and nine-foot-high upper floor;
              • Less than 10-foot setbacks on Wall Avenue and 18th Street;
              • Allow buildings that ‘appear’ to be separated.
         The Planning Commission made the following recommendations responsive to Lotus’ requests:
              • Plan already allows surface parking;
              • Changed from 4 story maximum to 5 story maximum;
              • Changed first floor height from 14-feet to 10-feet;
              • Maintain 10-foot setbacks on Wall Avenue and 18th Street;
              • Buildings along Wall Avenue, Washington Boulevard, and 20th Street can “appear” to be separated.
          With the proposed changes, the Planning Commission has recommended the Ogden Redevelopment Agency adopt the
project Master Plan amendment and that the City Council adopt the proposed zoning text amendments. For a copy of the
presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on the changes to the signage regulations for the Mixed-Use
Zone and feedback the City has received from multiple property owners in the project area regarding the proposed changes.
          Acting Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2024-25, entitled:
                   “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Section 15-39-8 to
                   revise Mixed Use Zone project standards; by amending Section 18-5-1 to revise Appendix A regarding signs; to
                   make technical changes; and by providing that this Ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting
                   after final passage.”
          Acting Chair White then called for public input regarding the proposal.
          Peter Barrera thanked the Council for their service; he owns six parcels of property on 18th Street, across the street from
the Plan area, and he is interested in developing those properties in the future. He does not understand the purpose of the text
amendments and believes this is a wasted opportunity in terms of providing affordable housing for the community. The Plan area
could be better utilized for senior housing and other affordable housing types and could help to ensure downtown Ogden thrives in
the future.
          No additional persons came forward to be heard.
          Acting Chair White invited Mr. Brierley to respond to Mr. Barrera’s concerns. Mr. Brierley stated that he has met with
Mr. Barrera; he owns property on the north side of 18th Street and the mixed-use Plan area could be expanded to include his
property as well. He has encouraged Mr. Barrera to petition for expansion of the Plan area, which would benefit him in the future.
He believes there will be opportunities for different types of housing uses, including townhomes, apartments, and single-family
homes.
          Council member Hyer stated that the general concept is mixed-use zoning is great, but many developers have not
embraced it yet; he asked if these amendments will help to make the mixed-use zoning more palatable. Mr. Brierley stated that
there is reduced demand for commercial space in the current market, but there is demand for residential development. If mixed-
use zoning is applied to larger areas, it is easier to develop.
          Council member Choberka asked if Mr. Barrera’s proposal could be considered as part of this amendment rather than
requiring him to go through the process of future amendments. Mr. Brierley stated that the Planning Commission’s vision for Mr.
Barrera’s property was somewhat different than what Mr. Barrera has proposed and that is why he has been advised to pursue his
own text amendment or zoning adjustment of his properties.
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR,
ORDINANCE 2024-25 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, HYER, MYERS, AND ACTING CHAIR WHITE. VOTING NO – NONE. Council
member Graf and Chair Richey were not present when this vote was taken.

Proposed Resolution 2024-8 requesting admission to the Firefighters
Retirement System
          A memo from the Management Services Department regarding proposed Firefighter retirement revisions came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated the Human Resources Division has submitted a transmittal recommending that the
Council authorize the City to elect to cover new EMS positions under the URS (Utah Retirement Systems) Firefighter Retirement
System. A resolution approving this benefit for the new positions has been prepared for the Council’s consideration.
          Deputy Fire Chief Slater summarized the memo and stated prior to 2023, EMS personnel were not eligible to be covered
under the Firefighter Retirement System. House Bill 183, which passed during the 2023 Legislative Session, authorizes employers
to elect to cover full-time certified or licensed EMS personnel under the Firefighter Retirement System. For this benefit to take
effect with URS, the Council must adopt a resolution to elect coverage in conjunction with the approval of the City budget.
Approval of this resolution will improve the Department’s ability to hire quality employees in these positions.
          Acting Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2024-8, entitled:
                    “A resolution of the Ogden City Council requesting admission to the Firefighters Retirement System.”
          ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS,
RESOLUTION 2024-8 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, HYER, MYERS, AND ACTING CHAIR WHITE. VOTING NO – NONE. Council
member Graf and Chair Richey were not present when this vote was taken.

Public Comments
          Blaine Richey stated that he approached the Council in June regarding a petition to close Taylor Avenue on the south side
of Weber State University (WSU). Since then, the City installed speed radar signs on both sides of the road, but it is his hope this
is a short-term solution to calm traffic until a closure gate can be installed. He believes the signs will help in the short term but
will become ineffective over time. Every road on the north side of the campus has been closed to through traffic and the same
consideration should be given for roads on the south side. The Campus Master Plan references road closures on the south side of
the campus, but the closure of Taylor Avenue has not been considered even though it has the capacity to benefit many more
residents than other roads. Residents realize that WSU is a huge community partner, but the concerns of Ogden residents should
not be dismissed as a result. He believes the University wants to keep the road open to provide connectivity to the Dee Events
Center, but there are other options for connectivity. A traffic study should be conducted to determine the appropriate solutions for
the area.
          Scott Jensen stated he was confused by the discussion of the proposed Ogden Bend Mixed-Use Zone text amendments;
he owns property on 18th Street, and he is unsure how this will impact him. He is currently being harassed by Code Enforcement
and feels they are overstepping their authority; instead of working with landowners, they are eager to cite and fine them. This has
been the case ever since redevelopment activities have been underway in the area. Many property owners in the area feel the same
as him; they feel preference is being given to big developers at the detriment of longtime residents and property owners in the
area. He has not been adequately informed of these changes.
          Chris Barrigan echoed Mr. Richey’s comments about the proposed closure of Taylor Avenue near WSU. His
neighborhood has been greatly inconvenienced and Mr. Richey has done a great deal of research into the matter. He asked that the
Council take his comments and the requested closure under advisement and approve the road closure because it is the right thing
to do for the community.
          Tim Nestoriak also echoed the request for the closure of Taylor Avenue south of the WSU campus; he discussed the
increased traffic over the years and the impact that it is having on the safety of residents living on the street. Other roads have been
closed and this has forced traffic onto Taylor Avenue, which is unfair to residents living there. He appreciates the radar signs but
agreed they will only be effective in the short term. A gate would not be too expensive and there are other options for routing
traffic through the area as desired by WSU.
          Stuart Richards also echoed the comments made regarding the requested closure of Taylor Avenue; he also does not
believe the radar signs will have a long-term impact on the traffic and rates of speed on the road.
          Linda Sanchez stated she was grateful for the large American flag flying over Washington Boulevard during Pioneer
Days that brought people together and encouraged hope and peace. She stated sometimes when she listens to Council meetings,
she gets the feeling the founding fathers and the U.S. Constitution has been forgotten and the younger generation does not know
about the history of the nation. She asked for a ceremonial item to be included on future agendas to provide some education about
the Constitution.
          Teresa Bramwell commented on the changes to the Ogden Bend Mixed Use Zone; she was not involved in Ogden when
the taking of the land occurred to make up the Ogden Bend Plan area, but she has researched the current ownership of the land and
found it has been sitting vacant in its current state since 2014 with no action. She noted the mailing address for the landowner is a
vacant building in Provo City and she still does not have answers about the current owner. She understands the frustration of the
long-time landowners and residents of Ogden City who are frustrated by this situation and by being harassed by Code
Enforcement.
          Brooke Barrigan echoed the sentiments expressed by her neighbors regarding the requested closure of Taylor Avenue.
She relayed personal experiences that she and other residents have had due to increased traffic on Taylor Avenue; there are several
children, some of them disabled, who live on Taylor Avenue, and they are not being cared for and protected by the City.
          Emily Beeli stated she was pleased by Mr. Brierley’s presentation regarding the proposed amendments to the Ogden
Bend Mixed Use Zone area; she is pleased by reduced restrictions for property owners in the community because residents should
be free to do what they want with their homes and properties. She stated she has faced restrictions on her home and property, and
it has been very costly and time consuming to try to deal with those restrictions.
          Acting Chair White invited Administration to respond to the comments about the requested closure of Taylor Avenue.
          Mayor Nadolski first addressed Mr. Jensen’s comments and advised him to meet with representatives of the Community
and Economic Development Department to have his questions answered. He then addressed the comments regarding Taylor
Avenue, noting the petition to close the street has not been acted upon and that the radar signs are temporary in nature. Efforts are
underway to get the signs located in their permanent condition, but the City wanted to have something in place before school
resumes. He stated he is not sure that the road closure will be the final action, but more work will be done in an effort to respond
to the concerns of the residents. He then noted he agreed with Ms. Sanchez’s comments regarding the impact the American Flag
had on the community over the course of the Pioneer Days celebration. He added he will need more specific information about
Ms. Beeli’s concerns regarding restrictions on landowners in the community.
          Acting Chair White also addressed Ms. Sanchez’s comments and noted that each year, Council member Hyer does a
good job of recognizing Constitution Day. She addressed Ms. Beeli’s comments as well and indicated that in addition to City
Codes, the City is required to adhere to State and National Building Codes, and that could be contributing to some of the
regulations that are concerning to residents.
