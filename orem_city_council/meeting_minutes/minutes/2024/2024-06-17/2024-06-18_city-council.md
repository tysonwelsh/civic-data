Utah Renewable Communities (URC) Programs Presenter: Christopher Thomas and Dan
Dugan, URC Board

Mr. Dugan is a council member from Salt Lake City. He introduced himself and Mr.
Thomas. the chair for Utah Renewable Communities. Mr. Thomas explained that the Utah
Renewable Communities (URC) is a coalition of 18 communities partnering with Rocky
Mountain Power to design a new clean energy program. The URC program aims to offer
customers a choice to supplement their current energy mix with additional clean energy at a
minimal cost increase, with a goal to achieve net-100% by 2030. They work to empower the
communities to be the change for a sustainable tomorrow.

Mr. Thomas continued to explain the reason why it is important to join the URC. It gives
energy choices, it will help make climate progress by 2030, it supports clean energy
development, it reduces air pollution and no other program is as impactful for as many. When a

community has new clean energy as part of the power system it reduces pollution because it
means that other power plants don't have to produce as much. By adding 400 metric tons of solar
that would prevent 700.000 metric tons of carbon dioxide. In Utah County, it would prevent
1,500 Ibs of PMw.5, NOx, SO2, and VOC pollution (est.) annually. Mr. Thomas moved on to
how the program will accomplish these things. The program will acquire utility-scale clean
energy, it will match consumption with clean energy by 2030, it will host a competitive
solicitation this year, it will resource connect to PacifiCorp transmission, and it will be online by
December 2029. The program could acquire 1,200,000 annual MWh. In the communities that
have been included, 80% of the residents are part of it, 30% of the commercial and 5% of
industrial.

Mr. Thomas discussed potential projects that include solar, wind, and geothermal battery
storage in Utah, Idaho and Wyoming. Between the program and the grid, it is believed that they
could achieve net 100% renewable energy by 2023. The City Council asked questions about
funding for the program. Mr. Thomas and Mr. Dugan explained that there is some federal grant
money available. They spoke about the cost to participating customers. The estimated cost for
the program is between $2 and $7 extra per month, on average, for the typical household. There
is a plan to offer enhanced bill credit for up to $7 per month for qualifying households. The
initial rate will be set by the Utah Public Service Commission and may be adjusted once per year.
There were discussions about the opt out vs opt in choices.

Mr. Thomas explained who would part of this program, which is the 18 URC
communities, Rocky Mountain Power and State of Utah agencies (ie. Utah Public Service
Commission, the Division of Public Utilities and Office of Consumer Services). In the 2024
Utah legislative session, SB214 S1 made changes to the Community Renewable Energy Act,
removing the requirement that a municipality or county adopt a resolution no later than
December 31, 2019, stating a net-100% renewable energy goal. At its June 2024 meeting, the
URC board adopted Resolution 24-05, laying out a pathway for communities to join the URC
effort. The pathway to join the URC includes a final payment by August 31, 2024, adopting the
required participation ordinance within 90 days of program approval by the Utah PSC and
Orem’s cost would be $71,474.52 paid over two fiscal years. After these costs, the program
should be self-sustaining, meaning that administrative costs will be covered through the program
rate paid by participating customers.

North Pointe Waste Special Service District Presenter: Neil Schwendiman, North Pointe Solid
Waste District

Mr. Tschirki introduced Mr. Schwendiman from the North Pointe Solid Waste Special
Service District. Mr. Schwendiman gave a history of North Point. The services operations

include municipal solid waste (residential curbside, commercial front load, roll off, small
commercial and public self haul), construction and demolition waste, green waste, clean
concrete, household hazardous waste and recycling. He reviewed Orem statistics and costs over
the years. The future of North Pointe Solid Waste is an updated master plan from 2021 and
completion of the master plan in 2022. The master plan scope included determining the
feasibility to expand current location vs. building a second transfer station within the district,
determining the condition of the facility and recommend improvements, and provide estimates of
all aspects of the master plan. The master plan findings were the installation of new stormwater
infrastructure and paving repairs, replace damaged siding to the transfer station buildings, repair
structural issues in transfer station buildings, conduct an engineering assessment of concrete
tipping floor, make site operational improvements to workflow and customer services, study the
two viable options to expand the current facility, and expanding the current site remains the most
cost-effective option.

Mr. Schwendiman spoke about the Northern Utah Environmental Resource Agency
(NUERA) which includes in 2022 lining a new cell which is 26 acres and should last about 5
year, in 2022 installing a gas collection system, recently acquired an additional 140 acres with
the SITLA lease for the Bayview landfill, the SITLA lease extended through December 31, 2082
and the current estimated life of the landfill is 99+ year. He summarized his presentation with

2023-2024 School Year Safety in Review Presenter: Scott Spieth, Police Captain and Neil
Skougard Police Sergeant

Captain Speith reported on the 2023-2024 school year safety review. He began with a
chart showing the upward trend in general youth arrests over the last 3 years, but despite the
trend, data shows that our schools continue to remain safe locations. He believes that officers in

events at Orem Schools, they have a Cookies with Cops program that provides positive
interactions with law enforcement, and the COP reading program for classes from kindergarten
to 3rd grade.

Victim Assistance Presenter: Renee Flitton, Victim Advocate Supervisor, Heidi Francis, Victim
Advocate and Aiza Stevens, Victim Advocate

Ms. Flitton introduced herself and her department, Ms. Francis and Ms. Stevens. She
explained the purpose of the victim assistance department. They work with victims of crime,

on the rise, which is a concern for them.

Public Hearing - Enterprise Fund Transfer Budgeted for FY 2024-2025 Presenter: Brandon
Nelson, CPA, Finance Director

Mr. Nelson explained that the state legislature requires a public hearing for all Enterprise
fund transfers that get made out of the Enterprise funds and into any other fund, particularly into
our general fund. The Enterprise fund provides basic services. The cost of supplying these
services to City properties is included in the utility rates. The state auditor requires cities to
charge themselves the retail rate of these services and pay for these services from the general
fund. To pay from the general fund the City has three choices: 1) raise taxes, 2) cut services, or
3) transfer funds. The City has opted to transfer funds. The City is now recording that as revenue
to those particular Enterprise funds. The net result to both the general fund and the enterprise
fund is zero. Mr. Nelson reviewed these funds and transfers in his slideshow presentation.

Public Hearing - Ordinance - Adopt the City of Orem FY 2024-2025 Budget Presenter:
Brandon Nelson, CPA, Finance Director

Mr. Nelson is presenting the final budget proposal for FY 2024-2025 in the amount of
$160,124,695. He reviewed the budget calendar which included the following dates: January 5-6
Council determined goals and area of focus at the retreat, in the March 12 work session revenues
and compensation was reviewed, in the March 28 work session operational, fleet, and IT changes
were reviewed, in the April 23 work session capital items were reviewed, in the May 14 city
council meeting the City Council approved the tentative budget and June 18 is the final approval
of the budget. The budget highlights include: 1) projected FY 2024-2025 revenues increased
$7,779,834 over FY 2023-2024 budgeted revenues, primarily due to franchise taxes, ambulance
fees, water user fees, water reclamation user fees, and interest earnings revenues. 2) funded the
city’s compensation program including step adjustments and merit increases for all other
benefited employees. 3) Funded market adjustments for employees based on a market analysis
completed by the city’s HR division. 4) No health insurance increase. 5) Fleet replacement
totaled $3,475,500. 5) Capital projects totaled $17,139,261.

Mr. Nelson displayed the following budget items in comparison to previous years. The
FY 2024-2025 budget revenue overview is revenues $135,375,910, transfers $24,706,785, and
appropriation of surplus $42,000. For a total of $160,124,695. He explained the major revenue
funds and the general fund revenue. The FY 2024-2025 budget expenditure overview is
personnel $63,726,954, operations $71,415,243, and capital $24,982,498. For a total of
$160,124,695. He explained the expenditure by fund, general fund additions, fleet replacements,
capital projects and debt services funds. Mr. Nelson gave a summary of the enterprise funds,
which include the water fund for $19,645,573, water reclamation for $15,648,864, storm water
for $6,492,002, recreation fund for $4,556,200, and solid waste fund for $5,457,000. In
comparison with other cities, Orem has the lowest total utilities by comparison. Orem’s average
cost is $117.48 and the average is $149.42.
