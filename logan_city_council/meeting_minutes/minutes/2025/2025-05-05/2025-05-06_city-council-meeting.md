Mayor’s Budget Message for FY 2026 Budget Presented to City Council May 6, 2025
I present to you the Fiscal Year 2026 Mayor Proposed Budget. I thank the Citizens of
Logan, the City Council, and the Departments of the City for their cooperation in
establishing this balanced budget. While we do not have all the resources, we wished we
had, we are committed to doing our best with the resources that we do have.
In the general fund, which is shared by many departments including police, fire, public
works, and parks and recreation, as well as all other general government services we
provide, we continue with a collaborative approach to the Target Based Budgeting
principles we have used for many years. The goal again is to give departments flexibility

to address what they see as their most critical budgetary needs while achieving the
strategic objectives set by Council and the Mayor.
We are projecting fiscal year 2026 sales tax to remain relatively flat. Sales tax is the most
significant source of revenue to the general fund and can vary significantly with the flow
of the overall economy. We will continue to monitor the current economic uncertainty
and are hopeful that local and statewide sales will stabilize and begin again to grow
sustainably.
Property tax revenues to the City do not necessarily rise as property values rise. While it
is true that a specific property valuation performed by Cache County may influence the
specific amount a certain taxpayer might pay in property tax from one year to the next,
the total amount of property tax revenue that comes to the City from all existing
taxpayers remains the same. This process is mandated by the state legislature. In other
words, unless the City raises the property tax levy through “Truth in Taxation,” property
tax revenue from all existing taxpayers remains flat. We believe it is better policy to raise
the property tax levy through small incremental increases, than through large property
tax rate corrections done periodically. To that end, we are proposing to raise the general
fund and library property tax levies by approximately 3 %.
Parks and Recreation is proposing to add a position to the park areas budget. This
position, while new to the general fund, was previously in the recreation center
maintenance fund budget. With the dissolution of the recreation center maintenance fund,
this position is proposed to be moved to the general fund.
We are proposing to add two new Firefighter EMT Advanced positions to the emergency
medical services fund. As calls for service increase, we must continue to hire additional
EMT staff, as revenues permit.
Our employees are a critical asset for the city and we must reward and retain
them. Although amounts may vary, we have budgeted an average of 4 % Pay for
Performance increase.
The increase in our insurance premiums for this coming fiscal year is 9.9 %, which is a
welcome change compared to 29 %, 15 %, and 7 %, in the three previous fiscal years.
Health care premiums for employees are paid entirely by the City, so this is a significant
benefit for our employees. This approach is particularly beneficial to those employees on
the lower end of the pay scale. We believe our employees value and appreciate our
approach to funding employee healthcare, and we sincerely value and appreciate our
employees.
In summary, my number one goal as Mayor is wise fiscal management. We are
accomplishing a wide variety of capital projects and investment in infrastructure, but we
do so in a fiscally responsible way.
As Mayor -- along with my outstanding team -- we pledge to do our very best to provide
excellent services and quality of life for our citizens as we use our resources wisely and
meet the challenges ahead.

RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a recommendation of approval
to the Municipal Council for a rezone of five (5) properties totaling approximately 1.36
acres located at 310 West 1400 North (TIN# 05-134-0001; -0002; -0003; -0004; -0005)
from Mixed Residential 20 (MR-20) to Mixed Residential 12 (MR-12) in the Bridger

Land Use adjoining the subject property
North: CS: Commercial Uses East: MR-12: Residential Uses
South: NR-6: Residential Uses West: MR-12: Residential Uses

PROJECT
The proponent is requesting to rezone five (5) properties for a total of 1.36 acres of
property from MR-20 to MR-12. Developed in the 1990's as a five (5) 4-plexes totaling
20 dwelling units in the subdivision. Originally, the five (5) buildings were each
individually owned with co- ownership of the land maintained and governed by the five
building owners via the West Pointe Homeowners Association (HOA). The intent of the
subdivision in 2022 was to change the nature of the ownership structure from a
"condominium," where only the buildings are individually owned, to a condition where an
owner owns both the building and the land underneath. The upzone to MR-20 was required
to legally process the new subdivision. The purpose of the subdivision was to legitimize
the current division of improvements & ownership and tie those improvements &
ownership to the real property located underneath. No new structures were built, and the
density did not change. The Municipal Council required the property owners to place a
deed restriction that required an HOA, or similar entity, to maintain the common areas,
e.g., driveways, open space, etc, with the agreement to downzone the parcels once the final
plat was recorded. The final plat was recorded in June 2023 and the representative is now
applying for the downzone as agreed upon in PC #22-037.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), adopted in 2008, identifies these properties as Mixed
Residential (MR) The General Plan, a nonregulatory visioning plan, describes MR areas
as being intended for a diverse range of housing options located near employment centers
and service areas. Mixed Residential is typically located near commercial zones.

LAND DEVELOPMENT CODE

of housing options, including multi-family housing. MR-12 is a lower mixed residential

The FLUP and General Plan show this entire block are zoned as MR-12 areas. The rezone
to MR-20 was site specific in order to subdivide the parcel that has existing 4-plex
structures. Leaving the site zoned as MR-20 could allow for more units to be built than
already exist. The

MR-12.

RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission recommend approval to the Municipal
Council for a rezone of approximately 4.47 acres of property located at approximately 754
West 600 North (TIN# 05-061-0004) from Public (PUB) to Commercial Services (CS).

Land use adjoining the subject property
North: IP: Industrial Uses East: COM: Commercial Uses
South: PUB: Public Uses West: PUB & CS: Public & Commercial Uses

Background
In January of 2025, Cache Valley Transit District (Connect) completed their new modern
and larger facility located in North Logan City. All operations and equipment from this
location at 754 West 600 North have moved to the new property, leaving this facility
vacant. The applicant is requesting a rezone from Public to Commercial Service with the
intention of subsequently selling the land. The 4.47-acre property currently contains an
office/administration building, shop building, and a large vehicle carport. Landscaping and
paved parking lots complete the site improvements, with fencing enclosing the parking and
shop areas. The property is currently accessed via three driveway approaches onto 600
North and 800 West.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) identifies this area as Public (PUB). The public
designation is reserved for public facilities such as government offices, safety services,
public schools, and similar public services. A change to the FLUP to recognize the change
from a public ownership and/or use to private ownership will be handled within the General
Plan update process.

LAND DEVELOPMENT CODE
The Land Development Code (LDC) describes the CS zone as a designation that fills the
need between industrial and commercial land uses. CS areas are intended for light
manufacturing, warehousing, wholesaling, construction support services, and limited
commercial. CS zoning is not intended for residential or heavy manufacturing.

EXISTING ZONING
The current PUB zoning designation would be appropriate if the property were to be
purchased and used by a public entity. The applicant has contacted several local
government entities, including the City of Logan, to see if there was interest in the property
for a public use. According to the applicant, there was no interest from public entities. The
surrounding area is currently a variety of land uses and zoning designations. To the east,
the 11-acre property contains commercial self-storage units. North of the site is currently
vacant and previously contained a landscaping business. To the south and west, the City of
Logan owns and operates the City's Service Center and Light and Power Department
building. 600 North acts as a larger boundary in the Bridger Neighborhood, with Industrial
zoning to the north and a mix of Commercial, Mixed Use, and Commercial Service zoning
to the south.

SUMMARY
With 600 North being a boundary between Industrial and Commercial zoning in the
Bridger Neighborhood, staff considers the proposal for CS appropriate. If this property is
purchased by a private entity (most likely buyer), the FLUP designation of PUB will be
inappropriate and will be evaluated during the next FLUP amendment cycle. The
surrounding area is a mixed bag, with storage units, service centers, warehousing, and
manufacturing. The CS zone permits or conditionally permits land uses that are in a similar
character with the current surrounding land uses and built environment. The CS zone
permits office, automobile service and repair, heavy truck service and repair, contractor
supply, wholesales, warehousing, gas stations, truck stops, lumberyards, and similar uses
further specified in the LDC 17.11.030. Staff recommends the Planning Commission send
a favorable recommendation on to City Council.
