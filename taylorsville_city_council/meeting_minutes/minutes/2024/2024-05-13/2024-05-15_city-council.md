3.         REPORTS


           3. 1     Salt Lake County Public Works Report—Crystal Hulbert


 in Salt Lake County Public Works Operations Director Crystal Hulbert explained that now that the
weather was warming up, her crews were transitioning from their winter snow plowing duties to paving,
road maintenance and other warmer weather responsibilities.



Council Member Harker asked Ms. Hulbert to remind city residents what to do when they needed to report
a pothole that needed to be filled. She explained that residents could call a 24- hour hotline   if it was a
particularly large pothole. Otherwise, they could send an email to Salt Lake County operations. Finally,
they could make a report using the Citizen-Vue app.

          3. 2      Taylorsville- Bennion Improvement District—Mark Chalk



      General Manager and CEO Mark Chalk reiterated that his organization was celebrating the 50th
anniversary of the Safe Water Drinking Act. He reminded those gathered that there was a time when there
were no regulations or laws requiring that drinking water be safe for consumption.

He then reviewed the water totals for the 2024 winter season, including the current conditions of various
Utah reservoirs.


Mr. Chalk also discussed the possibility of future flooding before providing a brief overview of daily sewer
flows. He explained that there had been an increase of approximately 800,000 gallons per day, or 24 million
gallons per month.




He reviewed a list of items that should never be flushed down toilets or put down drains of any type. He
explained that this included wipes marketed as " flushable," indicating that they still clogged the sewer

system.




          3. 3      Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling Report—Pam Roberts


 ElManager and CEO Pam Roberts provided the City Council with an update regarding the first quarter
of 2024.


She offered her congratulations to WFWRD employee Jr. Vigil who received the 2024 National Waste and
Recycling Association( NWRA) Driver of the Year Award. She explained it was a direct result of the safe
and responsible manner he drove, his overall safety record, and the positive way he represented the solid
waste industry.

Ms. Roberts provided a comparison between the number of landfill vouchers that were redeemed for the
quarter, pointing out that although only 44 were redeemed by Taylorsville residents during Q1 of 2023, 131
were     redeemed   during the same time frame of 2024. Conversely, while all communities serviced by

WFWRD redeemed 640 during Q1 2023, that number dropped significantly( down to 338) in Q2, possibly
because the voucher program had attempted to go paperless.

Regarding the voucher program, Ms. Roberts explained that although there were paper vouchers available
at city hall, if one wished to use the electronic voucher, he/ she must download a QR code available on the
WFWRD or City websites. Simply taking a picture of a code was not sufficient.

There were three different municipal solid waste processing facilities in the Salt Lake Valley, and each had
a different fees. To effectively manage costs, WFWRD used the " price and proximity" method when it
came to delivering waste and recycling. That is, they considered a city' s proximity to a processing center
and the price of each center when determining which to deliver to. This method resulted in a 2023 cost
savings of$217,624 and$ 37, 648 thus far in Ql of 2024.


Ms. Roberts reported the following tonnages and diversion totals for Taylorsville over the course of the
quarter:

           3702 tons of curbside refuse delivered
           Approximately 40 tons of green waste diverted from landfills
           Approximately 19 tons of glass diverted
           737 tons of curbside recycling diverted
           Taylorsville residents diverted 18% of their waste


Ms. Roberts responded to a variety of questions from the council members regarding recycling and waste
disposal.

5.        PLANNING MATTERS


          5. 1    Discussion and Review of the Taylorsville Land Development Code Update Project
                   Chapter 13. 04: Development Standards)—Jim Spung



      Senior Planner Jim Spung explained that at some point the City Council would be presented with an
ordinance to formally approve the update, although it would not go into effect immediately. Forms would
need to be changed and staff would need to be trained, so he proposed a 4- to- 6- month lag time before it
went into effect once it was adopted.


A purpose statement was being added in order to help the public understand why it was important to regulate
development. There  was also a new applicability statement ( 13. 04. 020) which would address which

development actions would trigger the compliance code.


Mr. Spung then highlighted Chapter 13. 04 of the Land Development Code which addressed development
standards. Among other topics, the chapter focused on the following:
          Grading and Drainage
          Sensitive Lands
          Access, Connectivity, and Circulation
          Site and Building Design
          Exterior Lighting
          Operation and Maintenance


He gave a brief explanation of the types of issues that were addressed in each code section.

                                      property tax increase would result in roughly $ 325, 000 of increased
revenue, leaving a deficit of$ 207,000. Therefore, a greater increase was necessary. He pointed out that

Council Member Barbieri commented that although she did not enjoy raising taxes, she preferred to pay for

committee. The comments he had prepared were primarily in reference to the tentative budget which had
been adopted at the previous meeting as it was all that was available on line. He agreed that roads were
important and that they needed to be maintained. He also felt it was important to prioritize wages to avoid
losing experienced staff to other agencies that paid better. He felt the proposed tax increase was reasonable.

Lynn Handy explained that tax increases were necessary to support the residents' way of life. It was critical
to provide police protection, economic development, competitive employee wages and road maintenance.
If the City failed to keep up with cost increases, it would become increasingly difficult to get caught up. He

Chair Cochran also agreed, explaining that people generally wished to live in a city that was safe and well

responsible.




Council Member Knudsen was pleased and grateful to see that items had been cut from the budget. He did
not like the idea of increasing taxes but agreed that it was necessary. He wanted to continue to be a" pay as
