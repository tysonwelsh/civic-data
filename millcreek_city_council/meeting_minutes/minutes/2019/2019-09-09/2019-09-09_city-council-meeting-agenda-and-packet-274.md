1. Discussion of a Resolution and Interlocal Agreement for a Boundary Adjustment with
Salt Lake City for an area bordered by 1300 East Street, Highland Drive, Miller Avenue
and Woodland Avenue, and the roundabout at approximately 2300 East Street 2700
South Street
Mayor Silvestrini said the Council would discuss the item then take action on it at the
September 23™ Council meeting. Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek worked with Salt Lake City
Council Members and staff on the interlocal agreement. He said the Salt Lake City Council
considered the matter at their meeting on September 3™ and the Millcreek Council was
present. John Brems handed out an amended interlocal agreement to the Council and
highlighted the changes. The changes were; in Section 3, language about disparagement was
removed, and in Section 19, each city would bear its own litigation costs and expenses. Mayor

Silvestrini said Salt Lake City would agree to two boundary adjustments in the areas of 1300
East Street, Highland Drive, Miller Avenue and Woodland Avenue, and the roundabout at
approximately 2300 East 2700 South. The County Surveyor will supply local entity plats for
the cities by mid-October. Millcreek has agreed to pay Salt Lake City the current property tax
revenue ($61,000 per year) for the area in the Millcreek city center for the next ten years and
any increase in revenue the property generates will go to Millcreek. Council Member
Marchant said even though Millcreek would pay Salt Lake City what they had been
previously receiving, Millcreek would get the future revenue and increases. Brems said the tax
rates between the cities may be different. Mayor Silvestrini said the advantage of the
agreement is that Millcreek will own the property and can plan for it in the city center.

Mayor Silvestrini gave a history on the drafting of the interlocal agreement. He said Millcreek
initially approached Salt Lake City in a collaborative way to acquire the entirety of the
Brickyard Plaza, then the State Legislature took the matter up, but Salt Lake City would not
give up the Brickyard Plaza without a fight. He said it would have ended in a bidding war
with property owners in the Plaza. He said this agreement was a good deal for Millcreek. The
agreement also has the two cities working together collaboratively on future transportation
and planning efforts. Council Member Jackson said she was happy with the outcome. Mayor
Silvestrini said he had received comments on the roundabout property. The roundabout has
not been maintained by the owner, the Department of Transportation, in Salt Lake City. He
said owning the property will help keep the property well maintained as a gateway to the City.
Council Member Marchant said the highest priority was having full control over the city
center area. He said without the Salt Lake City piece, the City could not have a cohesive city
center.

Mayor Silvestrini said the Council will address the agreement as an action item at the
September 23" Council meeting and will then set a date for the public hearing on the
boundary adjustment for November. Both cities will adopt an ordinance on the boundary
adjustment and then the adjustment will go into effect July 1, 2020, per state law.

2. Planning Items First Reading:

a. ZM-19-015, (1) rezone from R-1-21 to R-1-10, (2) adjust the boundary of the Foothills
and Canyon Overlay Zone (FCOZ) and Residential Compatibility Overlay Zone
(RCOZ), and (3) request a setback reduction from 30 feet to 18 feet for a project
located at 3898 East Thousand Oaks Circle
Robert May said the item was a multi-level request. May highlighted a map of two cul-de-
sacs abutting each other; Thousand Oaks Circle and Covecrest Drive. The six parcels and
lots of property between the cul-de-sacs are under the ownership of the applicant, and the
request is to reconfigure them into a five-lot subdivision. Lots 4 and 5 would be rezoned to
R-1-10, Lot 1 would remain R-1-10, Lots 2 and 3 would remain R-1-21, and Lot 4 will
have FCOZ removed and RCOZ added. Mayor Silvestrini asked if the removal of property
from the FCOZ would restrict the City’s ability to designate the building area on the lot.
May said no, RCOZ would allow that and would also limit the height. May said staff
found that the request was in compliance with the General Plan Future Land Use Map and
the proposed rezones and boundary adjustments were consistent with the neighborhood.
He recommended that the reduction of the setback be approved through the subdivision
plat process and that the rezone be approved with a development agreement that limits the
density and prohibits a through street connecting the cul-de-sacs. The Mt. Olympus

Community Council recommended approval of the rezone and map amendment with the

b. ZM-19-014, rezone from R-1-8 to R-2-8 subject to a development agreement for a
single duplex located at 3419 South 1300 East
Blaine Gehring said the applicant was seeking to rezone property located at 3419 South
1300 East from R-1-8 to R-2-8 to facilitate construction of a new duplex. The subject
property is a .38-acre parcel and is adjacent on the south to a new planned unit
development (PUD) by the same developer. There is an existing single-family home
between the subject parcel and the PUD. Currently the subject property is located adjacent
to R-2-8 Zoning to the south and across the street on 1300 East and adjacent to R-1-8
Zoning on the east and west. The property has an existing single-family dwelling located
on the premises which will be demolished. The duplex will be similar to those in the PUD,
though not part of it. The Planning Commission sent a favorable recommendation of the
rezone subject to a development agreement with the following condition: each unit shall
have similar appearance, with slight variance of color and material to the units in the
adjacent PUD known as The Meadows of Millcreek.

2.2 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-30, Approving an Interlocal
Cooperative Agreement with Salt Lake County for Public Works Services

John Miller said the contract was for one year until June 30, 2020. The agreement is to
continue services with Salt Lake County for the budgeted amount with the same services
provided as last year. The agreement will cover services while the City does a survey for
other public works service options. Council Member Jackson asked if there would be
better recordkeeping and accounting for costs. Miller said there will be better accounting
for the public works operations. He said the administrative overhead was lacking details.
Miller said the past year contract was for $4 million which included snow removal,
pavement preservation, potholes, and general maintenance. He said because of
accounting issues, the County was not billing all the shared services, such as truck
maintenance, to all the contractees. The County then increased the contract amounts for
Millcreek, Taylorsville and Holladay. Millcreek’s contract went up to $5.7 million.
Millcreek contracted with an independent consultant to initiate a feasibility study of
whether to continue services with the County, do the services independently as a city, do
the services with a service district. Millcreek has a strong partnership with Taylorsville
and Holladay. The cities have decided they enjoy the services provided by the County but
do not feel some of the costs are justified. Mayor Silvestrini said he has met with the
Mayors and staff of the other cities and the County. He said Millcreek is doing the study
with Holladay and Taylorsville.
