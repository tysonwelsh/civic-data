DISCUSSION ITEM(S)

Murray City Center Form Based Code - Review of the Latest Form Based Code Draft for the
Downtown Area

Zachary Smallwood introduced Mark Morris to provide an update on the form-based code. Mr.
Smallwood said the full draft of the code was provided in the packet.

Mr. Morris provided a brief summary of the process they’ve been through. He said that the steering
committee met and worked on the draft and highlighted some changes that were made. Mr. Morris
reviewed the questions that were brought up in the last Planning Commission meeting he attended.

There was a question about the type of trees that would be planted. They’ve chosen a species that
will be lower maintenance. He said they’ve abbreviated the code’s section regarding food trucks
and carts by referring to the city’s code on food trucks and vending carts. He said they clarified the
requirements for food carts, stating that there must be a minimum 100 feet between vending carts,
that vending cart must be located no closer than 30 feet from a single-family zone, and that they
shall not be located within 100 feet of the primary entrance of an existing restaurant use.

Commissioner Pehrson asked if they should add something about Salt Lake County code for food
carts. Mr. Morris said when a business license is obtained, the applicant will have to get a permit
from the Salt Lake County Health Department. They agreed to add verbiage stating the applicant
must comply with Salt Lake County Health Department code.

A discussion was had regarding food carts. Commissioner Pehrson asked about the logistics at
events where there are multiple food carts, but there’s the requirement for them to have 100 feet of
separation. Mr. Smallwood said they can obtain a special permit that allows the code to be more
flexible for special events. They want to encourage more food carts to increase vibrancy in the
downtown area.

Mr. Morris highlighted various changes made to the text by the Steering Committee.

A discussion was had regarding prohibited uses. The Planning Commission appreciated that the
permitted and prohibited uses were clearly detailed in the table. Mr. Morris said the uses focus on
enhancing walkability in the community.

A discussion was had regarding the street lighting. The Planning Commission appreciated the
proposed improvements to the lighting.

                                                Page 2
Commissioner Hacker asked why there was nothing in the code to address ADA accommodations.
Mr. Morris and Mr. Smallwood said that it is overseen by International Building Code and ADA
requirements. They don’t need to put it in the form-based code.

A discussion was had about bicycles and e-scooters. The Planning Commission asked if
requirements should be addressed in the code. Mr. Smallwood said those things don’t fall within
their purview. They are addressed by Engineering. They discussed making sure there are bike racks
addressed in code.

Mr. Morris spoke about signage, saying that they added guidelines for types of electronic signage
that are permitted and what’s not permitted. A discussion was had regarding criteria for sign
approval. Mr. Pehrson wants to make sure that the approval isn’t subjective. Mr. Smallwood stated
that the approval isn’t subjective. The approval is only to ensure that the sign meets the
requirements. Staff agreed to revisit the verbiage about neon signs that’s clearer.

A discussion was had regarding medical cannabis pharmacies. The text is conflicting as to whether
they are permitted. Mr. Morris said he would confirm that the text agrees with state regulations.

A discussion was had about the timeline for adoption of the code. Mr. Morris said, once the text is
revised, the Planning Commission will need to recommend it for approval by the City Council. The
City Council would then vote to adopt the code, which will replace the current MCCD code. Mr.
Smallwood said they’ll have a public hearing in April.

The Planning Commission asked questions regarding Rockworth’s involvement and compliance
with the form-based code, building heights, residential stories and setbacks, requests for zone
changes, permitted building materials, storm water management, existing and replacement
trees/tree spacing, restrictions of bulb-out intersections, how pocket parks will work, size
requirements for open spaces, programming for open spaces, and EV charging stations. Mr.
Smallwood indicated that Rockworth has been working hand in hand with city staff to ensure that
their proposal will meet the form-based code requirements and has resulted in an overall better
form-based code because of their input.

A discussion was had regarding public parking. Commissioner Pehrson expressed concerns about
having enough parking. Mr. Morris said they are trying to encourage a more efficient use of parking
based on uses at different times of the day. Mr. Smallwood said they can look at the parking more
closely. They talked about how the public parking credits will work. Mr. Morris said they want to
avoid having too much parking as well. Unused parking is a waste of potentially productive space.
Mr. Wilkinson said this is pertaining to off-street public parking. They want to allow businesses to
count that as part of their parking requirement.

Commissioner Pehrson asked how the appeal process moves forward in the form-based code. Mr.
Morris said that appeals won’t be decided by one staff member only. They discussed enforcement if
an appeal is denied, and the applicant does not conform. Mr. Smallwood said that is addressed in
the administrative chapter of the code.

A discussion was had about residential hospitality requirements. Mr. Morris said hospitality
businesses will not be allowed to have sleeping rooms at ground level. The ground level is meant for
active uses. He will change the text to define that more clearly.

Commissioner Klinge asked about pedestrian pass-throughs. Mr. Morris showed a map with
potential locations for connections. He said some of them will be vehicle connections. They are
designed to break up large blocks of development. Property owners are permitted to create their
own connections as well.

Mr. Smallwood asked the Planning Commission about their comfort level in moving forward to the
next phase in adopting the code, once the text was modified with comments from the meeting. The
Commissioner’s indicated they felt comfortable moving forward.

Mr. Morris emphasized that the code can be revised after it’s implemented, and they’ve had a
chance to see how it works. The commissioners agreed that they are willing to move forward once
the text is revised.

The Planning Commission thanked Mr. Morris for his presentation.
