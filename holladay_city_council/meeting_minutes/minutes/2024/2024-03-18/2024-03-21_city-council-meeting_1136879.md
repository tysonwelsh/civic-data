IV.     Public Hearing on Proposed Amendments to Chapter 13.14.031 Accessory Dwelling Units.
Mayor Dahle praised the quality of the staff report and explained that Council Members rely on City
Staff to provide the information that is necessary to make responsible decisions.

Carrie Marsh, Planner explained that what is before the Council is a proposed ordinance amendment
to the existing Accessory Dwelling Unit (“ADU”) Ordinance. In 2021, the State mandated that
internal accessory dwelling units (“I-ADU”) be permitted uses within residential zones.
Municipalities were allowed to place additional regulations on external accessory dwelling units (“E-
ADU”). The code was then amended to only allow E-ADUs on lots that are half an acre or larger. A
series of public hearings were held with the Planning Commission in 2021 and then it came to the
City Council.

Holladay City Council Meeting Minutes 03/21/24                                                2
Ms. Marsh explained that in the Planning Department, residents often apply for permits to add an
ADU to their property, often in the form of a garage with a unit above. The Planning Department is
unable to issue a permit for existing structures that are not part of the main structure for parcels that
are less than half an acre, as that is not in line with the current code. She clarified that those structures
can be built and used for personal use, but those units cannot currently be rented out.

Last year, direction was received from the City Council to look at the ADU Ordinance and specifically
to look at converting existing accessory buildings into permitted E-ADUs. Ms. Marsh explained that
this would include some existing buildings with occupiable space. Those could be converted to
become a rentable ADU. The proposed changes to the code are in line with that direction and look
at some of the current issues preventing the conversion of an existing accessory structure.

Ms. Marsh noted that the Staff Report includes a Summary of Changes Comparison Chart. She
reported that there were several meetings held with the Planning Commission and the public hearing
was open for all three Planning Commission Meetings. The Staff Report includes the current
language, the original City Staff recommendations, and the Planning Commission recommendations.
She clarified that the Planning Commission was not comfortable with looking at the conversion of
existing structures. The Commission recommended allowing E-ADUs on lots smaller than half an
acre, but added some other restrictions on the height and proposed that there be increased setbacks.

Mayor Dahle reported that there will be initial discussions now and during the Work Meeting.

        a.      3900 South Update – Avenue Consultants
Mayor Dahle noted that Avenue Consultants was hired approximately nine months ago to work on
the second phase for 3900 South, to put a trail between 2300 East and the Jordan River Parkway. The
project was to look at a concept to develop further and develop some cost estimates. Blair Tomten
from Avenue Consultants shared information on the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan. It
included Holladay, Murray, Cottonwood Heights, Taylorsville, and Midvale. It created a regional
network of active transportation routes. 3900 South was the number one project across the
participating cities. During the Mid-Valley Active Transportation Plan process, there was a lot of
public outreach conducted as well as open houses and surveys. 3900 South was a clear priority.

A map of the six-city regional network was shared. Ms. Tomten pointed out 3900 South on the map
and noted that there are already bicycle lanes up the hill from 2300 East. This project will take a
shared-use path all the way west to the Jordan River. It was next explained that the purpose of the
project is to refine and set the project scope, seek community input, and ensure that the vision includes
the voice of the public. Additionally, the intention is to complete a 30% design and understand the
impacts and implementation costs. The goal is to take the first step towards implementation.

For this project, it was important to make sure that residents from Holladay, Millcreek, and South
Salt Lake City all had the opportunity to provide input about what they would like to see. The public
communication started last year and there were a number of outreach events. A summary of the
survey responses was on the website as well as photographs from outreach events.

The cost design and estimate information was shared. This was looked at in three segments. The
segments were designed to make sense of the different cities in the area. Segment 1 is where South
Salt Lake is located, Segment 2 is Millcreek, and Segment 3 is both Millcreek and Holladay. The
desire was to end each segment in a logical location, so it goes from 2300 East to 900 East. Some
aerial images were shared to illustrate the areas that are being discussed. The location of the shared
use is on the south side of the road through Holladay and Millcreek. No travel lanes will be taken
away, but things will be shifted to accommodate what is contemplated.

Mayor Dahle asked about the right-of-way that will need to be purchased for this to move forward.

will be a lot of temporary easements to rebuild driveways and tie-in landscaping that will be disturbed
by construction. There will be very little real acquisition needed. It was noted that there are a lot of
cyclists that use 2000 East. A rendering of Highland Drive was shared next, which included a

         b.     Highland Drive Update- Joe Bolton.
Assistant City Engineer, Joe Bolton shared some background information and explained that in 2021,
five potential cross-sections for Highland Drive from Arbor Lane to Van Winkle were presented to
the City Council. From those five options, the City Council narrowed them down to two cross-section
concepts. The public was surveyed and the five-lane cross-section was preferred. During that time,
high-level data collection and traffic modeling was conducted. The findings were presented to the
City Council in November 2021. Based on those findings, the Council chose the three-lane cross-
section. City Staff applied for grant funding in the fall of 2022. WFRC reviewed the application but
wanted more information about the traffic impacts on surrounding areas. As a result, a more in-depth
traffic study was conducted in 2023 to respond to the feedback received.

The 2023 traffic study recommends against the three-lane cross-section, as it will cause all
intersections to fall below acceptable levels of service by 2030. As a result, City Staff recommends
moving forward with the five-lane cross-section option instead. The cross-section will still maintain
a center turn lane, a 10-foot shared-use path, and buffer zones. It will also keep traffic at acceptable
levels of service. The first study was higher level in nature and did not account for Holladay Hills or
the Holladay Crossroads being built out. That will add quite a bit of traffic to Highland Drive. Since

an additional study was done.

Mr. Bolton discussed the level of service. He explained that this is the average delay at an

The preference of the Council was the three-lane cross-section, but there were concerns expressed
