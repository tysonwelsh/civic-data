HOUSING CONNECT 2024 AUDITED FINANCIAL STATEMENT 
• The audit report for the housing authority revealed three (3) significant deficiencies findings, including an ongoing 
issue with the Interfund Balances. There were no material weaknesses and there is a Clean opinion for Uniform 
Guidance (Single Audit), no noncompliance, and no significant deficiencies or material weaknesses in compliance 
internal controls.  
• We did have the Interfund Balances issue repeat again this year –  Interfund Balances are where the authority is 
funding expenditures for the other programs out of a single disbursing account. As long as the authority kept track 
of them and reconciled, HUD would not make an issue of them. The OIG (Office of Inspector General) told HUD 
two years ago to get Interfund Balances down close to zero and should represent current transactions. This year, 
Controller Biesinger and his team went through and reconciled the Interfund Balances, but there weren’t enough 
funds to settle all accounts.  Auditor Porter stated the finding will go away after next year’s audit  when the 
following action is undertaken. He mentioned a resolution could be passed by the Board of Commissioners prior 
to the end of the year to write off interfunds contributed in the past by Housing Connect to cover underfunded 

2 
 
grants or other pro grams which now cannot afford to pay the interfund balances. The Board of Commissioners 
could also pass resolution(s) to convert other interfund balances to promissory notes in cases where funds can be 
generated in the future in order to remove them from the interfund balances. 
• There was no instance of fraud or illegal activity discovered; smithmarion received  full cooperation from 
management; and no material audit adjusting journals were noted.  Auditor Porter discussed that many of the 
differences between the unaudited and audited trial balances were due to the agency’s conversion to the MTW 
program. He made the suggestion to Controller Biesinger to use BDO to review the agency’s accounting of MTW 
program transactions. The last finding  was that the Unaudited FDS submission was significantly different from 
Audited FDS submission. These differences were directly correlated to the second finding, Internal Control over 
Significant Account Balances; and the Corrective Action Plan for the second finding will also address the last 
finding. Our goal for next year, 2026, is to have the Housing Connect  audit completed in six months, by June 30, 
2026. 
• Auditor Porter discussed a new rule in 2026 that if the agency earns more than $500 in interest in HCV or Public 
Housing or MTW, that money will have to be set up as an accounts payable to HUD. We should not remit this 
money to HUD until HUD compels us to. Interest earned under Business Activities is not subject to this. 
Auditor Porter stated that in 2026, the financial statements will look dramatically different. Government grants 
will be moved from Operating Revenues to Capital Contributions, so it will look like a massive loss, but it will still 
be the same, it’s just how it is presented.

• Resolution #2025 -1133: 2026 Annual Plan w/MTW Supplement to PHA Plan, Housing Choice Voucher 
Administrative Plan, including Agency Specific Waivers: COO Timmerman presented. We are asking for  approval 
of our Annual Plan with MTW Supplement, including our HCV Administrative Plan and Agency Specific Waivers. I 
presented a draft to the Board at the August meeting; we’ve had both a Public Comment period and a Public 
Hearing. What I will present to day is any changes that have been made and any public comment received, and 
then the Board can take action on it.  
• After feedback from the Board in August, we did amend our 5 -Year Goals. We increased affordable housing 
opportunities to 1,000 through a number of different ways, and the rest of the goals remained the same, with the 
exception where we changed a bit of the focus of our Resident Services Department. We will be doing that by 
supporting 600 residents with opportunities  to increase economic self- sufficiency and build wealth, promote 
housing stability and decrease barriers to housing for 1,000 households. It’s a little bigger reach, but the message 
was heard, and it’s good for us to be challenged.  
• In Chapter 2, we added a chart of Moving To Work policies and applicability of the policies to specific program and 
voucher types. In Chapter 25, we made updates to our Emergency Housing Voucher section as HUD has told us 
the funding will be ending at the end of 2026. We’ve met with HUD and with CFO Aniban and Controller Biesinger 
and the accounting team; we are choosing to absorb those households into our portfolio. We’ll be putting these 
households on a Housing Choice Voucher (HCV), and our goal is to do that starting October 1, 2025, and nothing 
will change for the household. Vice-Chair Nguyen complimented the team on figuring out how to do this without 
disrupting the families.  
3 
 
• In Chapter 4, this section of our admin plan is essentially making sure that we have everything documented so 
that we're in line to be able to prioritize these households. So, we've added a preference to make it easier for 
those households  to get on to another type of voucher. And the other preference we are adding is to give 
preference to applicants who lose federally funded rental assistance  because the program funding ends. This 
preference applies to families currently or formerly assisted by a Housing Connect-administered voucher program. 
• We officially received comments from the Resident Advisory Board (RAB) which I’ve shared, and we’ve also 
received public comments from three different individuals or groups. The first was regarding an agency -specific 
waiver we are requesting from HUD to be able to use a Certificate of Occupancy with new development or new 
renovation, in place of an inspection. W e've vetted that. We will make sure that the building, the architect, and 
the general contractor adhere to HUD guidelines for housing quality standards. The comment we received was a 
resident concerned we would be trying to get potential residents in quickly and put them in substandard housing. 
And I replied clarifying that is not the intent, and the Certificate of Occupancy ensures all of the standards  will 
continue to be met.  
• Public comment number two was received from Brian Roach at the Department of Health and Human Services 
with the Division of Integrated Healthcare. The request was to prioritize populations or preference for households 
who are losing temporary rental assist ance through the Medicaid Expansion program. It's a Medicaid housing-
related services program. As these services expand , it includes up to six (6) months of transitional rental 
assistance, and they are requesting a household be prioritized to be able to ha ve rental assistance to ensure a 
smooth transition and continued assistance. I did respond asking how many households he anticipates needing a 
voucher. And Assistant Director Roach responded they do not have an estimate at this time. We get many requests 
for preferences, and what we're recommending at this time is that we d on't move forward with the preference, 
and once they have additional information and we can understand more, then we can reconsider. 
• Public Comment number three is from Nate Cripps with the Disability Law Center regarding the need for more 
integrated housing developments and scattered site housing for those with serious mental illness.  They support 
the agency and what we’re doing but they want to ensure we’re not placing disabled households into properties 
where there’s only those with disabilities. They want us to have scattered site models and ensure folks have a 
choice. The Disability Law Center is not a big fan of the Permanent Supportive Housing model. We’ve decided 35% 
of our vouchers will be Project -Based Vouchers and I believe the Disability Law Center thought that was all 
Permanent Supportive Housing when in reality, we are also doing housing for other populations such as families. 
We’ll get a response to them and provide additional context.  
• COO Timmerman continued summarizing the RAB’s discussion. We talked about preferences and how as an 
agency, we have leaned into our waiting list being in order of date and time. It's very difficult to prioritize one 
population over another when there are so many people in need that qualify based on their income. We want to 
be able to serve niche populations and these priority populations, and it's something which continually comes up, 
and something that, as an agency, we will continue to work towards, because I think that's a priority for all of us. 
The RAB felt strongly we should not be doing a preference, specific to the Medicaid group. They stated that they 
understand the need to prioritize a specific population, including this population, for a rental subsidy.  But 
managing the waiting list by date and time feels most appropriate. They did request that this population be 
considered as we develop or bring in additional vouchers, so that's something for the board to think about as 
we're developing properties and communities.
