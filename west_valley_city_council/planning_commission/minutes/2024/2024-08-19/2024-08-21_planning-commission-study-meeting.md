ZONE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATION

ZT-9-2024: Petition by WASATCH COMMERCIAL DEVELOPERS, LLC requesting an amendment
to Sections 7-1-103 and 7-6-301 of the zoning ordinance to add a definition for the term “data center”
and to allow data centers as a permitted use in the LI (Light Industrial) and M (Manufacturing) zones
and as a permitted or conditional use in the BRP (Business/Research Park) zone. (Staff – Steve Pastorik
at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov.

Discussion: Steve Pastorik presented. Steve read a portion of the applicant’s application where they
defined “data center” “as an establishment engaging in the storage management processing and or
transmission of digital data and housing, computer and or network equipment systems, servers,
appliances and other associated components related to digital data operations”.

The applicant, Wasatch, has acquired several properties in Lake Park Corporate Center. One of those is
a property that used to be owned by Discover Card, but it is not developed. Discover Card had their
main building, but they also own property to the southwest of that. Wasatch is looking at building
several buildings on that property. For commercial uses in the BRP zone, there is a restriction that states
that no more than 60% of the square footage of the buildings in a BRP zone lot can be a warehouse or
                                                                                                Page 1 of 4
light industrial. Steve then read the applicant’s justification for the proposed change: “Data centers are
not a ‘typical warehouse’ type. In order to make them financially successful, the majority of the square
footage use needs to be dedicated to the core business. Limiting to the 60% rule would not allow us to
attract users, nor allow Wasatch to make an investment.

Data Centers are a growing requirement across multiple business sectors worldwide. Lake Park is a very
attractive location for this type of business, especially with Lake Park being in a Qualified Opportunity
Zone.

Wasatch would work closely with WVC to assure that this Data Center would be aesthetically pleasing,
not looking like a typical ‘industrial’ Building. Like WVC, Wasatch does not want our development to
look like, or to be confused for a distribution / heavy industrial development.

Another thing to note is that Data Centers on the whole are very low impact to the surrounding area.
There are very few employees (5-10), thus very low traffic and impact to the local area, as well to WVC
services (Fire, Police, Medical, etc.).”

Commissioner Winters asked if there will be customers at the data center. Steve replied that data centers,
relative to the to the size that they consume, have very few employees.

Commissioner Woodruff asked what kind of parking requirements would be assigned to a data center.
Steve replied that with this change the City doesn't have a parking standard. The closest regulation is for
a warehouse which requires one space per 2,000 square feet. But in this case, the data center would need
even less than that. Even though the City doesn’t have a standard in the code, the applicant can provide
information and make a request for a reduction in parking.

Jody added that the other data centers that have been reviewed had quite a bit of office space that were
calculated under the standard of the normal parking square footage. The ordinance should allow parking
for the different uses that a data center might have.

Commissioner Winters asked if these centers tend to be smaller than 100,000 square foot buildings. Jody
replied that the center on Beagley Road is about 100,000 square feet plus an outdoor storage area.
Commissioner Winters noticed the application asked for a permitted or conditional use and if that will
be a decision that Planning and Zoning will be asking of the Commissioners. Steve suggested that the
City make them conditional.

Commissioner Lovato asked if there is someone that reviews the usage of power and water on data
centers. Steve replied that Rocky Mountain Power would handle that and maybe the developer will be
required to put in a new transformer to have sufficient coverage to serve the building.

DISCUSSION ITEMS

1. Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone

Discussion: Steve Pastorik presented. The Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone was put in place in 1996.
The boundaries were I-215 to the west, 3100 South to the north, 3500 South to the south, and east of the
                                                                                             Page 2 of 4
property that fronts the east side of Decker Lake Drive to the east. At the time the Maverick center was
being built there were hotels and restaurants being built around the Maverick center. The intent was to
have an entertainment district with uses that were supportive of the Maverick Center and having design
standards that centered around that.

We found out recently, when the city first adopted the overlay zone there was never an ordinance
recorded to rezone the properties. Later in 2002, the area north of 3100 South added an overlay zone
with a recorded ordinance. In 2006 the overlay was reduced when the zoning was changed for the eGate
Apartments just south of Decker Lake. More recently, the overlay was lifted for the new apartments
being built right next to the Trax station. Steve showed a map showing properties south of 3100 South,
where it was thought the overlay was in place, but it is zoned C-2. Steve then showed a list of business
types that the overlay prohibits but would now be allowed in the C-2 zone, such as a fast-food restaurant
with a drive-up, hardware store, RV sales/warehousing, batting cages or mini golf.

State law requires that by the end of next year that the City have a station area plan around each of our
light rail stations. The City has a plan for Fairbourne Station but not the other three. West Valley City
was able to obtain a grant administered through Wasatch Front Regional Council that has been used to
hire a consultant to help with this process. By the end of next year, the City hopes to have a plan for the
Decker Lake area and to have adopted regulations that would help implement the plan recommended by
the Planning Commission. Steve recommends a plan in 2 phases. The first phase would be to repeal the
text of the overlay from the code, remove the overlay from the zoning map, rezone M zoned property
within the overlay to either C-2 or C-3. Phase 2 would be to enact new regulations designed to
implement a plan that will be adopted by the City.

Commissioner Lovato asked how long it would take to move from phase 1 to phase 2. Steve replied that
the City is ready to move into phase 1 right away and phase 2 needs to be completed by the end of 2025.
Commissioner Winters asked if the City should replace the overlay zone with conditional use until phase
2 is completed. Steve replied that the overlay zone does require everything be conditional use, and the
City must approve that use; a conditional use cannot be denied. If the City wanted to have some
restrictions, they would have to rezone and put an overlay in place.

The Commissioners agreed that phase 1 need to take place to minimize risk. There were no
Commissioners who disagreed.

Brandon Hill added that the State is going to have another legislative session that intervenes, and the
requirements are likely going to change every year for the next 10 years.


2. Alpine homes – Building Elevations for Kiowa Court PUD

Discussion: Steve Lehman reminded the Commissioners that the property was previously rezoned by
the Planning Commission and City Council to allow for twin homes. Then, the applicant, Chris Clifford,
ended up being a casualty of the Maui fires so he pulled out to deal with his property there, and
subsequently sold this lot to Alpine homes. When Alpine Homes met with Steve Lehman and Steve
Pastorik, they said they would like to see single-family detached homes as opposed to attached homes.
During that meeting Alpine showed one elevation but the Development Agreement requires a second
elevation.


                                                                                                Page 3 of 4
Steve presented the second elevation to make sure the Planning Commission is comfortable with what's
being proposed. The second elevation had the same elements of Chris Clifford’s approved elevation
with a shed roof over the garage, a covered porch, 10-12 gables, different window sizes, and brick and
stone being 20% of the elevation. In addition, the new subdivision will have guest parking spaces, a
perimeter trail, community amenities and two-car driveways. The square footage in these homes will be
2,400 square feet.

In the past, flooding has been a problem in this area, so the City just adopted a package to allow for a
storm drain to go out through this property out to 3500 South.

Commissioner Wood thinks the Development Agreement should have a third elevation. Commissioner
Winters said he would like to see more than two color variations and liked the traditional look of the
elevations. Commissioner Winters asked if there would be basements. Steve replied that these homes are
required to have basements, and the water table is low enough that there will be basements.
