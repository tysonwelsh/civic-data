II.    NEW BUSINESS

1. Discuss Design and Strategy Preferences for the Bonanza 5-Acre Site
Redevelopment:
Steve Swisher and Lisa Dove-Swisher, GTS Development, David Brint and Carly Brinla,
Brinshore Development, Katie Wilking, Berkshire Hathaway, and Joel Jennings, Method
Architecture, presented this item. Steve Swisher reviewed the goals for this project and
hoped construction could begin fall of 2026. He displayed a drawing of his vision for this
mountain town village. Council Member Dickey thought this version was harmonious
with established buildings in town. Swisher explained the site was accessible from all
sides. The village green was 1.15 acres of programmable open space. He noted there
were two entrances to the underground parking area.

Swisher displayed a solar analysis for the site. Council Member Dickey asked if it would
change by reducing the height of Building One to three stories instead of four stories.

Swisher didn’t think it would make a big difference. In the summer, the shade from
buildings would have a positive effect. In the winter, three stories would be slightly better
for less shade in that open space.

Council Member Rubell thought this site still felt like a housing development that was
forcing community gathering. He was not a fan of buildings blocking the sun. Council
Member Parigian asked about the housing number. Brinla stated the Council supported
the middle option which would provide 112 units. Council Member Parigian didn’t think
enough residential units were in this development and he felt this area was where
housing needed to be. He didn’t understand why the Council didn’t want more housing.
Council Member Rubell agreed this was a good place for housing, but he wanted to get
the HOPA project going. He thought if this district was done right, housing would be
close by with connecting trails, and parking would be in place which would enable
developers to build with parking reductions and shared parking agreements. He
asserted shared parking could be a lever used to reduce costs. He expressed concern
with the traffic circulation on the corner. He agreed with the strategy and suggested
more space between buildings and shorter buildings or more variation at the site.

Council Member Toly was not sure about having a four-story building after seeing the
shadows. She noted there was less open space in the middle of the site and asked
what changed. Swisher explained the buildings moved a bit. Also, the first time they
measured, they included the frontage protection zone (FPZ). This time they did not
include the FPZ and he stated this version was more realistic because they couldn’t
program space all the way to Kearns. Council Member Toly indicated she received a lot
of comments from the public regarding a desire for more open space. Swisher indicated
the Library Field was 1.67 acres, just to give an idea of the size of the space. He
thought the open space would feel expansive when the project was completed.

Council Member Ciraco agreed the HOPA project would give the City a lot of affordable
housing so he was not as sensitive about increasing the housing at this site. He heard
the public wanting a gathering space more than an affordable housing project.
Regarding the shadows, he thought the shade would be beneficial in the summer, so
this wasn’t completely negative. Council Member Dickey agreed with Council Member
Ciraco’s comments. Regarding affordable housing, Council Member Dickey looked at
the neighborhood in total and agreed there would be a lot of affordable housing. This
would be the green space for the rest of the neighborhood. He felt the open space was
not as open as the public wanted. He suggested doing a solar study with the three-story
building so the Council could compare the two. He wanted to understand the subsidy
required of the City, so they could determine if this had the strategic importance to the
community for Council to make this size of investment. Council Member Ciraco felt it
would be helpful to know how much of the open space was located in the FPZ. Swisher
stated they would have site plans at the next meeting, and they would address that
then.

Council Member Rubell felt the massing was heavy and the longer buildings restricted
the site. He hoped to see Buildings One and Two feel more open. Swisher suggested
creating three buildings from those two. Brinla stated splitting the buildings would
decrease the number of residential units and increase the cost. Council Member Rubell
clarified he asked for a lighter feel to the site, but not necessarily more buildings.
Council Member Dickey didn’t want to keep scaling things out, eventually they would
break the financial model, and he was concerned about that. Swisher stated they would
discuss cost at the end of the discussion.

Swisher indicated this site was a key area for open space and they would continue to
look at that. Council Member Parigian stated the splash pad would not be used year-
round, and wanted to have things at the site that were used all year. Brinla noted they
wanted to make the splash pad an art installation that could be enjoyed year-round.
Brint stated a splash pad could be used 3-4 months of the year, but then other areas
would be used at other times of the year. Council Member Rubell asserted that each
Council member had ideas but that wasn’t necessarily direction for the developers.
They expected the consultants to come back with the feasibility of these suggestions.
He asked that the recommendation be something that would be best for the site.

Wilking discussed the retail spaces for the area. She envisioned a welcoming flexible
environment where the community would naturally gather. Some options included a
brewery, restaurant/café, coffee shop, bike/outdoor shop, fitness studio, daycare,
makerspaces, life skills hub, University of Utah extension, and family-friendly draws.
Council Members Rubell and Ciraco liked all the ideas presented. Council Member Toly
asked how many retail spots would be available. Wilking stated there would be flexible
space sizes and they could pick the right size for the right business. They would narrow
down the uses that residents would want to see there and then they would make the
spaces work. Council Member Toly asked if the co-working space would be around
3,000 square feet. Wilking stated it depended, and noted a coffee shop could be a co-
working space. Council Member Dickey felt the family friendly draw was key.

Swisher stated there would be a creative space for arts. They talked to PC Create about
having a retail space for their art. They also talked to Brian about having music on the
green. Brinla stated she talked to Park City Summit County Arts Council, and they
proposed a multidisciplinary art space, where classes and performances could take
place and events could happen without requiring a long-term lease. Swisher added that
in their discussions with arts organizations, there was not much interest in having a
permanent spot. In talking with Jocelyn Scudder, Arts Council Executive Director, she
had stated they were finalizing their master plan and this kind of space was identified as
a need. Council Member Toly noted changing rooms were needed in this design, to
which Brint indicated they planned for a kitchen and storage space and they would look
at a changing area as well. Council Member Parigian asked if this was a black box, to
which Swisher stated no, but it was in response to Council and stakeholder feedback.
Council Member Parigian liked this concept. Brinla clarified this was a practice space
but not a theatre-type space. Swisher stated this was a space that could be used for an

hour or for a couple of days. Council Member Rubell suggested working with staff so
GTS could see what was happening with other projects around town to see how the
projects would complement each other, and discussing that at the next meeting.

Mayor Worel stated there were not many places for teens to gather in the area and she
felt this site would be welcoming to them. She suggested getting feedback from the
Youth Council. Brinla asked if the multidisciplinary art space concept was something the
Council wanted them to incorporate into the site. Council Member Rubell supported it if
it was complementary to other projects. He thought it would be cool to have a
community theatre set up that would accommodate uses that weren’t available now.
Council Member Parigian didn’t want another arts and culture district, and he thought
this was too big and too much. He thought a small place that local residents could rent
would be better. Council Member Ciraco referred to the community center and asked
that those uses not be duplicated in this project. Council Member Dickey thought it was
too small for a theatre but too big for other uses and stated they would have the
community center. Brint stated this space could be sectioned off.

Council Member Toly asked if outdoor dining would go into the village green. Swisher
thought the ends of Buildings Three and Six would be the best spots for outdoor dining.
Council Member Parigian asked how many restaurants were envisioned for the site.
Wilking estimated one sit down restaurant, a minimum of one coffee shop, and a
brewery or a third food type. Council Member Parigian didn’t think this served the locals
because there weren’t as many choices. Wilking stated they were aiming for a hang out
vibe. Swisher added they wanted a balance of uses that would appeal to a variety of
people. This area would evolve as the City evolved.

Brinla reviewed the proposed AMI/unit mix for the residential units. They planned on
having 19 units at 30%-40% AMI, 48 units at 50%-60% AMI range, 22 units at 70%-80%
AMI, and 23 unrestricted units at 80%-120% AMI. They liked having a mix of AMIs so as
a tenant’s income increased, they would not have to move out of their units. Council
Member Rubell thought serving the community workforce was the goal and he wanted
the housing to go to the local workforce. Brinla stated they would do targeted marketing.
Council Member Rubell wanted a priority for community workforce. Council Member
Toly suggested master leasing with local businesses. Brinla stated she would look at
that but she had to follow fair housing requirements. Brint stated they could master
lease the unrestricted units, but they could not do that for the restricted units.

Council Member Rubell supported housing for workforce and the community. He didn’t
want to rely on marketing to reach the community. If priority could not be given because
of funding, they he wanted to look at other funding mechanisms. He asked to discuss
the options at the next meeting, even if it meant that the project was more expensive.
Council Member Ciraco indicated that 30% AMI was not doable in Park City and stated
the HOPA project would have that low AMI. Brinla offered to make the units in that
category all 40% AMI if the Council desired. Council Member Dickey felt the Housing
Team could share data on how the existing housing stock was filled.

Swisher stated the parking garage needed to be ventilated, waterproofed, and have an
eight-foot ceiling. One level with 300 spaces would roughly cost $21,674,120. An
additional level of parking would total 554 spaces and would cost $44,399,312. Council
Member Rubell clarified he favored additional parking on this site to enable other
projects to have more open space. He asked if there was a recovery analysis on this, to
which Swisher stated no. Council Member Rubell asked to see that so the Council
would know how long it would take to pay for the parking, and then talk about it at the
next meeting. Brinla asked why Council Member Rubell wanted the additional level of
parking when underground parking could be built with a different project. Council
Member Rubell replied speed to market and this was a great location for capturing
vehicles. He noted Transit would also be at this site.

Council Member Toly indicated this was a huge amount of money for parking and she
didn’t want it used to park skiers. She asked for information from the City Engineer and
Transportation Team to see how it would affect traffic. She thought it would be less
expensive to build a parking garage at Gordo. Council Member Rubell indicated he
hadn’t seen data that Gordo parking would be less expensive and he asked staff to
bring back that data. Council Member Ciraco thought the additional parking on this site
could offset the code changes to minimize parking. Council Member Rubell felt this
opened up many opportunities and added it could also enable the uses at this
community gathering space that would be promoted. Council Member Ciraco supported
seeing a recovery analysis for this parking. Council Member Dickey didn’t want out-of-
town cars to use this parking but he felt shared parking was a good idea. Since this was
at the edge of town, he didn’t know if that was the right spot for shared parking, but he
was open to exploring it.

Brinla displayed the project revisions and the associated cost implications. Mayor Worel
asked what the additional cost was for ventilation and water pumps in the parking
garage, to which it was indicated 15%. Council Member Rubell indicated there were
strategies that could be employed to offset these additional costs that were not taxpayer
subsidies and he gave some examples. Brint stated he would leave those strategies to
the City to work out, but he needed an exact number to put in a pro forma in order to
finance the project.

Heather Sneddon, Deputy City Manager, asked if the majority of the Council wanted a
cost recovery analysis for the parking and data on what the traffic implications would be
with all that parking. Council Member Rubell also asked to know more on the traffic
circulation for the area. The Council agreed to have staff bring back data. Council
Member Dickey also wanted to know what was being accomplished with the additional
parking spaces. Council Member Toly asked if the type of use was needed in order for
staff to understand a cost recovery. Sneddon indicated staff would look at it.

Council Member Parigian stated the Council was against more housing because it
generated more traffic, yet they wanted additional parking which would add more traffic.
Council Member Rubell stated this was strategic to have a say in keeping the workforce

local in order to reduce traffic, but the additional parking would allow residents to park
there and catch a bus to another area. Council Member Parigian asked how having a
resident park there or someone out of town park there would be different. Council
Member Rubell explained that the parking would not increase the number of cars in
town but would capture the cars before they got deeper into town. Council Member
Ciraco stated the additional parking at this site would also help the City reduce parking
requirements in other developments. He thought that would result in having less asphalt
at other sites. Council Member Rubell asked for a traffic analysis to see if the parking
would work. Regarding housing on the site, he preferred fewer units so there would be
less density.
