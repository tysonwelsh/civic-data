Niederhauser is going to request $5.7 Million dollars for the homelessness services.

double that. The remaining 40o/o will be divided up with the cities that have homeless
shelters. Midvale is receiving 17% less than what was received in 2020. The City was
using those funds for police force in the area of the shelter. Midvale was excluded from
the grant funds because of seeking funds for police force only. The Utah Homelessness
Council has a goal to average less than 50 days of stay in homeless shelters. The current
average is 66 days stay in the homeless shelter. Non-recurring stay is another goal. The
main goal is to reduce returning homelessness of 29%. The state made this goal so in
some cases we are winning and some cases we are losing. The intent is to make the
visits rare, brief, and non-recurring. He said he also attended the UFA (Unified Fire
Authority) meeting. Member fees will be increased 5.5% for the next year. UFA is
currently hiring and training 16 new fire fighters. UFSA will likely have a property tax
increase because the revenues will fall below the minimum '15% retention in their budget

Stevenson.

       B.     CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO,2O21-O-22 AN ORDINANCE APPROVING
              AND ADOPTING THE AMENDMENTS MAKE TO THE PROJECT AREA
              PLANS FOR THE BINGHAM JUNCITON RDA, JORDAN BLUFFS RDA,
              AND MAIN STREET CDA
Kate Andrus said during the previous Redevelopment Agency Board Meeting, the Agency
Board adopted amendments to the three project area plans, Bingham Junction RDA,
Jordan Bluffs RDA, and Main Street CDA. The amendments to the project area plans,
however, do not take effect until the amendments are also adopted by the city council by
ordinance. The proposed ordinance would simply adopt the amendments to the plans
that have already been adopted by the RDA Board. All the amendments to the project
area plans do is allow transferring funds between project areas to fund the
Midvale City Main Street Revolving Loan Program.

The proposed resolution is to permit the Agency to transfer $1,500,000 from the Bingham
Junction RDA to the Main Street CDA in order to fund the Midvale City Main Street
Revolving Loan Program. This is a legal requirement outlined in Utah State Code Section

Rori Andreason said the past year has been challenging at best in regard to COVID and
economic conditions. Over the past year, the city has experienced a 33% turnover rate,
which greatly affects the service level to our Midvale residents.

Needless to say, other cities are experiencing the same issues and are taking measures
to hire and retain employees- Therefore, in an attempt to slow the "great resignation"
certain employee retention measures have been identified that staff believes might help
slow the turnover issue. Staff is proposing the implementation of the following employee
retention measures:

1.     Two days of Personal Time Off (PTO) to be used no later than May 1,2022.
       Employees must work with their department heads to schedule the time off.

2.     Employees who have been in their positions for at least four years will be moved
       to the mid-point of their pay range. This policy will take effect as of January 1,2022,
       requiring a $97,200 budget allocation for salary and benefits. Funds are available
       to implement this strategy due to personnel cost savings.

3      A $1,000 retention bonus will be awarded to those employees who are fully
       employed with the city as of June 1, 2022. Only employees hired prior to this
       retention measure being approved by Council will be eligible for the retention
       bonus.

4      POLICY - All fulltime employees not operating under an employee performance
       improvement plan or other disciplinary proceedings shall achieve mid-point of their
       pay range within 4 years in a position.

5.     Employees who are assigned to be on-call during a week where there is a holiday
       shall count the holiday as time worked for overtime purposes.

A Resolution has been prepared outlining the above-mentioned employee retention
measures for Council's consideration. Staff recommends approval and implementation of
the identified employee retention measures.

FISCAL IMPACT: Funding for the (4 year) mid-point policy is $97,200, allocated through

BACKGROUND:

Brandon Fry is the owner ofparcels ofground, totaling approxim alely 2.06 acres. The parcels are
located within Sandy City and border Midvale; however, access to the parcels is offof300 East in
Midvale. Mr. Fry would like to develop the property in Midvale and under the City's requirements.
In order to do so, a boundary adjustment with Sandy City would need to occur.

The Utah Code outlines a process for boundary adjr.rshnent between rnunicipalities. l0-2-419 in the
Code identifies this process. In short, each municipality must adopt a resolution indicating an intent
to adjust the boundary, followed by a public hearing no less than 60 days after adoption ofsuch
resolution ofintent. Upon conclusion of the public hearing, the legislative bodies involved may
adopt an ordinance approving such boundary adjustment. Following such ordinance, a filing with
the Lieutenant Govemor, within 60 days, must occur. The boundary adjustment then takes effect on
either July l'1 or January l't ofthe following year, depending on whether a certificate of issuance by
the Lieutenant Govemor is issued either during the preceding November l't through April 30rh or
May l't through October 3 I'1.

Part of the process also involves a zone designation for the property upon completion ofthe
boundary adjustment. Adjacent parcels in Midvale are zoned SF-l and SFI-DO (Duplex Overlay).
Both zones are Single Family. Mr. Fry proposes a concept of l2 duplex structures totaling 24 units;
a corresponding density of approximately l1.l dulac. While this type of development may
conceptually fit within the SF1-DO zone, staffhas not completed an in-depth analysis regarding
adherence to all provisions found within the SFl-DO zone. This in-depth review would occur upon
approval of a boundary adjustment and zone designation by the City.

Should the property be placed in Midvale City, and the zone designation of SFI-DO given, the
following reviews would then need to occur: (l) Small Scale Master Plan, (2) Conditional Use
Permit, (3) Preliminary Subdivision Plan, and (4) Final Subdivision Plat. While many of these
processes rnay occur sirnultaneously, each process will requires its own approval; the Planning
Commission being the approval body for both the Small Scale Master Plan and Conditional Use
Permit; the City Council approving both the Prelirninary Plan (upon a recommendation from the
Planning Commission) and Final Plat. Each process is outlined in Chapter 17 of Midvale's
Municipal Code.

The developer commissioned LYRB to prepare an Economic Impact Analysis of the conceptual
development; the analysis finding that, if approved and developed, the proposal would yield
approximately $19,200 to the City's General Fund over a twenty (20) year period.

the Council regarding a potential boundary adjustment with Sandy City.
