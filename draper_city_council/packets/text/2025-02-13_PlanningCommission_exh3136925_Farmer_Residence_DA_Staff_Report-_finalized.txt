BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Development Agreement for approximately
0.32 acres located on the south side of 12650 S., and east of 900 E., at approximately 902 E.
12650 S. (Exhibit C & D). The property is currently zoned RA1. The applicant is requesting
that a Development Agreement be approved to allow for a front yard setback reduction for
the corner lot to accommodate the development of the site with a single-family home.

The subject property is currently vacant. The property originally contained a 1,386 ft²
single-family residence built in 1907 which was removed from the property in
approximately 2012 after a fire damaged the home. A detached garage that had been
added to the property in approximately 1946 remained on the northwest corner of the
property until it also was demolished in 2023.

Solid Construction Group, LLC applied for a building permit in April of 2024 to construct the
proposed home. The permit was returned to the applicant for revisions and has not been
issued. The proposed home did not comply with required setbacks for the property. The
submitted site plans also show a detached three (3) car garage located south of proposed
home located within the same requested setbacks as the house. The applicant is now
requesting approval of the attached development agreement (Exhibit H) on behalf of the
property owners to permit the construction of the home in the initially proposed location
on the property with a reduced front yard setback of twelve feet (12’) measured from the
property line adjacent to 900 East. Staff notes that the proposed development plan
represents an approximately eighteen-foot (18’) reduction to the required front yard
setback per the provisions of ordinance for setback measurements from private rights-of-
way.

Per DCMC 9-10-075, legally created parcels are allowed at least one driveway access to a
public street. As part of the development agreement the applicant is agreeing to forego
access to 12650 South in favor of their preferred driveway connections to the private street
(900 East). As outlined in the statement of benefits provided by the applicant, the
ownership of the land on which the private street is located is in question (Exhibit H). As
part of the agreement the property owner assumes all risks in connecting the 900 East and
agrees to indemnify and defend the City from any claims related to ownership of the road.

Development Agreement. The proposed development agreement has gone through three
reviews and still had redlines at the end of January. On January 29, 2025 the attorney for
the applicant requested that the application be forwarded to the Planning Commission
without the additional comments being addressed and agreed to make the requested
changes. During the writing of this report the applicant’s attorney provided an updated
draft agreement to the City on February 5, 2025, which is included as Exhibit I, and stated
that the applicant has accepted all changes. The draft does show requested changes and
comments in track changes for reference.

The statement of benefits required to be provided by the applicant as per DCMC
Subsection 9-5-200(C)(4) is included as Exhibit H in this report.

The pavement of the existing private road on 900 East is approximately twenty to twenty-
one feet (20’-21’) wide. As part of a development request, the private roadway is required to
be brought into compliance with the minimum private road standards. The minimum
required width for a private road serving six (6) to ten (10) single-family dwelling is thirty-
one feet (31’), which is comprised of twenty-six feet (26’) of pavement and two and one-half
feet (2 ½’) of curb and gutter on either side of the pavement. The proposed development
plans request a reduction from the required pavement width of twenty-six feet (26’) to
twenty-four feet (24’), with no curb and gutter installed. The City Fire Marshal has agreed to
accept the proposed twenty-four feet (24’) of pavement as sufficient for compliance with
the fire code if the Development Agreement is approved. The proposed site plan (Exhibit G)
shows a small portion of the proposed roadway widening occurring on neighboring
properties to the south, staff supports the proposed widening in this area only if the
owners of the neighboring properties provide signed written consent for such to the owner
of the subject property for this application and the City prior to installation of the additional
pavement width in those areas.

Per DCMC Section 9-10-075 both primary and secondary driveways are required to be a
minimum depth of twenty feet (20’). This is measured between the sidewalk, which is
typically the property line, and the garage. The proposed development plan (Exhibit G) has
driveways that are twelve feet (12’) in depth between the garages and the property line,
with variable depths between the proposed private road expansion and the garages at
twenty-seven feet (27’) to thirty-nine feet (39’).

The preservation of public parking on 12650 South is listed as one of the benefits to the city
identified by the applicant in the statement of benefits (Exhibit H). The benefit the property
owner would receive in exchange if the development agreement is approved is the ability
to construct a larger home with shorter driveway depths than would otherwise be allowed
under the ordinance. Staff has raised concerns about the potential parking issues, and
potential elimination of this benefit to the city that would be created if an Accessory
Dwelling Unit (ADU) was later added to the property. Staff has made repeated requests
that provisions limiting the property to construction of a single dwelling unit be added, but

Criteria For Approval.

The DCMC does not contain criteria for review and potential approval of a Development
Agreement. It does, however; contain a list of items that are required as part of an
Agreement and limitations to a Development Agreement, as found in DCMC Subsections 9-
5-200(C) and (D). This section depicts the required materials for such requests as:

        C.    Procedure: All development agreements, upon proper execution, shall be recorded
              with the respective County Recorder's Office, shall run with the land, and shall be
              binding on all successors in the ownership of the affected property. A development
              agreement shall contain, at a minimum, the following:
               1. A legal description of the land subject to the development agreement.
               2. The restrictions or conditions to be attached to the property including
                   development standards and the provision of public facilities.
               3. The configuration of the project as shown on a project master plan.
               4. A statement of the benefits and value the development agreement will have
                   for the City as a whole, including, but not limited to, assurances of design
                   standards, dedication and improvement of open space, parks, trails,
                   amenities, or infrastructure such as public rights-of-way or utilities.
               5. The time frames for performance by the parties.
               6. A description of the various City approvals required before the
                   commencement of construction and other procedures that will be required
                   after approval of the development agreement.
               7. Provisions for enforcement of the terms and conditions of the development
                   agreement.
               8. Provisions for making amendments to the development agreement.
               9. The time limitation of the agreement.
               10. Such other terms as may be proposed and agreed to between the City and
                   developer or subdivider.
        D. Limitations:
               1. A development agreement under this section may not:
                   a. Limit the City’s authority in the future to:
                       i. Enact a land use regulation; or
                       ii. Take any action allowed under Utah Code Annotated Section 10-8-84,
                           as amended;
                   b. Require the City to change the zoning designation of an area of land
                       within the City in the future; or
                   c. Contain a term that conflicts with, or is different from, a standard set forth
                       in an existing land use regulation that governs the area subject to the

The deeded legal description for the property is included as Exhibit B. Staff notes that the
proposed site plan (or project master plan) does not match with the deed description for a
small portion of the southeast corner of the property.

As the development agreement contains terms that conflict with, or is different from,
standards set forth in existing land use regulations that govern the area subject to the
development agreement, the development agreement is being processed in accordance
with the same procedures for enacting a land use regulation under Utah Code Annotated
Section 10-9a-502, which includes the requirement that a public hearing be held before the
Planning Commission, and that the Planning Commission make a recommendation
regarding the Development Agreement to the City Council.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission review the request based on the findings
listed below and the criteria for approval, or denial as listed within the staff report. The
Planning Commission should also review the request, hear from the applicant, and receive
public comment prior to making a decision.

If the Planning Commission decides to make a positive recommendation to the City Council
for the request, staff recommends they include in the recommendation the following
conditions of approval:

        1.       That all requirements of the Draper City Engineering, Public Works, Building,
                 Planning, and Fire Divisions are satisfied throughout the development of the
                 site and the construction of all buildings on the site, including permitting.
        2.       That all requirements of the geotechnical report are satisfied throughout the
                 development of the site and the construction of all buildings on the site.
        3.       That all recommended provisions identified or included by staff in the
                 amended draft presented to the planning commission be included or
                 addressed in the final development agreement presented to the City Council.

                                                                                              Page 14 of 29
8.1. Notice. If Master Developer or the City fails to perform their respective obligations

hereunder or to comply with the terms hereof, the Party believing that a Default has

occurred shall provide Notice to the other Party.

8.2. Contents of the Notice of Default. The Notice of Default shall:

   8.2.1. Specific Claim. Specify the claimed event of Default;

   8.2.2. Applicable Provisions. Identify with particularity the provisions of any

   applicable law, rule, regulation or provision of this MDA that is claimed to be in

   Default;

   8.2.3. Materiality. Identify why the Default is claimed to be material; and

   8.2.4. Optional Cure. If the City chooses, in its discretion, it may propose a method

   and time for curing the Default which shall be of no less than thirty (30) calendar

   days duration.

8.3. Meet and Confer, Mediation, Arbitration. Upon the issuance of a Notice of

Default the parties shall engage in the “Meet and Confer” and “Mediation” processes

specified in Sections 5.2 and 5.4. If the claimed Default is subject to Arbitration as

provided in Section 5.5 then the parties shall follow such processes.

8.4. Remedies. If the parties are not able to resolve the Default by “Meet and Confer” or

by “Mediation”, and if the Default is not subject to arbitration then the parties may have

the following remedies, except as specifically limited in 9.9:

   8.4.1. Law and Equity. All rights and remedies available at law and in equity,

   including, but not limited to, injunctive relief and/or specific performance.

   8.4.2. Security. The right to draw on any security posted or provided in connection

   with the Project and relating to remedying of the particular Default.

                                                                                   Page 15 of 29
       8.5. Public Meeting. Before any remedy in Section 8.4 may be imposed by the City the

       party allegedly in Default shall be afforded the right to attend a public meeting before the

       City Council and address the City Council regarding the claimed Default.

       8.6. Emergency Defaults. Anything in this MDA notwithstanding, if the City Council

       finds on the record that a default materially impairs a compelling, countervailing interest

       of the City and that any delays in imposing such a default would also impair a

       compelling, countervailing interest of the City then the City may impose the remedies of

       Section 8.4 without the requirements of Sections 8.5. The City shall give Notice to

       Master Developer of any public meeting at which an emergency default is to be

       considered and the Master Developer shall be allowed to address the City Council at that

       meeting regarding the claimed emergency Default.

       8.7. Extended Cure Period. If any Default cannot be reasonably cured within thirty (30)

       calendar days then such cure period shall be extended so long as the defaulting party is

       pursuing a cure with reasonable diligence.

       8.8. Default of Assignee. A default of any obligations assumed by an assignee shall not

       be deemed a default of Master Developer.

       8.9. Limitation on Recovery for Default – No Damages. Anything in this MDA

       notwithstanding no Party shall be entitled to any claim for any monetary damages as a

       result of any breach of this MDA and each Party waives any claims thereto. The sole

       remedy available to Master Developer shall be that of specific performance.
