    1. Housing and Housing Affordability
Mayor Young indicated the number one thing for the City of Orem is housing and housing
affordability and inquired to the Senators and Representatives as to things that are on the
legislative docket in regards to housing and housing affordability and how that may affect Orem.

Representative Peterson stated that housing affordability is a huge issue across state and they
have had a task force that has been working on this issue. He doesn’t think anybody has a magic
or silver bullet to solve this issue. He stated they all know it is starting to impact people who are
wanting to come into the state or even people who want to stay in the state.

Senator Bramble stated that one of the challenges they have at the legislature is the intersection
of state policy versus usurping local land use. He feels the legislature generally is going to be a
bit reticent to step very far into that arena.

General discussion continued regarding legislative and local issues as related to affordable
housing.

Mayor Young feels it is in the best interest of the cities to allow them to work on the affordable
housing issues within their own cities and would hope to get the legislators support allowing the
city to manage those particular issues.

Mr. Macdonald indicated that the state does have some funds available for housing affordability
and rely on the city to set zoning in place where they can put those funds to good use.

General discussion continued regarding the growth in Orem particularly with Utah State
University and how affordable housing is related to that type of growth. Discussion continued
regarding the difference between high density and affordable housing. Making sure that all cities
are responsible for fulfilling the need for affordable housing and making it fair across the county.

Mayor Young stated that the message the Orem Council would like to leave them with is they
really want to be in control of their own city and asked them not to impose anything on a macro
basis that would negatively affect the ability to do that.

General discussion continued regarding short term rentals and programs allowing the City to
locate short term rentals in their area, college exemptions for those cities with universities in
their boundaries and incentives for older home renovations.

   2. Economic Development

economic development incentives. Governor Cox has been focused on kind of retooling the

    3. Taxation and Sales Tax
Mr. Davidson stated the sales tax formula adjustment is done by splitting 50% being based on
population the other 50% is based on point of sale and there has been some discussion in the
years past about modifying that formula. He indicated that in his recent discussion with Senator
Bramble he suggested that was not a focus or a priority in the senate but there’s two houses and
they are always concerned. He continued to explain that if there is a shift in moving more of a
population based type formula could have devastating impacts on the city and inquired as to the
thoughts of the legislature.

Representative Stratton responded that they recognize that there is reliance and uncertainty in
things and that would be a very disruptive process to go though. He indicated that he is not
aware of anything however there are a lot of bills that are still protected and they are not aware
of at this time. If it was something that was to be brought up and even considered there would
have to be mitigating efforts to hold it harmless and allow proper adjustment but he does not see
that as viable.

    4. Growth
Mayor Young indicated growth seems to be the number one issue facing cities and towns across
the state of Utah. Along with housing concerns there is also concerns of traffic infrastructure,
the cost of land, materials and labor which is making it difficult to keep up with services and
delivery demands. He stated that in recommending new laws, consideration should be given to
the cost of such measures at both the local and state levels. He inquired as to any thoughts the
Senators or Representatives had on growth.

Senator McKell stated he had been working with a group of house members, and a group of
senators dealing with the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) money. They are tasked to deal
with water and they will be seeing a proposal allocating 25 million which would come to Utah
County to help improve water quality on the lake. What that means for Orem City is with the
current water reuse conservation project of 9.3 million dollars, they would take a portion of that
25 million dollars and allocate it hopefully giving 1.5 million dollars to help with the growth and
the water quality project in Orem. There is also projects in Provo, Payson, Timpanogos Special
Service District, Salem and Springville that they are trying to get in the budget.

Senator McKell excused himself and left the meeting at 6:18 pm.

Discussion ensued regarding metering and the reduction of water consumption with jetering
being part of the project as well.

    5. Public Safety
Mr. Davidson stated in 2011 the state made modifications to the Utah State Retirement System
and created a tier II system for retirees. There is a bill that is pending from Representative Gwen
that would make adjustments to the tier II retirement system and essentially change the public
safety retirement back from a 25 year system to a 20 year system with an increase in benefits.
The city is not in opposition of putting more dollars in the pockets of public safety officers, the
challenge they are facing relates to recruitment and retention and where they put the limited
monies they have. Do they put them in wages or do they put them in retirement benefits. They
need to have a conversation as to where the money is best spent.

General discussion ensued regarding more money in wages or in the retirement system for the
future. The cost that the city would have to contribute would be in the hundreds of thousands.
Discussion continued regarding mental health and that the city was very supportive of whatever

Senator Bramble stated he does not see that bill moving forward. What is being ignored is a

    6. Transportation
Mr. Davidson stated this is not really an item of concern, more of an item of appreciation for
ongoing support of transportation. The City of Orem this last year have received from the
legislature some very generous allocations of millions of dollars for two specific projects in the
city. 1600 North from about 1200 West to State Street. A consideration of future expansion and
the means to build that expansion or expand that corridor as well as improvements from Geneva
Road from University Parkway to about 1800 south. Those are much appreciated and needed
improvements and the city wanted their legislative representative you to know that Orem is
proceeding forward with UDOT Region 3 in the environmental process for 1600 North as well as
making plans for improvements along Geneva Road corridor. He continued to mention other
projects that have been discussed as concerns which included:
     University Parkway Interchange - to improve access in and around Utah Valley
        University
     Direct access point from I-15 onto the UVU campus to alleviate congestion at the
        interchange and at Sandhill Road.
     Center Street – being a local road that cuts across four state highways.
     Aging structure at the mouth of Provo Canyon that at some point in the future will need
        some attention.
He concluded that those are some of the projects that lie ahead as it relates to transportation
within the City of Orem. But first and foremost he expressed the cities thanks for literally the
tens of millions of dollars that UDOT and the State Legislature allocated to the 1600 North and
Geneva road projects. They very much appreciate the leadership of their local delegation in
getting those funds for much needed projects.

Representative Peterson stated he feels the delegation has been unified in making sure that they
look at those priorities and feels he can speak or all of them in saying they are very supportive of
making sure those projects move forward.

General discussion ensued regarding where in the process each of those projects were which
helps the delegation to know what they support are being supported and successful.

Mr. Spencer stated that Alpine School District seems to be closing a lot of schools in the Utah
County area and the city recently purchased the Hillcrest school. His question was with the
bonding he feels the residents have already paid for the schools and inquired as to why they have
to pay for it again if they are closing it and selling it. He inquired as to why they would have to
buy back that school when the tax payers have already paid for it.

Senator Bramble stated what they would want to consider is the split of the Alpine School
District.

General discussion ensued regarding funding and the sale of schools that have been paid for by
bonding and the issue of them being two different entities with different laws that govern how
they do things.

Mrs. Millett commented on possible ways to for the police to increase their salaries and their
wages. She stated she would like to understand or see if there was a way in the future they could
possibly reduce the property tax exemption to help find ways to fund the police a little more.

Senator Bramble responded that although he appreciates the thought, in order to do that they
would have to 1) change the constitutional provision that primary residential properties get that
exemption, and 2) the certified tax rate. If you change that, you are telling all those lower
income folks that are renting that they are going to be paying higher rent.

General discussion ensued regarding the need to change the constitution if they want to pursue
that option and the impact it would have on lower income residents and public education.

Mrs. Lauret inquired about the need for truth and taxation anytime the city wants to change the
rate. She asked because of inflation especially this last year, it would be nice to be able to tie that
amount we have to inflation so they would not have to have a yearly truth in taxation for that.
She feels our buying power goes way down year after year.

Representative Stratton stated you have to look at the purpose of the truth and taxation. We all
have to stand accountable and justify how were using the funds. The policy of the truth and
taxation is to meet that need and we should feel good that we are able to make our case to the
public and justify the use of those funds.

General discussion ensued regarding doing a possible physical analysis on that issue and the
process that would have to be done in order to change that law.

Mr. Peterson commented regarding the Alpine School District subject and stated he hoped they
would have support from our local officials if there was a feasibility study done to see what
would be the best education for our children. He commented that Orem has had 3 schools close
with more planned. They would like support for moving forward with a feasibility study to find
out what is possible for Orem and maybe other adjoining cities. Alpine School District is one of
the biggest districts in the country.

Representative Stratton stated he thinks that receptiveness is generally what you would get from
the legislature if they were to consider and look to see if there are better options, go through an
analysis and make sure it is not biased, there is an independence to it and there are checks and
balances to make sure we have good information. Nothing like good accurate information that
will lead to good public policy.

Senator Bramble voiced his agreement to the comments made by Representative Stratton.

General discussion ensued regarding the steps that need to be taken when splitting a school
district and lessons to be learned from those that have already gone through the process.

Mayor Young inquired about the legislation allowing some sort of construction redevelopment of
Utah Lake.

Representative Stratton stated that in 2018 the legislature passed bill 272 that lays out a pathway
if certain high thresholds are met to look at the potential of a transition to benefit the lake, State
and the environment. There has been some discussion regarding that bill and they are looking to
see if they can make a good bill better. He continued to explain some of the proposals that has
been discussed and also points of concerns that have been raised.

Mayor Young asked if there were any more questions or concerns the council had for the
Senators and Representatives.

Mayor Young and several City Council Members voiced their appreciation for the Senators and
Representatives for meeting with them and understanding that help is a two way street and if
there is anything the city can do to help in their efforts to please let them know.

Representative Stratton thanked the Council for their comments and discussion. He stated the
legislative session is a fast pace 45 days that takes on its own personality and they cannot
forecast what its going to be like this go around. He stated there are some key issues but this
discussion has been very helpful to understand the Council priorities and input.
