Linear Block Definition Amendment
          Council Deputy Director Symes explained a petition has been filed by Thaine Fischer to amend the definition ofa linear
block. The amendment would change the definition of a linear block to include only one side of a street rather than both sides. The
intent of the amendment is to allow additional drinking establishments within the downtown area, particularly on 25 th Street. The
Planning Commission has recommended denial of the petition.
          Planning Manager Montgomery made a presentation illustrating the present conditions on 25 th Street that led the
petitioner to seek the amendment to the linear block definition. According to Section 15-2-13 of the City Code, a linear block is
defined as including both sides of the street. The definition of linear block for measurement purposes comes into consideration
when dealing with non-owner-occupied vacation rentals, food trncks in a right-of-way, duplex lot size in the R-2 zone, and
distancing requirements for taverns or private clubs. The petitioner has asked that the defmition be revised to refer to only one side
of the street. At present, there are 78 restaurants with some type of service and30 bars or clubs in the City; there are currently five
taverns/clubs on 25 th Street. The linear block provision was enacted to address matters such as noise, disorderly conduct, spillover
effect of high concentrations of similar uses, and hours of operation. One consideration for the City Council relates to the
allowance for downtown nightlife; however, there are some questions about whether night life should be family friendly or age-
restricted and what is the proper mix ofnight life activities. Additionally, there are residential uses in downtown Ogden that must
be considered when determining the appropriate character of the downtown area. Mr. Montgomery discussed data pertaining to
police calls for service on 25 th Street. The highest volume is between 10:00 p.m. and 2:00 a.m., Friday through Sunday, which
causes concern as police activity is higher where there is a concentration of drinking establishments. Staff has researched
regulations in other cities and found the following:

                    Council discussion of the petition centered on historical adjustments to the ordinance regulating the defmition of a linear
         block. Several Council members commented on the improvements that have been made on 25 th Street because of efforts aimed at
         improving the reputation of downtown Ogden. They debated whether approval of the petition would impact these improvements
         or lead to deterioration of the current enviromnent on 25 th Street. The Council ultimately concluded to direct staff to provide
         current infonnation regarding the number of tavern establishments in the City as well as to solicit input from the area Convention
         and Visitors Bureau.
                    Chair Choberka invited input from the petitioner. Mr. Fischer stated he has been approached many times by high-end
         restaurants located in other cities about locating in his building on 25 th Street. These projects would have required an adjustment to
         the linear block definition, and he has had to tum these establishments away. He said that he feels the City's preservation of the
         current ordinance is based upon unconscious bias. People who are asking for the ordinance amendment have made a massive
         investment in Ogden's downtown. He is not asking for an adjustment to the defmitions of taverns and private clubs. He simply
         wants to amend the ordinance by removing the word 'linear' to allow for new concepts and improvements on 25 th Street. It is
         common for a nightlife district to be consolidated in one area of any given city. That is what has been happening on 25 th Street and
         he does not believe the fear that there will be a spread of tavern uses throughout the City is warranted. This type of use is market
         driven and there is not a market for a congregation of taverns in other areas of the City. He concluded that preservation of the
         current ordinance will continue to stifle economic development in downtown Ogden.
                    Chair Choberka allowed input from Jared Allen, Restoration Realty. Mr. Allen stated that he is currently working on a
         project at 2518 Lincoln Avenue, but his use would also front onto 25 th Street if the proposed ordinance amendments were
         approved. He reiterated Mr. Fischer's sentiment that the current ordinance is restricting economic improvements in Ogden's
         downtown. Growth of the high-end nigl)tlife market can provide positive contributions to the City.
                    Mr. Montgomery noted that the City's Planning Commission recently acted to provide a recommendation to the City
         Council regarding adjustments to other regulations for taverns in the City. He is unsure when that recommendation will be
         considered by the Council, but it may be possible to consider the two matters at one time. He added that according to the City's
         current ordinance, if 50 percent of an establishment's sales are from food, the linear block regulation does not apply. This led to
         discussion of the difference between a tavern (21-and-older establishment) and a restaurant that serves food and alcohol. Mr.
         Fischer noted that the State Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control regulations override City regulations. An establishment
         must pursue either a restaurant license or a bar/tavern license regardless of the City's regulations pertaining to the percentage of
         alcohol and food sales.
                    Deputy City Attorney Stratford said that Mr. Fischer is correct in that the State regulations override City regulations;
         however, for the purposes of the defmition of a linear block, the difference between a restaurant and bar is important. Even if the
         State of Utah defmes an establishment as a tavern, if at least 50 percent of that establishment's sales are from food, the restriction
         ofnot more than two taverns per linear block does not apply to that establishment. The Planning Commission's recommendation
         that will be submitted to the Council includes recognition of the fact that some establishments that serve alcohol are primarily
         restaurants while others are primarily taverns. The Planning Commission believes establishments that primarily serve alcohol
         (taverns) should adhere to the linear block definition, which restricts the number per block to two. Mr. Fischer stated that
         preservation of the current ordinance will restrict very popular uses. He reiterated that at least two establishments have expressed
         interest in locating on 25 th Street but chosen not to pursue that location because the current ordinance is too restrictive and would
         require adjustments to their successful business model.
                    Council member Lopez suggested that the Council visit tlrn types of establishments in Salt Lake City that would not be
         allowed to locate on 25 th Street based upon the current ordinance. Council member Nadolski supported that suggestion and added
         that it may be that decisions regarding this matter are based upon long standing stigmas regarding the types of establishments that
         serve alcoholic beverages. He advised the Council to be careful when making decisions based upon bias or preconceived notions.
                    Mr. Symes drew the discussion to a close by summarizing feedback from the Council. Staff will work to gather current

         would restrict amendments to the linear block defmition to the Central Business District or 25 th Street.

Fiscal Year 2020 Budget Amendment- Seismic Retrofit and Remodel Fire
Station #4
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey explained Administration is proposing a budget amendmenttransferring $1,073,900 from
the Medical Services Fund for Fire Station #4 improvements. This action would result in a net adjustment of the FY2020 Ogden
City budget of $2,147,800 to reflect all fund transfers.
          Fire Chief Mathieu explained the cmTent budget amount was based upon an outdated bid estimate for the project. After
completion of a competitive bidding process, Administration learned that construction costs have increased dramatically. The
proposed budget adjustment includes costs associated with relocation of the staff and equipment currently housed at Fire Station
#4. He concluded this is the last fire station project to be completed as recommended in the 2007 management audit that was
conducted for the Fire Department.
          Council member Nadolski asked the current balance of the Medical Services Fund. Comptroller Stout responded that
after the transfer is completed; the balance will be approximately $1.2 million. The amount to be transferred is just under 50
percent of the current fund balance.

Fiscal Years 2021-2025 Capital Improvement Plan
         Ms. Mabey explained that as part of the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) process, the City Council discusses and
considers projects proposed in the Plan, incorporates changes as desired, receives public input and adopts the Plan. The CIP is
intended to prioritize capital project funding for the FY202 l budget. Council Staff will review the proposed FY202 l -2025 CIP.
This includes review of Administrative priorities and establishing Council priorities for the Plan.
         Mr. Stratford briefly referred to a memo he provided to the Council addressing concerns that have been expressed
regarding the motion by Planning Commission to include additions to the CIP. The Planning Commission review of the CIP
should be focused primarily upon consistency with the City's General Plan. They typically do not provide recommendations that
would result in budgetary determinations. Two commissioners voted in opposition to the motion because they felt that it went
beyond the power of the Commission. Mr. Stratford indicated his memo does discuss the Planning Commission's authority. The
Commission can make recommendations related to ranking of CIP projects and can recommend adjustments; however, the
recommendation they have submitted to Council includes a change in the scope or status of a CIP project, which they do not have
the authority to do.
         Ms. Mabey summarized the Planning Commission recommendation and suggested amendments to the CIP as follows:
               1. Removing Airport CIP projects AR002 and AR008, as these were determined to be currently inconsistent with
                   the General Plan (this could change upon completion of the Airport Master Plan and by adopting General Plan
                   changes);
                        a. AR002: Airport Offsite Land Purchase - Parking; and
                        b. AR008: Northwest Airport U-70 Ingress Egress
              2. Modifying the urgent status of CIP project GF032: Golf Course Irrigation System Replacement.
              3 . Reclassifying Marshall White Center repairs from Necessary to Urgent;
              4. Modifying the Administration's CIP priorities to list Marshall White Center improvements as the first priority;
              5. Incorporating a new Marshall White Center project to replace the swimming pool and adjusting CIP Plan
                   forecasted expenditures by $2 million for swimming pool repair and/or construction; and
              6. Exploring potential and incremental development of a new center on the footprint of the Marshall White Center
                   and for the current center to remain open during construction with a functioning pool.
         There was discussion regarding the circumstances that led to the Planning Commission voting on a motion that was
broader than allowed based upon the body's authority. The Council then moved to discussion of the Commission's
recommendations regarding projects aimed at improving the Marshall White Center. Mr. Stratford stated Administration is
evaluating all the needs at the Marshall White Center as well as recreation needs for the entire City. They are developing a project
recommendation that will be submitted to the Planning Commission later this month for their review of consistency with the
General Plan. This will primarily be a planning project that will provide for the assembly of a group of stakeholders tasked with
developing recommendations for broader projects at the center.
         Ms. Mabey showed a presentation to provide the Council staffs overview of the FY2021-2025 CIP. The objectives of
the Plan include scheduling, financing, and constructing City capital improvements in the upcoming FY and projected for the
following four FYs. CIP projects are those exceeding $30,000 adding to the City's "physical plant", projects resulting in the
acquisition or long-tenn lease ofland, and/or feasibility studies and evaluations. CIP funding sources include the General Fund,
federal and state grants, B&C road funds, utility enterprise funds, bonds, and other miscellaneous sources. The CIP identifies City
needs, establishes long-tenn goals for investment, ensures expenditures are consistent with the General Plan, and informs the
budget and sets priorities. Like the budget itself, the CIP is a policy document that reflects the values and goals of the City (both
the Administration and City Council). Ms. Mabey reviewed the timeline for considering the FY2021-2025 CIP, noting that the
