VIII.   ACTION ITEM

       A. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2025-R-10 AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR TO
          SIGN A SOLID WASTE SERVICE AGREEMENT WITH ACE DISPOSAL.
Glen Kennedy said the City’s current solid waste handling agreement is set to expire June
30th, 2025. The major solid waste services include residential curbside cart pickup of
garbage and recycling, neighborhood dumpsters, glass recycling dumpsters, bulky waste,
and recycling material processing. The City released a Request for Proposals (RFP) in
November inviting vendors to provide proposals for a new agreement for these services.
The RFP closed in December with the City receiving three proposals from Waste
Management, Republic Services, and ACE Disposal.

An evaluation committee consisting of the PW Director, PW Deputy Director and the City
Treasurer individually rated the proposals without pricing. Pricing and the scores of the
proposals were revealed to the committee by the City’s procurement/contract analyst prior
to selection, subsequently, ACE Disposal was determined to be the best option.

IX.    DISCUSSION ITEMS

       A. DISCUSS UNIFIED POLICE DEPARMENT FY2026 BUDGET PROCESS.
Matt Dahl said Midvale City joined the Unified Police Department (UPD) in 2011. UPD
provides law enforcement services to all its member communities through a combination
of local precincts and shared services. The budget for these services is adopted annually,
and member communities fund it through fees based on a formula considering law
enforcement cases, population, and property valuation in each community. The UPD
Board of Directors, comprised of representatives from each community, approves the final
budget.

In 2023, UPD adopted a policy requiring it to establish a proposed budget increase
maximum (PBIM) in March of each year. The PBIM aims to simplify the budget process
by setting a maximum increase early in the annual budget cycle. If UPD proposes a
budget increase exceeding the PBIM, a supermajority of UPD Board members must
approve it. The PBIM also sets expectations for UPD by indicating what member
communities believe they can afford in the next fiscal year.

On February 19th, the UPD Board discussed the budget and PBIM. The formal adoption
of the PBIM will take place on March 20th. Staff intends to discuss the PBIM with the City
Council and seek guidance on how the Mayor and staff should approach the UPD Board’s
adoption of the PBIM.

Mayor Dustin Gettel said the first proposed budget number was given last week which
was 7% across the board with Midvale being at 6%. That equals a 20-25% property tax
increase. He is planning to go back to the Board and explain how burdensome this
increase will be on residents.

Matt Dahl’s said he would like to add that we are early in the budget process, and the
budget retreat is planned for March 20th. Mariah Hill and her staff will be putting together
more detailed information to be presented. He reminded the Council that they were
expecting a much larger property tax increase last year and as they reached the end of
the budget process, they were able to make adjustments that reduced the tax amount.
He went on to explain what he is expecting from the current and projected sales tax
statistics and how those funds can be used. He also explained that the UPD budget
number is the maximum and they can work on solutions to keep the number below the
maximum.
