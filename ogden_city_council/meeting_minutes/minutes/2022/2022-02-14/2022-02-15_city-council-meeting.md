Proposed Ordinance 2022-7 approving the request to rezone 2530-2544
Porter Avenue from Multiple Family Residential East Central Zone (R-3EC)
to Mixed Use (MU); AND

and Porter Avenue

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed rezone at 25" Street and
Porter Avenue came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated this rezone request is the first of a two-part process,
the second part being an amendment to the Mixed-Use (MU) zone. Additionally, developments within the MU zone must have
development agreements, so a development agreement will be considered with this rezone petition. This rezone petition was
originally considered by the Planning Commission in 2019, with the Commission recommending approval of the rezone and
development agreement. Several Commission meetings were held, and the original petition changed throughout the process. The
original petitioner, Mike Vowles, proposed building a mixed-use project with townhomes, apartments, and a mixed-use building.
In order to do so, it would be necessary to rezone from R-3EC to MU. Mr. Vowles, however, did not move forward with the
project, and the property was sold to Heritage Land Development. The new property owner changed the site plan and would like
to construct 21 townhomes on the property. The development would also include live-work units within some of the townhomes.
An additional memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed zone text amendment
pertaining to the Mixed-Use Zone (MU) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the purpose of this zone
text amendment is to create a section within the MU zone text that establishes specific requirements for the rezone at 25" Street
and Porter Avenue. Creating specific standards for the MU zone in this area may help protect surrounding properties from
unwanted uses that do not match their zoning designation.

Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and stated the Planning Commission found the rezone petition to
be consistent with the East Central Community Plan. They recommended approval of the rezone. In addition to recommending
approval of the rezone, the Planning Commission recommend approval of the attached development agreement. Significant
requirements include:

7. Townhomes must be built in a modern version of the Arts and Crafts architectural style.

8. Townhomes are limited toa maximum height of 30’ for townhomes with road frontage, and 40’ for townhomes
adjacent to existing homes.

9. The existing right-of-way along the east property line shall be maintained and access allowed for those who
have recorded rights to the right-of-way.

10. Murals shall be placed in certain locations within the development.

11. A 6’ cedar fence shall be placed along the southeast property lines with gates for those with legally recorded
rights-of-way.

12. Any live-work space shall maintain quiet hour from 9:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m.

Nine neighbors spoke during the March 6, 2019, Planning Commission meeting in opposition to the rezone.
Incompatibility with the neighborhood, problems with townhome projects, limited access, and impacts to the historic district were
the most common complaints. Two neighbors spoke during the April 3, 2019, Planning Commission meeting in opposition to the
rezone. Incompatibility with the neighborhood and protection of the historic district were their main concerns. Other concerns
include the following:

1. Parking for the office at 549 25" Street will be removed.
2. The loss of the right-of-way located in the middle of the block.
3. Proposed building heights do not match the rooflines of neighboring homes.

Discussion among the Council and Mr. Montgomery centered on the overall density of the project, the definition of live-
work units, traffic circulation in and around the project, and the public art that will be integrated into the design for the project.

Council member Choberka then inquired as to noticing requirements for this type of application; she noted she received
notification of this meeting, but she has heard from some of her neighbors that they did not receive the notice. Mr. Montgomery
stated that notices of the Planning Commission public hearing were sent to all tenants in the area; this includes property owners
and renters. Generally, the Recorder’s Office uses the same mailing list as was used by the Planning Division. Council member
Choberka stated she is concerned about taking action on the proposed ordinance tonight after hearing from some of her neighbors
that they did not receive the notice. Mr. Montgomery offered to review the mailing lists used by both the Planning Division and
the Recorder’s Office to determine if any names were left off.

Chair Nadolski invited input from the applicant.

Marty McFadden thanked Mr. Montgomery for his thorough presentation, which accurately represents his desires and
plans for the subject property. He is trying to be as respectful and considerate of the neighborhood as possible. He expressed
willingness to answer questions any Council member may have.

Council member Choberka thanked Mr. McFadden for his desire to invest in the Ogden community. She noted she lives
on Jefferson Avenue, and she had heard from many of her neighbors about the project. She asked Mr. McFadden to address
concerns regarding traffic and fire protection for the project area. Mr. McFadden stated he has met with the Fire Marshal and
understands that having two points of access is critical for fire protection; the easement that currently exists has dictated the layout
of the roads and access to the property. Council member Choberka asked if the access matter has been resolved according to the
Fire Marshal’s direction. Mr. McFadden stated that he has proposed a change to the easement that reflects the pathway of the
proposed development and that is currently under review. Council member Choberka asked Mr. Montgomery if the access issue
must be resolved in order for the City Council to approve the MU zoning, to which Mr. Montgomery answered yes. Council
member Choberka then addressed the design of the roofline of the proposed project and indicated one of her neighbors had
recommended that the roofline reflect surrounding development by including points rather than flat roof lines. Mr. McFadden
stated he has committed to take that recommendation to his architect to see if that is structurally possible in the type of project he
is pursuing. Council member Choberka then asked if there is an opportunity to reduce the height of the buildings directly behind
two existing residences. Mr. McFadden stated that he has heard that request from residents in the area; he noted he has already
adjusted the plan for the project when considering what was originally proposed. He could eliminate one floor of the buildings,
but the owner of the project is not supportive of that adjustment.

Council member Richey referenced the parking for some of the homes on the west side of Porter Avenue and asked if
there is any other parking area available to those residents. Ifthe road eventually becomes a one-way street, he is concerned about
eliminating on-street parking for those residents. Mr. Montgomery stated that the area could be improved and better utilized to
increase parking opportunities, but that would be at the expense of those property owners.

The following residents spoke to their concerns about the proposed project: Travis Pate, Cindy Toone, Debra Darrington,

The concerns they expressed include the impact the project will have on this historical area of the City; failure to provide
proper notice of the proposal prior to the Planning Commission public hearing; lack of working-class housing in the project;
inadequate parking; the sentiment that the proposed project does not fit in with surrounding development; increased traffic on the

Council member Choberka thanked those who addressed the Council and indicated she understands their
concerns; when a developer brings forward a project that does not align with a community plan for a specific area, it can be
concerning to the residents who live there. She reiterated her concern that noticing of this item may have been inadequate, which
may have led to a lack of representation on the part of the community.

Council member Blair asked Mr. Montgomery to express the concerns about the access to the project and
whether the developer is using an established right-of-way or easement for the project. Mr. Montgomery stated there is a right-of-
way that wraps around the homes on Jefferson Avenue and many different individuals have ownership or the right to access that
right-of-way. Council member Blair asked if the developer has ownership in that right-of-way. Mr. Montgomery stated he would
need to perform a title search to determine the exact answer to that question.

Chair Nadolski asked Mr. Montgomery to readdress the question regarding noticing of this agenda item. Mr.
Montgomery stated that the Planning Division provides the mailing list to the Recorder’s Office; he will check to see if there are
any discrepancies in the noticing list. Chair Nadolski asked City Attorney Williams to also address the issue of inadequate
noticing. Mr. Williams indicated that he has heard allegations of improper noticing in many cases, but they usually do not pan out:
in this case, the people who have complained about not receiving a notice did, in fact, know about this meeting or they would not
have been able to complain. It is his point of view that if those individuals were concerned about this application, they would have
attended the meeting. Council member Choberka stated that others may not have received notice and have not complained or
contacted their neighbors. Mr. Williams reiterated improper noticing is a common complaint he hears, but it rarely is proven to be
factual.

Chair Nadolski invited Mr. McFadden to readdress the Council. Mr. McFadden stated that there is a great lack
of context regarding the number of revisions that have been performed for this project; he has taken into consideration the input he
has received from the neighborhood and has adjusted his proposal responsive to that input. He stated this has increased the cost of
the project, which has reduced the return the owner will realize on this project. However, the owner wishes to pay respect to the
neighborhood while still providing a quality project. He stated he understands the concerns of the neighborhood and desires to pay
homage to the historical and architectural elements of the community and noted that he does not believe it is possible to develop a
project that will please every individual in the community.

Council member Choberka stated that she appreciates Mr. McFadden’s responsiveness to the residents and to
the Council. She reiterated her concern about noticing of this agenda item as many residents believed that final action had already

Council Policy Analyst Watkins then stated there are some numbering issues in the text of the ordinance and it
may be necessary to adjust the ordinance before final action. He added Council staff can review the mailing list that was used to

Chair Nadolski stated that the Council has several options before them tonight, including adopting an amended

Proposed Resolution 2022-4 approving amendments to the Own in Ogden
Down Payment Assistance Program

A memo from Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
amending the Own in Ogden Program guidelines. The memo stated the proposal includes increasing the amount available for zero
interest, deferred payment loans. The proposal increases the amount available for persons purchasing their primary residence in
Ogden from $5,000 to $10,000, income-qualified Ogden City employees and new hires from $10,000 to $15,000, and an increase
from $10,000 to $15,000 for qualifying full-time, state-certified K-12 classroom teachers and administrators. These changes are
the result of increased housing costs in Ogden.

Community Development Manager Ogden summarized the memo and stated it is proposed that the current
budget for the Own in Ogden program continue to be used to fund the loan program. The budget adopted for the Own in Ogden
program with the FY2021-2022 Annual Action Plan was $250,000 funded with HOME federal grant funding.

7. Modify the Civility and Decorum section to clarify the reason and purpose for limiting outward expressions of
support or disagreement with a given position or statement.

8. Adding a Signage section to provide guidance for meeting attendees.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound
on each of the proposed amendments as follows:

e Regular Meetings - Meeting Schedule Change. In July 2021, the Council reduced the number of regular City
Council meetings from three per month to two per month on a trial basis. There have been no negative impacts
to workflow or other unintended consequences. The proposed amendment to the Regular Meetings section will
formalize these changes for the meeting schedule going forward, acknowledging that the budget review process
may require additional meetings.

e Acknowledgments. The Council office receives many requests to formally recognize individuals, organizations,
events, and other observances. Meeting time constraints and limited staff time make it difficult to grant all such
requests. This additional section would provide space on the agenda to allow Council members to informally
recognize special guests, events, observances or other items of interest to the community.

e Decorum and Civility —-Outward Expressions of Support or Disagreement. The proposed language clarifies the
purpose for limiting clapping, booing, and other outward expressions of agreement or disagreement with any
position. The Council wants to ensure all in attendance feel safe and free to express a differing opinion.

e Signage. A proposed new section on Signage outlines the limitations and expectations for those attending
meetings who want to communicate through signage.
