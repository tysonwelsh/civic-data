Fixing American’s Surface Transportation (FAST) Act - Brigitte Mandel, Utah FHWA Office President Obama signed the FAST Act into law on December 4, 2015. The Act is a long-term authorization and provides 5 years of funding for infrastructure, planning and investment. $305 billion was authorized over all transportation modes over FY-2016-2020. $70 billion was transfer to the Highway Trust Fund to keep it solvent. Surface Transportation Program (STP) name changed to Surface Transportation Block Grant (STBG) Program. The FAST Act provides:  $226.3 billion for highways over 5 years $225.2 billion contract authority $1.1 billion from general fund  Builds on program structure and reforms MAP-21  Continued focus on accelerating project deliverer  Adds a new freight formula and expands the freight network  Adds a new discretionary program for nationally significant freight and highway projects  Provides new tribal self-governance option Mountainland MPO Regional Planning February 4, 2016 Page 3 Apportioned Programs: National Highway Performance Program Surface Transportation Block Grant CMAQ Highway Safety Improvement Railway-Highway Crossings Metropolitan Planning National Highway Freight Program The National Highway Freight Program:  $1.2 billion apportion to States by formula  Eligible activities: construction, operational improvements, freight planning and performance measures  No State Plan, no freight formula funds beginning FY 2018  Freight Network to include: critical urban freight corridor with State & MPO consultation, critical rural freight corridors identified by State, Primary Highway Freight System, national multimodal freight network, State Freight Plans Planning and Performance:  Encourages MPO consultation with other types of planning actives  Enables participation by private transportation providers  TIPs/STIPs/long range plans must include facilities that support intercity transportation  New planning considerations: resiliency/reliability, storm water mitigation, and enhancement of travel/tourism Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) Selection Process 2019-2020 Funding - Bob Allen The 2016 TIP Selection Process: The process starts with Project Ideas from sponsors, then Concept Reports, and finally TIP project selection. Selected projects are funded with the MPO federal funds and county sales taxes. There is approximately $30-$40 million available in 2019-2020 with the local match of 6.77%. Sixty-six Project Ideas totaling $194 million were submitted for TAC Committee to review. North County - 16 Project Ideas, $42 million Central County – 23 Project Ideas, $94 million South County – 23 Project Ideas, $56 million Countywide – 5 Project Ideas, $2.1 million TAC Committee reviewed and heard sponsors presentations and then ranked the Project Ideas. Each jurisdiction had 1 vote to score the idea 1-5 (1 low, 5 high) to help determine if the project idea should move forward to the concept phase. The TAC vote is non-binding and sponsors, even with a low score, can move forward with their ideas and turn in a concept report. See the attached 2016 Project Ideas Ranking table for results. Automobile Emissions Project 2015 DAQ Research Paper - Dr. Randy Martin and Joe Thomas Automobile emissions are a large portion of Utah’s air pollutant emissions. Utah is focusing on reducing emissions as a core component to the plan to improve the air quality by studying, emissions inspection and maintenance programs, anti-idling programs, driving modification, and outreach programs. The study is measuring the difference in emissions between cold starts, hot starts, and continuous idling. Discover if emissions are different under different weather conditions, and how long a catalytic converters can remain at optimal temperature. Cold Start: a vehicle off, stored outside for  12 hours Cold starts results in higher average emissions across vehicle types and pollutants Hot Start: a vehicle after on-road drive, shut off motor and start after specified period of time Hot starts results in lower average HC emissions as compared to the equivalent idle Idle emissions: continued measurement for  5 minute after start HCs similar to a hot start with a greater NOx emissions Summary questions: 1. How long does my engine need to warm up after a “cold start?” Warm up Engine by driving it normally for more that 2-3 minutes idling is not needed 2. During short stops, is it better to let my engine idle or shut off the restart it? A good rule, always turn off the engine when in “park.” Idling for 5 minutes produce 4 times more VOC, 3 times more NOx and 10 times more CO, than restarting the engine. We plan to continue the research on cold starts, hot starts and idling. Local Corridor Preservation Land Holding Follow-up Recommendation - Jim Price After the Local Option Corridor Preservation Policy way adopted some questions have come up. How soon after a highway project utilized any preserved right-a-way should the excess property be sold and Mountainland MPO Regional Planning February 4, 2016 Page 5 the proceeds returned to the preservation funds. After discussions with MPO Finance Committee, staff is recommending the following change to the Corridor Policy: “Staff shall prepare and present an annual report to Regional Planning Committee on the status and disposition of all preserved properties.
