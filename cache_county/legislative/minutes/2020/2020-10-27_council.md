Cache ValleyTransit District Report (CVTD) — Todd Beutler & Shaun Bushman —

Prior to the report Chairman Ward noted that Council member Erickson has received
several questions and concerns from citizens regarding the Transit District and
encouraged citizens to attend the CVTD board meetings. The next meeting is
scheduled for November 18, 2020.

Bushman said he would be glad to meet with any citizens and address their concerns
and questions. Bushman reported on budget, routes, ridership, zero fare, COVID-19
and grants. Bushman and Beutler responded to Council member questions on the
following:

-Worthen asked how the cost of $3.00 a trip is figured — per person? Bushman
said a trip is any time someone gets on a bus anywhere .

-Tidwell asked what the ridership is on the Preston route. Beutler replied there is
consistent ridership of 15-25 people. Tidwell asked for pre-COVID and present
ridership numbers. Ridership pre-COVID was between 5,500-6,500/day and is
now about 3,000/day—down about 50%, which compares with national trends
during COVID. Tidwell asked if there are routes for young school children.
Beutler said there was a time when school children were choosing to ride CVTD
instead of school busses and that has been discouraged. School busses will not
service anyone under a mile from school and some of those students will ride
CVTD at no charge.

-Erickson asked why there is no charge. Beutler responded it is not cost
effective with the equipment/maintenance needed to monitor rider fare.
Continuing assessments will indicate when a fare is economically sound.

-Council member Erickson asked if CVTD’s COVID funding is through the
CARES Act. It is. Erickson said there is a time frame in which those funds must
be used and Bushman mentioned the CARES funding was put in the fund
balance. Beutler answered COVID funds are not put in the fund balance. Money
from local sales tax goes in the fund balance Erickson asked what kind of fund
balance CVTD is carrying. Beutler will email Erickson those figures.

Erickson asked how the land purchase was acquired while still being frugal.
Beutler said Federal grants provided most of the money for their land purchase.
Erickson asked why CVTD received the 2016 Top Transit District in the nation
award. The award is given by an association they participate in which reviews

PUBLIC HEARING: October 27, 2020 at 5:30 p.m. — Ordinance No. 2020-10 — Butcher Shop

Rezone — Request to rezone 2.27 acres from the Agricultural (A10) Zone to the Industrial (I)
Zone. Located approximately 3565 West 2200 South, Wellsville — Director Chris Harrild
reviewed the Planning Commission and staff recommendations.

Chairman Ward opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.
William Jensen, property owner; Josiah Olsen, Dakota Olsen, Forest Olsen, Melody Olsen, Randall

Olsen, Jake Olsen and Providence Mayor John Drew spoke in favor of the rezone citing a need for the
service as other shops are booked up to and/or through 2021.

Matt Olsen, Madison Parrish and Travis Murray oppose the rezone citing concerns about what the
Industrial Zone allows on the property and asking why the proponents can’t utilize a different location on
their property for the facility.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Tidwell to close the Public Hearing. Worthen
seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 5-0. White & Zilles absent.

PUBLIC HEARING: October 27, 2020 at 5:40 p.m. — Ordinance No. 2020-11 — BGTS

Properties Rezone — Request to rezone 5.91 acres from the Agricultural (A10) Zone to the
Commercial (C) Zone. Located 350 West 8700 South, near Paradise.

Chairman Ward opened the Public Hearing and invited public comment.

Bryon Bostrum, Financial Controller for BGTS, stated this property is an Avalon Hills Healthcare facility for
young women with eating disorders. The property was purchased in 2006. They have been limited to
eight beds and want four more. They are applying for a Commercial rezone because the Agricultural
(A10) Zone no longer allows eating disorder treatment centers.

Dr. Bonita Quakenbush, founder of Avalon Hills, and Bob Thomas, CPA with Avalon Hills, spoke in favor
of the rezone.

Vic Little objected to supporting the facility through the Mt. Pisgah Road and feels a rezone will open the
door for more commercial businesses which will affect Paradise Town.

There was no other public comment.

ACTION: Motion by Vice Chair Worthen to close the Public Hearing. Erickson seconded
the motion. The vote was unanimous, 5-0. White & Zilles absent.

e Discussion of Dog Impound Facility — Sheriff Jensen reported on the progress of the
study to identify the size of facility needed. He anticipates a 7,000 — 9,000 square foot
facility at about $220 a square foot to build. It will be designed to add on to as the need
increases in ten or more years. The cost of the proposed design is around $60,000 -
$65,000 which Jensen can pay out of his budget. Jensen asked if the Council members
are good with that. They are. He will bring back two different designs — one at $1.7
million and one at $2 million for review and approval by the Council.

Council member Erickson asked if secure storage might be a part of the design. Jensen
will have the architect look at storage possibilities as well.
