been filed by Shawn Strong to amend the Lynn Community Plan to allow the option of either R-5 or R-3 zoning on approximately
four acres of property at 140-152 West 2nd Street. The Lynn Community Plan currently allows for the R-3/CO, P-1/CO or C-1/CO
zoning options on the property directly adjacent to the corner 2nd Street and Wall A venue but restricts the property to the west to
low density residential. Mr. Strong has acquired the property on the comer which recently received approval for a townhome
project under the R-3/CO zoning and is interested in acquiring the additional four acres to the west. The petition to amend the
community plan would allow expansion of an updated multi-family project. The initial petition requested the option of the R-5
zoning but was updated to include either the R-5 zoning or the R-3 zoning with a conditional overlay approval. The Planning
Commission reviewed both options and is recommending denial of the petition for either the R-5 or R-3 zoning option.
          An additional memo from the Community and Economic Development Department explained a petition has been filed by
Shawn Strong to rezone approximately four acres of property located at 140-152 West 2nd Street from R-1-6 (Single family
Residential) to R-5 (Multi-family Residential). Mr. Strong has amended his petition to request the approval of a rezone to R-3
(Multifamily Residential). The rezone petition was submitted in conjunction with a petition to amend the Lynn Cmmnunity Plan
to allow the option of either the R-3 or R-5 zoning on the subject property. The Commission did not find good reason to
recommend approval of the community plan amendment allowing the higher density zoning, and, as a result, the Commission
found that the petition to rezone the property was not consistent with the existing community plan and has forwarded a
recommendation of denial for both the community plan amendment and the rezone.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the
petitions. He presented an aerial photograph to orient the Council to the location of the property to the zoning request that is
subsequent to the Community Plan amendment. The Community Plan provides for R-3 zoning with a professional/institutional
(PI) land use overlay opportunity. The Council previously took action to adjust the zoning for the 3 .13-acre parcel on the corner of
Wall Avenue and 2nd Street, but the petitioner would like to increase the overall property size by adjusting the zoning for the
adjacent 4.72 acres of property and developing both parcels. This would provide for increased density in the area. He noted that
since the Lynn Community Plan was adopted, there have been 483 single family homes, 14 duplex, 66 apartment units, and a new
elementary school constructed in the Plan area. The Planning Commission has considered these trends and the petition's
consistency with the General Plan. They evaluated the manner in which higher density will reflect the neighborhood character, if it
will isolate neighborhoods, and if it will offer a transition between intense and less intense uses. They also considered whether 2nd
Street and Wall Avenue can be defined as a 'center' and Mr. Montgomery reviewed other areas in the City that have been
identified as having potential for future development as a 'center'. This includes, but is not limited to, urban mixed-use centers
and neighborhood village centers. He presented a slide that identified the density on properties in close proximity to the subject
property; most of the development that has occurred in the area consists of single-family subdivision projects. The Community
Plan discusses development options for the four comers of 2 nd Street and Wall Avenue; any zoning consideration should be
conditional overlay zoning with a development agreement to ensure design and use compatibility with the character of the area.
The proposed project requires the acquisition of four properties from four different owners and Mr. Montgomery discussed the
considerations and questions that were raised during the last City Council meeting; there was extensive discussion of the types of
dwelling units the applicant intends to build on the subject properties. There are many different types of single-family units and
several units can be included in one project with a single tax identification number. The building type to be constructed was called
into question as many wondered if the units are attached or detached; if attached, the units share common walls such as side walls
or walls and ceiling/floor. He noted that existing property owners want to sell because they can no longer maintain their properties
due to size, isolation, and costly maintenance for older structures. He then discussed the options that exist if the plan amendment
and rezoning are not approved. The Hart/Jones property is pa1t of a five-acre Planned Residential Unit Development (PRUD) that
allows seven dwelling units per acre; this is an infill subdivision with minimwn lot sizes of 4,500 square feet. The Giboney
property is also part ofa five-acre PRUD with the same density and could be used for a six-lot subdivision with six accessory
dwelling units (ADUs). He then stated the zoning options identified in the Plan for the northeast corner include R-3/CO, PI/CO, or
CI/CO. He then discussed zoning considerations, noting the R-1-6 zoning designation allows single family detached homes and
PRUD developments for properties of five acres or larger. The R-2 zone allows single family or duplex development and PRUD
developments if the property is five acres or larger. Finally, the R-3 zone allows for single family to multiple family projects or
PRUD developments if the property is eight acres or larger. The subject property is 4.72 acres, meaning the petitioner could not
pursue a PRUD project according to cmTent zoning guidelines. Mr. Montgomery then discussed the Commission's consideration
of the plan amendment; they contemplated whether the character of the area has changed from what was anticipated when the
Community Plan was developed. He offered a comparison of aerial photographs of the area from 1993 and 2019; the photographs
illush·ated the infill development that has occurred in the area immediately sun-ounding the subject property. Residents have
expressed concern about traffic impacts of the project, especially since 2 nd Street includes center medians and it can be difficult to
get in and out of prope1ties fronting 2 nd Street; however, it is imp01tant to understand the medians were installed to mitigate h·affic
impacts associated with increased business development of Business Depot Ogden (BDO). The General Plan and Lynn
Community Plan calls for single-family detached units on 2 nd Street. The Commission considered whether there are special
circumstances that warrant consideration of a Community Plan amendment; this includes things like regional changes,
transportation changes, and any conflicts with the General Plan sh·ategies and objectives. The Plam1ing Commission ultimately
determined that an amendment of the map and text of the Community Plan would be required if the R-5 or R-3 zone is suppmted;
however, they voted unanimously to recommend denial of a Plan amendment.
