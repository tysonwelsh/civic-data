      Restaurant, Fast Food; SC-2 Zone; Mountain Summit Management/Matt Young
      (applicant) [#23127; parcel 20-25-351-012]

Matt Young, applicant, said they hope to receive approval to build a Culver’s Restaurant. They
anticipate a 120-day construction timeline.

Ray McCandless gave an overview of the area, including access points. There appear to be no impacts
to the neighboring properties. A lot line adjustment shifted the property line that added parking. Forty-
eight stalls are required and 48 are shown on the plan. The drive-through lanes wrap around the

1.     Planning Commission approvals do not include Public Safety, Fire, Building and Safety, or
       Engineering approval.
2.     Any appeals to the decision of the Planning Commission may be made to the Board of
       Adjustment within thirty (30) days pursuant to City Code, Section 15-5-3.

3.    Dahle property; 7106 South 2200 West; Future Land Use Map Amendment from Low
      Density Residential to Medium Density Residential and Rezone 1.6 acres from A-1
      (Agricultural 1-acre lots) Zone to R-1-8C (Single-family Residential, 8,000 square foot
      lots) Zone; York Howell & Guymon/Paxton Guymon (applicant) [#23315, 23316; parcel
      21-28-228-011]

Paxton Guymon, applicant, said the property owners have lived here for more than 50 years. The
current agricultural zone is a holding zone for future development. They want to divide the property
into six lots, and they consider this a redevelopment project. The existing home will be demolished,
and six new homes will be built. The Dahles will move back to the property in a new home in order to
stay in the neighborhood. He gave some statistics that show why Utah is in a housing crisis. They are
asking for medium density residential. Their product will fit on 4,000-5,000 square foot lots, but they
will fit nicely on 8,000 square foot lots. They are contemporary style homes that target an entry point
market, which helps with the housing crisis. No homeowners’ association is planned for this project.

Brian Berndt said the land use amendment is from low to medium density residential with 8,000 square
foot minimum lots. He reviewed the current zoning for the surrounding area in comparison with the
land use. If they were to bring the land use in the area to be in line with the current zoning, then the
property to the south and east would be in the medium density designation. There was some question
about access to the vacant property to the west, but the canal company usually allows one access point
within a certain distance.

Jay Thomas asked for clarification to staff’s finding on criteria 2.

Brian Berndt said when considering deciding criteria, you don’t want to approve something that is a

4.    Text Amendment – Landscaping; Amend the 2009 City Code Chapter 13 Landscaping
      Requirements and Section 13-2-3 Definitions; City-wide applicability; City of West
      Jordan (applicant)

Larry Gardner said that this code is being amended to satisfy the contract requirements of Jordan
Valley Water Conservancy District by adopting their efficiency standards and water saving
landscape practices. Landscaping is an obvious way to address water usage issues. The 2010
landscape code focused mostly on plant selection. The purpose and focus of this amendment are on
aesthetics under the overarching theme of efficiency and conservation. The code exempts city-
owned and maintained properties because the mission and level of service that the city has to
maintain are different than a business or a residence. Also, the city is conscious of water
conservation. The ordinance has never addressed wasting water, so there is now a provision if the
city chooses to enforce it. Soil preparation and retention requirements will be reviewed on the plan
set and at the time of bond release. It increases the amount of bed coverage at the time of plant
maturity, which reduces evaporation from the soil. The water calculation worksheet was
eliminated. Spray sprinklers have to be high efficiency with a self-pressuring regulating device.
Tree species and varieties have to be selected from those that thrive in the climate for the area.
Many trees have been removed from the street tree selection. Turf in commercial and residential
parkstrips will be prohibited. At least one model home in a residential development will be
landscaped using water efficiency and Localscapes design. This code also clarifies the regulations
for tree encroachment into the right-of-way. Staff recommended that the commission take public
comment and continue the item to the next meeting so that input from Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy District is reflected.

A discussion of rainwater collection policies was held. Staff will review the State of Utah policy
and add any appropriate language to encourage it. Suggested wording included, “West Jordan
encourages residents to harvest rainwater in accordance with State Code (code reference listed).

5.     Text Amendment – Official Titles; Amend the 2009 West Jordan City Code regarding
       Change of Municipal Form of Government Revisions, for Land Use Regulation Titles;
       City-wide applicability; City of West Jordan (applicant)

Duncan Murray said this amendment follows up on a similar ordinance considered by the city council,
but it is for the land use titles 5 and 8 through 15. The amendments update some names and titles to
reflect the change in form of government. The reference to ‘papers’ is being changed to ‘documents’ or
‘records.’ Titles 5 and 9 will now clarify that representatives to multi-city boards are appointed by the
mayor with advice and consent of the city council. Section 13-3-1 makes it clear that development fees
are non-refundable. State code allows cities to only charge fees that compensate for employee time
spent, so the city is entitled to keep the costs once the time has been expended.

Trish Hatch opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

               Amend the 2009 West Jordan City Code regarding Change of Municipal Form of
