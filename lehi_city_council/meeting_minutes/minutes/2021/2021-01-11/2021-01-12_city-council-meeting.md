Pre-Council, 5:30 p.m.
1. Welcome and Invocation
Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present. Councilor
Albrecht gave the invocation.

2. Select A Mayor Pro Tempore
Councilor Albrecht nominated Councilor Southwick as Mayor Pro Tempore. Councilor Hancock
seconded the nomination. Councilor Koivisto nominated Councilor Condie. Councilor Hancock
seconded the nomination. Mayor Johnson asked the Councilor to vote by secret ballot. The vote
was 3 for Councilor Southwick and 2 for Councilor Condie. Councilor Southwick was selected as
Mayor Pro Tempore.

3. Presentations and Reports
3.1) Traverse Mountain Area Plan Amendment Discussion
Brad Kenison, Assistant City Engineer, explained that the amendment is needed for public
facilities to add a water and pressurized irrigation tanks as well as a power substation. The water
tanks work well at the proposed location because of the elevation. The culinary tank would be
underground, and the PI would be in a reservoir. The area plan states that the culinary tanks are
dedicated to the City at no cost. He said that the area they are proposing isn’t currently programed
for a park or trail system. Joel Eves, Power Director, stated that the new proposed location would
be the best for cost savings. He also noted that Lehi City would need to purchase the property from
the developer for the power substation.

3.2) Update on Lehi City’s Environmental Sustainability Efforts
Todd Munger and Steve Roll presented on the City’s sustainability efforts. Mr. Munger stated
that they will continue to educate the public. He reported on a project that they did at all the
schools in the city that showed the PM levels during student drop off and pick up. He said their

7. Consideration of Ordinance #03-2020, approval of the Traverse Mountain Area Plan
   Amendment eliminating a maximum street length.
   Matthew Loveland, with Fieldstone Homes, was representing the applicant. He stated that this
   land in Hidden Canyon is very unique with its own set of challenges. The new plan allows
   them to work with the topography in a more natural way, but because of that, they need to
   amend the block length requirements. He stated that the new plan allows for less earth work,
   reduces retaining walls, reduces cut and fill, decreases the area of disturbance to maintain the
   natural vegetation and reduces the number of units. They are permitted 176 units and they are
   proposing 120. He stated that they are asking for 700 more feet of road than what is allowed
   by the Area Plan.

    Councilor Albrecht inquired if they are planning to sale the remaining density. Mr. Loveland
    stated that it could only be transferred to the Highway Commercial zone and they do not own
    property there. He stated that they could put in the maximum allowed density, but felt that
    wouldn’t be right for the community.

    Mr. Struthers stated that staff is recommending approval of this. They believe the exception is
    worth avoiding a lot of grading. He stated that the City code allows for exceptions to block
    lengths, such as for topography, but the Area Plan does not.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public comments.

    Rob Ludlow stated that Fieldstone Homes is not in good standings with the Traverse Mountain
    HOA because they have not paid for private park impact fees. He doesn’t agree with planning
    by exception. The exception isn’t good for the health and welfare of the community because it
Lehi City Council Meeting                         4                                    January 12, 2021
    will allow for the homes to be higher which becomes more of a fire hazard. It’s also more
    difficult for a snowplow or firetruck to turnaround when the block length is increased. He
    wants them to follow the rules.

    Mr. Struthers stated that compared to the current approved plan, this would actually bring more
    homes into the bottom of the canyon. He said this is a smaller footprint with less grading. Mr.
    Struthers stated that any lots next to open space will have to meet criteria for defensible space
    for fire prevention.

    Montina Hamilton asked why they couldn’t find a way to make a road connection and not
    make an exception. He stated that if the city is gaining something then they should do it, but
    they should find a way to stay in the code even if they need to drop some lots.

    Travis Isakson stated that the number of allowed density isn’t real because the market isn’t
    allowing it, or the topography is such that it won’t work, so we shouldn’t feel like they are
    saving the city on density. He wants to make sure they do what they said they would do with
    the park impact fees.

    Jason Harris is a resident of Traverse Mountain and represents Fieldstone Homes. He stated
    that the private park impact fee is not an issue of this Council and believes it is not legal. He
    said they are not in bad standing with the HOA. He said that they can fit the 176 units, but they
    have chosen not to. He stated that a connection is not possible due to the topography and
    elevation.

    Councilor Koivisto doesn’t feel comfortable changing the Area Plan and believes this is a big
    exception.

    Mr. Harris stated that they’ve learned a lot as they’ve developed on Traverse Mountain and
    that all they need to execute this plan is a minor block length change.

    Council and staff discussed other options that may allow for an exception without amending
    the Area Plan.

    Councilor Condie stated he understanding that it can be frustrating to amend an Area Plan, but
    if the footprint is less, and the grading is less and it gives us a better plan and is a positive for
    the whole community then why not do it. He said that the only negative he sees here is just that
    it’s an amendment.

    Councilor Koivisto stated that she was concerned with the snowplow and firetruck issues that
    were brought up and was concerned with amending the Area Plan.

    Mayor Johnson asked which route would be more beneficial for firetrucks and snowplows, the
    one being proposed with the added block length or with cul-de-sacs.

    Chief Craft and BJ Benson stated that firetrucks and snowplows are not a concern with a longer
    connection. They have more concerns and issues with cul-de-sacs.

13. Consideration of Ordinance #02-2021, approval of the Traverse Mountain Area Plan
    Amendment, changing the land use designation from Open Space to Public Facility on
    approximately 5.2-acres located at approximately 5700 North Flight Park Road.
    Mayor Johnson stated that this was discussed in the Pre-Council meeting. Brad Kenison,
    Assistant City Engineer gave a presentation. He noted that the public facilities are proposed
    to be on designated open space. Joel Eves showed where the power substation would be
    located. He noted that it will be located close to the large transmission lines that are already
    there. Mr. Kenison stated that the water tanks are located at a specific elevation to help with
    pressure zones.

    Mr. Kenison stated that the city intends to pay the developer for the power substation
    property, but the developer will donate the land for the water tanks.

    Mayor Johnson asked for public comments.

    Jared Morgan with GWC Capito, is a partner with Geneva. He stated that the revenue
    generated from the sale of the substation property will be dedicated to the Traverse Mountain
    Trails Association.

    of the property back to the Traverse Mountain Trails Association. He stated that they are still
    taking away open space and are losing that open space backdrop. He was concerned that the
    appraisal was too low and suggested another opinion. He also appreciated the power
    department paying for that property which will no longer be open space, but he disagrees
    with the water department’s approach. He noted that the Area Plan shows there was $100,000
    budgeted for property for water tanks to be placed outside of the Traverse Mountain Area
