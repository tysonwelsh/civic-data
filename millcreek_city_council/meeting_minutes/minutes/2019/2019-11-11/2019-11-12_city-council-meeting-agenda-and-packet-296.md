Proclamation Recognizing Helen Loraine Blood “Teacher Extraordinaire”
WHEREAS, Helen Loraine Blood, aka “Miss Blood” recently passed away at the age of 83; and
WHEREAS, for over forty (40) years of her life Miss Blood dedicated herself to teaching children who
attended Millcreek Elementary School; and
WHEREAS, Miss Blood was far more than a classroom teacher in that she cared personally for each
child who was one of “her students”; and
WHERAS, Miss Blood made a significant difference in the lives of literally thousands of students and
their families; and
WHEREAS, Miss Blood was a great teacher who went above and beyond the daily classroom
curriculum in that she taught each student self-worth. She continued to follow her students many years
after they left her classroom by attending her student’s games, activities, wedding receptions, and by
always being a stable force for good in the Millcreek neighborhood; and
WHEREAS, Helen Loraine Blood’s students adored her, and she adored them. Her students were her
family; and
WHEREAS, Miss Blood, with a contagious smile, a positive attitude, and a love of her students was
and still is, in the hearts of all “her students”, a “TEACHER EXTRAORDINAIRE”.
NOW, THEREFORE BE IT KNOWN, that the Mayor and City Council of Millcreek do hereby with
heart felt gratitude proclaim this 12" day of November 2019 to be HELEN LORAINE BLOOD DAY.

2.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-37, Millcreek’s Intent to Study
and Consider Participation in the Community Renewable Energy Act of Utah; Kurt
Hansen, Legislative Policy Director
Mayor Silvestrini said the Public Service Commission had issued proposed draft rules for
H.B. 411. Kurt Hansen said a committee of stakeholders had been involved in making the
rules and the rules would be reviewed by the Public Service Commission. Mayor
Silvestrini said the rules follow the statute and establish the regulations of the opt-out
options for customers, the study costs to determine the rate, and how the rate would be
determined. He said the Council would not sign a contract with Rocky Mountain Power
or adopt an ordinance until the cost for Millcreek residents was known. He said he
recently attended the Central Wasatch Commission retreat and spoke with the mayors
from Salt Lake City and Park City about the program. Those cities consume more energy
than Millcreek, but Rocky Mountain Power has assured them that the renewable energy
will cost the same amount or less than what they are currently paying for their municipal
needs. Council Member Jackson said she spoke with the Chair of the Public Service
Commission and an assistant attorney general overseeing utilities and they did not believe
the cost would be less expensive for customers. She said there were no known facts at
that point so it was hard to determine what would happen. Mayor Silvestrini said the
Council wanted to be on the path for renewable energy but wanted to know what they
were getting into to protect the residents of Millcreek. The program is a process and

3. Financial Matters
3.1 Presentation of Fiscal Year 2018-19 Audit; Jeff Miles, HBME, LLC
Mayor Silvestrini said he was pleased Millcreek had a clean audit. Jeff Miles said he was
with HBME, LLC, formally Hansen, Bradshaw, Malmrose & Erickson. He then went
over the audit report. He said the audit opinion on the City’s financial statements was
clean (page 1). He said the City’s total assets increased by about $3 million (page 5) and
most of that was related to property tax receivables that the City was not generating
before. The net position of the City increased by about $6 million by the end of the fiscal
year. The City technically had no debt obligations because it was paid off on June 30%
but on July 1‘ received a new tax revenue anticipation note. He said on page 6 was the
income statement by the City and income was up by about $9 million, most of which was
from property taxes. The Capital Grants and Contributions revenue line item was down
due to the restructuring of how the court was handled by the City.

He mentioned the City had to recognize retirement assets and obligations, so the
Statement of Net Position showed the City’s standing in the retirement system (page 5).
Council Member Marchant asked if the amount ($866,502) reflected how well the Utah
Retirement System (URS) was doing. Miles said a little bit. He said on page 9 the net
pension liability was $855,153, so the City was net positive $10,000. Johnson said
municipalities need to recognize the liability of pensions. Miles said about 5 years ago
cities had to start reporting pension liabilities. Council Member Marchant said he was
looking forward to seeing those trends in the future. Miles said the City’s revenues had
increased by $5.8 million and about $2 million of capital assets that were purchased
during the year. There was no new debt issued in the year but there was in the
Community Reinvestment Agency after year end, so there will be long term debt in the
books next year. The audit is not required to test controls but gain an understanding of the
City’s internal controls. The auditors did not identify any deficiencies. He said page 41
was areport on compliance items required by the State Auditor’s Office, which included:
budgetary compliance, fund balance, Utah Retirement Systems, cash management,
restricted taxes and related revenues, Open and Public Meetings Act, and Treasurer’s
bond. He did not identify any compliance issues with those items. Council Member Uipi
commended Laurie Johnson on informing the Council about the City’s finances.

Council Member Jackson moved to accept the audit report for Fiscal Year 2018-19.
Council Member Marchant seconded. Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. All Council
Members voted yes. The motion passed unanimously.

5.1 Mayor’s Report
Mayor Silvestrini congratulated Council Member Catten and Council Member Jackson
on their reelection to the Council. Council Member Uipi congratulated him on his
reelection. Mayor Silvestrini said he wished voter turnout was higher though it did break
40%. He said he attended the Central Wasatch Commission (CWC) retreat and they

create a specialized national recreational area, add a new wilderness area on Grandeur
Peak, and get more federal protection on the water shed. The bill would also implement
the principles of the Mountain Accord with compromises from environmental groups and
ski resorts on land exchanges at fair market value. The ski resorts do not want to do the
land exchanges anymore so the CWC has paused advancing the legislation until there
was a consensus. The CWC also discussed restructuring the stakeholders committee,
transportation improvements to the Cottonwood canyons, and the need to address its
funding. Mayor Silvestrini said he participated in a Veterans Day event at Mill Creek
Elementary School and at St. Mark’s Hospital.

3. It was a financial win for our city to exit SLVLESA, the funding arm in the county that provides
