43            Land Use Authority Discussion
44
45   Mayor Staggs explained that Councilmember McCay had asked for clarification and discussion
46   on land use authority of the Planning Commission and the City Council.
47
48   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the City Council serves as the
49   land use authority for zoning changes, general plan amendments and text amendments to the
50   ordinance. The Planning Commission is the land use authority for site plans, residential
51   subdivisions and conditional use permits. The ordinance amendments are how City Council
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     March 21, 2023




 1   gives direction to staff and the Planning Commission as to what standards need to be met by
 2   developers.
 3
 4   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that the Parkside subdivision resulted in discussions
 5   about whether the finished product matched what was envisioned when the rezone was first
 6   granted. Because of this, Council questioned whether they should see the subdivision approval
 7   after the rezone. In the 2023 session of the legislature, the legislature passed SB 174 which
 8   makes clear that administrative land use decisions do not include the legislative body
 9   specifically as to subdivision approval. Application of administrative portions of the subdivision
10   approvals should go to a body separate from the legislative body.
11
12   Mr. Carter explained there are other things he could recommend. He said the SD designation in
13   the code allows the Council to have uniquely tailored development requirements for rezones
14   which could include lot counts and height restrictions. He suggested that the Council rely on the
15   SD designation but allow staff enough time to get the job done correctly.
16
17   Mayor Staggs said in talking with Councilmember McCay that they were concerned with
18   delegating situations such as incompatible uses or higher density to the Planning Commission.
19   In response, Mr. Carter said that SB 174 does not allow the legislative body to make decisions
20   once it comes back for site approval.
21
22   Councilmember McCay suggested that applications could come back to Council if there were
23   more than 10 units, larger than 4,000 square foot buildings, or if the surrounding zoning is
24   different. Mr. Carter said another tool the Council could use is a development agreement which
25   enables the Council to serve as both the zoning authority and administrative body. This does
26   require the developer to invest money upfront in their design before they know they have the
27   approval they want.
28
29   Councilmember McDougal said that if the Council wants to get specific then they need to go the
30   SD route. If a developer asks for an exception, then they can use an SD designation or require
31   a development agreement. Mr. Carter agreed.
32
33   Mr. Lethbridge said the City does not have properties that are zoned for multi-family. This
34   means that any project would come through the zoning process to redevelop. At this point, the
35   Council could identify the concerns and adopt a development agreement.
36
37   Mr. Carter reiterated that under the statute, the Council can make decisions on zoning and not
38   on subdivision applications. The Council can use a development agreement at the rezone to
39   designate subdivision requirements so that there is no need for it to come back to Council.
40
41   Councilmember McDougal said a development agreement would clearly outline to residents
42   what a development would look like which has been the biggest obstacle in the past.
43
44   Mr. Lethbridge asked that the Council remember that the legislative power to set those
45   standards and designations lies with them. If there are areas of concern, or things that continue
46   to be the same or consistent then they can codify this so it is part of the code.
47
48   Councilmember McCay said she would like the ordinance to require a development agreement
49   so that it was automatic when the rezone is for multi-family units or larger than 4,000 square
50   feet.

30            Ordinance No. 23-07 – Residential Chicken Ordinance, Adopting Amendments to
31            the Residential Chicken Ordinance
32
33   Mr. Lethbridge explained that the Riverton City ordinance currently allows up to 6 residential
34   chickens to be kept on residential property of 10,000-square feet or larger. 10,000 square feet is
35   ¼ acre. Based on recent requests by residents and inquiries by the City Council. Staff has
36   reviewed various approaches to allowing chickens on lots below 10,000 square feet. One
37   method was a ‘sliding scale”, used in Herriman and other communities. The sliding scale allows
38   for keeping a progressively smaller number of residential chickens in lots of different sizes. The
39   zoning results in residents of a 5,000-7,999 square foot lot being allowed to keep four (4)
40   chickens, and residents of an 8,000-10,000 square foot lot are allowed to keep six (6).
