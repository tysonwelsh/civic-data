II)     ITEMS FOR DISCUSSION

1. Park City Arts and Culture District - Proposed Phased Approach and Financial Plan:
David Everitt, Deputy City Manager, Stephen Swisher, GTS Development, Jason Glidden,
Affordable Housing Manager, Julia Collins, Transportation Planner, Jed Briggs and Erik Daenitz,
Budget, presented this item. Mayor Beerman reviewed that 10 months ago Council was moving
forward with the Arts and Culture District and now Council was frustrated because the pandemic
slowed down the goals established in Vision 2020. He indicated the land was purchased to
meet community goals and this district would help with the community's critical priorities. All
things considered, the City was in a great position to move forward with those goals. Matt Dias
thanked staff for their work on this presentation over the past few weeks. He appreciated
Council considering this project thoughtfully. He stated the goal was to preserve and protect the
City. He felt the City was strong with protecting land and the Historic District, but it was slower
with resident services, such as the MARC and sidewalks. He felt this district would be a

destination for locals to enjoy music, art, and other culture experiences and it would include a
housing component. He knew there were obstacles, with construction that had skyrocketed and
the COVID pandemic uncertainty. He asked staff to create a path to achieve this goal that was
viable. He stressed this was a win no matter what Council decided because of the location of
the land.

Everitt briefly summarized the purpose for each of the buildings in the district and stated he
would mainly talk about Building One, which would have housing units on the top levels and
artist studios on the main level. He indicated the guiding principles of the district were to
prioritize affordable housing, invest in Transit and active transportation, prioritize spaces that
would foster economic opportunities, and manage for flexibility for the next two to four years.

The scope of the project was revised and Building One would have 50 housing units. There
would be a total of 75 housing units over the entire site. The Transit infrastructure was moved to
the surface instead of being underground, active transportation enhancements were added on
the perimeter, above ground parking was reduced, there was additional ingress/egress to the
underground level, a plaza level drop and load point was added along the western edge, and
housing units were moved to higher floors for additional flex spaces on the plaza. Everitt viewed
the area as a mobility hub.

Swisher explained the design for the district. He noted most of the parking was underground
and only a few spaces were available on the surface for the affordable housing units. Council
Member Joyce asked if staff looked at artist spaces in other areas because he thought a 12x15
space seemed small. Swisher stated the space would be set up with flexible dividers so some
studios would be larger and some would be smaller.

Council Member Gerber asked if there was concern with having bus pullouts on both sides of
Bonanza since people would cross in front of those buses. Everitt stated they could work on
what would make the connection work better and they were aware of the situation.

Everitt reviewed the cost of construction for the project. He noted the project was broken down
into components necessary for housing, transit, walkability and other costs. If the entire project
was built at once, it would cost approximately $88.4 million. He broke down the project into
phases. Phase One would cost $57.9 million. Briggs indicated this phase would be funded by
bonds, the senior center funds, the firehouse sale proceeds, housing fees-in lieu, some
Transient Room Tax (TRT) funds, sale of land, and from repurposing some Capital Fund
projects such as equipment replacement. The Transportation Fund would cover transit costs
and the walkability GO bond would cover the walkability aspect of the project.

Council Member Doilney estimated the cost related to housing would put each unit at $430,000,
which was $900 per square foot, and asserted that seemed high. He asked if the built-ins added
to that cost. Glidden affirmed that the built-ins added up and the underground parking was
expensive as well. Council Member Doilney asked how many units from other projects were
being set aside in order to have these units and what would be the assumed land sale price if it
were on the open market. Everitt stated the land sales amount reflected the market rates in
2017 when the letters of intent were signed between the City and Kimball Art Center and
Sundance. Dias noted the previous Council made that deal.

Mayor Beerman indicated the TRT bonding policy was set at 65% so in the case of an economic
downturn, the City could pay its debt. He asked if the budgeted $5.6 million TRT funds were
based on an estimate of contingency funds that had not yet been collected. Briggs stated some
of the funding had been built up and some would be built up during construction to use a total of
100% of TRT funds. Mayor Beerman asked if that amount took into account the lower TRT
revenue that was coming in. Briggs stated it was down now and would slowly ramp back up to
normal TRT revenue. Dias noted this was compliant with the City’s TRT policy. Mayor Beerman
stated the City normally had the funds and then began a project, but this proposal was
committing to a project without having the funds in place. Council Member Gerber thought the
TRT funds had limited uses. Everitt stated there were no restrictions on how TRT money was
spent. Council passed a resolution dedicating the money to the Arts and Culture District.

Council Member Joyce referred to the $6.8 million in revenue for the sale of the land to Kimball
Arts Center and Sundance. He asked what would happen if the City moved forward and the
other entities weren't ready to buy. Briggs stated the construction timeline showed there would
always be money. Everitt stated the sale of the land would have to happen by June, 2022.
Council Member Joyce didn't want to fund Phase One and then not know how to do the other
phases. He would rather wait and construct the entire project at once. Everitt stated staff could
work on how to fund the other phases and present that Thursday. He thought the bulk of the
cost was in Phase One so the other phases would be more manageable. Council Member
Joyce thought it would be important to know how the City could get to Phase Two. He thought
the budget worked for Phase One, but all sources had been tapped. He hesitated with
proceeding if all the other City projects were killed in the process. Everitt stated the other
projects wouldn't be killed, but they might be delayed a couple years. He also noted that after
Phase One, Council could change direction on things they wanted to see, and those could be
implemented in subsequent phases. Council Member Joyce was fine that Phase Two wasn't
completely lined out, but he wanted to start that discussion.

Council Member Doilney stated Phase Two could include infrastructure on the site, with a land
bank that could be regulated with how it was built out. He asked Council Member Joyce if Phase
Two had to follow immediately or could it wait so other projects could proceed. Council Member
Joyce asked if the City wanted to spend $57 million to build Phase One if it wasn't clear that it
would have an Arts and Culture District at the end of the project. Sundance and Kimball Arts
Center might back out of the deal and then the Arts and Culture project could fall through. He
wanted to be sure that Council wanted Phase One in that scenario.

Council Member Henney asked if there would be a discussion on the affordable housing
pipeline. He wanted to understand what would happen to other housing projects if all the
Housing Fund was put into this project. He also asked how much of affordable housing parking
was underground. Everitt stated some stalls were at the surface and the remaining stalls were
underground, and those two costs were combined. Council Member Henney thought parking
costs were a burden for affordable housing occupants. He suggested having housing without a
parking requirement and offer the units without a parking option. He thought those occupants
could park at Richardson Flat and take Transit into town. Everitt stated the cost of the unit was
not what would be charged in the rent. Council Member Henney stated the renter wouldn't be
charged, but the taxpayer was ultimately paying for the parking. Glidden stated he was working

Council Member Worel stated 1% of the cost of municipal capital projects was set aside for
public art and asked if this had been factored into the project. Everitt stated the public art would
not be in Phase One. Council Member Worel noted the staff report touched on unspent funds
from projects expected to be under-budget, including public art, and asked if that was the
current Public Art Advisory Board (PAAB) budget. Briggs stated the amount in the report was
from unspent projects in the PAAB budget. He would look into that more and report back.

With regard to the Housing pipeline, Glidden indicated the Woodside Phase 2 project was not
feasible since the senior center was remaining in place, and he proposed changing the housing
pipeline to give that project’s budget to the Arts and Culture District for rental housing. Upon
completion of the project, the City would then bond for the Homestake Housing project and use
the rental revenue to pay the debt service on the bond. Once the Homestake project was
completed, the City would bond again based on the rental revenue from both the Arts and
Culture District and the Homestake units to fund future housing projects. He thought the shift
moving towards rental housing would be best for furthering the City's housing goals. It would
help the City’s workforce that could not afford to buy homes to have a place to live, and the
rental model would utilize housing funds more efficiently. The average cost of condos and
homes was unaffordable. He noted affordable housing pricing was too high for workforce as
well.

Daenitz stated a challenge of building a for-sale unit was the rising cost of inflation and
construction. Council Member Doilney asked how much the units would rent for. Daenitz stated
a studio would rent for $1,400-$1,450, including the HOA fee. Mayor Beerman stated if the
rental model was in the Arts and Culture District, it would continue with the Homestake project.
Renters would complain to the City about maintenance and neighbors. There would be a new
set of work with the rental model. He noted the City would be a different kind of landlord than a
private market landlord.

Council Member Doilney asked if using the Woodside Phase 2 funds would make the Arts and
Culture District project more viable. Glidden stated the rental market with the Homestake model
would create more revenue than the other housing projects. He was working on tools that would
allow the City to work in partnerships and reduce the capital the City would need to contribute to
these projects. He noted he would discuss this further in the future.

Council Member Worel asked for data on the demand for micro units. Council Member Doilney
stated there were no micro units available in the area because they were in such demand.
Glidden stated he would bring back data on micro units. Council Member Joyce asked to
determine what groups the City was targeting when referring to 50% AMI. Council Member
Gerber indicated she heard from Main Street merchants that it was difficult to find staff. She
thought there were ways to adjust the prices, such as Council Member Henney's idea of
eliminating parking. She knew there was a strong need for the units. Council Member Joyce felt
the merchants were talking about seasonal hires and not year round employees. Council had
always wanted the employers to be responsible for seasonal housing. Council Member Gerber
agreed, but noted there was no workforce at a manager level or below that lived in town.

Council Member Doilney thought the parking spaces could be rented for a monthly rate and that
revenue would supplement the housing. Council Member Gerber asked if a discussion on
housing funding would be combined with the Arts and Culture District discussion. Glidden stated
a housing funding discussion was planned for February.

Julia Collins discussed 10 active transportation projects around the Arts and Culture District that
were scheduled for the next three years. She presented three scenarios, with costs ranging
from $1.25 million to $25 million.

Mayor Beerman opened the meeting for public input.

Aldy Milliken, Executive Director, Kimball Arts Center (KAC), stated art could impact residents
and tourists. He explained the purpose of KAC and noted it employed many individuals. The
30,000 square foot building planned for the Arts and Culture District would serve 100,000 +
people per year. He felt Park City was behind other cities with regard to arts, which diversified
and strengthened the economy. He looked forward to a collaborative process with the City going
forward.

Teri Orr stated the City purchased the land in a zone where a developer could have built eight
story structures. It included KAC and Sundance, and formed a project that would enhance the
area. There was toxic soil there and it would have to be mitigated. She indicated the major
source of funding was TRT funds, and since those funds were diminished she hoped the project
could be phased. She reviewed the history of Park City and stated the charm was disappearing.
She suggested building housing and transit and open spaces. She suggested looking at where
arts had taken over in other cities. She noted buildings for sale in Park City that could be
transformed into an art corridor. She thought the slate should be wiped clean and Council
should look for the best use for those five acres, which would include housing and community
resources.

Ed Parigian supported the rental model for housing moving forward. He stated regular people
couldn't afford to live here. He encouraged Council to have the housing discussion now. He also
liked the Arts and Culture District and hoped to see it built as a beautiful local village.

Alex Butwinski supported the Arts and Culture District when it was announced, but he was
concerned about the cost. He agreed with Orr. He was really concerned about the Kimball Arts
Center and Sundance stepping up to do their part in coming into the district.

Ed Parigian stated the Vail property to the south should be acquired by the City.

Angela Moschetta agreed that it was great to acquire this property, but she thought the district
was misguided. She hoped the City could pivot more since the arts were hurting before the
pandemic and now it was even worse. She supported seasonal housing so that the bars and
restaurants could survive. There was no evidence Sundance and the Kimball Arts Center would
follow through so the district should be reimagined with more affordable housing, micro
restaurants and micro retail. She also hoped work could be done on programming and
activating Main Street. She also suggested putting transit hubs at the City borders and getting
resort owners involved.

Mary Christa Smith pointed to the Vision 2020 process and stated the top priority was traffic and
congestion. She hoped this project would help with the flow and traffic issues. The current
district was a dead zone and residents were waiting to connect this area with other areas. She
knew it was tempting to value engineer this part, but she hoped for an upgraded transportation
plan.

Mayor Beerman closed the public input portion of the meeting and stated this would be
discussed for 1.5 hours at the Work Session on Thursday.
