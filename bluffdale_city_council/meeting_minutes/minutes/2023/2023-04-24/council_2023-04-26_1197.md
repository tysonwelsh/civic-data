       8.1     Resolution No. 2023-18 - Adopting the Tentative Budget for the 2023-2024

Mr. Kartchner gave an overview ofthe budget process. He reported that the City keeps its books
in fund accounting, unlike a business. The City is required to approve a Tentative Budget once a
year by the first meeting in May. They always attempt to do it sometime in April to allow the
Council more time. The Tentative Budget is just that, a tentative starting point. They will go into
it in more detail over the next several weeks going into more detail and listen to Council feedback.
The budget that will hopefully be passed tonight will differ from the Final Budget that needs to be
passed by June 22 according to state law. It was, however, that the Council pass it on June 14
since the June 28 meeting will be after the deadline.

Mr. Kartchner stated that if the Coxmcil requests a property tax increase as part of the budget, an
additional hearing will need to occur in August called a Truth in Taxation Hearing. Due to a new
fund being set up, this will be part ofthe proposed budget.

Once the budget is passed for a fiscal year, the Council can amend that budget as many times as
they want. They typically look at finances aroimd February and revenues and expense trends and
recommend budget adjustments at that time. The current budget does not address what the future
will bring. It includes estimates and projections ofwhat expectations are but nothing ever happens
exactly like the projections. Additional budget adjustments in the future are necessary to
accommodate any nuances to the economy or requirements ofthe City between now and then.

The initial budget creation begins by providing all the budget heads with a detail of what they have
budgeted in the past and what their current expenditures are. The department heads are asked to
go through their department budgets in detail and identify what changes they may see that they
need for the upcoming year. This includes reductions and increases for a variety of things. Once
that is prepared,it is passed over to himselfand Finance Manager,Stephanie Thayer to incorporate
the other calculations.


They have found during this process that a significant area is the Capital Projects Fund, which is
primarily managed by the Engineering Department and includes street renovations, new water
tanks, or infrastructure projects. Those big-ticket items frequently take more than a single year to
accomplish, and even when they are shorter-term, they will often cover multiple fiscal years.
Because of how fund accounting works they need to budget the expenditures for the year they're
expected but sometimes they do not know exactly which year a project will be completed. As a
result, they budget additional funding in the succeeding year to finish the project. This leads to
the same dollars being budgeted in two separate fiscal years. He urged the Council to remember
that they only spend those monies once even when they are incorporated into two different years.

One thing stafffinds particularly challenging is projecting revenue. Mr. Kartchner explained that
revenue is part science, part art, part philosophy, and part luck. Historically, they have done a
pretty good job of estimating and projecting what revenues will be, however, one area they have
not done a great job with over the last several years is sales taxes. They have been higher than
estimated because people are spending more on Amazon, which benefits the City.

Mr. Kartchner explained that revenues are impacted by decision events that are frequently outside
of the City's control. Positive or negative changes in the economy impact revenue. If a major
developer comes in and develops a large housing project impacts revenue. They do not have any
control over how that is going to happen.

Mr. Kartchner presented a slide showing the different types offunds the City uses and budgets for
each year. The General Fund is unrestricted with a variety ofrevenue sources with various levels
ofpredictability. One ofthem is property taxes. Property taxes are the most stable revenue source
the City has. Because of the way the State calculates the Certified Tax Rate, property taxes stay
the same year-to-year in dollars. The only increase that occurs is due to growth. Because the
