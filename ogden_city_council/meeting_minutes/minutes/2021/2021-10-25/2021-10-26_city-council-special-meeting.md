       )              A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a resolution supporting an application to the LeRay
            McAllister Critical Land and Conservation Fund for extension of the Bingham/Stone Farm conservation easement. The memo
            stated Ms. Anna Keogh has submitted an application to the Fund to secure an extension of an existing thirty-acre conservation
            easement on the Bingham/Stone Farm. The application requires the support of the City Council.
                      Ms. Keogh apologized for the confusion and miscommunications regarding this issue that led to the need to hold a
            special meeting this evening. She thanked the Council for being willing to meet. She then presented the histmy of the area and the
            Bingham/Stone Farm particularly and discussed the justification for adding eight acres to the conservation easement of the Fann.
                      Council member Choberka stated she read the supporting documentation for this request, but it not entirely sure the
            purpose of including the additional eight acres in the conservation easement. Ms. Keogh stated that if the land is included in the
            easement area, it will be preserved and protected in perpetuity as a farm. The land can be sold, but whoever buys it would be
            required to continue to farm it. She discussed ownership of the property and indicated all owners are in favor of preserving the
            land as a farm. She added that if the property is placed in the easement, the LeRay McAllister Land and Conservation fund will
            pay the owners approximately 30 percent of the appraised value of the land.
                      Council member Lopez inquired as to the value of the land and the anticipated payout for including the eight acres in the
            conservation easement. Ms. Keogh stated she is unsure the value at the moment as an appraisal has not been completed.
                      Council member Stephens thanked Ms. Keogh for the information provided tonight as it has provided important
            clarification for this request. He then asked Ms. Keogh to discuss the wildlife on the fann. Ms. Keogh stated there are geese that
            stop at the fann during migration, hawks, buzzards, deer, fox, skunks, and raccoons. Council member Stephens then stated that if
            Ms. Keogh is going to have trailways on the property, she should contact the City for advice on trail improvement.
                      Vice Chair White asked why the eight acres were not included in the original conservation easement. Ms. Keogh stated
            that is because the LeRay McAllister Fund did not have enough money to purchase all40 acres of the fann; they purchased 30
            acres and signed an agreement indicating they would buy the remaining acreage when there was adequate funding.
                      Council Executive Director Eller-Smith then presented a map to orient the Council the boundmy ofthe existing easement
            as well as the eight acres that would be included in the easement if this proposal is approved.
                      Council member Nadolski also thanked Ms. Keogh for the information provided and indicated that it has addressed the
            concems that he expressed during the work session meeting last week.

asked if the main reason for this request is to preserve the farmland, rather than to provide an opportunity for an operating
business to continue. Ms. Keogh stated that the main reason for the conservation is to preserve the history of the area and farming
activities. This led to discussion about opportunities for expanding uses on the property that would benefit more residents or
groups in the community.
          Chair Blair introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2021-15, entitled:
                     "A resolution of the Ogden City Council supporting the 2021 application to the LeRay McAllister Critical Land
                     Conservation Fund for extension of the conservation easement at Bingham/Stone Farm located at
                     approximately 4111 Street and Century Drive."
          Chair Blair then called for public input regarding the proposed resolution.
          Tammy Creager stated Ms. Keogh's focus is on the history of the area, but her focus is on the future of the area. She
noted that she and her husband have tried to purchase land around their home to make it possible to preserve open space and
farmland in the area. She would like to create a community garden in the neighborhood or expand agricultural uses to create food
sources for the local community. This contributes to fiscal responsibility for the entire community, and she is hopeful the Council
will vote to support the conservation easement.
          Angel Castillo thanked the Council for considering this issue; she spoke about this matter in the last meeting and restated
her support for the conservation easement. There is great historic value in the area, and it is also important to create spaces where
food production for the local population can take place.
          No additional persons came forward to be heard regarding this matter.
          Vice Chair White asked why it is necessary to include the property in an easement rather than just relying upon the
family to preserve the land as a farm. Ms. Keogh stated that the easement guarantees the land will be preserve in pe1petuity, which
is not possible if the land is simply kept in the family. Additionally, the conservation easement will provide some compensation to
the present owners of the land, and she feels they deserve that compensation for the sacrifices they have made to fann the land.
          COUNCIL MEMBER NADOLSKI MOVED THE RESOLUTION BE ADOPTED, INCLUDING MAP OPTION

          Council member Hyer stated he visited the subject property when the "Meet the Shoshone" event was held in the
neighborhood; he admonished Ms. Keogh to look for ways to create public amenities on the farmland to give people the
opportunity to learn the history of the area. Ms. Keogh stated she would love to do that and hopes to host many public events on
the farm.
          Council member Stephens stated that he also feels strongly that it is valuable to preserve remaining farmland in the City;
this gives people the opportunity to 'get back to their roots' by visiting such an area.

City Council comments
         Council member Hyer thanked the Council for considering the request from Ms. Keogh tonight and indicated he feels the
preservation of her land will make a positive contribution to the community.


     There being no further business to come before the Council, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE MEETING
ADJOURN AT 5:51P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER NADOLSKI, ALL VOTING AYE.
