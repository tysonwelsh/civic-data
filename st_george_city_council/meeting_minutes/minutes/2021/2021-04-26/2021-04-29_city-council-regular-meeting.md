DISCUSSION REGARDING THE ZONE CHANGE FOR RILASSANTE AT DIVARIO
(PA17):
     City Manager Adam Lenhard explained there have been several meetings regarding
     this request. He mentioned a number of neighbors are opposed.

      Community Development Director John Willis stated that the Council recently
      approved PA3; the next phase is PA17 which is proposed to be a high-density
      residential area. He showed and outlined the Divario land use map.

      Matt Ence, representative for Divario, provided a PowerPoint presentation covering
      the following topics: Development agreement review; Background; Benefits to the
      City; Owner contributions exceed $2.5 million (so far); Benefits to the owner; What
      do you want to see happen with PA17?

      Councilmember Larkin explained SID is a Special Improvement District.

      Resident Bob Routsong commented that the City needs to discuss traffic if 3,100
      units are proposed in this area.

      Councilmember Curtis asked how many project areas are in the development.

      Rick Rosenberg, representative for the applicant, noted there are 18 planning areas,
      he showed the areas outlined on a map. He explained that SID 89-1 was the SID
      that put in the Sunbrook infrastructure in to the golf course clubhouse – Sunbrook

      Drive, the water and sewer, storm drainage, etc. He noted the assessments have
      been paid. After being asked by Councilmember Larkin, Mr. Rosenberg explained
      that if the density was moved to a different planning area, there would need to be
      further discussion and the development agreement would need to be amended,
      creating a significant delay. He commented there has been a lot of detail and design
      that has gone into this particular site.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard stated that he does not believe an amendment to the
      agreement would be a substantial burden to the City is not prepared to take on
      quickly. Although there is a master plan for this property; however, staff agrees that
      the development agreement is clear that every subsequent development needs to go
      through the zoning process. He believes that the Council’s preference is to see the
      density move to a different planning area.

      Mr. Rosenberg noted the development agreement allows for 3,196 units. It also
      allows has flexibility that if density is not achieved in one area, it can be moved to
      another. The property owner is concerned with time and additional expense. He
      mentioned the developer has not been able to achieve the allowed density in some of
      the low-density areas. City Manager Adam Lenhard asked Mr. Rosenberg to clarify
      why he believes that as he is not sure if he agrees. Mr. Rosenberg explained he
      believes this is because the development agreement totals up to 3,192 units, making
      assumptions of density on each pad. Looking at the design and construction plans,
      comparing the number of units on the ground, the numbers are significantly less
      than what is on the chart. He stated the number could be made up somewhere else.
      They have spoken internally regarding the possibility of a better area for high
      density.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard reiterated that he wants to find a compromise; the City
      is a willing partner. He asked the Council if they would entertain proposals and
      recommend shifting the densities around as a potential solution.

      Councilmember Hughes acknowledged development agreements are in place to get a
      good outcome for the City and future residents of a development. Nearby residents
      are concerned with high density and increased traffic. He does not have any issues
      with moving the densities around; however, he would want to work within the
      development agreement. Higher density is needed, but the Council needs to think
      about the nearby residents.

      Applicant Mark Teepen noted he met with some of the residents, developers, and
      City staff. Traffic-wise, this piece (PA-17) is capable of serving the number of homes
      being proposed. They are under contract to sell the land; therefore, the potential
      buyer would need to agree to moving densities. Alternatively, they looked at PA-11.
      Infrastructure does not exist further down; moving the density may create a great
      delay. If they move the density, they run the risk of violating a disclosure
      agreement. After being asked by Councilmember Larkin, Mr. Teepen stated that PA-
      12 has not been developed.

AMEND GENERAL PLAN/ORDINANCE:
    Consider approval of an ordinance changing the general plan from MDR
    (Medium Density Residential, 5-9 dwelling units per acre) to HDR (High
    Density Residential, 16-22 dwelling units per acre) on approximately 7.51
    acres generally located at 1700 West Canyon View Drive known as Canyon
    View Drive General Plan Amendment. Case No. 2021-GPA-002

      Planner Dan Boles presented the request to amend the general plan from MDR to
      HDR on approximately 7.51 acres generally located at 1700 West Canyon View Drive
      known as Canyon View Drive General Plan Amendment. He provided a PowerPoint
      presentation covering the following topics: Aerial map; Existing general plan map;
      Zoning map; General plan amendment exhibit. The developer could build 5-9
      dwelling units per acre currently and under the R-3 zone, they can build up to 13
      dwelling units per acre. The Planning Commission recommended denial as they were
      concerned with adding more high density to this area. The request is to go up to 16-
      22 dwelling units per acre.

      Councilmember Hughes commented that some of the concerns from residents relate
      to traffic. These are the kinds of places the Council would like to see higher density
      as there is access to public transportation as well as amenities.

      Councilmember Larkin explained her concern is that the northern piece is adjacent to
      single-family homes. It would make sense for that property to be zoned differently.
      She would like to see the southside be developed as a planned development and
      single-family in the northern portion.

      Applicant Greg Whitehead stated this is a strange piece and a strange request
      because they already have R-3 zoning. He explained their initial thought was to do a
      PD-R; however, staff stated that the request would default to the existing general
      plan with a density of 5-9 dwelling units per acre. He has no issue showing a plan
      similar to the one on Riverside Drive. It would be nice to pick up a few more units.
      With the hillside, they are looking at 18-19 dwelling units. He is okay with removing
      the one-acre to the north. If that piece is zoned as he has applied and come back
      with a PD-R, he can put in a single-family home on that property. He does not
      believe the Planning Commission understood why a project that is zoned R-3 would
      request a general plan that is similar. If the general plan change is approved, they
      would come back to request PD-R.

      Councilmember Larkin noted she can see the benefits; however, she feels there may
      be an issue with making the entire area PD-R. One of her concerns is if the property
      were to be sold. She would like to see the Council approve the density on the
      southern portion differently than the northern portion.

      Mr. Whitehead stated there are separate owners currently. If the northern piece is
      excluded, the densities already exist because it is zoned R-3. He feels this is a good
      compromise to cleaning up the corner.

      Councilmember Hughes noted before the Council approves a PD-R zone change, they
