HILLSIDE DEVELOPMENT PERMIT:
     Consider approval of a hillside development permit to allow disturbance of
     areas in the 20-30%, 30-40% and 40% and above slope areas on

      approximately 16.47 acres generally located at the northwest corner of
      Blackridge Drive and 250 West Street to be known as Black Ridge Hotel.
      2021-HS-008

      Assistant Public Works Director Wes Jenkins presented the request for a hillside
      development permit to allow disturbance of areas in the 20-30%, 30-40% and 40%
      and above slope areas on approximately 16.47 acres generally located at the
      northwest corner of Blackridge Drive and 250 West Street to be known as Black
      Ridge Hotel. He reviewed the following from the staff report: Aerial map; and
      Original and proposed site maps. A slope analysis was done showing that there were
      portions exceeding the 20-30%, 30-40%, and exceeding the 40%; however, when
      the Hillside Review Board visited the site, they determined the site was already
      completely disturbed.

      Councilmember Larkin asked about AGEC report. She noted it says that the fill
      slopes should be graded by overbuilding.

      Mr. Jenkins replied he thinks that means the developer will need to provide
      additional fill. They will not go further into the slopes that have not been disturbed.
      He noted there is a large cut on an adjacent parcel to the west that even the hotel
      may not mitigate. He added there will be a cut at the south end of this property - the
      Hillside Review Board requested this cut be mitigated as much as possible. Another
      concern is with the rock line slope that the Board felt needed to be addressed
      aesthetically. He mentioned that the secant wall sits within the landslide area. The
      developer has done some additional research on developing and landslides and was
      required to obtain a third-party review of the landslide study. It was determined
      that a different factor of safety of 1.3 could be used if they meet certain parameters.
      Based on this study they can meet the factor of safety of 1.3; therefore, mitigation
      will not be required. The soil moisture will need to stay in a similar condition as it is
      currently. Landscaping is recommended to be desert landscaping with minimal
      watering. They will have a pool with a liner including a drain system. The Hillside
      Review Board put a condition on landscaping, that it have a monitor on meters
      showing the amount of water being used. It will have an alarm that will monitor
      water usage and notify the developer of a possible leak.

      Councilmember Larkin noted the AGEC report recommended zero water in the
      landscaping.

      Mr. Jenkins explained AGEC would like no water in the landscaping; however, doing
      that would not meet the ordinance. It is recommended that they have plants that
      require minimal water to get started and keeping them alive. AGEC agreed with this
      recommendation if the alarm is utilized for water usage. He noted that the original
      plans included lush landscaping.

      Councilmember Curtis inquired if there were concerns with water coming from other
      sources.

      Mr. Jenkins explained that one of the concerns is what happens upslope as they
      cannot control that. He then showed the future road alignment. The developer will
St. George City Council Meeting
November 18, 2021
Page Six

      need to collect water from the road and get it off the site, not only to protect the
      road, but to protect any development downstream of any runoff.

      Councilmember McArthur declared he has a conflict with this item because he was an
      agent involved in the sale of the property; however, he spoke with the City Attorney
      who indicated it is okay that he votes on the item. Although he has concerns about
      hillsides, because this property is already disturbed, he feels what is being proposing
      will benefit the property. He asked what will be disturbed that is not currently
      disturbed.

      Mr. Jenkins replied not much more will be disturbed as the area is already cut pretty
      badly.

      Councilmember McArthur commented this hotel with the mitigation will be costly.
      The developer will be concerned and do everything they can to mitigate what they
      can with how much they are spending.

      Councilmember Larkin agreed the proposal will be a good addition to the area. She
      asked about landslides and the reports from AGEC and the third-party review.

      Mr. Jenkins explained that staff met with AGEC who explained the parameters and
      stated why the 1.3 factor of safety is acceptable, after which staff felt comfortable
      with the request. He mentioned on the northeast corner, there was an asbestos pipe
      that was excavated. AGEC has tested 12-15 pits and found ten fragments of
      asbestos pipe. The environmental expert on the site stated the site is not on the
      state’s registry. They will be required to provide a letter from the state recognizing
      the property is not an asbestos cell. After being asked by Councilmember Lakin, Mr.
      Jenkins indicated the developer will be required to have construction observation. A
      final grading report will be required; this report will indicate compaction tests that
      were done and will also address any concerns.

      Councilmember Larkin asked if staff’s recommendation that the site is completely
      impermeable.

      Mr. Jenkins explained they will need to keep the moisture the same as it is right
      now. The site will need to be monitored for moisture content.

      Wayne Rogers with AGEC explained the impermeability factor is already there with
      the clay soils anyway. He noted the problem with clay soils is that if there is water
      ponding, it sucks the water down; that is what they want to avoid. Currently, the
      site is such that it does not drain very well. All of the precautionary measures
      suggested keeps the risk low that there be an increase in moisture content
      significant enough to cause the slope to weaken. They will work with the City on the
      drainage.

AMEND TITLE 10, CHAPTER 13A OF CITY CODE/ORDINANCE:
    Consider approval of an ordinance amending Title 10, Chapter 13A of City
    code as it pertains to certain public streets in the hillside overlay zone.

      Planner Dan Boles presented the request to amend Title 10, Chapter 13A of City
      code as it pertains to certain public streets in the hillside overlay zone. He provided
      a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Proposed language; and
      Planning Commission recommendation – they recommended #3 be stricken. Staff
      recommends #3 remains in the ordinance.

      After being asked by Mayor Pro Tem Hughes and Councilmembers Larkin and
      McArthur, Community Development Director John Willis explained that the Council
      works within a certain window and staff has to make sure the ordinance is in line
      with what the Council is able to do. This amendment would give the Council the
      ability to review certain public streets as part of a hillside permit.

      Councilmember McArthur commented he thinks the ordinance is being changed to be
      able to reroute Tech Ridge Drive.

      Mr. Willis noted that there are some applications and Tech Ridge could be one of
      them.

      Councilmember McArthur stated he would like to discuss the topic in detail before
      changing the ordinance.

      Mayor Pro Tem Hughes agreed. He asked if the ordinance passes, would the Council
      no longer see some requests.

      Community Development Director John Willis explained the Council would still review
      the requests as part of a hillside permit request.

      Councilmember McArthur commented that he feels like this amendment is being
      done in reverse. He is surprised it is coming before the Council without the details.

      Mr. Willis explained the language would allow the Council to look at and apply the
      proposed standards when considering a hillside permit. If the proposed language is
      not in the ordinance and a request violates the ordinance, an applicant could not
      come if for approval as it does not meet the current code.

      Councilmember Larkin commented if roads are currently failing, this is an
      opportunity for the Council to review these roads and find a solution.
