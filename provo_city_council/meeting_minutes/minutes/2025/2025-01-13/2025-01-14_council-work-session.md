Item 1: A Presentation Regarding the 2025 Utah State Legislative Session (25-005)

Presenters: Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, and Michael Sanders, Policy Analyst

Ms. Dayley and Mr. Sanders provided an overview of the upcoming 2025 Utah State Legislative
Session, which runs from January 21 to March 7, 2025. Key components of their presentation
included the following:

        Bill Tracker: A dedicated tool was introduced for the Municipal Council, containing bill
         numbers, Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) positions, and options for council
         positions.
             o Weekly updates on the tracker will be provided every Monday.
             o The tracker will identify fiscal impacts and proposed code changes relevant to the
                 city.
        Council Action:
             o Council members were encouraged to email staff if they wished to take action on
                 specific bills.
             o Staff would provide detailed background information and options for council
                 action on flagged bills.
             o Councilors may respond individually, as groups, or collectively as the full
                 council.

Additional resources, including ULCT and Wasatch Front Regional Council bill trackers, were
shared for broader legislative context.

Governor’s Budget Recommendations: These were also briefly highlighted during the
presentation, with a focus on city-related impacts.

Mr. Sanders outlined the steps for council members to engage in legislative advocacy:

    1.   Email council staff and fellow councilors about bills of interest.
    2.   Staff will add these bills to the tracker and provide relevant background information.
    3.   Councilors can reply with specific actions they wish to take.
    4.   Staff will provide support and ensure updates are shared with the council.

Councilors discussed the proposed process, including the types of bills to prioritize for action.
Ms. Dayley emphasized that their focus would be on housing and zoning priorities, as well as
operational and budgetary impacts on the city.

Item 2: A Resolution Regarding Funding for the Airport Apron Expansion
Project (25-003)

Presenter: Jimmy McKnight, Public Works Business Manager

Mr. McKnight provided an overview of a proposed resolution to secure a loan from the Utah
Department of Transportation (UDOT) to address a cash flow issue related to the airport apron
expansion project. The primary points of his presentation included:

       Loan Details:
           o A $14.5 million loan with a 3.08% interest rate.
           o The loan serves as a bridge until $16.9 million in funding from the Mountainland
               Association of Governments (MAG) becomes available in 2028.
           o Repayment of the loan is planned upon receipt of MAG funds.
       Project Scope and Funding:
           o The project supports the full buildout of a 10-gate terminal expansion.
           o Total funding includes $16.9 million from MAG and $11.9 million from the
               Federal Aviation Administration (FAA).
       Approval Timeline:
           o The Transportation Commission is scheduled to formally adopt the loan terms in
               February.
           o A council resolution supporting the loan is required to proceed.

Discussion:

Councilors discussed the scope and future implications of the project. Questions were raised
regarding the specifics of the terminal expansion and potential needs for further airport facility
upgrades. Mr. McKnight assured the council that groundwork and site preparation for the
expansion area had already been completed, reducing the likelihood of future disruptions during
construction.

Additional concerns focused on the project’s timeline and how the funding aligns with terminal
upgrades. Mr. McKnight emphasized that the apron’s completion is timed to integrate seamlessly
with the terminal expansion, ensuring operational efficiency upon project finalization.

Item 3: An Ordinance Regarding a Text Amendment for the Prohibition of Sham
Property Transactions (PLOTA20240309)

Presenter: Jessica Dahneke, Planner

Ms. Dahneke introduced a proposed text amendment designed to clarify the prohibition of sham
property transactions in connection with owner occupancy requirements. Key components of the
amendment include:

       Clear Guidelines: The amendment establishes explicit and enforceable guidelines,
        especially for properties held by trusts or LLCs.


Provo City Municipal Council Work Meeting – January 14, 2025                            Page 3 of 10
       Ownership Changes:
          o Changes to the ownership structure of a trust or LLC within the first year of
             obtaining a rental dwelling license, or repeated changes during the rental period,
             will be considered sham transactions.

Discussion:

Councilors deliberated potential additions to the amendment, including:

       Rebuttable Presumption for Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs): The possibility of
        introducing a presumption against eligibility for ADUs for properties owned by LLCs or
        trusts unless there is definitive proof of owner occupancy.
       Disclosure Requirements: The council highlighted the need for disclosures on how
        ownership changes might impact ADU status.
       Owner Occupancy Requirements on Sales: Suggestions were made to extend owner
        occupancy requirements to subsequent property sales to ensure long-term compliance.

Expert Commentary:

Brian Jones, City Attorney, explained the council's ability to establish overarching guidelines
regarding owner occupancy requirements in development agreements. Such guidelines would
help ensure uniformity and clarity in council policy.

       Supporting Views:
           o Councilor Katrice MacKay expressed satisfaction with the amendment’s aim to
              prevent misuse of rental dwelling licenses.
           o Councilor Rachel Whipple highlighted the importance of fair treatment for
              individuals holding properties within trusts or LLCs and emphasized the necessity
              of owner education on the new requirements.

The council collectively acknowledged the importance of these measures in upholding housing
policy integrity and preventing exploitation through sham property transactions.

Item 5: An Ordinance Amending Provo City Code to Clarify and Update
Standards for Flag Lots (PLOTA20240332)

Presenter: Dustin Wright, Planner

Mr. Wright introduced proposed amendments to Provo City Code aimed at updating and
clarifying standards for flag lots. The key objectives of the amendments include enhancing land
use efficiency, increasing the city's housing stock, and ensuring safety measures are in place.
Major provisions of the amendment are as follows:

       Width Requirements:
           o The ordinance reduces the required width for the "pole" portion of flag lots,
              offering greater flexibility for property development and optimizing land use.
           o For adjacent flag lots, the combined required width is reduced to 25 feet, down
              from the previously mandated 40 feet. This change promotes more efficient use of
              available property.
       Land Use and Housing:
           o The amendment is part of a broader initiative to increase housing availability by
              making better use of existing land.
       Driveway and Fire Safety:
           o Provisions include reducing driveway widths to minimize conflicts between
              pedestrians and vehicles.
           o Enhanced fire safety measures require homes situated more than 200 feet from the
              street to be equipped with fire sprinklers. These requirements were developed in
              consultation with the fire marshal to ensure compliance with safety standards.

Councilors engaged with Mr. Wright to seek clarifications on the proposed fire safety

Item 9: An Ordinance Regarding Rooftop Solar Rates (25-004)

Presenters: Scott Bunker, Energy Director, and Charlie Little, Management Analyst

Mr. Bunker and Mr. Little presented a detailed analysis and recommendations for amending
Provo’s rooftop solar rate policy. Their presentation aimed to address concerns about the current
subsidy structure and its impact on Provo’s power customers.



Current Compensation Rate:

       The current rooftop solar compensation rate is 6.7 cents per kilowatt hour.


Provo City Municipal Council Work Meeting – January 14, 2025                           Page 8 of 10
       This rate exceeds Provo Power’s cost for purchasing solar power at wholesale market
        rates, which prompted a review of the policy.

Proposed Methodological Approaches:

    1. Wholesale Energy Credit: Aligns the credit with the market rate for bulk-purchased
       solar energy, estimated at approximately 4 cents per kilowatt hour.
    2. Energy Plus Capacity Credit: Accounts for energy savings and avoided capacity costs,
       valuing rooftop solar at approximately 4.5 cents per kilowatt hour.
    3. Power Plant Avoided Cost Credit: Considers savings from avoiding additional fossil-
       fuel power plants, estimating the credit at around 4.8 cents per kilowatt hour.

Financial Implications:

       Maintaining the current rate results in $135,000 annually in lost general fund revenue.
       Adjusting the rates could save up to $100,000 annually.

Council Discussion:

       Environmental Benefits: Councilor George Handley supported higher reimbursement
        rates to account for rooftop solar’s environmental benefits, emphasizing the importance
        of renewable energy incentives.
       Ratepayer Fairness: Other councilors debated the balance between sustainability goals
        and the need to ensure fairness across all ratepayers.
       Grandfathering Terms: There was extensive discussion about protecting existing
        rooftop solar customers to preserve their return on investment amidst potential rate
        changes.

Policy Recommendations:

       Scheduled rate reviews and adjustments to reflect market dynamics.
       Transparency and consistency in rate methodologies to maintain public trust and
        encourage investment in solar energy.

Procedural Motion:

       Councilor Handley made a motion to postpone the night meeting vote and return to a
        future work meeting to discuss rooftop solar rates and other sustainability incentive
        proposals. Councilor Christensen seconded.
       The motion was approved with a vote of 5-2, with Councilors Bogdin and MacKay
        opposed.

The council acknowledged the complexity of balancing municipal fiscal responsibilities,
ratepayer equity, and environmental stewardship. Discussions highlighted the need for thoughtful
policy evolution, emphasizing transparency, community engagement, and sustainability-driven
incentives.
