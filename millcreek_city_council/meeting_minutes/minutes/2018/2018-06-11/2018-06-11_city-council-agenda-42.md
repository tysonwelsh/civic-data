2. Planning Matters
2.1 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-35, Amendment to Title 19
Establishing Updated Standards for Accessory Structures in the R-1, R-2, and R-4
Zones
Francis Lilly said the Planning Commission offered a favorable recommendation to
approve the amendments on accessory structures. The purpose of the code changes is to
streamline the approval process as far as making code easier to read and making
accessory structures a permitted use instead of a conditional use. There is a change in the
definition and that is that they are not intended for human occupancy. Lilly said there is a
consensus in the region that a maximum of 20 ft. is an appropriate height for an accessory
structure. He proposed an exception to the Council that if someone’s home is adjacent to
a commercial or multi-family zone, that the accessory dwelling could go up to 24 ft. in

height.

Lilly showed a rural to urban phasing diagram with transect zones. He said usually the
requirement is on the more intense use to step down in the transect zones, but this
ordinance gives residents the ability to step up. He said the Planning Commission
recommended keeping the height at 20 ft. Lilly showed a picture as example of a
residence that backs commercial in the Canyon Rim area. If residents are allowed to build
an accessory structure, they would be able to cut off views of adjacent commercial
property. As a standard, the staff is proposing that accessory structures can be 10% higher
than the main structure up to 20 ft. and in some cases a 24 ft. high structure.

Mayor Silvestrini questioned the wording in the design standards in 19-14-070 subsection
F. and said it was not clear. He read the subsection in question, “Accessory buildings may
have a pitched roof, or shall incorporate the same roof type as the main structure.” Lilly
said residents can either have a pitched roof or they must match the roof of the main
structure. The Mayor asked what the Planning Commission’s reasoning was on that. Lilly
said it was intended to make sure the garage is compatible with the home.

Lilly said all the community councils recommended allowance for 24 ft. in height except
Mt. Olympus who said 20 ft. Council Member Jackson asked about schools and church
buildings as institutional uses. Lilly said the intention of institutional uses was for water
tanks, schools, and churches. Council Member Jackson recommended the language say
institutional structure instead of institutional use and the Council agreed.

Mayor Silvestrini invited the public to address the Council.

Paul Jeppson, 2986 E. 3215 S., said the picture Francis Lilly showed is of his backyard.
He said it would be nice to view the mountain without a delivery truck next to it. He said
the houses on the street with garages have that desirable blockage from light and sound
from the commercial property.

Sara Shaw, Loran Heights Dr., said she wished her neighbor had a flat-roofed garage
instead of pitched. She suggested the height limit be 20 ft. with a flat roof and 24 ft. with
a peaked roof. Lilly said accessory structures must comply with the residential
compatibility overlay (RCOZ) standards.

Council Member Jackson said this 24 ft. height provision could benefit a lot of areas in
her district to provide for additional privacy. Council Member Uipi agreed and said she
thought of the recent discussion with a project backing Loran Heights Dr. and how it may
be nice for the residents to have an option.

Mayor Silvestrini said he does not think that the 20 ft. height is solving the problem of
people occupying the accessory structures and that the extra height is warranted as a
buffer. He said it also frees residents and the Planning Commission from the conditional
use application process.

John Brems said the language is about the roofs in subsection F. is clunky. Council
Member Jackson said the work “incorporate” is awkward. There was a discussion about
how to make the provision read better. Council Member Catten suggested the section say,
“’,.have the same roof type...” Brems said to delete the last sentence in the section and
replace it with something similar to, “Accessory building must have a pitched roof unless
the main structure has a flat roof in which case it may have a flat roof.”

A member of the audience said that some houses have both a pitched and flat roof as part
of the design. Lilly said if a house has a pitched roof, then the accessory structure could
have either roof type. Council Member Jackson said it would have to still fit within the
RCOZ envelope. She explained that RCOZ constructs a tent over the property and the
accessory structure must fit within certain parameters of it, so if the flat roof were to go
outside of the envelope, it would not be allowed.

Council Member Jackson moved to approve Ordinance 18-35 with the modifications of
eliminating the wording of option a; redefining the rear space as institutional structure,
commercial, or multi-family; changing the language in section F as defined by John Brems;
and allowing the 24 ft. height exceptions in a transitional zone. Council Member Uipi
seconded. Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.

nonconforming in the zone by having less than 10,000 square feet. May said there are
several flag lots along Evergreen Ave.
