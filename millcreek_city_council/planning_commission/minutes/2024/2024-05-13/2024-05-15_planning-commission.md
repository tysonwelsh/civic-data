   1.2 Consideration                   of ZT-24-001,            Updates        to the Accessory              Dwelling         Unit      (ADU)           Code
       Sean Murray           said the ADU            code was adopted               in 2021, with         minor      changes made since then.
       Millcreek      staff was proposing                  changes     to how        detached       ADU       sizes are regulated,               which        lots
       can have internal             and attached          ADUs,       and consideration            of allowing            for detached          and
       attached     ADUs           on flag lots. He explained                the existing       detached         ADU        size, which          cannot
       exceed      50% of the square footage                    of the habitable          area of the building              footprint       of the
    primary         residence         and not exceed            850 square feet. The proposed                     code is that an ADU                    caru"iot
    exceed the gross square footage                          of the existing         main building           footprint        and can be up to a
    maximum            of 1,000         square feet, whichever               is less. He said this change                   comes from            concerns
    from       speaking        with     residents.     The current           detached      ADU        ordinance          severely       limits     the
    ability       for some residents              to build      a detached       ADU       in a size that makes               sense. Many              of
    Millcreek         lots are large with             smaller        homes     on them. The current                ordinance         punishes
    homeowners               with     more modest           sized homes         when      they go to build           a detached          ADU.           The
    current        code reads that a detached                   ADU      can only        cover up to 25% of the rear yard area. The
   proposed          code reads that a detached                    ADU       can cover up to 50% of the rear yard area.
    Residents         will    still have to meet setback                 regulations,        ADU       max size regulation,                 and total lot
    coverage         rules. The current              code reads that no property                  that is less than 6,000 square feet can
   have an internal                 or attached      ADU.       The proposed            code removes            this minimum             lot size for
   internal        and attached             ADUs.     Detached         ADUs         still require     a minimum             8,000 square foot lot
   size. This would                 increase     the ability       for residents        to be able to do an internal                  ADU.         Other
   changes          included         changing       occupancy          requirements         from      2 adults       and any number               of
   children         in attached         and detached           ADUs      to the Millcreek            standard        that no more than 4
   unrelated         adults may occupy                one dwelling           unit    at a time.     Parking       requirements            have been set
   to one parking             stall per ADU           and moved          to the parking           chapter.       The proposed            code removed
   language          outlining         guesthouses         found      in R-1-21        zones.


   Murray          said the General             Plan considerations             included        Goal N-2,         and strategies          1.1, 1.5, and
   1.6, and Infill            Goal GP-9,          strategy      9.5. The proposed            ordinance          helps reduce          barriers          to
   building         new ADUs            within      Millcreek        which      allow     residents       access to additional              rental
   income,         provide         housing       for family      members,           and increase       property         values.      Streamlining
   ADU         processes        and standards          will     help citizens         conduct       small      scale infill     in the city.

    Murray         reviesved        the staff findings          and conclusions              from     the staff report.
       Findings:
        1. The current             size restrictions          for detached           ADUs     based on primary                    building      footprint
        punishes          homeowners           with     smaller      homes       when they wish                to build        a detached        ADU.
        2. Rear yard lot coverage                   requirements            of 25% max for detached                       ADUs        do not affect            all
        properties         proportionally           and can severely             limit    the feasibility             of a resident          being     able to
        build       an ADU         on their property.
        3. The max size requirements                         for detached        ADUs        do not always                allow      for citizens       to build
        detached          ADUs       that are financially            feasible,       or comfortable                for multiple        people        to live in.
        4. Internal         and attached         ADU         restrictions      on lot sizes limits                 property       owners      with     smaller
        lots firom accessing              the benefits         ADUs         can bring       to their property.


       Conclusions:
            1. Removing            the 50% rule for detached                  ADU        sizing     will    lift    the limitation           on residents
            with    smaller       homes    from being           required       to build      ADUs          that are too small to be feasible.
            2. Increasing          the rear yard coverage              for detached           ADUs         to 50% from              25% will         allow
            residents      that have irregular           lot configurations,              shapes, or circumstances                       to build      a
            detached       ADU       that works        for their property.
            3. Increasing          the potential       max size of an ADU                  will     make      detached          ADUs         more
            financially       feasible     and livable          for people       residing         within     them.
            4. Removing            the minimum          lot size requirements                for internal            and attached         ADUs         will        give
            residents      with     smaller     lots the ability         to access the benefits                    ADUs       can bring.
