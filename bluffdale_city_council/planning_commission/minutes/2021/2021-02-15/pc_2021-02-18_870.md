Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman, presented the staff report and noted that the Planning
Commission previously considered and approved the plat amendment application for the subject
property, by which the total acreage was adjusted from three Jots to two. The western lot, Lot
403, wi ll be larger and the lot to the west, Lot 404, will be a littl e smaller. The proposed
construction is for Lot 403. The frontage for the subject prope1ty w ill be West Honda Park
Drive. The prope1ty will be accessed from Concord Park Drive and Heritage Crest Way. The
subject property is part of the Heritage Crest Industrial Park.

Ms. Oakman reviewed the surrounding property and stated that Lot 403 wi ll include two
buildings, designated as Buildings A and B. There wi ll be 14 units on the condo plat. Thirteen
units will be in Building B. Building A w ill have one unit. The applicants were allowed a five-
foot setback and they were determined to meet the req uirements for utiliti es and fire protection.

Ms. Oakman next reviewed the Landscaping Plan, which includes the Rocky Mountain Power
access easement and is impacted by the Rocky Mountain Power Corridor. The requirements for
providing a residential area buffer were met with a six-foot fence and a landscape buffer. T he
Lighting Plan was also determined to be in compl iance with the lighting standards set forth in the
City Code.

In response to Commi ssioner Flynn' s question regarding the ownership of Lot 404, Ms. Oakman
stated that it is the same prope1ty owner and will be part of the Heritage Crest Industrial Park
when it is deve loped. The Rocky Mountain Power Corridor restricts the allowable height of
trees.

Given the limitations imposed by the Rocky Mountain Power Corridor, C hai r Cragun asked if
the residential buffer fence cou ld be eight feet rather than six, as proposed. She asked if the
neighboring residents had been consulted. Commissioner Flynn stated that he has walked the
trail and concurred that an eight-foot fence might be better.             City Planner/Economic
Development Director, Grant Crowell, stated that the Plann ing Commission could li st that as a
finding in the motion. It was noted that the industrial park was built 20 years before anything
else. C hair Cragun commented that the parking req uirements have been greatly exceeded on the
proposed site plan.

One of the applicants, Matt Kelly, repo1ted that the power corridor has made it easier to provide
additional ample parking. As a result, they have attracted amazing buyers. They are not
trad itional industrial users and will be less no isy tenants.

Debbie Cragun moved to approv e the Marble Arch Site Plan, Application 2020-56, subject
