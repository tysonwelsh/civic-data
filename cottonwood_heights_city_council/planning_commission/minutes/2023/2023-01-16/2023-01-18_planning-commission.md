35   The one Business Meeting agenda item was reviewed and discussed. It involved a public hearing
36   and possible action from AJ Rock, LCC for a proposed amendment to the PDD-2 Ordinance.
37   Community and Economic Development Director Michael Johnson described the proposed text
38   amendment and stated that it is a legislative item involving a change to the adopted City ordinance.
39   The ordinance is specific to the 20-acre development site located at the northern end of the gravel
40   pit. The applicant presented the request one to two years ago and at that time went through the
41   development process for the site. It was ultimately approved. The applicants were present tonight
42   with an updated application.
43
44   Mr. Johnson reported that the City has a Planned Development District (“PDD”) Ordinance that
45   allows for the creation of an ordinance governing specific sites in the City. It is not eligible to be
46   used everywhere in the City. One of the areas where it applies is the gravel pit. Another is within



     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 01/18/2022                                         1
 1   major redevelopment areas along Fort Union Boulevard. A property owner/developer can use the
 2   PDD tool to create a specific development plan and ordinance that applies only to one property.
 3   In this case, AJ Rock/Rockworth Development purchased the northern 20 acres of the large gravel
 4   pit property. In the total scope of the operation, it is small and consists of the northern side. Some
 5   of the tradeoffs of using the PDD are that developers are allowed more flexibility in terms of
 6   densities, uses, building height, and massing. They create an ordinance, new zone, and
 7   development plan at the same time, which is very labor-intensive up front. Typically, a city council
 8   will approve a rezone without seeing the specific development plan, which comes in later.
 9
10   There are currently two PDD areas in the City. One was identified as the Walsh property, which
11   is behind 1300 East in the northwest corner of the City. The location was described. Mr. Johnson
12   reported that new ordinances are technically created every time a PDD project is approved. The
13   proposed amendment is only for the PDD-2 Ordinance. Mr. Johnson described what was approved
14   for Rockworth Development, which is a mixed-use development site. The location was identified
15   on a site map displayed and included several development types. One was a 325-unit apartment
16   building with 15% of the units being deed-restricted affordable units. One requirement of the PDD
17   tool is that a percentage of the housing be affordable. Affordability is determined using the Area
18   Median Income (“AMI”) and is defined as 30% of total household costs going toward housing.
19   An affordable housing tool would be affordable to a family or individual making 80% of the AMI.
20   The AMI will vary by city.
21
22   Mr. Johnson noted that the apartment building will be the first phase of the project. A
23   condominium building was also proposed for which the Planning Commission recommended
24   approval. It is a 10-story building with 90 to 99 units. The City Council was concerned with the
25   proposed units so the developer decided to come back later and address the density and massing.
26   The condominium location was approved and the number of units and height will be required to
27   return for a full PDD amendment. A site rendering was presented.
28
29   Throughout the PDD-2 approval process, the applicant conducted numerous site hazard analyses
30   to investigate the potential impact of any future mitigation necessary for various natural hazards
31   present on the site including slope stability, liquefaction, debris flow, rockfall, fault lines, hillside
32   remediation, and mitigation measures for development around the existing water aqueduct. While
33   the site was initially scoped and studied in detail, the approval included a condition that additional
34   scoping would be required as each phase of the project prepares for full engineering design and
35   development. Part of this scoping includes additional fault trenching of each building footprint,
36   which is a requirement that is typical of development in sensitive land areas throughout the City.
37   Since approval was granted, the applicant has been working on additional site engineering
38   including supplemental geotechnical and geologic hazard studies, and preparing full site
39   engineering and construction plans. He noted that the applicant has completed additional trenching
40   for each future building on the site. One fault line near the proposed apartment building was found
41   to have a different orientation where the setback will reduce the size of the apartment building
42   slightly.
43
44   Mr. Johnson reported that a large courtyard and public plaza are proposed, both of which are
45   allowed by the PDD-2 Ordinance. Massing diagrams were displayed. Changes included:

13   Mr. Johnson noted that there is an approved provision in the PDD-2 Ordinance that states that
14   material changes to the Ordinance or Development Plan arising from a submittal and/or the
15   discovery of unanticipated geologic hazards shall require an amendment to the Ordinance
16   following review, recommendation, and/or approval by the approving City bodies and must go
17   through the full PDD process. The applicant’s proposal better defines the term “material changes”,
18   which would allow them to make minor modifications that are a decrease and less intense than
19   what was approved as part of the Staff review. The measurement process was discussed.
20
21   Commissioner Chappell stated that if the language changes slightly, the square footage will be
22   impacted and result in a significant change to the project. From a structural building standpoint,
23   she felt that 10% has a history of creating a cumulative change. She questioned whether there is a
24   limit. Mr. Johnson reported that the 10% would be based on the development as currently. If there
25   is a scenario where they continue to make modifications, staff would identify the issue and state
26   that it is beyond what is considered a material change. It would then be necessary to go through
27   the full PDD amendment process.
28
29   Commissioner Mills recommended that language be added to specify that the change is to a
30   particular structure. Senior City Planner, Samantha DeSeelhorst was supportive of the small
31   percentage and a language clarification. She confirmed that the text amendment is just for this
32   project. She felt that the existing ordinances may serve as a template for other PDDs in the future.
33
34   Mr. Johnson reviewed the following Staff analysis and findings:
35
36          •   The proposed language requires any expansion of use, massing, etc. to follow the
37              prescribed public process. Additionally, the proposed language would also require any
38              substantial reduction or loss of building to adhere to that same public amendment
39              process. It would allow for minor decreases in building intensity, unit counts, heights,
40              etc. (no more than 10% of the original approved Development Plan) to be processed
41              administratively.
42
43          •   The 10% reduction does not apply to the public trail and courtyard elements of the site,
44              so modification of those amenities would not be allowed outside of a full PDD
45              amendment process.
46

 1          •   The proposed text amendment would not allow for any increase in building height,
 2              massing, and density without a full PDD amendment. The proposed amendment is
 3              narrowly scoped to allow for slight building modifications typical of a large, multi-
 4              phase development of this type, without jeopardizing or modifying the original intent
 5              of the approved plan.
 6
 7   The proposed language modifications were discussed. Commissioner Ebbeler understood that one
 8   of the City’s primary concerns was with the trigger for affordable housing. He suggested that
 9   clarifying language be added so that this trigger would not have an impact on the affordable
10   housing counts. Mr. Johnson stated it creates an impact as the affordable housing counts represent
11   a percentage of the total units. If the number of units is decreased, they would lose some of the
12   affordable housing. Staff was comfortable with the language. Language could be added to specify
13   that the 10% is measured from the originally codified Development Plan.
14

17   Ms. DeSeelhorst stated that notices are sent to the Holladay City Recorder who passes them on to
18   City leadership and the affected entities.

16
17          1.      The 10% reduction would be measured from the originally codified development
18                  plan.

20          2.      The 10% reduction is limited to a specific building.
21
22          3.      The addition of a colon so all the items of this 10% reduction are included
23                  individually.
24
25          4.      Represents a comprehensive 10% reduction and is not able to be continually
26                  reduced.
27
28   The motion was seconded by Commissioner Shelton.
29
30   Mr. Henriod stated that they were agreeable to the recommended additions and was confident that
31   they will be able to work with Staff. He asked for clarification of the term “massing”. He noted
32   that the additional required trenching has been completed with exception of the hotel pad and the
33   future condominium pad due to the existing amount of fill. Chair Allen believed the term
34   “massing” involves volume and was in reference to the footprint. He supported eliminating the
35   term if it is problematic.
36
37   Commissioner Mills moved to AMEND the motion by eliminating the terms “building” and
38   “massing”. The amended motion was seconded by Commissioner Ebbeler.
39
40   Commissioner Mills clarified that the amendment means that the measures in place are adequate to
41   determine that this is not considered a material change and building massing, as a result of the
42   previous list, would not make it material.
43
44   Vote on amended motion: Commissioner Smith-Aye, Commissioner Mills-Aye, Commissioner
45   Anderson-Aye, Commissioner Shelton-Aye, Commissioner Ebbeler-Aye, Commissioner
46   Chappell-Aye, Chair Allen-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
