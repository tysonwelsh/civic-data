GENERAL SESSION

Chair Snow called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:02 p.m. He

Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

1.     Chad Jones, representing Marcus Lonardo, requests Rezone approval for a
       property located at 160 East 7200 South from Single Family Residential/Duplex
       Overlay/7200 South Overlay (SF-1/DO/7200 S) to Mixed Use/7200 South Overlay
       (MU/7200 S) (Adam Olsen)

Mr. Olsen stated that the applicant is requesting rezone approval for a property located at 160
East 7200 South from Single Family Residential/Duplex Overlay/7200 South Overlay to Mixed
Use/7200 South Overlay to allow for the construction of a 17- unit, 3 story townhome project
with a flex space on the ground level. Mr. Olsen stated that what is being brought before the
Commission tonight is only a rezone request and added that the proposed townhome project
presented would be the intended plan of the development to take place at this location if the
rezone were to be approved. If a positive recommendation is made the next step would be a
request for approval from City Council. If approved, the applicant would then need to request
approval for the overall concept of the development.

APPLICANT:

Mr. Jones stated that he feels that the proposed project would provide a buffer between the
single-family neighborhood and the 7200 corridor. He added that the area has changed quite a bit
with new development and that the location provides easy walking access to many restaurants
and stores which are located nearby. Mr. Jones stated that he wants to create a community that
provides a full work and live space. He stated that he is open to the possibility of having the

PUBLIC HEARING:

Chair Snow opened the public hearing.

Sarah Sherwood, residing at 105 E Nicoletti Drive stated that she has concerns with the proposed
development which would affect the historic Ramanee neighborhood. She does not believe that
the proposed project enhances the area and will have a lasting negative impact on the area for
years, citing increased traffic, increased crime and the impact due to lack of available parking on
both Nicoletti and Ramanee Drive She added that she has collected over 100 signatures in
opposition to the project and provided the petition to the Commission and Staff.

With regards to increased traffic, Ms. Sherwood reference a study conducted by Indiana University
Public Policy Institute called Land Use and Violent Crime, which she stated found a correlation
with high density residential units and serious violent crimes. She stated that this opportunity area
is already a major thoroughfare that attracts crime, and that this project would only increase the
crimes. She stated that she personally analyzed police data from recent years and found that
multiple crimes have increased in the area. With regards to increased traffic, she stated that she
was able to obtain information from an organization called “neighborhood scout” that stated that
most people drive alone and have 3 vehicles. She stated that this is in opposition to the belief that
this would provide enhanced walkability, and this would be not a necessity to the residents that
would occupy the development.

Ms. Sherwood stated that she also has concerns with how the information was presented by staff
to the Commission and the public. She said that what was published in the packet stated that no
objections had been received prior to this scheduled meeting. In contradiction to this, Ms.
Sherwood provided the Commission with an email that was sent to staff on December 8th and an

8th meeting which although was postponed, only showed that the requested rezone satisfied the
requirement of goal # 3. The agenda for the February 2nd meeting now states that the requested
rezone meets an additional goal that was not previously stated. She feels that the information that

In addition to concerns regarding the developments negative impact on the neighborhood, Ms.

Ms. Sherwood requested that at the very minimum this item be tabled until a traffic study is

DISCUSSION:

Garret Wilcox, Deputy City Attorney, stated that this item is a legislative decision. He added that
city code does limit the discretion to approve the rezone application only if one of the three criteria
are found to fit the rezone. He added that if any one of the 3 criteria is found then the commission
has the ability to approve the application. If they find that it does not meet the any of the criteria
listed, then the commission must deny the application.

Commissioner Smallwood stated that he disagrees with the statement made that renters are
unlikely to have a business at their home. He added that the city cannot dictate if a property is
rented or sold as this would violate fair housing laws and private property rights. Lastly, he stated
that he is disappointed that the public comments that were emailed prior to the meeting were not
provided to the Commission.

Commissioner Tippetts stated that eventually something will develop on this property, and it may
be the case that there is never agreement amongst the neighbors on what should be built there. She
added that what is being considered tonight is just the rezone. The Commission is not considering
the 16 townhomes, which would come before the Commissioner in another public hearing. She
agreed that the Commission should have been provided the emailed public comment.

Commissioner Anderson stated that she is concerned that residents do not feel like this request has
been transparent.

Mr. Olsen stated that the Commissioners have the ability to table the item so that they can
reconsider the item at a time when they have been provided a more comprehensive packet. He
added that there is no wrong decision in this matter.

3.     Zoning Ordinance Text Amendment to Chapters 17-7-1.4.A.2(a), 17-7-2.4.A.2(a),
       17-7-3.4.A.2(a), and 17-7-4.4.A.2(a) to address projections into side yard setbacks.

The city proposes an amendment to Chapters 17-7-1.4.A.2(a), 17-7-2.4.A.2(a), 17-7-3.4.A.2(a),
and 17-7-4.4.A.2(a) of the Municipal Code. The amendment would allow projections of
skylights, sills, cornices, chimneys, flues, eaves, and ornamental features to encroach/project
three feet (3’) into a side yard setback, rather than two and a half feet (2.5’) as currently allowed.
Stairs and balconies may already encroach 3’. Staff feels that by allowing the items above a 3’
encroachment, consistency will be achieved, as opposed to some items being allowed a 3’
projection while others only a 2.5’ projection.
