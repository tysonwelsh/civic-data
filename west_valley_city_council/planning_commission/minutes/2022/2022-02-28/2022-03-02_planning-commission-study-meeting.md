ZONE CHANGE APPLICATIONS
Z-1-2022   Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting a zone change from R-1-8 (Single
           Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet) to A (Agriculture,
           minimum lot size ½ acre). The property is located at 3846 S 2200 W on 1.31
           acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov.)
           (Continued from the February 23, 2022, public hearing.)

Discussion:      Steve Pastorik explained that the property owner is concerned that by signing the
                 development agreement, he would need to follow the housing standards as
                 outlined, and the City would still have the ability to change the zone in the future.
                 Steve stated that he spoke with Brandon Hill, Assistant City Attorney to come up
                 with language for the agreement that would allow the property owner to develop
                 his property using the standards for the R-1-8 zone along with the housing
                 standards as outlined in the development agreement. He noted that the idea is to
                 have this proposal tabled until the property owner signs the development
                 agreement and then it can be removed from the agenda.

Z-5-2022         Petition by NORTHROP GRUMMAN INNOVATIONS SYSTEMS, INC.
                 requesting a change to the zoning map by reducing the number and changing the
                 location of Overpressure Zones. The property is located in the southwest portion
                 of West Valley City. (For questions, please call 801-963-3282 or send an email
                 to planningandzoning@wvc-ut.gov.)

Discussion:      Steve Pastorik explained that approximately one thousand eight hundred notices
                 were mailed out regarding this application. He made a video explanation for the
                 residents to view, answering questions about the Overpressure Overlay Zone and
                 the proposed changes hoping to reduce the number of phone calls about Z-5-2022.
                 Steve then played the video for the Commission.

                 Commissioner Woodruff asked if the City had any obligation to verify the
                 applicant’s information. Steve stated that he could ask the applicant for
                 something depicting the area before and after the facilities had been removed.
                 Brandon Hill explained that the purpose of the Overlay Zone is to ensure that
                 areas within a certain distance of hazardous activities on the ATK/Northrup
                 property are subject to higher building standards to prevent damage from
                 overpressure events. He said that the Overpressure Zone is a protection for the
                 applicant, and if removed, it causes them more liability than it would the City.

                 Chair Meaders asked if this application is approved would the City be liable for
                 any damage from Northrop Grumman in the areas no longer within the
                 Overpressure circles. Brandon said the subject location had housed explosives
                 before West Valley City’s incorporation. He explained that the City is not
                 required to make someone build a structure that protects them from all potential
                 damage.

                 Steve stated that he had asked the applicant what would become of the part of the
                 property that they are no longer utilizing. He then read a letter received by
                 Northrop Grumman and emphasized the statement that there are no future plans to
                 house any rocket power explosives in the east corner of the facility.

                 Commissioner Lovato asked if the residents living close to the subject property
                 should have any reason for concern. Steve explained that explosives were
                 handled at the subject location starting in the 1900s, and many of the homes
                 surrounding the subject property were built before West Valley City’s
                 incorporation. He said that the applicant has fewer explosive materials in their
                 facility, so there should not be an increased concern for the neighboring residents.

    There being no further business, the meeting adjourned at 3:56 p.m.
