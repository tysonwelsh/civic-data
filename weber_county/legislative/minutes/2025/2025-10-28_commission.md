G. ACTION ITEMS:
   1. APPROVAL TO ADOPT THE TENTATIVE OPERATING AND CAPITAL BUDGET FOR WEBER
      COUNTY FOR THE 2026 CALENDAR YEAR.
            Steffani Ebert (Clerk/Auditor Chief Deputy): As required by state statute, I am presenting the tentative
            operating and capital budget for 2026. A copy was provided to you this morning and it will be online until the
            November 25th 6pm public hearing; adoption of the final budget will be on December 16th. This year’s budget
            process began in August when preliminary budgets were sent to each department. After reviewing their
            preliminary budget, each department submitted their requested budgets. After budget hearings in September,
            the Auditor’s office met with the Commission to review and compile the information for this tentative budget.
            One of the main focal points of this year’s budget process was employee compensation. After RFPs were
            received, a contract was awarded last September to Baker Tilley to review the County’s job descriptions and
            wages against several peer agencies. The results showed many positions were very close to or within market,
            with the exception of many of our Sheriff department’s sworn staff. Baker Tilley presented several options
            for implementation; County leadership worked on selecting option that were both fiscally responsible and fair
            to the employees. The recommendations are being phased in and new pay scale minimums and maximums
            are being implemented. Sheriff sworn staff pay has already been raised to the new minimums. A proposed
            market adjustment of 2% minimum is included in the tentative budget in lieu of a cost of living increase. A
            revised pay scale is being implemented for our public safety staff. The tentative budget also includes a 1.5%
            pay-for-performance increase. Medical insurance will see a 6.9% increase and dental will see an increase of
            about 30%. However, due to the good fiscal management of our Commissioners, our Clerk/Auditor, our
            County departments, and our former comptroller Scott Parke, no planned tax increase to implement these
            market study adjustments. We will see a net decrease in employee count from our 2025 original budget to the
            2026 budget of about 16 FTEs. This includes an increase of a part-time victim witness coordinator and part-
            time legal assistant in the Attorney’s office, and a community outreach and volunteer coordinator at the Animal
            Shelter. The tentative budget also includes reduction of a captain position in the jail, some clerical staff in
            other County departments, two of our planner positions, the Planning department, and several employees in
            our Health Department who were funded through grants that have expired. As for capital improvements, we
         anticipate some improvements in our Weber Center parking as well as a building remodel; there is still some
         work on medical and mental health wing at the Sheriff’s office. We did complete Children’s Justice Center
         this year that was funded primarily through ARPA funds. There will be an open house of the remodeled OECC
         on November 4th; that was mainly funded through tourism funds as well as some County capital funds. There
         will be some extreme changes to our municipal services budget due to the incorporation of Ogden Valley City.
         There is some uncertainty how our budget will be affected in relation to sales tax and some of the services that
         will be provided.
         Commissioner Froerer: Steff, I want to say thanks for all of the work you have been doing to prepare this
         budget. This shows we do more with less through being conservative with taxpayer dollars. I hope the
         employees, especially public safety employees, will recognize what we were able toe accomplish for them in
         the way of pay increases based on the pay study. I want to thank Chair Bolos for her hard work on Public
         Safety because that has been a major effort. There are two ways to look at a budget; there is the revenue side
         and the expense side. I commend Chair Bolos for having us sweep the corners for expenses.

         Commissioner Harvey: Madam Chair, having sat thorough 31 budget sessions, you have done a wonderful
         job in leading this budget effort. Steffani, wow! Thank you for the extra effort and the incredible lift. I am
         so grateful you are on this Weber County team.
         Chair Bolos: I echo my gratitude to Steffani. We lost our comptroller right before budget season; had you not
         been such a great support to him, I don’t think you would have been able to do what you’ve done. I also want
         to thank Emily Wilde who has been a partner throughout the pay study. The public will always ask where we
         get the money without raising taxes. We have allocated some of our future capital budget money and the
         Sheriff’s department reduced their costs by $300k. We also had departments look at their training and travel
         budgets where reductions in some of the larger budgets were made. I wanted to say something about the
         remodel; it is not for a facelift but instead for the safety of our employees.

         Commissioner Froerer moved to adopt the tentative operating and capital budget for Weber County for the 2026
