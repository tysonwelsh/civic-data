DISCUSSION REGARDING THE GENERAL PLAN AND DOWNTOWN MASTER PLAN:
     Community Development Director John Willis introduced Whitney Ward with VCBO
     Architecture. Ms. Ward provided a PowerPoint presentation including the following
     topics: Agenda; and Lifestyle.

      A discussion took place regarding the idea of creating neighborhood planning areas
      that recognize the distinct nature of the area similar to what Salt Lake has done with
      Sugarhouse and other neighborhoods.

      Planning Commission Member Emily Andrus commented that she likes this idea as it
      helps maintain the small-town feel. From a traffic standpoint, it would be nice to add
      some commercial in each of these areas so that residents do not have to travel too
      far. Ms. Ward noted this concept can help with water, transit, transportation
      planning, and connecting areas of the city as well as walkability and access to trails.

      Mr. Willis added that each neighborhood determines what it wants to be and focus
      on, essentially creating a master plan.

      Ms. Ward stated they envision to include public outreach to define the areas moving
      forward. They have identified opportunities for housing density, creating more
      equity in each area, and giving the same opportunity to all areas. Ms. Ward
      explained they will try to identify priorities in the general plan for the investments
      based on gaps in the system and potential to influence the vision. Further, she
      explained how this will affect the growth of the community and market driven items.

      Ms. Ward continued with her PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
      Q19 – As we consider how to grow responsibly, how important are each of the
      following (question and response from survey); Responsible growth: Water
      conservation, and Infrastructure needs; and Responsible growth: Future policies, and
      Education of citizens.

      A brief discussion took place regarding water conservation efforts, available rebates,
      and education of citizens.

      Ms. Ward continued with her PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
      Responsible growth: Development patterns within the city; Responsible growth:
      Enabling adaptability; and Responsible growth: Neighborhood-oriented commercial
      centers.

      The Council discussed the fact that residents do not always understand why
      neighborhood commercial makes sense, at times, causing developers to either sell

      other commercial, employment, or retail areas?

      Councilmember Larkin noted she spoke with some members of the Arts Commission.
      They pointed out that many of the local artists cannot afford to be in the Arts
      District. She asked what downtown is supposed to function as since it has been
      called both an Arts District and a Historic District and if there are plans to make
      spaces for up-and-coming artists to exist downtown. She believes downtown is at
      the center of everything else and needs to be kept alive.

      County Commissioner Almquist commented that is something is named an arts
      district, money needs to be put into the area to make it a viable arts district. It
      needs to have all of the elements and additional investment.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard pointed out that the data is a huge insight on what
      residents would like to and expect to see downtown. Staff is working on a Request
      for Proposals for City-owned property in the downtown area. He mentioned that the
      current budget included funds for a permanent outdoor stage, adding another
      element to the Arts District. He then commented that this is great data for policy
      making.

      Councilmember Curtis noted there are plenty of examples of other cities with vital
      downtown areas. He mentioned that historic preservation is significant to him.

      Planning Commission Member Kemp compared the downtown stores to
      advertisements in a magazine. Without content, there is no reason to open it. He
      stated there needs to be more content in the downtown area – a reason for people to
      go there. If there is nothing more to do than eat, there is no reason to go there as
      people can deliver food to a different location.

      Ms. Ward noted the next portion of the presentation will cover some ideas on what
      they believe the content could be.

      Planning Commission Member West commented as a resident, she would like an
      understanding of Main Street and St. George Boulevard. She agreed that downtown
      is missing content, missing vibrancy filled with small businesses that support the
      downtown vision.

      Economic Vitality and Housing Director Shirlayne Quayle stated that downtown has
      the special opportunity for local business to get on their feet, grow, and then to
      move into other areas of the City.

      Ms. Heller continued with her PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
      Downtown’s growth and redevelopment opportunities: Growth in downtown;
      Property value analysis & direct observation; DSU integration – additional height
      discussion; and Potential additional height areas.

      The Council took a brief recess.

      County Commission Members Almquist and Snow left during the recess.

      Ms. Heller and Ms. Ward continued with the PowerPoint presentation covering the
      following topics: Downtown business support: Downtown’s roles of engagement
      findings; Support for downtown businesses: preferred tools; Options for physical
      enhancements downtown; Parking study recommendations; Parking
      recommendations; and Possible downtown activation & management.

      A brief discussion took place regarding activation and management of the downtown
      area. The consensus of the Council is to explore the concept.

      Ms. Heller and Ms. Ward continued with the PowerPoint presentation covering the
      following topics: Expanded housing and mixed use: Downtown – housing & mixed
      use; Downtown land use; Existing multi-family; Existing single-family; Downtown –
      housing & mixed use; Development idea images – housing; Development ideas –
      vertical mixed use; Development ideas – horizontal mixed use; Downtown – infill
      housing concept for discussion; and Coming up: Downtown regulations to align with
      vision.

      Mr. Willis provided explained the infill housing concept provides the ability to
      incentivize property owners to tear down older multi-family units and develop a
      much better product.

      Ms. Ward added this concept would encourage reinvestment and upkeep of the
      properties. She explained one of the primary goals with these plans is to clearly
      articulate the City’s vision with regards to land use.

      Mr. Willis explained the general plan provides guidance; however, the proposed
      downtown master plan process will result in an ordinance being adopted.
