stated while he had requested additional information relating to the need for increased coverage,

subject to antennas being painted white and other departmental regulations being satisfied.

5. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to the C-2/CP-2, C-3/CP-3 and Group Dwelling
   Ordinances to set development standards for apartments and multi-family dwelling units.
   (Attachment I)

  Mr. Montgomery explained the City had adopted a pending ordinance in April during which time no
  new applications may be accepted for multi-family uses in commercial zones, anticipating new
  language would be adopted prior to its expiring in October. In considering an ordinance
  amendment, the Commission is to determine whether it is consistent with the General Plan. While
  the initial pending ordinance suggested multi-family uses may be removed from commercial zones,
  that is not the intent, but was so worded so the City would have maximum flexibility in order to
  study the issues and determine the best way to regulate these uses in commercial zones.

  He stated while there had been a few multi-family projects approved prior to 2018, at which time
  there were 500 units in commercial zones, and since that time there has been surge of applications,
  with 900+ units approved in 2018 and an additional 700 units approved in 2019 and early 2020. He

                                            Page 7 of 12
stated developers have found the approval process attractive and because there are few standards,
there has been the ability to build apartments at a low cost. He stated there is an abundance of
vacant or underutilized commercial property where apartments might be considered, and Staff is
concerned these areas need to be better regulated in order to become attractive to residents and to
be maintained as desirable housing over time. He stated it is important that the “right” development
is available, particularly for commercial areas, stating while Ogden has an over-abundance of
commercially zoned property, its main intent is not for housing. He reviewed land value and tax
revenue generated by similar sized properties in commercial zones, stating retail properties typically
have a higher value and generate more property taxes and sales tax than multi-family residential
uses. There also is the benefit of services to the community, and the creation of jobs. He stated
both retail and multi-family are desirable uses along major corridors, but some areas may be better
suited for commercial development. While commercial uses typically has a 20-30 year life-span,
housing is typically for the long-term, and is not easily converted to other uses and unable to adapt
to change over time.

He described multi-family housing as more than three dwellings in a building while a group dwelling
is defined as more than one building on a lot and may have multiple dwellings in each building. The
proposed ordinance amendment would address both mufti-family apartments and group dwellings
in an effort to create common ground between these uses, and to have standards more in line with
those of the multi-family residential areas. The proposed standards will address setbacks, open
space, density and parking regulations.

As the Commission is to consider compliance with the General Plan, Staff has reviewed the Plan,
which indicates corridors should accommodate high density housing and provide a mix of land
uses. Both residential and commercial uses compete for busy corridors, partly due to the
availability of transit. The Plan identifies locations it calls village centers as desirable locations for
mixed use commercial and residential uses. The intent is to implement standards which will both
protect existing and potential commercial areas as well as to allow quality multi-family housing to
develop. The proposed language would require properties which are larger than four acres to have a
mix of uses with a minimum of 25% of the project being retail or commercial uses. The areas
identified as village centers in the Plan would restrict residential to no more than 30% within a1000’
radius. Concerning parking, it has been found it is often underutilized and is expensive to develop,
and the proposed language creates options for properties where transit is available. While
residential areas require two parking spaces per unit, commercial could be reduced to 1.5 parking
spaces. Proximity to a bus stop could reduce parking to 1.3 per unit and low/moderate housing or
senior housing could be reduced to .8-1.5 units per acre.

Mr. Spencer then reviewed proposed amendments to the Group Dwelling chapter of the Ordinance.
He stated consideration has been given to building design, parking, site design, amenities and
density bonus options. The language is introducing a definition of mixed use group dwellings which
are only in commercial zones, for properties of more than four acres, where a percentage of
development is commercial. The proposed ordinance would require a solid material such as brick,
stone or masonry on the ground floor of any building, with upper floors allowed to be a variety of
other materials including fiber, stucco, metal or hardiboard. Building materials are limited to two
main materials with other materials allowed to be used as accents. Buildings are to have a

                                           Page 8 of 12
consistent design with projections to be in scale with the buildings, to be required at a minimum of
20% of the lineal frontage. Any projection of EFIS or stucco is required to extend from the base to
the cap, and large blank walls are to be avoided. Entrances are required to be prominent with
signage, lighting and a rooftop extension to be required as well as other features such as
architectural design, landscaping or canopies encouraged. Four building types are identified, and a
variety of building types is required in order to create interest. On projects of 2-4 acres, two building
types are required, and properties over 5 acres require three building types.

Parking standards are revised to match the regulations in commercial zones, with the addition of
bicycle parking, electrical car charging stations and if parking is below units, it is to be screened with
either a commercial or residential space. A minimum of one covered parking space per unit is
required. Site design improvements of a minimum of a 400 square foot plaza/gathering place to be
centrally located within the project is required as well as at least three additional features, which
may be a gas fire pit, tables and chairs, playground, water feature, artistic display, community
garden or grill. A 6’ sidewalk is required to connect the parking to the units as well as between units
to the amenity areas within the project. Density standards would be limited to that of the underlying
zone, with bonus density of up to 10% in residential zones and 20% in commercial zones.

Mr. Montgomery then addressed standards for apartments which would be a single building on a
lot. The proposed ordinance would require a minimum setback of 15’ in both commercial zones,
where previously the C-2 had a 20’ setback and the C-3 had none, in an effort to increase livability
and create better privacy for those who live on the ground floor. A 30’ rear separation is required for
buildings when next to a residential zone, while parking can be closer. On-site open space is to be a
minimum of 20%, excluding the setback areas. Open space is to be usable and provide amenities to
the residents, with the intent these are to be homes for people rather than people storage facilities.
Building material and design are identified with defined entryways and solid materials required to be
used for the ground floor, but a variety of materials allowed on upper floors. The Ordinance allows
the Planning Commission to vary the number of materials on a building based on its context with
the design of typical buildings in Ogden. He stated the draft ordinance addresses the concerns
identified in the pending ordinance while allowing development to move forward in commercial
areas.

time frame for the pending ordinance was begun in April of this year and expires after 180 days. He
stated the standards are not based on an individual property or zone, but would be effective for all
commercial properties or for all group dwelling projects. He stated the proposed 15’ setback is to

As there were no additional public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Southwick to
close the public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Sandau and passed unanimously.

Commissioner Sandau felt the proposed ordinance is encouraging growth and agreed that projects
of more than four acres should have a commercial component. Commissioner Boykin asked about
the variation of required setbacks, as well as their purpose. Mr. Montgomery explained the intent of
setbacks is to provide a transition space between separate properties or between a property and a
right-of-way in order to protect the individual property owner. He stated front setbacks are to
provide the property with a separation between the public realm, which may consist of sidewalk,
utilities, parking or travel lanes, and what is inside the building. While New Urbanism concepts
indicate setbacks might decrease gradually towards the downtown, and may not even exist in the
urban core areas to better accommodate pedestrian activities, setbacks are important to provide
security to residents. Setbacks are always measured from the property line, as rights-of-way are
not owned by the property owner and might be re-purposed over time and park strip areas might be
removed. A setback also makes pedestrians feel safe. He stated around the country, other
influences are prevalent, and homes might be closer to the street as the given culture sees more
value in back yard space, but in western states a front setback is common. Development should
also be in context with its surroundings. He also addressed timing of the ordinance adoption,
stating there has been much thought and discussion over the past several months. Commissioner
Boykin asked about long-term projections of ordinance changes, stating in New Orleans several
thousand people were displaced due to new regulations which had been adopted and residents
were unable to afford to comply with the new regulations after properties were destroyed by
hurricane. Commissioner Southwick felt the ordinance language as prepared is appropriate and
should be adopted so that there are better regulations for multi-family uses and creates a better
living space for its tenants. He expressed concern that if not acted on unfettered development of
commercial properties would continue. He felt the language proposed would create more attractive
use of commercial properties and create a better sense of community for the multi-family tenants.
