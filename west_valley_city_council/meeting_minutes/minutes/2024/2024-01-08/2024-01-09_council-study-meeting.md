      COMMUNICATIONS
      A.  STRATEGIC PLAN PRESENTATION- OFFICE OF JUSTICE AND
          TECHNOLOGY
          Eric Bunderson, City Attorney, stated that he oversees both Legal and Information
          Technology (IT). He noted that the biggest issue moving forward will be cyber
          security. He indicated that the City maintains over 28 sites that require extensive
          software and hardware needs. Eric noted that it is critical that staff stays current
          with technological advances by replacing and upgrading on a perpetual basis.

Councilmember Huynh arrived at 4:35 PM.

             Eric indicated that he will also be requesting police cameras for every vehicle on a
             risk management basis. He noted that these cameras will help supervisors address
             and hopefully reduce poor acceleration/stopping, distracted driving, etc. He noted
             that the most frequent risk that the City encounters is police vehicle accidents.
             Councilmember Harmon how many of these accidents occur each year on average.
             Eric replied about 7-10 a month. He noted that accidents are not always the officers
             fault but these cameras will provide a tool for supervisors to issue corrective
             measures to hopefully decrease at-fault risk.

             Eric stated that a challenge he foresees in the near future is personnel recruitment
             and retention. He indicated that he has lost several lawyers in the Legal department
             recently primarily to economic trends as they relate to the private and pubic sector
             as well as higher paying jobs. He noted that this is a trend he will be watching over
             the next few years.

             Eric stated that in the long term, he is carefully monitoring AI innovation. He stated
             that AI can calculate a legal analysis, with citations, quickly and accurately. He
             noted that he intends to continue experimenting with AI pilot programs to see what
             automation and AI can bring to the table and how this can improve efficiency and
             cost saving for the City.

      Councilmember Huynh asked if a lot of Legal work is being done in house. Eric
      replied that all prosecutors employed by the City prosecute all criminal non-

C.    LANCER WAY RECONSTRUCTION PROJECT
      Coby Wilson, Public Works, indicated that West Valley City will be reconstructing
      Lancer Way (3650 South) between 2700 West and 3200 West to improve safety
      and mobility. The work will include improving or constructing new elements such
      as park strips, trees, streetlights, sidewalks, curb and gutter, bike lanes, a center turn
      lane, and utilities. The project is currently in design, with construction anticipated
      for 2024.
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 9, 2024
                            -5-


      Coby indicated that a public meeting was held in August 2023 when the project
      was at 60% design completion. He noted that since that time, Rocky Mountain
      Power has consented to bury power lines. Coby stated that there are significant
      drainage issues in the area that need to be resolved and various options will include
      routing drainage to the north to 3500 South and utilizing the overflow City Hall
      parking lot (formerly the America First Credit Union).

      Coby stated that construction is required to be complete in 2025 but noted that there
      will be traffic impacts once construction begins. He detailed funding for the project
      which includes Federal and State funds as well as City funds tied to storm water
      utility and transportation sales tax.

      Councilmember Fitisemanu asked if residents have expressed any opposition or
      concern. Coby replied there has been a majority of positive feedback for the project.
      He indicated that the only issue raised by one resident was the lack of on-street
      parking proposed. Councilmember Fitisemanu clarified that the street would not be
      reduced to only one lane if construction extends over the 2024 winter. Coby replied
      that if construction is forced to halt, there will be one lane of traffic for each
      direction until it can resume again. Councilmember Harmon asked where
      pedestrian crossings will be provided. Coby indicated the location on the map.
      Mayor Lang noted that a resident in the area has requested an additional crossing.
      Coby stated that mid-street crossings are not generally encouraged due to safety but
      if another crossing is necessary, it can be added after the project is complete and
      the need is identified.

      The Mayor and Council had no further questions or concerns.
