Goal Foundation Annual Report
          The City Council has requested to receive information from local foundations and nonprofit organizations that have a
contractual agreement with Ogden City. These presentations help the Council to become more familiar with the objectives and
efforts of the organizations.
          Troy Callantine, Executive Director/Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of the GOAL Foundation, used the aid of a
PowerPoint presentation to describe the Foundation’s organizational mission and goals, successes and challenges over the past
year and any future plans for the organization. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the
meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Callantine centered on key performance indicators and metrics to measure
success of GOAL initiatives in the community; the significant support the Foundation receives from various City departments for
the special events they produce in Ogden; and GOAL’s participation in pre-planning events for the 2034 Winter Olympics in
Utah; and the recruitment and retention process for GOAL volunteers.

Fiscal Year 2023-2024 Annual Grants Report
         Assistant Finance Director Jensen used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025
Annual Grants Report. This report outlines the expenditures relating to local, state, and federal grant funds as of June 30, 2024.
The City managed more than $52 Million in various grant funds during the fiscal year and expended more than $13.7 Million
during Fiscal Year 2023-2024. Grant match, reporting requirements, and limitations on use vary from grant to grant. Some grants
received by the City are entitlements received annually – CDBG, Home, and Airport grants, for example – while others are one-
time, project-specific awards such as RAMP funding from Weber County. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.

WonderBlock Parking Update
          Public Services Assistant Executive Director/City Engineer Nielsen explained on January 17, 2023, the Redevelopment
Agency Board approved several agreements for the development of the WonderBlock project. This included a court parking
agreement, an operating agreement, and a land transfer and development agreement. Collectively, these agreements set forth the
working relationship between Ogden City, the Ogden Redevelopment Agency, and the developer, J Ficher. The real estate
purchase contract (REPC) allowed the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency to purchase seven (7) properties owned by the state of
Utah related to the Second District Court parking west of the court building at 2525 Grant Avenue. The REPC detailed the
agreement for the city to secure a portion of the existing parking as well as providing 125 stalls with the option of providing an
additional 100 monthly parking passes for use by the Court in the east parking garage with the WonderBlock project in exchange
for the subject property owned by the state. Now that the project is underway, Administrative staff have determined that placing
the court employee parking on the first floor of the parking structure as stated in the agreement is not feasible. As such, the
proposed amendments address the location of the court employee parking, visitor parking, changes to access, and future parking
management. As proposed, the designated court employee parking would be moved to the second floor with the additional visitor
parking located throughout the structure on several levels. The Board approved a first amendment to the original land transfer and
development agreement (LTDA) in October 2024. Since these changes are not material in nature, there is no action required by
either the RDA or the City Council.
