45   from the May 8th meeting).
46



     Lehi City Planning Commission                  1                            September 11, 2025
47   Jacob Curtis presented the item. Two major updates have occurred since the last time Planning
48   Commission heard this item: the amphitheater has been converted to a dog park, and a
49   conceptual plan of the overlook park has been submitted.
50
51   Adam Ostler was present, representing Clyde Capital Group. He gave an overview of the plan.
52
53   Commissioner Lockhart asked what other amenities were considered for the spot. Ostler said
54   they had looked at pickleball courts and playgrounds, among other options. It is a high-amenities
55   area with the surrounding trails. Commissioner Lockhart asked why the amphitheater was being
56   removed. Clyde Capital Group wanted to make sure the amenities that are put in are used. Their
57   research indicates that amphitheaters require lots of community planning as opposed to dog
58   parks which are more spontaneously used.
59
60   Commissioner Eyre asked for clarification on a box drawn on the development plan. It
61   represented a pickleball court, pump track, and playground area that have already been put in.
62
63   Commissioner Everett asked about the monetary value of the dog park vs the amphitheater, as
64   well as construction details for the dog park. The park will have a double-gated entry, and a
65   fenced area for off-leash play. The prices are comparable.
66
67   Commissioner Kunze asked what the play area would be made of. Ostler wasn’t sure.
68
69   Commissioner Lockhart read the listed required amenities as appears in city code and asked for
70   clarification on which have been met. All amenities have been met according to the original
71   agreement with City Council; the amphitheater will just be replaced with a dog park.
72   Commissioner Lockhart asked if a dog park would fulfil the need for a community gathering
73   space that the amphitheater would provide. Jacob Curtis agreed that it is not a traditional meeting
74   space, but that it is a space where people can gather and talk, rather than an event space like an
75   amphitheater. Kim Struthers mentioned that many of the amenities have been aged up as they are
76   expecting the demographic of the development to be older (a gym has been installed in place of
77   playgrounds for example). He emphasized that the commission should only focus on the replaced
78   amenity.
79
80   Commissioner Eyre stated that as the overlook park was always part of the planned community,
81   he doesn’t think of it as an additional amenity that makes up for the lack of an amphitheater.
82   Struthers explained that the developers are planning on making the park nicer than previously
83   planned. Commissioner Eyre expressed concern over taking value into consideration when
84   numbers were not presented to the commission. Commissioner Jackson seconded his sentiment.
85   He expressed frustration at the applicant not providing information to the commission.
86
87   Commissioner Eyre mentioned that he thinks that the exchange of a dog park for the
88   amphitheater is a positive one, and he agrees that the dog park will likely get more use. He
89   doesn’t think it’s possible to compare them one to one, but he sees it as an important part of a
90   neighborhood where there are no yards.
91
92   Commissioner Lockhart said that she thinks the intent of a community gathering place should be
93   met. She said it would be nice to zoom out and look at surrounding parcels to see where other
94   amenities lie nearby.
     Lehi City Planning Commission                    2                                September 11, 2025
 95   Kim Struthers brought forth the original approved package presented by the applicant, which
 96   listed the amphitheater’s price at $250,000. Commissioner Lockhart asked if the commission had
 97   previously approved amenities that don’t meet city standards. Struthers explained that there are
 98   amenities suggestions in the code, but that Planning Commission has the final decision on which
 99   amenities are approved.
100
101   Ostler returned to the stand to address some concerns. He clarified that the main concern of the
102   commission should be the dog park, as the amenities standards have been met. Commissioner
103   Lockhart clarified that the approved change originally included an amphitheater. She worries that
104   the community is losing benefits if this is approved, as the trails are a city-run project. Ostler
105   stated that $200,000 has been budgeted for the dog park. Struthers explained that the trails are a
106   big value for the city, and the developer put them in rather than the city having to.
107
108   Commissioner Eyre likes the idea of a dog park as an amenity. He thinks it would be a positive
109   and attracting feature for the neighborhood.
110
111   Commissioner Lockhart asked if the development would allow pets. It will.
112
113   Commissioner Jackson asked if any practical steps would be taken to make the park inviting to
114   people without dogs. Ostler explained that the dog run area would be fenced off. It will be
115   surrounded by trees to make it more of a green space, and there will be benches scattered around.
116   He also clarified that the park itself would be made out of grass.
117
118   Commissioner Eyre mentioned that the dog park at Willow Park is the cheapest the city could
119   build, but he still sees it heavily used at all times despite the fact that it isn’t close to any homes.
120   He feels the dog park would get a lot of utility in the area, especially with the proximity of the
121   units. Commissioner Lockhart still felt that it would be an exclusionary amenity.
122
123   Commissioner Eyre asked if the HOA would cover the maintenance of the dog park. Ostler
124   confirmed it would. Michelle Grow, Ostler’s coworker, came to the stand to explain that the
125   amphitheater had to be relocated to an area that wouldn’t have as much appeal due to the wind
126   study from the flight park. She mentioned that there is intent to put more amenities in the park
127   than are required, like a pet wash station, intentional shading, and high quality turf. Both the
128   overlook park and the dog park would be connected to the trail system, which would establish a
129   good pedestrian flow.
130
131   Commissioner Lockhart felt as though a dog park wouldn’t meet the intent of a TOD, and she
132   worried about losing the spirit of a TOD in the area, as no commercial development has gone in.
133   Kim Struthers explained that there is opportunity for commercial development nearer to the
134   station in the TOD, but only residential has been put in so far.
135
136   Commissioner Jackson asked if staff had any input on the intent of the amenity as a community
137   gathering place. Struthers emphasized that it is up to commission to decide if the dog park meets
138   the amenity criteria.
139
140                   Motion: Commissioner Everett moved to approve the proposed request to create
141                   a dog park in lieu of amphitheater, using an equivalent value as justification being
142                   placed into the clubhouse and the overlook park to be consistent with the
      Lehi City Planning Commission                       3                                  September 11, 2025
143                   amenities requirement as required by code, recognizing that at face value the dog
144                   park as a gathering place may not meet a TOD requirement for a gathering place,
145                   but could, depending on use of the trail system, that the amphitheater in its
146                   proposed location was unable to be constructed due to the flight park, and that this
147                   allows development in that space for a use consistent with the parameters of the
148                   wind study. He included all DRC comments.
149
150   Commissioner Eyre seconded the motion with the stipulation that the clause about the dog park
151   not meeting the definition of a meeting space be struck, as there is no definition in code for a
152   community meeting space. Commissioner Everett agreed.
153
154   Commissioner Lockhart asked the commission if they thought people would ride Trax to get to
155   the dog park. She clarified that the amphitheater wasn’t an impossibility, just that the
156   amphitheater in one specific location was not possible. The amphitheater could still be built.
157
158   Commissioner Everett withdrew his motion. He appreciated Lockhart’s comment on location and
159   said that the burden of proof should be on the applicant, not the commission, to justify the
160   replacement.
161
162   Michelle Grow returned to the stand to explain that where the amenity would be located, the
163   aesthetic and noise from surrounding commercial development would be less than ideal for an
164   amphitheater. Additionally, parking would be an issue. Commissioner Jackson asked where the
165   amphitheater was originally supposed to be. Grow didn’t know. Commissioner Kunze asked how
166   tall the buildings were in the development. Grow was uncertain but knew that they were shorter
167   than planned due to the wind study. Struthers shared that according to his memory they’ve gone
168   down to three stories from the original five. Commissioner Kunze asked if the wind study took
169   buildings into consideration. It did. Commissioner Eyre wondered why two four story buildings,
170   or even one story buildings, would have a different effect on the area than an open space like an
171   amphitheater.
172
173   Commissioner Kunze said that the planned amphitheater was a small, sixty seat venue. She
174   didn’t think that at that size it would be a good gathering space. Jacob Curtis clarified that when
175   the plan was originally approved, there were no other amphitheaters in the city.
176
177   Commissioner Everett expressed frustration at having to dig out answers as a commission rather
178   than being presented with pertinent information by the applicant.
179
180   Commissioner Lockhart stated that the TOD is supposed to be both an origin and a destination
181   point. She doesn’t know if a dog park would be a destination people traveled to. She explained
182   that she is cautious about swapping out amenities due to previous issues in Traverse Mountain.
183   She also wanted to preserve the TOD as it has been a big city project, and she didn’t want to
184   undermine it. Commissioner Eyre argued that a dog park would bring out more people than a
185   sixty-seat amphitheater would.
186
187   Commissioner Lockhart asked if the HOA would allow public access to the dog park, as it is
188   supposed to be a private amenity. Ostler explained that the intent is for the park to be maintained
189   by the HOA and used as a public space. Commissioner Lockhart asked staff if this was a normal

190   arrangement. Struthers explained that the amenities being discussed are meant to serve the
191   community living in the TOD according to his understanding of code.
192
193   Commissioner Lockhart asked what parts of the planned commercial development will bring
194   people in. The commercial development is being worked on, but residential has been the priority.
195   The commission discussed how commercial development could be a draw.
196
197   Commissioner Lockhart asked why the dog park is closer to transit than to residential
198   development if its intended to be for residents.
199
200   Commissioner Kunze mentioned that the trail ends at the dog park. The commission asked to see
201   the trail system. The commission expressed discomfort with Mountain Point Boulevard.
202   Struthers explained that it is a one-lane each way road with train tracks in the middle. It is a low
203   traffic road with protected bikeways on both sides.
204
205   Commissioner Lockhart said that she made the motion to table this item in May in order to help
206   the developers avoid the 12 month resubmittal period. She didn’t want to give a negative

208   location of the park and suggested that the commission table the item again in order to give the

291   3.4) Public Hearing and Recommendation of Lehi City’s request for a review of a
292   Development Code Amendment to Chapter 37, Connectivity Standards.
293
294   Jacob Curtis presented the item. This is primarily Mike West’s project. Many minor changes are
295   being implemented so that city code matches UDOT terminology, as well as other specific
296   details being reevaluated. Some changes have been influenced by recent projects.
297
298   Commissioner Everett asked if the ten foot wide path for culs-de-sac included landscaping. Kim
299   Struthers explained that the paths themselves would be ten feet wide, and that landscaping would
300   be included in the requirements.
301
302   Struthers explained that the major change to connectivity standards would allow developers the
303   opportunity to supplement a bike or pedestrian trail rather than connecting a stub road.
304
305   Commissioner Lockhart asked if this would allow the city to demand traffic-calming features on
306   streets not part of the parcel if overspill traffic comes into neighborhoods. It would be more of a
307   negotiation than a demand. Commissioner Lockhart asked if the Fire and Police Departments had
308   weighed in. Garion Rowett, the fire marshal, looked over the plan in DRC. He wants to use the
309   shared-use paths for emergency vehicles.
310
311   Commissioner Eyre asked if there is anything in the new code discussing curb cuts from a path
312   onto a road, as he has concerns for cyclists. Kim Struthers explained that they were included in
313   the most recent update to the city’s design standards. Struthers went on to say that the city has
314   looked into different indicative tiles for crossings that would make moving around easier for
315   blind residents.
316
317   Commissioner Lockhart shared that she sees the low traffic neighborhood circulation
318   improvements clause as a great example of putting residents first. She worried about not being
319   specific enough on the requirements for the multi-use paths, which would allow developers to
320   cut corners and result in the paths being unusable by emergency vehicles. Mike West has added
321   this note already.
322
323   Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.
324
325   Peter Ovard approves of the suggested changes. He thinks they make sense, and he agrees with
326   Lockhart on the implementation of item 6.
327
328   Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.
329
330                   Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to give a positive recommendation to the
331                   Lehi City Council with the findings that it is in line with he intent of the city’s
332                   code in that it adds and updates definitions, it adds provisions to the circulation
333                   plan requirement that the city may require modal filters, street closures, or access
      Lehi City Planning Commission                    7                                 September 11, 2025
334                   management improvements, it’s changing “trail” to “shared use path” throughout
335                   the traffic to be more specific and match UDOT terminology, and it removes the
336                   hard maximum block length requirements for the optional master plan trail and
337                   greenway exceptions. She included all DRC comments. Commissioner Eyre
338                   seconded.
339
340                   Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner
