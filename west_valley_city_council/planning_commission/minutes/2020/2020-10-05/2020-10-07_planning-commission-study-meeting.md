GENERAL PLAN/ZONE CHANGE APPLICATION
GPZ-7-2020   Petition by HOLIDAY OIL requesting a General Plan change from low density residential
             (3 to 4 units/acre) to neighborhood commercial and a zone change from R-1-8 (single unit
             dwelling residential, minimum lot size 8,000 sq. ft.) to C-1 (neighborhood commercial). The
             property is located at 6443 West 3500 South on 0.34 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-
             963-3545)

Discussion:       Steve Pastorik recapped the main issue with this application from the prior public hearing.
                  The code requires the setback to be based on the future right-of-way for new buildings or
                  new improvements. The subject location currently sits on an 80-foot ROW that will be
                  expanded to a 106-foot right-of-way in the future. This would require an additional 13 feet
                  of right-of-way dedication. There would not be enough room for the gas canopy when
                  looking to expand the landscaping on the subject property's east side. The applicant stated
                  he would look into expanding the west side of the drive approach.
                  Steve sent a site plan to the applicant proposing to flip the building to face north to
                  accommodate the setback requirement. The applicant stated this would not work due to the
                  lack of distance between the building and the gas canopy. Currently, the applicant does not
                  see a way to widen the landscape.
                Steve then told the Planning Commission it was up to them if they want to have a
                development agreement making an exception on the landscaping or deny the request.
                Commissioner Woodruff asked if other properties have met the setback requirements. Steve
                said they have. However, the difference with this property does not have much wiggle room
                north to south.
                Commissioner Winters asked if there was a way to look at other businesses around the
                subject location to see if the setback requirement will affect more than one business. Steve
                said he could look at other businesses along 3500 South. He explained currently, there is
                only one other business in the area, a hardware store. Commissioner Fuller stated the
                hardware store doesn't seem to have much landscaping, and he asked if the setback
                requirement will affect them in the future. Steve explained there that is an exception if
                adding the additional landscaping cuts into a building or parking spaces. In such situations,
                the property would get a pass and not need to add the additional landscaping.
ZONE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATION
ZT-7-2020   Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting a zone text change to create the Chesterfield
            Overlay Zone. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545)


Z-8-2020        Petition by WEST VALLEY CITY requesting a zone change to add the Chesterfield
                Overlay Zone. The properties are located generally between 2420 South and 2920
                South and between 1680 West and the Jordan River on 202 acres. (Staff – Steve
                Pastorik at 801-963-3545)

Discussion:     Steve Pastorik explained this application is to create the verbiage for the Chesterfield
                Overlay Zone and the next application, Z-8-2020, is to apply the overlay zone to the
                Chesterfield area. Steve then discussed the background of Chesterfield and how it is unique
                to the rest of the City. He highlighted significant differences between Chesterfield and the
                rest of the City that inspired the overlay zone's creation.
                1.   Chesterfield lacks improvements (curb, gutter, sidewalk, and storm drains).
                2.   Portions of Chesterfield are in a flood zone, restricting them from having basements.
                3.   Chesterfield has smaller homes, and 95% of those homes are single story.
                4.   Chesterfield has a higher percentage of homes that are rented as opposed to owned.
                Steve then explained the standards for the overlay zone and how it differs from the current
                standards.
                Commissioner Lovato thanked Steve for the work he put into this application. He asked
                Steve if there is wording within the overlay zone that states multifamily dwellings are not
                allowed. Steve explained the overlay zone does not add verbiage to allow for multifamily
                homes. He pointed out the RM zone requirement of being within a half-mile of a TRAX
                station. Many Chesterfield residents spoke up at that time, stating they do not want
                multifamily in the area. City Council excluded the TRAX stations in Chesterfield from that
                RM zone requirement. So, the only place that could add multifamily units would be on
                Redwood Road.
                Commissioner McEwen feels if there is an area that needs to develop, it would be
                Chesterfield, and he struggles with the overlay zone lowering the standards. Steve
                understands Commissioner McEwen's concern and explained that Chesterfield residents
                   have spoken against large developments in the area where the homes are smaller than the
                   rest of the City. The resident's resistance is what started the conversation about the overlay
                   zone.
                   Commissioner Winters likes that the overlay zone has higher standards; however, not so
                   high that there would be a considerable disparity in the neighborhood.

             Steve Lehman at 801-963-3311)

                   property, the applicant agreed to amend the original development agreement that would
                   apply to this property and would also affect the remaining phases of Woodhollow. The
                   amended agreement would up the standards previously approved for Woodhollow. Steve
                   then went over the proposed amendments
                   Commissioner Winters questioned where this property is zoned R-1-10; why is there a
                   7,500-foot lot allowed? Steve explained the smaller lot size is permitted as part of the
                   original agreement.
