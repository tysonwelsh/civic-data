what it would be like to visit their grave site and find that it had deep ruts in the ground.

ORIDNANCE 08-01-2023 TITLE 10 REVISIONS-HEIGHT REQUIREMENTS ADOPTED:

   City Planner Shauna Talbot explained to the council that while considering the Arrow Builders rezone
at previous planning commission meetings, the need to reduce the maximum allowed building height
from six (6) stories and seventy-five (75) feet to a more appropriate height was discussed. The planning
commission and staff are proposing the maximum height in the CU-2 zone be lowered to three (3)
stories and forty-five (45) feet, keeping in alignment with the current maximum in the CU-1 zone. Also,
the type of limited commercial in this zone, and the type of residential development, including multi-
unit development placed in this zone should be used as a buffer between residential and neighborhood
commercial uses that transitions into highway commercial uses along our transportation corridors that
may have denser, and intense uses, and higher maximum height allowances such as six story hotels.

   As the planning commission decides areas where the CU-2 zone would be appropriate, staff feels a
mix of CU-1 and CU-2 along main transportation corridors should be employed in an alternating fashion,
to mitigate heavy traffic needs, and to more appropriately blend with similar surrounding uses on an
existing block, or nearby. Because of the differing, but complimentary traffic patterns, and uses in the
two zones, this would be an appropriate planning method. Also, similar height requirements between
these two zones will add to the continuity of the alternating of these zones along transportation
corridors. If an area along transportation corridors needs more height the HC-1 zone could be an
alternative zone for such needs.

  Councilor Worwood moved to adopt Ordinance 08-01-2023 limiting the building height in the CU-2
zone from 75 feet to 45 feet. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a
unanimous vote.

K-9 TRUCK LEASE WITH JUAB COUNTY APPROVED:

   City Attorney Kasey Wright updated the council that the Millard/Juab County Drug Task Force
decided, through their Executive Board, to use some seized drug funds to purchase a used K-9 truck that
is fully outfitted for the Nephi City K-9 unit. The seized drug funds had been sent to Juab County and
they must be the entity to own and purchase the vehicle asset. For the drug task force and the Nephi
City K-9 unit to utilize the truck, a lease agreement has been drafted for Juab County and Nephi
City. The truck will be insured through Nephi City.
    It is anticipated that the lease agreement will be considered by the Juab County Commissioners in
their July 31st meeting. The lease agreement will provide, at very minimal cost to Nephi City a K-9 truck
for the drug task force. The city’s K-9 unit has already proven to be a valuable asset and a truck will aid
the K-9 unit in becoming even more effective.
    Councilor Parady moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the truck lease agreement with Juab County.
Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

UDOT AGREEMENTS FOR HIGHWAY 132 EXPANSION PROJECT APPROVED:

  Finance Director/Recorder Lisa E. Brough explained to the council that at the last regular council
meeting, the staff presented some offers for land to aid UDOT with the Highway 132 expansion
project. The Council approved those offers at the time. It was the understanding of the staff that these
would be for easements on land owned by Nephi City. UDOT has now sent the different agreements, for
the Council’s consideration, to actually purchase the land and own it. The offers that were made in the
last meeting contained prices for the purchase of these properties.
  The land sales will benefit the project and there is a local interest, from a safety perspective, to see
the highway widened. The funds are still recommended to be conveyed to the Water Fund to assist
with the costs associated with the relocation of the city’s main water line along Highway 132.
   Councilor Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the UDOT agreements. Councilor
Memmott seconded the motion. The motions passed unanimously.
