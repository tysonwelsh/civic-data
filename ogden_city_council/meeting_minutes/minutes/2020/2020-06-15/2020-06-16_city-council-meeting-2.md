Proposed Ordinance 2020-24 amending the definition of taverns, private
clubs and drinking establishments
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed zone text amendments
pertaining to drinking establislunent definitions came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petition has been
filed by Martin McFadden with Blue Ox Development to amend the definitions related to drinking establislunents as well as the
regulations for drinking establislunents in the Business Exchange Historical (BEH) zone. While these requests are closely
connected, the two requests will be separated into different items for Council consideration. The proposed amendments with this
item would eliminate the defmitions for tavern and private club and replace them with a new defmition for a drinking
establislunent. The proposed defmition for drinking establislunent in the City's code would cover the different types of alcohol-
serving establishments defmed in state code and regulated in the City's code. The proposed defmition fordrinkingestablislunents
would also clarify that for the purpose ofthe City's zoning andnumberof drinking establislunentsper linear block, alcohol sales must
exceed 50% oftotal annual receipts. A similar proposal to update the code related to the defmition of taverns and private clubs came
before the Council in 2016; however, no fmal action was taken on that proposal. The 2016 amendments came in conjunction
with a petition to amend the defmition of a linear block.
          Deputy Planning Manager Spencer summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on
the petition and the Planning Commission's recommendation. The proposed amendments are indirectly tied to Petitions 2019-24
and 2015-9 as well as State law changes; current City ordinances need to be updated to reflect State liquor laws; additionally, the
proposed ordinance amendment would accommodate proposed use for Ogden's own liquor only bar with no beer sales. Utah State
laws provide defmitions for restaurants and bars; restaurants must have receipts for 70 percent food sales and three types of
restaurants can serve alcohol: limited service, beer only, and full-service businesses. Bars and taverns are different; taverns are
beer-only bars. Bars must have food available, but patrons are not required to order food in order to purchase alcohol. Mr.
Spencer then noted the current City Ordinance classified businesses with more than 50 percent beer only sales as taverns and
private clubs, while restaurants have more than 50 percent food sales. The petitioner is proposing to operate a liquor tasting
room/bar only with no beer sales that that type ofuse does not fit within either of the City's ordinance defmitions. The proposed
wording changes in the ordinance include:
          •    Replace tavern and private club with Drinking Establislunent;
          •    Revise defmitions;
          •    City's defmition follows present city standards of 50 percent rather than the State's of 70 percent; and
          •    Revise wording in land use sections.
          Additionally, the defmition ofrestaurant will be changed to clarify Ogden's distinction from the State's. He summarized the
effects of the ordinance amendment proposal; the City ordinance would create a niche by allowing 'adult only' restaurants.
Restaurants that serve between 50 percent and 70 percent food sales are classified by State law as bars, not restaurants, and do not
allow minors. The regulation limiting the use to two per linear block applies only to 'drinking establislunents' which sell more
than 50 percent alcoholic beverages per City ordinances. The ordinance proposal does not change development standards thatthe
downtown has developed under for the past 20 years. He concluded the Planning Commission voted unanimously to recommend
approval ofupdating City ordinances to adopt' drinking establislunents' as a replacement for 'taverns' and 'private clubs'.
          Chair Choberka inquired as to the relationship between this proposed ordinance amendment and the proposal that was
made a few years ago to change the linear block defmition. Mr. Spencer stated that this proposed ordinance would not address the
same issues that the petitioner for the linear block defmition amendment was seeking.
          Council member Stephens asked if the alcoholic beverage establislunents named in the proposed ordinance must be
located a specified distance from churches and schools. Mr. Spencer stated that the State ofUtah provides required distances that
drinking establislunents must maintain from identified community locations and this ordinance will not impactthose regulations.

              It has been over 10 years since a plan has been created specifically for downtown Ogden; the MAKE Ogden plan is
    intended to guide strategic decision making to balance assets and build momentum toward a prosperous future. Tenets of the
    project include land use, preservation for historic assets, mobility /transportation, open space, equity, and branding. A borderofthe
    downtown area has been identified and the total area is divided into five proposed districts, including the Downtown District,

    have been given the opportunity to participate in a public process to provide their feedback regarding the types of projects and
    improvements they would like to see in each of the five districts. Now that these priorities have been identified, it is time to
    consider smart growth in the project area. The following 10 principles are considered the foundation of a smart growth approach.
                 1. Mix of land uses.
                 2. Take advantage of compact design.
                 3. Create a range of housing opportunities and choices.
                 4. Create walkable neighborhoods.
                 5. Foster distinctive, attractive communities with a strong sense of place.
                 6. Preserve open space, farmland, natural beauty, and critical enviromnental areas.
                 7. Direct development towards existing communities.
                 8. Provide a variety of transportation choices.
                 9. Make development decisions predictable, fair, and cost effective.
                10. Encourage community and stakeholder collaboration in development decisions
