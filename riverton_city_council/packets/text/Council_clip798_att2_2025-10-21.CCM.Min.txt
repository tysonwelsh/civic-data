24            Ordinance No. 25-23 – Amending Sections 18.90.010 “Table of Commercial
25            Uses” & 18.70.030 “Conditional Uses” to allow “Auto Dealership (New or Used)
26            as a Conditional Use in the Commercial Gateway Zone. Amendments Proposed
27            by Riverton City.
28
29   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the Council was considering a
30   text amendment to the Commercial Gateway Zone to allow auto dealerships as a conditional
31   use. The proposed amendment is not tied to a specific developer but would apply citywide
32   within the gateway zone, primarily located at major entrances and along the 12600 South
33   corridor. Currently, auto dealerships are not permitted in this zone, and the amendment would
34   allow them only on finished commercial sites with Planning Commission review and approval.
35   Proposed requirements include a permanent building meeting city architectural standards,
36   service bays limited to vehicles sold on-site, and prohibition of outside auto repair without a
37   separate conditional use permit. To mitigate potential impacts, the amendment limits
38   dealerships to one per 1.5 miles in the zone and restricts them to properties fronting 12600
39   South or Bangerter Highway with at least one acre. The Planning Commission reviewed the
40   amendment and recommended denial, citing concerns with the limited code section, but staff
41   provided additional language addressing architectural, site, and operational standards.
42
43   Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker opened the public hearing and asked for public comment.
44
45   Adam Jones, a Riverton resident and representative of the Utah Automotive Alliance, spoke in
46   support of the proposed auto dealership. He noted his familiarity with the prospective owner,
47   highlighting the owner’s reputation and community involvement. He emphasized potential
48   benefits for the local high school automotive program, including donations and engagement
49   opportunities. He also encouraged the Council to consider the significant sales tax revenue that
50   auto dealerships can generate for the City, while acknowledging the stipulations in place to
51   ensure proper operations.
     Page | 4 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1
 2   Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker asked for any additional public comment. There being none, she
 3   closed the Public Hearing.
 4
 5   Mr. Lethbridge explained that the City’s previous restrictions on auto dealerships were based on
 6   scale and location. Larger dealerships were primarily suited for the southwest part of the valley
 7   and weren’t appropriate for downtown or the east side due to property limitations and proximity
 8   concerns. Smaller, temporary dealerships didn’t comply with existing code, so there was little
 9   interest or viable opportunity for them in Riverton. Now, with new interest in developing
10   dealerships compatible with City standards, the Council is considering a code change,
11   specifically limiting them to the commercial regional zone to ensure compatibility and manage
12   impact.
13
14   Councilmember McDougal explained that about 8–10 years ago, Riverton’s zoning was
15   somewhat scattered. During the master zoning update five years ago, there was a deliberate
16   effort to consolidate commercial areas along 12600 South, keeping residential areas intact. This
17   approach concentrated commercial traffic and development, prevented piecemeal expansion in
18   cheaper areas, and preserved in-fill opportunities. As a result, long-vacant parcels are now
19   attracting interested, compatible businesses. The proposed code revision is part of fine-tuning
20   this approach, allowing the City to protect its interests while partnering with responsible
21   developers in areas where interest and compatibility align.
22
23   Councilmember Pierucci expressed support for the proposed structure, highlighting that
24   requiring a finished commercial site and a permanent building ensures long-term investment
25   and stability in the community. He appreciated that it allows responsible businesses to succeed
26   while avoiding “fly-by-night” operations. He also indicated openness to revisiting the 1.5-mile
27   limitation in the future, signaling potential flexibility to allow additional opportunities for
28   businesses to invest in Riverton, including considering other locations such as the commercial
29   gateway near 13400 South.
30
31   Councilmember Pierucci MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 25-23 –
32   Amending Sections 18.90.010 “Table of Commercial Uses” & 18.70.030 “Conditional
33   Uses” to allow “Auto Dealership (New or Used) as a Conditional Use in the Commercial
34   Gateway Zone as shown in Exhibit A. Councilmember McCay SECONDED the motion.
35   Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker

37   McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed unanimously.

13            Ordinance No. 25-24 – Site Plan Development Standards Amendments –
14            Amendments and clarifications to Section 18.215.030 “Site Plan Development
15            Standards.” Amendments Proposed by Riverton City.
16
17   Mr. Lethbridge presented proposed amendments to the City’s site plan development standards,
18   focusing specifically on landscaping requirements. The primary intent is to shift from rigid lists of
19   required trees and vegetation toward a more flexible approach that leverages the expertise of
20   Riverton City staff, including the City arborist and irrigation specialists. Staff would provide
21   recommendations to the Planning Commission and City Council rather than strictly following a
22   checklist, allowing for more professional judgment and adaptability in site plan review.
23
24   Mr. Lethbridge noted that while the proposed amendments address these concerns, further
25   alignment with the City’s water-wise landscaping standards may be warranted. He suggested
26   continuing the item to integrate this consistency and potentially return to the Planning
27   Commission for additional feedback before final approval. The Planning Commission has
28   already recommended approval of the current amendments.
29
30   Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker opened the public hearing and asked for public comment.
31
32   Rusty Lance asked who would be responsible for replacing trees that are damaged by public
33   traffic.
34
35   Mayor Pro Tempore Buroker asked for any additional public comment. There being none, she
36   closed the Public Hearing.
37
38   In response to Mr. Lance’s question, Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said the City’s response to tree
39   damage from vehicles depends on the situation. If a police report identifies the driver, the City
40   typically seeks compensation from the driver’s insurance, based on the tree’s value as
41   estimated by the City arborist. In some cases, the tree may not be replaced in the same spot
42   due to safety or traffic concerns. If compensation isn’t received, the cost falls to the City’s
43   taxpayers.
