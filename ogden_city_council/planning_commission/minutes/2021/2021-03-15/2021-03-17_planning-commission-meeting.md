   1. Discussion,
      A. State law changes to Ordinance
         Mr. Montgomery reviewed recent State law changes which will have an effect
         on zoning regulations in Ogden City.
            SB217- Housing and transit reinvestment zone
            This creates the ability for tax increment revenue to support projects within 1/3
            mile from the Frontrunner Station and contain mixed use, a minimum of 50
            dwelling units per acre and is 10% affordable housing. The bill is subject to
            approval of tax entities. This is consistent with the recently adopted Make Ogden
            Plan and is a source to assist with development costs. Commissioner Garner
            asked about ground waste that may be in this area. Mr. Montgomery responded
            that other funding is available for brownfield remediation. Commissioner Safsten
            asked if changes would be required to allow increased density in this area. Mr.
            Montgomery responded that there is no maximum density in the CBD District and
            no amendments would be required.

              HB 82 Single Family Housing Modifications (ADU amendments)
              Mr. Montgomery stated this regulation applies only to accessory dwelling units in
              the main building and allows cities to require up to one additional parking space
              for single family homes with accessory units. Cities are also allowed to file a lien
              on properties which are in violation and establish certifications for this type of
              use, as well as prohibiting the use on lots of less than 6,000 square feet. While
              Ogden already complies to many of these regulations, some changes may be
      required, as Ogden limits the use to two adults and their minor children and limits
      the ADU to 40% of the floor area of the home. The new law limits the use to
      three adults in the accessory unit but still requires it to be owner-occupied.
      Discussion related to enforcement, licensing and assuring basement rentals are
      safe and the fact there are many existing illegal basement rentals. Cities also
      have the option of limiting the use to 67% of the area in residential zones. Mr.
      Montgomery reviewed the process for inspections and licensing of these uses,
      and indicated the City has relied on reports from residents to identify these at
      which time the owner would be notified and required to become licensed. Mr.
      Stratford stated Ogden has not required additional parking based on the limited
      number of adults allowed, while additional parking might be warranted if three
      adults are allowed. As it is typically unknown who tenants will be in a particular
      case, the creation of additional parking spaces may or may not be desirable.

      HB 98 Local Government Building Regulations
      This bill is limited to single family and duplex uses or two-unit townhome
      buildings and limits design review standards to be established by cities unless in a
      local or national historic district or in an area substantially developed prior to
      1950. Mr. Stratford indicated while the Planning Commission can still
      recommend design standards but they are not enforceable. A private HOA would
      have no restrictions. Due to the East Central Bench National Historic District, the
      East Central standards are still applicable.

      HB107 - Subdivision plat amendments
      Requires 20-day notice to water providers within 100 feet of a subdivision.

      HB 409 Municipal and County Land Use and Development Revisions
      The main effect of this bill to Ogden City is the requirement for Planning Commission
      members to complete four hours of training per year.

B. East Central Plan Update
   Mr. Montgomery stated the City had held a virtual town meeting for the East
   Central Plan in October and Staff was disappointed with the lack of
   participation and involvement from the community. He would like input from
   the Commission as to how to proceed in order to complete the plan update.
   Typically after the initial town meeting, steering committees are established
   from volunteers within the community who then assist Planning Staff in
   satisfying their concerns and creating new plan language which is then
   presented to the entire community at an open house. He felt there is a risk to
   proceed without input from the community, but as there has been no interest
   from the community in serving on steering committees, Staff has not
   proceeded. He asked if it would be better to wait until risk of the COVID
   pandemic has passed and then to start over or to proceed. Commissioner
   Sandau stated community members need information to respond to, and
   suggested some options be created and brought to the community for input at
   an open house. Commissioner Schade agreed, suggesting some concepts
   can be developed and the City move forward in the plan development.
   Discussion continued relating to the complex demographics of this
   community, language barriers and the number of rentals in the community as
   well as alternative methods to get information to citizens within the
   community. Commission asked what information is now available from the
   initial town meeting. Mr. Montgomery stated survey results are available as
   well as input received from the meeting.

   Discussion continued on methods to reach community citizens, including
   working with church and/or school groups or PTA’s, providing information on
   door hangers, and involving Jr. High and High School students who could then
   involve their parents.

   Commission asked about what is trying to be accomplished by the update.
   Mr. Montgomery stated this amendment is limited in its scope and is not an
   overall plan update as is typical. The intent is to only update the plan in order
   to include the 9-Rails Plan, the implementation of the Bus Rapid Transit, and to
   allow accessory dwelling units in the R-1 areas within the community.
   Commissioner Southwick felt these items should be added to the plan, and as
   the scope of the plan is so limited, Staff should proceed and draft language to
   be presented to the community. Many Commissioners agreed and felt
   citizens may respond better to proposed plan language than to provide input
   where they are unsure of the process.

C. In-Person Meetings
   Mr. Montgomery stated side barriers have now been installed in the City Council
   Chambers and asked if the Commission desires to continue with electronic
   meetings or to begin meeting in person. It was suggested there could also be a
   format where both zoom and in person meetings might be an option.
   Commissioner Garner stated the mask mandate is due to be lifted on April 10th
   and many have received vaccinations. Mr. Stratford suggested if in-person
   meetings are considered that masks still be required but could be removed
   when speaking. Consideration was given to allowing the Commission and
   developers to meet in person but for citizens to remain as virtual attendees.
   Commissioner Southwick stated he is still concerned with the virus and felt in
   person meetings should not begin until June. Commissioner Blaisdell indicated
            she felt she does a better job as a Planning Commissioner when everything is
            live as she can pick up on body language and see the faces of those who are
            commenting. Commissioner Garner stated the virus is not over, and many are
            nervous about separation and felt masks still should be used, Discussion
            continued relating to a hybrid of person and virtual meetings for a time period
            and then to proceed with live meetings towards the middle of the summer. It
            was suggested the May work session might be in person with the first regular
            meeting to become either in person or hybrid in July.

As there was no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned
at 7:07 pm.
