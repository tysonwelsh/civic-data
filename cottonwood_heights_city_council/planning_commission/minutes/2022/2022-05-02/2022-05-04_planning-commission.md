29          1.1       Additional Discussion Items.
30
31   Assistant City Planner/Sustainability Analyst Samantha DeSeelhorst presented the Ordinance
32   update relating to the Riparian Protection Area component of the Sensitive Lands Evaluation and
33   Development Standards (“SLEDS”) Ordinance (Section 19.72 of the Zoning Ordinance.) She
34   reported that there had been no changes to the draft standards, and the update will provide
35   clarification to comments previously received. The main concept for the Ordinance was to
36   implement the standard practice of utilizing buffer areas in regulating the use of property adjacent
37   to a waterway. In this system, adjacent property is divided into various buffer areas with each
38   being regulated differently based on location. Remodels and additions are allowed if they meet
39   the Riparian Protection Area standards. A table detailing how structures will be treated in the
40   different buffer areas was displayed.
41
42   Senior City Planner, Andy Hulka continued the discussion regarding the SLEDS Ordinance and
43   stated that this is the first time this item will be open for public comment. Public notice was
44   provided to every property owner in the City. The effort began with a desire to update the Hazard
45   Maps with coverage of geotechnical and geologic hazards with the intent to clarify or have more

 4   Mr. Hulka addressed the steep slope section of the Ordinance, which currently states that no
 5   development is permitted on slopes greater than 30%. In the Definitions, development is defined
 6   as a primary structure, home, or new commercial building and did not include items such as roads,
 7   accessory buildings, and retaining walls. He clarified that the new language states as follows:
 8
 9          ‘They are clearly isolated from other areas that exceed 30%” was changed to state
10          that “The slope is not part of a larger, contiguous slope that exceeds 30%. “This
11          exception shall not apply to natural slope areas in excess of 30% connected to the
12          Wasatch Mountain Range” was changed to “This exception shall not apply to
13          natural slope areas in excess of 30% east of Wasatch Boulevard.”
14
15   Avalanche hazards were next discussed. Mr. Hulka explained that there are potentially several
16   properties where avalanche protection should be considered. The latest draft ordinance lists snow
17   avalanches as one of the hazards that the Development Review Committee (“DRC”) can require
18   additional studies, but no longer includes the Avalanche appendix.
19
20   Vegetation and re-vegetation plans are required in sensitive land areas for projects that require the
21   re-vegetation of a trail, open space, or hillside. A requirement was added to this section requiring
22   vegetation plans to be stamped by a Licensed Landscape Architect.
23
24   Mr. Hulka reported that the maximum grade requirements were removed from the Streets and
25   Ways section of the Ordinance. To maintain consistency with the street design requirements
26   contained in Title 14 of the City Code (Highways, Sidewalks, and Public Places), the maximum
27   grade section was removed. The maximum grade of streets in sensitive land areas shall be the
28   same as all other streets in the City (8%).
29
30   Mr. Hulka next reviewed Land Disturbance Permit and stated that most of this section was initially
31   removed to simplify the process for receiving a Land Disturbance Permit. After close review by
32   the Public Works Department, the requirements were left in the Ordinance with the clarification
33   that they apply to sites larger than one acre. Mr. Hulka noted that sites smaller than one acre may
34   be included if they are a phase or part of a larger development project and exceed one acre.
35
36   The Geologic Hazard Report was next reviewed. Mr. Hulka reported that the City Attorney is
37   concerned that setting a hard deadline of 45 days for review could be an issue. This draft includes
38   language that the City will “endeavor to complete each review in a reasonable time frame within
39   45 days.” Acceptable slope percentages were reviewed, and a more thoughtful approach was
40   suggested. Ms. DeSeelhorst recognized the concern to have the report provide more clarity and
41   yet be practical enough to apply to different circumstances.
42
43   Staff anticipates additional changes to be made after a full review by the City’s Geotechnical and
44   Geologic Hazard Consultant and once public comments are received. The deletion of verbiage
45   was reviewed. Mr. Hulka confirmed that applications are to be submitted to the front desk who in

36          3.1       (Project ZTA-19-001) A Public Hearing to Receive Comments on a Proposed
37                    Ordinance Update Relating to the Sensitive Lands Evaluation and
38                    Development Standards (“SLEDS”) Ordinance (Section 19.72 of the Zoning
39                    Ordinance.)
40
41   Assistant City Planner/Sustainability Analyst, Samantha DeSeelhorst presented the Staff Report
42   and stated that the matter will be discussed in two parts to address both the riparian content and
43   the remaining portion of the chapter. The Riparian Protection Guidelines are referenced in the
44   existing Sensitive Lands Ordinance but the chapter pertaining to the guidelines is missing. The
45   discrepancy is being addressed as part of the broader Sensitive Lands Evaluation and Development
46   Standards (“SLEDS”) update. The Planning Commission reviewed several drafts and the



     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 05/04/2022                                     4
 1   standards and language remain the same, however, the desire was to provide additional
 2   clarification.
 3
 4   Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that most riparian ordinances work off the concept of a property adjacent
 5   to a waterway being buffered into one of three areas. Staff proposed Areas A, B, and C working
 6   from the waterway edge. Each would be regulated differently to be sensitive to the ecosystem and
 7   habitat adjacent to those waterways. A map analysis of the waterways was displayed.
 8
 9   The maintenance or remodeling of existing homes was next discussed. Ms. DeSeelhorst stated
10   that if the structure does not meet the riparian protection area setbacks, interior remodels will still
11   be permitted. The proposed addition will be allowed if it meets the setback standards but will be
12   rejected if it encroaches on the waterway area. The most intense development includes the
13   construction of a new structure. It will be allowed if that structure meets the standards for the
14   protection area and setbacks. A table outlining how the Ordinance could work in practice was
15   described. Staff will continue to coordinate valuable local, state, and federal partners. They will
16   also follow up on feedback received from the Commission suggesting that Staff reach out to the
17   Federal Emergency Management Agency (“FEMA”), the Army Corps of Engineers, and the U.S.
18   Forest Service.
19
20   Senior City Planner, Andy Hulka reported that the Sensitive Lands Ordinance governs new
21   development in areas with geotechnical or geologic hazards. This began with a desire to update
22   the Hazard Maps with the intent to clarify or have more strict criteria to ensure that they meet the
23   current best practices. Technical updates were implemented along with feedback from the Utah
24   Geological Survey, which resulted in a rewrite of the entire chapter.
25
26   Commissioner Chappell stated that discussion during the Work Session included ongoing
27   refinement of the setback at surface faulting.
28
29   Chair Allen opened the public hearing.
30
31   Ellen Birrell acknowledged the Planning Commission and City Staff for their efforts and stated
32   that she views the SLEDS Ordinance as a very important safety measure for the City. She wished
33   to reinforce revisions to the Ordinance, preserve the Design Review Committee (“DRC”), and
34   proceed with caution since the purposes and goals specify a focus on safety. Language should
35   include protecting the public from natural hazards and wildlife habitat. She was concerned with
36   new language that includes a clause that protects the City’s infrastructure and financial health. She
37   believed the focus of the Ordinance should be on safety and not make it more financially viable
38   for developers.
39
40   Steve McIntosh commented that after reading the Ordinance correctly, Staff and the DRC make
41   recommendations on applications, however, the Planning Commission’s role is to approve or deny.
42   He believed the Planning Commission should be responsible for making the final decision and
43   keep applicants at an arm’s length and provide transparency for citizens. He expressed concern
44   with large retaining walls along Creek Road and recommended design standards to address safety
45   issues. He expressed concern with slope stabilization and previously failed areas with inadequate
46   compaction. His understanding was that those areas are subject to liquefaction. He suggested the

 2   Setbacks of 25 feet were recommended.
 3
 4   Diane Forster-Burke did not understand the emphasis on the role of the DRC from the previous
 5   SLEDS to the new iteration. She believed that including the DRC and its expertise of providing
 6   counsel to the Planning Commission was important. She suggested that the focus remain on safety
 7   and expressed concern with the setback from a seismic fault. She suggested that steps be taken to
 8   ensure as much safety as possible.

10   Alan Fletcher was concerned about extending the Riparian Zone along the creek and the
11   development area along the Old Mill subdivision remaining single-family. If something other than
12   single-family is approved, he suggested that additional setbacks be required to provide additional
13   room with the stream. With regard to high-density housing, he recognized there are more
14   appropriate areas. He appreciated the community forum that was held previously and believed the
15   property where Ream’s Food Store was formerly located would be an appropriate location for high
16   density. He was opposed to it being at the proposed Old Mill location.
17
18   Woody Noxon commented that SLEDS is a special ordinance and is not about dimensions,
19   setbacks, units, and quantity. The ordinance maximizes public safety for residents and
20   demonstrates that Cottonwood Heights has conducted its due diligence in the evaluation of public
21   safety to avoid liability regarding future events. He believed it should be used as a baseline to
22   compare recommended changes. He read a portion of the Ordinance and believed that the SLEDS
23   Ordinance has not been followed in the Doverhill Drive area as fault issues are currently being
24   addressed to allow for mitigation. He prepared a graphic presentation for the SLEDS Ordinance
25   procedure that includes detailing activities and decisions required to complete proper compliance
26   with the SLEDS Ordinance. He suggested that a list of questions be presented when considering
27   each change.
28
29   Will McCarvill gave his address as 3607 Golden Hills Avenue and stated that the current Ordinance
30   is designed to protect lives when development occurs in areas with dangerous structures such as
31   earthquake faults. He believed the proposed revision strips away oversights and approvals
32   intended to protect lives and property where development on dangerous lands occurs. Checks and
33   balances will be removed to potentially favor development on sensitive lands over lives. It was
34   his opinion that development will be treated like any other development in the City and no longer
35   be special. He emphasized the need to strengthen the ordinance and recommended the
36   Commission reject the Economic Development Department’s proposed revision and focus on
37   protecting the citizens.
