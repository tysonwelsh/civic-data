Chair Nadolski and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council,
Redevelopment Agency (RDA), and Municipal Building Authority (MBA) meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 City Budget

General Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Budget Discussion: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith noted there has been a
question about the budget request related to the Airport Technician position and she has invited representatives of the Community
and Economic Development Department to attend the meeting tonight to discuss that issue.

Department Director Cooper provided general context about the position and indicated Airport Manager Garrett will
provide more detailed information. He noted that there are workforce deficiencies at the Airport; there is a great deal of work to do
and areas to take care of outside of just managing the airlines at the facility and the position is needed to continue operations at the
Airport even if there are no commercial airlines located at the Airport. Mr. Garrett added there are currently three maintenance
positions at the Airport, and he summarized the work performed by the maintenance team, including vegetation control and snow
removal. He indicated that ten years ago there were four people on the maintenance team and that has been reduced to three and
they are unable to keep up with the amount of work to be done at the facility. The position request is warranted regardless of
whether a commercial airline is located at the Airport.

There was brief discussion among the Council and Mr. Garrett regarding operational changes at the Airport based on the
loss of commercial airlines at the facility; Mr. Garrett emphasized he feels the Airport will secure another commercial airline
within 18 to 24 months and work must be done to help with that endeavor. Mr. Cooper added that the City has adopted an Airport
Master Plan that includes hosting a commercial airline at the facility; just because there is presently no commercial air service at
the Airport does not mean that directive of the Plan should be abandoned. He would like to continue with operations at the Airport
that have been based upon that Plan.

Continued discussion among the Council and Mr. Garrett centered on characteristics of the Airport that contribute to the
difficulty in securing a commercial carrier at the facility, with Mr. Garrett noting that many of the comments and claims that have
been made by those concerned about the state of the Airport are untrue or incorrect. This included false information regarding
funding sources for the Airport and the impact that the FY2022 Airport budget requests have on the rest of the City budget.

Council member White asked how many currently work at the Airport that are assigned to commercial aviation, to which
Mr. Garrett answered one; she is assigned to security, but also accounting for the facility, and she was employed at the Airport in
advance of the City securing a commercial carrier. Council member White asked if it is correct that the Airport staff did not grow
dramatically upon the location of a commercial airline at the Airport, to which Mr. Garrett answered yes.

Schedule A-1: Deputy Comptroller Sorensen explained changes to the budget included in the Schedule A-1 document
would increase the budget by $1,455,600 and include requests by Council to fund two new positions for sustainability and
recreation, provide grant funding for a water conservation program, fund on-going support for a Mexican cultural exchange
program, true up Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Entitlement program funds, increase Sanitary Sewer operations,
and provide additional funds to complete Fire Station Number Four construction.

The Council engaged in discussion with Administration regarding the basis for each of the amendments included in
Schedule A-1. Council member White suggested a budget amendment for FY2022 relating to the water conservation program,
rather than funding the program in FY2023. Comptroller Stout indicated there is not sufficient time to advertise and hold a public
hearing for a budget amendment prior to the end of the budget year.

Certified Tax Rate Review: Comptroller Stout reported the prior year certified tax rate was .002397 and there was a 19
percent value increase over the course of the past year; given the current taxation formula, Weber County has recommended that
the City’s certified tax rate be lowered to adjust for inflation, which would be a tax rate of .001940. In order to fund the budget
totaling $19,385,525, the tax rate must be increased to .002306, which would be advertised as a tax increase of 18.83 percent. She
spoke to Weber County today and they indicated the value of the average parcel in Ogden City is $400,000, which means that
taxes for the average parcel would increase from $437 to $520 annually. This is $82 per year or just under $7 per month.

There was discussion among the Council and Administration regarding the impact the tax increase will have on
commercial properties versus residential properties. There was also a discussion about historical actions taken by the Council
relative to the City’s certified property tax rate. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith noted that Council staff can place a
property tax calculator on the website to allow individual property owners to determine how the tax increase will impact them
based upon their property value.

Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Budget Amendment — Water Conservation Program

Comptroller Stout explained Administration is proposing an amendment to the FY2022 Budget to recognize and
appropriate funds from General Fund Fund Balance for transfer to the Capital Improvement Plan Fund Water Conservation
project. These funds will be used to implement a program designed to encourage residents to convert park strips into water-wise
plantings. The program is being designed to complement the Weber Morgan Water Conservancy District’s Rip Your Strip
program. Landscaping ordinance amendments and program guidelines are in the final phase of completion and will be considered
by the Council as soon as possible.

Fiscal Year 2021-2022 City Budget Amendment _— WonderBlock
Development; and
Fiscal Year 2021-2022 Redevelopment Agency Budget Amendment —
WonderBlock Development

Administration is proposing an amendment to the FY2022 Budget to recognize revenues from Ogden City General Fund
Fund Balance for transfer to the Redevelopment Agency Continental Project Area to cover pre-development costs for the
WonderBlock project. She discussed the relationship between this budget and the City’s allocation of American Rescue Plan Act
(ARPA) funds; the City was eligible to receive ARPA funding from the State of Utah because of this project and the fact that it
includes a housing element that meets specific housing criteria. However, the ARPA money cannot be used on this project and
will, instead, be dedicated to the Marshall White Center (MWC) project.

Council Business

Fiscal Year 2023 Budget Message: Council Communications and Public Engagement Coordinator Garside presented the
Council Officer’s budget message, which provides an overview of the City’s budget for residents. He moved through the
document, which includes updated information on the budget, including the Council’s Schedule A-1 amendments to the budget.

Discussion among the Council and staff centered on the messaging in the document and the fact that the reader could
interpret the messaging to mean that the entire property tax increase is due to the need to provide compensation increases for City
employees; Council member Choberka noted it is important to communicate that there are many other projects in the budget that
require funding and employee compensation is not the only matter of importance related to the proposed tax increase. The Council
asked for more time to review the document, with Mr. Garside noted that it does not need to be adopted until the August meeting
during with the final budget will be adopted; he invited the Council to provide feedback regarding information included in the
document.

Truth in Taxation Communication Plan: Mr. Garside discussed his ideas for communicating information regarding the
City budget and truth in taxation process to the public; this includes a press release, advertisements on the City website and social
media channels. There was also a brief review of the meeting schedule for meetings in which the final budget and proposed tax
increase will be discussed. The Council and Administration also discussed the number of government entities across the State of
Utah that are considering a property tax increase.

Compensation Increases for Elected Officials: Chair Nadolski reported that Council leadership met with Administration
regarding the concerns that have been expressed regarding elected officials’ salaries and the Mayor has communicated that he
would like for the Council to approve the same increase for him as they plan to approve for Council members. He then facilitated
discussion among the Council regarding the amount of salary increase they are supportive of; the Council concluded they support
basing wages upon accurate benchmark data

Following the compensation discussion, there was dialogue about the manner in which information is shared between
Council leadership and the rest of the Council; some expressed frustration with not having sufficient information upon which to
base a decision, or the manner in which decisions made by a majority of the Council can change based upon communication
outside of a meeting of the entire Council.

The meeting adjourned at 5:51 p.m.
