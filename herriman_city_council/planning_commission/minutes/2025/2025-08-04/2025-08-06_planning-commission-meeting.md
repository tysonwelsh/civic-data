   1.4. Discussion Regarding the Transportation Master Plan Update – Bryce Terry, City Engineer

https://www.herriman.gov/uploads/files/5661/Item-1.4-TMP-Staff-Report.pdf

City Engineer Terry and consultants Jeremy and Ian from WCG presented an update on the
Transportation Master Plan. City Engineer Terry explained they were midway through the process
and seeking Planning Commission feedback before creating a draft plan for formal recommendation.
The presentation included population projections showing a 70% increase in Herriman by 2050,
reaching over 115,000 residents, updated cross-sections for various road types, including major
arterials, minor arterials, and collectors, and future traffic volume projections for 2035 using a
travel demand model. The consultants explained how they use a "no build" versus "build" scenario to
identify needed road improvements and where future capacity issues would occur. The roadway
projects were organized into three phases: Phase 1 (next 10 years), Phase 2 (10-20 years), and Phase
3 (20+ years). Commissioner Jacobson raised concerns about median types and their costs,
suggesting the Planning Commission should influence decisions about road aesthetics.
Commissioner Oberg questioned the proposed road connection from Herriman to Bluffdale (Project
31), noting the significant traffic relief it would provide despite being shown as a Phase 3 project.
Commissioners also identified several problematic traffic areas needing attention, including the
four-way stop on Rosecrest Road, traffic around Providence Hall schools, the Mountain Ridge High
School area, and the intersection at 13400 S and 5600 W. The group discussed potential solutions
including traffic calming measures, improved crosswalks, and potentially a pedestrian bridge in the
Providence Hall area. Terry invited commissioners to review the online story map and provide
additional feedback before the plan's finalization.

   4.1. Review and consider a recommendation for a Master Development Agreement with LTF
        Real Estate Company, Inc. for a commercial development located at 4684 West 12600
        South (Public Hearing) – Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
        Applicant: Nate Walter (authorized agent)
        Acres: ±13.6
        File No: M2025-093

https://www.herriman.gov/uploads/files/5664/Item-4.1-Lifetime-MDA.pdf

Community Development Director Thomas presented the Master Development Agreement (MDA)
for LTF Real Estate Company (Lifetime Fitness). He explained the MDA addressed three major
issues: a landscape berm in lieu of a precast fence along the north property line, permission for a
major corridor sign despite the property being less than the required 20 acres, and building
elevation materials exceptions from city requirements. The landscape berm would include varying
cross-sections to provide buffering from the pool deck and parking areas to the residential
neighborhood. The sign would follow all other requirements in the ordinance but would be
permitted despite the site being under 20 acres. The building material request would reduce the
required percentage of brick and stone and allow for more than 20% stucco.

Dan Beavers, representing Lifetime, explained their prototypical athletic country club would
August 06, 2025 Planning Commission Meeting Minutes                                       Page 4 of 11

include fitness programming, a kids program, Life Cafe, Life Spa, a work lounge, interior pools, and
pickleball courts. He noted they intentionally designed the facility to minimize impacts on
neighboring properties.

Chair Bradford opened the public hearing.

During the public hearing, resident Glenn Larson expressed concerns about the fence along the
park area that teens frequently jump through, suggesting a precast fence would be more
appropriate. He also raised the issue of potential light pollution from the pool area affecting nearby
homes, and noise from outdoor music during summer months. Additionally, he questioned what
might be developed on Lot 2 in the future. Larson noted that the community sees Lifetime as a
good fit overall, indicating support for the development.

Commissioner Jacobson moved to close the public hearing, Seconded by Commissioner Garcia and
all voted Aye.

In response to these concerns, the applicant clarified that all lighting would comply with city
requirements and be focused toward the pool deck rather than neighboring properties. They also
confirmed they would maintain the existing fence along the northern boundary.

Commissioners discussed the fence/berm issue extensively. Blake Thomas explained that installing
precast walls adjacent to existing vinyl fencing has created problems in previous developments
ending up with double fences, which led to the berm proposal with additional setback.

Commissioner Powell moved to forward a positive recommendation of approval to City Council of
item 4.1 Review and consider a recommendation for a Master Development Agreement with LTF
Real Estate Company, Inc. for a commercial development located at 4684 West 12600 South.

Commissioner Oberg seconded the motion.

   5.2. Review and consideration of a Conditional Use permit for the site plan approval for an
        Indoor Recreational use known as Life Time located generally at 4684 W 12600 South in
        the C-2 Commercial Zone.
        Applicant: Nate Walter (authorized agent)
        Acres: ±12.6
        File No:C2025-067

https://www.herriman.gov/uploads/files/5667/Item-5.2-Lifetime-CUP.pdf

Planner Howa presented the site plan for the Lifetime Fitness Center, consisting of a 102,000 square
foot, two-story building with an outdoor pool area and six pickleball courts. The site would have two
access points initially (one off Legacy Boulevard and one shared with Maverick off 12600 South),
with a third ingress planned after the widening of 12600 South. The plan includes 603 parking stalls,
significantly exceeding the required 340 stalls based on the applicant's operational needs. The site
would have 31% landscaping, well above the 8% minimum requirement. The applicant requested
waivers for removing some parking lot islands while providing landscape caps at drive aisles and
removing landscape requirements for a park strip with a utility easement. The building would be 42
feet tall with a 10-foot parapet wall which brings it to 52-foot parapet above the entrance. Materials
would include brick, stone, stucco, metal awnings, and metal accents. Commissioners discussed
potential issues with the public amenity plaza located near the Maverick, with Commissioner Oberg
expressing concerns about potential delinquency in that area. The applicant noted the plaza's
location near their main entrance would provide increased visibility and activity.

Commissioner Jacobson moved to approve item 5.2 Review and consideration of a Conditional
Use permit for the site plan approval for an Indoor Recreational use known as Life Time
located generally at 4684 W 12600 South in the C-2 Commercial Zone. With staff’s
recommendations 1. Address all recommendations and requirements from other agencies. 2.
This approval is contingent on the approval of the pending Master Development Agreement
(MDA). City Code shall be followed unless an exception or deviation is addressed in the MDA
and approved by the City Council. A. All exterior elevations shall have a minimum of sixty
percent (60%) brick or stone, and the stucco finishes shall be reduced to twenty percent (20%)
on each elevation unless otherwise approved in the Master Development Agreement (City File-
M2025-093). B. A six-foot (6’) precast fence shall be installed along the north boundary of the
property adjacent to residential uses unless otherwise approved in the Master Development
Agreement (City File-M2025-093). C. All signs shown on the site plan will require a separate
permit unless otherwise approved in the MDA (City File- M2025-093). 3. Provide elevations for
the trash enclosure. The trash enclosure shall be finished to match the primary structure,
utilizing brick, stone or stucco and the gate shall be fully opaque. 4. Provide a conveniently
located bike rack near the primary structure for customer and employee use. 5. Update the
landscaping plans to include a landscape table. 6. Provide a photometric site plan.
