1. Discussion regarding possible amendments to Chapters 1 and 2 General Plan in 
the Snyderville Basin General Plan- Pat Putt, Community Development Director, 
Jennifer Strader, Senior Planner   
 
Director Putt said that very high on the County Council's work program for 2021 is to 
update the General Plan and the Development Code for each side of the County.  The 
Council would like to take action on these by the end of the year.   
 
Director Putt said the plan is that each meeting will start at 4:30 p.m. with a work 
session.  During that time, they will review a particular element of the General Plan and 
the Development Code.  The County Council is asking the Planning Commission to 
forward recommendations for each document as well as any policy considerations or 
issues the Commission would like them to further review.

Vice-Chair Cooke referred to Policy 2.5.  He asked if the word "discouraged" should be 
changed to "prohibited."  For the things the Commission doesn't want to allow, it would 
be better to use the word "prohibit" than "discouraged". 
 
Director Putt responded that generally the reason "discouraged" is used because there 
may be a situation where the Commission may want to approve or utilize some action 
or provision.  The word "discouraged" is often found in architectural guidelines.  
Obviously, the more precise they are, the better the Code will be.  
 
Attorney Brackin said a lot of language was carried over from the 2004 performance-
based zoning plan.  Not every aspect of the Code was changed.  To the extent that they 
can, they should say that something is "prohibited" or "not allowed."  There are times 
when flexibility is good to have.   
 
Vice-Chair Cooke responded that any time something is prohibited an applicant can go 
to the Board of Adjustment to seek a variance.  They have been instructed by the County 
Council to tighten up the language.  The Commission shouldn't become the entity that 
monitors style; however, they do want to follow the County Council's direction to clean 
up the language.  They have encountered applications where the word "prohibited" 
would have been preferred.   
 
Attorney Brackin said the Commission has broad discretion with what they can do in 
the Code and the vision of the community.  If they don't want to have a certain style of 
homes, they are free to say that.  For example, if they want all structures to be two 
stories, they could make that a requirement.  They are free to create a vision of what 
their community is going to look like and the Code that will get them to that vision.

Planner Robinson said this is a re-division of the Residences at Blackstone Units 1 and 
2, which were original units.  A background of the project was given.  The townhomes 
were approved in 2014.  The owner who acquired the two units, wanted to combine 
them into one 6-bedroom unit instead of the planned two 3-bedroom units.  They went 
through the needed process for this to happen in 2016.  It was approved and recorded.   
 
Planner Robinson said the owner would now like to re-divide the units to have two 3-
bedroom units in order to sell them individually.  The current configuration has proved 
to be difficult to sell.  Planner Robinson said this does not increase any density.  It will 
not cause harm to any property owner.  Staff recommends that the Planning 
Commission consider the application and send a positive recommendation to the 
County Manager for the Residences at Blackstone Units 1 and 2.

Vice-Chair Cooke said this item for discussion is looking at all of the commercial zones 
in the Snyderville Basin.  Planner Milliner gave a brief history of the suggested 
amendments.  He said there are three zones they are looking at.  All are Commercial 
zones.  One of the major changes is that the uses have been moved to the beginning of 
the chapter.  The Use Table will be deleted.  The changes have been highlighted in 
yellow.  The reason for the changes is to make the document more user-friendly.   He 
went through the suggested changes, along with the reason for the alterations.

Commissioner Fine asked if the suggested increase in height of three feet will impact 
any ridgelines located within these zones.  Planner Milliner said that currently they 
don't have a lot of ridgelining in any of the commercial zones.  He thinks the impact 
would be minimal.     
 
Planner Milliner said that in the neighborhood commercial zone there is a 5,000 square-
foot maximum.  It is proposed that number be eliminated and replaced with a 5-acre 
maximum area.  Staff believes this will allow the opportunity for something greater 
than 5,000 square feet, but something that would still be compatible with the 
neighborhood.  Many houses are larger than 5,000 square feet.  He added that 5 acres 
would not be enough for a big box store.   
 
Planner Milliner said the 20,000 square foot maximum would be eliminated in the 
Community Commercial zone.  In its place, they would rely on the master plan 
development process.  Vice-Chair Cooke said that having the 20,000 square foot 
maximum seems to be doing what it was intended to do.   
 
Commissioner Simons said that under the right circumstances, she thinks a big box 
store would be acceptable.  Vice-Chair Cooke said he worries if this limitation is 
removed, they may not get a community benefit with the project.  Planner Milliner 
responded that any structure greater than 10,000 square feet would be required to go 
through the master development process.   
 
Commissioner Conabee said he thinks the Development Code should specify if the 
goal is to prohibit big box stores.  Vice-Chair Cooke suggested this is put into basket for 
review after the NMU 1 zone is finalized.  It would be good to see how all of the 
commercial zones fit together.
