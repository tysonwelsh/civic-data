3.2) Public hearing and consideration of Diane and Robert Myers’ request for conditional
use approval of an 8-foot fence located at 2295 North 600 West.
Jacob Curtis presented information on the agenda item.

The applicant was not present.

Commissioner Newall opened the public hearing.

Kailynne Osbourne read a comment that will be submitted to the public record from James A
cox. She stated she is referencing the flag lot request that states the Planning Commission can
authorize an 8-foot fence and would like it to meet the standards. She is asking the Planning
Commission to grant the request. She stated it will only have positive results for each boundary
and property. She clarified this will help with privacy, and balance, and help mitigate living on a
corner lot. Since the property owners have been granted an ADU, there is higher traffic, parking,
and no foliage. She is in support of the fence. She stated the existing chain link fence is 6 feet, 8
feet, and then 5 feet and is not visually appealing. She would like a full 8-foot fence along the
property line. She stated an 8-foot, solid fence would be consistent and visually appealing. She
would like to see if they would be allowed conditional approval and the property owners can
decide if they will use it or not. The flag lot narrative that she requested a year ago read that she
emphasized privacy on the south lot line because it is consistent and visually appealing.

Commissioner Newall stated there was an online public comment that was in favor of the item.

Michelle Stallings mentioned by approving the flag lot, it seems kind of the neighbors to respect
them by installing the 8-foot fence. By asking for the conditional use and the neighbors offering
to pay for the entire 8-foot fence, that is awfully kind of them to do.


Commissioner Newall closed the public hearing.

Diane Myers, the applicant, was present. She stated if they can afford the 8-foot fence, they
would like to install the fence. They would like to get enough space to not obstruct the view
when they are pulling onto the street. The cost of an 8-foot fence is expensive, but if they are
allowed to have a conditional use, they would like that. The commissioners stated if the
neighbors would like to share the cost, it gives the neighbors the opportunity to do so.
Commissioner Jackson stated if they would like to keep the fence, they can keep the fence if it is
installed properly and not infringing someone's property rights.

        Motion: Commissioner Jackson moved to recommend approval of Diane and Robert
        Myers’ request for conditional use approval of an 8-foot fence located at 2295 North
        600 West; including all DRC comments and findings stated in the report. Commissioner
        Carlson seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Newall, yes; Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
        Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Carlson, yes. The motion passed
        unanimously.

3.5) Public hearing and consideration of Nathaniel Wall’s request for approval of the Alpine
Bible Church Concept located at 710 West Main Street, including an exception for exceeding
the 25-foot maximum setback in the Urban Village Design District.
Jacob Curtis presented information on the agenda item.

The applicant, Nathaniel Wall, was present.

Commissioner Newall opened the public hearing.

Mac Smith stated he is concerned with the route of SR-73 and the traffic. The assisted living area
was a concern because of the traffic and the auto sales area blocks traffic where they unload cars.
His proposal has 100 parking spaces and will not allow traffic to get in or out without a separate
entrance. The assisted living and auto parts are not in a good area, and they did not accommodate
traffic or 100 parking spaces. The entrance and exit onto SR-73 are not a good idea. He would
like some thought put into getting in and out of the development.

Velda Buchanan is concerned about the entrance and exit. The car lot near this request unloads
vehicles onto Main Street and creates safety issues. Her concern is there is a lot of uncertainties
about what this will be. She would like clarification on what the proposal will be. She would like
to know how much traffic this will bring. She also has questions about the fence height where
her backyard will be about to the property line. The car lot lights stay on all night and she is
concerned about the lighting with the request. The access is too congested to have that much
traffic flow.

Commissioner Newall closed the public hearing.

The applicant stated the reason he is here is for the setback. If he can get some leniency on the
setback, he can move forward. He is happy to work with the neighbors and be a good steward to
the community. Commissioner Eyre asked how far of a setback. The applicant would like as far
of a setback as possible to put space between the building and Main Street. Mike West stated this
would be a supported exception due to the location of the building being in the Urban Village
Design District and being so close to Main Street. The commissioners, applicant, and staff
discussed the exception for the setback at length.

        Motion: Commissioner Bunker moved to approve Nathaniel Wall’s request for
        approval of the Alpine Bible Church Concept located at 710 West Main Street,
        including an exception for exceeding the 25-foot maximum setback in the Urban
        Village Design District; including all DRC comments and findings stated in the
        report. Noting the exception for the setback. The setback is warranted because of traffic
        on Main Street on Sundays, people coming in and out of the building, and the safety of
        pedestrians. Also, the depth and size of the property and the comparative of buildings and
        similar situations, recommendation of 150 feet or aesthetically appealing or the property
        to the east. Commissioner Jackson seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Newall, yes; Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
        Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Carlson, yes. The motion passed
        unanimously.

3.7) Public hearing and recommendation of Jim Bradshaw’s request for review of the
Griffith Estates Zone Change from A-5 (agriculture) to R-1-22 (residential/agriculture) on
3.43-acres located at 300 East 1630 South.

3.8) Public hearing and recommendation of Jim Bradshaw’s request for review of the
Griffith Estates Concept, a 9-unit townhome PUD (Planned Unit Development) subdivision
located at 300 East 1630 South
Agenda items 3.7 and 3.8 were heard simultaneously.

Jacob Curtis presented information on agenda item 3.7

The applicant, Steve Potter, and Jim Bradshaw, were present.

Commissioner Newall opened the public hearing for 3.7

Teresa Mott stated she has some concerns. They have been having problems with flooding with
the irrigation ditch that floods the property. There is a ditch that drains into Utah Lake and she
would like to know if the developer will maintain that ditch or if will it flood their property.
Once the property is developed, their crops will be shaded which will affect their hay. She asked
about the fence line because they have cows on the property. If they bring in fill dirt, will the
runoff flood their property? She is concerned with the children from the development getting
around the cows and the ponds or being forced out of the property. She is concerned about being
forced out of the community. They have farm equipment on the farm. In the proposal, they are
exchanging property for nine condos. The city is going to have to maintain the property and she
doesn't feel that is a fair trade because the property is unusable. She is concerned about the
development affecting their property.

Paul Kolan is concerned about the ditch. He stated Greg Allred put in a new pipe because
everyone knows the ditch floods and is fast moving. They are supposed to have 10 acres to put
this stuff in and donate the rest to the City. The property that is being donated is trash property.
When the lake rises, the new development will be underwater. He is concerned about the kids
getting into the ditches and equipment and getting hurt. He stated they will put up no trespassing
signs but would like a fence built. The land being donated is worthless.

Grant Kolan stated when the dirt is dug out, the water runs through the dirt like a sponge with
carpet on top of it. The sewer line was run across the bottom of the field. Now when water runs
out, the water hits the line like a dam and it is a pond. The water filters through the dirt and he
knows if they dig it out and fill it in, their property will get all of the water and will flood. They
are fighting it as it is.

Chance Kolan wanted to add with the PUD it had to be 10 acres and the whole property is 7.3
acres and they only want to rezone part of it. This development is going right over the ditch
where there are flooding issues. He said it is like a floating carpet. He thinks it will be like trying
to keep a building afloat.

Commissioner Newall closed the public hearing for 3.7

Commissioner Newall wanted to clarify this was for a zone change.
Commissioner Bunker addressed drainage, fencing, the height of the buildings, and the property
line.

        Motion: Commissioner Jackson moved to hear 3.7 and 3.8 simultaneously.
        Commissioner Bunker seconded. All in favor.

Jacob Curtis presented information on agenda item 3.8

The applicant, Steve Potter, and Jim Bradshaw, presented information on the item. There are
power lines that are above the property that is dedicated to the City for the Pony Express Trail.
They have dedicated the top and bottom portions for the trailway extension and will provide the
best use of the property. The ditch will be rerouted to the canal that the City maintains. There
will be a fence included in the plat which is a requirement.

Commissioner Newall opened the public hearing for 3.8

Grant Kolan stated the only outlet of the irrigation pond is at the bottom of their field and will
flood their property and sit like a pond.

Teresa Mott stated she doesn't think the height won't affect the crops. If he builds it and affects
their crops they are in trouble. They would like some type of study done so they aren't impacted.
They would like to know for sure.

Commissioner Newall closed the public hearing for 3.8

Steve Potter stated there was a question about the soil and will do a soil study.

Commissioner Eyre asked if it wasn't a PUD how many units would be allowed. Jacob stated
possibly 7 total single-family lots if the zone change stays the same. Ross Dinsdale stated it can
be allowed. The base density for the project is based on the flood plain. Any irrigation ditches
require approval from the owner and will have to work with them. Greg Allred has been in the
area and is aware of this. Fencing requirements have to be met. Geo-tech investigations have to
be done. Lehi City and American Fork have a plan to connect 1900 South and connect to
American Fork to help alleviate traffic to Pioneer Crossing (Pony Express Parkway Proposed
Project). The power lines go through the North end of the property. Mike stated the Utah Lake
Shoreline overlay includes a cluster development. The property that is donated will be native and
unusable.

        Motion 3.7: Commissioner Carlson moved to recommend approval of Jim Bradshaw’s
        request for review of the Griffith Estates Zone Change from A-5 (agriculture) to R-
        1-22 (residential/agriculture) on 3.43-acres located at 300 East 1630 South; including
        all DRC comments and findings stated in the report. Commissioner Jackson seconded.

        Vote: Commissioner Newall, yes; Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
        Commissioner Bunker, yes; Commissioner Carlson, yes. The motion passed
        unanimously.
