Public Hearing

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following requests:

Application #30889 – Wright Development Group is requesting approval of a zone change from
R-M/zc (residential) to C-2/zc (commercial) on 0.93 acres of property located at 3486 South 8400
West.

                Diana Martinez, Salt Lake County Planning and Development Services Division,
reviewed the application stating Wright Development Group is planning to tear down an office
building that is no longer in use and put in a 7-11 convenience store. Currently, there are four
zoning conditions on the property. Planning and Development staff are recommending the
following two conditions be maintained: Limit building square footage to 5,000 square feet, and
limit the height of future buildings to 1 story and 25 feet to the midpoint of the roof. The Planning
Commission recommended approval of the request with those conditions.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to open the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

               Logan Johnson, representative, Wright Development Group, stated Wright
Development wants the re-zone before incurring costs on a site plan. If approved, it would begin
production in a couple of weeks and possibly start construction early next year.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to close the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

Reports from Municipal Service Providers

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD)

               Ms. Pam Roberts, Director, Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District
(WFWRD), reviewed the recycling program stating WFWRD collects the recycling and takes it to
two processing facilities in the valley – Rocky Mountain Recycling and Waste Management.
These two facilities sort the recycled materials and sell them on the market. This year, it is costing
vendors about $100 per ton to have recycling processed, although that can be reduced depending
on how clean the recycling is and the commodities in there, such as metal. This year, WFWRD
is paying about $60 a ton for recycling; last year, it paid about $50. WFWRD pays $32.85 for
garbage. Annually, it is costing WFWRD about $1.4 million to recycle, and $3.3 million for
garbage. The life expectancy of the Salt Lake Valley Landfill is about 60 to 70 years, but that will
be shortened if the recycling program is discontinued. Also, if the program is discontinued, there
will be an added cost for garbage. The increase for homeowners would be $0.75 per month. The
increase to homeowners for recycling would be $1.50 per month.

WFWRD sent out a 2019 districtwide random survey, with a very detailed explanation of the costs
of recycling and garbage, to see how consumers felt. She reviewed the following results from the
survey, which 6,000 people responded to, 284 of whom were from Magna:

    79 percent districtwide supported continuance of recycling services over the materials
     going into the landfill, and 63.7 percent of Magna respondents supported recycling
     services
    79.1 percent districtwide supported the $1.50 increase for recycling over the $0.75 percent
     increase to have materials go to the landfill, and 63 percent of Magna respondents
     supported the $1.50 increase for recycling, which was a 12 percent increase from a 2018
     survey result that increased the fee to $1.00
    74.4 percent districtwide supported the continuation of weekly recycling collection rather
     than pulling back to bi-weekly collection

As a result of the survey, she will be asking WFWRD’s Governing Board to continue the recycling
program, with the anticipation the board will have to implement a fee increase in 2021. Besides
the cost for recycling, WFWRD is paying its employees below market, so will have to increase
wages to remain competitive. WFWRD is also in the process of hiring a new sustainability
coordinator who will be part of a sustainability recycling green committee to help educate about
responsible recycling.

Unified Fire Authority

                Council Member Prokopis stated the Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA) will be
staring its budget meetings soon, as it is on a calendar year budget cycle. It is planning to
construct six or seven new stations, five of which will be rebuilds, and will start identifying funding,
working on architecture and design, and securing the property. There will probably be a bond to
fund that next year. The UFA has completed its entry level testing for applicants seeking to
become a firefighter. Over 650 applicants showed up for the written exam, which is twice as many
as last year as a result of more publicity. The top 93 applicants will be interviewing next month.
Then, the agility test and fitness test will be early October. The UFA is looking to hire sometime
in February. The UFA has also been doing an outreach program to get more women in fire
service. It has had several open houses and an event at the training tower where attendees were
shown what the job entailed. Another training session is coming up and will be held in Millcreek.
Right now, there are approximately 400 sworn firefighters and only 12 of them are female. That
is lower than the national average.

             Council Member Hull stated the UFA is also working on hiring two emergency
preparedness people. Then, a firefighter from the Magna Metro Township received an award at
the awards banquet.

Resolution 19-09-01

               Greg Schulz reviewed Resolution 19-09-01 amending Policy #2 – Purchasing,
Contracting, and Procurement, which adds a section to include the use of approved vendors for
the purchase of products and services. This would allow the Magna Metro Township to use the
vendors the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District have approved for its uses. It would
enable the Magna Metro Township to get subdivisions and permitted uses moving faster.

                 Council Member Pierce stated it is not uncommon for jurisdictions to piggyback
off other jurisdictions’ contracts. However, sometimes a contract is very specific to the service or
type of service, so even though a vendor is approved, the details in their contract could restrict
the Magna Metro Township’s project.

               Paul Ashton stated it is doubtful the Council will use this very often, but it is a tool
in case it needs it.

       A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL AMENDING
       POLICY #2 – PURCHASING, CONTRACTING, AND PROCUREMENT ADDING
       AN ARTICLE TO THE POLICY TO INCLUDE THE USE OF APPROVED
       VENDORS FOR THE PURCHASE OF PRODUCTS AND SERVICES

                                         RECITALS

   A. The Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) is a municipality pursuant to Utah Code §§ 10-2a-
      401 et seq.

   B. The Magna Metro Township Council (the “Council”) is the municipal legislative body for
      Magna pursuant to Utah Code § 10-3b-501.

   C. On January 9, 2018 the Council adopted Ordinance 18-01-01 Creating Policy #2 –
      Purchasing, Contracting and Procurement (“Policy #2”) to provide a process for Magna to
      procure goods and services for the operation of the municipality.

   D. During the Council meeting of August 13, 2019 it was directed by the Council that Policy
      #2 be amended to add a section to allow the Purchasing Agent and Council to procure
      goods and services using the Approved Vendor Lists maintained by the State of Utah and
      Salt Lake County, respectively.

   E. During the development of the Policy #2 amendments, it was discovered the Magna Water
      Improvement District Approved Vendors List also provided a valuable resource for the
      Municipality to procure services.

   F. Using Approved Vendors can make Magna’s procurement of goods and services effective
      and efficient; and save the municipality money.

                                       RESOLUTION

        THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, the Magna Metro Township Council hereby approves
the attached amendment to Policy #2, hereinafter to be known as Exhibit A, to allow the use of
Approved Vendors for the provision of goods and services for the municipality.
