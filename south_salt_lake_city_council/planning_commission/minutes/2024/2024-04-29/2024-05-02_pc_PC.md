1.      PUBLIC MEETING – A Petition for Approval of a Conditional Use Permit for a 218-
        unit Multi-Family Dwelling Development, located at 2252 South 300 West.

ACTION ITEM

Applicant:     North 300 West, LLC Represented by Kris Longson
Address:       2252 South 300 West

Planning Consultant, Alexandra Halleck, presented the Staff Report and stated that the request is
for a Conditional Use Permit (“CUP”) Application for North 300 West located at 2252 South 300
West. The application has been before the Commission a number of times but tonight it is a Petition
for a CUP for a 218-unit multi-family dwelling. The applicants are looking to develop the project
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                   3
May 2, 2024

in two phases consisting of Lot 1 of the Times Square Subdivision. The area is zoned Downtown
District where multi-family requires a CUP.

The four-step application process was described. Step 1 involved an ordinance amendment that
was reviewed and approved by the Planning Commission and the City Council in June and July of
2023. The matter went before the South Salt Lake Design Review Committee in August of 2023.
The Planning Commission approved a Preliminary Plat in September of 2023 and a Final Plat in
January of 2024. They are now on the fourth and final step for the CUP.

The property was identified on a map displayed. The subject parcel is currently vacant, as the
existing office building was demolished in 2021. As part of that demolition, the developer left the
existing parking lots, curb, gutter, and sidewalk, which will be addressed through the subdivision
process. The Phasing Plan was addressed. Ms. Halleck reported that Phase 1 includes the
easternmost portion of the proposed building along 300 West where there will be a lobby, the main
office, a portion of the future parking structure underneath on Level 1, two brownstone units, and
apartments above. The applicants are also installing a temporary parking lot. Phase 1 includes 64
studio and one-bedroom apartments, 9 two-bedroom apartments, and 76 temporary surface parking
stalls. Phase 2 includes a two-level structured podium parking structure that attaches to Phase 1. It
also includes 119 studio and one-bedroom apartments, 26 two-bedroom apartments, and 182
structured parking stalls.

All of the infrastructure along Bugatti Avenue, Parley’s Trail, and 300 West will be designed and
implemented as part of Phase 1. The only difference is that the infrastructure is internal to the lot
where the parking structure will be located during Phase 2. The issue of how to address parking
for Phase 1 while Phase 2 is under construction was addressed. Ms. Halleck explained that the
developer has set aside multiple other properties in the vicinity directly adjacent to this parcel.
They consist of existing parking and parking around existing structures. 151 stalls have been set
aside. The matter was reviewed by the Legal Department who felt that if the CUP is approved, it
will restrict those parcels and prevent them from being developed or used for some other purpose.

The Phase 1 elevations were presented. Ms. Halleck reported that all of the south, north, and east
facades will look the same as at full build-out. What will look different in Phase 1 is where the
parking structure connects. The applicants will be required to go through a permit process with the
Building Department and the Fire Marshall to certify that it is safe for occupancy on one side while
construction takes place on the other.

The Phase 2 elevations were at full build-out. Renderings of each building facade were displayed.
The building is modern with the leasing office and a small portion of the amenities facing Bugatti
Avenue and 300 West. Brownstone apartments will face both Bugatti Avenue and Parley’s Trail.
There is a large podium deck on top of the parking structure with amenities. Phases 1 and 2 will
include Electric Vehicle (“EV”) parking stalls, visitor bicycle parking, secured bicycle parking, a
plaza for public art (to be approved by the South Salt Lake Community Development Department
and the Arts Council), 40% community open space throughout the project, washers and dryers in
every unit, on-site management, upgraded floor coverings, on-site recycling, and water-efficient
fixtures. Phase 1 includes a main-level lobby and social area, a mezzanine game room and social
area, a designated mail and package room, a fitness center, dog wash stations, conference rooms,
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Meeting                                                  4
May 2, 2024

tenant storage units, a ground-level community Dog Park, BBQ areas, a temporary pickleball
court, and a temporary playground. The temporary parking to be located in the BBQ area will be
converted when the second-level amenity space is updated. Phase 2 includes a pool and hot tub
area with a large outdoor lounge and cabana area, outdoor dining and seating, a new BBQ on top
of the podium, fire pits, steam rooms, a club-level locker room for the pool area, secured bike
storage, and a secured and enclosed two-level parking structure. Ms. Halleck stated that the
developers are proposing numerous amenities and the request complies with the Downtown
ordinance.

The Phase 1 Site Plan was presented. It includes the temporary parking in the center of the project.
The developers are proposing a temporary playground, BBQ, and pickleball court along Parley’s
Trail, street trees, a large new plaza, a Dog Park, and connections to Parley’s Trail along Phase 1.
The parking structure will be expanded as part of Phase 2. One of the new additions includes a
new fire lane hammerhead that will serve the back of the building. It will also serve as a Utah
Transit Authority (“UTA”)/Rocky Mountain Power transformer substation. The brownstone units
on the ground floor will have front doors leading out to Parley’s Trail as well as front doors along
Bugatti Avenue.

Ms. Halleck commented that several things in the General Plan support this project and referenced
the following:

   •   Land Use & Neighborhoods Goal 1 – Continue to welcome new residents and businesses
       into South Salt Lake.

   •   Land Use & Neighborhoods Goal 2 – Concentrate higher density development near
       transportation and transit in mixed-use neighborhoods.

   •   Land Use & Neighborhoods Goal 3 – Enhance urban and streetscape design in the city to
       support South Salt Lake’s distinct image and enhance community pride. Ms. Halleck stated
       significant streetscape infrastructure is proposed along 300 West and Bugatti Avenue.
       There has been a lot of discussion about how to interface the project with Parley’s Trail as
       well.

   •   Environmental, Social, and Economic Sustainability Goal 1 – Reduce vehicle miles
       traveled and improve air quality. The project is adjacent to the TRAX station and is on
       Parley’s Trail. The applicants plan to utilize the positioning of this project in relation to
       those two facilities.

In terms of the Conditional Use Review Standard, the request complies and has to be reviewed
with the Utah Code as well as the Conditional Use Standard Section of the South Salt Lake City
Code. The project was also reviewed by the Design Review Committee, which identified the
following:

   •   The amenities and ensuring that the project meets all of the requirements for amenities,
       which it does.

   •   Parking to ensure that there is enough parking on site. The developer is petitioning for a
       parking rate reduction of 20%, which is allowed per the Code. The parking numbers
       presented comply with the 20% reduction based on the amenities provided on-site.
   •   Parley’s Trail and public sphere interface. Because it is a three-sided project, there should

Staff has been reviewing the project for some time and found that it complies with the ordinance.
A draft CUP was provided that has been legally vetted. Staff recommended that the Planning
Commission approve the CUP subject to the findings, conditions, and conclusions of law
enumerated in the Staff Report. The Planning Commission has the option to approve, deny, or
continue the matter.

Commissioner Ewell did not realize that the first building was split between two phases.
Ms. Halleck clarified that the Preliminary and Final Plat that the Planning Commission reviewed
in September and January, was for multiple lots. The development only covers one lot in the
subdivision, which is Lot 1. Lot 1 contains two phases.

With regard to phasing, Condition #18 requires that construction of Phase 1 commence within two
years of the date of the approval or the CUP will expire and no further construction or occupancy
may commence without a newly issued CUP. Construction of Phase 2 must commence within four
years of the date of the approval. It was clarified that the CUP covers both phases.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Slifka, Ms. Halleck explained that the applicants
have two years to start Phase 1 and four years to start Phase 2. In order to start Phase 2, Phase 1
will have to be constructed. Ms. Ungricht stated that the developer will have to start Phase 2 within
four years but it would not have to be completed within that time. Ms. Halleck explained that
“unbundled parking” means that the parking is not necessarily assigned to a specific unit. She
stated that most people will pay for parking for the sake of convenience and parking is factored
into the leasing cost. The potential for on-street parking was also discussed.

The applicant, Kris Longson was present representing BLOX Ventures. He stated that they meet
the parking requirements. People tend to want to park by their unit and there is adequate parking
to allow for that. Sometimes people have extra cars in which case they will have to park on the
street or in a designated public parking area. He reiterated that they have adequate parking for all
of their residents.

Mr. Longson reported that the project is being done in two phases. He reported that projects are
being shut down because they are not financeable. This project, however, is in an opportunity zone
and the owner has funds to build the first phase with a small financing package. The project is set
up with the intent to build the full project. The developers are going to great expense to build a
portion of the parking deck that is required to complete Phase 1. All of the core features of the

building, other than the amenity deck, are being built in Phase 1. They chose to place Phase 1 on
300 West so that it has frontage and amenities. They considered that the best solution to move
forward now. Phase 2 includes nearly all of the units other than what is on the podium. They will
be able to capitalize on the improvements that are to be built first.

Commissioner Spencer asked if all of the apartments will be market-rate units. Mr. Longson
confirmed that they will be. They are in the Housing and Transit Reinvestment Zone (“HTRZ”)
and they are talking about utilizing those funds for this phase or the next. If they do, they will meet
the affordable requirements that have been put in place by the City.

Commissioner Ewell asked how the parking structure will block off the parking structure to ensure
safety. Mr. Longson stated that there will be a solid wall there with a mural.

Commissioner Slifka asked what would happen if there are issues with the financing for Phase 2.
Mr. Longson stated that if it never becomes affordable, they will not build it and lose the CUP for
Phase 2. He stated that they cannot predict the market and hope it will change. They have the
incentive to complete the project but it has to be economical in order to move forward.

Commissioner Bellina asked about the traffic signal near RC Willey and if the sensitivity will be
adjusted. Ms. Ungricht stated that it is at the intersection near the entrance to the project. She
indicated that in the Traffic Study might not have accounted for a change in the sensitivity of the
traffic signal. They are currently trying to figure out the best traffic flow for 300 West and ways
to move traffic more efficiently.

Commissioner Slifka was concerned additional pedestrian traffic could also create slowdowns
since currently there is not a lot of foot traffic. Ms. Ungricht stated that at the intersection where
TRAX crosses, there are multiple agencies to deal with including the Utah Department of
Transportation (“UDOT”), UTA, and the City of South Salt Lake coordinating that. They have
been in discussions but it is a slow process. Currently, the only place one can cross TRAX safely
is on Haven Lane near RC Willey. The strip of land that is being landscaped will have a wider
sidewalk that will feed people into Parley’s Trail. If pedestrians follow that flow of traffic, they
should be able to walk on the large sidewalk strip and connect to Parley’s Trail.
