Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) Modifications: UDOT Funds - US-89 Provo Modification Request - Bob Allen UDOT Region 3 has a pavement reconstruction project PIN 11897 on US-89 in Provo that is funded for $10.3M. Provo would like to add raised medians along this section of roadway. UDOT would like to: • Add $2.3M HSIP (Federal Safety) funds to this project paid directly to UDOT. • UDOT will add $7.7M pavement and reconstruction funds to extend the limits of the project further south in order to alleviate concerns from Provo City about tying into a drainage system that is over capacity. • Provo will add $1.5M funds for their share of the drainage, lighting, sewer, and to extend the drainage system. The new project value for the extended project with Provo City betterments will be approximately $21.8M. Mayor Frost moved that the TIP be modified to extend the US-89 project in Provo and to add $11.5M in funding as presented. Mayor Fugal seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. TIP Project Selection -$40M Project List Finalization - Bob Allen The MPO funds projects in the TIP every two years. The Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) has reviewed each submitted concept report and created a ranked project list following the process established by the Regional Planning Committee (RPC). That process included: Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee June 7, 2018 Page 4 • Reviewing submitted concept reports • MPO staff scoring each project • Visiting each project for a field review • Voting to create the ranked priority list of projects Projects are funded by federal funds (CMAQ and TAP), and local funds (County Funds and MAG Exchange). County funds generate an estimated $9-10M annually. MAG Exchange is a process in which MAG exchanges federal funds (STP) with UDOT for state funds to streamline the federal environmental process, Buy America, Davis Bacon Wages and UDOT structures. The TIP Project Selection is currently in the final end of the concept report. At this time in the process, RPC must approve a final ranked list. MPO staff will then create a funding scenario according to the list for RPC approval August 2, 2018. Federal Highways has contributed approximately $1.8M in additional funds, which will allow for Eagle Mountain Pony Express Parkway Phase 3 (project # 18) to be completed. Approximately $41.7M will be allocated to the projects in 2021 and 2022. Mayor Brunst asked if there is an alternative to Lehi Club House Drive (project #15). Mayor Fullmer asked that if Lehi doesn’t go through with their project, will the projects below it receive that funding. Bob Allen said they would. Commissioner Lee expressed concerns regarding MAG Zero Fare BRT (project #11). He stated that a commission studied the BRT several years ago and determined the projected ridership would be 14K people per day on that line with a cost in place; there is now a proposal for zero fare with projected ridership increasing to 21K. He made the recommendation to test the model again to make sure the study is correct, and if it is charge a fare, and keep the ridership at the 14K. He is concerned that not charging a fare will create an entitlement. Commissioner Lee requested that the same consideration be made for the MAG Zero Fare BRT that is being made for Lehi Club House Drive. He would like to organize town hall meetings to discuss the realities of the projected ridership numbers and to get the publics input. Mayor Fullmer expressed concern over the ranking of the Vineyard Utah Lakeshore Trail (project #29). Corridor Preservation Funds Application - Provo City - Jim Price Provo City has made application for Local Option Corridor Preservation Funds for the purchase of a residence at 740 West 800 North, Provo. The property is located within the 820 North Corridor, #19 on the Eligible Corridors Map, and within the first phase of the Regional Transportation Plan. The property lies within the city’s preferred alignment for the future project. The homeowner approached the city about the purchase and has certified that they are a willing seller. The residence has appraised for $224,500. Including anticipated closing costs and appraisal fees of $7500, the total request is for $232,000. Upon approval, the remaining available Corridor Preservation Funds would be $6,686,000. Mayor Pray asked what the $7500 of acquisition costs consists of. Jim Price stated that it is for closing costs and appraisal fees, which is an upper value of what they are requesting. Mayor Pray asked if the city negotiates the closing costs as would take place in a typical purchase, and recommended that those costs be negotiated and divided. Jim Price said that the Local Corridor Preservation Funds process is following Provo City’s acquisition process. The proposed purchase complies with established policies and practices and is a proper use of the Local Option Corridor Preservation Fund. Staff recommends the application be approved. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee June 7, 2018 Page 6 Commissioner Lee moved that the Regional Planning Committee approve the Provo City Corridor Preservation application as submitted, with a one-year sunset from date of approval, and with the recommendation that Provo City try to reduce the acquisition cost as much as possible. Mayor Frost seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Although Utah only violates the EPA’s standard 5-10 days a year, the effect is a region-wide feeling of confinement. The mountains, valleys, and wintertime weather mean inversions will always be an issue to some degree, but limiting how much emissions are emitted on those days can make a difference. With the introduction of Tier 3 fuels and vehicles, as well as improvements to transit and trails, vehicular emissions are expected to be cut in half in the next 10 years, even as population increases. Soon Area Sources (homes and small businesses) will be the biggest polluters. Provo Clean Air tool kit provides options individuals and businesses for how to improve air quality.
