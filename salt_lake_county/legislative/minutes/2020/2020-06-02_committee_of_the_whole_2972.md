Mayor Jennifer Wilson  updated the Council on the County ’s involvement in the protest 
over the death of George Floyd, who was killed by police in Minneapolis,  on Saturday, 
May 30, 2020.  The Emergency Management team assembled at the command center to 
monitor the event in case it accelerated.  
Sheriff Rosie Rivera  stated there is mutual aid agreement between law enforcement 
agencies that when one agency needs help, other agencies will be there.  The Sheriff ’s 
Office was notified Saturday at 6:00 a.m. that officers may be needed, and when the Salt 
Lake City Police Department asked for help, police officers, corrections officers, and 
Public Safety Bureau officers all showed up in masses.  One of the challenges with this 
continuing to go on, is that agencies have to work within their own budgets.  There is no 
cost charged for mutual aid.   
Chief Matt Dumont , Salt Lake County Jail, stated he was notified Saturday morning that 
the scheduled peaceful protest could turn violent.  Throughout the day, members of his 
command were gearing up and making sure resources were in line anticipating a lot of 
people would be booked into jail.  The Davis County Jail stepped up and booked 34 
people into jail there.  Then, Salt Lake County booked 7 people before things started to 
slow down.  Security will also continue at the Oxbow Jail and surrounding buildings.   
Council Member Newton  asked how the Sheriff ’s Office and UPD were going to 
proceed in the future to address people’s concerns.
Sheriff Rivera  stated the Sheriff ’s Office and UPD are putting a list of the training being

Presenters: Mayor Jenny Wilson, Jill Miller, Associate Deputy Mayor; Clint 
Mecham, Salt Lake County Emergency Manager; Gary Edwards, Director, 
Health Department (Approx. 30 mins). 
Discussion/Direction
Mr. Gary Edwards , Director, Salt Lake County Health Department, 
reviewed the statewide statistics relating to COVID -19, stating there were 583 
cases the week of May 24-30, compared to 597 cases the week of May 
17-23.  Then, there were 11,524 tests done in Salt Lake County the week of 
May 24-30, and 11,022 the week of May 17-23.  There are hotspots in Salt 
Lake County and increases in other communities.     
Mr. David Schuld , Intelligence Section Chief for COVID -19, delivered a 
PowerPoint presentation showing a snapshot of the trends for the total 
number of cases, active cases, COVID -related facilities in Salt Lake County, 
recovered cases, tests in Salt Lake County, the hospitalization rate, the 
number currently hospitalized, case rate increases, doubling rate, and the 
positivity rate.  He also displayed charts on the daily confirmed cases in Salt 
Lake County, the rolling seven -day confirmed case count, the rolling 
seven-day positivity rate, and an estimated active and recovered case count 
in Salt Lake County.  
Council Member Newton  asked if cases were expected to increase having 
gone to a yellow phase.

Officer; Rod Kitchens, Director of Budget and Planning, Mayor's Finance; Bob

Discussion/Direction
Mayor Wilson  stated the 2020 proposed June budget has been tricky with 
the added effects of COVID -related expenses.  Once the County gets out of 
COVID crisis, the budget recovery should be swift.    
Mr. Doug Macdonald , Econowest Associates, presented a PowerPoint 
reviewing the economic overview showing the October 2019 projection for 
the 2020 taxable sales and sales forecast; world real growth declines; U .S. 
gross domestic product (GDP) declines; consumer confidence falling, U .S. 
business equipment and software falling, U .S. motor vehicle spending 
dropping; what could go wrong if IHS Markit Ltd ’s pessimistic scenario 
played out; Utah seasonally adjusted unemployment rates;  Utah ’s non -farm 
industry profiles and the numeric changes; unemployment claims; Utah 
unemployment growth; Utah wages declining; Salt Lake County new 
residential construction; the County economy regarding new residential 
construction; car and truck sales; seasonally adjusted sales tax; the County ’s 
first quarter sales; 2020 hits and shock impacts from COVID-19. 
Council Member Snelgrove  asked if the liquidity pumped into the 
economy in the last few weeks might trigger inflation.
Mr. Scott Smith , Econowest Associates, stated when there is slack in the 
economy, such as there is now, inflation has a hard time existing.  The 
Federal Government has been trying to inject liquidity into the system and it 
can stop markets from seizing, but it cannot cure solvency.  If it tries to cure 
solvency by printing money, it will get inflation.  That is not where the 
country is now.  
Mr. Macdonald stated the forecast is only 1 percent inflation this year, and 
similar going out a couple years.  
Council Member Snelgrove  asked if the situation in Salt Lake County and 
America could bring on negative interest rates as it has done in Europe.  
Mr. Smith stated there seems to be some resistance to negative interest
