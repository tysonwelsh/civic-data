The following are the minutes of the Planning Commission Meeting held on Thursday, November 19, 2020 at
6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 W. Herriman Main Street (12090 South), Herriman,
Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in City Hall, on the City’s website, and
delivered to members of the Commission and media.

Kaseke, and Heather Garcia

Staff Present in Person: City Planner Michael Maloy, City Engineer Jonathan Bowers, HPD Cody Stromberg,
Communication Specialist Destiny Skinner, and Deputy Recorder Wendy Thorpe

Chair Chris Berbert called the work meeting to order at 6:02 pm.




City Planner Michael Maloy informed the Commission this was Assistant City Planner Bryn MacDonald’s final
meeting with Herriman City. She had attended more than 250 meetings during her time with Herriman City.

The Valhalla development was presented to City Council for discussion and should be on for vote at the next

Commissioners agreed no further information was needed, and all items could be discussed during the regular
meeting.


FFKR Susie Petheram presented the Annual Moderate Income (AMI) Housing Report update. She stated
housing budgets should be no more than 30% of gross monthly income. Moderate income was defined as 80%
of the AMI, lower income was 50% of the AMI. Herriman median income was $94,800 and the Area Median
Income (AMI) at the County level was $87,900, with an average of $1,583 per month allocated to a housing
budget for either mortgage or rent.

                           Commissioner Adam Jacobson joined the meeting at 6:10 p.m.

Herriman made progress and currently has enough moderate housing units available, but lacks lower income
housing units. The Census data will provide the City with updated information regarding housing and
demographics. She reviewed the strategies chosen by Herriman City and the types of units available. Accessory
Dwelling Units (ADU) may be utilized, where allowed, to lower housing costs. Development rights were
vested as follows. Of the single-family units 10,250 were vested, of those 5,699 have been built. 6,585
multifamily units were vested, 4,737 of which have been built.

Approximately 68% of multi-family units were affordable, and 9% of single-family units were affordable.
Commissioners asked if incentives were offered to builders. City Planner Maloy stated some funds from the
County were designated for Herriman, but no programs have been implemented yet. Commissioners proposed
offering an incentive to developers to make 10% of each development affordable, as a way to spread them
throughout the community.

Commissioners asked when the General Plan would be presented. FFKR Susie Petheram responded it was
scheduled to be on the December 16, 2020 Joint meeting agenda.

                          Commissioner Jackson Ferguson joined the meeting at 6:33 p.m.


City Engineer Jonathan Bowers reviewed the vision for recreation in the Juniper Canyon area. These amenities
would be implemented in phases with each stand-alone phase presented as a conditional use item. He

requested feedback from the Commission with regards to desirable amenities. The disc golf area was identified,
and Commissioners requested keeping it away from homes. Decisions were made with input from residents at
neighborhood meetings. Commissioner Jackson Ferguson lived near the area and offered support for the
improvements. Phase one amenities included a trailhead and projects as funding allows. Two pump tracks were

Commissioners discussed an article about a Bill in the Legislature that would allow Accessory Dwelling Units

Commissioner Jackson Ferguson inquired if the City had discussed the Home Builders Association Bill, which
would restrict municipality design guidelines. City Planner Maloy stated that was one of the Bills the City and
Council were watching.

Assistant City Planner MacDonald informed the Commission that a cell phone box was proposed on a school
sign tower. She asked if they would like to review co-locations. The Commissioners responded in the

Assistant City Planner MacDonald informed the Commission items 3.1 and 3.2 would be discussed
simultaneously. The applicant requested preliminary plat and site plan approval for 416 units, including
325 townhomes and 62 single-family lots, for a project known as Mountain Ridge Phases 3-4 and 9-16.
The property location was at 14000 S Sentinel Ridge Boulevard, east of Mountain View Corridor, south of
13800 South. The entire Mountain Ridge development was approximately 105 acres. Phases 1-2 and 5-8
were previously approved by the Planning Commission. The property is currently zoned R-2-10 with a
Planned Development Overlay Zone.

The Planned Development Overlay Zone required each phase shall submit an application for approval of a
site plan pursuant to Section 10-5-12 of the Herriman Land Development Code. The PD Overlay zone
required that a preliminary site plan be approved concurrently with the rezone request. A preliminary site
plan was approved by the City Council with the PD Overlay zone. Each phase now comes back for final
site plan approval to show compliance with the preliminary plan. The ordinance stated that a planned
development shall not be subject to the lot width and lot area requirements of the underlying zone. The
approved design guidelines and elevations were reviewed. The development included a ten acre park.
According to the development agreement a separate five acre park was to be held as commercial for a
couple years. Commissioners confimed the open space requrements were met without the five acre park.

Applicants Brandon Watson and Brandon Parr with Edge Homes were available to answer questions.

the best options for traffic calming measures, pedestrian access, and traffic flow options. They agreed further
work was needed before approval. The applicants agreed to work with City staff to identify solutions for
pedestrian crossings, safe walking routes, trail connectivity, proper slip road locations, and traffic calming

City Engineer Jonathan Bowers and City Attorney Chase Andrizzi reviewed the purpose for implementing
these water efficiency standards. The water efficiency standards set new recommendations and requirements
for both indoor water fixtures and exterior landscapes and irrigation systems. These standards, must be
adopted by ordinance. With Herriman’s continued growth and water system expansion, we have exceeded our
current contract, and must amend our contract to reflect current and future water demands. If Herriman
chooses not to update our contract, any water purchase above our current contract 3,067 AF, would be
November 19, 2020 – Planning Commission Minutes                                            Page 9 of 10


charged at the Block 2 rates. Block 2 rates are $600/AF higher than Block 1 rates. The rates would increase
after the 90 day waiting period. Developers with vested rights on Master Development Agreements would be
encouraged to adopt the new standards. Property maintenance violations would be addressed by City Code
Enforcement officers.
