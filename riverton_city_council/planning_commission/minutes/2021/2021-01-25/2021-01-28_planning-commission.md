       a.     HOME OCCUPATION, DERRICK STOKES IS REQUESTING A
              CONDITIONAL USE-HOME OCCUPATION PERMIT FOR A MOBILE
              MECHANIC BUSINESS AT 2150 WEST GREGORY AVENUE.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the request is for a
Conditional Use Home Occupation for a mobile mechanic business to be located at 2150
West Gregory Avenue, which is an established neighborhood. The business will be
operated from an established home and involves mechanic diagnostics repair to take
place at the homes of customers. No additional employees will come to the home since
the work will be done off-site. The applicant plans to keep supplies in a work truck in front
of his home or in his garage. A photo of the front of the property was displayed. Mr.
Prestwich stated that with home occupations, there are fixed standards, qualifications,
and conditions. There is also a list of prohibited home occupations. If a home occupation
is not on the prohibited list and meets all of the fixed standards and qualifications, it is
entitled to approval.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the application was before the Planning Commission because
of the items on the prohibited list including storage, service repair, sales, or rental of
ambulances, tow trucks, recreational vehicles, watercraft, automobiles, ATVs, or other
motorized vehicles. The other involves auto body repair or motor vehicle repair.
Mr. Prestwich stated that previously, the Commission determined that a mobile mechanic
business does not reach the required threshold. Prohibited home occupations are
intended to be activities that take place at the home. Mr. Prestwich gave the example of
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                               2
January 28, 2021

DMR Automotive, which was approved in April of 2020. The request was similar with less
impact. Staff recommended approval based on the ordinances and what the Commission
has approved previously.

Mr. Prestwich reported that he visited the home earlier in the day. Notice of tonight’s
public hearing was sent to property owners within 300 feet. One response was received
from a neighbor who expressed support.

Mr. Stokes stated that he intends to serve the elderly or lower-income individuals who are
unable to use a traditional mechanic shop. Being mobile reduces labor costs, parts costs,
and storage. Mr. Stokes indicated that he will use only hand tools in his work to reduce
noise. There will be no noise generated at his home, which is where he will park his work
vehicle and keep his tools. Mr. Stokes reported that he will visit up to three customers
per day.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Rushton regarding parts deliveries,
Mr. Stokes stated that he has an off-site mailbox. No large delivery trucks will come to
the home. Large items will be picked up.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Russell moved to APPROVE Application PLZ-20-2051 Auto Correct

              REQUESTING A CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR EXPANDING A
              QUASI-PUBLIC USE IN A RESIDENTIAL ZONE AT 11869 SOUTH 2700
              WEST.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and displayed a rendering of the Gospel Hope
Church located on 2700 West just south of 11800 South. The subject property is a
narrow, deep lot that is approximately 2.04 acres in size. The church is in front with a
parking lot in the back. The property is zoned RR-22 Residential with one-half acre lots
and animal rights. In the RR-22 zone, quasi-public uses are listed as conditional. Options
available to the applicant were described. Development of one-half acre residential lots
was underway on the property to the south.

A rendering of the church was displayed. Mr. Prestwich reported that the commercial
standards of the lower one-third of the wall are brick or stone. When the church was
approved in 2002, that standard was met. The building is set off from the road by
approximately 30 feet. The intent is to add on to the rear of the building, which is about
6,300 square feet in size. Mr. Prestwich reviewed the pertinent sections of the Code and
stated that the architecture, landscaping, setbacks, building heights, and building
materials can be addressed with the site plan. The applicant’s sketch was displayed,
which included the Worship Center, the existing church, and the proposed addition. An
expansion of the parking to the east was also proposed. Staff reviewed the concept plans
and had several suggestions for the applicant.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the current church site includes a six-foot white vinyl fence
that was approved with the site plan. The neighboring project was only required to install
fencing along one side where it abuts the R-4 zone. Staff requested that the Commission
discuss whether the six-foot white vinyl fence will be acceptable as it exists or if masonry
will be required. They should also specify the type of fencing to be required where there
currently is none. Mr. Prestwich recommended masonry.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the matter was properly noticed as a public hearing. No
written comments were received, however, he received a phone call earlier in the day
from a woman who was concerned about items that would be addressed with the site
plan.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Rushton regarding the retention pond,
Mr. Prestwich stated that it is a private pond.

Commissioner Rushton asked if vinyl fencing is appropriate on the north and east.
Mr. Prestwich stated that the ordinance does not allow for much flexibility and
recommended that the Commission give staff and the developer direction on the fencing.
He clarified that the lots do not have animal rights but the properties to the north and
south do.

4.     DISCUSSION ITEMS

      a.      SLR, CPA-1 Residential Neighborhood Community-Scale Plan.

Mr. Prestwich reported that SLR properties consist of approximately 600 acres on the
west side of the City. They include zoning and development agreements that differ from
the rest of the City. Approval involves a three-step process. Mr. Prestwich stated that
the Planning Commission will see the project three times as it moves toward completion
and construction. Mr. Prestwich likened the project to Mountain View Village. The
Commission discussed whether the houses should be turned if frontage roads should be
utilized, and how to connect the frontage roads. He stated that they will come back with
a phasing plan and then a final plan.

Mr. Prestwich reported that this is the first step in the process. A public hearing will need
to be held but the matter was brought before the Commission for discussion first due to
the context and location of the property. The matter was noticed for a public hearing in
February. Notice was also sent to the neighbors.

Mr. Prestwich presented a rendering of the property and the surrounding area. He
provided an overview of the two documents that govern what takes place there. The
documents set out different CPA areas, each with different requirements. In CPA 1,
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                    9
January 28, 2021

single-family detached, multi-unit homes, single-family attached housing, multi-family
residential, multi-family residential upper floors, and home occupations are permitted. Mr.
Prestwich reported that in the SDD ordinance, permitted uses in CPA 1 within 250 feet of
the north property line shall be limited to single-family detached residential.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the review process follows a three-step process. First, they
do a very high level and a concept level at a community scale plan. The next step is a
block scale plan where they get into more detail and show the project feasibility. They
investigate traffic and stormwater in greater detail. The final step is a detailed plan or plat
that would be approved. They then move on to construction. Each step is a separate
application. The community-scale plan requires a public hearing and the public will be
notified. The other two are decision items.

A visual presentation of the site plan was displayed. Mr. Prestwich identified single-family
detached, clustered houses, townhomes, a clubhouse, some green space, and
community space. He identified a row of units turned toward the street and some that
back the street. With regard to the open space, Mr. Prestwich reported that it will be
privately maintained. The developer will set up an HOA and maintain it. He identified the
road types on the site plan. Some were shaded red and others purple. The red denoted
public streets and the purple, private streets. All will need to meet the street requirements
in the SDD.

Mr. Prestwich requested feedback from the Commission on Sunday Drive. He reported
that Sunday Drive is proposed as a public street. The first plan showed that it not
connecting, however, the engineers and planners want it to connect. They acknowledged
the concerns of neighbors who do not want traffic from Sunday Drive. Mr. Prestwich
stated that Sunday Drive was built to connect. It shows a connection on the Master
Transportation Plan but there is no connection there. Mr. Prestwich invited feedback from
the Commission. Chair Cannon was pleased that Sunday Drive does not connect to the
new project. Those who purchased homes along Sunday Drive do not want to be next to
heavy traffic. He suggested that staff find a way to push traffic to the carrier route.
Commissioner Russell agreed.

Commissioner Rushton pointed out that when Phases 2 and 3 in Parkside were approved,
the developer indicated that there was dialogue regarding the project and CPA 2 providing
access to open space, parks, and amenities. Commissioner Rushton asked about the
timing and if it will be an HOA parcel. Mr. Prestwich had also heard that a connection
was a possibility but he had not yet seen anything for CPA 2.

Benson Whitney was present on behalf of the property owner, PRI SLR Group. In
response to a question, he identified where the 250 line runs. He explained that the
homes are not attached and are single-family homes in a courtyard configuration. Mr.
Whitney explained that the 200-foot line was at the back property line between the cluster
and the single lots. The clustered homes are two-story single-family detached units.

Chair Cannon asked if a builder is looking to purchase the property. Mr. Whitney stated
that PRI SLR will develop it, keep it in their portfolio, and maintain it. In response to an

which is a subsidiary. The company he works for currently is City Creek SLC. Mr.

Mr. Whitney stated that this type of development has not yet been done in Utah. It has

With regard to amenities, Mr. Whitney stated that there will be two dog parks. One to the
north for smaller dogs and the other to the south for larger dogs. A maintenance company

were told that exterior fencing must be masonry. The interior fencing will be gray vinyl.

Commissioner Rushton asked if the HOA will provide areas for seniors. Mr. Whitney
stated that the project will not be age-restricted but they are focusing on that demographic.
Of the single-family homes, every other home will be a rambler. Each unit will also have
a basement for storage. Mr. Whitney stated that they will do six-month and one-year
leases. Renters will not pay an HOA fee with all costs being included in their rent. Rents
were expected to be $1,100 to $2,800 per month. Mr. Whitney stated that there will be
no nightly or Airbnb rentals. They had not looked into home occupations and would seek
guidance from the Planning Commission.

Mr. Whitney stated that there will be no official HOA. There will be a leasing office and a
model home that will remain open permanently. They have 10 different product types
that range from two to four-bedroom units. The basements will be finished in some at an
additional fee. He reiterated that they would prefer to not do Sunday Drive unless it is
required. Chair Cannon pointed out that Sunday Drive was not built as a carrier street to
accommodate heavy traffic.

stated that they had not investigated that. He asked if they would want public streets
throughout the project. Chair Cannon’s opinion was that one or multiple owners would
not make a difference in terms of the traffic generated. Mr. Prestwich stated that there
are pros and cons to both. The Commission determined that the decision should be left
to staff. Mr. Whitney stated that they will install individual water meters. Garbage
collection services had yet to be finalized.
