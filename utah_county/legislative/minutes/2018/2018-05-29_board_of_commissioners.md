2.    PRESENTATION OF COMPENSATION STUDY RESULTS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
(Track A, 1:19:35)
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS
MINUTES – May 29, 2018
Page 7

Utah County Human Resources Director Lana Jensen explained a market study is about pay structure, as it is the duty
of the Human Resources Director to set pay, ranges, and mid-points to provide compensation. The market point of a
job is based on market data, scope and difficulty of the job, and internal equity. Ms. Jensen reviewed the process of
gathering and reviewing data for the last six weeks, including feedback from Department Heads regarding employee
responsibilities, career ladders, and job titles. Each Department Head was invited to identify any specific positions
which did not correlate with their organizational structure.

Lana Jensen noted the estimated cost to move every General Fund employee the same number of steps as determined
for their position would be $1,081,000 to implement this year, beginning Pay Period 15. That includes additional
benefit costs but not employees of the Utah County Health Department nor the Utah County Department of Drug and
Alcohol Prevention and Treatment, as they are not financed through the General Fund. The cost there would be
approximately $400,000, as well as another $500,000 for other non-General Fund employees. Ms. Jensen pointed out
the committee reviewing the compensation study feels strongly that full implementation would be in the best interest
of the county. It could enable retaining current employees, the hiring of new employees, and overall morale increase.

(Track A, 1:28:50)
Lana Jensen commented on the Utah State Code County Personnel Management Act which orders the county to
provide ‘equitable and adequate compensation.’ She quoted the requirements for ‘preparation, maintenance, and
revision of a position classification plan for all positions in the career service… in order to maintain a high quality
public work force.’ Ms. Jensen further expounded on County Pay Plan rules. She mentioned Utah County ranks 29th
of the 29 counties in Utah when considering number of county employees per residents, with 17.9 employees to 10,000
residents compared to Weber County’s 30 employees or Salt Lake County’s 50 employees for the same population.
Ms. Jensen and Commissioner Lee discussed the market study. Commissioner Lee commented on $1,000,000 set
aside in the 2018 Budget to address salary discrepancies for those ‘furthest out’ from market range, and Ms. Jensen
noted that could lead to a less-equitable overall implementation. Commissioner Lee stated he is in favor of back-pay
for employees, and Ms. Jensen mentioned that may cause complications with the WorkDay system. She summarized
it would cost approximately $2,000,000 for full implementation of the salary study. Ms. Jensen also expounded on
the study process at the request of Commissioner Ivie.

(Track A, 1:41:10)
Utah County Deputy Attorney Robert Moore commended Lana Jensen, Merritt Fullmer of the Utah County Human
Resources Office, and county Department Heads for their work and participation in the market study review. Mr.
Moore and Utah County Assessor Kris Poulson mentioned assessing and collecting monies being maintained outside
of the General Fund. Utah County Attorney Jeffrey R. Buhman talked about the importance of a viable work force
and competing against other agencies. Health Department Director Ralph Clegg, Public Works Director Richard
Nielson, and Children’s Justice Center Director Rebecca Martell expressed support for full implementation of the
market study, speaking of hardships and the collaborative efforts throughout the process with Utah County Recorder
Jeffrey Smith, Utah County Surveyor Gary Ratcliffe, Mike Brower of the Utah County Sheriff’s Office, and Scott
Finch of the Utah County Attorney’s Office. Utah County Information Systems Director Neil Peterson was the last
to emphasize the importance of moving forward; Commissioner Lee noted the positive comments and reminded the
audience the Board has not yet seen the recommendation.
