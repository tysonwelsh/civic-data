Utah has enacted HB300, a new election law that introduces important changes to our
voting process.

- Opt-In for Mail-In Ballots: Starting in 2029, ballots will no longer be mailed
automatically to all active registered voters. To continue voting by mail, you'll need to
opt in. This can be done when registering to vote, renewing your driver's license, or
through the lieutenant governor's website. Once you've opted in, your mail-in ballot
status will remain active for eight years unless you fail to vote in two consecutive general
elections.

- Voter Identification Requirements: Beginning in 2029, voters who choose to vote by
mail will be required to provide the last four digits of their driver's license, state ID, or
Social Security number on their ballot return envelope. For in-person voting, a valid ID
will be necessary.

- Ballot Submission Deadline: Beginning in 2025 so this election, all mail-in ballots
must be received by no later than 8 p.m. on Election Day to be counted.

RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommended that the Planning Commission recommend approval to the Municipal
Council of the proposed amendments to Chapters 17.21 Gateway Overlay and 17.33
Temporary Signs.

REQUEST
This is a proposal to add a fourth commercial node location along Highway 89/91 south of
Logan City at the 1700 South/Park Avenue intersection in the 17.21 “Gateway Overlay”
chapter and a new Special Event Temporary Signs section in the 17.33 “Signs” chapter of
the Land Development Code (LDC).

SUMMARY OF CHANGES
The proposed addition of a commercial node and associated setbacks at 1700 South/Park
Avenue and Highway 89/91 follows the recommendations of the South Corridor Plan
adopted in August of 2011. The South Corridor Plan encourages more concentrated
development around traffic signals and more open pastoral stretches in between. The 1700
South intersection has the potential to develop on all four quadrants. The Blacksmith Fork
River runs along the north side of this intersection and development along this river will
be subject to all other applicable code sections.

The proposed addition to the LDC 17.33.110 Temporary Signs applies specifically to
special events and brings more clarity to these types of events. The new regulations have
specific timelines, total sign numbers, sizes, and placement standards for temporary signs
associated with special events. The proposed addition allows up to eight temporary signs
and two banners placed per event. Event signs cannot be placed more than seven days prior
and must be removed 24 hours after the event.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
The changes to the 1700 South/Park Avenue commercial node will set the same regulations
as the three other southern commercial nodes already adopted. It will create a more uniform
develop pattern at every traffic signal along this corridor and will improve access and safety
for entering and exiting this stretch of Highway.

Prior to this temporary sign proposal, short-term special events had to adhere to the general
temporary sign section, which is broader in nature and not always applicable to short-term
events. These two amendments bring more clarity and equality to these two code sections.
Staff recommends approval of both amendments.

GENERAL PLAN
The Land Development Code was prepared and adopted to implement the vision expressed
in the General Plan. The purpose of these code changes is to update and clarify provisions
in the Land Development Code relative to corridor gateways and special events.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a recommendation of approval

acres located at 310 West 1400 North (TIN# 05-134-0001; -0002; -0003; -0004; -0005)

Land Use adjoining the subject property
North: CS: Commercial Uses East: MR-12: Residential Uses
South: NR-6: Residential Uses West: MR-12: Residential Uses

PROJECT
The proponent is requesting to rezone five (5) properties for a total of 1.36 acres of
property from MR-20 to MR-12. Developed in the 1990's as a five (5) 4-plexes totaling
20 dwelling units in the subdivision. Originally, the five (5) buildings were each
individually owned with co- ownership of the land maintained and governed by the five
building owners via the West Pointe Homeowners Association (HOA). The intent of the
subdivision in 2022 was to change the nature of the ownership structure from a
"condominium," where only the buildings are individually owned, to a condition where an
owner owns both the building and the land underneath. The upzone to MR-20 was required

Executive Summary

Overview of Special Event Licensing
In 2024 Logan City issued over 80 special event licenses for events ranging from
mass gatherings on Center Street, such as the Pumpkin Festival, to fundraisers that
bring out a small group of participants. Currently, our licensing process applies to
all events equally and sometimes lacks the nuance necessary to address the various
scales and impacts of differing events in Logan City. In particular, the number of
large-scale special events is increasing in the city. Because of this, impacts from
special events on our neighborhoods, infrastructure, residents, staff, and
community resources are also increasing. To reduce these impacts and maintain an
efficient and effective special event review process, Logan City Business
Licensing is proposing an amendment to the special event application and
licensing code.

Areas Addressed in the Proposed Amendment

   •   Defining a Special Event - The current definition includes all events that
       take place on public property and those on private property that are opened
       to the public for a fee. The purpose of special events is to provide a
       licensing process for temporary events that occur on public property or
       events at locations where the use exceeds the licensed or permitted use at
       the location. The proposed amendment addresses this by making impacts of
       the event a consideration in what is defined as a special event.

   •   Special Event Scale - The number of events that involve mass gatherings
       is increasing. The proposed amendment creates a three-tiered system that
       will place more responsibility on event organizers to have clear plans in
       place to address the impacts of larger events. The threshold for the three
       tiers will be less than 250 participants, less than 1,000 participants, and
       1,000 or more participants.

   •   Application - The proposed amendment revises the application deadline
       for special events based on the tier of the event. The smaller Tier 1 events
       have a shorter deadline, while Tier 3 applications will have a longer
       deadline and an application window.
   •   Multiple-day/occurrences - The proposed amendment creates
       criteria for evaluating multiple-day events.




                                                                             17 | Page
            Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ April 15, 2025
   •   Logan City Venues - The proposed amendment empowers city staff to
       develop unique policies for the various city venues to ensure that impacts
       to the neighborhood are minimized and that the venue is adequate to
       support the event.

   •   Film Shoots - The proposed amendment includes a new code section
       on licensing requirements for film shoots, including the requirement to
       inform adjacent properties of the filming schedule and provide contact
       information.

   •   Fees - The current fee structure for Special Events treats all events,
       independent of size, the same. The revised fee schedule will charge a
       higher review fee for larger events that require additional staff review time.
       In addition, Tier 3 applications will include a deposit that can be applied to
       cover the costs of city services or clean up after an event. Also, the new fee
       section empowers city departments to require city services that are
       determined to be necessary to protect public health, safety, and welfare.

   •   Review and Denial Criteria - The proposed amendment revises the
       review and denial process for applications and empowers city staff to make
       decisions that will protect public health, safety, and welfare. City staff are
       empowered to impose restrictions or conditions on an event or require
       changes to the location or time of an event or deny an application if it is
       deemed necessary to protect the health, safety, and welfare of the public.

   •   Scheduling Conflicts - The proposed amendment creates evaluation
       criteria for events that have conflicting schedules and locations. Logan City
       sponsored events, or other governmental events, will have first priority,
       followed by established/repeat events, followed by first-in-time filing of an
       application.

   •   Signage - Concurrent with the LMC 5.04 amendment, a Land Development
       Code amendment is proposed to create rules for offsite commercial signage
       that is associated with a licensed special event. Currently, our code
       prohibits most offsite commercial signs, which has led to an enforcement
       issue for special events.
Special Event Application Fees
Special event applications require a multi-departmental/division review. Typically,
a special event application review involves staff from Business Licensing,
Planning and Zoning, Parks and Recreation, Public Works, Streets, Environmental,
Fire, Police, and Legal. Other Divisions and Departments are assigned as needed
based on the type of event. The various reviewers each have their piece of the
special event to consider before they can sign off on an event. Depending on the
                                                                             18 | Page
            Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ April 15, 2025
event, the review can include the consideration of street closures, security and
police coverage, emergency services, fire and building safety, adequate sanitary
and garbage facilities, park use and reservations, and insurance and
indemnification. Departments and Divisions that review applications also meet
monthly with event organizers to review applications. Generally, staff time spent
on an application review is directly related to the size and complexity of the
special event.

Currently, all special event applications are charged the same two fees: a $75
business license application fee and a $75 fire review fee. The fire review fee was
adopted to offset the cost that the Fire Department incurs to review special event
applications. There is also a fire review fee applied to most other types of business
license applications. No other Departments charge an additional application review
fee. No change to the Fire review fee is proposed in this amendment.
Instead of a "one size fits all" approach, this proposed amendment would create a
tiered fee schedule. Tier 1 applications - $75, Tier 2 - $150, and Tier 3 - $300. This
increase in fees is directly related to the amount of time spent by staff on the review
and management of special event applications.

Business Licensing currently has one staff member dedicated to Special Event
management. It is estimated that 50% of her time is spent on Special Events. The
current business licensing revenue for special event applications is estimated at
$6,000 per year (80 special event applications x $75 application fee). Fire review
fees are estimated to be the same. Not calculated here is the time spent by other
departments to review special event applications. As shown below, the current
revenue from special event applications is inadequate to cover the staff time spent
on special events. Even with the proposed fee increases, the gap between expenses
and revenues is only narrowed and not closed entirely.
