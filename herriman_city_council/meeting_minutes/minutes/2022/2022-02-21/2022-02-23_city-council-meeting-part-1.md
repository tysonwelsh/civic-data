    2.1. Veterans Memorial Flag Project Presentation - Mountain Ridge High School DECA Students
         - Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager
Assistant City Manager Thomas said she was originally approached about the Veterans Memorial Flag
project at the end of 2021, and relayed her desire to have the students involved present their idea to
the Council.

Ms. Bella Jones explained DECA was a nonprofit organization who worked with high school and college
students to prepare them to be business leaders within the community. She said the students wanted
to construct a veteran’s memorial in the City, to honor Herriman residents who served. She hoped the
memorial would bring attention to some of the trails in the City. Ms. Lorin Winterton said before the
memorial was constructed, the group would look to host a Veteran’s appreciation day, as it would be
a good way to connect with potential donors and generate excitement about the monument. The group
added their hope to place the monument at the Mustang Loop trailhead, located in Juniper Canyon, as
it already had established facilities.

Ms. Jones and Ms. Winterton mentioned several family members who served in the military, and
thought it was important to show the community’s support for military service, which would also
extend to military families. Ms. Jones suggested people who visited the memorial could write messages
on a rock to leave at the base of the memorial to show support. She added the benefit of the memorial
would be to honor those who protected their freedoms, and it could also create a sense of unity within
Herriman. She thought it would also entice people to visit the City to explore the trail system. The
students briefly discussed the logistics of having someone designated to raise and lower the flag, as
needed.

    2.2. Fleet purchasing discussion - Monte Johnson, Operations Director
Director of Operations Johnson went over a couple of issues in regard to recent vehicle purchases. He
explained the purchases would not make it into the current year's budget, and would need to be rolled
over into the next fiscal year. He said the vendor had notified the City their order dates were being
shut down for the year as their factories had reached production capacity. He explained when the City
had to push replacement vehicles out, it put the City at risk for unnecessary and expensive repairs,
potentially causing issues for services. He reported the vendor would be willing to reserve build slots
for a list of vehicles, and clarified the build slots could be cancelled at any time, with no obligation. The
vendor would require the reservation request be on letterhead paper to acknowledge the government
request. He asked for direction from the Council, and they relayed their desire to reserve the build
slots for the necessary replacement fleet vehicles.

    2.4. HPD Capital Equipment Purchase Procedures - Cody Stromberg, Deputy Police Chief
Deputy Police Chief Cody Stromberg Cody stated some of the capital equipment for the police
department was reaching the end of its life cycle. He asked for direction on how to go about the
replacement of the equipment, and outlined some proposals. City Manager Cherpeski asked if he
would prefer to buy replacements as they wore out, or make larger purchases ahead of time. Deputy
Police Chief Stromberg offered an example of tasers, and observed the five-year warranty. If the
replacement happened while they were still covered, it would be an overall cost savings to the City.
He agreed it would be cost efficient to purchase in smaller quantities as the equipment neared the end
of the life cycle, even if it meant replacements happened prior to the equipment being completely out
of service. He also noted some items, such as body cams, radios, and tasers, were now available via a
subscription service, which would also decrease the costs of replacements. Deputy Police Chief
Stromberg preferred to use the subscription services, where it was applicable. He relayed his concern
of subscriptions was to ensure proper oversight. The Council supported the idea of using a
subscription service for replacement, whenever possible. Councilmember Shields noted it was integral
to the success of the police department all equipment was up-to-date and effective.

    9.1. Discussion and consideration of a request by Herriman City, to approve an amendment of
         Title 10 Pertaining to Regulations for Accessory Structures. (File No. Z2021-054) - Clinton
         Spencer, Planning Manager
Planning Manager Clinton Spencer offered a summary of the proposed updates for accessory
structures. He noted staff had worked on this amendment with the Planning Commission for a long
time, and were excited to see it through. He explained the goal had been to consolidate all the
accessory building ordinances into one chapter, since currently, the content was spread throughout
the Code, and observed the difficulty in finding all relevant information. He said staff worked to update
ordinances to better reflect the values and needs of the community. He stated the architectural
standards had been changed, to ensure future accessory structures were in line with community
values. He noted agricultural buildings would have exceptions, to allow for the associated unique
needs. He observed larger lots would provide for more flexibility, and lots less than a half-acre had
more strict rules about size and unit height.

    9.4. Discussion and consideration of a recommendation to amend City Code 10-16-1 regarding
         land uses in the C-1 (Commercial) Zone (File No. Z2021-124) - Michael Maloy, City Planner
Planning Manager Spencer indicated the applicant desired to locate a church on the property, located
at 5320 W Herriman, which was not currently zoned to allow for a church. He said the City also wanted
to remove warehouses from being permitted in the C-1 zone. He added there were two areas in the
City that were zoned as C-1, and both areas had warehouses. He explained the text amendment would
make both of those warehouses legal non-conforming, and would be allowed to continue operating.
He added the Council could always change the zone again later if desired. He summarized the proposal
would make a church a conditional use in the C-1 zone, and remove warehouses as a permitted use.
He said the Planning Commission had recommended approval.

Councilmember Ohrn did not like to change the City ordinances based on just one property, although
based on the research she had seen, she thought this change made sense. She worried people would
not understand what a legal non-conforming use was, and clarified the warehouses could always
request the zone be changed to make warehouses a permitted use. Planning Manager Spencer
confirmed.

Councilmember Shields commented churches and schools were important, and much needed in the
community; however, were often built in areas which had been designated for commercial
development. He voiced the concern this resolved an issue for one property, but may cause issues for
other commercial developments down the line. He worried this would set a precedent which land
designated for commercial use could be easily taken away. Planning Manager Spencer explained a
zoning condition could be placed on properties to not allow churches on a specific site.

Councilmember Ohrn moved to approve Ordinance No. 2022-06 amending the Herriman City Code
to allow churches as a conditional use and prohibit personal service warehouses in the C-1 zone.
Councilmember Hodges seconded the motion.

The vote was recorded as follows:
