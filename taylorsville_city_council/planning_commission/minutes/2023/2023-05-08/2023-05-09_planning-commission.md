3- 1.    Mr. Spung presented this item.           I   Mr. Leonardo Alarcon, property owner, is requesting the Planning
         Commission consider an amendment to Lot 401 of Cannonwood Subdivision, Plat D, to create one new
         residential flag lot at 6277 South Cannonwood Drive in Taylorsville, Utah. The property is an undeveloped
         flag lot that was created through the recording of the Cannonwood Subdivision, Plat D, amending Lot 300.
         The     Planning Commission reviewed and approved File # 10S2020 in a meeting held July 27, 2021.              The

         subject property is in the Single- Family Residential ( R- 1- 10) Zoning District ( minimum 10,000 square foot
         lot) and is in the" low density residential" future land use map designation in the City' s General Plan. The
         property is 28, 310 square feet in area. It fronts onto Cannonwood Drive with a 25- foot-wide area between
         Lot 403( 6281 South Cannonwood Drive) and parcel number 21- 22- 203- 011- 0000( 1629 West 6235 South).
         A six- foot tall privacy fence of various materials surrounds the propertyonthe east, south and west sides.
         There is no fencing installed along the northern property line now.        CJ In 2020 there was an application
         request to subdivide a portion of this land to create the existing flag lot( Lot 401). Tonight' s request would
         be to further subdivide Lot 401 to create an additional residential lot, making two lots instead of just one.
         Lot 401 is the boundary of the area being discussed tonight. Flag lot standards require that the furthest
         back lot has direct access to a public right- of-way. The Taylorsville Land Development Code says that
         portion of the flag lot shall be contiguous to a dedicated public street, which it is. It must be a minimum of
         20' wide, which it is and the maximum length cannot exceed 220 feet. This request would exceed that
         length, which would be allowed if approved by the Planning Commission and Unified Fire Authority
         approved the layout. Commissioner Quigley asked what the distance actually is compared to the 220 feet.
         Mr. Spung said the north leg is just under 157 feet and the north- south leg is just under 283 feet. Those
         combined is just under 440 feet. An emergency turn around is to be provided if the leg is over 150 feet
         from a street.

6. 1     Ms. Bergeson     presented    this item.   J The City of Taylorsville is proposing to amend Section 8. 12. 140
         Backyard Chicken Permit, to allow flexibility for the placement of chicken coops on irregularly shaped
         parcels. Currently the ordinance states that a coop and enclosure is not to be permitted in front or side
         yards. For most of the lots in Taylorsville, this standard is reasonable, and residents can work within the
         standards and meet these requirements. Recently a Backyard Chicken Permit application was submitted
         that brought attention to irregularly shaped, infill parcels. The applicant stated that the rear yard is not the
         most suitable location for the coop, and the site plan illustrated that the front yard is designed in a way not
         typical to most of the single- family developments in Taylorsville. In that location, the front yard is nearly
         three times the area when compared to the rear yard and in fact, placement in the rear yard would put the
         coop closer to residences than if it were allowed to be permitted within the front yard. Although the coop
         is far from neighboring residents and the main dwelling, Staff cannot approve the permit due to the clear
         restrictions in the ordinance. In anticipation of more applications on large lots with similar conditions, Staff
         has revisited the Backyard Chicken Permit Section of the Taylorsville Municipal Code and proposed revisions

         that would give the Director the authority to allow flexibility when considering the placement of the coop,
         subject to certain standards.


         6. 1. 1    Proposed changes to Enclosure Standards:
                    a. Chickens must be contained within an enclosure or fenced area at all times.

                    b. All enclosures shall have a maximum opening of two- and one- half inches.
