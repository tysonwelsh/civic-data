City staff is proposing an ordinance change to amend Section 7-6-301 and add Section 7-7-104.5
to include use-specific regulations that would prohibit blood plasma centers on all properties
with frontage along Redwood Road and on 3300/3500 South between 2700 West and the Jordan
River. For reference, a blood plasma center is defined in the Zoning Ordinance as “an
establishment where patrons receive compensation for donating blood plasma”.

By way of background, incidents involving homeless persons increased significantly in the City
when the homeless resource center in South Salt Lake at 3380 South 1000 West opened. The
City has been tracking these homeless incidents, which include crime, for several years now. The
highest concentration of these incidents has consistently been the area surrounding the
intersection of 3500 South and Redwood Road. This concentration of homeless incidents has
only increased with the opening of the State’s temporary homeless shelter operating at 3381
South Redwood Road.

Staff recently received an inquiry about a blood plasma center moving into a location within
close proximity of the temporary homeless shelter operating at 3381 South Redwood Road.
Given the already high concentration of homeless incidents in this area, staff is concerned that a
blood plasma center will only make the situation worse. The quotes below from online articles
link blood plasma centers with homeless persons:

      “As a matter of fact, you’ll see a lot of homeless people at the plasma center. There’s
       lines around the corner. It takes hours to do sometimes.”
       https://www.kvue.com/article/news/local/austin-plasma-donation-affordability/269-
       19d7ebea-5e83-4631-a34e-66f3dd33d03a

      “I’ve been in eight different homeless shelters in several states, and I realized there’s a
       whole culture of people living homeless that get by decently using all the help they can
       get,” Simmons said. “Blood centers, that’s an easy way to get cash.”
       https://www.columbiatribune.com/story/news/local/2017/12/27/some-donors-bypass-
       plasma-screen/985535007/


      “One of the best ways to make money when you’re struggling and homeless is by
       donating plasma. Millions of people donate plasma for cash every year and some
       homeless people have even slowly saved up the money they’ve made over time and used
       it to get back on their feet.”
       https://homelessadvice.com/how-to-make-money-donating-plasma-when-homeless/

The intent of this ordinance is not to prohibit blood plasma centers altogether but to steer them to
areas with much less homeless incidents.

BACKGROUND


The First Baptist Church of Granger is requesting approval for a two-lot subdivision at the
southeast corner of 3800 South and 2700 West. The proposed subdivision will also amend lot 74
of the Wright Subdivision. Property to the north is zoned C-2, property to the east and south is
zoned R-1-8 and is part of the Wright Subdivision No. 10.

ISSUES:

The proposed subdivision is located on property that was rezoned in November 2023 from the R-
1-8 zone to the C-2 zone. The subdivision will consist of 2 lots on 2.9 acres. Lot 1 is approximately
1.4 acres in size with lot 2 being 1.5 acres in size.

Access to the subdivision will be gained from 3800 South and from 2700 West. The City
Engineering Division is evaluating the right-of-way along 3800 South and believes that additional
right-of-way will be required. This determination will be made and information passed along to
the applicant as the plat is further evaluated.

It is anticipated that the existing Church will be demolished in favor of a new commercial
development that will include a restaurant on lot 1 and retail building on lot 2. As part of the
rezone, a development agreement was reviewed by the Planning Commission and approved by the
City Council. There were 4 specific items addressed in this agreement.

      Streetscape and trees installed on 2700 West shall match the tree type and spacing as
       installed on the west side of 2700 West.
      All buildings to be single story.
      Masonry wall along the residential boundaries shall be 8 feet in height.
      Patrons visiting the restaurant shall not cross a drive through to enter the building.

Cross access and parking easements will need to be created during the subdivision or site plan
review process. Site improvements will be addressed as part of the Permitted Use application.
The amended plat simply cleans up existing property lines and reconfigures the property into two
developable lots.
