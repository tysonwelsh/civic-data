Item 1: An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding the minimum lot size
requirement in the Agritourism Overlay (AT) Zone. (PLOTA20240383) 0:06:42

Dustin Wright, Planner, presented the proposed text amendment to allow one-acre lots in the
agricultural zone to be eligible to apply for the Agritourism Overlay (AT) Zone. He explained
that the change would only apply to new applications, as no properties are currently designated
with the AT Overlay. The amendment would continue to require each applicant to undergo a
rezone process, including staff review, Planning Commission consideration, and final Council
approval.

Council members discussed concerns about the potential impacts on nearby residential
neighborhoods. Some questioned whether a commercial zone might be more appropriate for the
types of uses proposed under the AT Overlay. Wright, along with Aaron Ardmore, Planning
Supervisor, clarified that the AT Overlay permits specific uses—such as bed and breakfasts and
event centers—that may not be allowed in commercial zones. They also noted that the overlay
limits permitted uses to one-third of the lot area.

Item 3: An ordinance amending Provo City Code Title 14 regarding group quarters in the
Professional Office (PO) Zone. (PLOTA20240386) 0:23:22

Hannah Salzl, Planner/Sustainability Coordinator, presented the proposed amendment to Provo
City Code Title 14 to allow group quarters as a permitted use within a defined portion of the
Professional Office (PO) Zone. The amendment would apply specifically to areas with frontage
on Center Street, excluding properties listed on the historic registry. Salzl explained that group
quarters refer to dormitory-style housing that provides private rooms along with shared facilities
such as kitchens and common areas.

Salzl noted the proposed change would apply to a limited 3.5-block area and is intended to
increase housing affordability. She emphasized that the amendment would not allow short-term
rentals. As an example, a project located at 256 East Center Street was discussed, which would
convert a former assisted living facility into approximately 20 private rooms tailored for use by
young entrepreneurs.

Council members asked questions regarding parking requirements, lease structures, and how
group quarters differ from other housing types. Discussion followed regarding the potential
impacts and benefits of the proposal. Some Council members expressed support for increased
affordable housing opportunities, while others raised concerns about density and compatibility
with existing neighborhoods.

Item 4: A discussion regarding the future land use maps for the Station Area Plans. (25-
032) 0:43:13

Hannah Salzl, Planner/Sustainability Coordinator, presented draft future land use maps
associated with station area plans near UVX and FrontRunner transit stations. She explained that
these plans are mandated by the state and must be implemented through zoning changes by the
end of the year.

Salzl outlined the development process to date, which included stakeholder engagement,
multiple development scenarios, and public input. The primary goal of the plans is to promote
increased density and mixed-use development near transit hubs while maintaining compatibility
with surrounding neighborhoods.

Council members asked questions about specific locations and potential impacts of the proposed
land use changes. Discussion focused on the challenge of balancing transit-oriented development
with the preservation of established neighborhoods. Some Council members expressed concerns
about the accelerated timeline and emphasized the importance of additional Council involvement
in the process.

The Council agreed to continue reviewing the draft land use maps and committed to providing
feedback to staff within the next two weeks. An additional discussion is planned for a future
work meeting prior to finalizing the plans for state submission.

Item 5: A discussion regarding proposed time-limited off leash hours for dogs in city parks.
(25-015) 1:22:19

Policy Analyst, Melia Dayley and Councilors Handley and Christensen presented a proposal to
allow time-limited off-leash hours for dogs in city parks, suggesting the hours of 6:00–8:00 a.m.
and 6:00–8:00 p.m. daily. They explained that this proposal was intended as an interim measure
while the City plans and constructs dedicated dog parks over the next three to five years.

Councilor Hoban voiced opposition to the proposal, citing public safety concerns and expressing
a preference for enclosed, designated dog park areas. Police Chief Beebe also advised against the
proposal, citing anticipated enforcement difficulties and related challenges.

Council members expressed mixed views. Some supported the idea as a way to address current
dog owner needs, while others opposed any implementation of off-leash hours in open parks due
to safety concerns. Alternative ideas were discussed, including the possibility of installing fenced
areas within existing parks or creating a permit-based program.

Following discussion, the Council voted 4–3 in favor of a substitute motion. Councilor Garrett
moved to discuss potential off-leash hours (limited to 6:00–8:00 a.m.) at each of the upcoming
five district meetings to gather community feedback on designating one park per district for off-
leash use. Councilor Whipple seconded the motion. The motion passed 4-3 with Councilors
Bogdin, Hoban and MacKay opposed.

Item 6: A presentation regarding the FY2026 Budget: Administrative Services (Facilities,
Finance, Justice Court, Information Systems/Cybersecurity, and Recorder). (25-025)
2:57:21

John Borget, Director of Administrative Services, introduced the FY2026 budget presentations
from each division within Administrative Services.

Judge Steve Schreiner reported that the Justice Court processed nearly 10,000 cases over the past
year and continues to meet all required operational standards. He welcomed Council members to
visit the court at any time.

Rhiannon Newton, Court Administrator, noted that the Justice Court has no unfunded needs or
budgetary requests for FY2026. She reported on case disposition timelines and stated that the
court is actively working toward achieving a 95% case resolution target.

Finance Director Dan Follett provided an overview of the City's financial performance metrics
and departmental accomplishments. He highlighted the City’s maintenance of strong bond
ratings and recognition from the Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) for
excellence in financial reporting and budgeting.

Government Records Access and Management Act (GRAMA) requests. She reported a
continued increase in GRAMA requests and outlined efforts to improve response times and
records processing efficiency.

Travis Savage, Facilities Director, presented on energy efficiency initiatives and the completion
of key capital projects, including the new Fire Station 21. He also provided an update on future
plans, including the airport terminal expansion project.

Josh Ihrig, Director of Information Technology, summarized departmental service metrics,
cybersecurity initiatives, and recent technology projects. He emphasized IT’s role in supporting
all City departments and the department’s focus on optimizing current resources in the upcoming

taken on the budget items at this time.

Item 9: An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding the criminal offense of battery.
(24-035) 1:32:37

Matthew Griffiths presented a proposed ordinance to establish a new offense of battery within
Provo City Code. The ordinance is intended to address instances of unwanted physical contact
that do not meet the legal definition of assault. Griffiths explained that the ordinance would
apply to conduct such as unwanted kissing or touching, which can be difficult to prosecute under
current law.

Griffiths referenced recent local cases that prompted the proposal and noted that similar
ordinances have been adopted in other municipalities and states. Council members asked
questions about enforcement and the potential scope of the ordinance. There was discussion
about ensuring that the ordinance would not inadvertently criminalize accidental contact. The
Council indicated the proposal would be reviewed further prior to consideration at a future
meeting.

Item 15: A resolution approving a services agreement between the Redevelopment Agency
and Provo City. (25-039) 4:11:54

Brian Jones, City Attorney, presented a proposed services agreement between the
Redevelopment Agency (RDA) and Provo City. The agreement formalizes the City providing an
employee to serve as the RDA Director, with the RDA reimbursing the City for 80% of the
employee’s compensation.

Administrative Services Director Borget, clarified that the 80% figure is an estimate subject to
annual review and adjustment as part of the RDA’s budget process.
