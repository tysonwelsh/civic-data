staff’s opinion was that the proposed classification allows Gerber Collision and Glass to maintain its
non-conforming business operations, which will be a form of support to the existing and future business
in the City as mentioned in Economic Development Goal #3 set forth in the General Plan. Based on the
information submitted in the applicant’s supporting documentation and analysis of the relevant Code
sections and General Plan goals, staff recommended the Planning Commission move to approve the
Preliminary Subdivision Plat. The Planning Commission can approve, deny, or continue the matter.

3.      PUBLIC HEARING
        A Petition to the Planning Commission to Forward a Positive Recommendation to the City
        Council for an Ordinance Amending Multiple Sections of Title 17 in the South Salt Lake
        City Municipal Code to Update Regulations Related to the City Facility (CF) Land Use
        District.
        ACTION ITEM
        Applicant: South Salt Lake

Mr. Shum reported that both the City Facility Land Use District Code Amendment and the Zoning Map
Amendment were included in the request. The first petition was for the Planning Commission to
forward a positive recommendation to the City Council for an ordinance amending multiple sections
of Title 17 of the City’s Municipal Code to update regulations related to the City's Facility Land Use
District. The second petition is to amend the Zoning Map on the subject properties to change the zoning
from TOD to the City Facility Land Use District.

Ms. Ungricht reported that the purpose of tonight’s meeting is to address the redevelopment of the
City’s Public Works Campus. The current Public Works Campus is near the end of its life cycle. The
desire was to begin construction this summer. A rendering of the proposed design was displayed. The
City has been acquiring properties for some time to provide for a larger footprint and more space as the
City continues to grow. It was noted that the site on Oakland Avenue will eventually be abandoned.
The two proposed applications are the first steps in the process.

Mr. Shum presented an overview of the proposed ordinance amendments. First, the Definitions Section
will be updated with the “Storage Outdoor” to include screening as a requirement. The Land Use Matrix
Section will also reflect the addition of the parking lot use, which will become a permitted use in the
City Facility Land Use District. “Parking Lot” currently is not an allowed standalone use in the City,
however, it is an essential function needed for City operations. Mr. Shum reported that the police car
lot is separated from the station by a road. The new parking lot is not feasible elsewhere. The Police
Department wants to plat their lots but are unable to do so because of the current Code restrictions. The
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                                7
April 3, 2025

desire is to have the Code Amendment in place so that the Police Department can plat the parcel. The
intent is to allow the City Facility Land Use District to allow standalone parking lots as a permitted
use. This regulation has prohibited the City from providing public services in the most efficient and
effective manner as City facilities often need to store materials such as salt or dirt and equipment such
as vehicles and tools on City facility sites. As a result, staff felt that “outdoor storage” was needed for
the City to provide better services to the public. A standard will be added for solid screening. Solid
fencing will be required around outdoor storage in all City Facility Land Use Districts. The proposed
changes were presented to the City Council who expressed support.

The City Facility Description Section was presented. The purpose of the City Facility Land Use District
is to serve and protect the health, safety, and welfare of the public in South Salt Lake City. The
Community Development Director or his designee will be given the responsibility of reviewing and
approving all City Facility Developments based on the following three requirements:

       1.      The new development have a demonstrated need and not be contrary to the public
               interest.

       2.      The project must be consistent with the General Plan and applicable City ordinances.

       3.      Projects need to be consistent with the health, safety, and welfare of persons residing or
               working in South Salt Lake City.

A change was proposed to the City Facility Land Use District description to include more specific
standards to help ensure that all City Facility Developments will be consistent, promote the public
interest, and be consistent with the General Plan. Staff also included a specific standard regarding
screening to prohibit razor or electrical fences. The next change proposes the removal of building form
requirements from City Facility developments. Instead, the Community Development Director will
review and approve the building form inside the design standards in City Code. Staff has discovered
that there are many types of facilities throughout the City that fit the City Facility umbrella such as fire
or water stations and every facility will have different needs. As a result, building types in the design
standards do not fit the needs of the City and City Facility developments have been removed from the
design standards. It will instead be monitored by the Community Development Director or his designee
according to the three criteria set forth.

With regard to development standards, the setback standards will be removed along with the build-to
standards for City Facility developments. This is to ensure that City facilities can operate efficiently
and avoid unnecessary development expenses for things that are essential to the function of the facility.
With regard to subdivision and platting, a new cross section was added specifically for Oakland
Avenue. A separate cross-section design is intended to provide better and safer access for larger city
vehicles such as snow plows or utility trucks. The easement requirement was also removed from the
City Facility Land Use District. Staff will ensure that the easement requirement returns once the
property is sold for private development.

Mr. Shum presented the General Plan considerations and stated that the proposal meets the City’s goals
and strategies set forth in the General Plan. The Code Amendment will help the City serve the
community more cost-effectively and efficiently. Staff recommended the Planning Commission

forward a positive recommendation to the City Council to approve the ordinance amending multiple
sections of Title 17 of the South Salt Lake City Municipal Code to amend the Regulations and Standards

A question was raised about the design standards. Mr. Shum stated that they plan to remove it because
the City Facility Land Use District covers utilities and services. It was noted that the proposal does not
seem to fit in. The Fire Station design does not necessarily meet the City’s design standards as they are
not geared toward that purpose. Staff also discovered that many of the design standards create scenarios
where the Public Works Building will create an unnecessary expense that is not essential to the function
and cost the City more. The intent is to ensure that the building improves the streetscape and the design
standards create obstacles that are difficult for the City facility to meet. For that reason, staff asked that
they be removed but have the Community Development Director or his designee serve as the safeguard.

Ms. Ungricht reported that the proposed Public Works Building meets most of the flex building
standards. The front facade goes above and beyond what any flex building would meet in the Code but
the back portion goes along tracks and likely would not meet the window percentage. The intent of the
Code will be met. The front-facing portion is what will be visible to the public while the back-facing
portion will not be. As a result, they are being as cost-effective as possible. They are also working on
the sewer lift station for which there is no design standard. The same is true for the Fire Station. For
that reason, staff was proposing a Code change. Staff knows what is best for the City but there are
checks and balances in place. An audit has been done with other cities where similar there are similar
allowances for city buildings.

Commissioner Pechmann’s only concern was with the burden of ensuring that the building design is
not at the discretion of one person. He asked if the City had considered creating a limited design
standard so that the building matches the neighborhood and perhaps has more than one person watching
over it. The job of the Planning Commission and the Community Development Department is to ensure
that everyone follows the standards that are in place. His concern was with the public not knowing or
being able to look up a standard. He thought that perhaps a committee might be better. He stated that
the opposite is also true and has seen fire stations that are gabled and look very attractive. Commissioner
Pechmann did not consider that to be money well spent. He also did not want a fire station that looks
like the old one on 900 West.

Ms. Ungricht commented that for the Public Works Campus, there are a handful of City Staff members
who meet weekly. For any large City facility, more than one person signs off on it. A handful of staff
review and approve the design before it gets to the Building Permit phase. She stated that the level of
review depends on the scope and scale of the project.

Chair Carter commented that the City used to have a Design Review Committee established for land
use projects in the downtown. It was chaired by the Mayor and usually presented by staff. The feedback
from applicants was that it was an artificial and unnecessary step that should be done away with. As a
result, they have pared back some of the peer and administrative review as the process has been tested
with the City Council. The City Council has the authority while the Planning Commission is a conduit
in the process.

Mr. Weidenhamer stressed the importance of the voices of the Planning Commission Members being
heard and potentially making a recommendation that seeks third party or additional consideration. Staff
would continue to represent the Commission’s views accurately to the City Council. Commissioner
Slifka asked if it would be appropriate to have something in writing specifying who would have
oversight with these types of buildings. Mr. Weidenhamer responded that it is implied because the
budget for the buildings cannot be approved. Nothing can be spent on any of the proposed buildings
unless approved by the City Council. Commissioner Pechmann stated that if an undesirable building
comes forward, the City Council can ask that it be revised. The Planning Commission is essentially
exempting itself from the specifics of the design standards but not the spirit.

Chair Carter stated that the City Council is comprised of elected officials who are signing off on
projects. If they are not in line with the desire of the general public, they will face the consequences.
He asked about the outdoor storage and was of the understanding that it is not allowed in any other land
use district. Mr. Shum confirmed that was the case. Mr. Weidenhamer clarified that there are different
types of outdoor storage. One is for essentials such as sand, gravel, and dirt. There are also police
evidence cars that need to be outside. The decision was made for the City to hold itself to different
standards and allowing outdoor storage has been of concern to some in the City. It creates a visual
burden but staff feels there is a strong benefit to the public by allowing the City to treat itself differently.
They have to be able to store some items outside because the cost of indoor storage is incredibly
expensive and would not serve the residents well.

Commissioner Slifka was concerned that when it is windy sand, salt, and other materials being stored
outside will blow around. She would want those items to be contained. Mr. Weidenhamer stated that
they will be contained on the east side of the site. There will also be a salt shed on the southwest corner
that will be fully enclosed with a roof. Items that could potentially blow around are lighter and will be
enclosed on at least three sides.

Chair Carter opened the public hearing.

Jeff Kane thanked the Planning Commission for their efforts. With regard to checks and balances, his
understanding was that any design will come before the public who can comment. Chair Carter reported
that the public can ask questions and make comments. Mr. Kane commented that there are checks and
balances in place currently involving the public.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Chair Carter asked about procedure and if the City were to build a new police station if there would be
an opportunity for the public to provide input. Mr. Weidenhamer stated that due to the specifics of the
site, staff has worked closely with the adjacent property owners. They have met with them individually
and talked about design standards, cadence, construction impacts, the sidewalk, etc. If they were to
build a police or fire station in a neighborhood like this one, it would be much different. There would
have to be very intentional neighborhood meetings and discussion about specifics such as sirens, noise,
and storage. He was confident that if this was in a neighborhood or an infill area the process would be
very different and would have started with much deeper community conversations regarding need and
location. The process would have been much different in terms of approval. In this case, staff did not
