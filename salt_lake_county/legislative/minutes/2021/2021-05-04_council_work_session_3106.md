3.15 Update on Salt Lake County ’s Coordinated Response to 
COVID-19
The Council may take action, including votes, on any 
necessary legislative matters related to the ongoing State of 
Emergency
21-0541
Attachments: Staff Report
County Council Briefing -- 05042021
Vaccination Briefing to Council -- 05042021
Presenters: Mayor Jenny Wilson; David Schuld, Intelligence Section Chief; 
Gary Edwards, Health Department Executive Director (Approx. 30 mins). 
Discussion/Direction
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  stated the County is starting Covid vaccine walk -in 
clinics.  Children ages 12-15 will soon be eligible for the vaccine.
Mr. Gary Edwards , Director, Salt Lake County Health Department, stated 
Salt Lake County Covid cases have plateaued since the end of March.  This 
is welcome as compared to a significant increase.  Although testing remains 
constant, there has been an increase in cases among 10- to 19-year-olds.  In 
some cases, these infections can be traced back to someone who has 
traveled, but the wide variety of variants through the state make it difficult to 
determine the source of an infection.
Mr. David Schuld , Covid -19 Intelligence Section Chief, delivered a 
PowerPoint presentation.  He reviewed key numbers, an active case curve, 
infections by zip code, cases by age, the rolling 14-day comparison, testing 
analysis, and known and potential exposure sources.
Mayor Wilson  stated First Lady Jill Biden will visit one of the County ’s 
vaccination clinics during her visit to Utah on Wednesday.
3.16 Discussion and Approval of Animal Services Patrol Request 21-0539
Attachments: Staff Report
Animal Services Budget Request..pptx
Presenter: Talia Butler, Director of Animal Services (Approx. 30 mins). 
Discussion/Direction
Page 8 of 12
Council Work Session Meeting Minutes May 4, 2021
Ms. Talia Butler , Director, Animal Services Division, explained the 
proposed patrol request and delivered a PowerPoint presentation.  She 
reviewed the call volume in County parks, call response times, the focus 
areas of County owned parks, and the goals of the project.  The two -year 
pilot program would include two new FTE ’s, two temporary employees, two 
vehicles, and new signage.  People are using the parks inappropriately and 
rules need to be enforced.
Ms. Melinda Seager , Director of Intergovernmental Affairs, South Jordan 
City, expressed concern about this plan as the areas in question are South 
Jordan’s jurisdiction.
Council Member Snelgrove  stated jurisdictional and signage confusion 
could lead to delayed response times.
Council Member Stringham  noted the wide variety of opinions on this 
issue from many different municipalities.  Additional help is needed for 
problems in the parks and she is not opposed to funding that.  However, 
more work needs to be done on the proposal and she asked that the matter 
be tabled.
Mayor Jennifer Wilson  thanked Ms. Butler and her team for the effort put 
into this problem.  This is the first time she heard a city say it did not want 
resources from the County.
A motion was made by Council Member Stringham, seconded by Council 
Member Bradshaw, that this agenda item be tabled. The motion carried by a 
unanimous vote.

3.19 A Solution to a Sustainable Climate Future Presentation 21-0538
Attachments: Staff Report
CCL Presentation - Salt Lake County Council
Page 10 of 12
Council Work Session Meeting Minutes May 4, 2021
Sponsors: Council Member Jim Bradley and Council Member Arlyn Bradshaw
Presenters: Jim Wightman, CCL SLC Media Liaison and Marc Peterson, CCL 
SLC Steer Committee Member (Approx. 20 mins). 
Informational
Council Member Bradley  stated climate change affects the health and 
wellbeing of the earth, and the local environment.  This presentation by the 
Citizen Climate Lobby will explain an effective, efficient, affordable solution.
Mr. Jim Wightman , Media Liaison, and Marc Peterson , Steering 
Committee Member, Citizen Climate Lobby, delivered a PowerPoint 
presentation.  They covered climate change ’s impact on Utah, the current 
period of consequences, and increased priority of climate change by 
politicians.  Their solution involved charging a fee on fossil fuels at the 
source and returning 100 percent of the net revenue to households as a 
dividend.  This would create a strong economy, create jobs, and save lives.
