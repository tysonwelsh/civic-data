32   The first agenda item involved a rezone of property located at 1166 East Fort Union Boulevard
33   from Office, Research and Development (“ORD”) to Mixed Use (“MU”). The property consists
34   of 1.089 acres of property and four parcels. One parcel has an existing building on it and the
35   remaining three parcels consist of parking stalls in the existing parking lot. The rezone request is
36   consistent with the City’s Land Use Plan. The area has been planned for MU zoning since the
37   original Cottonwood Heights City General Plan was adopted. The area is also consistent with the
38   City’s Fort Union Boulevard Plan, which calls for MU development along the corridor.
39
40   Mr. Hulka reported that the MU (Mixed Use) zone is the most flexible zoning designation and
41   allows for a number of different uses. The primary reason for the rezone request was the setback
42   requirement. The parcels are relatively small and the ORD zone is typically intended for office
43   buildings with larger parcels. The setback for the ORD is 50-feet on all sides. The setback in the
44   MU zone with no adjacent residential is 20 feet in front and 10 feet on the sides and rear. Mr. Hulka
45   stated that the Planning Commission can reduce the setbacks further if necessary.

 7   Mr. Johnson discussed other items on the agenda, including the Planned Development District
 8   (“PDD”) approval of a rezone and development plan at the gravel pit site located at 6695 South
 9   Wasatch Boulevard. He also presented an agenda item related to an amendment of the affordable
10   housing provision in the PDD Ordinance. It stems from the applicant’s proposal for the gravel pit
11   application. 10% of their multiple family units will be affordable to households earning 80% or
12   less than the area median income (“AMI”) of the City. The PDD Ordinance currently requires
13   50% AMI. The applicant indicated that there are challenges with providing that number of units
14   at 50% AMI and is asking to change the requirement to 80% AMI.
15
16   There were discussions with the City Council a few weeks earlier and now the applicant is back
17   with a proposed text amendment to the AMI provision in the PDD ordinance. An approved
18   amendment would affect this application as well as future PDD projects in the City. Mr. Johnson
19   stated that the intention was still to provide affordable housing. Commissioner Ryser asked where
20   the 50% AMI number originated. Mr. Johnson clarified that it was in the ordinance. The current
21   housing proposal included 419 total residential units between the condos and apartments, which
22   means that 42 were required to be affordable units.
23
24   Mr. Johnson reported that the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development defines
25   affordable housing costs as 30% of gross monthly household income. The State of Utah’s
26   guidelines on affordable housing are defined as:
27
28      •   0-30% of AMI – extremely low income;
29      •   31-50% AMI – very low income;
30      •   51-80% AMI – low income; and
31      •   81-100% AMI – middle income.
32
33   The Utah Department of Workforce Services stated that: “Affordable rental housing for moderate-
34   income renters in Utah is becoming increasingly scarce,” which Mr. Johnson confirmed. He noted
35   that trends currently lean toward luxury apartments. The AMI for Cottonwood Heights City was
36   $86,207 in 2019. Using that data, the maximum affordable cost in the 30 to 50 percent AMI range
37   would be $1,078 per month. In the 50 to 80 percent AMI range, it would be $1,724.
38
39   Several affordable housing strategies from the City were identified including:
40
41      •   Implement zoning incentives for low to moderate-income units on a long-term basis;
42      •   Utilize a moderate-income housing set aside from a community reinvestment agency,
43          redevelopment agency, or community development and renewal agency; and
44      •   Allow for higher density or moderate-income residential development in commercial and
45          mixed-use zones, commercial centers, or employment centers.

 6   Mr. Johnson presented the staff report and stated that the request is for an amendment to the Below
 7   Market Rate (“BMR”) Housing Requirements of the Planned Development District Ordinance.
 8   He noted that the item was discussed extensively during the Work Session. An amendment to the
 9   ordinance would impact the gravel pit development as well as future PDD applications. The
10   current Code requires that BMR, senior, or disabled housing units be provided in all PDD
11   developments that have at least 50 units. In those developments, at least 10% of the units must be
12   affordable to households not earning more than 50% of the City’s area median income (“AMI”).
13   The amendment request is to change that household income threshold from 50% AMI to 80%
14   AMI. The current housing proposal includes 419 total residential units between the condos and
15   apartments, which means that 42 are required to be affordable units. At a federal, state, and local
16   level, affordable housing is defined as households earning 80% or less of the AMI.
17
18   Cottonwood Heights City updated the Affordable Housing Master Plan in 2019. The plan notes
19   that the AMI in Cottonwood Heights is $86,207. There were approximately 27% of households
20   in the City earning 80% or less of the AMI. This indicates a clear need for affordable housing
21   units. The City identified strategies as required by State Code to implement affordable housing,
22   including:
23
24          •       Allow for a higher density or moderate-income residential development in
25                  commercial and mixed-use zones, commercial centers, or employment centers.
26          •       Implement zoning incentives for low to moderate-income units on a long-term
27                  basis.
28          •       Utilize a moderate-income housing set-aside from a community reinvestment
29                  agency, redevelopment agency, or community development and renewal agency.
30
31   The proposed amendment does not harm the City’s ability to comply with the Affordable Housing
32   Master Plan or to meet the state goals. 80% AMI qualifies for affordable units just as 50% AMI
33   does. City Staff had been preparing a preliminary PDD revision:
34
35          •       A minimum of 20% of units shall be provided if affordable to 51-80% AMI;
36          •       A minimum of 10% shall be provided if affordable at or below 50% AMI;
37          •       A mix of the two may be approved by City Council.
38
39   The intent of the proposed revisions was to provide more flexibility for developers. Mr. Johnson
40   described the concept as a sliding scale and noted that the current Code does not offer flexibility.
41   Rockworth has indicated that there is difficulty in financing and creating the 50% AMI units. With
42   80% AMI there are more conventional options to attempt to finance the units. The staff
43   recommendation was to consider a modification to the BMR provisions.
