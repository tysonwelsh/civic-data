    H. Public Hearing: Ordinance 2020-13, Text Amendment – amending Chapter
       17.18.030.030 and creating Chapter 5.39 of the South Jordan City Municipal Code
       regulating model homes. (By Planning Director, Steven Schaefermeyer)

Planning Director Schaefermeyer reviewed the background information on this item. He said
they need to better regulate how long the model homes can be established. The changes add a
requirement for builders to get a business license. The requirements for the model home would
remain the same. Business licenses are renewed yearly. They can’t renew the business license

once there are no homes to be sold in that development. There is a grace period to sell the model
home. It also clarifies that model homes in one development can’t be used to sell homes in
another development.

Mayor Ramsey opened the public hearing.

Lauri Gale, 10883 S. Martin Gale Lane, asked why have this level of regulation for model
homes? It seems like overkill. She noted that she is a realtor by profession. This could make
things expensive for builders and could be hard to comply with.

Mayor Ramsey closed the public hearing.

Planning Director Schaefermeyer said this would require a business license, but it is a different
category than a Home Occupation license. In some cases, the builder would also need a
contractor license, but that is only required in the city where the contractor is based.

Planning Director Schaefermeyer said these regulations would not be unique to South Jordan.
They are based, in part, on what other high growth communities are doing. Renewing a business
license annually is not onerous. He said the reason for the change is because they have a builder
trying to take advantage of the current regulations. There are no remaining lots in McKee Farms.
He runs a model home, but also a contractor business out of the model home. It has created a
traffic concern and some problems with signage in this residential area. There may be a second
builder who thought they would also move their business to a model home.

Council Member Shelton asked how much would the annual business license cost? Mr. Preece
said he believes the initial fee is a little over $100, and then approximately $49 for an annual
license.

Council Member Harris asked if this would be required per model home? Mr. Preece said that is
the way it was designed.

Council Member Zander said she is in support of this. She said there are many builders in
Daybreak that have several model homes. She asked if it would be equitable to say one license
per builder, or one license per development? Mr. Preece said that is at the prerogative of the City
Council. The fees are based on the cost to administer the license. He said the current Ordinance
doesn’t require a business license, so this is a way for the city to recoup the cost of the service
they are receiving. Council Member Zander said she is happy with the Ordinance, but she would
like to be thoughtful of the builders with several model homes.

Mr. Schaefermeyer said they could have one license per builder that lists each model home.
From an administrative standpoint, they would need to determine which models homes are active
and which ones aren’t. They do not generally have problems with model homes. This is a new
trend that hopefully will end.

Council Member Harris recommended they pass the Ordinance without the requirement for the
business license, and then only track it if it becomes a problem. Mr. Preece said that is where
they are at with the current policy.

Council Member Marlor said he is okay with the concept of one license per development. They
should prohibit doing home sales in South Jordan for homes outside of the city. Mr. Preece said
the other point of the proposed code requirement is a separate license for each location. They
looked at doing it through business licensing for tracking purposes. They could look at paying
one fee, but having separate licenses.

Council Member Zander said she is okay with one fee per builder, she is not okay charging one
fee per door. She wants the builders in Daybreak under one fee. She does not like to put a heavy
handed blanket policy when there is one misbehaving model home.

Mr. Preece said there is concern that the issue was starting to be a trend.

Council Member Harris asked if this can be enforced without a business license? Mr.
Schaefermeyer said if they take the licensing out, they need to figure out another mechanism.
Mr. Preece said they are having to prove that there is a business that is not allowed in the zone.
This simplifies that. Council Member Marlor said they need the Ordinance to let the builders
know that they can’t be selling houses all over the valley from one location.

Council Member Harris said he is opposed to it because he does not want to take action on all
builders.

City Attorney Loose said if they do a fee or not, they can address it in the fee schedule. They
need a process for enforcement. If they don’t do a business license, they have to put it
somewhere else that would be similar. With business licensing, there is an appeal process. The
difficulty of the process is minimal. In the case of the McKee Farms model home, people have
entered the neighbor’s home thinking they were going into the model home. They can’t find the
model home because the area is built out and so people enter near the sign. If they give someone
a permit, there has to be an appeals process.

Mayor Ramsey said she shares Council Member Harris’s concerns. She wants to be a city that is
easy to work with. City Attorney Loose said they could require one permit per builder. It would
only be needed for the model home. Mr. Preece said they can call it a model home permit
instead, but they still have to figure out the due process.

City Attorney Loose recommended they table the issue and let staff work on it more. Council
Member Harris asked that staff make it as easy as possible for the builders, but still accomplish
what the city needs.

Council Member Marlor made a motion to table Ordinance 2020-13, and let staff provide
further clarification with the idea that they have one license per builder who has a model
home in the city. Council Member Zander seconded the motion.
