DESCRIPTION OF REQUEST
Mrs. Jen Archuleta (Applicant) has submitted a request for the Planning Commission to consider a potential modification to the previously approved Conditional Use Permit for the 7-Eleven convenience store located at 711 W. 9000 S. (See previous staff report attached in Legistar at Sandy City, Utah - Calendar (legistar.com))

BACKGROUND
The Planning Commission reviewed this request on December 17, 2020. After listening to testimony given by the applicant and other residents, the Planning Commission discussed the factors that need to be considered and the facts related to them in order to modify or revoke a Conditional Use Permit pursuant to Title 21-33-6. The Planning Commission tabled this item and asked for additional information to be gathered by staff that can help inform whether the Planning Commission should modify, revoke, or sustain this Conditional Use Permit.

Staff met with two Planning Commission members to identify exactly what information would be helpful to the group. The information gathered was (1) a comparison of the two different Planning Commission findings and conditions on the October 18, 2018 and March 21, 2019 meetings (Exhibit #1), (2) a summary of the criteria found in Title 21-33-6 and discussion of each criteria from the December 17, 2020 meeting (Exhibit #2), and (3) BNA Consultants were asked to review the previous light and sound studies and compare those to current site conditions (Exhibit #3).

There were a couple other questions/concerns that were raised by the Planning Commission members from the December 17, 2020 meeting. They are identified below with a staff response.
    1. Information regarding the fence line.

        Previous to the 7-Eleven being built, there was a six-foot masonry wall along the rear property line of the residential lots. During the 7-Eleven site plan and conditional use permit review, the developer agreed to install a 10' masonry wall between the commercial and the residential property and it was to be constructed with comparable construction techniques and materials as the existing fencing on the residential properties (see conditions of approval from March 21, 2019). The previous light and sound studies were based off a 10' masonry wall between the residential and the commercial properties. The new masonry wall was built in the same location as the previous wall, however, there is a grade difference between the commercial and residential properties. The grade difference increases from east to west. At the southwest corner of the property the site was raised approximately seven feet from existing grade.

        The City has verified that the wall was properly installed as per plan location and height.

ANALYSIS
The Planning Commission should consider the relevant sections of the City Code regarding a modification or revocation of a Conditional Use Permit (below).

Section 21-33-6 – Modification or Revocation

All approvals of Conditional Use Permits are conditional upon additional reviews, after the use has been established, to determine if the actual detrimental impacts and effects have been mitigated or eliminated to meet the General Standards set forth herein and to evaluate whether they continue to be effective in doing so. If the use does not meet the General Standards, additional conditions may be imposed to achieve compliance. The Planning Commission, on its own motion or upon a staff recommendation or after receipt of an applicant's request, may hold a hearing upon the question of modification or revocation of a Conditional Use Permit granted under or pursuant to the provisions of this Section. The Planning Commission may modify the conditions under which the conditional use was granted or revoke the Conditional Use Permit if the Commission finds:

(1)     The actual detrimental effects or impacts are greater than anticipated.

(2)     The permit was obtained by misrepresentation or fraud.

(3)     The use for which the permit was granted has now ceased for at least six consecutive calendar months.

(4)     One or more of the conditions of the permit have not been met.

(5)     The holder or user of the Conditional Use Permit has failed to comply with any City, State or Federal law governing the conduct of the use.

     This is a revised report from the BNA original sent 24 November 2020. All changes will be italicized and underlined as shown.

1    INTRODUCTION
     Sandy City issued an RFP requesting an analysis of the existing site lighting and noise of the 7-Eleven property located at 711 W. 900 S., Sandy, UT 84070. Comprised within this report are the results of the light and noise study, including light spillage onto six (6) adjacent properties, an analysis of the possible impacts on the residences of vehicular traffic from the site, and possible mitigation efforts for both lighting and noise. Finally, at the end of this report, a comparison analysis of the results between these findings and those from the original noise and photometric study conducted by Spectrum Engineers, dated "received" 20 December 2018.
     On Wednesday, November 18, beginning at 10pm, light and noise level readings were taken in the backyards of six (6) residences directly to the south of the 7-Eleven store and along the north side of the property wall (the 7-Eleven side). Height above finish grade measurements were taken of the building-mounted fixtures on the south, east, and west exterior walls of the 7-Eleven as well as the pole-mounted fixtures. Visual observations were made of the impact of headlights on the homes from vehicles entering the property from the west and east entrances.
     A second visit was made to the site on Friday, November 20, beginning at 10pm, to continue and finish the noise analysis.

2.1  Photometric (refer to Figures 1 & 2)
     Vertical readings were taken at several locations along the north facing sides of the homes at approximately 7'-0" above finish grade. There is a sharp shadow-line from the barrier wall that separates the homes from the 7-Eleven along the north, exterior walls of the homes. Moving west, the shadow drops lower and lower on the walls until it is no longer a factor but by then the homes are far enough away that readings are still at essentially zero. Horizontal foot-candle readings were also taken at several locations in each yard. All readings on the walls and in the yards were at zero fc except yard #2 where vertical readings were 0.1 fc.
     Vertical and horizontal foot-candle readings were taken along the north side of the fence as well. All readings were zero except when nearing the west side of property #2 where verticals went from 0.1 fc to 0.7 fc then dropped back down to zero.
     The building-mounted floodlights were measured to be at 11'-0" above grade. The pole-mounted fixtures were measured to be 20'-0" above grade (17'-0" poles on 3'-0" concrete bases). All fixtures appear to be the same type that were indicated to be installed on the construction plans. The building-mounted fixtures do provide significant glare to the residences of homes #1, #2, #3, and #4 with direct line-of-site to the LED modules. The two pole-mounted fixtures on the south side both have house-side shields on them, which reduces the glare and direct view of the LEDs for homes #1, #2, and #3. For homes #4, #5, and #6, which are downhill, west of the 7-Eleven site, the shields are less effective, yet the glare is significantly reduced due to distance. Distance, like lighting shields, is another factor in reducing the glare for the building-mounted fixtures.
     Headlights from vehicles entering the site from the east entrance were observed impacting homes #1 and #2 depending on the type of vehicle and the speed the vehicle was traveling. Higher speed seems to induce more "bounce" to the vehicle elevating the headlights a bit but, again, this depends on the vehicle. As vehicles are driving south on the east side of the 7-Eleven to exit the east side, the headlights, for the most part, hit the wall and did not impact the homes. Although, the taller the vehicle, the higher the headlights that could cause some spillage onto home #1, however was not observed during testing.
     When vehicles entered the site from the northwest entrance, initially there was headlight impact on homes #4 and #5, and a bit less on home #6. Then, if vehicles turned east along the drive and then south, headlights impacted home #3 more significantly and for a longer duration.
     The topography of the site is such that vehicles entering from the east are little impacted by it, but vehicles entering from the northwest and those that traverse the site from north to south are driving uphill, which tends to aim the headlights up impacting the residences.

3.1  Photometric
     There are several options to help reduce the glare and the level of foot-candles on the south side of the property, predominately coming from the building-mounted fixtures:

Option 1
    • Reduce the number of building-mounted fixtures.
        o There are four (4) fixtures on the south side of the building. At least one of these could be removed – possibly (2) – and still have adequate lighting for driving, walking, and safety. However, this solution could possibly be at odds with 7-Eleven corporate requirements.

Option 2
    • In addition to removing fixtures (refer to Option 1 above), or as a stand-alone solution, the fixtures on the south, northwest and southwest halves of the building could be dimmed to at least 50% of output after 10pm. The two pole lights on the north side could also be dimmed.
        o Further investigation would be required to determine if the fixtures have dimmable drivers already installed and if a different lighting control system would be needed to provide this functionality.

3.2  Noise
     By definition, noise is sound that has become a nuisance, which is relative to the beholder. What could be pure sound, including background sounds, to one person could be perceived as noise to another. Therefore, I recommend one of the following options:

Option 1
    • Provide taller barrier walls at the property line in order to limit the line-of-site between 9000 S. and residents.
        o The confines with this are costs for constructing such a high barrier as well as near limitless height definition to satisfy the need to remove line-of-site sound directivity.

Option 2
    • Instead of providing a taller wall at the property line, provide a barrier wall along 9000 S.
        o This will be closer to the source and limit the line-of-site better than constructing a massive, unsightly wall close to the properties.
Option 3
    • Provide large, matured trees or other greenery that will limit the line-of-site as also referenced in the lighting recommendations.
        o The plants would need to be dense in order to effectively break the sound up. However, during winter when the leaves have fallen, the effects of the dense leaves will limit performance.

lens/lamps of the fixtures from the 1st level windows of the closest homes and in the backyards
as well.
        In conclusion, while taking actual footcandles readings of the impact of headlights at
the 2nd level windows of the homes was not possible within the scope of the November 2020
report observations of what is actually occurring seems to be greater than the Dec 2018 report
indicates in large part due to the fill that was added to the site during construction.

5    CONCLUSIONS

        The main issues with any excessive lighting and noise are the line-of-site between the
sources and residences. Essentially, if the source can be seen, it can be heard. Distance and
frequency bands also play a role in the ability of hearing noise sources; however, these cannot
be controlled. Providing barriers, eliminating or dimming some lighting sources, or a
combination of these as indicated in the "RECOMMENDATIONS" section above should help
mitigate many of the issues.
        The limitations to the test measurements are those pertaining to the particular days
and times indicated in this report and any extraneous circumstance is not included.
