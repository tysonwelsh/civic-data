   2.1. Discussion relating to the purchase of sound equipment – Wendy Thomas,
        Assistant City Manager
This item was discussed after item 2.5.

Assistant City Manager Thomas stated staff received five bids back for sound equipment and
pointed out the Council would need to consider with the new non-profit group, there would
be events which would be part of the MOU. She said the Council would need to determine if
there was a desire to rent or lend the equipment out for those events. Councilmember Steve
Shields thought it should affect the amount of money the groups received if they did not have
to pay to rent the equipment.

Assistant City Manager Thomas discussed they had faced challenges in finding secured
storage. Councilmember Shields commented they had been working Jesse Edgel, who had
indicated when the equipment was all packed into cases it would all fit into an 8x10 room just
fine. There was discussion about places it could be stored.

Assistant City Manager Thomas said another thing to consider was Herriman did not have
anyone on staff who could use the equipment effectively, and Councilmember Shields said
there were a lot of tier one sound technicians who could be contracted.

Councilmember Shields said there were two console options, the Midas one and a smaller,
more sophisticated one, which he expected most of staff would be able to operate pretty
easily. Councilmember Jared Henderson asked what it would cost annually to contract with
someone to operate the equipment, to which Councilmember Shields replied they would not
need any sort of special operator for almost all City uses, except for big events like the rodeo
and Town Days. He estimated those days would cost around $150 per night.

Councilmember Shields reported Mr. Edgel had indicated he would like the interested parties
who would be responsible for the equipment to come out and unbox, identify, label, and
understand how it all went together. Councilmember Shields said Mr. Edgel had expressed
his willingness to teach the basics on how to run it, and Councilmember Shields further stated
September 28, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                    Page 3 of 14

the equipment as well.

   2.2. Friends of Herriman Parks, Trails, Arts and Culture Update – Wendy Thomas,
        Assistant City Manager
This item was discussed after item 2.3.

Mr. Chris Berbert presented on the non-profit organization setup. He indicated the purpose
of the organization was to build a community of individuals who would donate time,
resources, and money to growing the parks, trails, arts, and culture of Herriman. He said at
this point, they needed to establish board members. He reported at this time, they were
September 28, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                     Page 4 of 14


working on their bylaws, and the website had been established, the buildout of the donation
portal was underway, and the first project was underway as well.

John Cazier introduced himself as vice president of the Friends of Herriman. Mr. Cazier
reported the first project was the Eric Dowdle who was commissioned to put together a
puzzle for the organization which would help with funding. He reported Herriman Baseball
was going to be a sponsor on the puzzle and the proceeds would be returned back into the
baseball program.

Mr. Berbert reviewed some of the roles for the non-profit organization and noted it would
establish an avenue for monetary donations to support committees like the Arts Council. It
would also provide a way for community involvement, help to enable a vision for future of
City in the arts, parks, trails and culture. The organization would review and approve annual
budgets, look over and confirm tax returns before filing 990 form to keep non-profit status.
It would help with marketing, ensuring other needs of committees with City, and soliciting
donations.
The Committee responsibilities include to schedule, plan and run events respective to its
committee. Budgets would be submitted to the Board to approve annually and could help
fundraising efforts for respective committees as well as recruitment efforts.

Mr. Berbert touched on the Arts and culture quote: Roots have to be strong to grow a
community. The branches that come as it grows is what makes it beautiful. There is nothing
that would drive the world leading economy like creativity and there is nothing that drives
creativity like the arts. It is vital that the organization foster them and invest in them.

The Parks and trails could promote physical activity and community engagement and provide
both environmental and mental health benefits as well-designed parks have been shown to
reduce stress and foster community interaction.

Assistant City Manager Thomas indicated the group was waiting for the 501c3 status and the
operational plan would be forthcoming in the next year. She observed this could create a
duplication of committees and would need to look to see if those needed to be transitioned
out from City jurisdiction. The Council indicated there was no need to have two different
committees functioning similarly.

Mr. Berbert indicated as the committee continued to grow, it was important to maintain a
positive City relationship. The Council agreed.

   2.3. Consideration of the proposed Master Development Agreement (MDA) for Teton
        Commercial at 11840 S and 11918 S Mustang Trail Way in the C-2 (Commercial)
        Zone. (File No. M2022-065) – Michael Maloy, City Planner
September 28, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                        Page 5 of 14


City Planner Michael Maloy said this had been reviewed and recommended for approval by
the Planning Commission. He gave a presentation reviewing the project located at the corner
of 11800 South and Pioneer Street.

City Planner Maloy stated the site was 18.3 acres with potential pads along the frontage roads
for some sort of anchor tenant. He said the MDA included a proposed waiver of repayment
by the City for some trail improvement along 11800 South. He added the MDA also maintained
there be 60-percent brick or stone on all the buildings, but it did allow for 35,000 square feet
of CMU on the split face block. City Planner Maloy said overall, they wanted to maintain a
greater consistency in design and signage throughout the project, and said the General Plan
supported this as C-2 zoning. He also noted the plan included sidewalks which would be put
in by the developer to coincide with the new General Plan’s emphasis on walkability in the
area.

City Planner Maloy discussed the need to adjust how restaurant parking was calculated and
said they had looked at projects in other cities to get a sense of what the standard included.
In this case, the applicant wanted to have two parking spots per one hundred square feet of
space, or one spot per fifty square feet, which he noted was in line with what was generally
done throughout the region.

City Planner Maloy next presented the proposed color board, which he indicated was similar
to the anthem commercial. He then listed the setbacks would be 20 feet from back of curb,
with ten feet of required landscaping.            He shared the Planning Commission’s
recommendations, which identified some items to be considered for amendment, including
parking. City Planner Maloy said the statement from the City Engineer also supported parking
reductions, as well as only landscaping in setbacks next to the public street. Councilmember
Ohrn asked what the benefit would entail by reducing the setbacks. City Planner Maloy
replied smaller setbacks helped with walkability of the area and made it more pedestrian
friendly. City Planner Maloy also discussed the plan was still to maintain the park strip and
trail.
