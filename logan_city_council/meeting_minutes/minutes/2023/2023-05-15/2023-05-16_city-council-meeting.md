MAYOR/STAFF REPORTS:

Flood Update – Sam Odd, Public Works Program Manager

Sam Odd, Public Works Program Manager gave an update on the flood status in Logan
City. He stated that daily, multi-checks are being done on the Logan and Blacksmith Fork
Rivers. All departments have coordinated together in watching the river. During the
night, the Police Department is watching key locations for debris or other concerns.
Crews are going out daily to clear out debris from under bridges and other problem areas.
As far as the flood outlook, the Blacksmith Fork River has reached the flood stage and
appears to be turning downward. That is the prediction at this time, but that may change.
By next week, the Logan River will begin to rise nearing flood stage and will continue to
rise. The Logan River will be carefully watched.

It is estimated that 40,000 sandbags have been filled. There have been 7 sandbagging
events in neighborhoods that have a propensity for flooding.

Councilmember Jensen, who resides in the Sumac neighborhood asked if the sandbags
currently in place along the river are the approximate level the river will rise.

Mr. Odd answered that this is the approximate estimate based on what the flood plain is

PUBLIC HEARING - Consideration of a proposed resolution adopting an
amendment to the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Program Year
2019 (PY2019) Annual Action Plan (AAP) – Resolution 23-15 – Mike DeSimone,
Community Development Director

At the May 2, 2023 Council meeting, Community Development Director Mike
DeSimone addressed the Council regarding the proposed resolution.

Amendment Summary

Mr. DeSimone stated that in response to the Coronavirus Pandemic (COVID-19) the U.S.
Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) awarded Logan City an initial
Community Development Block Grant Coronavirus (CDBG-CV) allocation of $299,912
in early 2020, and a second CDBG-CV allocation of $207,849 in 2021, all of which was
authorized by the Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security Act (CARES Act),
Public Law 116-136, which was signed by President Trump on March 27, 2020, to
respond to the growing effects of this historic public health crisis. The total CDBG-CV
allocation for Logan City was $507,761. We have spent approximately $297,597 with
$210,163 remaining.

The purpose of the CDBG-CV funding was to support the “prevention, preparation, and
response” to the coronavirus pandemic (COVID-19). The $297,597 were used to fund
emergency food and shelter, rental assistance, PPE’s, telehealth systems, non-profit
operational support, and vaccine clinics.

In transitioning from immediate needs relative to the pandemic, the remaining covid-19
funds can be reprogrammed to meet broader CDBG goals of serving low/moderate
income residents while still adhering to the original intent language to “prevent, prepare,
                                                                                    5 | Page
               Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ May 16, 2023
and respond” to the coronavirus pandemic. We are proposing to amend the 2019 Annual
Action Plan and allocate the remaining funds ($210,163) for trail improvement projects
serving low to moderate income Logan residents. More specifically, we are proposing to
use up to $160,000 on the 1800 South (Spring Creek) Trail.

Proposed Annual Action Plan (PY2019) AMENDMENT 3

     •   Allocate $210,163 of remaining CDBG CV Funding towards Trail
         Improvements serving low/moderate income Logan residents designed to
         prevent, prepare for or respond to future coronavirus pandemic impacts by
         providing suitable outdoor recreational space meeting social distancing &
         spacing requirements. Approximately $160,000 will be used for the 1800 South
         (Spring Creek) Trail. According to HUD guidance, the use of remaining CDBG-
         CV funding for public facilities are allowable provided the following three
         criteria are met:

    1. Eligible Cost. Construction costs associated with a public facility such as a trail
         or sidewalk serving low/moderate income residents are all eligible costs under
         CDBG.

    2. Tieback to Coronavirus. The proposed project (Spring Creek Trail) will
         increase the width of the existing 1,300’ trail from a 6’ wide sidewalk to a 10’
         wide trail able to safely support more people with greater levels of social
         distancing. The trail runs north from 1800 South and ties into the existing trail
         system running parallel to the Logan River. The tieback to coronavirus is that by
         adding additional trail width, we are better able to provide safe outdoor
         recreational amenities that meet minimum social distancing parameters and
         therefore “prevent, prepare for, or respond to” the coronavirus pandemic.

    3. CDBG National Objective. The location of this specific trail improvement
         project is within an area containing a significant percentage of low to moderate
         income residents; therefore, this project meets a CDBG national objective.

(Continued from May 2, 2023) - REZONE – Consideration of a proposed rezone

16 – Russ Holley

At the May 2, 2023 Council meeting, Planner Russ Holley addressed the Council
regarding the proposed rezone. At this meeting, the Council determined to continue the
Ordinance. The Council requested a more specified deed restriction to limit the number
of 6,000 sq ft. and number of 4,800 sq ft. units on the property if the lots could not be

developed according to the design. The Council also requested further comments be
obtained from the school district.

City Attorney Craig Carlston stated the proponent offered a deed restriction limiting the
density to 5.3 units per acre. The deed restriction applies to all recorded parcels. The deed
restriction will be recorded tomorrow and continue to apply even if there is a subsequent
purchaser. There is a maximum of 84 townhomes to be constructed instead of the
originally intended 126 units. There will be 6,000 sq ft. lots with a minimum of 24 lots. If

Councilmember Simmonds reiterated that the Council listened to the neighborhood who
are willing to accept a compromise and requested the developer along with staff cut down
on density to reassure the neighborhood and mitigate the impact of the development.

Vice Chair A. Anderson echoed Councilmember Simmonds' comment. With the number
of townhome units being lowered, it should resolve the issues of traffic and parking. The
housing availability in the community needs to be addressed as well. A concern has been
raised by State Legislation that if communities do not add housing, the State will step in
and regulate housing taking the ability away from the communities y to regulate their
own.

The Council echoed Vice Chair A. Anderson and Councilmember Simmonds remarks.

Councilmember Simmonds asked if the developer had agreed to do all the street
improvements.

Mr. Carlston answered that the improvements have been included in the deed restriction
as part of the building phases which will include sidewalk, curb, and gutter.

Mr. Holley responded they are currently working on a zone overlay where a project may

has a wetland.

Vice Chair A. Anderson clarified the Council does not meet nor discuss as a quorum
behind doors. Rather the developer is communicating with staff and as necessary with the
Council to express concerns. Hence, why Mr. Carlston presented the deed restriction to

(Continued from April 18, 2023) - CODE AMENDMENT – Consideration of a
proposed Code Amendment 17.13, 17.14, 17.15 & 17.62 (Public Zone & Homeless
Shelters). Logan City is requesting a Code Amendment to the Land Development
Code, Chapter 17.13 “Public Zones” to include a purpose/intent language for non-
profit uses in a public zone; amend Chapter 17.14 “Public Zone Uses” to include
behavioral and mental health receiving centers, homeless resource centers and
homeless shelters in the public zone as a conditional use; amend Chapter 17.15
“Development Standards: Public Zones” to include a proximity standard for
homeless resource centers and homeless shelters; and amend Chapter 17.62
“Definitions” - Ordinance 23-10 – Mike DeSimone

Community Development Director Mike DeSimone addressed the Council and stated that
at the April 18, 2023 the Council deferred action on this item to allow time for
representatives from the Bear River Mental Health and Bear River Health Department to
meet with City representatives to discuss a future Receiving Center to be located in
Logan. Based on those discussions, the proposed approach to zoning and project review
remains unchanged while the proposed Land Development Code language in Section
17.15.050 regarding a proposed proximity limitation has been narrowed. He has also
added an additional statement to the definition of Community Service in Section 17.62
specifying that “temporary housing for domestic violence survivors or victims” are
included as a community service.

Councilmember Simmonds thanked Bear River Mental Health Services for the honest
discussion. The Council echoed the same sentiment.

Vice Chair A. Anderson inquired if case management services are still permitted under
the new code.


                                                                                 9 | Page

Mr. DeSimone answered case management services have not been mentioned or changed,
case management would still exist as is.

Vice Chair A. Anderson asked if it would be possible to state how the restrictions for
close proximity were determined lest they be considered detrimental.

Mr. DeSimone replied the intent is not to stigmatize the homeless facility by using the
restrictions used for alcohol as set by the State. Rather we are trying to consider what
areas are not compatible or suitable for this type of facility due to traffic, areas, schools,
etc. That is why we set the process as wide via two-level process to permit it through the
city.

Councilmember M. Anderson spoke in favor of the receiving center having been in a
similar situation as a caregiver and uncertain where to go to receive support. The Cache
County Sherriff's Department is in favor as well.

Councilmember Simmonds stated the intent to move forward with the existing definitions
is to create a path forward for a receiving center. Should the need arise to adjust, change,
or add to the definition of the ordinance, it can be done as needed.

Vice Chair A. Anderson said the WAB Warming Center Board had requested the Council
continue the ordinance to involve further discussion to include them as well; and with this
concern in mind, she elected to not vote in favor of approving the ordinance.
