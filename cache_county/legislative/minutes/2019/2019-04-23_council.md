Proclamation — Utah Family Month/Kindness Campaign — The Family Place —

Esterlee Molyneux, Executive Director, stated April is usually Child Abuse Prevention
Month. This year The Family Place is asking that the period between Mother's Day
and Father’s Day be adopted as Utah Family Month. Molyneux briefly reviewed The
Family Place’s purpose and operations and delivered the “Be Kind Utah” challenge
to record one million acts of kindness in the state of Utah in a forty-day period.
Molyneux invited the Council to a statewide press conference on Wednesday, May 1,
2019 at 1:15 p.m. to launch the “Be Kind Utah” campaign. Molyneux thanked the
county for its support and invited the Council to a May 15, 2019 lunch at Herm’s to
learn about The Family Place.

o Presentation of Findings of Community Survey on Substance Abuse and
Opioids - USU Student Group — Student Researchers collected 676 surveys and

found a large percentage of people believe opioids are a problem but don’t know
what help is available in their community. Full study results are available at

jessica.lucero@usu.edu. Dr Jessica Lucero said public health is doing a good job
and churches are a good avenue for point of contact, but sees a need for community

awareness campaigns.

e Corporation of the Episcopal Church in Utah — Vice Chair Worthen asked Deputy
Auditor Dianna Schaeffer to report on findings. Schaeffer indicated the statute
states “exclusive use” and the non-profit organizations renting space from the
Episcopal Church are not religious organizations. The Cache County Assessor's
Office and the Cache County Auditor’s Office do not believe an exemption can be
granted on the Champ House and land because of the non-religious uses
occurring therein. Therefore, they recommend a partial property tax exemption
based on religious use be granted. Clerk/Audior Zollinger reminded the Council
the Church can appeal to the state if it desires.

ACTION: Motion by Council member Zilles to follow the recommendation of the
Auditor’s Office for a partial property tax exemption for the Corporation of the Episcopal
Church in Utah. Tidwell seconded the motion. The motion passed, 5 aye — Erickson,
Tidwell, White, Worthen & Zilles and 1 nay —- Borup. (Borup favors approval for the full
exemption) Ward absent.

e Discussion — Update on Criteria for Rural 2 (RU2) and Rural 5 (RU5) Zone Rezone

Requests — Chris Harrild said the Planning Commission appreciated the February
meeting with the Council. Since a sunset of RUS and developing criteria for the zone
were both “no go’s” Harrild said he decided to just look at the context of the surrounding
properties in the interim. Harrild discovered the county GIS maps need to be refined to
give more context — adjacent properties, homes, uses, condition of properties, roads,
etc. Roads will be a major qualification — if the road doesn’t meet county standards, it's
a“no.” This approach will be used until the general plan is completed. Director Runhaar
interjected development in the county does not benefit the county. Cities have clearly
delineated roads, water lines, sewer systems, etc. The county does not. Everything we

MINUTES OF THE HYRUM CITY HISTORIC PRESERVATION COMMISSION
FEBRUARY 26, 2019 IN THE KINDRED READING ROOM,
50 WEST MAIN STREET, HYRUM, UTAH.

CONVENED: 6:17 PM

THOSE IN ATTENDANCE: Chairman John Petersen, Commission Member Steve Downs,
Commission Member Jami Van Huss, Commission Member David Bates, Recorder Janna
Bushman, City Council Liaison Craig Rasmussen, and potential committee member Patrick
McGill.

WELCOME and REVIEW OF MINUTES: John Petersen welcomed everyone to the meeting,
thanking them for their attendance with a special welcome to Patrick McGill. Janna Bushman
moved to approve the minutes from November 27, 2018, David Bates seconded, all voted aye,
and the minutes were approved unanimously.

BUSINESS:

1. Officer Elections

John Petersen read through the commission bylaws concerning officer elections. Afterwards the
commission nominated and seconded the following: Roger Allen as Chairman, David Bates as
Vice Chairman, and Janna Bushman as recorder. All votes were unanimous.

2. Elite Hall and Foundation Update
Jami Van Huss shared with us that Aggie TV did a story about Jazz Nights at Elite Hall bringing
more positive publicity to the building and its uses.

The commission reviewed the materials for the Elite Hall RAPZ presentation. They were
impressive. The data shows that Hyrum City is very deserving of funding. It also showed the
regular and growing use of Elite Hall as a center for community and area-wide events. The
RAPZ grant is due next week and will be finished for submission tomorrow.

David Bates is continuing to pursue the possibility of having a significant statewide event hosted
at the Elite Hall to show the Hall’s potential as a revenue source for Cache Valley and to
strengthen the likelihood of receiving a RAPZ Grant.

Organizers for the Cache Valley Cowboy Rendezvous have approached the Elite Hall
Restoration Committee with the opportunity to run two food booths as a fundraiser on Friday and
Saturday, March 2nd and 3rd. Jami will send everyone a link to an online sign-up to help.

John Petersen shared a story from Elite Hall’s past of the regular Friday night dances held there
for years after the end of World War II, and how people came from all across the valley and
throughout the region (telling a specific story about travelers from Pocatello, Idaho).

After reviewing the IRS website and meeting with Craig Rasmussen, Jami concluded that the
Elite Hall Foundation would be best served to re-apply rather than create a new organization. She
will commence that paperwork once the RAPZ grant and CLG grant are submitted.

Elite Hall continues to be used regularly and community events (specifically, Jazz Nights) prove
to be big successes with great attendance and good will felt by everyone.
