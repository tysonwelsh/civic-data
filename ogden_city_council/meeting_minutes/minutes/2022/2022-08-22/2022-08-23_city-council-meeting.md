Acknowledgements: Rebecca Tierney of the Ogden City Diversity Commission stated that in honor of Education Month
and the back-to-school season, the Diversity Commission would like to recognize four outstanding educators making a difference
in the community on a daily basis. She read the biographies of Trena Roueche, Christina Graff Charbonneau, Gisela Lopez
Alonso, and Kim Richards.

Council member White stated she is so appreciative of the hard work that teachers have performed in the recent years as
the education system was crippled by the COVID-19 pandemic; students will do great things because their educators gave them
the ability to do so.

Acting Chair Lopez stated he is an educator as well and he is excited to see fellow teachers recognized for their efforts.

Council member Richey stated these four teachers are representative of so many other great educators in the community
and he thanked them for the service they provide.

Recognizing retired Weber State Basketball head coach Randy Rahe for his
contributions to the Ogden community through his career with the Wildcats

A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of Weber State University (WSU) Head Coach Randy Hale for his
contributions to Ogden City came before the Council for consideration. Council member Richey read the certificate of
recognition issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Caldwell and presented Coach Hale with the certificate.

Mr. Hale thanked the Mayor and Council for recognizing him; he stated his time with the University has gone by very
fast and he is grateful that WSU took a chance on him. It has been an honor and privilege to be the head coach of the program and
he took his role very seriously. He, his staff, and players have always tried to represent the University in what he felt was the right
way; they treated everyone with respect and integrity, and he tried to build a strong culture for the program. The team received
great support from the school, but also from the entire Ogden community; he has had chances to move to other jobs throughout his
career, but he loved this community and wanted to raise his family here. He thanked everyone for their support.

Council member Richey stated that when he was anew member of the Board of Directors of the Wildcat Club, he had the
opportunity to travel with the basketball team to a game at UCLA; Coach Hale and his wife treated him very well and like he was
part of the team and his family.

Public Comments

Don Mueller stated he is a resident of Ogden City and a graduate of Weber State University (WSU); he has operated a
real estate appraisal business for the past 45 years in Ogden and he wished to address the ‘flip your strip’ program this evening.
He lives in the Mountain Pines Subdivision, which is located at roughly 46" Street and Skyline Drive. He made an application as
soon as the program was launched; he believes he followed all program guidelines, but his application was rejected, and he was

informed he would not receive a rebate. He is aware that the City has $100,000 available for rebates, along with a matching
$100,000 from the Weber Basin Water Conservancy District. He acknowledged that his community is gated and private, but
beyond those gates, there are yards, sidewalks, and park strips just like in every other neighborhood in the community and the
residents that live there are very intent on saving water. The homeowner’s association (HOA) for the neighborhood has assessed
every homeowner $1,800 to remove sod and install appropriate landscaping. The HOA was dismayed that their application was
rejected because he knows that there is money available. He stated that after continued discussion with a City staff member, he
was able to get approval for the section of park-strip along Skyline Drive, but he would like to make the entire subdivision a
model subdivision for the community, and he hopes the City will consider amending the program to make funding available for
private subdivisions.

Angel Castillo thanked the Council staff for their efforts to make City Council meetings widely accessible through Zoom
and YouTube broadcasts. She was grateful to be able to participate in the 4:00 p.m. work session to give input regarding the
developer selection process for RDA projects. She then addressed the ‘flip your strip’ program; if the City wants to encourage
people to perform projects aimed at conserving water, it is up to the City to make sure that it is accessible to all taxpayers. Those
who live in the private community referenced by Mr. Mueller are taxpayers who contribute to the community and if they are
interested in creating a ‘model subdivision’ as he mentioned, they should be rewarded.

Sebastian Benitez stated tonight he is here to thank the Mayor and Council for making a dream possible by completing
the sidewalk around Wasatch Elementary; this will improve the safety of children that walk to and from the school. He thanked
several individuals who were instrumental in bringing that project to fruition. He then stated that he would like sidewalk along
Taylor Avenue between 32™ and 36" Streets, but for now he is grateful for the work that has been completed thus far.

Travis Pate discussed historic preservation efforts in the Ogden School District in the last years; citizens have voted
against bonds that would have eliminated historic schools. But, when presented with a bond that would preserve and improve
upon existing historic schools, the bond passed. Ogden City’s efforts should mirror those of the school district when it comes to
opportunities to redevelop and improve historic buildings in Ogden’s downtown. He suggested that the fire prevention systems at
various historic buildings be inspected to determine if improvements should be made to prevent devastating fires. He then
provided information about the Art and Bloom event that will be held in downtown Ogden this weekend.

Timothy Renee Felker discussed the City’s activities at the Ogden Airport; starting in 2013, he began to create a plan for
his retirement in 2019 or 2020. His business relates to maintenance and repair of aircraft, and he purchased a hangar at the Airport
to house his operations. He visited with the current Airport Manager to discuss moving the fence line near his hangar to provide
public access to Airport Road, similar to arrangements for other hangars in the area. The Airport Manager told him that would not
be a problem and that it could be worked out, but as part of that discussion they discussed hangar leases for the long-term and he
learned that the City considered the residual value of hangars to be theirs and that lease renewals would be ending in the near
future based on a ‘yet to be released’ plan. He stated he was shocked to learn this since the three previous Airport Managers had
told him he would be able to renew his lease so long as he was meeting the terms of the lease. Shortly after learning this new
information, he changed his business plan because he feels the hangar is critical to his success. He has spoken with other hangar
owners and listed his hangar for sale, but the best offer he got was 50 percent of his asking price. His asking price was only
slightly higher than his original investment in the hangar, which was $82,000 in 2014. He had always planned to get most of the
money he had invested, if not all of it, back to fund his retirement. The way it looks now is that he will not be able to do that;
Ogden City included a provision in his lease that requires the improvement to be removed within 60 days of the end of the lease.
The City Attorney has stated in public meetings that the hangar owners would not take their hangars down, but that is incorrect,
and he plans to take his hangar down.

Heath Satow stated he was glad to hear the Redevelopment Agency developer selection process discussed during the
work session this evening. He agreed with Council member Hyer on anumber of the points he made during that discussion, but he
believes this is only the beginning of the process; unfortunately, he sees little improvement coming from this discussion unless the
City drastically increases the percentage of the projects that are widely advertised through a request for proposals (RFP) process.
He appreciates the new requirement for the Board to be notified when the Agency enters into an exclusive negotiation with a
party, but he truly wants to see more developers selected through a public process rather than a private process. The chart
information that was presented to the Council tonight were somewhat misleading; no matter how the information is sliced up, only
20 percent of the properties that have been acquired using public funds have been publicly advertised to for proposals from
developers to determine who has the best idea and will provide the best level of investment. He would prefer that each property is
publicly advertised in order to ensure the best option for each property; the Board should value the properties more than they
currently do. He concluded competition is healthy and should be encouraged.

Mayor Comments

In Mayor Caldwell’s absence, Chief Administrative Officer Johnson reported Administration has spent a great deal of
time talking about the flip-your-strip program and eligibility for HOA communities. The City Attorney’s Office has felt that park
strips in private communities should not be defined as public space and there may be different processes required to grant funds to
residents living in those communities. When the program was first launched, decisions were based upon that advice, but
Administration is willing to consider changes to the program or reconsider applications that have been denied. He pointed out that
residents who live on streets with no sidewalk are also not eligible for the program. He acknowledged that the program is not

Council member Comments

Council member Choberka addressed Mr. Pate’s comments and asked if there is a requirement in the building code for all
new buildings to have fire suppression systems. Mr. Johnson stated there are many factors that must be considered when
determining whether fire systems are required; he agreed that it is important to consider such improvements in order to protect
historic buildings in the community.
