3. Presentation and Reports
3.1) Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Presentation and Priorities Discussion -
Mountainland Association of Governments.
Claudia Saldana with Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG) presented the
Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) priorities for Lehi's upcoming five-year
Consolidated Plan. She outlined that Lehi receives approximately $300,000 annually from HUD,
which can be invested in housing, public services, public facilities, improvements, and economic
development. Using a community survey, she highlighted residents' priorities, with housing
emerging as the top concern, followed by streets and utilities, community services, and business
support. The presentation involved having council members allocate five total points across
various funding categories to help determine future investment priorities. Claudia noted they
would continue gathering input from other stakeholders, including service providers and residents,
to create a comprehensive plan. She plans to conduct a separate survey specifically for Lehi
residents to ensure more localized feedback, as the initial survey had limited participation from the

5. Consideration of Resolution #2024-55 authorizing and approving the Power County
   Power Project Power Sales Contract with Utah Associated Municipal Power Systems;
   and related matters.
   Joel Eves, Power Director presented both Contracts to the Council. They engaged in a
   comprehensive discussion about two power project contracts with Utah Associated Municipal
   Power Systems (UAMPS), involving a 360-megawatt base load plant in Power County Idaho
   and a 200-megawatt peaking power plant in Millard County. The projects are strategically
   designed with an initial growth projection of 5%, transitioning to 1% growth by 2035. Mr.
   Eves clarified that the total costs are shared among the project participants. He explained how
   the Project Management Committee (PMC) will oversee critical decision-making and Lehi will
   be a committee member. There was discussion about the PMC's authority, financing
   mechanisms, and long-term implications.
   There was discussion of Lehi’s future power needs, emphasizing flexibility and careful
   planning.

3.2 Discussion of Transportation System in North Lehi (From Pre Council Agenda)
Luke Seegmiller, Lehi Traffic Engineer, presented an update to Lehi’s transportation system.
The transportation discussion revolved around a proposed freeway-to-freeway connector project
linking I-15 and Mountain View Corridor. Mayor Johnson and city staff met with UDOT
representatives, including Director Carlos Braceros, to discuss traffic projections and potential
road improvements. They explored a new alternative (4b) for the freeway connector that better
aligns with existing interchanges, with the potential to significantly reduce traffic on Porter
Rockwell Road and 2100 North. Mayor Johnson noted that the meeting was notably productive,
marking a shift in UDOT's approach, as they acknowledged and seriously considered the city's
traffic projections, which had been a point of contention in previous discussions. The
presentation also highlighted alternative transit options, including expanded bike lanes and
pedestrian pathways, as part of a comprehensive mobility plan for the region.
