       3.      Public Hearing and Recommendation to the City Council for a General Plan
               Amendment    to Adopt the Taylorsville Expressway Station Area Plan ( 1G24 —
               GPLAN- 000427- 2024; Applicant: City of Taylorsville; Mark McGrath)

Mr. McGrath introduced the agenda item, explaining that the requirement for this plan
came        from   the State       legislature,      specifically    UCA § 10- 9a- 403. 1.      He    mentioned        that

Taylorsville had three stations on the new BRT/ MidValley Express system that required
what was essentially a small master plan. Mr. McGrath stated that they had been working
on the plan for well over a year.
Mr. McGrath then introduced the consultant team: Rob Weaver from KGRW Architects,
Bob Northfield from BCT Design,                   Erin Talkington         from RCL Co.,       and Andy Hubbard, of
Great Basin Engineering.
Mr. Weaver           presented         an   overview     of the    discussing the team
                                                                    station   area    plan,
composition, objectives, and existing conditions of the three BRT station areas along
Taylorsville Expressway. He explained the diversity of proposed housing choices. He then
turned the time over to Erin Talkington to address current economic conditions.

Ms.     Talkington        then    addressed       economic          and    market
                                                                       highlighting the
                                                                                     strategies,

importance of identifying redevelopment sites and taking an incremental approach to
development. She also mentioned various funding tools and strategies for encouraging a
wider range of housing types.
Mr. Weaver returned              to the     podium      to   address      the land   use   vision    for the   area.    He

discussed the density ranges and the importance of mixed- use development near the
stations. He also explained the green open space network and transportation hierarchy
proposed in the plan before introducing Bob Northfield.
Mr. Northfield        explained        that the hope was for the creation            of a dense,     walkable,   vibrant

community with a variety of different types of housing. He also reminded the planning
commissioners that the property was privately owned, so there was no way to tell how
long it would take for the plan to come to fruition. He described Taylorsville Expressway
as a spine, with the three BRT stations acting independently, but also being unified by
common goals and planning principles.
Mr.    Weaver         then     addressed       the     Planning      Commission        regarding      implementation

possibilities, again pointing out that none of the properties were currently owned by
Taylorsville. Still, presented a spreadsheet with various action items to take place either
in 1- 5 years or More Than 5 Years. Some of these items were zoning/ regulatory in nature,
while others were more focused on economic and market strategies. Still others pertained
to housing, transportation, utilities and open space. He asked Ms. Talkington to address
some of those economic and housing strategies that the city might employ.
Ms. Talkington discussed the city incrementally budgeting for" beefing up" redevelopment
areas, explaining it would not have to be a huge amount of money right away. Instead, it

Mr. Harper appreciated the planning commission' s thoughtful consideration of the plan.
He stressed that they had the opportunity to share their vision for Taylorsville with the city
council. One of the goals was to maximize the public investment in areas of mass transit
and to ensure that there were multiple types of housing available nearby. Some details
might change over time, but he hoped the green space would remain. He encouraged the
commissioners to read through the rest of the plan, study it, and ensure they were
comfortable with the vision it presented.

MOTION: Commissioner Wendel moved to table the item for additional discussion
                      at the April 8th meeting. The motion was seconded by Commissioner
                      Young and passed unanimously.
