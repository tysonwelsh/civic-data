Proposed Ordinance 2022-2 approving the request to rezone 975
Washington Boulevard from Community Commercial Zone (C-2) to
Regional Commercial Zone/Conditional Overlay Zone (C-3/CO)

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed rezone at 975 Washington
Boulevard came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Wasatch Peaks Credit Union is petitioning to rezone their
property from C-2 to C-3/CO to allow for an impound/repossession lot. The planned impound lot is located just west of the credit
union and would be developed as an accessory use to the main building. Repossession lots are not allowed in the current C-2 zone.
While rezoning to C-3 is technically spot zoning, the Planning Commission found that placing a conditional overlay on the
property would prevent any unwanted uses from taking place on the property. Because of the conditional overlay, it would be
necessary to also approve a development agreement. The proposed development agreement would help ensure that the property
can only be used for an impound lot connected to a financial institution. Additional visual buffers—such as a 6’ fence and
landscaping—would be placed around the impound lot to lessen any negative visual impacts to the homes across the street.

Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and stated the Planning Commission found the rezone request to
be consistent with the Lynn Community Plan because the rezone would support the expansion ofan existing permitted business. It
is proposed that a conditional overlay be included with the rezone, to restrict the use of the property to a credit union and an
accessory impound lot. Other uses will not ever be allowed on the property unless it undergoes another rezone. The development
agreement was formulated to enforce the increased restrictions on the property. The agreement will require the following:

e No use may take place on the property other than a financial institution and its associated impound lot.
e The development must follow the approved concept plan.
e The impound lot must follow Utah State Tax Commission standards, and tow trucks must meet all necessary
state certification requirements.
The screening fence around the impound lot shall be made of a 6’ EcoStone fence.
Screening gates will match the EcoStone fencing.
The two parcels must be combined to create a single lot for the project.
Vehicle traffic on the site shall direct loading and unloading activities away from Kiesel Avenue. All loading
and unloading shall occur within the screened area with vehicular exiting onto Kiesel Avenue.
Establish a financial guarantee for the landscaping.
Loading and unloading can only take place between 8:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m.
No dismantling or salvaging will occur on the property.
There will be a 30’ landscaping buffer along Kiesel Avenue.
e Interior access lanes will be paved with asphalt.

Chair Nadolski introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2022-2, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the zoning map of Ogden City, as adopted by Section 15-3-3 of
the Ogden Municipal Code, to reclassify as Regional Commercial Zone/Conditional Overlay Zone (C-3/CO)
property heretofore classified as Community Commercial Zone (C-2), generally located at 975 Washington
Boulevard, and providing that this ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final
passage.”

A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder have
at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.

Chair Nadolski then called for public input regarding the proposed ordinance.

No persons came forward to be heard regarding this matter.

Proposed Ordinance 2022-3 approving the request to rezone 3432 Ogden
Avenue from Single Family Residential Zone (R-1-6) to Regional
Commercial Zone/Conditional Overlay Zone (C-3/CO)

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed rezone of 3432 Ogden
Avenue came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Lindquist Mortuaries is seeking to rezone the property for
the purpose of expanding their existing crematorium/columbarium building. The property currently has a home on it, and the
petitioner is planning on demolishing the home and extending the existing mortuary to the south. The property is under the R-1-6
zone, but it will be necessary to rezone to C-3/CO to accommodate mortuary services. The C-3/CO zone would match the zoning
of the mortuary to the north, and the development agreement has been written as an amendment to the existing development
agreement that the mortuary has with the city. The Planning Commission unanimously recommended approval of the rezone,
citing consistency with the General Plan and the community plan.

Deputy Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated the Planning Commission found this petition to be
consistent with the General Plan, including the T.O. Smith Community Plan policies. They recommended approval of the petition.
The rezone would include a development agreement that would limit the use of the property to a crematorium, and it would set
forth other conditions to create a buffer next to residential uses. The development agreement would include the following
conditions:

e The crematorium will be the only allowed use on the property.

e Only one building may be on the property.

e The rezoned parcel must be consolidated with the parcels to the north to create a single property encompassing
the crematorium business.

e A screening hedge or 6’ tall screening fence of vinyl or masonry or wood must be installed on the southern
property line to screen the business from homes.

e The lawn must have berms on the east side of the pavement area to buffer the access area from the home to the
east (or obtain a waiver of fence requirement from the Planning Commission).

Proposed Ordinance 2022-4 approving the request to amend the Municipal
Code regarding changes to standards for an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU)

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed zone text amendment
relating to accessory dwelling units (ADUs) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated in November 2021, the
Planning Commission and City Council held a combined training and discussed amendments to state code regarding accessory
dwelling units. As a result of the state’s amendments, the City was required to update the zoning code to comply with new state
regulations. The most significant change is the removal of restrictions on how large an internal accessory dwelling unit (ADU)
can be. This zoning code amendment would allow I[ADUs to be any size. The amendment would also remove a requirement for
owners to get a business license for ADUs. Other minor changes are being proposed and are listed below. It is important to note

Proposed Ordinance 2022-5 approving the request to amend the Municipal
Code to modify regulations regarding mobile food trucks and mobile food
trailers

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed zone text amendment
regarding mobile food trailers came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Councilmember White submitted a
council initiative form to amend the City’s zoning ordinance to allow mobile food trailers to operate within city rights-of-way ina
similar manner to mobile food trucks. The proposed ordinance was reviewed by the Planning Commission which forwarded a
recommendation of approval to the Council. The proposal would allow mobile food trailers, as defined in the Code, to operate
with nearly all the same standards as mobile food trucks which are permitted in most commercial zones including the Central
Business District (CBD) zones.

Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and stated the current proposal is to amend the City’s zoning
ordinance to allow mobile food trailers to operate within the public right-of-way in a similar manner to mobile food trucks.
Operation would be allowed in the C-2, C3, CBD and CBD-I zones under conditions set forth in the ordinance. The Planning
Commission reviewed the proposal at its November 3, 2021, meeting and forwarded a recommendation of approval to the
Council. The Planning Commission’s recommendation was made based on the finding that the proposed amendment will be
consistent with the General Plan and will be in the best interest of the City. One Commissioner voted against the recommendation
indicating that the mobile food trailer operator should obtain a business license from Ogden City so that Ogden City may benefit
from the tax revenue. It should be noted that state law allows a mobile food trailer operator to obtain a business license in one city
and operate in another. The City cannot override the state law in that regard.

Council member Hyer stated he shares the concern of the Planning Commission who voted in opposition to the text
amendment, but he wondered if there is a remedy to that issue as state law trumps the City’s ordinance on that matter. Mr.
Montgomery stated that is correct.

Chair Nadolski introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2022-5, entitled:

“An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah, amending the Ogden Municipal Code by amending Section 15-13-37 to
revise provisions regarding mobile food trucks and mobile food trailers; amending Subsection 15-34-2.A to
amend uses in the Intensive District; amending Subsection 15-38-4.A to revise special regulations regarding
mobile food trailers; amending Subsection 15-38-5.M to revise general uses; and by providing that this
ordinance shall become effective immediately upon posting after final passage.”

A copy of the proposed ordinance was deposited with the Deputy City Recorder and ordered that the City Recorder have
at least one copy available for public inspection in her office during all business hours.

Chair Nadolski then called for public input regarding the proposed ordinance.

Cindy Toone stated that she lives adjacent to the property at the corner of 25" Street and Porter Avenue, which was
discussed during tonight’s work session. She is very concerned about the size of the proposed mixed-use project that would be
