Daria Evans, living in the Villas neighborhood, questioned removing the fee for businesses and

Councilmember McCumber replied with the business impact. He felt the fee was unwelcoming

7.   WORK SESSION
     7.1. EZ Park Presentation and Discussion
     Dave and Cam Terry, with EZ Park, presented their parking enforcement program and
highlighted its success in other cities. They emphasized an approach that avoids towing, noting
that towing often creates conflict, and instead demonstrated an automated camera system that
reads license plates and issues digital citations. The program also includes digital permitting,
with reporting provided directly to the city, and is designed to be scalable as a comprehensive
parking management solution. They noted that signage would be installed by EZ Park to inform
residents, with an estimated implementation timeline of 30 days.

     The Council discussed parking enforcement operations, including citation costs, permitting,
and towing procedures. Councilmember Wood asked about patrol practices and costs.
Councilmember Wood questioned permit pricing and the number of vehicles allowed per permit,
it was clarified that the Council could establish those limits. Councilmember Holdaway
requested that enforcement information be directed to the Code Enforcement Officer.

    Jacob Schooley, living in the Lake Front neighborhood asked about the customization of the
escalation policy as well as for clarification on the monthly fee for a pass. A discussion ensued.

    Steven Moss, living in the Bridgeport neighborhood, asked for clarification on data storage
and where specifically the license plate data would be stored, expressing data privacy concerns.

     8.3. Adopt Resolution 2026-20 To Form A Community Events Non-Profit
     The Council discussed a resolution to establish a community events commission and
facilitate the creation of a nonprofit organization to support city events and encourage
volunteerism. The intent was to shift events toward a more community-driven model, with the
nonprofit enabling fundraising and tax-deductible donations. Councilmembers asked clarifying
questions about the relationship between the commission and the nonprofit, governance
structure, and legal considerations, including oversight, leadership roles, and the process for
drafting and approving a charter.
    City Attorney Jesse Riddle advised that the resolution primarily serves as a starting point,
with additional structure and policies to be developed later. The Council debated language to
ensure appropriate oversight and clarity, ultimately amending the proposal to authorize the
mayor to lead the nonprofit’s creation, require council approval of appointments and the
nonprofit charter, and allow the commission to elect its own leadership.
   Mr. Pearce asked for clarification on the process of having the mayor appoint the chair of the
commission and not having the commission itself vote later to appoint the chair from their
membership.
    Motion: COUNCILMEMBER MCCUMBER MOVED TO ADOPT RESOLUTION 2026-

     The Council discussed a proposed neighborhood commission (Item 8.5), with initial
suggestions to move the item to a future work session for further refinement. Discussion

to engage directly with the Council regardless. Alternative approaches were discussed, including
relying on existing processes such as traffic calming petitions to ensure representative input.
