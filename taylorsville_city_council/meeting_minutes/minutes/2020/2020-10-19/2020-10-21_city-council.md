1.        WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

      Chair Harker called the meeting to order at 6:30 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance. A
roll call was conducted, wherein all councilmembers were present.

          1.1   Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Youth Council
          (Opening Ceremonies for November 4, 2020 to be arranged by Mayor Overson)

Brandon Sorensen lead those gathered in the Pledge of Allegiance before Savannah Medico read
a quote, “Where you see defeat, others see victory.” She explained the importance of viewing
one’s hardships and struggles as opportunities to learn and grow.

          1.2          Mayor’s Report

     Mayor Overson reported that the 2020 U.S. Census had been concluded. She explained that
federal funding was received based on the final census figures and the data she had showed that
Taylorsville had a 76.8% participation rate.

She had recently attended two ribbon cuttings for new businesses. The first was at TVille Nutrition
which had opened earlier in the year at the Crossroads of Taylorsville. The business had delicious
meal-replacement shakes and protein drinks that tasted even better than they looked. She wished
the business every success.

The new Target store had also just held its soft opening. It was beautiful inside and she encouraged
everyone to take advantage of the opportunity to shop there.

Mayor Overson provided an update on the progress of the new performing arts center. Some of
the interior woodwork and furnishings would be delayed due to the pandemic. She anticipated
that the project would be complete in January and a June date had already been reserved for an
exciting event that she could not yet announce. The Mayor expressed appreciation to the Tourism,
Recreation, Cultural and Convention (TRCC) board for providing funding for the project, despite

having to make so many cuts on a variety of other worthy projects. She was grateful for the City’s
partnership with Salt Lake County.

Progress was also being made in the area in front of city hall and she expressed her appreciation
and for the city employees who were constantly on the lookout for potential grant funding.

Along with Rep. Jim Dunnigan, Mayor Overson had recently visited Chair Harker’s third grade

The students learned about what they could do to contribute to the community and about the

Speaking of voting, Mayor Overson pointed out that there was a ballot box outside of city hall and

via the City’s website and weigh in on what they would like to see the land development code
address. The City’s general plan was also being updated which was another year-long endeavor.
Both projects would affect Taylorsville for many years to come and public input was welcome.

Mayor Overson explained that the justice court had been functioning well under very difficult
circumstances. Taylorsville continued to mourn the loss of Judge Kwan, with his quick wit and
humor, and his ability to change people’s lives.

speed limit signs in the area which had a direct effect on the safety of neighborhood children. He
asked that the City appropriate funds to provide more speed limit signs.

          3.1          Youth Council Report – Brandon Sorensen

      Kevyn Smeltzer of Salt Lake County Public Works reviewed the Mutual Aid Interlocal
Agreement for Utah Public Works Emergency Management. He explained the agreement was
necessary to ensure that Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) reimbursements were
available in the event that resources were used during a declared emergency. He pointed out how
critical it was that local agencies worked together.

Councilmember Armstrong asked if the agreement created a new entity. Mr. Smeltzer responded
that it did not. It was simply an agreement for cities to help one another during emergencies. He
referred to a recent windstorm wherein some cities were hit harder than others and ended up being
overwhelmed and needing assistance from neighboring municipalities. This agreement allowed
those assisting agencies to be reimbursed for their costs by FEMA. Councilmember Armstrong

asked if the City would be directed to purchase resources. Mr. Smeltzer indicated it would not. In
the event of an emergency or a request for assistance, any entity could choose whether or not they
were in a position to assist. The agreement was currently being administered by Leon Barrett of
Salt Lake County Public Works, but it was hoped that it could eventually be handed off to the
County’s Emergency Operations Center. Over 30 agencies in the Salt Lake Valley had already
signed the agreement and utilized it after not just the windstorm but the 4.5 earthquake that took
place earlier in the year.

Mr. Taylor pointed out that in essence, Taylorsville already had access to the assistance since Salt
Lake County Public Works had authority to work in Taylorsville. The bills for the extra work
would simply be passed from the county to Taylorsville.

Chair Harker asked how long the agreement had been in existence. Mr. Smeltzer responded that
it had been around for several years.

Scott Harrington indicated that the agreement was helpful in obtaining reimbursement from
FEMA. Oftentimes the federal agency would decline to reimburse a city if they did not have such
an agreement in place prior to the emergency.

Chair Harker asked if it was the City Council or the administration that would sign such an
agreement. Both Chair Harker and Councilmember Burgess were interested in having the
administration review the agreement.

6.        FINANCIAL MATTERS

          6.1    Resolution No. 20-41 - A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville approving a
          Coronavirus Aid Relief and Economic Security Act Funding Agreement between the City
          and Salt Lake County – Scott Harrington

     Scott Harrington explained that this was a second round of funding through the CARES Act.
The guidelines for spending the first round of funding had been a moving target. If the funds were
not spent according to strict government guidelines, they would have to be paid back. The first
round of funding had provided the following:
    • Approximately $1,067,000 went toward remote access, safety improvements to city hall,
       increased physical separations, social distancing improvements and reduction of touch
       points
    • $150,000 for technology upgrades, increasing public access
    • $50,000 for computers to allow staff to serve the public remotely

     •    $150,000 to local businesses adversely affected by COVID
     •    $62,000 in other COVID related expenses involving increased work done by Information
          Technology, the City Attorney’s office and public outreach and information
     •    $125,000 for increased police, fire and sanitation services
     •    $125,000 for a remote office program for city staff
     •    $18,000 for improved and upgraded video conferencing capabilities
     •     $9,500 for improvements to council chambers and the nearby Joint Information Center
          (JIC)
     •    $11,800 signage upgrades

Councilmember Armstrong asked what the state required regarding these funds. Mr. Harrington
replied that expenses needed to be related to the COVID pandemic and a certain amount would
need to go directly to the community. Councilmember Armstrong was aware that Salt Lake
County was awarding grants with CARES Act money. He asked if Taylorsville was doing so as
well. Mr. Harrington responded that as long as a business could demonstrate that it was adversely
affected by the pandemic, it was eligible for financial assistance. CDBG funds were also being
provided to assist the community. Councilmember Armstrong asked how businesses in need found
out about available funds. Information regarding available funds had been provided on the city’s
website and through social media. Twenty to thirty businesses had applied for the CDBG funds
and then $150,000 had been turned over to Chamber West to distribute to the local business
community. Barbara Riddle of Chamber West would provide a report at a future date.
Councilmember Armstrong stated he did not recall authorizing the expenses. It was pointed out
that the resolution approved earlier in the year had authorized both the receipt and the expenditure
of the funds in general.

Mr. Taylor explained that the city had spent and would continue to spend any CARES Act money
in accordance with the CARES Act guidelines.

Councilmember Barbieri pointed out that Chamber West had done a remarkable job of getting the
word out to the business community about available funds.

Wayne Harper reiterated that there were two different funding programs related to COVID. The
CDBG fund program included letters, emails and social media blasts letting the community know
that financial assistance was available. Salt Lake County also had some programs which thus far
had assisted approximately 27 different Taylorsville businesses. With the funds that Taylorsville
forwarded to Chamber West, they were literally going door to door to contact each business in the
City with a storefront, letting them know that as long as they could prove a negative impact in the
event of an audit, they could apply for that financial assistance. Additionally, Chamber West
would be mailing out $30.00 “Shop Local” coupons for use at any of the businesses that had stated
they had been adversely affected.

Mayor Overson was in support of the “Shop Local” initiative because it identified businesses in
need and also assisted residents by providing them with a coupon that they could use to purchase
whatever essentials they needed. In short, the program helped both businesses and residents of
Taylorsville.

7.        OTHER MATTERS

          7.1          Taylorsville City Hall Construction Projects Update – Ben White

       Ben White showed various slides, explaining the status of the city center project outside city
hall. The contractor had until May 1, 2021 to complete the work. He reminded those gathered that
the amphitheater and playground depicted in the slides had not yet been funded. He then explained
that in the future, there would be touchless doors on both the north and south sides of city hall.
The road that was currently closed would open again by the end of the week. The doors would be
the last portion of the project and would not be delivered until early December. The furniture on
the east side of the building wasn’t due until early December.

Regarding the west side of the second floor of city hall, there was a smaller impact. The plans for
this area were currently in flux, but he hoped that framing would begin the following Monday and
that the sheet rocking would be in within 2-3 weeks.

Councilmember Armstrong asked about the status of the wall on 3200 West. Mr. White responded
that the sidewalk was walkable but there was a space between the wall and sidewalk that still
needed to be filled in.

Chair Harker asked about the status of the wall that John Eugster had asked about during the
previous meeting. Mr. White explained it had been fixed although there was still some landscaping
that needed to be finished.

Regarding speed limit signs that Mr. Eugster had mentioned earlier in the meeting, Chair Harker
asked for Mr. White’s opinion about increasing the number of speed limit signs in Taylorsville.
He responded that there were two particular streets within the city for which he wanted to find a
solution for the speeding problems. However, he felt that new speed control devices tended to be
effective only when new. Once drivers got used to their presence, they tended not to notice them
anymore. Chair Harker inquired if there was a standard which determined how many speed limit
signs there should be and how far apart they should be spaced.

Mr. Taylor responded that there was a general traffic standard. Chair Harker felt Mr. Eugster had
some valid points regarding speed limit signs.

Chair Harker thanked Mr. White for his hard work and took a moment to express her appreciation
for all city staff for their constant willingness to step up when needed.
