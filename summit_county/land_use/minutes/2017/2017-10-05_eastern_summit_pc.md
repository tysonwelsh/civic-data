3.   Public hearing and possible action regarding an amendment to Chapter 11-3 Zoning 
Districts and Requirements to possibly adopt the Village Overlay Zone. – Ray Milliner, 
Principal Planner  
 
Planner Milliner shared the background. One slight change had been made since the last 
time the Planning Commission heard about the Village Overlay Zone. The word “historic,” 
when referring to eligible communities was changed to “existing.” This is a significant 
change because it allows the Village Overlay Zone in areas that are not traditionally the 
historic town centers. There had been discussion about size limitations, but there was no

consensus on that subject. Staff is asking the Commission to review the Village Overlay 
Zone document, hold a Public Hearing and possibly forward a positive recommendation to 
the County Council. 
The public hearing was opened.  
David Lake stated he is curious about the proposed reservoir. Chair Hanson answered that 
the reservoir information will be discussed in the Work Session later in the meeting. 
The public hearing was closed. 
Commissioner Wilde stated he would like more time to study this Zone and is not ready 
to send a recommendation to the Council. This does not need to be rushed through, since 
the Council already has a lot that they have not taken action on. A Staff presentation of 
potential impacts, including an example of what the application process would look like 
would be helpful. 
Commissioner Sargent stated he is unclear on how the process will unfold. How the 
overlay development relates to the Master Plan Development (MPD) process should be 
presented. The concept is forthcoming and proactive in maintaining the identities of our 
existing village centers. One concern surrounds Finding of Fact 2 that ensures the rural, 
agricultural small-town character shall remain even in the presence of growth and change. 
If the Village Overlay Zone is not handled correctly, with a very clean and clear process 
with County Staff and the citizens involved, it could destroy the rural, small-town character 
of Eastern Summit County. The Village Overlay Zone could enhance the small-town 
character by concentrating development where the infrastructure is located, in an

organized development plan. The provision language going forward, 11-3, District Intent, 
should emphasize the focus areas of the Village Overlay Zone. 
Commissioner Sonntag stated this Zone or something like it is definitely needed. 
Currently, there is no Zone that includes mixed use or multi-family in unincorporated 
Eastern Summit County. The Village Overlay would trigger the MPD process to allow for 
mixed uses in these areas. It is a good idea to start with these existing “village” locations. 
Commissioner Willoughby stated the concept of the Village Overlay is needed, but the 
process is still not clear. Commissioner Willoughby is uncomfortable sending the Village 
Overlay Zone to the County Council before the one to six and one to twenty zones are 
addressed. The tools for large development are being provided, but the tools are not in the 
hands of the property owners who would like to develop for their families. They have been 
left with the one to forty Zone and something needs to be done for those families before the 
tools for larger development are provided. 
Commissioner Clyde stated he received a phone call from a landowner in Hoytsville 
claiming he represented twenty property owners and 950 acres that will not be annexed 
into Coalville, who stated he is in favor of the Village Overlay Zone as a great tool and 
encouraged the Planning Commission to move forward with it.  
Currently, any property owner could apply for the rezone process, so the Village Overlay 
Zone is not a radical change. The Village Overlay Zone (VOZ) shows that the Planning 
Commission is sensitive to the long-time communities and that current zoning thwarts 
development. This is a positive step for those communities. It does need to be clear that 
this Overlay Zone is dependent on proper infrastructure, including sewer districts,

improved roads, water systems, etcetera, and needs to be backed up by the County having 
Special Service Districts to help finance the VOZ. It would be foolish to increase density 
without infrastructure. The density should be in the existing towns and the VOZ gets it 
done in an organized way. This may be an alternative to the Highway Corridor Zone that 
the Council does not like, and would allow the Council to move forward with the other 
zoning. 
Chair Hanson stated she had also received phone calls from the public endorsing this VOZ. 
One concern is the definition of a “traditional town site” and who decides where it is. That 
is a matter of opinion unless there is a description. Director Putt answered that is why the 
original draft had the word “historic” to describe the communities that are ideal for this 
Zone. Unincorporated communities that have been in existence for many years as a 
community center but never fully developed were the focus for the VOZ, with legitimate 
planning, in a way that meets the General Plan objectives. This is not the process to start a 
village afresh, but would probably be covered by the Resort Commercial Zone. The VOZ 
process would start with the Council designated a planning study boundary, that would be 
mapped. This could be initiated by a proposal from a group of property owners, by the 
Planning Commission, or by County Council. The study area would then go through the 
Master Plan process. The property owners, local residents and the Planning Commission 
would be involved. The VOZ would go through the Planning Commission. The Planning 
Commission would forward a recommendation, including zoning establishment, densities 
and uses to the County Council for a Public Hearing. The result would include a Zoning Map 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
October 5, 2017

and a Development Agreement. The process language can be included in the proposed 
amendment. 
Commissioner Clyde was skeptical that all the landowners would sign off on a 
Development Agreement. A Zoning Map treating the area like a municipality would 
probably work. Commissioner Sonntag argued that a Development Agreement would be 
required for the infrastructure issues. Commissioner Sargent stated there needs to be 
some understanding of how responsibilities will be assessed and identified. 
Director Putt stated that theoretically one person could initiate the VOZ process, but the 
Planning Commission and the County Council would need to support the application. That 
individual would have a strong voice in the planning process to determine zoning, but the 
end result will ultimately go through the Planning Commission and the County Council. The 
hope is to create a better working dialog of affected stakeholders to have an early say in the 
design process. Regardless of who initiates the process, a VOZ would go through the 
Planning Commission for recommendation to the County Council. 
Commissioner Sargent recommended Staff include a process and general criteria for the 
study area. Commissioner Sargent asked what the basis for five units per acre was. 
Director Putt answered that initial plats for some of these communities showed density 
around three units per acre. Also, the Planning Department has heard from community 
members that they would like to build homes for family members. With infrastructure 
questions answered, more affordable lots can be built to address this problem. 
Commissioner Sargent stated that density is not the enemy as long as there is proper 
infrastructure.

Commissioner Clyde asked if there is a minimum required area for a VOZ. The area should 
not be too small or too large. There should be a minimum size and possibly a ceiling. 
Commissioner Willoughby feels there could be political issues with a ceiling that would 
leave some neighbors out of the process. Director Putt stated every community is different, 
with different needs. The reason the minimum or maximum area was not defined is the 
boundary discussion will be part of the public planning process. Commissioner Clyde 
stated there should be a minimum number given with the caveat that it can be adjusted at 
the discretion of the County. The area should be big enough to be a village and bring in 
several property owners. Director Putt stated this is a planning tool for communities to 
recreate or advance themselves and not necessarily be bedroom communities to 
municipalities. 
Commissioner Sargent stated he received a text from Michael Crittenden in support of the 
Village Overlay Zone. 
Commissioner Clyde made a motion to request the Village Overlay Zone be brought 
back to the Planning Commission with more information and detail concerning 
geographic size and infrastructure mechanisms. Commissioner Sargent seconded the 
motion.

1.  Discussion regarding a Development Agreement Amendment for a proposal to amend 
the Development Agreement Amendment for the South Point of the Promontory Specially 
Planned Area to add 735 residential units. The applicant is also requesting 190,000 sq. ft. of 
commercial density; Browns Canyon Road; Tom Ellison, representing South Point Utah 
LLC, Applicant. – Amir Caus, County Planner 
 
Commissioner Sonntag recused himself from this portion of the meeting. 
Planner Caus opened the Work Session with a recap of what has been discussed concerning 
the South Point Development. At the end of the last meeting concerning South Point, the 
applicant had been directed to present a more substantial benefits package. The applicant 
will present first, what the Code requires, then present the benefits at another meeting. 
Tom Ellison stated his intent is to have a more comprehensive discussion about the 
development proposal. Mr. Ellison stated this is a legislative matter. When a Development 
Agreement is approved, it is a legislative action that is made after approval by the Planning 
Commission. The amendment to this Development Agreement will go through the same 
policy recommendation process. Mr. Ellison stated the Staff Report was given to the 
Planning Commission with old information just as he was getting his new information. 
There are housing studies that are not completed that Mr. Ellison will include in his 
discussion. Mr. Ellison went over the Summary of Revised Proposal and Community 
Benefits. The additional community benefits include the Summit County Residency 
Program, the agricultural protection fee obligation, the affordable housing fee obligation, 
the regional park land donation, the donation of the water storage reservoir (with no lake 
front residential lots), the increased school site donation, improved community parks and

community trails, the Children’s Justice Center donation, a fire station site donation and 
transit site donation. Mr. Ellison stated that a major policy change is giving up golf and that 
creates other opportunities for public recreation. The recreation is public and not just for 
the residents. Snyderville Basin Recreation would be over the public recreation projects. 
The access to the community park is through the development, which is not gated and open 
to the public. The entire development is about 800 acres. 
Commissioner Clyde asked if there is an agricultural preservation fee and affordable 
housing fee proposed on the commercial space. Mr. Ellison answered there is not. The 
employee housing units are being linked with the commercial space, so there is not a 
separate employee housing fee included. Sixteen to twenty percent of the employees in this 
development will be housed in the employee housing. 
Chair Hanson asked if the agricultural protection fee is something the purchaser will pay 
through the sale of the units or is the fee coming out of pocket from the developer. Mr. 
Ellison answered it is at the sale of the lot. 
Commissioner Clyde stated he would have to see a lot of geo-technical information about 
the proposed reservoir including that it would hold water, it is a safe site, how it would 
work, and the timing and credit. If the site for the reservoir was found to be unsuitable, 
would the additional 700 units of density go away? Mr. Ellison stated the reservoir is not in 
current planning, but the developer will provide a statement from Mountainland Regional 
that the site is viable. The developer is setting aside land that would otherwise be sold for 
real estate to provide the County an option. Mr. Ellison will get more information about 
that land donation.

SPA was approved were discussed. The South Summit School District has a need for 
additional land. There is a need for additional water storage. There is anticipated

Commissioner Sargent stated the details mean nothing until there are some basic criteria 
for approval measured and understood, and how is it being addressed and achieved. The 
impacts versus the benefits need to be shown. Issues that keep coming up are the 
agricultural protection fee and the affordable housing fee. Those fees should be at least the 
same in today’s dollars, if not more than they were when the original Development 
Agreement was approved. When an applicant states they are losing or giving up something,

discretionary proposal, and the action the Planning Commission will make for approval or

landowners in the vicinity. That will be a tough sell. Most everyone in the room is probably 
in opposition to the project. The applicant needs to show how there is not an adverse effect 
to the property owners in the vicinity.
