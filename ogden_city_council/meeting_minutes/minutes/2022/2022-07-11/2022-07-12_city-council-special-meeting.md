Proposed Ordinance 2022-38 adopting a Certified Tax Rate and Final
Budget for Fiscal Year July 1, 2022 through June 30, 2023

A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023 budget came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated the Council will hold the first of two public hearings on the proposed final FY2022-2023 Budget
which includes Schedule A-1 changes and a proposal to increase the certified tax rate by 18.83 percent. The tax rate increase will
generate just under $3 million in additional property tax revenue that will be used for increases to employee salaries and benefits
and to fund additional positions, including five police officers, a police grant manager, two homeless outreach coordinators, six
fire fighters, a recreation supervisor, and a sustainability coordinator.

Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
the proposed budget; she offered a comparison of the FY2022 adopted budget with the FY2023 proposed budget, indicating there
is a 17.25 percent change, or a $37 million increase. She discussed the proposed property tax increase component of the budget,
indicating that the City has heard a great deal of feedback from the public regarding their property valuations. She noted that the
average property has increased in value by 34.43 percent from 2021-2022. Residents can contact the Weber County Assessor’s
Office and the Clerk/Auditor’s Office to dispute their property valuation and apply for property tax relief. Additionally, residents
have indicated the City should not need to increase taxes because the property tax revenue should have increased as a result of
increased values; Ms. Eller-Smith explained the State of Utah property tax laws are designed for revenue neutrality and in order to
capture increased revenue associated with inflation, a property tax rate increase is necessary. Ogden City’s current rate of
0.002397 accounts for just 17 percent of an Ogden resident’s total property tax bill. The proposed rate of 0.002306 is actually
lower than the current rate but will make it possible for the City to capture an additional $3 million in revenue associated with

revenue neutrality. The Mayor has proposed a tax rate of 0.002306, which is actually lower than the current rate, but must be
advertised as an 18.83 percent increase since it is higher than the rate recommended by the County.

Proposed Ordinance 2022-36 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to
revise fees relative to planning and zoning

A memo from the City Comptroller regarding proposed amendments to planning and zoning fees came before the
Council for consideration. The memo stated the Planning Division has performed a thorough analysis of its application review
processes and analyzed current planning application fees and costs and have developed a proposal to amend the fee schedule
related to planning application fees. The study calculated the average cost of processing each type of application, determined what

Minutes of Special Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah, July 12, 2022

the cost recovery would be, and developed a fee schedule proposal that would help the Planning Division recover a larger portion
of the actual cost of reviewing planning applications. Recovering a larger share of the cost of reviewing applications would allow
the Planning Division to have the resources needed to provide a wider range of planning services including more robust long-
range planning.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated the Planning Division has performed an extensive study to
determine appropriate application fees for the many land use and planning applications the division receives. As cited in the study,
of its average annual expenses for reviewing and processing applications of $625,000, the Planning Division recovers only about
13 percent, or about $80,000, of its expenses through application fees. State law allows fees to be assessed for reviewing land use
applications; however, fees may not exceed the reasonable cost of processing an application. The proposed fee structure is
intended to improve this deficiency but is still estimated to recover only 54 percent of the cost of processing applications. The
proposed ordinance also includes an automatic annual increase based on a set consumer price index (CPI). The CPI chosen to be
used is the United States Bureau of Labor Statistics Western Region customer price index for all items derived for urban
consumers. A similar approach has been used with the City’s utility rates since 2012.

Council member Hyer inquired as to the types of land use applications for which there is no application fee. Mr. Brierley
noted a resident can obtain a no-cost land use permit for residential accessory structures, fences, and landscaping adjustments.

Council member Choberka asked Mr. Brierley if the Planning Division has received any public feedback regarding this
proposal. Mr. Brierley stated the amount of feedback has been surprisingly low and the feedback he has received has been
positive.

Council member Hyer stated that people have been unwilling to obtain a permit to operate short-term rental properties; he
noted that adding a fee for the permit will not help that situation and he asked if Administration has plans to encourage permits for
that type of land use. Mr. Brierley stated that the best way to encourage people to obtain permits is to make the process as
convenient as possible for them.
