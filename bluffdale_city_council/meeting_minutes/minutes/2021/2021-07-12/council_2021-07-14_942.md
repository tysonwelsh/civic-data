Blake Leonelli from Waste Management presented an update regarding recycling and reminded
the Council that Waste Management is a partner with the City and wi ll work with the community
in w hatever way the Counci l requests . Mr. Leo nelli provided a review of 2020 based upon the
aud its and the to1mages received by Waste Management from Bluffdale City. He reported that
the City had 728 tons of recyclable materi al, which equates to 1.5 million pounds of waste that
was diverted from the landfill and repurposed. Mr. Leone lli explained that the diversion number
presented to the Council represents the percentage of what comes into Waste Management that is
diverted from the landfill. Bluffdale's diversion number of 12.5% is on the low side. A good
diversion number would be in the range of 15 to 20 percent with a goal of achieving the higher
20 percent diversion rate. Mr. Leonelli commented that in December 2020, Bluffdale saw a

spike in its recycling number that continued to trend to the current year. Summer months are
typically down, but he expressed excitement over the trends he has seen in the City.

Mayor Timothy asked about the impact of the weight of green waste on the summer data.
Mr. Leonelli explained that they factor out the green waste and attributed the lower summer
numbers to summer activities. He repo11ed that November and December typically see the worst
diversion rates, wh ich makes the spike they saw in December 2020 so encouraging. He
applauded the City for these numbers and seeing the trends continue.

Mr. Leonelli rep011ed that as a result of a major audit they just completed in a nearby city, the
trends they discovered are that the containers are not full and the set-out rate is around 50%. He
indicated that Waste Management could do the same type of audit for Bluffdale. He stated that
they have new technology called "Smart Truck" that photographs the material as it is emptied to
see if it is contaminated or if a bin is overfilled. This technology is available to Bluffdale stai1ing
next month. With this technology, Waste Management can send an email to the City or the
customer warning them that their container was contaminated or overfilled. It could also be
utilized to fine customers who set out contaminated or overfilled containers.

Mr. Leonelli clarified that the technology and the data will be available to the City but it would
be up to the Council to determine how the data is utilized . Mayor Timothy addressed potential
issues with imposing fines on customers for overflowing containers. Mr. Leonelli pointed out
that warnings are generally effective in correcting issues of overfilling. They have also seen that
recycling behaviors change with the imposition of fines for contaminated containers. He
commented that cities vary in how they utilize the data with some confiscating the container and
others imposing fines. He reiterated that the warnings generally remedy the issue. Mr. Leonelli
offered to make a presentation on this new technology at a future Council Meeting.

Council Member Gaston asked about education efforts for recycl ing, particularly for the younger
community. Mr. Leonelli responded that they could implement education efforts at the City's
request but noted the there is a cost associated with drivers posting sti ckers on containers. They
would be willing to work with the City, the residents, and the community to provide thi s
education. Waste Management would be willing to assist with those costs in an effort to change
the recycling behaviors of the community. He offered that some cities request a heat stamp on
the containers with imprinted recycling information. In another city, Waste Management applied
stickers to the containers. He noted that the sky is the limit in terms of what the City can request
in terms of education for residents.

Council Member Gaston stated that Bluffdale has a lot of first-time homeowners and education
regarding what can and cannot be recycled. Council Member Crockett commented that the
problem does not li e only with first-time homeowners and stated that there seems to be a lot of
misinformation regarding what can be recycled. Mr. Leonelli stressed that in the past 18 months,
there was a shortage of good, recycled material , which is why education is so impo11ant. He
offered their slogan of " When in doubt, throw it out." The "right" recyclables are cardboard,
paper, plastic bottles, and metal containers. The data suppo11s recycling pick-ups every other
