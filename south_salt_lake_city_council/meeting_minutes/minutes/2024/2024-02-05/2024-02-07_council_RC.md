NO ACTION COMMENTS

       1. SCHEDULING. The City Recorder informed those at the meeting of upcoming events,
          meetings, activities, etc. Next Meeting—February 28th @ 7pm. **City Hall and City
          offices will be closed on Monday, February 19th in observance of President’s Day.

       2. CITIZEN COMMENTS/QUESTIONS.
          South Salt Lake resident, Susan Bowlden, talked about the ‘Curly Me’ event that was
          held at The Co-Op Center for POC teens and said that it was tremendously valuable
          to the community. She also addressed safety concerns she believes exist at the
          South Salt Lake Community Center, which also houses The Co-Op, that are due to
          poor lighting in the parking lots and bus stops and provided some suggestions on
          how the City can fix it.

              South Salt Lake resident, Lorrina Heisey, wanted to address recent incidents that she
              said have raised safety concerns in the community. She talked about how elected
              representatives hold the responsibility to uphold the highest standards of conduct
              and that their actions set an example for the community they serve. It is essential to
              encourage respectful dialogue within the Council and when interacting with
              constituents. She finds the recent behavior that has targeted women on social
              media troubling and wants to ensure that all community members are treated fairly
              and respectfully. She said that stalking individuals and posting their personal
              information online is unacceptable and harmful. She referenced the City Council’s
              Code of Conduct and Ethics guidelines and said that it is time to address any gaps by
              adopting clear standards to foster a civil culture. She then talked about safety
              concerns she has for kids in her neighborhood near 700 West and River Run Dr.
              where neighborhood kids cross the road during early morning hours. She is
              concerned that with poor lighting, no crossing guards, and no flashing lights, it could
              result in a school aged child getting hit or killed.

           South Salt Lake resident, Jeff Kane, spoke about the Mayor’s Appointment item on
           the agendas and said that he admires and trusts the Mayor as a leader. He also
           reflected on recent events from the Police Department and shared his thoughts on
           how he wishes there was a way to have shown more support to the officer that was
           involved.

           South Salt Lake resident, Kelli Pierce, shared her thoughts on the Unfinished
           Business item on the agenda and said that she would like the Council to make things
           easier for residents to put ADU’s on their property. She noted how ADU’s are
           making their way in, and that people can buy them ready to go from places like
           Amazon. She said that she also believes that it’s important to have reasonable rules
           around ADU’s so that people can have water and plumbing.

       3. MAYOR COMMENTS.
          Mayor Wood highlighted a few City events and gave some updates on City-related
          legislative news.

           The Co-Op Community Center hosted its third ‘Curly Me: Change the World with
           Her’ event on Saturday, February 3rd. It was well-attended, and kids had the
           opportunity to see and talk to BIPOC professionals that look like them. She thanked
           the City’s staff that were there to provide support for the event.

           Give Pulse is a platform that the City uses to retain volunteers for different City
           events and projects. As a City on the move, the Mayor noted how the City can
           always use volunteers to help at the animal shelter, plant trees, clean up parks, etc.
           As the weather starts to get warmer there will be more volunteer opportunities for
           the community to participate in. Residents can sign up through the City’s website on
           the ‘How do I’ tab under the ‘Volunteer Opportunities’ section that will take them to
           the City’s Give Pulse portal.

           Mayor Wood then gave some updates on the current legislative session. SB 121 is a
           Senate Bill that is known as LEAP (Long Term Education Achievement Program), a
           pilot program that is based off Promise South Salt Lake’s model so that it can be
           replicated in other communities across the State.

       4. CITY ATTORNEY COMMENTS.
          City Attorney, Josh Collins, also spoke about the State’s legislative updates that they
          are closely following. He said that every year there are a lot of issues and bills that
          affect the City and local government. He encouraged the Council Members to look
          over the different bills that are being considered and to reach out if they have any
          questions.

       5. CITY COUNCIL COMMENTS.
          Council Member Williams thanked the Mayor for the legislative update information.
          She talked about the importance of STEM programs for kids, especially young girls,
          that encourage them to pursue careers in the math or science fields. She also
          encouraged anyone who is in those fields to consider donating some of their time to
          volunteer with the Promise after school programs.

       6. INFORMATION.

               a. 2023 Fitts Park Community Garden Report
                  Neighborhoods Director, Sharen Hauri, introduced Madalyn Covey who works
                  for Wasatch Community Gardens and manages several gardens throughout
                  the valley. She used a PowerPoint presentation that showed the mission
                  statement as well as the six main programs that Wasatch Community Gardens
                  offers. The City has two community gardens located in Fitts Park and
                  Harmony Park. Fitts Park serves 32 households, comprised of 79 individuals,
                  of which 59% fall into the ‘low-to-moderate’ income category. The Fitts Park
                  Community Garden was able to produce 3,200 lbs. of produce in 2023 and
                  have 170 volunteer hours given.

                   A copy of the PowerPoint presentation is attached and incorporated by this
                   reference.

               b. Lead & Copper Pipe Survey
                  Senior Executive Assistant, Misty Woods, went over the results of the survey
                  that is part of the department’s compliance with the Lead and Copper Rule
                  that went into effect in December of 2021. As part of the Rule, the City must
                  reach compliance by October 2024. Testing is done every three years to
                  monitor drinking water at customer taps and ensure that concentrations in
                  the water don’t exceed the limits that are allowed. In the last five years, the
                  City has not had any lead over the limit.

                   A copy of the PowerPoint presentation is attached and incorporated by this
                   reference.

               c. Housing Navigation and Community Engagement
                  Promise Director, Kelli Meranda, and LMI Housing Navigator, Rebecca Yund,
                  talked about the services that are offered through the Promise South Salt
                  Lake program. It is geared to offer support to youth and their families by
                  connecting them to education, safety, and opportunity-based resources.
                  South Salt Lake is one of the most economically and socially diverse
                  communities in the State. Ms. Meranda talked about the work that Family
                  Liaisons do to connect families to social services and community resources.
                  She also talked about The Co-Op Center and how it offers free membership
