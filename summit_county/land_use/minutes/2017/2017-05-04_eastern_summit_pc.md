5.   Public hearing and possible action regarding a proposed Plat Amendment to the H & G 
Subdivision Amended Lots 2-4 Subdivision adding an additional 10 feet to the existing Right of 
Way area for HG Court; 663-693 E HG Ct, Coalville, UT; H&G-3-AM, H&G-4-AM; Clifford 
Pedersen, Applicant. – Sean Lewis, County Planner 
 
Planner Lewis shared the background of the application. The H & G Subdivision was platted in 
September 2000. This subdivision is located along Chalk Creek Road near Coalville. This is a 
five-lot subdivision. It was amended in 2010 to increase the sizes of Lots 2 and 4 and amend the 
agricultural parcel. An applicant wanted to build a home on the property and was going through 
the Engineering process and the County Engineer did not feel like the subdivision plat and the 
private driveway that crosses lots 2, 3 and 4 was wide enough. There is an awkward intersection 
where that road meets Chalk Creek Road. The County Engineer requested there be a plat 
amendment to add ten feet (10’) of right-of-way added to meet the existing driveway/private 
road. The parcel boundaries are not being changed. There is just square footage being added to 
the right-of-way. Staff is recommending approval according to the Findings of Fact and 
Conclusions of Law found in the Staff Report. 
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
May 4, 2017 
Page 3 of 22 
 
Commissioner Clyde asked why this is coming back retroactively when it was approved in 2010 
with the road platted the way it is. If someone decided they did not want to grant the ten-foot 
easement, would that block the Plat Amendment? What is the policy for retroactively going back 
to change a plat that has already been changed twice in relatively modern times? Planner Lewis 
answered this is the policy, if it is to be fixed properly. Everyone seems to be on board with this 
and the ten feet is at the request of the County Engineer to make the curve in the road. There are 
different interpretations of Engineering policy. The Code requires a ten-foot separation from the 
property line and this amendment will make the road meet Engineering Standards. 
Commissioner Clyde asked if it will be standard to have a general review. Planner Lewis 
answered this was an option given to the applicants if they would like to go forward with this to 
make it right on the plat. The plat was recorded and could have been left as-is but the neighbors 
were good enough to work with the County to come in to conformance to the current standards. 
 
The public hearing was opened.  
Cliff Pedersen, the applicant reiterated that he has spoken to the adjoining property owners and 
all of them are in agreement with this change. This is what has to happen in order to get access to 
the lot and to have a wide enough right-of-way in order to use their properties. There are no 
objections on Mr. Pedersen’s part. 
The public hearing was closed.

6.   Public hearing and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit for a proposed 
commercial kennel; 121 E 3200 N, Kamas, UT; MVE-2; Breanna Wright, Applicant. – Sean 
Lewis, County Planner 
 
Planner Lewis shared the background of the application. Staff has received several phone calls 
concerning this application. The public has been active on this application. Ms. Breanna Wright 
is purchasing a home off the Upper Loop Road from Ms. Trisha Painter. Currently, the property 
is used residentially and Ms. Painter uses the property to house her own six dogs and brings in 
foster dogs. At any given time, Ms. Painter could have a number of dogs being housed in her 
home overnight. Ms. Painter also allows a de facto day care, where friends with dogs can drop 
them off to Ms. Painter for a couple of hours or stay and visit. At any given time during the day 
there have been up to thirty dogs at a time on this property under the current ownership. There 
has been no commercial activity because Ms. Painter has never charged anyone to have the pets 
on her property. There has never been a Conditional Use Permit. Ms. Painter has a kennel permit 
through Animal Control. Planner Lewis verified with Animal Control that there have been no 
complaints, issues or citations given by Animal Control to this property in at least a year.  
Ms. Painter is looking to sell her property. Ms. Wright, the potential purchaser, owns a dog 
walking business in the Snyderville Basin area. Ms. Wright goes to people’s homes and walks 
their dogs and would like to expand her business to allow for overnight boarding. This has come 
before the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission because this is a commercial business 
and requires a Conditional Use Permit. Staff has not provided a number of dogs to be permitted 
at the site, but the number of dogs discussed has been up to forty-five or fifty at a time. There is 
definitely not enough kennel space for fifty dogs to be housed overnight without leaving them in 
the elements, which is not what the applicant intends to do. The applicant intends to have dogs 
during the day for a few hours, with up to ten occasionally being boarded overnight. This would

be reasonable at that location. Staff has not recommended or put in any limitation on the number

The Conditional Use is an allowed use in this zone, but there may be impacts that need to be 
mitigated. When Staff looked at this application, it was decided the impacts that need to be 
mitigated are: where dogs are being housed, fencing to protect dogs from running out and 
disturbing neighbors, and noise control. There have been reports of scared horses. 
A kennel operation, whether commercial or private, has been operating on this location for a 
couple of years. The new proposal does not greatly increase the impacts. In Staff’s analysis of 
the new proposal, if the number of dogs is kept consistent with what Ms. Painter has had, then 
the impacts would be neutral from a commercial use and the non-commercial use that has been 
occurring for years. If there is an increase in the number of dogs, then there would be an increase 
to the impacts, that could require more mitigation or possible denial of the permit. 
There are no new structures being proposed and the applicant complies with all the other 
Standards. There are three different areas that can be fenced off for the dogs so they can be 
physically separated within the back yard. There is an indoor kennel structure in the garage that 
has a 784-square foot footprint that has eight rooms that can be used to house dogs for indoor, 
overnight boarding. Staff recommends that if the impacts are not greater than the current use, 
then there are findings to grant the Conditional Use Permit.
