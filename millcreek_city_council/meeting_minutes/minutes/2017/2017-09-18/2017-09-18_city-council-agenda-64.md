3.1 Report of the Mayor

Mayor Silvestrini addressed the homeless concern in the Salt Lake Valley. He said it is in the entire
valley. He indicated since Operation Rio Grande commenced, it started to affect the outlying
communities. The operation is pushing the population away from the Rio Grande and Pioneer Park,
which is now moving toward Millcreek. He went to explained he went ona ride-along last Tuesday with
UPD, and the City is monitoring the situation.

Mayor Silvestrini addressed the camps along the Jordan River, and he said there is about three blocks of
camps located in public park space. He stated that UPD and the Health Department are making an
effort to clean the site up because there are issues with sanitation and public safety. He presented
pictures of the camps. He pointed out there is material ranging from tents, Coleman stoves, shop
vacuums, tools, to anything you could imagine. He noted this particular site was not in Millcreek; it was
located between 3400 South and 3900 South. The Jordan River in Millcreek is wildland and vegetated
land.

Mayor Silvestrini explained the occupants of the camps had been given warnings not to be there;
however, there were still 15 individuals there. He explained how UPD handled the situation, and he was
impressed with the way the officers handled it. He went on to say, our police force is actively
monitoring the homeless situation. The City will deal with it humanly. He went on to say, most of us do
not realize what exists down there. He asked, if you see a camper, call the non-emergency number and
let them know; get them some help. He said they do not want to go to the Rio Grande because they do
not feel it is safe, and it is a difficult problem.

Discussion followed regarding drug addiction and /or mental health associated with the homeless
population.

Mayor Silvestrini said most of the individuals were cooperative, and they were trying to get out. He
stated it is a human tragedy and the stories are sad.

Mayor Silvestrini indicated he had attended a committee meeting of the Board governing the Unified
Police. He pointed out there are 1.2 million people in the County. He said during the meeting, they
discussed whether there should be a Police Chief for Unified Police. He explained the Sheriff is
proposing to appoint Shane Hudson as Chief of Unified Police if it is approved by the Board. He noted
that a lot of these issues had arisen by the Board due to the issues that Unified Fire experienced. They
want to make sure there is control to manage the department.

3.2 Reports of City Council

Council Member Catten reported on the Mosquito Abatement District. She explained the District
decided not to do an aerial spray for the West Nile virus; however, there has been one death in the
County associated with West Nile. She went on to say, Millcreek has two traps and each trap has tested
positive for West Nile. She said they were not completely out of the clear. She recommended not
leaving standing pools of water and using repellant. There are still a lot of mosquitos out there, and
everyone still needed to be cognizant of what is out there.

Item 8. Other Matters

8.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution No. 17- , to Approve an Inter-Local Agreement for

the Allocation of Assets and Liabilities upon Withdrawal of Millcreek from Salt Lake Valley Law
Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

Mayor Silvestrini introduced the item. He pointed out a typo in Paragraph B, (it instead of its). He
explained the inter-local agreement and what it entailed. He noted by statutory requirements, the City
could be required to do an additional feasibility study, which could cost an additional $37,000 if the two
parties did not agree on the withdrawal. However, the only issue was a matter of negotiation of the
amount of fund balance the City would take with them.

Mayor Silvestrini explained the methodology that was utilized to determine Millcreek’s fund balance,
and because of the methodology, the City will split the difference with SLVLESA. He said the City had an
agreement with SLVLESA’s finance officer, and the Proposed amount to be distributed after the split is
$2,035,000.

Mayor Silvestrini addressed Paragraph 5, 2017 Property Tax Proceeds. He pointed out there was still a
strikethrough in the paragraph. He noted that SLVLESA would receive the 2017 property taxes, and
SLVLESA will pay incurred expenses. The City will be responsible for payment of its proportionate share
of any property tax refunds related to the 2017 and prior property taxes. Mayor Silvestrini informed the
Council they shared the numbers with Lewis and Young, and Mr. Burningham gave them some talking
points. In addition, Mr. Burningham is comfortable with the numbers.

Council Member Uipi asked for clarification of how long Millcreek will be with SLVLESA; will it be through
December 21 or December 28, 2017. Mayor Silvestrini explained SLVLESA’s note is due December 21,
2017, and they, SLVLESA , will pay for the entire year of 2017. The City will take over on January 1, 2018.

Motion: Council Member Uipi moved to adopt Resolution No. 17-73, a Resolution to Approve an Inter-
Local Agreement for the Allocation of Assets and Liabilities upon Withdrawal of Millcreek from Salt Lake
Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA) as amended to fix the typos. Council Member Catten
made the second.

Discussion followed the motion. Mayor Silvestrini clarified this was not a withdrawal from the Unified
Police Department. It was a way for Millcreek to make arrangement for financing UPD. He indicated with
the assistance from Mr. Brems and Jason Burningham, several lending institutions had indicated
eagerness make a loan to Millcreek. He explained for Millcreek to be legally compliant, it cannot extend
a debt beyond the fiscal year. The City will use the money to contract services with UPD in the same
manner as other cities. He continued, it is our hope that making this decision, we will be able to fund

police services to a greater extent.

Mayor Silvestrini explained property taxes will no longer be collected by SLVLESA in 2018. Millcreek will
collect the property taxes instead and residents will have the same level of service.

It was inquired why the City would have to borrow money to pay UPD. Mayor Silvestrini explained the
money to pay for police services is only collected once a year, which is when property taxes are paid, but
that the police must be paid year-round. He noted that SLVLESA has always had to borrow money to
pay for those services as well; and sometime in December, they pay the note off with property taxes.

He referred back to the inter-local agreement with SLVLESA, he pointed out in Paragraph 5, no one
knows what amount of money the City will receive; however, that is the money the City will receive to
pay UPD for the first five months. He went on to explain, the City would not receive the money at first.
The City will have to get a note — just like SLVLESA, unless the Council hears otherwise from the voters of
Millcreek to save money on interest payments by increasing taxes enough to get caught up. He said it
would be like paying 13 mortgage payments per year instead of 12.

Mayor Silvestrini addressed the financing options and procurement process, and what items he
evaluated prior to moving forward with deciding to withdraw from SLVLESA. He said first, they had to
determine if there were any willing lenders; second, determine if there were any regulatory
requirements or not; third, make sure the City’s auditors had no issues with the City’s structure of
financing UPD. The City’s auditors are fine with it to their understanding, and they did not see any
negative impact. Mayor Silvestrini said he went through this process because he did not want to be the
first mayor that did not have a police force. Council Member Marchant suggested the City look at trying
to eliminating the financial gap over a reasonable amount of time and the need to borrow money to pay
for police service. Mayor Silvestrini indicated he would also like to eliminate that need; however, the
City will need to be more financial secure.

Call the Question: Members Voting Aye: Mayor Silvestrini, Council Members Catten, Marchant, Jackson,
and Uipi. The motion passed unanimously.
