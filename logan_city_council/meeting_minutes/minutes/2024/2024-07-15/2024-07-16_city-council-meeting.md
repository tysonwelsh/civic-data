PacificCorp Rate Increase – Mark Montgomery, Light and Power Director (20:06)

Light & Power Director Mark Montgomery addressed the Council. Typically, the
UAMPS (Utah Associated Municipal Power Systems) board increases transmission rates
by 2-3% every year. This year, Rocky Mountain Power dropped a 45% transmission
increase which is largely due to fires caused by negligent powerlines in the Northwest
which have been passed down to their customers including the City. UAMPS has filed an
appeal to FERC (Federal Energy Regulatory Commission) stating the increase was
drastic nor is their area the responsible party, who should take the cost for the negligence
of another area as the City is served by the Pacific Core East section, not the Pacific Core
West. The projected estimate is $80,600 more in projected transmission fees. The Light
& Power department budgets conservatively, and should be fine, but the final amount
may be more which may require a surcharge fee.

Mr. Montgomery clarified that as an entity, the City is required to pay the 45%
transmission increase by Rocky Mountain Power, however, the hope is that FERC
determines otherwise on the appeal resulting in the refund of the payment made.

Mayor Daines added that Rocky Mountain Power proposed to the Public Service
Commission an increase of 30%. The Public Service Commission may deny the request
entirely, approve it entirely, or only a partial amount.

Chair A. Anderson asked if there is a timeline for the appeals process.

Mr. Montgomery answered the appeal process will be slow, there is no estimate at this
time.

Consideration of a proposed resolution endorsing changes to the Logan Wastewater
Pretreatment Ordinance – Resolution 24-28 – Craig Carlston, City Attorney (37:15)

City Attorney Craig Carlston addressed the Council regarding the proposed resolution.
He explained that the Utah Division of Water Quality (DWQ) has reviewed the proposed
revisions to Logan City’s Wastewater Pretreatment Ordinance found in Logan Municipal
Code §13.12.005 et seq. (“Pretreatment Ordinance”). The DWQ must provide public
notice and a comment period for the proposed revisions to the Pretreatment Ordinance
                                                                                      4 | Page
               Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ July 16, 2024
before the Logan Municipal Council can adopt these revisions. DWQ has found that the
proposed revisions of the Pretreatment Ordinance can be approved for public notice and
comment by DWQ. Section § 403.9(b)(2) requires the Logan City Municipal Council to
provide DWQ with a statement endorsing the revisions to the Pretreatment Ordinance
prior to the DWQ public notice and comment period.

Councilmember Johnson requested further clarification on the changes being made.

Issa Hamud, Environmental Director explained the ordinance is already in existence and
most of the changes are minor and are required/recommended by the DWQ.

Tyler Richards, Operations Manager clarified that the exception is 13.12.280, F (2) -
Administrative Fines. Administrative fines in the past have been used as a means of
enforcement action. The amount of fines for recurring violators was not where it needed
to be resulting in an adjustment of fees. The change creates a new category for chronic
violators with the fine being significantly higher.

Vice Chair M. Anderson inquired if the reason is that it is cheaper to pay the fine than for
the violator to dispose of the material.

Mr. Richards responded that this is sometimes the case where paying the fine is the
cheaper option.

Councilmember Johnson asked if there were many violators.

Mr. Hamud replied that the change is a result of a particular business, who is a recurring
violator.

Mr. Richards added that in his time in Pretreatment, administrative fines have only been
used twice for the same entity.

PUBLIC HEARING – Application to the Council of Governments (COG) 2024
Funding Cycle for the street improvement projects located at 1000 North 600 West
and 1400 North 200 East – Sam Odd, Public Works Program Manager (51:17)

Public Works Program Manager Sam Odd addressed the Council regarding the
application for funding. COG or CCCOG (Cache County Council of Governments)
provides transportation funding through a .25 country-wide sales tax. This year $6 to $7
million in funding is available. To apply for and receive funds, the City must follow the
Transportation Fund Program requirements including a public hearing addressing the
topic, the concept of the project, the estimated cost, and making the jurisdiction aware of
their financial responsibility if the project is funded.

The first project is the 1000 North 600 West intersection which requires improvements,
the estimated cost is $5 to $6 million, and the City is requesting from COG is $3.2
million. The City's obligation is at least 8% which is $260,000, the match already in the
budget is 40% at $2.4 million.

The second project is 200 East; 1350 North to 1800 corridor. This is a project in
conjunction with North Logan City. The estimated cost is $8 million, the City provides
$2 Million. The request to COG is $7 million which will be split between the City and
North Logan. The City’s obligation is at least 8% at $160,000 and is budgeted for Fiscal
Year 29. The project is within the five-year plan.

Councilmember Johnson asked if the estimates on the first project will be close to the
actual cost as two projects of similar size have recently been completed.

Mr. Odd responded that estimates would be reasonably close to the cost.

Councilmember Simmonds requested confirmation if traffic improvements include a
traffic light on the first project.

Mr. Odd confirmed that at the intersection there will be a traffic light installed.

Vice Chair A. Anderson inquired if the funding received from COG could be used for
property acquisitions if needed.

Mr. Odd answered that the funding received could be used for property acquisitions if
needed.

Councilmember Johnson said the second project is slated as part of a five-year plan but
are there any changes happening further south.

Mr. Odd responded that it would be estimated in FY 2029, that it would occur around the
same time or would directly follow the project.

Chair A. Anderson opened the meeting to a public hearing.
