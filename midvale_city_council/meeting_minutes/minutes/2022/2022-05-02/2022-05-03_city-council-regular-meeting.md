   . Unified Police Department (UPD) Budget Overview
Sherriff Rivera and Lisa Dudley, UPD CFO, were present to discuss the Unified Police
Department Budget. A discussion took place regarding the general fund, shared services,
and fund balance.

Lisa Dudley said UPD has no debt, and they have a rotation policy for vehicles. So, there
is not the need for a large fund balance like cities have. The fund balance has decreased
over the years. UPD has one source of revenue and that's member assessment, The
changes in the budget over the past 12 years was discussed. She said the fund balance
has been used over the years to balance the budget. That fund balance is no longer
available to use. The Midvale Precinct budget was discussed in detail. The proposed
COLA and increases in market were discussed as well as fuel costs for all departments.
The precincts will now have their own fund balance. The proposed budget increase to the
City if a portion of the precinct's fund balance is used will be approximately 15.9%
increase. lf a portion of the fund balance is not used to offset the increase, the amount
would be 18%.

Council Member Bryant Brown said when Midvale joined the Unified Police Department,
they were promised there would be no increase over 10%. He expressed his frustration
and displeasure with these continual double-digit increases. The council approved a 16%
mid-year increase for the police officer salaries and now the city is looking at another 18%
increase.

Mariah Hill said the process of adopting the fiscal year 2023 budgets requires several
steps that you, as a City Council, are required to take to be compliant with state statute.
UCA 10-6-1 1 1(1) requires the Budget Officer to present a tentative budget for each fund
within the City. The budgets must be submitted no later than the first regularly scheduled
meeting in May. I will present budgets for each fund on Tuesday, May 3rd on behalf of
the Budget Officer (City Manager). At that time, I will brief you on fiscal year 2023 revenue
estimates, provide a brief overview of expenditures, and present budget highlights along
with any proposed program changes. UCA 10-6-111(3) further requires the City Council
to adopt the tentative budget at this meeting. The Council will then review and amend, if
necessary, the budgets prior to their final adoption. During the next several weeks, staff
will be discussing the details of each department budget with the Council. As a reminder,
the purpose of a tentative budget is to have a working tool from which we can start the
formal adoption process. The budget document can be amended prior to final adoption.
At the time of final adoption, all budgets must be balanced with expenditures equaling
revenues.

       B.      DISCUSSION OF EFFECTIVE DATE OF NEWLY ADOPTED DISTRICT
               MAP
Lisa Garner said pursuant to Utah Code Annotated S10-3-205.5(2xb)(ii), Midvale City
Council adopted a map defining new district boundaries on April 19,2022 ("New District
Map"). There has been some question as to the election implications and effective date
of the New District Map. To ensure that the Midvale residents know who their city council
representative currently is and to clearly establish residency expectation for the 2023
municipal election, council members have asked to discuss the option of taking legislative
action that will clarify any ambiguity. State and county laws are silent on this issue, but
the City has received some guidance from the Election Divisron of Salt Lake County
Clerk's Office.

That guidance is as follows:
1. That the effective date of the New District Map should be January 2,2024, which
      is the date the new council member will be sworn in after the 2023 election.

2.     Candidates seeking election to the city council for districts 1,2, or 3, in the 2023
       election, will need to reside in the district for which they are running based on the
       New District Map.

3.      Candidates in the 2023 municipal election who are also existing council members
        for districts 1 , 2, or 3 will be considered the incumbent candidate.
Council Member Dustin Gettel stated waiting for January 2024 will create confusion. He
said it makes sense to approve now the new map or in January of 2023, so the council
members that are running in election will be in the new district map.
Council Member Quinn Sperry said as long as the city makes it clear to the residents
when the district map is approved.

      C. CITY COUNCIL ANNUAL GOALS REVIEW AND UPDATE
Matt Dahl said as part of developing the Fiscal Year 2022 Budgel, the Midvale City
Council adopted its first set of consolidated annual goals. Over the course of the past
year, staff provided quarterly progress updates on the projects, programs, and processes
that were implemented in support of the Annual Goals. Staff provided the most recent
progress report in March 2022.

Midvale City staff will also provide a final progress report following the conclusion of the
Fiscal Year 2022. fhe Midvale City Council's Annual Goals included 14 goals that cover
a wide range of issues. Given that this was the first set of consolidated annual goals that
the Midvale City Council had adopted, there has been discussion throughout the year
about the need to revisit the list to see how it and the goal setting process can be
improved. Additionally, through the implementation of the goals, staff has identified
several issues that require review and consideration by the City Council.
First, the list of goals encompassed most City activities and did not provide clear guidance
on the City Council's focus for the year. Second, several goals overlapped in their
objectives and corresponding projects, programs, and processes. Third, some of the
goals have been completed. Finally, the Council provided mid-year guidance on issues
that are important to them that may not have been captured in the goals or that had their
importance diluted when included with all of the priorities. For these reasons, staff is
seeking guidance from the City Council on how to update the Goals for Fiscal Year 2023.
Below, staff is providing the list of Fiscal Year 2022 Goals and a proposed list of Goals
for Fiscal Year 2023.

The proposed goals for Fiscal Year 2023 reflect a consolidation of many of the annual
goals from Fiscal Year 2022 and support many of the policy decisions that went into the
development of the Fiscal Year 2023 Tentative Budget. Goals were also removed if they
had been completed, were part of basic city services, or were perceived to be a lower
priority for the City Council during Fiscal Year 2023. The proposed list is a starting point
for a discussion, but in the end should reflect the goals of the City Council. Staff is seeking
to have the final list of goals approved by the City Council for inclusion in the final Fiscal
Year 2023 Budget. Staff will be prepared in the meeting to discuss the Fiscal Year 2022
Goals, the proposed goals for Fiscal Year 2023, and the projects, policies, programs, and
processes that will correspond with each goal.
