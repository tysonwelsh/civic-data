       8.1     Ordinance 2023-15- An Ordinance updating Code for Nuisance Legal Action
               (StaffPresenter,Samantha Smith),


Prosecuting City Attorney, Samantha Smith presented a proposed Ordinance that will update the
Code for Nuisance Legal Action. Ms. Smith reported that she handles prosecutions for the City of
Bluffdale. Under the current Code, before the City can initiate a criminal prosecution for
maintaining a nuisance, the City must provide a Notice of Violation and give the subject a
                                BLUFFDALE CITY COUNCIL

minimum of 10 days to remedy the nuisance. This gives people the opportunity to remedy
problems on their property, however,the problem is how the City is providing notice. The notice
must currently be provided pursuant to the Utah Rules of Civil Procedure, which is expensive.
Methods include personal service, which costs $35 to $50 per attempt, or notice by Certified Mail,
which costs about $7.90. The mailings rarely make it to the property owner. Code Enforcement
Officer, Russ Cramer, has reported that most certified letters are returned. The City is spending
money on notices that are not being received but fulfill its duty to notify.

Residents get very upset because they are being prosecuted without being aware of the problem.
The proposed amendment allows for notice to be provided by posting at the property, handing it
to the individual personally, or sending it by regular mail. The proposal was presented to the
Planning Commission which recommended that the Code be updated to require the City to use two
methods to provide notice. Ms. Smith stated that this was not her recommendation and she felt it
was unnecessarily duplicative. For context, she stated that a criminal case can be initiated by
having an officer hand the subject a citation or the prosecutor can file a document and request a
summons firom a judge. The summons can be sent by regular mail, which costs $0.63. If the
subject misses their court date,a warrant is issued. In the context ofproviding a Notice ofViolation
before prosecution commences,sending it one ofthree ways is very targeted at providing a Notice
ofNuisance and will achieve the goals of both the residents and the City.


Council Member Hales brought up the Rules of Civil Procedure, where mailing notification is
permissible with exceptions based on age and incapacity. This proposal bypasses that altogether,
which means that in those cases it does not fulfill the Rules of Civil Procedure. He felt that the

proposal needs to include the exceptions to Rule 4. Ms. Smith's interpretation was that Rule 4 of
the Utah Rules of Civil Procedure is fulfilled if the subject accepts service by mail rather than
personal service.


Coxmcil Member Hales suggested a modification to the text to state that all service will be done
according to the Rules of Civil Procedure. Ms. Smith recalled that Rule 4-D provides that service
by mail is only permitted if the receiving party accepts it, which is why they hire process servers

       8.2     Ordinance 2023-20 - Text Amendments to Code 11.150.080. 11.160.290, and
               Other Related Text Language Regarding Assurances for Completion of
               Improvements with Applicable Adopted Citv Engineering Standards Related
               to Development(StaffPresenter^ Grant CrowelD,

Mr. Crowell presented the proposed text amendments regarding assurances for the completion of
improvements. He reported that the Planning Commission has bundled some amendments in
response to clarifications to the Utah State Code regarding what cities can and cannot require bonds
for. For example, in most cases, it is not permitted to bond for private landscaping, which is an
enforcement mechanism Bluffdale has used for years to ensure that fi*ont yards are completed since
people generally want their bond money back. This leaves criminal proceedings as the only
remaining enforcement option and treats non-compliance as a Class C Misdemeanor.

The Engineering Department has also had cases where public improvements go through
commercial sites, such as adding culinary water lines to new commercial buildings. Sometimes
these improvements are financially assured through the platting process but in many cases, they
are not. It was noted that the City can still bond for public improvements.

Under Site Plan Ordinance 11.150, the City still requires improvements to be put in, including
landscaping, but can only bond for certain things. The language is modeled after the subdivision
ordinance bonding language. Front yard landscaping has been required in most subdivisions for a
long time and certain standards must be met. They cannot simply require a bond. Mr. Crowell
pointed out an error in the language that needed to be amended as noted by Council Member
Crockett to read,"Each single-family dwelling unit shall landscape the lot or parcel from the front
ofthe unit to the street. Required landscaping shall be completed within one year from the date of
the Certificate ofOccupancy and all landscaping shall adhere to the City's adopted water efficiency
and other applicable standards."

Not being able to bond makes it difficult for the City as they have to keep a list and assign it to
Code Enforcement to check 12 months after each Certificate ofOccupancy is issued. Enforcement
issues were discussed. The proposed ordinance ensures compliance with State law and increases
the responsibility for public improvements.

       8.4     Ordinance 2023-23- Amend Sections 11.90 and 11.100 of the Bluffdale City
               Code Related to Eliminating the Minimum Lot Area Requirements and
               Reducing Frontage and Setback Requirements for the Heavy Commercial and
               Light Industrial Zone. Along with Other Related Provisions (StaffPresenter,

Mr. Crowell presented an ordinance to amend the City Code to eliminate minimum lot area
requirements and reduce frontage and setback requirements for the Heavy Commercial and Light
Industrial Zones. He characterized the one-half-acre lot area standard in the Heavy Commercial
Zone as archaic given that most commercial zones have no lot area. The Heavy Commercial and
Light Industrial Zones are very similar and exist side-by-side. He demonstrated a table ofLot Area
Requirements in similar zones that show almost no minimum lot area requirements with the
exception of Professional Office, Heavy Commercial, and Light Industrial. Setbacks are similar
with some that can be reduced to zero, which means that the standard is effectively zero. Many
zones have little or no requirements or represent single areas that are already developed or zones
that exist in the ordinance but not on the map. Some ofthese requirements get in the way ofcurrent
right-of-way acquisition efforts. Mr. Crowell reported that he gets a lot of questions about the
reasoning behind some ofthe requirements but many were written decades ago with no explanation
ofthat reasoning. He favored a more general approach.

Mayor Hall commented that in reviewing the City Code and the research involved in this project
took months of work. She thanked Mr. Crowell for his efforts. Mr. Crowell pointed out that a lot
ofideas around planning have evolved since some ofthese requirements were put in place.

Council Member Aston asked why side and rear setbacks are being reduced instead of being left
as they are or eliminated. Mr. Crowell recalled a discussion with the Planning Commission about
the setback requirements. They had been 20 feet but a total reduction ofup to 20 feet was allowed,
which was confusing. He did not have a specific answer but felt that a reduction from 20 was
appropriate.

Mr. Crowell provided backgroimd information on a Resolution Authorizing an Interlocal
Cooperation Agreement with Salt Lake County Housing to allocate funds to the Salt Lake County
Housing Trust Fund. This is the implementation item for work that has been done on the Housing
Plan, the General Plan, and yearly reporting and budgeting. They were tasked with finding a
partner to utilize some of the money that was set aside from Economic Development Areas
("EDA"). A fair fund balance has been accumulated and they sought partners to utilize it with.
They contacted the Salt Lake County Housing Trust Fund and worked with Dina Blaes, Mike
Gallegos, Josh Narvaez, and Kersten Swinyard. There is no dedicated housing staffin Bluffdale
so they sought expertise elsewhere to make sure they can be effective, utilize the money, and be
able to report back to the State on what they are doing.

An Interlocal Agreement with Salt Lake Coxmty has been drafted to take some RDA and put them
to good use with the County Housing Trust Fimd. The Salt Lake County Council approved the
Agreement. $2.5 million has been budgeted from the RDA but $85,000 more will be required to
make the contract, which will require a head nod on a future budget amendment. An RDA attorney
has reviewed it. This is a five-year agreement for $2.5 million, which will be allocated to the
Housing Trust Fund, which is administered by the County. There is an associated administration
fee for their staffto help implement it.

Under the guidance ofthe General Plan and housing goals,they developed a Scope ofWork. There
has been a lot of focus on Accessory Dwelling Units ("ADU") and they want to work with the
County and will likely hire a Consultant to further develop ways to encourage and educate people
on ADUs, including any pitfalls, available financing, and facilitating permits. The Council has
identified ADUs as easier to do in this community than other options the Legislature has provided,
so they want to pursue it. This would account for a small amount ofthe budgeted funds.

They want to use the bulk of the funds for affordable housing units, workforce housing, or
moderate-income housing in the City. The Housing Trust Fund will administer Requests for
Applications for designated ADUs but also larger projects. Some funds will be available for large
projects that want to dedicate a portion to moderate-income housing. They will work with County
staffto develop the application. The County would like to find representatives from Bluffdale City
to serve on the Housing Trust Fund Advisory Board.

Bluffdale could meet the requirement by sending a check to the Olene Walker Housing Trust Fund
and using those funds anywhere in the State. They are, however, hoping to get developer interest
