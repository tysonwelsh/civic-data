AWARDS, CEREMONIES, AND PROCLAMATIONS
Councilmember Christensen read a proclamation declaring September 28, 2024 as
National Good Neighbor Day in West Valley City.

Randy Whitehead expressed pride in the partnership between the "My Hometown"
initiative and West Valley City, emphasizing the shared goal of building community,
uplifting individuals, and embodying the values highlighted in the city's proclamation. He
highlighted the importance of kindness and friendship among neighbors as central to the
quality of a neighborhood. Mr. Whitehead praised the city council's vision to make West
Valley City known as the "friendliest, kindest, most inclusive, and most beautiful city." He
encouraged everyone to play a role in executing that vision, as it starts with individual
actions. With 250 volunteers, the "My Hometown" initiative is dedicated to working with
Mayor Lang, the city council, Ifo Pili, Craig Thomas, and their teams to achieve the
objective of community building and individual upliftment. He closed by calling on
everyone to showcase what it means to be a good neighbor, expressing gratitude for the
partnership and support from the city.

PUBLIC COMMENT PERIOD
Karina McLellan from Ability Inclusion Services, a nonprofit serving adults with
disabilities in Salt Lake County and surrounding areas, introduced the organization to the
City Council and the community. She highlighted several key services provided through
their community day program:
1. Creative Arts Department: Participants express themselves through painting and
    mixed media crafts, showcased in their annual art show. This year, it will be held on
    Thursday, October 24th, at their West Valley center. Attendees can bid on art pieces
    through a QR code or online for those unable to attend in person.
2. Adaptive Fitness Center: The program offers indoor and outdoor fitness facilities,
    staffed by exercise specialists and supported by students from the University of Utah,
    Salt Lake Community College, and Joyce University, helping participants improve
    physical functionality.
3. New Developments: Ability Inclusion Services is currently building a library and
    sensory room to enhance their community engagement offerings.
4. Community Inclusion Focus: They strive to ensure their clients engage with the
    broader community through visits to zoos, parks, museums, and other local venues.
    This is aimed at countering the isolation and depression often faced by people with
    disabilities.
5. Transportation Support: The organization partners with UTA for door-to-door
    services and maintains a fleet of accessible vehicles to facilitate outings and
    appointments for clients who cannot drive.
6. Drug Testing Kits: Ability Inclusion Services holds state contracts to provide drug test
    kits for law enforcement and other agencies, offering streamlined solutions to meet
    specific needs.

7. Residential Program: The organization operates three properties in West Valley and
    South Salt Lake and plans to build a 12-unit complex. Their residential program assists
    with daily living skills, grocery shopping, and case management.
Karina emphasized the lack of ADA-compliant housing in Utah and thanked the Council
for their support in addressing this critical issue to create a more inclusive environment for
the population they serve.

Mike Markham highlighted an issue with a catwalk that connects the back of Valley Junior
High to Alice Way. He proposed installing a gate that would open 15 minutes after school
starts and close 15 minutes before it ends. He recounted a recent incident where around 50
students were found wandering in the neighborhood, ignoring neighbors' attempts to guide
them back to school, leading to the need for police intervention. He expressed frustration
that school authorities believe they lack the power to manage students once they leave
school grounds and suggested that a gate could help mitigate these issues and reduce the
time police spend rounding up students. Mike also commented on the "My Hometown"
program, suggesting that if West Valley City funds are being used, all neighborhoods
should benefit. He noted that some houses in his area have not been painted in over 45
years and offered to cover the costs of paint if someone could assist with the work.

Jim Vesock encouraged everyone to keep the Christensen family in their thoughts and
prayers. He acknowledged the hardships the family has faced in recent weeks and
expressed gratitude for the service that Don Christensen and his wife have provided to the
community over the years. Jim emphasized the importance of support and compassion
during difficult times, urging everyone to remember the family in their daily reflections.

Frank Pedroza raised concerns regarding a dangerous situation at the crosswalk located at
4800 West and approximately 3700 South. He described an incident where a large SUV
sped through the crosswalk, disregarding the flashing lights meant to alert drivers to the
presence of pedestrians. Frank expressed frustration at the challenges he faced when
attempting to report this incident to the West Valley non-emergency police line, noting a
lack of familiarity from dispatch regarding the location. He emphasized the urgency of the
matter, highlighting that a similar incident had occurred involving his partner just a month
prior. Frank pointed out that if an injury or fatality were to occur, it would cast an even
worse light on the community and its safety measures. He urged the Council to consider
taking action to improve safety at the crosswalk to protect children and crossing guards,
concluding his remarks by thanking them for listening.

Josh Randall shared his thoughts on improving West Valley City, expressing his desire to
see it reach its full potential. Having recently run for a U.S. Senate seat and considering a
future role on the council, he spoke about the importance of creating an environment that
fosters community pride and safety, especially for families. He emphasized that making
West Valley’s high schools the best in Utah is a goal he shares with his wife, with whom

ORDINANCE 24-40: AMEND SECTIONS 3-11-103, 3-11-105. AND 3-11-106 OF
THE WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN
PROVISIONS GOVERNING THE EMPLOYEE DISCIPLINE HEARING
OFFICER
Mayor Lang presented proposed Ordinance 24-40 that would amend Sections 3-11-103, 3-
11-105. and 3-11-106 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Amend Certain Provisions
Governing the Employee Discipline Hearing Officer.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The proposed ordinance will update the appeal process for employee discipline
       cases. Key changes include clarifying appeal filing requirements, establishing a
       specific timeline for hearings, outlining general hearing procedures, and detailing
       the evidence to be considered by the Hearing Officer.

       This action is necessary to ensure that the procedures for handling employee
       discipline appeals are clear and consistent with state law and best practices. The
       amendments aim to streamline the appeal process, provide clearer guidance for all
       parties involved, and protect the rights of employees while ensuring fair and timely
       hearings.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

RESOLUTION 24-141: APPROVE THE PURCHASE OF NETWORK SECURITY
PRODUCTS AND RELATED EQUIPMENT
Mayor Lang presented proposed Resolution 24-141 that would approve the Purchase of
Network Security Products and Related Equipment.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       1. Network Security Enhancements from Manage Engine:
              Description: The implementation of Endpoint Central will enable the
                 management of all desktops, laptops, and mobile devices, allowing
                 employees to install approved software and request IT helpdesk
                 assistance. AD Audit will monitor and analyze login and network access
                 activities, as well as changes to user accounts. ADSelfService Plus
                 Professional will provide a centralized platform for password
                 management and policy enforcement.
              Quoted Cost: $40,000
       2. Server Upgrades and Virtualization:
              Description: This project involves replacing outdated servers used for
                 virtualization to improve resource utilization and maintain redundancy,
                 ensuring the City's IT infrastructure remains robust and efficient.
              Quoted Cost: $27,424.54
       3. Network Infrastructure Expansion:
              Description: Many network switches and related equipment at city
                 facilities are outdated, with some no longer receiving security patches
                 or supporting modern features. Upgrading this equipment will enhance
                 security and reliability by enabling the use of modern features and
                 ensuring the ability to apply necessary patches.
              Quoted Cost: $252,840.88

       These projects are essential to address current vulnerabilities and to support the
       City's ongoing operational needs.
