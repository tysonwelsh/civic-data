SMI-6-2025
Lexington Park Minor Subdivision
2309 West 3650 South
RM Zone/2 Lots

BACKGROUND
Table Rock Capital is requesting minor subdivision approval for the Lexington Park Minor
Subdivision. The proposed subdivision is located on property that was approved as an apartment
complex in 1990. The density for the apartment project was approved by development agreement
when the property was given its RM Zoning. The subdivision is bordered on the north and south
by existing rental units and the east by 3 existing single-family dwellings. The project is bordered
on the west by I-215.

STAFF/AGENCY CONCERNS
Since the project is existing, there are no staff or agency concerns. In addition, if the new lot were
to be developed in the future, the property owners would be required to submit a plat amendment,
at which time a more thorough review of subdivision requirements would take place.

ISSUES
The property owner acquired Lexington Park in May 2022. When staff inquired about the need
for a second lot, the owner explained that the purpose for the subdivision is to create an additional
lot that could potentially accommodate future residential housing. While the property is zoned
RM, additional housing would necessitate an amended development agreement. The applicant
explained that their only opportunity to create the lot is now due to financing reasons. Subdividing
the land would allow the property owner to explore new financing options and unlock the
development potential of the underutilized portion of the site.

The property owner explained to staff that the 3-acre portion of the project site located to the west
of the existing rental units requires costly maintenance and has not been very well maintained over
the last few years. City ordinance does not preclude the owner from creating a new lot, nor would
it violate any subdivision requirements. Should the applicant submit an amended development
agreement in the future and if the City Council approved such a request, a new subdivision
application would be required. It would be at that time that the City would impose development
standards regarding the completion of 3650 South and other subdivision requirements.

STAFF ALTERNATIVES
A.   Approve the Lexington Park Minor Subdivision subject to staff approvals.

B.     Continuance to allow the applicant an opportunity to address issues raised during the public
       hearing.

Discussion: Steve Lehman presented. The applicant, Natasha Alarid representing Table Rock
Capital, was present. There were no questions or comments from the commission or public.

Motion: Commissioner Winters motioned to approve SMI-1-2025. Commissioner Porter
seconded. A voice vote was taken, and all were in favor of the motion.

CA-1-2025
Intermountain Fuel Delivery (Outside Storage)
2565 S. Decker Lake Lane
M Zone (2.45 acres total/.29 acres outside storage)

The applicant, Mario Verde representing Intermountain Fuel Delivery, is requesting
conditional use amendment approval for outside storage at 2565 S. Decker Lake Lane. This
property is zoned M, manufacturing and the West Valley City General Plan designates this area
as Light Manufacturing. The surrounding zone is Manufacturing on the north, south and west
sides. The property to the east is currently zoned RM, Residential Multi-Family. The surrounding
use includes warehouse, office and light manufacturing and Redwood Elementary to the east.

The portion of the property being reviewed for this use appears to have been originally part of
the Redwood Elementary grounds. In 2007 there was an approval for outside storage (C-35-
2007) for snow machine equipment. Since this equipment was all tracked the surfacing was

approved with gravel. Also, since the lot was adjacent to Redwood Elementary, no landscaping
setback or masonry screening wall was required. That tenant has since left the site, and Mr.
Verde would like to lease a small portion of the yard for his fuel delivery business.

Intermountain Fuel Delivery has one employee that will come to the site, and the company will
not be using any portion of the building, which is occupied by a separate tenant. The company
delivers dyed (off road) diesel fuel, or clear (on highway) diesel fuel to construction sites and
backup generators for buildings.

A list of assets that will be stored on this property is as follows:
(1) 2009 Peterbilt tank wagon
(1) 2020 GMC dually
(1) 2019 Thundercreek MMT 690 fuel trailer
(1) shipping container to hold some tools, extra meters, and sorbents
(1) forklift
(9) ul142 rated double wall self-contained rental fuel tanks

During the Study Meeting the Commission expressed some concerns about the use and potential
environmental impacts. The applicant has since provided additional information to outline the
measures taken to ensure safe protocols are in place. This includes the following:

   •   The Peterbilt tank wagon goes through strict inspections every year to make sure all of
       the equipment, tank welds, and truck are all clean, not cracked or broken and are in good
       working order.
   •   The double wall UL142 rated rental tanks leave the yard empty, and are filled on the
       customer site, then the rental tanks are empty before being picked back up.
   •   Working on tank inerting when they are put back into inventory.
   •   Carry spill cleanup kits are located on all trucks, which include sorbents and other
       cleaning material that will also be stored on the property.
   •   Employees pass all background, TSA, tanker, and hazmat testing required by the State in
       order to drive the Peterbilt legally.
   •   Intermountain Fuel Delivery has an account with Enviro Care in case there was a spill
       emergency.
   •   Carry a $4,000,000 pollution insurance policy.

This application has been reviewed by the West Valley City Fire Department. They have
indicated that a Hazardous Materials permit may be required, however it is likely not necessary
with empty/inert tanks. They will do a review to ensure all safety protocols are in place as part of
the business license review. The West Valley City Public Works Department has also done a
review and may require a Long-Term Storm Water Management Permit. However, again with
empty and inert tanks this is typically not a requirement. All these reviews and procedures are in
place to ensure that all environmental and safety protocols and measures are in place. Also, a
West Vally City business license will be required to ensure necessary contact information is
available to the City. All existing fencing must be maintained in good condition. Also, the gravel
surface must be maintained and kept free of weeds.

Discussion: Jody Knapp presented. Commissioner Winters asked if there was a point when the
City would require the gravel to be paved. Jody’s response was that typically outside storage
requires hard surface, but gravel is an exception for tracked equipment. She added that Public
Works feels more comfortable leaving it gravel for this use because if this area was paved there
may be stormwater runoff impacts.

The applicant, Kristy Verde was available online. She didn’t have comments to add.

Commissioner Durfee commented that he would like to see slats added in the chain link fence.
Other commission members didn’t feel that was important at this time due to the limited scope of
the requested amendment.

Motion: Commissioner Porter motioned to approve CA-1-2025 subject to the 8 conditions in the
Staff Report. Commissioner Drozdek seconded. A vote of roll call was taken, and all were in favor
of the motion.
