         3.1. Formation of Fiber Optic Utility – Nathan Cherpeski, City Manager
      City Manager Nathan Cherpeski began the fiber optic utility discussion acknowledging the
      challenges in initiating such a project. He turned the time over to Strata Networks Engineer
      Bart Angus to lead the presentation. Engineer Angus informed the Council the high-level
      design for the fiber optic utility had been completed and highlighted the project area
      boundaries which were divided into areas and explained the design of serving areas. Mr.
November 8, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                             Page 3 of 11


      Angus presented the timeline summary for the design, mainline construction, premises
      installations with network operations commencing sometime in 2025.

      City Manager Cherpeski recommended taking a step back and looking into the financials more
      as he wanted to understand the interest rate percentage as it was the driving factor on
      feasibility. He suggested looking to see what other communities are doing to form a utility
      and revisit the feasibility study. Councilmember Henderson highlighted the need to talk with
      the bounding counsel. The Council agreed.

      Councilmember Shields thought the City could consider a phased approach, potentially
      focusing on specific areas initially to establish higher take rate and secure more favorable
      financing. He also offered the option to move to an opt-in model and pledge sales tax for the
      utility, which was not a favorable path forward; however, wanted to recognize there were
      options available to move forward.

          3.2. Consideration to enter into a reimbursement agreement for Cost of Pressure
               Reducing Valve – Jonathan Bowers, Public Utility Engineering Manager
      Public Utility Engineering Manager Jonathan Bowers began by discussing the East Ridge
      project from 2015, emphasizing the necessity of a hydraulic analysis for water system
      installations to ensure no adverse effects on the City. The analysis indicated the need for a
      new tank and pipeline to address low pressures in an adjacent area.

      During phase one of the project, a tank was constructed. However, when the calibrated model
      was later employed, it revealed that a Pressure Reducing Valve (PRV) and the tank were no
      longer required for redundancy. The developer had already purchased the PRV based on the
      initial model, leading to the current situation where the City was asked to buy the unused
      PRV. The Council asked if it should be reimbursed and repurposed.

      Manager Bowers presented a map of the development, indicating the location of the
      purchased PRV. The Council raised questions about the necessity of the PRV, potential
      budget implications, and the possibility of reimbursing and reselling the PRV for a better
      price. The Council offered direction toward reimbursing the cost and repurposing the
      equipment.

          3.3. 2024 PRCA Rodeo Discussion – Lorren Mitchell, Events Manager
      Events Manager Lorren Mitchell noted the 2024 PRCA rodeo in Herriman would be marked
      as the 18th year hosting the event. She introduced the Rodeo Committee who had explored
      the possibility of expanding the rodeo to three days due to consistently sold-out
      performances over the last three years. They also considered increasing prize money to
      attract top-ranking cowboys.
November 8, 2023 – City Council Minutes                                               Page 4 of 11


      The Council reviewed the budget breakdown for 2023 and a conservative estimate for 2024,
      factoring in the proposed changes. The discussion included potential adjustments to
      accommodate the expansion, such as adding seats and exploring VIP packages. Rodeo
      Committee member Dee Oakeson highlighted the success of the PRCA rodeo over the years
      and expressed the need for expansion to maintain interest. He emphasized the importance of
      increasing prize money to attract a diverse group of cowboys.

      The Council delved into the significance of PRCA rodeos, distinguishing them from amateur
      events and acknowledging their professional standards. They discussed the competitive
      landscape, with only Eagle Mountain being another city in the vicinity to host PRCA rodeos.
      The conversation expanded to include the efforts of Patrick, who handled marketing for
      Herriman, showcasing the potential economic impact and positive community exposure. City
      Manager Cherpeski discussed the possibility of utilizing TRCC money for future
      improvements, including additional seating.

      The Council acknowledged the event's popularity and the potential to attract sponsorships
      and expressed enthusiasm for the rodeo's positive impact on local businesses. The Council
      expressed support of having an additional performance during the Herriman PRCA Rodeo in
      2024.

         3.4. Discussion on the assessment of Ice Ribbon fees – Anthony Teuscher, Deputy
              Director of Parks, Recreation and Events
      Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events Anthony Teuscher led the discussion about
      assessing fees for the ice ribbon. He presented various numbers and information to help the
      Council make an informed decision about whether to increase fees and if deemed necessary,
      by how much. The ice ribbon, in its 7th season, had seen a rise in seasonal employee wages
      from $9 to $14 per hour. The current fees were outlined, including adult admission at $3, child
      admission at $2, and skate rental at $3.

      A comparison with surrounding facilities was presented, and last year's revenue of $166,000
      was analyzed. Deputy Director Teuscher delved into operational expenses, including payroll,
      maintenance, and utilities, amounting to over $100,000. The bond payment for the facility,
      which included the ice ribbon, was discussed, with a breakdown attributing about 21-percent
      or $323,000 annually directly to the ice ribbon until 2033.

      The Council expressed concern about potential fee increases affecting the public, and the
      discussion extended to include new ticketing software, Roller Pro, which was expected to
      reduce fees for end-users. Various fee adjustment scenarios were considered, including
      doubling fees and increasing skate rentals to cover operational and capital costs. Council
      consensus determined to raise fees for adult admission at $6, child admission at $4, and skate
      rental at $4. The Council also considered maintaining discounted rates for seniors and

      veterans at $3. These adjustments were planned to take effect January 1, 2024, with pre-
      registration starting next Monday for the upcoming season.

          3.5. Discussion of proposed amendments to the fiscal year 2024 budget – Kyle Maurer,
               Finance Director
      Finance Director Kyle Maurer addressed the Council, explained the focus on carrying
      forward unused budget from the prior year to support ongoing projects. He mentioned a
      reduction in sales tax receipts, and proposed a budget adjustment of approximately $1.1
      million, attributing it to a capital lease for vehicles and cost reduction in animal services.

      Director Maurer emphasized the one-time nature of the funds and hinted at the need for a
      more permanent adjustment during the upcoming budget discussions. City Manager
      Cherpeski expressed the intent to propose a solution as revenue projections needed
      adjustment. Funds were allocated for the school safety shuttle and various project carryovers
      were presented. There were concerns about the sales tax adjustments being raised, and
      Mayor Palmer inquired about market trends. Director Maurer noted stability in local sales tax
      revenue, particularly from major contributors like Amazon and grocery stores; however,
      challenges arose due to a lack of detailed information on statewide pool trends.

      Director Maurer spoke on the park impact fee fund, general Capital Projects fund, and water
      impact fee funds and included updates on land purchases, right-of-way acquisitions, and
      developments for water. He informed the Council the budget amendment would be
      considered at the next Council meeting.

         3.6. Consideration of New Policy Regarding Crossing Guards – Bryce Terry, City

      9. Reports, Presentations and Appointments
         9.1. Presentation from Latinos in Action, Copper Mountain Middle School
      Copper Mountain Middle School Latinos in Action (LIA) expressed gratitude for the
      opportunity and introduced the mission of LIA, which is to empower Latino youth for college
      and career readiness through culture, service, leadership, and excellence in education.

      Various students from LIA shared their roles and backgrounds. Mission pillars were outlined,
      emphasizing personal assets, excellence in education, service, and leadership. Statistics were
      presented, indicating an increase in Latino English speakers at Copper Mountain Middle
      School.

      The LIA team emphasized the importance of community involvement, cultural understanding,
      and support for Latino students. They sought financial assistance for activities such as
      cultural nights and events, translation services, tutoring, and supplies for educational
      initiatives. Mayor Palmer raised concerns about the tight budget, but expressed interest in
      supporting LIA's goals, offering ideas for collaboration, and suggesting avenues for funding.
      The Council proposed exploring alternative funding sources and offering their support to
      help LIA achieve its objectives.

          9.2. Presentation of the Audited Financial Statements for Fiscal Year Ended June 30,
               2023
      Hinton Burdick CPA McCay Hall presented the results of the audited financial statements for
      fiscal year ended June 30, 2023. Mr. Hall explained the objectives of his firm when they
      performed an audit. He stated when an audit was conducted, the firm adhered to the audit
      standards generally accepted across the country. Mr. Hall commended the Finance
      Department for their efforts in no material findings.

      Mr. Hall reported that the financial statements received a clean, unmodified opinion,
      indicating their material correctness. The presentation highlighted the city's commendable
      progress in addressing past material weaknesses and significant deficiencies. No such issues
      were reported for the current year, showcasing the finance department's dedication and
      improvement.

      Mr. Hall discussed the key financial figures which included the total net position of the city,
      which stood at $598 million as of June 30, 2023, with a detailed breakdown provided. Mr. Hall
      emphasized the importance of monitoring the unrestricted net position over time as an
      indicator of the organization's financial health. He presented a detailed analysis of five-year
      trends, covering the general fund balance sheet, cash trends, revenue and expenditure
      trends, safety enforcement, fire safety area, community development and renewal agency,
      capital projects, and water fund revenues and expenses.

          11.1. Review and consider a request by Brandon and Anna Parker (property owners) to
                amend the General Plan Future Land Use Map from Hillside and Agricultural
                Residential to Neighborhood One for ±5.3 acres of property at 13286 S Rose
                Canyon Road – Michael Maloy, City Planner (City File No. G2023-108)
      City Planner Michael Maloy oriented the Council to the location of the proposal and
      highlighted the current zoning map and suggested it would have a domino effect on
      surrounding properties. He explained the proposed aimed to be a friendly amendment,
      considering the preservation of true large lot agricultural uses while being adjacent to
      existing agriculturally used properties. The community seemed generally supportive during
      the outreach process, favoring larger lots, although some concerns were raised during the
      Planning Commission's public hearing regarding irrigation, drainage, and large animal rights
      associated with potential rezoning.

      Planner Maloy presented a conceptual layout of nine lots with a density of 1.7 units per acre,
      below the maximum allowed by the requested rezone. This proposal also allowed for the
      preservation of the existing home and orchards. He asked the Council if there were any
      questions. There were none.

      The Council raised concerns about the conceptual nature of the presented layout and the
      potential for changes post-rezoning. The applicant expressed openness to placing
      restrictions on the development, ensuring compatibility with the desired larger lots in the
      neighborhood.

      Councilmember Henderson moved to approve Ordinance No. 2023-19 amending the General
      Plan future land use map for 5.3 acres of property located at 13286 South Rose Canyon Road
      from Hillside and agricultural residential to neighborhood one. Councilmember Hodges
      seconded the motion.
