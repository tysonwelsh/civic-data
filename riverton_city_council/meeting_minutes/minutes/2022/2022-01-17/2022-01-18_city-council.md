         Discussion on City Property used to Access Private Property (re: Glen & Kimberly
         Johnson) – Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director and Ryan Carter,
         City Attorney

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the Council will be focusing on
one particular piece of property where the homeowners have requested access to private
property owned by Riverton City. However, he said that there would be ramifications for other
properties in the City based on the decision tonight. The property in question is on Lovers Lane
and owned by Glen and Kimberly Johnson. The private property they are requesting access to
is the City’s open space property. Mr. Lethbridge explained that the Council needed to
determine if there was a process by which someone could apply or make a proposal to use
public property as a temporary access point. He displayed a couple of examples of previous
requests to access City property. Up to this point, the City has said at a staff level that public
property is not available for private use or access.

Councilmember Stewart said that he would like to require a bond to use this public land to cover
repairs.

David Brickey, City Manager, said the bonding process is very timely and not always successful
in getting payment. He suggested some other means for obtaining cash value and said that
money would be returned to the applicant if there was no damage done to the public property.
He believes there is more compliance if the City has cash in hand.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, explained that if the City were doing a road cut permit, they would
require a fee at $0.25 per square foot, an insurance policy against third party liability, and a
bond of some kind. He said that calculating a bond is easier when talking about a road cut
permit than any other type of landscape because the cost is fixed. He believes that a cash bond
and an insurance policy should be required.

Councilmember Wells commented that homeowners need to be able to get to the back of their
house to landscape. He believes that the only thing affected in this particular access to public
property is weeds. The property owners are willing to pay a bond and replace any damage that
is done.

Councilmember McDougal said that he is against granting use of City property. He does not
want to put more burden on staff. When contractors are using public property, it shuts down
public lands and the trails are no longer usable because of damage. The City should not bear
the cost and have city workers tracking it and following up on it. He understands that
landscaping without the use of the public land will cost the homeowners more but he thinks this
is a cost that the homeowner should bear, not the City.

         Redistricting Process – David Brickey, City Manager

David Brickey, City Manager, said that the census concluded on November 16, 2021 and under
state statute the City has 6 months to approve new districting lines. He said that two
Councilmembers had visited with Casey Saxton and worked on possible redistricting plans
using the existing structure of the five districts. He presented four redistricting maps and said
that he would send these to all of the Councilmembers. These maps are included in the packet.

Mr. Brickey explained that he wanted to send a message to the County that district lines should
be linear and straight. As growth develops in District 1 and 2, Riverton will signal to the County
that it would like to maintain this particular district line. He would like the redistricting maps to
be approved by the end of March so that he can send the maps to the County. He asked for
input from the Council.

Councilmember Stewart appreciates the effort that Councilmember McCay has made with the
redistricting maps. He said that he likes Option 4 because it keeps his district smaller in
anticipation of future development.

Councilmember Buroker commented she would like to be educated about the County’s
responsibility for the redistricting process. She also said she would be interested in revising the
line on the southern end that moves across Bangerter in the green area. She said there were
discussions at one time to recede that area to Bluffdale but residents in this area did not agree.

Councilmember Buroker reported on the meeting she attended which was sponsored by the
Speaker of the House, Brad Wilson, about the Great Salt Lake. She learned that snow pack
needs to melt over the entire summer, not all at one time. When it melts all at one time, it flows
into the lakes and it is not able to be accessed. Because the Great Salt Lake has receded so
much, the dust sits on the snow and causes it to melt faster. There are toxic metals that have
been found in the soil and these metals create harmful health effects which have been
measured showing that Utah has a higher incidence of lung cancer. She said that they talked
about Owens Lake in California which was the dry lake bed created when the massive aqueduct
was built to provide water to Los Angeles. This has cost California billions of dollars because
they had to mitigate the impact of that dry lake bed and their message to the state of Utah is to
keep water in the Great Salt Lake. The final message of the meeting was for cities to look at
ordinances that discourage water conservation and to fix these ordinances. She thought the
Speaker did a good job of putting this information together and she was glad to be there.

         Ordinance No. 22-04 – Grundman Rezone, 1.93 Acre At 12317 and 12325 South
         Redwood Road, Proposed Rezone from RR-22 to RM-6. Applicant-Cory Grundman
         with Dredge Management LLC

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said that this is a public hearing for an
application to Rezone 1.93-acres of land located at 12317 and 12325 South Redwood
Road. The subject property is two parcels on the east side of Redwood Road. Each parcel is
narrow and deep running from Redwood Road eastward nearly 500-ft to the boundary with
Riverton Chase Subdivision. Each parcel has a house, the north house is about 970-sf and
was built in 1918, the house on the south is about 1,090-sf and was built in 1914. The houses
sit near the front of the lots, close to Redwood Road. Zoning in the area is mostly residential.
The subject properties are Zoned RR-22 (Single Family, half-acre lots, with animal rights), this
RR-22 zoning continues southward to the LDS Church just north of the Spirit Corner. Properties
to the north, are zoned R-4 (Single Family 10,000-sf lots) to the east the Zoning is R-3 (Single
family 14,000-sf lots). On the west side of Redwood Road the zoning is R-4. The applicant has
requested a rezone to RM-6 with the stated intention to “Develop 10 lots to build single family
homes”

Cory Grundman, Applicant, commented that he had a development plan in mind and the plan
would work best with RM-6 because of the narrowness of the property. He said that he was not
aware of the historic district standards.

Councilmember McCay asked if the developer had a site plan and if the site plan had attached
units. Mr. Grundman responded that his development plan had 9-10 detached homes.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the development plan shows a private road going down the
middle of the property and a stub road to the south to provide future interconnectivity. This will
likely be the design regardless of ownership. He said that some residents had raised concerns
about drainage issues which he explained would be addressed at the next step of the
development process, not during the rezone process.
