3. Public Hearing Petition #2021-6 to rezone, property at 768 W 24th Street from R-1-5 to MU to
   allow live/work townhome project. (Attachment 3)

  Mr. Pierre Langue stated he desires to purchase this property and construct live/work
  residential townhomes. The dwelling units are proposed to be 1500 square feet with small
  commercial spaces on the ground floor facing 24th Street. Access to the units would be from



                                                  3
the rear.

Mr. Simpson stated he has now received updated plans from the applicant which address
some of the concerns identified in the Staff recommendations, but that the MU zoning
requires the establishment of a Development Agreement, and for that agreement a general
layout of the project is required. The petition is to rezone the property from single-family
residential to MU to accommodate the use proposed. The plan shows existing parcels being
combined, as well as the use of a recorded right-of-way which has not been developed. This
right-of-way was platted prior to the installation of both the railroad or the interstate and was
anticipated to provide connection through the area at that time, but now has no ability to
connect, and has never been improved. This right-of-way will be required to be vacated prior
to the project being approved. He reviewed the MU Ordinance, indicating the main MU area is
the Ogden River project, with another area which has previously been approved but not yet
developed on Porter Avenue. Consideration is given to surrounding land uses, and uses in this
area are a mix of commercial, residential and service businesses. The MU Ordinance requires
a development agreement to be adopted with the rezoning and the proposed project does not
satisfy the standards of that zone.

The proposal is for 25 units in five buildings with commercial space on the ground floor facing
24th Street with apartments on the second level. Each townhome will have a two-car attached
garage. A fitness center also is included for the residents of the project. While the West
Ogden Community Plan does list an option for MU zoning, it was anticipated the zone would
be triggered by the installation of the full I-15 interchange.

He reviewed the revised site plans and indicated a pedestrian connection has been added..
However, a detailed landscape plan is also required. He noted the plan shows units facing the
railroad tracks and suggested the utilities be located near the tracks with units relocated. The
gathering areas should be away from vehicle traffic areas and buffers should be provided to
provide a separation between buildings. The MU zone is specific in its architectural design,
requiring three types of buildings with a variation in rooflines. There also is a requirement for
identification of a base, body and cap in its architectural design to fit with the architecture of
Ogden as well as consideration being given to being compatible with existing architecture in
the area. The application shows buildings that are boxy with a futuristic design which do not
comply with these standards. Plans should include adjacent buildings and buildings should
complement the surrounding area. He suggested the applicant pattern the architecture after
buildings in the Ogden Riverbend project. As there are a number of items yet to be addressed
to meet the standards of the MU zone and the intent of the West Ogden Plan, Staff
recommends the item be tabled so the project can be designed to meet these standards. He
also noted some distinct design standards also may need to be developed and added to the
MU Ordinance to address specific needs of the West Ogden community.

Commissioner Safsten stated the architecture of this area is eclectic and would be difficult to
match. He agreed a buffer should be provided between the residential uses and the existing
railroad tracks. Commissioner Stoker asked if the intent is to mirror the design standards of
the Ogden River project or to create specific design standards for this area. She felt the
base/body/cap architectural design is appropriate for Ogden but felt some contemporary



                                               4
design could be used as accents. She noted this area has its own setting and the MU zone
would be much smaller than the Ogden River project. Commissioner Schade suggested the
northern driveway be relocated to the other side of the building and the accessway could
provide a buffer to the railroad tracks. He expressed concern with the proximity of the western
units to F Avenue and suggested a glass storefronts be added to the F Avenue side of the
building facing 24th Street. Commissioner Garner felt the MU zoning is appropriate for the
West Ogden community but agreed there should be a buffer between units and the railroad
tracks, and felt the view for residents could be improved. Commissioner Southwick stated
this would be a good project for the West Ogden area. He asked if the units would be offered
for sale or become rentals as well as whether proposed workspace is tied to the tenant on the
upper floor. Mr. Langue responded while the design allows for units to be sold as individual
units, he is unsure as to whether they will be rented or sold. Commissioner Sandau felt the
architecture should fit with the houses in the West Ogden community, suggesting that a
pitched roof be considered..

MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to table action until the September
             1st meeting of the Commission asking the developer to refine the plan to better fit
             the MU Ordinance and other recommendations of Staff. Motion was seconded by
             Commissioner Schade.

 Mr. Langue stated his purchase offer expires on August 15th and asked if action could be
expedited. Mr. Montgomery stated the upcoming work session has 2-3 agenda item as well as
a discussion item which will take a good deal of time. Mr. Stratford noted because of the
regulations of the MU Ordinance, a design concept and development agreement are both tied
to the rezoning request and should be in place before the item is forwarded to the City Council.
He stated only if the Commission feels conditions of approval could be established to satisfy
these items should they forward a positive recommendation to the City Council. Mr.
Montgomery noted that there will be no additional Planning Commission meetings before
August 15th and the rezoning is not approved by the Commission, but is a recommendation to
the City Council. The item could not be prepared to be sent to the Council until these items are
worked out with Staff. He also stated the project is dependent upon Cahoon Street being
vacated, and if that were to be denied, the project would need a complete redesign. He stated
there are enough concerns with this project that it is not ready to be approved. is unaware of
what the final project will look like.

Commissioner Southwick felt the request is consistent with the West Ogden Plan and
suggested if each of the items listed by Staff were to be accomplished, the rezoning would be
appropriate. Mr. Montgomery responded, indicating the MU zone was created mainly for
redevelopment projects, and ordinance language needs to be amended to address
architectural items for this particular area as it is outside a redevelopment district. He stated
there are many unknowns such as architectural design, rooflines, building orientation location
of open space, etc. and at this point, he felt the Commission does not have enough information
to forward a recommendation to the City Council.

At this time, Commissioner Safsten withdrew his previous motion.

7. Conditional Use Permit/Preliminary Group Dwelling for the Villas at the Pointe, 1120 South
   375 West (Attachment 7)

   Mr. Derek Terry stated he is proposing construction of townhomes and commercial
   development, and has been working with Staff for the past several months, stating the
   Commission had previously approved a alternative transportation design in an effort to
   accommodate a potential overpass along 12th Street to eliminate wait time for rail service in
   the area. The proposal being considered at this time includes both apartment buildings and
   townhomes consisting of 880 units, stating the existing zoning would allow almost double
   that amount. He stated he is aware Staff is recommending the item be continued, but desires




                                               10
a vote on the preliminary plan this evening so he will be able to close on his purchase of the
property. He stated he would return with updated plans prior to final approval being sought
to assure ordinance regulations are satisfied. He stated access to open space has not
changed and garden space is provided between units. He stated Staff expressed concern
about tree preservation, but indicated due to the slope of the property, some will need to be
removed in order to accommodate the development. He stated he has met with the Urban
Forester who has identified some unhealthy trees. Some trees are on an adjacent property,
which will not be removed and will provide some buffer to the adjacent community. He is
requesting preliminary approval, stating he can address the conditions identified by Staff
prior to its being reviewed for final approval. He felt the code is subject to interpretation and
felt the footprints of the buildings would not be changing, but the design concepts could
change to meet the recommendations of Staff.

Mr. Brierley stated this is a proposal for a residential component of an overall project
consisting of 211 units as a group dwelling, 88 apartments and 123 townhomes. The plan
also includes a clubhouse and pool as well as a play area as amenities. He stated while a
landscaping plan and a tree preservation plan have been prepared, Staff is concerned about
the retention of existing significant trees and groves on the lot, citing Ordinance requirements
which indicate trees and shrubs should be retained. There is a significant grouping of mature
trees along the north property line which provides a buffer between this property and the
residential area to the north, and Staff recommends these trees be retained. He stated not

8. Public Hearing, Petition to Amend 15-21-2 to allow warehouse buildings larger than 50,000
   square feet in the BEI zone. (Attachment 8)

  Mr. Robert Smith indicated he is part of a development team interested in purchasing property
  with the intent to construct two buildings. While a tenant has not yet been identified for either
  the existing regulations restrict the size of warehouse buildings in the BEI zone. This petition
  is to amend the code to allow warehouse buildings larger than 50,000 square feet. He noted
  the industrial park already has some covenants and restrictions which will assure building
  type and landscaping are compatible with the area. He stated as tenants are unknown, and
  needs may change over time, it would be well for options to be in place for the use of these
  buildings. He felt the existing regulations might limit potential tenants who may need more
  warehouse space. The zone also has specific regulations which will be satisfied. He stated



                                               13
the appearance of the buildings would be the same as to whether they were building were
used for manufacturing or for warehousing.

Mr. Montgomery stated the petition is to eliminate the size restriction for warehousing in the
BEI zone. He stated there are three remaining lots within the BEI zone as well as a green
space corridor separating each of the lots. The intent of the BEI zone was for research and
manufacturing industries, creating more jobs than would exist if used solely for warehousing.
While warehousing is still allowed as an accessory to the industry, the intent was to keep it
small scale. He stated the first question before the Commission is to consider whether the
intent of the zone has changed. He also indicated warehouse uses typically generates more
truck traffic while manufacturing and research generate more employee traffic. He expressed
concern with the lack of access to this area and areas where traffic is slowed due to railroad
crossings and narrow roads. He suggested one option might be to allow a larger warehouse
facility on Lot #8 as it is larger and is on the edge of the development and could be a transition
to the remaining lots in the park. This option would allow storage with manufacturing uses.
Staff recommendation is to deny the petition as proposed but to approve an option to allow
Lot #8 to have warehousing up to 110,000 square feet.

Commissioner Schade expressed concern about the status of the access road to this area,
stating the County-owned road needs improvements. Mr. Montgomery answered the West
Ogden Community Plan had suggested a traffic signal be installed at D Avenue and 24th Street
to help pedestrian crossings which would entail working with Weber County on the road
system in the area. Commissioner Safsten stated the lot owners feel the existing ordinance
limits as they plan for future uses in the area. He stated the buildings need to be functional
and felt amending the ordinance would provide a wider net for attracting potential tenants to
the area. He stated this is the newest, most attractive business park in the community and
would likely attract the right users. He felt the market could dictate appropriate uses now and
in the future, suggesting due to the existing road system distributors or large warehouse uses
would locate elsewhere. Discussion continued relating to the request of the petitioner and the
recommendation of Staff, noting Staff’s recommendation would limit the size of warehousing
as it currently exists on all lots in the BEI zone but would allow larger warehousing, up to
110,000 square feet on Lot #8;

Mr. Sam McConkie, stated as part of the development team, he is aware of the work done by
Ogden City to create a good light manufacturing zone. He expressed concern with the lack of

tenants for the remaining three lots.

area and while they are not anticipating solely distribution or warehousing tenants, they want
a successful building for now and for the future needs of the area. He stated if roads were
