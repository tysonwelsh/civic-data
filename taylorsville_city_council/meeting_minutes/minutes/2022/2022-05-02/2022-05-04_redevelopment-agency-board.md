2. Resolution No. RDA 22-02- A Resolution of the Board of Directors of the Redevelopment
   Agency of Taylorsville City, Utah, Approving a Real Estate Purchase and Sale Agreement
   between the Redevelopment Agency of Taylorsville City and JT Foothill Village, LC., JRT
   Investments, LLC, and Boyer West Point, LC. for Certain Real Property Located at
   Approximately 3815 West 5400 South - Tracy Cowde/1

   9:17 PM - Because of some questions from some constituents, Mr. Cowdell reviewed the
   history and timeline of the RDA's purchase of the property and the rezone that was
   subsequently done. The RDA is a separate legal entity from the City itself and is able, under
   state law, to be more nimble in some areas, such as purchasing property. He said that the
   ultimate goal of making this purchase was that the RDA would have maximum flexibility and
   some control over what was done with the 16.5 acres of the adjoining center. The property,
   which is approximately 1-1/4 acre, was purchased with bond proceeds, and the design was
   to sell it at the right time.
   Over time, concern grew about the vacant Kmart center and the changing face of retail in
   general. The hope was to have a new owner purchase the center and put something
   substantial there instead of just making small incremental changes in the center over the
   years. By owning this corner property, it gave the RDA and the City a little more say in the
   future of the center than the city's regulatory control alone.

   The RDA decided to purchase the property at market price. The RDA has been renting out
   part of the property for several years, which has helped to pay off the bond. Staff helped to
   recruit a reputable developer for the entire center. Mayor Overson and staff members -
   specifically Wayne Harper, John Taylor, and Scott Harrington - recruited just such a
   developer in the Thackeray Company. And now the city has entered into a series of
   agreements with them to reach a plan that will develop the property into a mixed-use
   center. Part of the agreements included having the RDA sell the corner parcel to them at
   essentially the same price that was paid for it.

   The city created a new SSD Zone for the property and agreed to some off-site and other
   improvements. The city has been working with various partners to secure funding and
   arrange to do the needed work for the project.

   The RDA will be able to recoup most of the purchase price and pay off the bonds with the
   payments over the next five years from the developer. Thackeray has been a fair and
   cooperative partner in every respect. There is no interest being charged to them with this
   arrangement because the larger plan is to spur redevelopment in this section of the city, not
   to make a profit on the land purchase. We have deliberately kept it simple to facilitate this
   goal.

   It is important not to look at this sale in a vacuum. It is part of a deal that will bring a half­
   million-dollar development to the city. Wayne Harper will be updating the City Council in the
   future on this project.

   Board Chair Barbieri asked if the building will be razed; Mr. Cowdell said that it will be.
   Board Member Burgess asked if there is any concern about not charging interest to the
   developer for this payment. He was assured that the amount is minimal when considering
   the overall benefit of this project.

   Mr. Cowdell encouraged the Board to adopt Resolution No. RDA 22-02.

   9:29 PM - Board Member Knudsen MOVED to adopt Resolution No. RDA 22-02. Board
   Member Burgess SECONDED the motion. Chair Barbieri called for a roll call vote, The vote
   was as follows: Harker-yes, Cochran-yes, Burgess-yes, Barbieri, and Knudsen-yes. All
   Board Members present voted in favor and the motion passed unanimously.
