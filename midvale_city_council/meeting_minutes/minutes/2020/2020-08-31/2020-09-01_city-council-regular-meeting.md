A. CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2020-0-15 APPROVING PROPOSED
TEXT AMENDMENT TO MODIFY LIGHTING AND - SIGNAGE
STANDARDS IN THE JORDAN BLUFFS SUBAREA 4 ZONE
Alex Murphy said in 2017, the City adopted a zoning ordinance for Subarea 4 of the
Jordan Bluffs zone. This ordinance established goals for the district, review process
requirements, and standards for residential development for the ~40 acres in the subarea.
Wasatch Residential Group owns all the private property in the district and is approaching
completion of multifamily residential projects on two of the three parcels available.
As these projects have gone through review and construction, Wasatch has identified
specific lighting and signage requirements that they feel interfere with their ability to
provide high-quality developments and, as a result, desires to see these standards
modified. Pursuant to the text amendment process described in §17-3-1 of the Midvale
City Municipal Code, Staff and Wasatch have been discussing the required changes and
presented a draft text amendment to the Planning Commission for recommendation. In
the meeting on August 12, 2020, the Planning Commission recommended the Council
adopt the proposed changes.
This text amendment proposes changes to the following sections:
17-7-10.11.4 Residential development standards.
This section includes changes to the lighting standards in subsection F. Specifically,
subsection (F)(1) has been modified to more clearly delineate the separation between
requirements and (F)(6) has been added to grant exceptions to specific lighting
requirements for uplighting, streetlights, and string/bistro lighting. As proposed:
e Uplighting would be allowed up to a defined limit of lumens, based on property
area, provided no light trespass occurs across property lines.
e Streetlights would be exempted from the 100,000 lumen/acre limit.
e String or bistro lighting would be exempted from the cutoff, down direction, and
shielding requirements. l

17-7-10.11.9 Signage.
The proposed changes to this section would allow temporary signage. Temporary
signage was not originally approved when this zone was adopted. As proposed:

e Banner signs up to 80 SF would be permitted on building walls and fences.
Signage on temporary construction fencing would be exempt from this size
limitation.

e Other temporary signs (balloons/inflated displays, flags, feathers, etc.) would be
permitted for up to 30 days per quarter. A temporary sign permit would be required
for each display period. Other zones allow similar signage for up to 14 days per
quarter; this proposal would be the longest period allowed and, if approved, would
likely be expanded across all other zones too.

e Signs for grand opening periods are also proposed. Other zones allow grand
opening signage for up to 30 days in the first 90 days after opening. The proposed
language would extend that period, starting with the first Certificate of Occupancy
for a project and ending up to 18 months or 30 days after the final Certificate of
Occupancy for a project, which ever comes later.

the process:

park. A water line and storm drain line may also run through the area requested to

road. If removal of the trees is necessary, the developer will be expected to
complete their removal at his own expense.

4. The adjacent park’s irrigation system may run through the area to be vacated. If it
does, the developer will be expected to reroute and reconnect the system at his
own expense.

C. CONSIDER RESOLUTION NO. 2020-R-39 WAIVING THE REQUIRED

NOTICE AND DECLINIG TO PROVIDE SERVICE TO HOLMES HOMES
AND GARDNER PROPERTIES ON JORDAN BLUFFS TO ALLOW
SANDY SUBURBAN IMPROVEMENT DISTRICT TO ANNEX THE
PROPERTIES INTO THEIR DISTRICT

Lisa Garner said Holmes Jordan Bluffs, LC and Gardner Jordan Bluffs, LC own properties

that are being developed into the Jordan Bluffs Condo Project. Approximately two-thirds

of the project area is located within the Sandy Suburban Improvement District (SSID).

The remaining third of project area is within Midvale’s wastewater service area.

In order for the City to provide wastewater service to the northern third of the project,

Holmes and Gardner would be required to install an expensive lift station, and the City

would be required to assume ownership and provide continuing maintenance. This would

be expensive for all parties. However, if the entire project was served by SSID, the

existing grade would allow Holmes and Gardner to develop the project without

constructing expensive wastewater infrastructure. The City would also be able to avoid

the significant costs of providing ongoing maintenance for a lift station. This would

represent a significant cost savings for all the parties involved.

State law does not allow Midvale and SSID to simply adjust the service boundary lines.

Instead SSID is required to annex the service area within Midvale. This annexation can

be initiated by petition of the property owners. If the property owners petition SSID to

initiate the annexation, Midvale can assist in streamlining the process by waiving statute-

required notice and declining to provide wastewater service to the affected properties.

This would eliminate up to 40 days from the annexation process.

Because the annexation and service of the Jordan Bluffs Condo Project by SSID is

mutually beneficial for the City, Holmes, and Gardner, Staff is requesting the Council

suspend its rules and pass the attached resolution to streamline the annexation process.

This will allow Holmes and Gardner to proceed with the annexation process as quickly as

SSID will allow.

FISCAL IMPACT:

None

Staff recommended the City Council approve Resolution 2020-R039 waiving notice and

declining to provide service to the listed properties in the Jordan Bluffs Condo Project.

This resolution will streamline the Sandy Suburban Improvement District annexation

process for Holmes and Gardner.

Matt Dahl said Salt Lake County receives annual funding allocations from the U.S.
Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). The HUD funding is passed
through to cities within the county, including Midvale, through a variety of programs and
processes. Salt Lake County has been working on the renewal of their Urban County
certification, which is required in order to receive their annual allocation of funding from
HUD. Salt Lake County was notified on August 19th by HUD that contractual issues were
identified that must be addressed by September 18th in order to receive the Urban County
certification. HUD provided guidance regarding the language that needs to be modified
in the current interlocal agreements between Salt Lake County and its partner
communities, which must be updated before HUD will certify that the agreements meet
the requirements in section V of the Notice for Urban County Qualification for FY 2021-
2023.

The Salt Lake County District Attorney’s Office has prepared a new interlocal agreement
that combines the three current interlocal agreements between the County and Midvale.
The new agreement will address issues related to participation in the Community
Development Block Grant, Emergency Solutions Grant, and HOME programs. Staff will
be prepared to discuss the proposed changes to the interlocal agreements during the
meeting. If the City Council finds the proposed changes acceptable, the final version of

| the combined interlocal agreement will considered for approval on September 15th.
