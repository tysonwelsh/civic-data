URA is partnering with cities and communities in this effort. There is a lot of material that is

were five events held. There have been five events in 2025 already and the average is between
125 and 150 vehicles. Statistically, the number of events and the number of vehicles has increased.

        b)      Central Wasatch Commission – Lindsey Nielsen, Executive Director.
Mayor Dahle reported that there will be a presentation from the Central Wasatch Commission
(“CWC”). Executive Director, Lindsey Nielsen, introduced herself to the City Council. She
explained that the CWC has invited Holladay to become a member of the CWC. There are eight
other jurisdictions currently participating in the organization. The intention of the CWC is to
collectively address the issues facing the Central Wasatch mountains. Ms. Nielsen has been with
the CWC for seven years.

The CWC was created in 2017 by an Interlocal Agreement. It is an Interlocal Governmental Entity,
which means it is composed of other governments. The CWC came out of the Mountain Accord
process, which was a collaborative response to the fact that decisions impacting specific systems
in the mountains were happening in a piecemeal manner. Decisions happening in one system were
having impacts on all of the other systems. The four systems that the CWC uses as a guidepost
are: the economy system (the Cottonwood Canyons ski resorts and small businesses),
transportation system (how people get to the mountains and how people travel around the
mountains), recreation system (the recreational opportunities in the mountains), and the
environment system (the land, water, and wildlife). The goal of the Mountain Accord was to work
collaboratively with representatives from each of the four systems to sustain the mountains for
years to come. The Mountain Accord process lasted for approximately three years and it resulted
in a charter that was signed by over 100 stakeholders. The charter called for many things, including
the creation of a government entity to carry out the plans that were devised by the experts during
the Mountain Accord process. That entity is the CWC.

The CWC involves locally elected officials operating by consensus. The intention is to work
together in pursuit of one unified goal, which is the stewardship of the four systems in the Central
Wasatch. The organization facilitates conflict resolution, convenes, and considers ideas about
what to do in the mountains. Ms. Nielsen review who is involved in the CWC. She reiterated that
the CWC is a consensus-driven organization. Land and watershed protection decision-making is
approached through consensus. Ms. Nielsen explained that all CWC Board Members have equal
footing and an equal voice. She next reviewed some of the projects the CWC has been involved
with over the years, including the Short-Term Projects Grant Program, Central Wasatch
Symposium, and Central Wasatch Dashboard. There is also stakeholder and youth involvement
in the organization through the Stakeholders Council and the CWC Youth Council.

Each of the member jurisdictions, including the Ex-Officio Members, contribute to the CWC. The
organization is member jurisdiction funded. There are also yearly appropriations from the State
of Utah. The member fees have been broken down into three tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3. The
larger tier is Tier 3, the medium tier is Tier 2, and the smaller tier is Tier 1. What determines the
tier level is population size, tax revenue, and geographic location. Ms. Nielsen offered to answer
questions.

VII. Presentation of the 2025-2026 Tentative Budgets – Gina Chamness.
Ms. Chamness reported that during the first regularly scheduled meeting in May, the City Council
must be presented with and approve a Tentative Budget. Over the past several years, a plan has
been implemented for financial sustainability and infrastructure improvements in the City. The
bond that was issued three years ago allowed the City to make significant improvements to
neighborhood streets and stormwater infrastructure. Throughout the summer, many of those
stormwater infrastructure projects will be completed

Ms. Chamness noted that the budget was done with the priorities of the Council in mind and also
prioritizes employees. There is a cost of living adjustment (“COLA”) for Staff as well as some
targeted market increases to make sure that Holladay attracts and retains high-quality Staff to best
serve residents and meet the outlined goals. Ms. Chamness reported that sales tax is approximately
a third of the overall General Fund revenue. Sales tax has stagnated, so there is a flat sales tax
projected in the budget. The budget does not propose a property tax increase. The City Council's
decision last year to implement an inflationary property tax increase was referenced. Since there
is not anticipated to be much in the way of sales tax growth, the Tentative Budget does not include
new positions or significant new programming. However, it does analyze the cost of the two part-
time positions that were approved in a budget amendment approximately one month ago.

The component of growth in this budget reflects inflationary increases among some programming
and partner agencies. Ms. Chamness reported that the largest is from the Unified Police
Department (“UPD”). There will be an opportunity to further review those changes over the next
several weeks. She looks forward to having continued conversations with the City Council prior
to Final Budget adoption. There is a schedule that breaks up the budget discussions by department.
Next week, the Council will discuss the following: Capital Projects, Parks, Public Services, and
the Stormwater Fund.
