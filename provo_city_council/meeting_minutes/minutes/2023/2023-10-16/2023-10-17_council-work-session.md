9.      A discussion regarding the redistricting process. (23-073) (03:00:49)
        Michael Sanders, Policy Analyst, Presented.

        Mr. Sanders says that this item comes from a history request by Councilor Travis Hoban
        and Councilor Shannon Ellsworth. Several councilors expressed dissatisfaction for the
        process last year. This presentation aims to go over state law for redistricting processes,
        examples from other cities, and recommendations from staff.
        The presentation will focus on redistricting basics, best practices, processes, and what to
        do for 2032 redistricting.
        Redistricting Basics:
                     This is governed by the state code. The State requires that each district
                    needs to be equal population. The council also needs to set up the school
                    board. This process needs to happen every ten years. The Council needs to
                    have an accurate map sent out within 10 days. The county is responsible for
                    creating the voting precincts. The city adds the actual population to the
                    precincts and then the city makes the council district maps.
                     Councilor Rachel Whipple asked if the county does not know what the
                    population is as they are creating the precincts.
                     Mr. Sanders said the county does know the population.
        Best Practices:
                     There needs to be a substantially equal population and competitive
                    elections. Contiguity, compactness, and communities of interest are also
                    important and helpful for the redistricting process. There also needs to be
                    incumbent protection. These practices are left to the city to use and establish
                    the districts.
                     During staff research, staff found useful resources. This included the U.S.
                    election assistance commission and that focused more on transparency. The
                    public is allowed to weigh in. The National League of Cities also wants
                    transparency and wants to avoid gerrymandering. They advocate for defining
                    the process through a resolution of the council.

        Process:
                        The process for 2012 and 2022 were very similar in that they had public
                     outreach on social media and blogs to encourage citizen involvement. Each
                     year, the GIS department created an online mapping tool that citizens could
                     use to create district maps.

        Examples:

                     maps.
                      West Jordan had the city recorder work with the legal department and the
