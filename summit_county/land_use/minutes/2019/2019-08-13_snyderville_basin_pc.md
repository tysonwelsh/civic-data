1. Discussion regarding amendment to Section 11-4 of the Snyderville Basin 
Development Code for Accessory Building- Ray Milliner, Principal Planner  
 
Planner Milliner reminded the Commission that the subcommittee has been discussing 
accessory buildings.  Commissioners Dickey, Fine, and Cooke are members of the 
subcommittee.  The group decided they want to engage in a discussion with the entire 
Planning Commission.   
 
Planner Milliner said the subcommittee came up with language that is quite restrictive.  
He noted the language is only a starting point for discussion.  It divides accessory 
structures into three categories.  There will be a major and a minor category.  The third 
category is shipping containers.   
 
Planner Milliner explained the reason to create a major and minor category.  Over the 
years, the idea of lot coverage has been discussed.  Some of the problems they 
encountered had to do with the size of the lot.  The language severely limits a one-acre 
lot.  A larger parcel will be allowed more coverage.   
 
Planner Milliner outlined the difference between the minor and major accessory 
buildings.  A minor structure will give the property owner the ability to build a garage 
or storage shed, such as Tuff Shed.  It could not be as tall as the home.  Minor accessory 
buildings would be allowed anywhere within the Snyderville Basin.      
 
A major accessory building will be permitted as a Conditional Use.  Lots will need to be 
larger than one acre.  There will be a 26-foot height limit.  The reason they included 
shipping containers is due to the public’s interest.  They would only be allowed in 
commercial zones.  They could not be stacked.  
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
August 13, 2019 
Page 18 of 26 
 
Staff requests feedback from the Commission on the language.   Where are the good 
ideas?  Where should changes take place?  Planner Milliner said there are members of 
the public who would like to speak if the Commission Chair feels it is appropriate.

this is a problem that requires attention.  The majority of the subcommittee believes

the problem the subcommittee faced was to come up with a one-size-fits-all solution.
