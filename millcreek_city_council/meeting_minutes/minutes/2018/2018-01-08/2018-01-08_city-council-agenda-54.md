2. Discussion on Impact Fees
Mayor Silvestrini introduced the topic by saying that Millcreek has had some recent
development applications filed, and a lot of cities charge impact fees to new development to

The issue came up when considering offsetting costs to purchase park space in the Meadowbrook
area, but they can be used for other services throughout the City. He said the impact fees need to
be figured through a study.

John Brems said in order to have an impact fee, the City needs to have an impact fee facility plan
study and impact fee analysis. He said that the fees collected can only be used for new
development, not maintenance or repair. He explained the appropriate use of impact fees and
feels the only reasonable one for the City to look into at this point is for parks. Council Member
Jackson asked about areas of the City where multi-family housing is going in, if the system can

the money will be used for. Francis Lilly foresees 1,000 new housing units developed over the

thinks if the City was able to plan for a town center, that the City would then be able to use the

surrounding cities except Murray; it tends to be the rule not the exception. Stephens believes
impact fees are needed for parks and roads.

Mayor Silvestrini said he thinks there needs to be more internal discussion among staff and then

and road impact fees make sense right now. Council Member Catten asked how long it takes for
a study to be done. Brems said 6 months to a year, then perpetual re-dos, but the initial overview

4.2 Discussion and Consideration of RES 18-02, Authorizing the Issuance and
Sale of Not More Than $5,100,000 Tax and Revenue Anticipation Notes,
Series 2018, of Millcreek City, Utah and Related Matters

Mayor Silvestrini said that Millcreek made the determination to leave the Salt
Lake Valley Law Enforcement Services Area, SLVLESA. The entity levied a
property tax to pay for police services and Millcreek will be levying a similar
property tax to pay for police services. The City has to borrow money, called a tax
revenue anticipation loan, to pay for services until the property taxes are collected
at the end of the year. The taxation process doesn’t begin until later this year with
a truth in taxation hearing. The City solicited proposals from multiple banks and
received 2. The Mayor said he looked for the best interest rates. He said the
interest rates were more variable and higher if the City received the money in
monthly installments, so it will be received in a lump sum with a fixed interest
rate. Jason Burningham, with Lewis Young Robertson & Burningham Inc.,
explained that the City only receiving two proposals was due to the complexities
of calendar year vs. fiscal year. The City’s financial advisor recommended to the
Council to accept the full draw option from Zion’s Bank at a 2.5% fixed interest
rate. He stated that State law requires the Resolution to be considered by the
Council prior to issuing the notes.

Mayor Silvestrini clarified that he wanted the Resolution to authorize a higher
amount than what the City will be paid, for contingencies and fees. Burningham
also added that Zion’s Bank has committed to doing the second part of the loan.
The Mayor said the reason there are two parts to the loan is that there is a
constitutional requirement to pay off all debts by the end of the fiscal year, so this
enables the City to do that by borrowing again the next fiscal year. He said this
solution was approved by legal counsel, the bank and its council, and by the
auditors. The first part pays for the first 6 months of police services, and the
subsequent note pays off the first note and the remaining six months for the year.
The Mayor added that when the property taxes are collected, the City will then
pay the entire loan off. He will be asking the public to pay a little more to be able
to get out of debt sooner later this year when approving the property tax amount.

Council Member Marchant moved to approve the issuance of the tax anticipation notes
through Zions Bank at 2.5% with a $5.1 million limit through June 30". Council Member
Jackson seconded. Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.

Mayor Silvestrini said the proposed amendment has been considered for months. He
said staff brought the ordinance back after making changes as directed by the

Francis Lilly shared that the Council and Planning Commission have seen this item
multiple times since August. He said the current zone code was adopted around 40-50
years ago by the County, which the City took on as it’s code. In an attempt to address
unresolved issues, city staff made an effort to draft a new zone to modernize
definitions and bring it up to community expectations. The new language would
amend the existing zone designations. Some of the changes to the code were: whether
recreational vehicle campgrounds should be removed, impose new design standards
on new construction, record a note on the plat that there are manufacturing uses in the
area, and change language with nonconforming uses which came from concerns
expressed by residents. Lilly went through the redlined amended language of the
ordinance in the staff report, particularly with non-conforming uses. He said that the
staff recommendation, in light of all discussion with property owners and the
Planning Commission, is that the alternate draft in the staff report should be adopted
by the Council.

Clinton Jensen and Vincent Rampton, both dated January 8, 2018. The Mayor said he

5.3 Discussion and Consideration of ORD 18-05, ZM-17-006 Rezone from R-1-8
to R-1-5 at 3350 S. 1110 E.

Ashley Cleveland introduced the rezone. There used to be a vacant single-family
dwelling on the property, but it was demolished in early 2017, which made the lot
non-conforming. The rezone would make the lot conforming to be able to then build a
single-family dwelling. She went over the differences between the two zones, which
involves lot widths, depths, and setbacks. Cleveland said when she explained what
was happening to the neighbors, they were pleased with the proposal. The community
council and Planning Commission recommended approval, as does staff.

Council Member Marchant moved to approve Ordinance 18-05 to rezone from R-1-8 to
R-1-5. Council Member Jackson seconded. Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. The
motion passed unanimously.

5.4 Discussion and Consideration of ORD 18-06, ZM-17-007 Rezone from FR-5
to FR-10 at 3311 S. Teton Dr.

Lilly said the property is 25.2 acres, which extends along Grandeur Peak. He said that
the Council had requested the rezone as part of a strategy to preserve the 25 acres as
open space in perpetuity. The City has an open space conservation easement on it.
The General Plan does place a great emphasis on preserving and expanding open
space in the community. The community council and Planning Commission
recommended approval.
