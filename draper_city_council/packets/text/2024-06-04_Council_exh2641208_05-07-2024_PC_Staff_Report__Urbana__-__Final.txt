SUMMARY AND BACKGROUND
This application is a request for approval of a Land Use Map Amendment, Zoning Map
Amendment and Development Agreement for approximately 3.82 acres located on the
west side of Lone Peak Parkway, at approximately 285 W. River Chapel Road (Exhibits C and
D). The property is currently zoned A2. The applicant is requesting that the applications be
approved to allow for the development of the currently vacant site with 30 single-family
lots.

The property has historically been used for agricultural, but has been vacant since the mid-
2000’s. In 2021, an application was submitted to rezone the property to RM2 for the
development of residential townhomes. That application was denied by the City Council. A
separate application was submitted in early 2023 for a different residential townhome
project, but that application was withdrawn before there was any action on the request.

Development Agreement. The applicant has proposed a Development Agreement (Exhibit I).

1.      Map Amendments:

       Other than noted below, we are not aware of any inadequacies of the facilities
       intended to serve this property.
       a.     The increase in density from one residential unit per two acres agricultural
              zoning to up to twelve residential units per acre on a total of 3.8 acres
              constitutes a sizeable increase in the amount of traffic to and from the
              subject area based on the subject parcel. One single family residential
              structure and associated agricultural uses constitutes up to nine daily trips
              and two peak hour trips, based on industry standard vehicle trip tables. With
              the acreage of this rezone, the increase in daily trips would be approximately
              for a maximum of 45 residential units consisting of a maximum of 25 peak
              hour trips and 300 daily trips. This represents a significant increase on the
              adjacent public local street in traffic but is a minor increase for Lone Peak
              Parkway, an adjacent minor arterial with a high capacity for traffic. The
              current level of service for Lone Peak Parkway is degraded by delays and
              backups associated with existing uses in the area. There are stacking delays
              that occasionally block access from nearby local streets onto Lone Peak
              Parkway. These delays are especially significant during beginning and ending
              school times.
       b.     Connectivity with the subject parcel to a public right-of-way is on River
              Chapel Rd, a local street. The closest classified street is Lone Peak Parkway, a
              minor arterial which is adjacent to the site. Access locations will be required
              to meet Draper City Standards as outlined in our Master Transportation Plan.
              No connection would be permitted onto Lone Peak Parkway as the fronting
              parcel length would not meet the Draper City Access Management spacing
              requirements.
       c.      There are existing storm drain facilities on River Chapel Road. Provisions for

               need to be addressed with any subdivision or site plan application, and shall

APPLICANT’S RESPONSE – LAND USE MAP AMENDMENT
                               Urbana Land Use Map Amendment

                             285 W. River Chapel Road, Draper Utah

                                             1-29-24



1. The current Land Use designation is Community Commercial. I am requesting the land use be
   changed to High Density Residential (8-12 du/ac).
2. The property is in a transitional area with single family residential to the south and
   commercial/car dealers to the north and east. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints
   has a chapel directly to the west. The current zone of A-2 is not appropriate for the developing
   area. Commercial use in this area will have a negative impact on the single-family homes to the
   south and the chapel to the west. Commercial will also contribute to the difficult traffic situation
   on Loan Peak Parkway. Residential single family has the lowest impact of any realistic use and is
   more harmonious with the single family and LDS chapel on the same block.
3. Small lot single family homes are a great transitional use buffering the larger single family lots to
   the south and the LDS chapel to the west from commercial and future commercial to the north
   and east.
4. This Land Use Map Amendment will help Draper City satisfy their requirement to comply with
   recently adopted legislation regarding Moderate Income Housing as shown in chapter 4 of the
   General Plan. As part of this plan Draper City has identified several strategies to satisfy the law.
   This proposed zone change satisfies several of these strategies.
        a. Strategy (F) reads “Zone or rezone for higher density or moderate-income residential
            development in commercial or mixed-use near major transit investment corridors,
            commercial centers, or employment centers”.
        b. Strategy (J) “Implement zoning incentives for moderate income units in new
            developments”.
        c. Strategy (W) “Create or allow for, and reduce regulations related to, multifamily
            residential dwellings compatible in scale and form with detached single-family
            residential dwellings and located in walkable communities within residential or
            mixed- use zones”.
   The property is located within a quarter mile of the 11400 South I-15 interchange. There are two
   bus stops on Loan Peak Parkway that adjoin our property, both are major transit corridors that
   will allow our residents to easily access public and private modes of transportation. The
   neighboring mixed use commercial properties to the north are major employment centers. The
   Walmart Supercenter and Sam’s Club are within walking distance of the property and are the
   largest commercial shopping centers in all of Draper. The neighboring mixed use commercial area
   and the major transportation corridors perfectly satisfy the strategies that the general plan has
   identified as ways to stratify the City’s moderate housing needs.
