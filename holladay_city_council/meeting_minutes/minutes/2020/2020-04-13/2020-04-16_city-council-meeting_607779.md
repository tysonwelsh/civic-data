III.   Public Comments.

large part to members of the community. Council Member Gibbons thanked Chief Hoyal and the
Police Department for their efforts. Council Member Quinn acknowledged Holladay citizens for
recognizing the importance of social distancing.

V.      Public Hearing on Proposed Amendments to Title 17 – Storm Drainage.
Mayor Dahle addressed Chapter 17.20 of the Code. Jared Bunch, City Engineer reported that the
proposal consists primarily of criteria to create a Storm Water Management Plan regarding
hydraulics. He explained that there is a difference between a plan and a program. The program
addresses quality while the plan addresses design criteria in more detail. Stormwater management
deals with various stormwater events and provides criteria to address events, topography, and storm
drain management. The Stormwater Management Program deals with education for stormwater
quality, storage, hazardous pollutants, and enforcement of stormwater facility maintenance.

Ms. Chamness asked if Chapter 17.20 addresses the requirements for the development of
stormwater facilities. She stated that the requirements the City is establishing will pertain to the
entire City. Council Member Fotheringham raised a concern about pool water draining and wanted
to ensure that the City is addressing all potential issues. Mr. Godfrey reported that Chapter
17.08.035 was added to address those issues. It was noted that chlorinated pool water dechlorinates
if allowed to sit for a period of time. Mr. Bunch explained that the fines were increased to $500 per
day from $50 per day. Mr. Godfrey stated that if municipalities do not address this issue somewhat
aggressively, the State may take action against the municipality.

Mayor Dahle opened the public hearing. There were no comments submitted electronically. The
public hearing was closed.

IX.     Consideration of Resolution 2020-17 Extending the City’s Emergency Proclamation.
Ms. Chamness reported that City ordinance limits emergency proclamations to 30 days. Action
beyond 30 days needs to be approved by the legislative body. Staff recommended extending the
declaration through the end of the fiscal year. The declaration allows the City access to federal
funds should they be needed. The declaration does not impact residents or City Hall. Mr. Godfrey
confirmed that the impact of the declaration allows the City to be eligible for federal funding and
also lengthens the term of business licensing.

Council Member Gibbons moved to approve Resolution 2020-17. Council Member Durham
seconded the motion. The Council roll call vote was as follows: Council Members Petersen,
Fotheringham, Quinn, Durham, Gibbons and Mayor Dahle in favor. Resolution 2020-17 was
approved by a unanimous vote.

XI.     City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness reported that the City processed three building permits electronically and the first
successful credit card payment this last week. She thanked Holly Smith, Jonathan Teerlink for their
assistance.

Ms. Chamness reported that the City also closed on the bond matter and ended up with a $242,000
savings.

The City also qualified for a rebate for electric vehicle chargers that will be installed in the Village
in the next few months.

Ms. Chamness met with Salt Lake County, the Municipal Services District (“MSD”) and the cities
of Millcreek and Taylorsville to explore changes to the County Public Works Contract that will
result in little to no cost increases over the next year. The MSD is funded entirely by sales tax and
will be impacted next year. Ms. Chamness was hopeful that the City will receive $50,000 to
$100,000 next year. The City will postpone approximately $170,000 in slurry services next year
because the work is contracted and will not impact current staffing. Vacant positions will also not
be filled. The City will continue to provide the same service level for snow plowing but eliminate
night plowing vacancies. Instead, they will share night snowplow drivers with neighboring
jurisdictions.

Ms. Chamness mentioned that overhead and expenses in the Mayor and City Council budgets were
addressed by the County and thought to not being suitable for jurisdictions. It was suggested that
they come out the County General Fund. The County agreed to eliminate nearly $200,000 in
expenses, which they will pay for from a different source. She was still working on a final product
and will continue communicating with Council going forward.

       a.     Financial Report.
Ms. Chamness reported that through March, the City is in a good position with sales tax projections
and is approximately 5% ahead. Property taxes were also doing well and only slightly less than
budgeted. This was likely reflected as a result of individuals being late paying their property taxes.

A few areas in the General Fund expenses that need to be adjusted were for the City Attorney and
City Engineering services. Ms. Chamness stated that she will likely recommend budget increases in
these areas that can be covered by savings in other areas. The City was in a good position moving
into the next quarter. She also identified the increment numbers for Millrock and Holladay Village,
which are tremendous and a reflection of how the areas have changed for the positive.

Mayor Dahle asked if there was value in looking at capital expenses for the fiscal year and
determining if any expenses could be put off this year. Ms. Chamness stated that that would be
difficult. In terms of proposed improvements, she did not think it would be easy. Mayor Dahle
remarked that since the City is moving into a strong fourth quarter, perhaps some things could be
adjusted to prepare for next year.

XII. Council Reports and District Issues.
Council Member Fotheringham reported that residents of Tobermory are concerned about their
ability to participate in the public process and Planning Commission public hearings for the
Kruckenberg development. They asked that a decision be postponed until a legitimate public
hearing can take place. He remarked that this may create an undue burden on the developer and
questioned whether there was a way to adequately address the concern. Ms. Chamness explained
that there has been a great deal of discussion about this. One competing interest that has not been
relieved by the pandemic is the need to deal with applications expeditiously. The City is currently
exploring ways for people to comment in real-time while moving the process forward. Waiting
until a public meeting can occur may take too long. Council Member Fotheringham supported that
idea of the City providing more than one opportunity to receive public comment. He stressed the
importance of being fair to both parties.
