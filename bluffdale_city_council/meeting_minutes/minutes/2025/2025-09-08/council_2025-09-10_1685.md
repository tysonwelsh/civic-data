7.2 Discussion - C-PACE (Commercial Property Assessed Clean Energy) Financing

Tool for Renewable Energy Projects. (Presenter, Theddi Chappell, Director, Utah

C-PACE Program).
Utah Commercial Property Assessed Clean Energy Financing (““C-PACE”) Director, Theddi Chappell
presented information on the program. C-PACE financing was enabled as part of Utah State statute
to promote energy efficiency and water conservation. A property owner can request that a voluntary
special assessment be placed on their property. This is the equivalent of a water or sewer assessment
and has no relationship to taxes or taxing authorities in Utah. The program applies to both existing
buildings and new construction and allows for a three-year retroactive financing period.
Benefits of the program include:

e It is performance-based with a focus on increased energy efficiency and water conservation
measures. The loan is underwritten based on building performance, not the owner’s credit.
| e It offers long-term, fixed-rate, non-recourse financing for up to 30 years.
e The ultimate goal of the program is to generate energy savings that offset or exceed energy
expenses. ;
e The program can be combined with utility, tax, and economic development incentives.
e No personal guarantees are required as the financing is based solely on building performance.
e More than 30 third-party capital providers ensure competitive lending options.
In response to a question raised by Mayor Hall, Ms. Chappell confirmed that C-PACE handles details
like verifying that construction meets energy efficiency and water conservation standards. The
purpose of the program is to take that burden off of the City because the State understands that
municipalities may not have the resources and specific engineering knowledge is required to ensure
that construction meets requirements.
The C-PACE program benefits:
¢ Owners and developers by lowering their overall weighted cost of capital.
e Cities and counties by promoting higher-quality development and attracting a wider variety
of developers and property sectors.
¢ Contractors by facilitating upgrades that are often value-engineered out and supports use of
high-efficiency systems.
e Lenders by future-proofing the investments, as energy efficiency and water conservation
measures positively impact property values.
Ms. Chappell stated that all types of commercial property are eligible for the C-PACE program,
including multi-family housing of more than five units that meets commercial code. Financing can
be used for energy efficiency and water conservation, renewable energy, electric vehicle charging,
and seismic upgrades. Includes soft costs such as energy audits, engineering and feasibility studies,
roof upgrades for solar installation, maintenance contracts, and the program financing fee.

Ms. Oakman compared the current coverage allowance per zone:
o R-1-43: 30%
o R-1-10: 40%
o R-1-43 CRO: 40%
Council Member Aston inquired about the original reason for the reduced maximum lot coverage in
the R-1-43 Zone. Community and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell indicated that the
maximum was 20% when he began working with the City in 2012 and had been increased to 30%
within the last few years. They receive requests for larger structures a few times per year, and the
property owner usually finds a solution. In this case, the Applicant decided to apply for the text
Amendment.
In response to a follow-up question from Mayor Hall, Ms. Oakman clarified that the applicant
proposed 35%, not 40%, but the City Council could approve a 40% maximum. Council Member Lord
stated that neighbors could complain about having a large structure next door, but 40% coverage is
already allowed in other zones. Council Member Wilding noted that the larger maximum could allow
more Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADU”). Mr. Kartchner added that larger buildings increase
property values, which increases property tax revenue.

and his mother, Claudia Anderson, was once the Mayor of Bluffdale. He wanted to build a new family
home, including a wellness center for the family. His daughter has Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia

3. Parks and Open Space:

e Existing facilities experience high usage, with almost 80% of residents using City
parks more than once per month, with most other residents using them a few times per
year.

e The most requested park improvements were more trees and shade structures, followed
by improved maintenance and cleanliness, which are all lower cost improvements.
Over 20% of residents indicated that no improvements were needed.

e The plan includes maps of existing and future parks, as well as a detailed inventory of
amenities.

e The current level of service (“LOS”) is 4.8 acres of park per 1,000 resident. This was
determined by dividing the 92 acres of existing park acreage (excluding the equestrian
center) by the 2024 population and multiplying the result by 1,000. Bluffdale has one
of the highest LOS levels among cities on the Wasatch Front.

e Park distribution is determined by type. Pocket parks with fewer amenities have a
smaller radius because residents usually walk to them. Community and regional parks
with more amenities have a one- to two-mile radius because residents are willing to
drive to them.

e City parks currently serve approximately 72% of residential households. The primary
reason residents visit a specific park is its proximity to their home. Including county-
owned and adjacent community parks increases the percentage to over 83%.

o Less than 70% of residents are within a 10-minute walk of a park, which
indicates the need for a stronger trail network.

e A realistic future LOS is 4.0, which will maintain the City’s standards and a high
service level in relation to other communities.
