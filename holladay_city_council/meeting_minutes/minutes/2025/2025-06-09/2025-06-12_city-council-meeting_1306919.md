        (Including General Fund, Capital Improvements, Debt Service, Stormwater, Grants and

Council Member Fotheringham noted that on Page 11, there is an important comment in the Policies
section, which states: “Maintaining appropriate funding for maintenance is of utmost importance. He

        b.     Discussion on Cottonwood Mall Urban Renewal Area ADL – Gina.
Ms. Chamness explained that the next item on the agenda is related to the Cottonwood Mall Urban
Renewal Project Area. This is the first in a series of presentations that came out of the Council Retreat
in February. At that time, a number of topics were identified and a request was made for additional
information. Ms. Chamness started by discussing Tax Increment Financing (“TIF”). It is a tool that
the Legislature has given municipalities to use to incentivize private development. These are called
project areas. The Urban Renewal Project Area type no longer exists, so the project areas are now
called Community Reinvestment Project Areas. That is the current law, but the concept is similar.

Once a project area is created, the RDA that created the project area is entitled to receive a portion or
all of the tax increment dollars that are generated in that area for a specified period of time. That is
usually between 15 and 20 years. The RDA can then use the tax increment dollars collected in the
project areas to incentivize development within the project area. That typically increases property
values and, in turn, increases the amount of revenues generated in the project area. After the
expiration of that tax increment collection period, the dollars that previously flowed to the RDA then
go back to the School District, County, or the City, which is what happened with the project area that
was closed a few years ago. That project area was generating approximately $1.1 million every year
for the RDA. At the end of that time, it returned to the City approximately $180,000 and a little less
than $700,000 to the School District. It was distributed regularly to the other agencies that
participated.

Redevelopment projects are commonly used for things like land purchases, installing infrastructure,
financial incentive agreements, and more. Generally, there should be a public purpose associated
with the development of that project area. .

Ms. Chamness shared a presentation slide that illustrates the boundaries for the Cottonwood Mall
Urban Renewal Project Area. At the time this project area was created, State Law required that the
RDA receive approval from the Taxing Entity Committee (“TEC”), which included all members who
were assessing taxes at that time. The law has since changed, but that is what was in place back then.

In order for the RDA to receive approval from the TEC, there were a few agreements that needed to
be made. TEC approved both a plan and a budget for the project area in 2008. Ms. Chamness noted
that the budget commits 75% of the property tax increment from all taxing entities that assess property
tax at the site to the RDA. TEC included several conditions, which included the following:

   •   Holladay commits 75% of its point of sale sales tax to project area expenses;
   •   When property and sales tax increment is equal to $122.5 million, no further contribution from
       any entity is required;
   •   100 units of affordable housing at 80% of AMI must be created in Holladay;
   •   The maximum contribution of property tax increment from all parties is $96.3 million; and
   •   The TEC is committed to a 20-year tax increment. Originally scheduled to begin between
       2011 and 2013, it was extended to 2017. The increment clock has already been “triggered”.

In 2021, the Legislature made a change that allowed the project area to be extended by two years
without approval of the TEC as a way to address the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. That is
what has happened, which means this project area has been extended through 2038. Ms. Chamness
next discussed and reviewed the terms of the Agreement for the Development of Land (“ADL”).
