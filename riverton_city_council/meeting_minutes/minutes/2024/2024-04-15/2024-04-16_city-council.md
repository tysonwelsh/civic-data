Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, began with a focus on the upcoming update
to the general plan, emphasizing the importance of the land use element. The Planning
Commission and City Council typically concentrate their efforts on this aspect of the plan. While
acknowledging the outdated text component of the general plan, it was noted that the priorities
list has served as a substitute. The primary consideration is the land use map, which projects
future land uses for different areas. The goal is to align the zoning map as closely as possible
with the general plan. Specific areas highlighted for future planning include the corner of 13400

South and 2700 West, the Butterfield property north of 12600 South, and portions along
Redwood Road and 12600 South towards Bangerter Highway. These areas are marked for
potential mixed-use development, reflecting uncertainty about their future use.

Mr. Lethbridge said that in addition to the land use element, the general plan encompasses
various components, including the transportation master plan and the moderate-income housing
element. These elements are reviewed every five years to ensure alignment with the City's
development goals. This year marks a significant review period for the general plan. A
consultant will assist in compiling statistical data, which is crucial for understanding current City
dynamics and projecting future growth and land use patterns. The update will also incorporate
water planning requirements mandated by state code.

Mr. Lethbridge said the text component of the general plan serves as a narrative outlining the
City's characteristics and overarching goals. It encapsulates broad objectives such as promoting
moderate-income housing and ensuring housing diversity. Specific areas of focus, such as the
western commercial district, may be highlighted, along with strategies to achieve citywide goals.
Priorities like trail development and traffic management feature prominently in discussions and
will be reflected in the text portion of the general plan update.

Mr. Lethbridge said the commercial designations in the general plan are categorized based on
intensity, similar to residential zoning classifications. Community commercial areas typically host
light retail and office spaces that integrate well with nearby residential zones. On the other hand,
regional commercial zones accommodate larger-scale developments, such as big-box stores,
and may include light manufacturing and industrial uses. The Suburban Land Reserve project
area, or Western Commercial District, is a unique zone highlighted in the general plan. When
considering commercial development, factors like building size, use types, and their impact on
surrounding properties are taken into account. The aim is to ensure that the chosen commercial
activities align with the character of the area and minimize adverse effects on neighboring
residential communities.

what is developed and what is not. Mr. Lethbridge will provide this to the Council.

Councilmember Pierucci said he had a strong desire to involve the public actively in updating
the general plan, seeking their input and ensuring community ownership of the plan. He said it
was crucial for the general plan to address the impending redevelopment and infill projects and
to outline policies that support successful redevelopment while maintaining the community's
character.

Mr. Lethbridge outlined the division of responsibilities between the City Council and the
Planning Commission regarding land use decisions. He clarified that the City Council makes
final decisions on general plan amendments, zoning changes, ordinance amendments, and
recommendations of plats. The Planning Commission serves as the land use authority for
residential subdivisions, commercial site plans, and conditional use permits, with the Council
retaining authority over decisions related to easements, rights of way, and financial obligations.
The Planning Commission also recommends action to the Council on general plan zoning
changes and ordinance amendments related to land use. He noted that while additional layers
of approval can be added through the conditional use permit process, the roles and
responsibilities outlined are unlikely to change at both the city and state levels.

Ms. Cluff expressed her perspective on conditional use permits for short-term rentals,

meaningful conditions. She highlighted a recent case where a potential rental was abandoned

          Surplus of Real Property Ordinance Discussion

Mr. Carter explained that the management of surplus property is outlined in City ordinance. He
said the Council had authorized by resolution the surplus designation of a property on the west
side of the City. Currently, the property is undergoing an updated appraisal before it will be
made available for sale to the public.

Mr. Carter highlighted the options available for selling surplus property, including auctions or
bidding processes administered by a contract administrator. The ordinance mandates that the
property must be sold for its appraised value. In a previous meeting, the question arose as to
whether the property should be listed on an MLS listing as an additional means of making it

that the City intends to sell it, along with providing contact information for inquiries.

          Ordinance No. 24-03 – Amending Riverton City Code Title 13, Chapters 5 and 10
          (Culinary and Secondary Water)

Mr. Carter said this ordinance centered around proposed changes to the utility code. The utility
billing department sought to modify regulations so that utility accounts would be established
solely in the name of property owners, rather than allowing them to be set up with renters. This
change aimed to streamline account management and ensure that any outstanding debts would
remain the responsibility of the property owner, even if tenants vacated the property without
paying. However, the proposed amendment was not adopted by the Council previously due to
the need for refinement in other sections of the ordinances related to the maintenance of fire
hydrants.

Mr. Carter said the issue regarding fire hydrant maintenance is nuanced and depends on the
property rights conveyed to the City through the subdivision process. If a fire hydrant is located
within a public right of way, it is the City's responsibility to maintain it. However, if the hydrant is
situated on private property or within a commercial subdivision that the City does not own or
have access to, then the responsibility falls on the property owner. The ordinance has been
amended to clarify these distinctions, providing a clearer understanding for stakeholders.

Councilmember McCay expressed agreement with the idea that utility accounts for rental
properties should be maintained in the owner's name to ensure accountability. However, she
raised concerns about the severity of the penalties outlined in Section 2 of the ordinance.
Specifically, she questioned the inclusion of imprisonment as a potential punishment for
violations, suggesting that it might be overly harsh. She believes that fines and water shutoffs
alone could address the issue without resorting to more severe penalties.

Mr. Carter emphasized that while most cases of utility billing account issues involve situations
where individuals fall behind on their bills, resulting in actions such as water shutoffs or sending
the account to collections, these instances typically do not lead to criminal charges. However,
there are rare occasions where individuals illegally tap into a neighbor's utility account to supply
water to their own property, constituting theft. In such cases, criminal charges may be pursued
to address the unauthorized use of utility services.

Councilmembers and staff continued discussion about whether to include the language in
Section 13.05.030, bullet number 2 which has language about giving false information on an
application and the punishment for that offense.

Councilmember McCay MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 24-03 –
Amending Riverton City Code Title 13, Chapters 5 and 10 (Culinary and Secondary
Water), excluding Section 13.05.030, bullet number 2.              Councilmember Pierucci
SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs
called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes, Haymond-no, McCay-yes,
McDougal-no, and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed with a 3 to 2 vote.

Michael Lucci, Founder of State Armor, a nonprofit policy organization focused on state policy
