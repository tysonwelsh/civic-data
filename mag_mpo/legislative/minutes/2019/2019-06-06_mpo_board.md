Federal Performance Measures Targets – Shawn Eliot, MAG The Fixing America’s Surface Transportation Act (FAST Act) is the latest federal transportation act that funds transportation and dictates transportation planning policy. A new element of the FAST act is performance measures. MAG and UDOT are required to report on specific performance measures and set targets for those measures. Safety is the first set of performance measures that the MPO must either set their own Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee June 6, 2019 Page 3 targets for the required performance measures, or agree to the statewide targets set by UDOT in consultation with their partners. MAG has already adopted the statewide targets for safety in 2018. It is proposed that MAG agree to the remaining statewide performance targets. Those measures and targets are found at: www.fhwa.dot.gov/tpm/reporting/state/state.cfm?state=Utah dashboard.udot.utah.gov/ MAG staff recommends that the Regional Planning Committee accept the remaining statewide targets for the required federal performance measures. Mayor Mann moved that the MPO Regional Planning Committee accept the remaining statewide targets for the required federal performance measures. Mayor Frost seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. TIP Modification Pony Express Pkwy Contingency Request – Shawn Eliot, MAG Eagle Mountain was awarded $9,801,350 of MAG exchange funds in 2016 to widen Pony Express Pkwy to five lanes. The project needs additional $1.3M for sewer trench repair and drainage improvements. Eagle Mountain is requesting contingency funds equaling $980,135. Unified Planning Work Program (UPWP) Approval – Shawn Seager, MAG The July 1, 2019 to June 30, 2020 (FY20) Unified Planning Work Program (UPWP) describes the transportation planning work undertaken by Mountainland MPO staff, UDOT staff and UTA staff within the Mountainland MPO planning area. The Work Program is an annual budget that illustrates to the members, Federal Highway Administration, Federal Transit Administration, and the general public the planned work, budget, and funding sources. This FY20 UPWP $2,289,982 budget is 4% less when compared to last year’s $2,388,155 budget with a $98,173 decrease from FY19. This is due to a decrease staff charging into this program and decrease in rollover studies. This budget proposes to spend $252k on new special studies including: 1. Joint Planning Studies with WFRC, UDOT, and UTA 2. Travel Demand Modeling 3. Planning Opportunities Contingency 4. New Exchange Project Management Consultant Contract Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee June 6, 2019 Page 4 In addition, it proposes to spend $20k on roll over studies and contracts from year previous. Mayor Hunsaker moved to approve the FY20 MAG Executive Summary of the Unified Planning and Work Program as presented. Mayor Larson seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Regional Transportation Plan (RTP) Draft Approval – Shawn Eliot, MAG Every four years, MAG is required to prepare and adopt a Regional Transportation Plan. This plan iteration it is called TransPlan50. MAG staff have worked collaboratively with the municipal, UDOT and UTA staff to develop a land use scenario, project lists, and funding estimates. The draft TransPlan50 document and accompanying appendices show where we are today, what we plan for the future, and how do we afford the investment. The plan meets all federal requirements, including being fiscally constrained and meeting air quality conformity. There are three phases in the plan: Phase 1 2019-2030, Phase 2 2031-2040, Phase 3 2041-2050. Before this meeting, both MPO TAC and MPO RPC have reviewed the list of projects and costs and approved them to be in the plan. Detailed information found at https://mountainland.org/transplan50-document Concerns were raised that autonomous vehicles have not been factored into the plan. Shawn Eliot stated that there is not enough information available at this time. MAG staff recommends the approval of the draft documents to be released for a 30-day public comment period and since MPO Regional Planning Committee is not meeting until August, baring no significant comments. Mayor Larson motioned that MPO RPC approve of the draft TransPlan50 document and related appendices to release to a 30-day public comment period as required by the MAG Public Participation Program and also recommend that, barring no significant comments received during the 30-day public comment period that would require a new air quality conformity determination, that the draft TransPlan50 document and related appendices be approved as the final document. Commissioner Ainge seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Conformity Determination Report Draft Approval - Susan Hardy, MAG An MPO with a population of over 200,000 is mandated by Title 23 CFR Transportation rules to create a Long-Range Plan (LRP) of transportation needs spanning at least 20 years. Although the FHWA requires the plan, it is the MPO governing body that approves and adopts the plan. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee June 6, 2019 Page 5 In order to be able to access the federal funding for the projects in the adopted plan, the FHWA has to concur with the MPO’s Conformity Analysis for the LRP. Without concurrence, the plan falls into non-compliance or non-conformity. The Conformity Determination Report is that analysis. Staff request that the Regional Planning Committee approve submitting the document for a 30-day public comment, and also to adopt the report as final contingent to not receiving comments that will require a major alteration to the plan. Commissioner Ainge motioned that MPO TAC recommends to MPO RPC approval of the draft TransPlan50 DRAFT Conformity Determination Report to be released for a 30-day public comment period as required by the MAG Public Participation Program and also recommend that, barring no significant comments received during the 30- day public comment period that would require a new air quality conformity determination, that the draft TransPlan50 Conformity Determination Report be approved as the final document. Mayor Larson seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Update Reports UDOT Region Three: None.
