Item 1: A presentation regarding the Provo City 2024 Sustainability Report (25-058)
0:32:50

Don Jarvis, Sustainability Advisor, presented the 2024 Sustainability Report. He provided an
overview of the City’s ongoing efforts and accomplishments in environmental stewardship. He
explained that three sustainability committees currently operate in Provo: the mayor's
sustainability committee, the employee sustainability committee coordinated by Hannah Salzl,

and the citizen sustainability committee. Mr. Jarvis noted that the citizen committee operates
with early morning meetings as a "Darwinian approach" to member selection, with ten members
appointed by the Mayor.

With support from Brigham Young University (BYU), the committee determined that Provo’s
tree canopy coverage is approximately 17%, consistent with estimates from Google. Mr. Jarvis
highlighted the "1000 Trees" program, which began in 2022 and is approximately halfway to
completion. He acknowledged the close collaboration between the committee and Provo Power,
led by a former forester.

Mr. Jarvis discussed the recent use of the C-PACE (Commercial Property Assessed Clean
Energy) program by the Rivers Edge Group for energy-efficient development, following its
initial approval in 2016. He also mentioned the reopening of the Rock Canyon Trailhead after
years of effort through a public-private partnership.

The committee places significant emphasis on education and outreach, including programs in
schools and public displays that encourage a shift to electric energy. Initiatives include tours of
all-electric homes in Provo, such as the Mayor’s residence. Provo Power also issued a small
grant to support the committee's activities. Provo has also maintained the designation of Tree
City USA for 40 years.

The committee regularly participates in community outreach at local events and schools, offering
education on air quality and energy efficiency. Mr. Jarvis acknowledged the increasing role of
BYU in sustainability, citing its receipt of a STARS International Award and the release of its
first sustainability plan.

The Green Business Award program was highlighted as a tool for recognizing local businesses
that implement sustainable practices. Mega Diamond received the top award for initiatives such
as reducing water use and CO2 emissions, and investments in LED lighting and renewable
energy.

An agricultural commission that reports to the sustainability committee is focused on agricultural
conservation and promoting urban gardening practices.

Councilor Whipple expressed appreciation for the Green Business Award, particularly its role in
showcasing local businesses such as Mega Diamond. Councilor Bogdin shared a personal
anecdote about a resident who adjusted their lawn watering habits after measuring water output.

Item 3: A presentation regarding Provo River channel enhancement (25-057) 0:49:09

Mike Mills, Executive Director of the Utah Reclamation Mitigation and Conservation
Commission, presented updates on the Provo River channel enhancement project. He reported
that a new diversion structure was completed in March 2025 to deliver between 10 and 15 cubic
feet per second of water to the original river channel.

To enhance water quality and aquatic habitat, an aeration system was installed to support fish
populations. Additionally, a small downstream dam is under construction at Utah Lake State
Park to help minimize water level fluctuations in the original channel.

As part of the recreational improvements, two non-motorized boat launch facilities will be added
at West Trailhead and Alligator Park. Three fishing platforms are also planned along the channel.
Mr. Mills noted that fish stocking efforts are underway, with 409 rainbow trout recently
introduced. Mosquito abatement continues through coordination with Utah County.

Infrastructure upgrades include repaving the trail that runs from Lakeshore Drive to Utah Lake
State Park, scheduled for early 2026. Delta Gateway Park, a new four-acre public space, is slated
to open at the end of June 2025.

Mr. Mills also shared that a working group has been established to consider a new name for the
remaining stretch of the Provo River, and input from the City Council was requested. He assured
that only a limited number of trees will be removed to accommodate the planned improvements.

Council Member George Handley suggested honoring Dale DeSpain and his family, who played
a significant role in the cooperative efforts to make the Delta restoration project possible by
giving up land and collaborating closely with the city. Handley proposed naming the remaining
stretch of the Provo River after the DeSpain family or finding another way to recognize their
contributions, such as installing plaques, to acknowledge those who helped make the project a
reality for the public good. He emphasized the importance of recognizing these key contributors
while exploring suitable naming options.

Item 4: A discussion and update regarding the Council Handbook (25-060) 1:19:26

Justin Harrison, Executive Director, presented recent updates to the Council Handbook. A
significant addition includes a new section addressing Redevelopment Agency (RDA) Governing
Board meetings, specifically authorizing the use of implied motions. This procedural change
aims to improve efficiency by removing the necessity for explicit motions during certain RDA
Governing Board proceedings.

Mr. Harrison also recommended adding similar content related to the Stormwater Service
District Governing Board. Including this in the handbook would clarify the procedures and
operations of the board alongside other board responsibilities.

Motion: Councilor Bogdin moved to adopt the proposed handbook update to incorporate the
Stormwater Service District section. The motion was seconded by Councilor MacKay.

The motion was approved unanimously with a 6-0 vote. (Councilor Christensen excused)

General Commercial (CG) Zone. Provo Bay Neighborhood. (PLRZ20230258) 1:22:22

Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented an update on a zone map amendment request for
property located at 2087 West Center Street. He explained that the property owner initially
pursued a text amendment to reduce the minimum lot size required for the agritourism overlay

zone. Following input from the Council, the request was revised to a zone change to General
Commercial (CG).

Mr. Ardmore noted that a development agreement accompanies the zone change proposal,
restricting the allowed uses on the property to six specified commercial uses. He explained that

Councilors reviewed the development agreement and acknowledged that it addressed their prior

and shape inherently constrain the types of commercial development feasible on the site.
