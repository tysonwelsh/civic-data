         2.1     Discussion of Moderate-Income Housing Plan Requirements. (Staff Presenter,
                 Grant Crowell).

City Planner and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell shared a presentation from the
Utah League of Cities and Towns ("ULCT"), called "Moderate-Income Housing Deep Dive: HB-
462 - The Nuts and Bolts for October 1, 2022, and Beyond." He reported that the Legislature
previously passed Senate Bill ("SB") 34, which was an update to the moderate-income housing
requirements. Some of the first Legislation on moderate-income housing impacted Bluffdale. He
explained that the City of Bluffdale was used as an example of a city that needs to further address
moderate-income housing. As a result, the Bluffs and Vintage on the Bluffs areas were approved.

Bluffdale was not the only city that was held as an example at that time, as other cities with
historically large lots received additional attention from the courts as well.

SB-34 asked cities to plan for moderate-income housing in the General Plan and report on
moderate-income housing on an annual basis. The repo1iing went through the Housing and
Community Development Department of the Utah Department of Workforce Services. In House
Bill ("HB") 462, the requirements were amended to add fmiher clarification and expand the
strategies or menu items that jurisdictions could choose from to create more flexibility.
Mr. Crowell discussed the purpose and objectives of the requirements, which were as follows:

   •   To meet the needs of people of various income levels that live, work, or desire to live or
       work in the community;
   •   To allow people with various incomes to benefit from and fully participate in all aspects of
       neighborhood and community life;
   •   To determine how the jurisdiction will provide realistic opportunities to meet the need for
       additional moderate-income housing within the next five years; and
   •   To receive consideration for funding of projects based on compliance with the moderate-
       income housing requirements.

Mr. Crowell reported that HB-462 was approved during the 2022 General Session. The Utah
Housing Affordability Amendments modified many of the provisions related to affordable housing
that was in the SB-34 Legislation. Some of the highlights were overviewed. For instance, HB-
462 clarified the moderate-income housing requirements and the timing to amend the General Plan.
It also required the inclusion of an Implementation Plan to bring the moderate-income housing
element to life. HB-462 would amend the list of strategies to use, outline annual reporting
requirements and add priority incentives and restrictions related to compliance.

The Implementation Plan was discussed. Mr. Crowell reported that concrete steps need to be
outlined to determine how moderate-income housing would be added to the City. There needed
to be a clear timeline included in the plan as well. Some of the strategy options were shared. There
were 24 strategy options for municipalities, some of which included:

   •   A rezone for densities necessary to facilitate the production of moderate-income housing;
   •   Demonstrate investment in the rehabilitation or expansion of infrastructure that facilitates
       the construction of moderate-income housing;
   •   Demonstrate investment in the rehabilitation of existing uninhabitable housing stock into
       moderate-income housing;
   •   Identify and utilize County general fund subsidies or other sources of revenue to waive
       construction-related fees that are otherwise generally imposed by the County/municipality
       for the construction or rehabilitation of moderate-income housing;
   •   Create or allow for, and reduce regulations related to, internal or detached accessory
       dwelling units in residential zones; and
