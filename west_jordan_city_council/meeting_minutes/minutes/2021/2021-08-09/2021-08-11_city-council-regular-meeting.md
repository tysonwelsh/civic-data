A.     FY2022 Budget Amendment
       1. Police Pay Plan Update
Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck explained goals of a recent review and proposed
update of police compensation: (1) increase recruitment; (2) improve retention; (3) be competitive
in the market; (4) resolve issues with compression; and (5) resolve issues with the pay plan. She
explained the review process and presented the following highlights:
       •   New plan focuses on attracting the next generation into law enforcement.
       •   Step increases are accelerated at the beginning of an officer’s career to provide a standard
           of living and increase retention.
       •   Plan provides modest step increases over the rest of an officer’s career.
       •   City remains highly competitive and assists with staffing levels.
       •   Lowers the annual increased cost over time (10-year crossover).

Ms. Steck presented a proposed update to the Police Pay Plan, and showed that the update would
slow projected growth of the Police Budget from 3.1% to 2.5% per year. She emphasized that the
cost of providing public safety exceeded the City’s property tax revenue, and presented two
proposed funding options for the Police Pay Plan update.

       Funding For Police Compensation ($1,169,528)
       Option A:
       • Sales Tax $825,653
       • Redirect majority of 2.5% property tax increase $350,000
       • Consequence: more vulnerable to changes in sales tax due to consumer behavior or
          external environment (i.e., inflation, COVID, etc.); reduce reserves in Risk and IT.
City Council Work Session Minutes – August 11, 2021                                            Page 2

        Option B:
        • Transfer to Capital $1,233,571 - $1,169,528 = $64,043
        • Consequence: reduce unrestricted General Fund reserves from $2.1 million to $1.2
           million and reduce reserves for the Community Arts Center from $5.9 million to $5.6
           million; both changes would impact City-funded improvement projects in the next year.

Chair Jacob commented that the proposed 2.5% property tax increase would not match the current
5% Consumer Price Index (CPI) rate.

Responding to a comment from Councilmember Pack, Ms. Steck agreed the comparison of West
Jordan police compensation to police compensation in other cities would remain fluid as other
agencies made changes to compensation plans. She stated a compensation analysis would occur
annually to ensure fair compensation.

Councilmember Whitelock asked if using sales tax and a lower amount from Capital would be an
option. Ms. Steck responded in the affirmative. Councilmember Worthen commented that sales tax
revenue was not guaranteed. Ms. Steck said she believed sales tax growth would continue.

Responding to a comment from Vice Chair Green, City Administrator Korban Lee agreed promotion
within the Police Department was possible if qualifications were met. Councilmember McConnehey
pointed out the proposed budget amounts presumed every position was filled upon
implementation. He commented it would take time to fill the positions, which would provide enough
breathing room to compensate for any overestimation of sales tax revenue. Councilmember
McConnehey said he would be fine with Option A.

Councilmember Worthen said she would not want the fact that the City offered a good compensation
package to be overlooked. Ms. Steck said she agreed.

Vice Chair Green referred to funds transferred to Reserves the previous year because of savings in
the Police Budget due to employee turnover, and said he would prefer to return the money in
Reserves back to the Police Department. Mr. Lee pointed out that both Option B and Vice Chair
Green’s suggestion could have capital projects funded from Reserves.

Ms. Steck showed estimated impact of Option B on the Capital Projects Fund. Vice Chair Green stated
delayed infrastructure maintenance and delayed capital projects would cost the City more over
time. He suggested using Reserves to partially fund street and park maintenance.

Councilmember Whitelock said she believed people would continue to spend money and sales tax
revenue would continue to grow. She said she would be comfortable increasing the sales tax
revenue estimate and using the estimated increase to fund the Police Pay Plan update.

Ms. Steck presented a General Fund 5-Year Plan, including truth-in-taxation in each of the five years
with the following proposed property tax increases:

        FY2022                  2.50%
        FY2023                  10.00%
        FY2024 – FY2027         2.50% per year

   Ms. Steck responded that perhaps instead of a 10% in FY2023, the 2.5% annual increases could be

           2. Other Compensation
   Ms. Steck and the Council discussed the idea of including an allowance to provide City
   Councilmembers tickets to City-sponsored events (to be used at their discretion) in the FY2022

   Office Director Alan Anderson shared what he learned regarding how other cities handled the issue.

   Staff and the Council discussed the Utah Ethics Act. Councilmember McConnehey commented that
   at some events the use of discretionary tickets given to the Council would represent loss of potential
   revenue to the City. The group discussed taxable compensation. Councilmember Pack suggested
   funding for discretionary event tickets should come from Council Contingency.

A citizen asked if the Council had considered Ranked Choice Voting. He felt it had become clear to
him that cities in Utah were being financially incentivized to make decisions regarding their cities’
voting method. He questioned why Utah was designated a beta test site for Ranked Choice Voting
and asked why nonprofit organizations were pushing Ranked Choice Voting as well as a national
popular vote eliminating the Electoral College. He asked why FairVote would consider a second or
third position to be more valuable and strategic. He expressed the opinion that a primary election
was when grassroots candidates were able to take time to knock on doors and get to know the
people, and said primary elections were probably more important than final elections. He
questioned how candidates would be able to guarantee the results of an election with Ranked
Choice Voting. He expressed the opinion that Ranked Choice Voting was complicated and
disenfranchised minority groups.

Debbie Marcosian stated she lived in a country with a constitution, and things needed to be as simple
and as righteous as they were in the past. She stated there was one vote per citizen. She said Ranked
Choice Voting was convoluted and complicated and would change things. Ms. Marcosian asked why
everything needed to be complicated. She said Ranked Choice Voting would disenfranchise
everyone. Ms. Marcosian expressed appreciation for the Council.

Zone was crafted to facilitate development on the west side of the City, with the Wood Ranch
development providing the catalyst. He explained the process involved in crafting the PCH Zone,
and expressed appreciation for work done by Larry Gardner and the Planning Commission.

Mr. Langford stated the PCH Zone would give the Wood Ranch Master Development Plan a great
deal of flexibility, and explained that approval of the PCH Zone would help in the process of drafting
a Master Development Agreement for Wood Ranch.

Referring to line 1802 of the document, Vice Chair Green asked if the City had criteria for
determining the identity, experience, and financial capacity of a master developer. Jody Hoffman
City Council Meeting Minutes – August 11, 2021                                               Page 4

responded that developer experience would be described in a Master Development Agreement to
ensure a certain level of developer experience if a project were transferred.

Council Chair Jacob opened a public hearing at 8:06 pm

Citizen Comments:
Gary Langston with Daybreak Communities expressed appreciation for efforts by staff, the Planning
Commission, and the City Council to get the Wood Ranch project to the current point. He commented
that Wood Ranch was a complex project. Mr. Langston said he felt strongly that the PCH Zone in its
final form should be approved with the Wood Ranch Master Development Agreement. He expressed
excitement about the property, and a desire to avoid any missteps or further delays. Mr. Langston
repeated the request to approve both the PCH Zone and the MDA at the same time.

Wade Budge, legal counsel for Daybreak Communities, said he echoed Mr. Langston’s comments. He
said the goal for the last year had been to work towards approval of the proposed ordinance
together with three other critical legislative acts – the MDA, a map amendment, and an annexation
ordinance. He expressed the opinion the Council would be well served to consider them all together.
He complimented City staff for their efforts.

Council Chair Jacob closed the public hearing at 8:11 pm

Council Member Lamb said he believed the developer, staff, and the City Council had taken time to
look at the proposal over and over again and commended everyone involved. He said he believed
the development would turn out great, and he was glad it hadn’t been pushed through too quickly.
Council Member Lamb said he was ready to move forward.

Responding to a question from Council Member McConnehey, Mr. Langford explained that the
proposed Code amendment would set the baseline for building the MDA. Council Member
McConnehey thanked staff for their thousands of hours of work on the issue.

Council Member Whitelock said she had thought the proposed PCH Zone was intended to be a
general hillside zone, and said it bothered her that page two of the proposed document referred
specifically to Wood Ranch. Referring to specific pages in the document, she questioned whether
the Council was comfortable delegating the ability to alter lot sizes and setbacks to the Planning
Commission as proposed, said restricting a developer or home buyer to earth tone colors felt like
government overreach, and said she would not want all open space to be 30 percent slopes.
Council Member Whitelock questioned prohibiting use of aluminum siding and said she had heard
comments that West Jordan Code made homes more expensive.

Mr. Langford and Ms. Hoffman explained the earth tone colors definition was not used in the PCH
Zone. Mr. Langford confirmed the Council would have the ability to apply the PCH Zone to any
development deemed applicable. He confirmed that only the City Council would have the ability to
change lot sizes and setbacks. The Planning Commission would not have the ability to change the
MDA. Mr. Langford and Ms. Hoffman spoke of MDA issues yet to be resolved.

Vice Chair Green said he believed the proposed document was a good playbook that was really

the MDA. He said he did not have a problem with approving the proposed ordinance and the MDA
at the same time as requested by Daybreak representatives. He suggested September 22, 2021 as a
deadline.
