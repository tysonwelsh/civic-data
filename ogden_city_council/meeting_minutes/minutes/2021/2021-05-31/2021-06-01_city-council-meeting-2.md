Public hearing: Fiscal Year 2022 Tentative Budget. Proposed Ordinance
2021-26 adopting the tentative budget for Fiscal Year 2021-2022
          A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022 Tentative Budget came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated each year during the first Council meeting in May, the Mayor is required to present a proposed
budget for the upcoming fiscal year. The Council accepts the proposed budget for review and, through a series of work sessions,
reviews the proposal over the next two months. A tentative budget is adopted after holding an initial public hearing. A second
public hearing is held at the time of adoption of the fmal budget. During the budget hearings, the Council outlines their proposed
changes to the budget and accepts public input. Once the fmal public hearing is closed, the Council makes its changes and adopts
the fmal City budget. By State law, a budget must be adopted by June 30. An exception to the timing is allowed if the Council is
considering a proposed property tax increase. This evening, the Council will consider setting a public hearing on the FY2021-
2022 Tentative budget for June 1, 2021. At this time, Administration is not recommending a property tax increase and is asking
the Council to adopt the Tentative Budget along with proposed Schedule A changes to the budget.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
Council staffs review of the FY2021-2022 Tentative Budget. The Council has adopted fmancial principles for FY2021-2022,
including:
               •     Conservative revenue estimates;
               •    Balanced budget;
               •     Employee salaries as a budget priority;
               •     Investments;
               •    Fund Balance;
               •     Operating Reserves.
          Additionally, FY2021-2022 budget goals and guidelines include establishing goals of the upcoming budget, setting
policy regarding tax rates, and establishing priorities for spending. The Mayor's tentative budget does not include a proposal to
increase property tax rates for FY22. High priority items included in the Mayor's budget include employee compensation plan
implementation, replacement of eliminated line items in the FY21 budget (travel and education for employees), fleet replacement,
transition to a self-insurance model for employee health insurance, and CIP process streamlining and funding. Relative to staffing
changes, five new positions will be created, one will be reclassified, and two renamed.

tentative budget; revenues are projected to increase by $7 million, or 11.98 percent. Using the same comparison, the total City

Elected Official Salary Ordinance Amendments
           Council staff reviewed proposed changes to the Ogden Municipal Code sections relating to the Mayoral and Council
member salaries. These proposed amendments are needed to align the ordinances with the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022 Salary
Schedules.
           Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported Council staff is proposing two ordinance amendments to align the
Ogden Municipal Code with the FY22 Salary Schedules being proposed in the FY22 Budget. Ogden Municipal Code §2-3-6
addresses compensation for Council members. The current code provides that Council members will receive the same cost of
living and perfonnance adjustments that are provided to non-merit employees. The proposed amendments remove the adjustments
for performance and provides that:
                    "Other pay adjustments approved for non-merit special employees shall not increase the mayor's salary.
                    Council members' salaries may also be increased through the annual budget process if a total compensation
                    benchmarking study of all other Utah cities shows an increase is warranted."
           Ogden Municipal Code §2-2-3 addresses compensation for the Mayor. As with the Council salary ordinance, the current
code provides that the Mayor will receive the same cost of living and perfonnance adjustments that are provided to non-merit
employees. The proposed amendments remove the adjustments for perfonnance and provides that:
                    "Other pay adjustments approved for non-merit special employees shall not increase the mayor's salary.
                    Subject to approval by the council, the mayor's salary may be increased through the annual budget process if a
                    total compensation benchmarking study of all other Utah cities shows an increase is warranted."
           Ms. Eller-Smith then noted that since the Council's last discussion of elected officials' salaries, Council staffhas further
discussed the issue and feels that it would be appropriate to pursue additional benchmarking by an independent consultant; while
the data provided by the City's Human Resources Division is sufficient, it is only based on salaries paid in other cities and Council
staff feels it would be appropriate to consider other factors when trying to determine the appropriate salary for Ogden City elected
officials. Staff will work to address that issue in advance of the FY 2023 budget process.
           Council member Hyer suggested that those serving in Council leadership positions should receive additional
compensation since the workload for Chair and Vice Chair is considerably higher than for the rest of the Council.
          The Council engaged in brief discussion with Council staff regarding the option of creating a compensation commission
that would evaluate compensation matters across the City, including elected officials.
