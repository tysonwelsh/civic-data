Study Session

1.0    Discussion: Fiscal Year 2022/2023 Budget

1.1    Finance Director Bob Wylie explained that he would give a brief overview of
       the expenditures for the year, and noted that the budget was not yet balanced,
       so the information given that evening would be very general, and was subject
       to change. He said that their overall budget for expenditures for the next year
       was $19 million, and he noted that a large percentage of that figure was
       payroll. He listed their main categories of expenses and noted that there was
       a decrease in Capital Projects compared to the last fiscal year. He discussed
       the General Fund and explained that the paychecks for most of the City
       employees came from that fund. He noted that there was very little change
       between the upcoming year and the last year. He highlighted that there was
       an increase for vehicles and gas, to reflect the rise in gas prices and that they
       had also increased the budget for vehicle repairs. He noted that there was a
       future fund transfer planned, that would transfer money from the General
       Fund to the ambulance fund.

       Councilmember Green asked if the top line was growing quicker than their
       operating expenses, and Mr. Wylie said that their revenue from sales tax was
       growing at a quicker rate than their expenses. He also noted that it was harder
       to anticipate their expenditures, but their income was fairly predictable.

2.0   Training: Ethics and the Open and Public Meetings Act

2.1   City Attorney Mike Barker noted that there had been no changes to the ethics
      law since last year, so all of the information was the same as the previous year.
      He explained that the purpose of the ethics ordinance was to foster trust with
      the public, and for the Councilmembers to remain above any accusations of
      impropriety.

      Mr. Barker spoke about the “no canvassing” section of the ordinance, which
      essentially stated that someone could not use their connections to City
      Councilmembers to obtain a position on the City Staff. He gave some
      examples of this and asked Recreation Director Rhett Ogden how it would
      erode public trust if the City did not follow this principle. Recreation Director
      Rhett Ogden discussed that the public deserved full transparency, and also
      that if they did not follow this rule then some people might get favors that they
      do not deserve.

      Mr. Barker next discussed discrimination and said that while they must listen
      to people of all backgrounds equally, it did not mean that they could not be
      discerning in ideas that were presented to them by the public. He said that
      the Councilmembers could also not give anyone special treatment for any
      reason. He asked Councilmember Vawdrey how discrimination would affect
      public confidence, and said that they needed to avoid the appearance of any
      kind of corruption. He reminded the Council that they were given disclosure

      forms every year and that they needed to fill those out annually, and recuse
      themselves when appropriate. He said if there were any questions about
      when to recuse themselves, they should speak to the legal department.

      Mr. Barker said that in the Code, the City Council could not accept an offer of
      employment with Draper City within two years after sitting on the Council.
      However, a sitting City Councilmember could apply for and accept a City Staff
      position but would have to resign from the City Council if they were offered
      and accepted the job. He said that City Councilmembers also could not
      appoint immediate family members to positions on the City Staff. He
      distributed a pamphlet to the City Council that contained more ethical
      guidelines and noted that the City Staff was responsible for ensuring that the
      City Council remained in compliance with the Open and Public Meetings Act.

      Mr. Barker referenced House Bill 439, and said that the bill would standardize
      the format of meeting minutes to show the “yes” and “no” votes on a particular
      item. He said that they would standardize the way that votes were recorded
      for City Council minutes as well so that the public would have complete
      transparency of how the voting process had gone. He said that the legal
      department would make that change, and several others, to the City Code, and
      they would present those changes to the Council later that year. He also
      discussed several other House Bills that had been proposed that year but had
      not ultimately been passed.

      Mr. Barker discussed what constituted an emergency meeting, and said that
      in the case of an emergency meeting, public notice would not be required, as
      it was for all other meetings. He explained that an emergency meeting would
      be in response to unforeseen circumstances, such as a natural disaster. He
      added that the event must require the urgent attention of the City Council to
      constitute an emergency meeting. He explained that in the event of an
      emergency meeting, they would have to give as much public notice as
      realistically possible given the circumstances.

7.0   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1537, an Ordinance amending the official Zoning
      Map of Draper City for approximately 1.14 acres of property from RA1
      (Residential Agricultural, 40,000 square foot lot minimum) to RA 2 (Residential
      Agricultural, 20,000 square foot lot minimum), and approval of a Development
      Agreement for the property located at approximately 498 East 13800 South
      within Draper City, otherwise known as the Jarvie Estates Phase 2 Zoning Map
      Amendment and Development Agreement.

7.1   City Planner Jennifer Jastremsky shared a land use map of the area in question
      and explained that the applicant wished to have their parcel rezoned into the
      Residential Agricultural 2 Zone and to divide their property into two half-acre
      parcels. She said that currently, they had access to the property from a private
      dirt lane, and the applicant wanted to add another private lane to have access
      to his property. She noted that a home could not be built on the land where
      the applicant had proposed to add a lane. She shared images of the plan in
      question. She explained that the existing lane did not comply with the City’s
      standards for private lane width, since it had been built before the ordinance

      had been updated.    She added that the Planning Commission had
      recommended approval on this issue on a 5-0 vote.

      Ms. Jastremsky also gave a short history of the Jarvie Estate Development, to
      give context to the public. She added that since it was a private lane, the HOA
      would be responsible for negotiating trash pickup. She said that the City would
      not automatically provide a garbage service since it was not a public road. She
      discussed that she had received some public comments in favor of the

      current dirt road because to subdivide they would have to run utilities down

      that the owner of the entrance of the lane specifically did not allow for any
      improvements to the lane, even though there was an easement.

8.0   Action Item: Ordinance #1538, an Ordinance amending Draper City Municipal
      Code 11-6 related to the encroachment permits and permit fees.

8.1   Public Works Director Scott Cooley explained that they needed to update
      Chapter 11 regarding their encroachment fees. He said that the name
      “excavation fees'' implied that digging was being done, so they needed to
      change the name to make it more inclusive. He said that they would change it
      to “utility or excavation work in City right-of-way or public property,” which
      allowed them to take the City’s interest into account when they received an
      application for an encroachment permit. He stated that the City could reject
      any application that was not complete and that an application would expire
      after 30 days if a permit had not been granted. He added that the City could
      also charge additional fees for after-hours inspections. He explained that the
      City already did most of the things included in the update, and this would just
      align with the ordinance.

      Mr. Cooley added that the ordinance stated that roads would remain open to
      public traffic while work was being done unless the City Engineer had
      approved it to close the roads. He stated that the permit did not allow for the
      permittee to trespass onto private roads, which he noted had been an issue in
      the past. He explained that the remainder of the document was regarding
      insurance and liability.

8.2   Councilmember F. Lowry moved to approve                   Ordinance    #1538.
      Councilmember Vawdrey seconded the motion.

8.3   A roll call vote was taken with Councilmembers Green, Lowery, Lowry, Roberts,

10.0   Action Item: Ordinance #1536, an Ordinance vacating the South Field Ditch
       Drainage Channel Easement.

10.1   Mr. Cooley said that in 1993, their Storm Drain Master Plan had included a
       facility, and explained that the City had been in the process of purchasing
       easements to plan for the facility. He said that in the early 2000s, the plans for
       that facility had been abandoned, and the City had begun to vacate the
       easements that they had purchased. He explained that the City had not
       vacated all the easements, and said that they had received a request from a
       resident to vacate the South Field Ditch Drainage Channel. He stated that the
       City did not need that easement, and had no specific purpose for it since the
       plans for the facility had been abandoned.

10.2   Mayor Walker asked if a trail could be added to that easement, and Mr. Cooley
       thought that would not be possible since it cut through private land.
