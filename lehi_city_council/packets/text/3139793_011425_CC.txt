32   2.1 Presentation and Update on Alpine School District- Stacy Bateman and Joylyn Lincoln
33   The Alpine School District Board representatives provided an overview of the schools located
34   within Lehi, highlighting the balanced enrollment between the two high schools and the growth
35   in enrollment at the junior high level. They discussed concerns about two elementary schools
36   exceeding 1,000 students and noting that it may need to be addressed through reconfiguration
37   and new school construction. The presentation also covered the district's strong concurrent
38   enrollment program, which allows students to earn college credits while in high school, as well
39   as the district's high graduation rates and ongoing capital projects to maintain and update school
40   facilities.

146   5. Consideration of Resolution #2025-03 approving an agreement with Geneva Rock for
147      the construction of a box culvert at Willow Park.
148      Allred explained that the project aims to address a bottleneck in the waste ditch by removing
149      the existing 60 or 72-inch steel pipe that was installed in reverse grade, causing the ditch to
150      back up significantly during runoff season. The new box culvert will be the same size as the
151      other box culverts in the waste ditch, which will lower the flow line and eliminate the choke
152      point. Allred noted that this will make an enormous difference in the area's flood control
153      capabilities.
154
155       Motion:     Councilor Hancock moved to approve Resolution #2025-03 approving an
156                   agreement with Geneva Rock for the construction of a box culvert at Willow Park.

165      between Lehi City and Utah County for the Dry Creek Lake Regional Recreation Area.

167      bathrooms, a four-plex, and fishing areas. Powell highlighted that the project has a primary
168      purpose of flood control, and the improvements downstream have made a significant
169      difference in the area's ability to handle flooding. He noted that the county's tourism tax is
170      funding the project, as they believe it will stimulate tourism in the region. Overall, Powell

239   10. Consideration of Ordinance #02-2025 the Public Facility Rezone 2024 located at various
240       locations.
241       Kim Struthers presented the public facility rezone for several city-owned properties,
242       including the Family Park property. Struthers explained that this rezone helps ensure these
243       properties remain in public use and makes it easier to identify city-owned land on the zoning
244       map. The Council had a brief discussion, with Mayor Johnson noting that there were some
245       gaps between parcels in the Family Park area. He suggested consolidating the property into
246       one or two larger parcels to resolve any overlaps or gaps. Overall, the Council was
247       supportive of the public facility rezone, recognizing it as a helpful "housekeeping" measure
248       to maintain accurate zoning records for city-owned lands. The Council approved the
249       resolution, excluding a small piece of property that was still being addressed.
250
251       Motion:     Councilor Hancock moved to approve Ordinance #02-2025 the Public Facility
252                   Rezone 2024 located at various locations, except for a small pedestrian access that
253                   is still being addressed; including all Development Review Committee Comments.
254                   Councilor Newall seconded the motion.
