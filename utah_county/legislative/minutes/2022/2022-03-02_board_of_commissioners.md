We the staff of the Children’s Justice Center would like to nominate our co-worker, Kris Kokol, -
for Utah County Employee of the Month.

Kris' tenure began at the CJC in 2007 as a MSW intern. Kris was a dedicated worker from day
one. She instantly became one of those interns you remember, and not in a bad way. Kris
showed up early, worked hard all day, stayed well past closing time and never was there a
word of complaint. She was eager to learn and inspired to help serve child victims of abuse in
our community. She took on projects that could have used 3-4 people to help but managed
them all by herself.

Luckily for us, Kris rejoined the Center after graduation (over 10 years ago) and is now the
Clinical Supervisor. She provides services to parents/guardians who bring their children to be
interviewed at the Children's Justice Center. She sits with parents during some of their darkest
moments and helps get them on the road to healing. In addition to managing multiple cases a
day, Kris supervises an incredible team of individuals. Their words really capture what a huge
impact Kris has not only on our staff, but the people that we serve.

One team member said, "Kris gives great support as a supervisor. She is aware of our needs and
makes a strong effort to maintain unity, teamwork, and work ethic."

Another shared, "Kris is always supportive and helpful. She never makes me feel bad for
doing things wrong. She understands work/life balance and helps me keep that in mind."

"| appreciate Kris' authenticity in everything she says and does. | love that Kris speaks from the
heart, especially when she is supporting her colleagues and team members. Kris understands
that being a good leader does not mean having all the answers or telling others what to do, it
means creating a safe and supportive environment where people can be vulnerable and honest.
| am grateful for Kris' example and will never tire of hearing her share her thoughts and feelings
because they are usually full of wisdom and heart."

"Where do | even start?! Kris is the backbone of the Center. Her compassionate heart is critical
to not only our staff but especially the caregivers she works with day in and day out. She can
quickly connect with people and comfortably sit with them during their darkest moments. Her
easy-going nature makes her a fun person to collaborate with on projects and tasks. She has a
wicked sense of humor and knows how to quickly diffuse contentious situations. There is a
reason our staff is continually seeking her throughout the day; she provides astute insight,

WHEREAS, after decades of foreign rule and control over Ukraine, and its neighboring
countries, that country won independence from the former Soviet Union, now Russia, in
1991;

WHEREAS, in February 8, 1994, Ukraine joined the North Atlantic Treaty
Organization’s (NATO) Partnership for Peace program to strengthen ties with European
members as an Enhanced Opportunities Partner;

WHEREAS, in December 5, 1994, the Russian Federation, the United
Kingdom, and the United States of America signed the Budapest Memorandum on
Security Assurances, wherein each country pledged to respect the Sovereignty of
Ukraine and its borders, and refrain from the threat or use of force against
Ukraine;

WHEREAS, in February 2014, in the wake of the Revolution of Dignity, the status
of Crimea and parts of Donbas, internationally recognized as part of Ukraine, came into
question. During those conflicts Russia annexed Crimea in 2014, with the War in Donbas
waged by separatists, supported by Russian volunteers and Russia itself, continued;

WHEREAS, throughout 2021 and into 2022, Russia conducted numerous military
exercises and amassed large caches of military equipment near the Ukraine borders; then,
on February 23, 2022, conducted an multiprong and unprovoked invasion into Ukraine from
the east, the north, and from its southern borders;

WHEREAS, while peace talks between Ukraine and Russian authorities tentatively
began then ended, many hundreds, perhaps thousands of Ukrainian civilians have died in
wanton slaughter, with cities, businesses, and sovereign Ukrainian lands laid waste at the
hands of Russian forces — all under the direction of Viadimir Putin;

WHEREAS, while Russian aircraft bombings, artillery, and rockets assault lands
throughout Ukraine, tanks, cluster bombs, thermobaric “Vacuum Bombs” cause widespread
and random destruction, killing and horrifying Ukrainian people of all ages;

WHEREAS, in response to Russia’s unfounded war and increased violence, the
Ukrainian military have been joined by the nation’s people, members of parliament, and
their president Volodymyr Zelensky in taking up arms in defense of their county’s capital Kyiv
and throughout all the nation;

WHEREAS, the United States, European and other countries, and many thousand
Russians are making repeated calls for an end to the bloodshed, and for the Russian forces
to withdraw out of a sovereign land; all have been ignored by Russian President Putin;

WHEREAS, many nations have begun economic sanctions to limit and significantly
disabie the Russian government from continuing to prosecute its war against Ukraine, as well
as provide critical armaments and supplies to aid the sovereign people in their own defense.

NOW THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the LEGISLATIVE BODY OF UTAH

COUNTY, UTAH:

(1) Hears, recognizes, and commends the resolve and restraint shown by a courageous
Ukrainian people for their right of sovereignty, and publicly recognizes and honors
the uncounted men and women and children who gave and are giving their lives to
maintain their independence and freedom;

(2) Stands with the people of Ukraine in the defense of their natural rights and for the
right to maintain the integrity of their sovereign, national borders;

(3) Denounces Russia’s baseless invasion of Ukraine and joins with the Utah State
Legislature and State Executive in standing in solidarity with the people of Ukraine;

(4) Commends and supports Utah’s House Concurrent Resolution 21 (2022) concerning
the conflict in Ukraine and encourages Utah’s governor, with the President of the
United States, to continue to seek nationwide support for the country of Ukraine;

(5) Encourages ongoing support and unity between the United States of America, the
European nations, and other allies and provide direct support and relief to the

from Ukraine, and to reaffirm Ukraine’s sovereignty;

(6) Supports strengthening alliances among nations of the world in support of
independence of each country, and for each nations’ citizens unalienable right to live
without fear of reprisal;

(7) Encourages the state Legislature and Governor to promote these principles, and for

and replacing the Ukrainian flag with the Utah County flag.

(10) BEIT FURTHER RESOLVED that a copy of this resolution be transmitted, by the
