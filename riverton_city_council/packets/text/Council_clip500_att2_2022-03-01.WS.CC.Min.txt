36   besides the Jordan River trail.

41   case numbers in this area justify this.

19   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained this is a public hearing for a
20   General Plan Amendment and Rezone application brought by the applicant for 1.29-acres at
21   3444 West 12350 South. The subject property is an existing corner lot with an existing home. It
22   is part of the Riverton Meadows Subdivision. The subject property is currently zoned R-1, which
23   is a single-family residential zone with acre lots and animal rights. All surrounding properties
24   (about 114-acres total) are also zoned R-1. The General Plan designation for the area is Estate
25   Density residential. This designation has a minimum lot size of 21,780-sf which is a true half-
26   acre lot. It is applied on the General Plan map across mostly existing neighborhoods. Zoning
27   designations appropriate for the General Plan Designation are R-1 (1-acre lots) and RR-22
28   (half-acre lots), which both have animal rights.

31   homes of equal size, leaving current home in place and building 2 more east facing homes.”

20            Ordinance No. 22-09 – Proposed Amendment to Sections 18.225.040(a) &

22            Proposed by Riverton City
23
24   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained this is a public hearing for a
25   Zoning Text Change proposed by Riverton City. The Zone text change involves Section 18.225
26   Accessory Structures. The amendment addresses two issues. The first is related to the allowed
27   height of accessory structures of any kind. The current ordinance allows a maximum height of
28   20 or 25 feet for accessory structures, depending on property size and distance from the
29   property line. However, the ordinance includes the limitation that an accessory building “may not
30   exceed the height of the main dwelling, measured at the highest pitch of the roof”. This has
31   proven to be a difficult standard to maintain, in part because that measurement is not always
32   intuitive or possible for a homeowner to make. Even Riverton City staff have difficulties
33   establishing the finished height of a home that has been in existence long enough that the City
34   no longer has plans on file, which typically are only kept for a few years. Staff is recommending
35   that the requirement for accessory structures not to exceed the height of the home be
36   eliminated.
37
38   The second is a requirement in the section dealing with accessory dwelling units (ADU). That
39   section includes a limitation that the accessory building containing an ADU cannot exceed 50%
40   of the footprint of the main dwelling. This limitation does not apply to accessory buildings in
41   general, only where an ADU is included. This has made the process of adding an ADU to an
42   existing building difficult for some property owners, where an accessory building was permitted
43   but exceeded the size requirement. Staff is proposing this limitation on the size of the
44   accessory structure containing an ADU be removed, allowing ADU’s in any accessory structure
45   which meets the requirements of the ordinance. Alternatively, the limitation may be amended to
46   limit the size of the ADU within the building. Should the Council so desire, staff can provide
47   assistance in crafting that language into the motion.
