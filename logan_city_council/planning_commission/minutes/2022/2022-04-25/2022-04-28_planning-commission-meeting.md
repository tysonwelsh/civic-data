 PC 22-019 Altura -Continued from the April 14 meeting. [Subdivision & Design Review Permit]
 Dan Larsen/Strata Land Holdings LLC, authorized agent/owner, request a subdivision permit to
 create 159 lots. The design review permit is to construct 7 5-plex buildings, 21 7-plex buildings,
 and 13 24-unit apartment buildings for a total of 494 units on 29.09 acres located at
 approximately 830 West 2075 South in the Mixed Residential Medium (MR-20) zone; TIN 03-006-
 0016; -0022; -0026. (Woodruff Neighborhood)


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a Design Review and Subdivision permit for 494 units
located at approximately 830 West 2075 South. There are four items that the commission requested
to be changed during the last meeting. The applicant has made the following changes to their
proposed plan:


30’ along the north of the property has been dedicated for 2000 South.
Two of the 24-plex apartment buildings have been reoriented, so the side of the building does not
face the street.
Additional sidewalks have been added for better connectivity from the apartment entrances to the
adjacent street.
The Commission will need to determine if the small change made to the rear gable is enough to say
that the rear façade is similar to the front and meets the 4-sided architecture requirement.
The LDC 17.09.040 requires multi-family buildings to have minimum variations, interesting façade
design, a mix of materials, and articulation for aesthetically pleasing projects. The MR-20 zones are
required to have minimum street-facing façade design variations from adjacent buildings to enhance
neighborhood character and create visual interest (limit “cookie-cutter” homes).
The LDC identifies eight (8) different categories and requires that at least four (4) out of the eight (8)
be noticeably different. The eight (8) categories listed are building color, materials, roofline, height
(number of floors), fenestration, architectural style, articulation, and porch design.
The applicant has chosen to vary the color, articulation, roofline, and materials between apartment
buildings A & B. The roofline category is the only category that lists a measurable difference
requirement of 20% in roof elevations. Staff has a concern with the proposed roof variation and the
Planning Commission will need to determine if the added gable on building B meets the roofline
variation of 20%.

PROPONENT: Jeremy Burbank said it is not clear how the 20% roofline difference should be
measured, so they tried to create what would be the most practical and look the best while keeping
the rear similar to the front. They are willing to adjust the roofline if needed to come to an
agreement with staff on the 20%. He talked about the colors and how they look similar on a screen
or a printout but how they are all noticeably different shades in person.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: There was a discussion between the commissioners about the variation of material,
color, articulation, and roofline on the rear facades of buildings A & B. The committee determined
that the variations of color, articulation and material are sufficient, but the rooflines do not meet the
required 20% difference, based on the total width of the building.

Commissioner Peterson mentioned that they could meet the 20% variation by removing the gables
but that would not result in a better change. Commissioner Croshaw said that the buildings have
enough variation and that the different trees and vegetation will also add variation. Commissioner
Lewis would like to see the 20% variation in the rear roofline of buildings A & B met. Commissioner
Heare agreed with Commissioner Lewis to change condition number 2 to meet the 20% variation.

 PC 22-024 Annexation Policy Plan Review-2022 Draft [Policy Plan Review] Mike
 DeSimone/Logan City are requesting the review and adoption of Logan City’s 2022 Annexation
 Policy Plan.

STAFF/PROPONENT: M. DeSimone reviewed the proposed Annexation Policy Plan which is
required under Utah Municipal Code 10-2-401.5. All Utah municipalities are required to adopt an
Annexation Policy Plan that guides the City’s future growth and expansion through the annexation
of unincorporated lands adjacent to Logan City. The function of the Annexation Policy Plan is to
identify those areas outside the community that presents the greatest potential for inclusion into the
city with a logical and coordinated approach to new development and public service provision. M.
DeSimone reviewed the reasons why the City is adopting an updated Annexation Policy Plan, the
duties of the Planning Commission during this process, and the elements of the Plan.

M. DeSimone outlined the six different growth corridors identified in the plan and the future population
growth and general land uses anticipated for each area. The amount of vacant land located within
the City was reviews. M. DeSimone also discussed the Planning Commission’s review and
recommendation process as well as the next steps in the Commission’s process.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Petersen asked if there would be any annexation areas designated
as agriculture lands. M. DeSimone said that property should be annexed if there are environmental
reasons to do so, but otherwise Logan City is looking at future urban growth therefore the prime


agricultural lands should stay in the county. Commissioner Lucero asked about the zoning and
development in the future annexation areas. M. DeSimone said that the annexation plan does not
include specific zoning or development recommendations. The purpose of the Annexation Policy
Plan is to identify the logical boundaries for extending the city. Specific housing types, roads, and
other development standards are part of the next level and will be included in the next General Plan.

The Commission moved to the next agenda item that is related to this item. The motion for this item
is included with the motion on PC 22-023.


 PC 22-023 Land Development Code Amendment-Annexations [Code Amendment] Mike
 DeSimone/Logan City request an amendment to the Land Development Code, Chapter 17.53 to
 provide general requirements for annexing property into Logan City. This amendment is in
 conjunction with the proposed Logan City 2022 Annexation Policy Plan also under consideration.


STAFF/PROPONENT: M. Desimone reviewed the proposed Land Development Code
Amendment to Chapter 17.53 which provides the general requirements for annexing property into
Logan City as well as delineates the review process the City will undertake in assessing
Annexation Petitions. Of importance is the clarification that requires annexation petitions to be
reviewed by the Planning Commission with the Commission making a recommendation to the
Council.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Lucero asked if the future annexation areas will be included in the
Future Land Use Plan (FLUP). Commissioner Guth said that it is hard to consider an annexation
and development plan without knowing what zone is planned for the site. M. DeSimone said that it
makes sense to include future growth corridors in the FLUP so that decisions regarding annexation
requests can be made in a manner consistent with the General Plan.
