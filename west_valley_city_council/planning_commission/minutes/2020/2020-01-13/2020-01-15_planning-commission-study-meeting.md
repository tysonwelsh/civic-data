ZONE TEXT CHANGE APPLICATION
ZT-17-2019  PETITION BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTIONG A ZONE TEXT
            CHANGE TO AMEND THE CRITERIA USED TO DETERMINE WHETHER A
            PROPERTY IS ELIGIBLE TO BE REZONED TO THE RM ZONE, PARKING
            LOT LIGHTING STANDARDS, AND THE MULTI-UNIT RESIDENTIAL
            DESIGN STANDARDS. (STAFF – STEVE PASTORIK AT 801-963-3545)

Discussion:

Steve presented the application. He explained how he has spoken to Randy Moore, a developer.
Randy suggested a few different options to Steve. One option would be to increase the minimum
street width from 100 ft to 80 ft. A second option is to keep the 100-foot street and cap the acreage at
10 units/acre. Randy’s last option would be to allow an exception less than an 80-foot street for a
senior development. Steve Pastorik stated that Randy’s main point seems to be the 80-foot right-of-
way. Steve Pastorik presented a map that illustrated the areas that would be allowed to apply for the
RM zone if we were to include the 80-foot right-of-way streets.

Steve stated that from a staff prospective, he doesn’t feel that the City Council will want to open the
ordinance to an 80-foot right-of-way. Steve stated that the land Randy Moore is looking to develop a
multi-family project on isn’t eligible with the current ordinance requirements. Steve Pastorik also
stated that he feels the City Council would not pass the 80-foot right-of-way. Commissioner Lovato
asked Steve Pastorik for an example of an 80-foot right-of-way as compared to 100 feet. Steve
explained an 80-foot right-of-way is a five-lane section.

Steve pointed out that one limiting factor is that there is not a lot of vacant land along the added 80 ft.
roadways, one exception being Parkway where most of the area is currently single family.
Commissioner Winters stated that opening it up isn’t his concern because this just allows them to
apply and then the Planning Commission will look at each application on a case by case basis.
Commissioner McEwen asked if we had a plan for development out west along with transit.
Steve stated the question is, do you put transit in along the high-density areas of the City and limit the
transit in the lower density area’s or do you try to cover everything with less frequent service? UTA
is leaning towards putting more transit in where there is more density. Commissioner McEwen stated
that his concern is we need to insure there is a good balance within the City.

Commissioner Fuller explained that it’s difficult to keep balance in the City when you have a lot of
developments in one area. Commissioner Meaders asked Commissioner Fuller how we could fix this
issue. He stated the only thought he has is to spot zone the area and the Planning Commission would
not want to do that. Commissioner Fuller then explained possibly using park and rides and bus lines
to open the transit more.

Commissioner Lovato asked if it is possible to develop senior property in the same location as another
multi-family development. Steve Pastorik explained that the RM zone is needed to support the
density for the senior developments.

Brett asked if senior property is a benefit to the City. Steve explained that the four senior multi-family
developments in the area have weathered well. With the lack of kids and other heavy traffic it is easier
to keep up and has worked well for the City.

Steve Pastorik explained the code in question makes smaller properties eligible and the areas around
the TRAX stations. Commissioner Winters is in favor of increasing the acreage if there is a density
cap. Commissioner McEwen stated that the concern with the acreage increase is they will come to the
Planning Commission and ask for an exception. Steve explained a neighborhood health audit. This
audit helps track the areas that need the most assistance and if the assistance is given, what is the
overall improvement. This information is not released to the public; however, it is great information
for the City to track the trends within each neighborhood.

Commissioner Fuller asked if we could find a way to permit senior developments in another zone.
Steve stated one option is to loosen the reins on the R-1-8 zone and then put in a PUD. He isn’t sure
that the City Council would be open to that idea.

One limiting factor is that the moratorium ends in the middle of March. The City Council would need
to look at this soon or we would be pushing beyond the moratorium. Steve explained that exceptions
can be placed in a development agreement once brought back to the Planning Commission for
approval.

Chairperson Meaders stated that she feels we should not open up the other three TRAX stations to
limit the development on the west side and use the 80-foot right-of-way.

C-18-2019        PETITION BY ENTERPRISE RENT A CAR, REQUESTING A CONDITIONAL USE
                 AMENDMENT TO ADD A WASH BAY TO THE SITE. THE PROJECT IS
                 LOCATED AT 4290 WEST 3500 SOUTH IN THE C-2 (GENERAL COMMERCIAL)
                 ZONE ON 1.11 ACRES. (STAFF- JODY KNAPP AT 801-963-3497)


Discussion:

Jody presented the application stating that Enterprise Rent A Car is looking to construct a wash bay to their
site. She explained the company has been there for approximately 10 years and now want to add an
additional building on the site for a detail and wash bay. There will not be any wash equipment installed it
will just be a manual bucket wash facility. Jody explained the new building will need to have block stucco
and a flat roof to match the existing buildings on site. Jody addressed that they will need to add in some
trees and landscaping around the lot. She recommended for Enterprise to submit a landscape plan for the
entire site showing new and existing landscaping to make sure the entire site complies with the landscaping
requirements. Commissioner Lovato asked if the landscaped area should be bermed. Jody stated that it
should be, however they are not going to ask Enterprise to dig up the landscaping that is already in place.


SUBDIVISION APPLICATION
SMI-1-2020 PETITION BY YONNI HINOJO, REQUESTING A PRELIMINARY APPROVAL FOR
           THE HUNTER CREEK SUBDIVISION. THE SUBDIVISION IS ZONED R-1-8 (SINGLE
           UNIT DWELLING RESIDENTIAL, MINIMUM LOT SIZE 8,000 SQUARE FEET) AND IS
           LOCATED AT APPROXIMATELY 3658 SOUTH HAWKEYE STREET. (STAFF – STEVE
           LEHMAN ATStaff 801-963-3311)


Discussion:

Steve L. started his presentation by explaining the background development of the two current
subdivisions, , Western Acres to the north and Bridlevale at Wallace Farms to the South. Steve L.
explained the subdivision to the north had a different owner than the one to the south and the property in
question was not part of either development. Hawkeye Street never connected between 3615 S to 3675 S.
Mr. Ballenger purchased the property in question and built a home on the land. At the time he was
unwilling to deed part of the land in front of his property to the city. Therefore, the land across from Mr.
Ballenger’s home stayed vacant and Hawkeye Street did not connect. Mr. Ballenger has since passed sway
and Yonni Hinojo, the new owner, deeded the land it to the city. Steve L. stated that the property across
the street can now be developed into a subdivision and a connecting road put in.
Steve L. stated that the lot is very large and the connection between the two subdivisions will allow
through traffic. Steve L. stated that one concern is possible complaints from the residents in relation to the
through street, however there has not been any complaints thus far.
