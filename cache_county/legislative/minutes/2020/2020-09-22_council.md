DEPARTMENT OR COMMITTEE REPORTS

2020 Cache County Fair & Rodeo Report — LaMont Poulsen said this was an

emotional and a great Fair and expressed his appreciation to Scott Wilkins, Trish Gibbs,
the County Council, the County Attorney's office and the Bear River Health Department.
Financially, the rodeo did well and recognition was given to law enforcement officers and
the highlight was the ovation given the prayer and national anthem. Because of the
cancellation of many rodeos, Professional Rodeo (PR) judges in the Professional Rodeo
Cowboys Association (PRCA) were present this year. The additional night added was a
success and Poulsen hopes to continue the four-day event. Poulsen expressed
appreciation to Jill Zollinger and Kim Gardner for the many years he worked with them.

Lane Parker remarked the rodeo helps make the Fair successful. Distancing and masks
were observed in the Event Center. The staff was well prepared with COVIC
precautions. There were fewer vendors, but that was to be expected. There is still
thought going on concerning whether the carnival was missed exceedingly or not. There
was a lack of big name entertainment because the carnival sponsors those. He
estimates $40,000 may have been lost. The Junior Livestock Show had a great auction
and made more money with fewer animals.

Council member Tidwell said the first day she walked on the fairgrounds it felt right.
Parker remarked that a virus shut down rabbit shows in the entire western United States.

Council member Borup said it wasn’t reckless or careless; we worked with the Health
Department. We did what we had to do. In the barns at the Fair he saw a youth he
knows, who has challenges, totally enjoying learning how to show an animal and he
thought this is the reason we did this.

Council member Erickson noted the chatter among those contestants who came was
that the Cache County Rodeo was THE rodeo this year. They were professionally
treated; it was professionally done. Erickson feels the character of the Cache Valley
community came through this year.

Poulsen added the rodeo was under budget in expenditures and still paid out over
$150,000.00 in prize money. The Junior Livestock Auction was $91,000.00 higher than
last year with thirty-five fewer animals. He thanked the Finance Office and Human
Resources for their support as well as law enforcement’s loan of an officer and dog.
