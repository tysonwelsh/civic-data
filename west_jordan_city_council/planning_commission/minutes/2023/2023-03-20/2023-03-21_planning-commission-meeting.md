2: LivAway Suites; 7372 & 7398 South Campus View Drive; Preliminary and Final Site Plan,
Conditional Use Permit for Hotel and for building height over 40 feet, and Amended
Subdivision Plat; P-O Zone; Ensign Engineering/Quintin Elder (applicant) [#29040,
29395, 29394; parcel 21-29-152-006; 21-29-301-004]
Aaron Converse, West77 Partners, said this will be the second groundbreaking of this brand, which
they will be pushing across the country. The other location is in Tennessee. The concept is economy
extended stay aimed at people such as construction workers and misplaced families who are moving.
Each unit has a kitchen and there is a fitness center. Their company is located in Lehi, so they wanted to
include Utah and this location made sense.
Tayler Jensen said the site contains two lots in the Jordan Landing Technology Park Phase III that will
be combined. The plat already calls out the avigation easement and the setbacks and easements will
remain the same. The area is appropriate for this type of use. The Design Review Committee reviewed
the project and recommended approval of the elevations, site plan, and extended building height. The
applicant accommodated cross access for the lot to the north to aid in future development. The parking
requirement is met with 126 spaces. There are three comparable hotels in the area with heights of 51
feet, 49 feet, and four stories tall. |
Based on the analysis contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission
approve the Conditional Use Permits allowing for a Hotel and a building taller than 40’ (48’6”), a Site
Plan, and Amended subdivision for LivAway Suites, an extended stay hotel located on approximately
2.52 acres at 7372 S Campus View Drive, with the conditions and requirements of approval listed.
Conditions and Requirements of Approval

anticipated detrimental effects of this use. If the Planning Commission disagrees with the Staff's

3. Copper Rim Phase 9; Continued from 3-7-23; Approximately 6400 West 7000 South;
Major Variation to the Preliminary Master Development Plan changing the location of the
Copper Rim Phase 9 Road and its classification from a Public Road to a Private Road and
to amend the “Landscape / Special Amenity Plan” from a Tot Lot to Community Planter
Boxes; CW Land Co./Ian Cahoon (applicant) [#29564]

Tan Cahoon, CW Urban, explained the request that will change the location of the loop road to connect

to Copper Rim Drive instead of 7000 South. This is because 7000 South will become more and more

congested and the slope of the road would be problematic at 12-17%. The new location would also be

safer for pedestrians on the Mountain View Corridor trail. Changing it from a public to private road will

require less grading and will help to preserve the natural ecology of the site. The view will be better

because the height of the retaining wall will be reduced, and it will provide more usable open space.

This layout will allow for closer, on-street parking for the residents. They also request changing the

playground to a planter garden, which makes more sense near an age-targeted community.

Ray McCandless said this is considered a major variation to the development plan and will be

considered for approval by the City Council. Staff did not have any concerns about the proposal to

change the road layout or with removal of the tot lot at that location.

Staff recommended approval as requested by the applicant subject to the applicant submitting a fully

updated preliminary development plan to reflect any proposed changes recommended by the Planning

Commission.

Conditions and Requirements of Approval:

The preliminary development plan will need to be fully updated with updated graphics and text to

reflect the proposed changes as recommended by the Planning Commission before being forwarded to

the City Council.

Trish Hatch asked if there were any concerns with emergency access. |

Ray McCandless said the ring road will be eliminated and the project will be served from private

driveways off of Copper Rim Drive.

Paul Brockbank indicated from the audience that the fire department was not opposed to the change.

Ammon Allen wanted to be sure that intersection spacing was adequate and that there will be no

problems with traffic backing up on Copper Rim Drive.

Ray McCandless explained that this plan is conceptual and the intersection spacing will be reviewed

more closely at the preliminary approval.

George Winn said this road layout makes sense in directing traffic away from the more congested area,

especially with an overpass for Mountain View Corridor on 7000 South.

Ian Cahoon said the traffic study conducted by Hales Engineering did not indicate any negative effects.
