LEGISLATIVE ITEMS
4. PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on a
proposed Text Amendment to the Bluffdale City Code to comply with new
requirements set forth in House Bill 476 for proposed Development Agreements and
Subdivision Plat Reviews (Application 2024-29). City of Bluffdale, Applicant. Caitlyn
Tubbs, Staff Presenter.
Associate City Planner, Caitlyn Tubbs presented the Staff Report and stated that the Amendment
was necessary to comply with House Bill 476, which passed in the 2024 Legislative Session.
House Bill 476 includes changes to the following:

e How the City can regulate Development Agreements and require the installation of certain
improvements.
e How project construction phasing is undertaken.
e The types of documents and development restrictions the City can record on a property.
e The time limit for City review of Preliminary and Final Subdivision Plats, as well as the

e Limits on the circumstances under which cities are able to withhold Building Permits and
Certificates of Occupancy.
Ms. Tubbs reported that the Amendments set forth in Application 2024-29 did not address all of
the legislative changes and additional minor modifications would be addressed in a future
amendment. She then reviewed the changes set forth in the Amendment.
The City will require Development Agreements when project-wide improvements are not
contained within one construction phase. For example, if a larger subdivision will have parks or
other open-space amenities and they want to construct the subdivision in phases, the City is able
to require a Development Agreement to ensure that those amenities are installed even if they are
not included in the first phase.
Language has been added to define when substantive reviews occur. The City is now limited to
four rounds of reviews, so they want to be very clear about what does and does not constitute a
substantive review. For example, applicants often come in for Concept Plan reviews and staff
provides advice in person or via phone or email. The language was added to specify that those
items do not constitute a substantive review.
When Subdivision Plats are reviewed, the Subdivision Improvement Plan is also reviewed. It
includes road rights-of-way, street cross-sections, and installation of utilities, curb, gutter, and
sidewalk. The City can now only require the Subdivision Improvement Plan at the preliminary
and final levels. The Amendment specifies that the Subdivision Improvement Plan is required at
the Final Plat level. However, if the Planning Commission believed it would better serve the City

to review those elements earlier in the process, that language could be adjusted to indicate that it
would be reviewed at the preliminary level. In response to a question from Chair Cragun,
Ms. Tubbs clarified that typically applicants run their Preliminary and Final Plats concurrently, so
they are reviewed at the same time. Staff does not believe the process will change substantially,
but they are now required to specify the level at which the Subdivision Improvement Plan is
required.
Minor changes to grammar and punctuation were also included in the Amendment. A future
Amendment will include language around allowable features in regard to residential rear yard
setbacks.
Notice was published as required by State Code, and no responses were received. Staff
recommended that the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City
Council.
In response to a question raised by Commissioner Luker, Ms. Tubbs clarified that the decision to
require the Subdivision Improvement Plan at the time of the Final Plat as opposed to the
Preliminary Plat was simply a matter of preference. The updates were drafted by a consulting
attorney who indicated that the majority of other cities he has worked with are keeping the reviews
at the Final Plat level, but approximately 10% elected to move them to the Preliminary Plat level.
Because the two applications are commonly run concurrently, it is a matter of preference. The
review is a fairly rigorous process, so it is uncommon to see modification requests after approval.
Chair Cragun opened the public hearing.
Dwight Nielson asked what constitutes a subdivision. Ms. Stubbs clarified that it is defined by
both State Statute and City Code. A subdivision occurs when a piece of property is subdivided
into more than one parcel.
There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.
Commissioner Swanson moved to forward a POSITIVE recommendation to the City
Council for the Text Amendment, Application 2024-29, based on the following Findings:
