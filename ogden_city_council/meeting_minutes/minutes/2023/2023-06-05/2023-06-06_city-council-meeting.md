Proposed Resolution 2023-15 scheduling a public hearing to allow public
input regarding proposed transfers from the City’s Utility Enterprise Funds
to the General Fund ,
A memo from Council staff regarding a proposed annual transfer from the City’s enterprise funds to the General Fund
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated as required by Utah State Code §10-6-135.5, a city that is proposing
to transfer funds from an enterprise fund to another fund for a purpose not directly related to the goods or services provided by the
enterprise fund must provide notice of the transfers to the users of the enterprise fund. The city must also hold a public hearing on
the transfers prior to adoption or the amendment of the city’s budget. In Ogden, a budgetary transfer from the City’s Water,
Sanitary Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse Utility enterprise funds to the General Fund has been done for many years; however, the
requirement to provide notice of the transfers and to hold a separate public hearing is a recent change to State Law. These utility
funds are accounted for as enterprise funds in Ogden and are operated with user fees charged for goods and services. In the
proposed Fiscal Year 2024 Budget, the City is proposing a transfer from these utility funds into the City’s General Fund to act as
the collection of franchise and property tax. In order to comply with state law requirements, a public hearing on the proposed
transfers is scheduled for June 20, 2023.
Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and presented a spreadsheet illustrating the proposed transfer
amounts from utility funds to the General Fund; the proposed transfers total $6,360,725. He also presented an example of the letter
that will be mailed to each utility customer in the City to advertise the public hearing regarding the proposed transfers.

A memo from the Fire Chief came before the Council to consider a resolution extending the State of Emergency declared
by Mayor Caldwell on May 2, 2023. The memo stated the proposal is to extend the state of emergency to August 15, 2023 to
provide time to deal with remaining runoff and implement a recovery phase.

Fire Chief Mathieu summarized the memo and stated the declaration of a local emergency provides a legal basis for
requesting and obtaining County, State or Federal government disaster assistance. The declaration also activates the response and
recovery aspects of the emergency operations plan and authorizes the City to furnish or receive aid and assistance under existing
mutual aid agreements. The Emergency Management Team recommends extending the emergency declaration until August 15,
2023. The City’s response to the flooding has exceeded the existing capacity of the services, personnel, equipment, supplies, and
facilities of the City. Because of the record snowpack and additional rainfall in the region, the threat of severe and imminent
flooding continues. The team projects that extending the state of emergency to August 15, 2023, will provide the necessary time to
deal with the remainder of the Spring runoff and implement a recovery phase.

aeronautical commercial. Tax increment generated at the Airport will be used for utility upgrades; roadway upgrades and
installation; building construction and upgrades; direct incentive for jobs/development; taxiway upgrades; and other Airport
expenses. The public benefits to be created by the CRA include:

e Future increased tax revenues

e Economic stimulation

* Up to $300-400 million investment potential
e Short term construction employment

¢ Direct employment at higher wages

e Direct purchase of supplies and services

e Economic diversification

* Hill Air Force Base (HAFB) support

Mr. Cooper summarized the process for selecting participants in the CRA; preference will be given to owners and
commercial airport lessees to undertake improvements or participate with developers. The Agency may encourage others to

Proposed Dinosaur Park Fee Amendments. The proposed amendments are being requested on behalf of the Ogden
Dinosaur Park and Museum Foundation. Any fee increases will not increase the City’s revenues but will help the Foundation

Proposed Water Utility Hookup and Service Line Fee Amendments. The proposed amendments are intended to
implement the consumer price index increases that have been established with the utility charges for services and to establish a fee
to service frozen water lines. :
Public Utility Manager Herd explained the Water Utility Division is proposing changes to water hookup fees and
proposing to establish a fee for thawing frozen water lines. Utility Enterprise Fund Consumer Price Index (CPI) calculation. As
part of the utility rates ordinance adopted in 2012, each of the four utilities included in the study was to have an annual increase
based on a set consumer price index. When the initial ordinance was adopted in 2012, the CPI was calculated using a Wells Fargo
CPI calculation. In 2015, a change was made to use the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics - Western Region calculation. While the
CPI calculations used since 2012 have helped to capture a general consumer price index increase, in 2021 the Administration
proposed what it considered a more accurate method. The annual increase would be a combination of two different indices. For
operations and maintenance, the western region CPI produced by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics would be used, and for
capital expenses, a Construction Cost Index (CCI) would be used. The two indices would then be blended to produce the increase
for that fiscal year. The two rates together would constitute the Blended Inflation Adjustment (BIA). The proposed split between
' the CPI and CCI impacts the Water Utility budget most significantly. While the other utilities have a different ratio of operations
and maintenance expenses (reflected with the CPI) to capital costs (reflected with the CCI), the Water Utility is the ratio used to
calculate the annual BIA increase. The BIA applies to Water, Sanitary Sewer and Storm Sewer. Because the Refuse utility does
not generally have capital expenditures, the CPI is used for the annual increase for that utility. The CCI index is the American city
and county January year over year index. The CPI remains as the western region BLS January year over year index. The proposed
fee amendments include an annual inflationary increase based on the operations CPI (BLS western region calculation). Since these
fees have not been included in the annual increases, Administration is proposing a large increase now to catch up to what the fees .
would have been if the CPI had been applied since 2012 along with the charges for services increases. The proposed one-time
increase is 35.7%. The fees will be increased by the CPI annually beginning July 1 each subsequent year. The CP] applied to the
Utilities’ charges for services beginning July 1, 2023 is 6.20 percent with the CCI at 6.70 percent.

Comptroller Stout then reported the City has received the proposed Certified Tax Rate from Weber County; the rate is
.002239, which is a decrease from the prior year rate of .002267. This rate will generate $19,056,331, with new growth to generate ( ;
an additional $578,941 in tax revenue. This reflects the fact that growth is slowing in Ogden as projected. If the City would have
maintained the current tax rate, which would have been advertised as a property tax increase, the City would have captured an
additional $245,551 in property tax revenue. She then presented a spreadsheet identifying Administration’s property tax revenue
projections and a comparison with actual revenues to be captured with the Certified Tax Rate; Administration’s estimates were
approximately $300,000 higher than actual revenues.

Discussion among the Council and staff centered on opportunities for the Council to learn about the formula and factors
that contribute to the calculation of the Certified Tax Rate; ranking of CIP projects and communication of the total amount of any
given CIP project that will be funded in the upcoming fiscal year or future fiscal years; and the scope and status of the Lester Park
project.

Council Business

Damaged Trees in Public Right of Way: Council member Blair asked for guidance from City staff for residents who
have experienced broken or damaged trees in the public right of way. Public Services Director Lowder advised the Council to
encourage residents to contact the City’s Parks Division, who will dispatch the City Forester to address the matter.

County Redevelopment Areas and Community Development Areas: Chair Choberka reported Council leadership has

invited the Weber County Community and Economic Development Director to meet with the Council in the near future to discuss
their changes to the County’s process regarding the creation of redevelopment areas and community development areas.

Central Weber Sewer District Board: Council member Hyer noted he learned last night at the Central Weber Sewer
District Board meeting that the District will be holding a Truth in Taxation hearing this year and he will need to make a
presentation to the City Council regarding the purpose of the proposed tax increase. He asked for an agenda item before the
August 12 District hearing.

Council Calendar: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reviewed the Council’s meeting calendar for the remainder of
the month of June and discussed various upcoming agenda items.

The meeting adjourned at 5:21 p.m.
