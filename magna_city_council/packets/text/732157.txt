II.      STATEMENT OF FACTS

        On March 31, 2010, Salt Lake County approved the CUP for the Property, with a number of
terms and conditions outlining the number of cars that could be parked or stored on the Property, where
cars could be located on the Property, fencing requirements, and other limitations. See Ex. B.

         In 2013, the Property was out of compliance with the applicable Salt Lake County Code. Salt
Lake County filed suit against the Property’s owners, Donnie Sweazey and Dawn Sweazey (collectively
the “Sweazeys”) in the Third District Court, Case No. 110917138. On September 16, 2013, the Third
District Court granted an order to Salt Lake County giving Salt Lake County the right to enter and abate
the Property in order to bring it into complete compliance with Salt Lake County Code (“2013 Order”).
The 2013 Order is attached as Exhibit C. The 2013 Order also included a monetary judgment against
the Sweazeys in the amount of $23,600 for accrued civil fines. See Ex. C. To this date, the $23,600 in
civil fines have not been paid.

         On December 3, 2020, after inspecting the Property and finding it in violation of the CUP and
other applicable municipal code provisions, Magna code enforcement officials sent the Sweazeys a
Notice of Violation (the “Notice of Violation”), attached as Exhibit D. The Notice of Violation detailed
that the Property was out of compliance as there were more than the maximum allowed vehicles and
open storage or tires, car parts, and other items. See Ex. D.

         Specifically, the Notice of Violation provided notice of the following violations:

              1. The CUP allows the storage of a maximum of 7 vehicles on the South Side of the
                 Building. More vehicles than allowed are being stored at this location.

              2. The CUP allows the storage of a maximum of 12 vehicles on the West Side of the
                 Building. More vehicles than allowed are being stored at this location.

              3. Open storage of tires, car parts, and other items visible from the street and/or above the
                 fence line in violation of CUP.

              4. Storage of vehicles on the Property in excess of the number allowed by the CUP.

              5. Vehicles stored on the public street in violation of the CUP.

See Ex. D at 2. The Notice of Violation provided the Sweazeys until January 26, 2021 to abate the
violations listed above.

        On March 15, 2021, Magna and the Sweazeys entered into a s settlement agreement
(“Settlement Agreement”) granting the Sweazeys additional time to bring the property into
compliance. A copy of the Settlement Agreement is attached as Exhibit E. Specifically, the Settlement
Agreement extended the deadline for Sweazeys to bring the Property into compliance to April 26, 2021,
giving the Sweazeys three additional months to bring the property into compliance. See Ex. E. Further,
under the terms of the Settlement Agreement, the civil fines continued to accrue in the amount of
$200.00 per day but were held in abeyance. See Exhibit E at ¶¶ 6-7.

        On May 3, 2021, after the compliance deadline passed, Magna’s code enforcement officers
inspected the Property and observed that the Sweazeys failed to bring the Property into compliance as
required by the terms of the Settlement Agreement and the CUP. The existing violations are detailed in
the inspection report (the “Inspection Report”) enclosed as Exhibit F.

        Before issuing this Notice of Hearing, Magna’s code enforcement officers, once again inspected
the Property on June 10, 2021. Magna’s code enforcement officers observed that the Sweazeys had
actually brought more vehicles onto the Property since the Inspection Report on May 3, 2021, putting
the Property into further noncompliance. Photos taken of the Property on June 10, 2021 are attached as
Exhibit G.

III.         GROUNDS FOR SUSPENSION OR REVOCATION

        As part of the May 3, 2021 inspection, code enforcement officers observed the following
violations of the CUP:

         1. CUP paragraph 1 states that if tires are stored in the north storage area “the tires (or other
combustible materials must be located a minimum of 3 feet from the property / fence line and may not
be stacked higher than 6 feet.” See Ex. B. In the Inspection Report, code enforcement officers noted
that the north storage area was completely full of tires and car part, over capacity, no walkway between
the tires, and stacked so high it was pushing the fence in the rear lot. See Ex. F.

         2. CUP paragraph 4 states that only 6 vehicles are allowed other than those being displayed
“for sale.” See Ex. B. In the Inspection Report, code enforcement officers noted 8 vehicles stored at the
daytime location and none are displayed as being for sale. See Ex. F.

        3. CUP paragraph 4 states that all vehicles at the daytime location must be licensed. See Ex. B.
In the Inspection Report, code enforcement officers noted that none of the vehicles at the daytime
location are licensed. See Ex. F.

        4. CUP paragraph 4 states that an additional 2 vehicles may be parked along the east fence line
provided they are parallel to the fence. See Ex. B. In the Inspection Report, code enforcement officers
noted 5 cars parallel parked on the east fence line. See Ex. F.

         5. CUP paragraph 5 states that 7 vehicles that are fully operable and ready to be sold may be
displayed on the south side of the property. See Ex. B. In the Inspection Report, code enforcement
officers noted 8 vehicles stored on the south side of the Property. See Ex. F.

        6. CUP paragraph 5 states that 12 vehicles that are fully operable and ready to be sold may be
displayed on may be stored on the west side of the Property. See Ex. B. In the Inspection Report, code
enforcement officers noted 12 vehicles storing junk on the west side of the Property. See Ex. F.

        7. CUP paragraph 5 states that an additional 5 compact vehicles can be stored against the north
fence. See Ex. B. In the Inspection Report, code enforcement officers noted 2 compact vehicles, 1 large
van, 1 small truck storing junk in the rear, and 1 large tow truck. See Ex. F.

    V.        Staff Recommendation

        Code Enforcement Officials provided notice on December 3, 2020 of numerous violations of
the CUP. Despite this notice and a three-month extension to bring the Property into compliance, the
violations of the CUP have not been remedied. Additionally, the May 3, 2021, inspection revealed that
3 of the 13 CUP Conditions are in violation. Subsequent inspections have revealed that additional
vehicles have now been brought to the Property. Accordingly, if the Property cannot be brought into
compliance after approximately 6 months, it appears the Sweazeys are either unable or unwilling to
bring the Property into compliance. In either event, the continued violation of the conditions of the CUP
warrant review by the Planning Commission and Staff recommends that the Planning Commission
revoke the CUP for failure to bring the Property into compliance.
