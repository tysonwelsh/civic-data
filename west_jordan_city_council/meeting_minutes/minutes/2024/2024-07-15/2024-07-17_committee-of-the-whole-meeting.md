 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
      a. Presentation of Citywide Survey Results – 2024
         Kyrene Gibb with Y2 Analytics presented results of the 2024 citywide survey. She said the
         overall quality of life statistics were consistent with 2023 results with a 76 out of 100
         overall quality of life rating, and 41% of respondents indicating they believed the City was
         heading in the right direction. Ms. Gibb noted the next most common response was that
         residents did not know or were unsure. She reported a good response representation
         across the City. Respondents indicated they liked the City’s location in Salt Lake Valley, the
         sense of community, and felt the City was well maintained overall. Ms. Gibb said most
         concerns expressed by respondents hinged around growing pains.

         The Council discussed results concerning Code Enforcement with Ms. Gibb. Council
         Member Shelton suggested digging into specific resident issues with Code Enforcement
         through future survey questions. Council Member Green wondered if respondents were
         upset with Code Enforcement because it was too strict or too loose.

         Council Member Bloom arrived at 6:20 pm.

         Council Member Green questioned why satisfaction with trails was so low in 2024 survey
         responses. Ms. Gibb noted that the trail response was the only statistically significant
         decrease from the 2023 survey, and there was not a clear indicator as to the reason from
         open-ended responses. Council Member Green expressed the opinion that the Council was
         wasting money if survey results were not used and considered in decision making. He
         suggested the Council try to do something about the responses that indicated residents did
         not feel a sense of community. Council Member Whitelock felt the Council made decisions

      b. 2024 Transportation Master Plan Update
         Public Works Director Brian Clegg introduced Jeremey Searle, Transportation and
         Planning Manager with Wall Consultant Group (WCG), who presented a 2024
         Transportation Master Plan Update, and answered questions from the Council. Mr. Searle
         showed the regional Travel Demand Model provided by Wasatch Front Regional Council,
         and spoke of planning for new growth. He felt the biggest transportation challenge in West
         Jordan was east/west travel and discussed options as well as planned road projects with
         the Council. Mr. Searle listed benefits of round-abouts versus traffic-signal intersections.

         Chair Jacob mentioned that resident concerns had been expressed about plans for
         intersections on the west end of 9000 South. Staff said the project was currently at the
         environmental study phase. Chair Jacob expressed a desire to explore alternatives for the
         design of the 9000 South/Duck Creek and 9000 South/New Bingham intersections. Council
         Member Green agreed. Mr. Searle noted that options considered were included in the
         Feasibility Study available for review. He commented that some intersection options were
         more expensive than others. Council Member Whitelock expressed concern about the cost
         of the planned projects.

         Mr. Clegg expressed a desire to bring the Transportation Master Plan Update back for
         Council approval as soon as possible. Council Member Green mentioned the river bridge
         crossing at 7800 South, and he wanted to make sure the planned project provided an
         improvement as a gateway to the City. He asked if Council expectations (aesthetics,
         walkability) for some projects could be mentioned in the update. Mayor Burton agreed
         with comments to improve the aesthetics as well as the traffic in the gateway area. Council
         Member Whitelock believed that while traffic engineers understood how to manage traffic,
         the Council was looking at the project from a community perspective. She requested a
         variety of options for the project to allow the Council to consider more than just traffic
         efficiency.

      c. Discussion regarding the Active Transportation Master Plan as related to Arterials
         and Collectors
         Council Member Whitelock said the federal government considered bicycles to be vehicles.
         She questioned whether the streets were the safest place for bicycles, mentioning that

         Amsterdam had made a conscious decision to welcome cyclists on streets, and asked what
         the Council wanted long-term for West Jordan. Council Member Bloom commented that
         Burbank, California was transitioning sidewalks to a mixture of bikes and pedestrians,
         which she felt created a friendly and safer environment. The Council and staff discussed
         bike lane options, and the need to consider snow removal.

         Chair Jacob believed there were several types of cyclists, and asked if they were all lumped
         into the same category. Council Member Shelton commented that ebikes made cycling
         more accessible to a greater part of the community and would like to build better
         infrastructure for bicycles by increasing width of sidewalks on main arterials, with the
         thought that more bicycles would translate to fewer vehicles on the road. Mayor Burton
         noted that plans for 9000 South included bike lanes. Council Member Whitelock clarified
         that bike lanes would be improved along arterial roads in conjunction with needed road
         projects.

         Council Member Green spoke of the cost involved with such improvements on east arterials
         in the City. He referred to bike lanes included in the planned 1300 West project, and asked
         if the Council Members were proposing to drop the bike lanes from the project and increase
         sidewalk width. Council Members Bloom and Whitelock confirmed they were proposing
         increased sidewalk width over bike lanes. Public Works Director Clegg noted the necessity
         of purchasing more right of way. Council Member Shelton emphasized the need to consider
         snow removal as part of the discussion. Council Member Green noted that residents would
         have an increased amount of sidewalk to plow if sidewalks were widened.

         Council Member Bloom reiterated that she wanted to include the suggested ideas with new
         construction to determine if widening sidewalks along certain arterials for cyclists was
         feasible. She believed road congestion was increasing and wanted an additional way to
         commute. Council Member Green expressed concern with land acquisition cost, and
         wanted to make sure the cost was not transferred to taxpayers. Senior Traffic Engineer
         Nestor Gallo answered questions about UDOT cross section requirements, and pointed out
         that sidewalks were designed for pedestrians and individuals with disabilities. Mr. Gallo
         listed many factors that needed to be considered. He spoke of funding available to the City
         for active transportation and trail studies.

         Mr. Gallo spoke of differing active transportation maps between area entities, and reported
         that he invited representatives of UDOT, Wasatch Front Regional Council, and Salt Lake
         County to meet at City Hall to discuss the maps and start creating loops and connectivity.
         Council Member Bloom suggested including cyclists in the community in those
         conversations.

         Council Member Green expressed the opinion that providing trail connections between
         some neighborhoods should be a higher priority than striping bike lanes on some streets,
         and asked staff to look at priorities on the Active Transportation Master Plan to see if there
         were some smaller projects that could be completed. He suggested the Parks Master Plan
         and the Active Transportation Master Plan should be synced.

      d. Discussion concerning Senior Housing overlay zoning and other overlay zoning
         amendment procedures
         Responding to a question from Council Member Whitelock, Community Development
         Director Scott Langford confirmed that if an overlay were removed from a property, the
         property would be left with the underlying zone. Chair Jacob believed an application for a
         rezone with an overlay was two different applications – the Council needed to determine
         if they were comfortable with both the new underlying zone and the overlay. Council
         Member Green suggested the Council should not consider applications for a rezone with
         an overlay.

         Council Member Bloom believed the proposed amendment was what the Council was
         looking for. Council Member Whitelock agreed as long as an application could not include
         both an underlying zone change and an overlay. Council Members Green and Bloom
         expressed agreement. A majority of the Council indicated that staff could bring the
         amendment back for action including the change discussed.

      e. Discussion of policy related to West Jordan City Code Title 14 Subdivision Regulations
         and Title 15 Permit Processing regarding changes to the Subdivision Development
         Review Process to comply with House Bill 476
         Senior Planner Tayler Jensen presented proposed Code amendments to comply with HB
         476. The City may not require subdivision improvement plans to be submitted at both
         Preliminary and Final Subdivision; initial reviews must be completed within 15 business
         days; Final Subdivision review must be completed within 20 business days; the City may
         not require more than four total review cycles for a subdivision. Council Member Whitelock
         suggested the language should include a reference to the State Code.

         Staff answered questions from the Council. Council Office Director Alan Anderson said the
         next step was for the proposed amendments to be reviewed by the Planning Commission.

       f. Discussion regarding August meeting schedule
          Council Office Director Alan Anderson outlined the August meeting schedule, with August
          13th scheduled as the Truth in Taxation meeting, and August 14th scheduled as a regular
          City Council meeting. He asked if the Council wanted to continue with separate days or
          hold a City Council meeting after the Truth in Taxation meeting on August 13th. He pointed
          out that the August 14th agenda currently included approval of the final budget.

           time for the Council to consider public comments. Council Member Lamb said the Council

           related and would email more details to the Council.
