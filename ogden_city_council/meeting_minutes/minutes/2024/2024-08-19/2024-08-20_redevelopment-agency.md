Proposed Resolution 2024-4 adopting the plan amendment to the Ogden
Bend Redevelopment Master Plan
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed Ogden Bend Master Plan
updates came before the Board for consideration. The memo stated the proposed amendment is meant to address changes in the
neighborhood as well as changing market conditions; several changes have occurred in the plan area since the master plan was
adopted. The changes are that one-quarter of the area has been developed, that certain areas are not likely to redevelop soon, and
that some property ownership has changed.
         Planning Director Brierley summarized the memo and summarized the recommended changes to the Plan as follows:
               1. Move part of the planned park from the corner of 18th Street and Wall Avenue to Grant Avenue and Park Street.
               2. Plan for a single-family residential development east of Lincoln Avenue and north of Park Street.
               3. Provide greater flexibility in building types and locations.
               4. Update the concept plan to reflect current development and make minor adjustments to layout.
         Mr. Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide images of the Plan area and to expound on the
implications of the proposed Plan amendments. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the
meeting.
         Acting Chair White asked if the public trails/sidewalks in the Plan area will remain open to the public if the proposed
changes are approved, to which Mr. Brierley answered yes.
         Board member Myers asked if the owner of the property located along Wall Avenue will be limited in their ability to
build apartment buildings in that area. Mr. Brierley stated that the Planning Commission has spent a great deal of time evaluating
the areas of the City where apartments are appropriate, and they feel that this Plan area is appropriate for apartments given easy
access to open spaces and gathering areas. The Planning Commission has recommended against apartments along corridors where
those same conditions are not present. Board member Myers asked if the development of the walking trails will be the
responsibility of the developer, to which Mr. Brierley answered yes, but noted that the improvements will be completed in future
phases because the entire Plan area will not develop at the same time.
           Acting Chair White asked if the minimum parking area is being adjusted in the Plan area. Mr. Brierley stated there will
still be requirements for a minimum number of parking spaces for the residential and commercial uses in the Plan area.
           Board member Myers asked if the undeveloped area to the west of Mountain America Credit Union is being controlled
by a developer and if there are plans to develop that area soon. Mr. Brierley stated he is unaware of any immediate development
plans for that area, but it is owned by a developer.
           Acting Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2024-4, entitled:
                    “A resolution of the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency Board adopting the Plan amendment to the Ogden
                    Bend Redevelopment Master Plan.”
           Acting Chair White then called for public input regarding the proposal.
           Laura Lewis asked if the City has enough apartment units at the present time to meet the demand for that type of
residential unit.
           Clint Stanger stated that he does not feel it is ideal to build the apartment units around the public park area; people
visiting a park do not want to look at tall buildings surrounding it. A similar concept was approved and built near the old
Slackwater building along the Ogden River and the apartment buildings changed the experience in that area; that type of use does
not enhance the outdoor experience. He then asked why the public park has not been developed since the amenity was included in
the original plan that was approved in 2011.
           Pieder Beeli referenced the property in the Plan area that is owned by Ogden City and noted that the City owns several
properties and businesses that compete with private enterprise. These properties should be made available to the private market,
which would benefit the taxpayers of Ogden City. He is concerned about the utilization of Ogden resources to compete with the
private sector. When the City engages in a transaction, it is different than a private citizen engaging in a transaction. This lack of
equivalence is lost on the Board.
           There were no further persons appearing to be heard.
           Acting Chair White invited Mr. Brierley to answer the questions regarding the housing inventory in the community. Mr.
Brierley stated that the Ogden Bend Master Plan is a mixed-use master plan and there is a desire for a variety of housing types in
the Plan area, including single family homes. There are other projects in the area that also include a housing element, but not
single-family homes and that is why it is important to designate an area in the Plan area for single family homes. He stated that if
the City were to designate the City property on 18th Street and Wall Avenue for single family homes, there would be several
consequences to doing so. The City would need to secure a different development partner because Lotus Development is not in
that business. Additionally, a corner property is not ideal for single family homes.
           Acting Chair White asked Mr. Brierley to address the uses surrounding the public park space. Mr. Brierley stated that
most of the development abutting the park will be single family homes, with apartment units on the other side of Grant Avenue.
Acting Chair White asked Mr. Brierley why the City has not yet developed the park. Mr. Brierley stated that the City owns a
portion of the property, but the remainder of the property designated as park area is still owned by other individuals and entities.
The City needs to purchase those properties before moving forward with the park development.
           Board member Choberka asked if the City could pursue infill development on the property it owns rather than partnering
with Lotus Development. Mr. Brierley stated that would be the option if a developer agreement cannot be reached; this would
make it possible to build single family homes. Executive Director Nadolski added that Lotus Development has expressed a
willingness to build single family homes in other areas of the City, but they have been vocal in their desires to build multi-family
buildings in this area.
           ON A MOTION BY BOARD MEMBER HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS,
RESOLUTION 2024-4 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – BOARD
MEMBERS BLAIR, CHOBERKA, HYER, MYERS, AND ACTING CHAIR WHITE. VOTING NO – NONE. Board
member Graf and Chair Richey were not present when this vote was taken.
