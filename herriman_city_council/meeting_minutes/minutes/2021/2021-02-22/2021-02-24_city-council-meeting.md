    2.1. Rosecrest PUD amendment discussion - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy previewed the project, and explained the applicant had submitted a request to
amend an existing MDA, which he said was an option within the current MDA. His aim was to understand
what information the City Council wanted staff to research so they would be prepared to make a decision on
the amendment.

City Planner Maloy offered an overview of the history of the Master Development Agreement and its respective
amendments. He stated on June 26, 2000, the original agreement had been established with 1,000 acres and
564 planned dwelling units. He said it was later amended in 2008 when an additional 4,000 acres were added
and an increase to the number of dwelling units. The increase had been due to a de-annexation from property
which had come into Herriman, and not all of the property annexed by Herriman had become a part of the
MDA at the time, which he noted would be part of the discussion as there was now a request to incorporate
the additional property within Herriman City limits into the MDA. He further explained there had been a
second amendment which had been approved in 2017, to clarify how density would be transferred between
certain pods. During this time, the City had also agreed to remove 28 acres of the project area from the MDA
into a separate MDA. City Planner Maloy stated this would be the fourth amendment proposed by the
development partner, and it would incorporate approximately 169 acres located south and would allow an
additional 219 dwelling units. He mentioned this MDA was interlaced with another agreement that the City
had with Wasatch Commercial. He also noted the MDA would expire in December of 2039, and this expiration
term was not proposed to be amended. The proposed amendment also took into consideration the
realignment of the collector roads that were originally anticipated, as over time they had realigned future
interchanges with Mountain View Corridor, and they felt it appropriate to align the road with a larger network
of roads.

    2.2. Discussion pertaining to the Harmony Woods circle right-of-way - Jonathan Bowers, City
          Engineer
City Engineer Jonathan Bowers reported they had received a phone call from a resident who lived in a
subdivision called Harmony Woods, located at the end of 7300 West. He displayed a map for context of the
resident's cul-de-sac, which was in a four-lot subdivision. The phone call had been in regards to what the City’s
responsibility was for maintenance of the roads. He said research had been conducted, and concluded the
roads in question were dedicated public rights-of-way, and so they were on the regular schedule for the snow
plows and general maintenance, although the maintenance was different in that location because they were
gravel roads and not paved. He indicated it was a 50-foot-wide right-of-way, and noted there was a portion of
the utilized road that fell outside of the public right-of-way. City Attorney Chase Andrizzi did not anticipate
this to be a huge risk from a legal standpoint. City Engineer Bowers pointed out the right-of-way extended to
the south, but there was not much out that way. Mayor Watts if the area to the south was the only public
access to the property, and City Engineer Bowers responded in the negative. He also added the area did not
have any storm drains, public water system, or a sewer system installed. As well, there was no asphalt, curbs
or gutters. He said the piece to the south had been anticipated for the 7300 West alignment, and showed it
was only nominally maintained. He pointed out the nearest fire hydrant from the lot was over 600 feet away,
although the current requirement was a home be no more than 500 feet away from a fire hydrant.

City Engineer Bowers outlined the Council’s options. Option one would be to offer no action, and leave the
infrastructure and property in its currently orientation. The second option was to construct a roadway and
utility improvements that were in line with Herriman City standards, and their final option was to vacate the
public right-of-way and leave the ownership and maintenance responsibilities to the surrounding property
owners.

City Engineer Bowers then offered various funding options if the City decided to construct the infrastructure
improvements. They could take money from the General Fund, but he added that the area was not eligible to

    2.3. Discussion of a proposed boundary adjustment with South Jordan - Jonathan Bowers, City
         Engineer
City Engineer Bowers presented a proposal he had received from the South Jordan Engineering Department
for a boundary adjustment. He referenced a map to orient the Council to the location of the proposed
adjustment. He explained the reason for the adjustment, and said the South Jordan staff reported the purpose
was to make the maintenance more equitable, and to put the abandonment of the old 11800 South alignment
under the control of one city, rather than having it split. It was clarified that 11800 South would become a
part of South Jordan.

City Engineer Bowers said they had water lines which went into that area, so they would need to ensure right-
of-way would be maintained to those lines in order to continue operations. He had also spoken with the
Herriman Police Department, who felt the adjustment created ambiguity and was inefficient for public safety
since there would be a shared intersection between Herriman and South Jordan. Deputy Chief Cody
Stromberg elaborated the idea was to move farther west, and go to the other side. He felt it created problems
when there were multiple jurisdictions responsible for one area. City Engineer Bowers also explained Herriman
City was required to maintain the back side of a development which touched the boundary line, and
recommended if the Council did decide to entertain the adjustment, they would need to ensure they would
keep the property in Herriman.

Mayor Watts relayed his opinion the corner of Mountain View Corridor was a valuable piece of commercial
property, which would be split down the middle by a City boundary line. City Engineer Bowers interjected to
indicate Jordan Valley did have a water line and several other utilities that ran through the land, although this
would not inhibit development. Mayor Watts thought South Jordan might want to make the piece of property
more valuable so they could sell it to a developer. Based on the comments from Deputy Chief Cody Stromberg,
he thought the City should not authorize the boundary adjustment as there would be no benefit to Herriman
City. The Council agreed, and Councilmember Ohrn commented she was confused by South Jordan’s
reasoning to adjust the boundary. They further discussed which whether or not it made sense for the boundary
adjustment since there were no cost savings for them, and they did not feel that South Jordan had a compelling
reason to make the adjustment.
