       1.     GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT AND REZONE, 4.17 ACRES AT 12747
              SOUTH SUNDAY DRIVE. CURRENT ZONE RR-22, REQUESTED ZONE OF
              R-3. APPLICANTS, SHANE AND AMBER PETERMAN.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the matter was
noticed as a public hearing with 395 notices sent out. A property rendering was displayed.
The property is currently zoned RR-22 (Rural Residential .50-acre lots with animal rights).
The original application was a request for R-2 or R-3 zoning, both of which require a General
Plan Amendment to low-density residential. Mr. Prestwich reported that after the item was
noticed, the applicant approached staff, and based on the feedback, she preferred the R-2
zone for 19,000 square-foot lots with no animal rights. Surrounding designations were
reviewed. He noted that the Cottages at Western Springs includes an SD designation. Part
of that approval required the properties to be one-half acre or larger to match what exists to
the north. He noted that no public comments were received.

Commissioner Cannon asked if the project is being platted as a full subdivision or private
lane and if they are able to give a zoning recommendation eliminating the animal rights.
Mr. Prestwich was unsure of the applicant’s intent but given the constraints, he assumed
that the applicant would seek the private lane and return to the Commission for a decision.

In response to a concern regarding animal rights, Mr. Prestwich reported that everyone has
a right to household pets in a residential zone. Lots in the R-1 and R-2 zones that are one-
half acre or larger are allowed to have farm animals. These may include two large animals,
five medium-sized animals, 10 small animals, or a combination thereof.

Lynn Broadhead stated that he owns property on the adjacent northeast corner. He
expressed opposition to the proposed rezoning. His property is zoned for animal rights and
he was frustrated with the proposed being developed without tearing down the original home
and barn to allow access.

Garrett Lindell, a property owner to the south, requested that the zoning remain as-is. He
expressed concern with access adjacent to his rear fence.

The applicant, Amber Peterman, stated that they intend to build homes for their three
daughters on the property. She confirmed that the existing barn will be demolished. In her
effort to be a good neighbor, the zoning request was changed from one-third acre R-3 zoning
to one-half acre RR-22 zoning. She encouraged kindness when others are considering her
request.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Lefgren asked for clarity regarding fencing requirements between a property
with animal rights and one without. Mr. Prestwich stated that there will be masonry fencing
requirement with no impact to the property with existing animal rights if a subdivision is
developed after the fact. He confirmed that the proposed zoning could potentially include
eight one-half acre lots.

Commissioner Cannon believed that the existing home with the power lines will encompass
over one acre of the property and the maximum number of lots would be four or five.

Chair Rushton asked about the zoning of the parcel that is west of the canal and south of
12600 South. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that the property is zoned SD and must include a
right turn lane. Additional units could not be added off of the lane. Valuation of a property
with animal rights versus a property without was discussed.

Commissioner Lefgren moved to recommend APPROVAL of PLZ-21-4005 Rezone and
General Plan Amendment of 4.17 acres of land located at 12747 South Sunday Drive
thus changing the General Plan to Low-Density Residential and changing the zone to
R-2. Commissioner Cannon seconded the motion. The motion passed with the
unanimous consent of the Commission.

       4.     ZONING ORDINANCE CHANGE, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING
              CHANGES TO TITLE 18 REGARDING RESIDENTIAL CHICKENS.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above matter involves a City-
initiated zoning ordinance change to Title 18 pertaining to residential chickens. The RR-22
zone allows for farm animals and requires a one-half acre lot to acquire animal rights. Lots
smaller than one-half acre may keep up to six chickens but no roosters. Mr. Prestwich
explained that the ordinance change would allow residents in an RR-22 zone with less than
one-half acre to acquire a rooster with the chickens. This does not expand for the allowance
of large animals but specifies that lots zoned RR-22 that are smaller than one-half acre but
large enough to be eligible for a Certificate of Occupancy to keep up to six chickens and one
rooster. It does not include other birds, ducks, geese, or fowl. Staff recommended approval.

Commissioner Cannon expressed concern about it becoming a noise issue. Mr. Prestwich
confirmed that staff occasionally receives noise complaints but explained that the request is
compatible since the surrounding neighbors have the same zoning and animal rights to allow
for roosters. He stated that only a few homes are not included due to the lot size limitation.
Commissioner Cluff questioned the reason for the ordinance change. Mr. Prestwich
confirmed that it was the result of a specific request to include a rooster with a property
owner’s existing chickens.

Dave Dennison gave his address as 1462 West 11940 South and stated that he proposed
the ordinance change request to allow the addition of a rooster to his chickens. He received
a complaint after taking in a stray rooster, which initiated the petition for change.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Brown moved to recommend APPROVAL of the Zone Text Change
amending Section 18.25.110 Regulating Residential Chickens. Commissioner Cluff
seconded the motion. Vote on motion: Commissioner Brown-Aye, Commissioner
Cannon-Aye, Commissioner Cluff-Aye, Commissioner Lefgren-Aye, Commissioner
Russell-Aye, Chair Rushton-Nay. The motion passed 5-to-1.

3.     DECISION ITEMS

       A.     AMENDMENT TO DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT; AMENDMENT TO THE
              “DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT FOR MOUNTAIN VIEW PLACE AT
              RIVERTON”, AMENDING SECTIONS 5.4 AND 2.2.2 RELATED TO
              RESIDENTIAL  DENSITY  AND  CONSTRUCTION,    AMENDMENT
              REQUESTED BY CENTERCAL.

Development Services Director, Jason Lethbridge, presented the staff report and stated the
above matter involves an amendment to the Development Agreement for Mountain View
Place at Riverton. Phase I has been substantially completed with Phase II under
construction. The Planning Commission recently approved a residential project in Phase III
called Salt Development. He explained that the original Development Agreement included
a limit of 250 residential units and a stipulation that all residential development include main
floor retail. The Salt Development included a ground floor commercial space as was
required by the Development Agreement. While moving forward, it had been determined
that the main floor commercial is no longer feasible. The developer is looking to convert the
retail space into residential units without implementing significant architectural changes.

The developer proposed an amendment to the Development Agreement requesting
additional units and increasing the maximum from 250 to 282. He also requested changes
to sections that stipulate that residential units must be located above the main floor retail.
Mr. Lethbridge reported that the request has been reviewed by staff and CenterCal
Properties to ensure that it accomplishes what is required without negatively impacting other
portions of the CenterCal Development Agreement.

Commissioner Lefgren asked if the total unit count would go toward the SLR project. He
expressed hesitancy with the changes and felt the matter should have been researched
previously. Mr. Lethbridge confirmed that the amended unit will not count against that total
and would be in addition to the CenterCal Development rather than subtracted from the SLR
total. It would introduce flexibility and does not require abandonment of the main floor
commercial. He explained that the amendments as they stand would not preclude the
potential for main floor commercial that may be introduced later. He reported that the
request is not a departure from what was originally envisioned as the Village area remains
the same and the project has evolved from what was previously presented. It was noted
that parking accounts for the residential use and they have sufficient parking to
accommodate the additional unit space. He confirmed that the intent is to ensure that the
project is well balanced with the Village area of CenterCal.

Commissioner Russell moved to recommend APPROVAL of the proposed
amendment to the Development Agreement to the “Development Agreement for
Mountain View Place at Riverton” amending Sections 5.4, 1.1, and 2.2.2 of Exhibit B.
Commissioner Lefgren seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous
consent of the Commission.
