2. Muiiequitos Event Center; 1633 West 7800 South; Conditional Use Permit for Event

Center and Personal Instruction Services; CC-C Zone; Mufiequitos Event Center LLC
(applicant) [#34841; parcel 21-34-201-048]

Michael Lowe, co-owner of Mufiequitos, said they recently purchased the subject building to use as

an event center and dance studio. They are moving from Taylorsville where they have been

operating for three years. During that time there have been no calls for law enforcement or

emergency services. The proposed location will be capped at 99 occupants and is primarily for

family events such as quinceafieras and anniversaries, etc. The building is 6,000 square feet. A

portion facing 7800 South will be rented to a future tenant. The larger space will be divided in half

with the event center and a dance studio. Typical hours of operation are from 6 p.m. to midnight. All

events are inside and they do not have a license for alcohol and will not serve alcohol. The dance

studio operates during the day. In Taylorsville they had about 10 to 13 events per month and only a

few of those were during the week.

Ammon Allen asked if they would allow third parties to serve alcohol.

Michael Lowe said they are not opposed to it as long as they have a license and operate as provided

in state law, but that has never happened.

Megan Jensen provided the project overview for two conditional use permits. The updated floor plan

was shown with half for the event center and half for the dance studio. At least two employees will

be present at each event. Dance classes are held during the week with up to 25 people.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the Conditional Use Permit for

1. Management of the reception center, the lessee, or other designated party shall be on site at
all times during business hours in order to monitor noise, crowd control, parking lot safety

uniform or similar identification.

Jay Thomas said the staff report mentioned 35 parking stalls are required, but if there are 99 people
at an event it could generate at least 50 cars.
Megan Jensen said the proposal meets the parking code, so there is not a recommended condition
regarding parking. She clarified that employees are included in the limit of 99 total occupants.
Jimmy Anderson asked if the 15 parking stalls that are allowed by the Jordan School District are
dedicated to this property only or if they are shared with other businesses.
Megan Jensen said they are just for this property as far as she knew.
Jay Thomas opened the public hearing.

Ammon Allen thought this was a good space located away from residential areas, and it meets the

MOTION: Ammon Allen moved, based on the information and findings set forth in the staff

as described in the analysis of section IV of the staff report. The conditions are
imposed to mitigate the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of the
proposed use. The motion was seconded by Trish Hatch and passed 7-0 in favor.

3. West Jordan Utah Temple and Meetinghouse; 7148/7152 S U-111 Highway;
Preliminary Site Plan; PCH Zone; Naylor Wentworth Lund Architects/Jeff Dodge
(applicant) [#34577; parcel 20-28-201-001, 002]
Jeff Dodge, architect, said they have been working on the project that is part of the Terraine planned
community for more than a year. This site has always been planned for a religious building and
meets the zoning. The overall site plan shows the temple on the highest point of a hilly site. A

meetinghouse and small maintenance building will be north of the temple. Landscaping is provided

well beyond the city requirements and includes natural turf, trees, shrubs, drought resistant plants,

and areas of rock mulch. The only site fencing is next to the building. Non-linear terraced parking is

broken up with landscaping and stone covered landscape walls. Site circulation has been carefully

roof, 71 feet to the top of the parapet, and 216 for the steeple. The zoning district has no height

panels between the windows. Each window has stained glass. Various paver treatments were shown.

The meetinghouse and maintenance building siting, parking, and two-tone red brick exterior were

reviewed.

Ray McCandless said staff likes the building design, site layout, parking and circulation

configuration, and landscape design. He explained the conditions of approval including the

requirement to record plat 5 prior to final approval.

Based on the analysis and findings contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning

Commission approve the Preliminary Site Plan for the West Jordan Utah Temple and Meetinghouse,

in the PCH/MDA zone on approximately 16.023 acres located at approximately 7148/7152 South U-

111 Highway with the conditions and requirements of approval listed in the report.

Conditions and Requirements of Approval

1. Per City Code, Section 13-7B-5, an approved preliminary site plan shall remain valid for one
year following the date of the approval and final site plan approval is void if the development
does not commence within two (2) years of the approval.

a. Site plan approval shall be subject to meeting all applicable City Code requirements.

3. Final site plan approval shall not be granted until Terraine Plat 5 is recorded.

4. A minor subdivision shall be approved before final site plan approval is granted.

Ammon Allen asked when Plat 5 is expected to be recorded.

Ray McCandless understood that it would be the first part of next year.

Jay Thomas opened the public hearing.

Scott Hardy, West Jordan resident, said he lives in close proximity to the site. He shared his

excitement and some of the 50+ positive comments received from residents in his area regarding

their support of the temple. Regarding a comment on light pollution, he stated that the wattage from

the proposed 2800 homes in Terraine would be far more than the illumination of the temple. In his

discussions with builders in this development, he was told that 80% of the buyers are interested in

having a lot that is near to and has a direct view of the temple.

Laura Jacobsen, West Jordan resident, expressed her support for the temple. She explained the

importance of temples for members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. The buildings

are typically peaceful, beautiful places that operate with minimal impact on neighbors. They also

represent commitment to faith, family, and community and place where she can feel joy and peace.
