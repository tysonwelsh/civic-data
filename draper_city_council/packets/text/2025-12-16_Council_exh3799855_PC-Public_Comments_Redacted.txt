Hi Todd,

I'm writing to express my strong opposition to the proposed rezoning of parcel
34061760550000 at 13782 S 300 E from residential to commercial for a potential Smith's
Marketplace grocery store.

1. Traffic Impact:
According to ITE trip generation data, a store of this size would generate over 7,000–10,000
vehicle trips per day, with 800–950 during the evening peak hour alone. In contrast, 100
single-family attached homes (such as townhomes) would generate around 650 daily trips and
just 58 during peak hour. This level of traffic would place significant strain on local roads like
300 East and negatively impact nearby neighborhoods.

2. Tax Revenue Cannibalization:
Multiple studies show that large-format retail stores often do not increase net local tax
revenue. Up to 84% of a new store's sales typically come from existing businesses. In cases
where stores are built near competitors, the revenue is simply redistributed, not expanded —
and struggling nearby stores may even close, leading to vacant storefronts and reduced long-
term value.

3. Support for Denser Housing:
We support responsible growth, including higher-density residential options like townhomes.
These bring needed housing while minimizing traffic and infrastructure burden. It's a more
compatible use for this parcel and better aligned with Draper's long-term livability goals.

We urge you to preserve the current residential zoning and prioritize smart, sustainable
development for Draper.

Thank you for your time and service.

Dear Planning Commission Members,

I am writing to submit my public comment regarding the proposed Bangerter Crossroads
Land Use Map Amendment and Zoning Map Amendment for approximately 17.88 acres
located near 13782 S. 300 E. After reviewing the staff memo and the criteria listed for
approval and denial of such amendments, as well as the agenda documentation noting this
item as a legislative request, I respectfully request that the Commission deny this
proposal.

Below are the key reasons, grounded in Draper City’s own standards:

1. The Amendment Is Not Consistent With the General Plan

This proposal is not a small refinement—it is a fundamental redirection of the long-standing
land-use vision for our neighborhood. Draper’s own findings for denial state that
amendments should be rejected when they are not consistent with the goals,
objectives, and policies of the General Plan.

The General Plan calls for thoughtful transitions between commercial and residential
areas. This amendment would place a large commercial center directly adjacent to
established single-family neighborhoods, with no transitional buffer.

The site is currently zoned RA1 (Residential Agricultural), which exists to preserve larger
lots, open space, and low-impact development patterns. A Regional Commercial
designation is a complete departure from that intent.

This drastic shift contradicts core General Plan principles, including neighborhood integrity,
compatible land-use transitions, and preservation of existing low-density residential
environments.

2. The Proposal Is Not Harmonious With Surrounding Development

This amendment creates an abrupt and incompatible land-use mismatch that would disrupt
the established character of the neighborhood. Draper’s denial criteria clearly state that
amendments should be harmonious with existing development.

Homes in this area were purchased with the expectation of low-density residential
zoning, not adjacency to a commercial center.

A commercial development of this scale introduces noise, lighting, increased nighttime
activity, delivery operations, and significant vehicle traffic—all incompatible with a
quiet, semi rural residential setting.

3. Adjacent Properties Will Be Adversely Affected

Draper City’s criteria require denial of an amendment when it would adversely affect
adjacent properties. This proposal would have direct and immediate negative impacts on
homeowners that cannot be fully mitigated by conditions.

Residents would experience:



      Increased congestion and cut-through traffic




      Loss of privacy




      Increased noise and nighttime lighting




      Negative impacts on home values




      Reduced safety and walkability

These concerns are not hypothetical—they are inevitable consequences of siting a regional
commercial center next to existing homes.

4. Infrastructure Is Not Adequate for Commercial Intensity

The infrastructure required to support a commercial center does not currently exist in this
location and would require major taxpayer-funded upgrades that have not been
addressed in the proposal.

Residents already experience:
      Significant congestion at Bangerter/13800 S during peak hours




      Insufficient road width and lack of turn lanes




      Stormwater issues that would be worsened by additional impervious surfaces




      Increased demands on police and fire services


Local roads—including 13800 S and 300 E—were never designed to carry commercial-
level traffic volumes, and retrofitting them would be costly, disruptive, and potentially
infeasible.

5. The Amendment Does Not Advance the City’s Planning Principles

There is no planning basis to justify converting a residential/agricultural pocket into a
regional commercial node.

This site:



      Borders long-established, stable residential neighborhoods




      Lacks natural or planned transitional areas




      Is not identified in the General Plan as a commercial expansion zone


Draper already has established commercial corridors—such as Draper Peaks, the
Bangerter/12300 S commercial area, and the Town Center redevelopment zone—where
commercial growth is intended and appropriate.

6. Residents Are Not Requesting This Change

This change is developer-driven, not community-driven. Residents will bear the burden
through:
      Increased traffic




      More noise and lighting




      Reduced property values




      Lower neighborhood safety




      Strain on residential streets




      Loss of the semirural character protected by RA1 zoning


This proposal benefits a private developer while imposing significant quality-of-life costs on
the families who already live here.

7. Better Locations Already Exist for Commercial Development

Commercial growth should occur where the city has already planned for it, not by
sacrificing stable residential zones. Draper has multiple designated areas for commercial
development, making this rezoning unnecessary and poorly located.

Closing Request

Under Draper City’s own criteria, this amendment fails multiple required findings, including
consistency with the General Plan, harmony with surrounding development, and
avoiding adverse impacts to adjacent properties.

For these reasons, I urge the Planning Commission to recommend denial of the
Bangerter Crossroads Land Use Map Amendment and Zoning Map Amendment. The
proposal poses significant long-term negative impacts to neighboring residents and does
not align with Draper’s established land-use vision.
Thank you for considering my comments. I appreciate your service and your careful review
of this important issue.

Sincerely,
Ryan & Susan Knapp
