 9            Adopting Water Conservation Standards – Ryan Carter, City Attorney
10
11   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said that they have prepared an ordinance that will go to planning
12   commission. He said that all new water conservation standards would be for new builds, not for
13   existing neighborhoods. He said he is concerned about the 250 foot limitation on grass or 35%
14   of the total landscaped area. He said that Riverton City has more secondary water connections
15   than any other city in the valley. He said that most cities have passed the ordinance but have
16   no plans to enforce it.
17
18   Councilmember Stewart said that in reading through some of the language, he was worried
19   about the thickness of the bark. He said he thought these were guidelines not a requirement by
20   code.
21
22   Councilmember Buroker said she appreciates the comment about secondary water which is
23   unique compared to other cities. She said that she would like to go back to Jordan Valley Water
24   Conservancy District and have a discussion about crafting an ordinance that will address the
25   uniqueness of Riverton City’s secondary water system. She said her initiative agenda is to have
26   the City lead the way with water conservation.
27
28   Mayor Staggs said that he has residents that have asked what the City is doing to conserve
29   water. He said that he has been able to tell residents that the City has adjusted the time clock
30   by 25% across the board and has designed the new park north of Jim’s to use very little water.
31   He said that the state lobbyists said that if the City can take actions to conserve water then it will
32   be good for receiving grant funding.
33
34   Councilmember McDougal said that the City can control City property. He said that the City is
35   planning on decreasing use of water from Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District and he
36   doesn’t want to give a lot of control to this entity. He said that he doesn’t want to tell residents
37   what they can and can’t do with their properties. He said he would like to adopt standards for
38   City property and businesses.
39
40   Mayor Staggs said that as part of the application for the Greenwell project, the State has put out
41   their scoring matrix which shows how the City can score high in order to receive money from the
42   county and the state. He said he talked to Stacy Olson and the City closed out 2020 at 119% of
43   contract with Jordan Valley and the City will need to negotiate a new contract if it exceeds
44   120%. He said that he believes this year with the drought and with conservation efforts, the City
45   will be at 110% or 115%. He said he believes the City’s dependence on Jordan Valley will
46   continue to decrease.
47
48   Jason Lethbridge said that there is an opportunity with developers building model homes. He
49   said there could be an incentive if the model home had landscaping which would promote water
50   conservation.

37       Ordinance No. 21-14 – General Plan Amendment and Rezone of 5.91 acres located
38       near 12900 South 1830 West, changing the General Plan Designation to Medium-High
39       Density Residential and the Zoning to RM-6-SD
40
41   Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said that the original motion included two SD
42   designations, for individual detached units only, rambler style that could have a bonus room. He
43   said the only designation being considered at this public hearing was the restriction for rambler
44   homes only with a bonus room.
45
46   Ryan Kent, Dragon Fly Development, said that he wanted to have more clarification as to
47   whether the Council would allow a second story or just a bonus room. Joe Gardner, Dragonfly
48   Development, showed various designs that would include a second story. He said that 50% of
49   the homes adjacent to the neighborhood are two story homes.
50
51   Councilmember Stewart asked if the bonus room was restricted to above the garage. Jason
     Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     August 17, 2021




 1   Lethbridge said that the bonus room could be anywhere above the home.
 2
 3   Councilmember Buroker said that that she likes the designs and said these designs would be
 4   driven by consumer demand. She clarified with the builder that the designs would be driven by
 5   consumer demand and stated that this is not a senior housing neighborhood, but is a family
 6   neighborhood.
 7
 8   Councilmember Wells said that this development is in an older neighborhood and has a different
 9   look depending on what side of the neighborhood. He said that it is hard to tell the height of the
10   home whether it be a bonus room or a two story home.
11
12   Mayor Staggs called for public comments;
13
14   Chad Barker said that originally there had been a vista style home planned. He said that he is
15   worried that the requests will continue and they will end up with duplexes.
16
17   Delmas Johnson said that during Planning Commission and the previous City Council meeting
18   they had agreed to certain requirements and feels like the second story is an encroachment.
19   He said he is concerned about how this fits into the current neighborhood.
20
21   Nick Bauman said he was opposed to two story or bonus room homes and thought that the
22   agreement was for single family and single level homes.
23
24   One other comment was received via email from the following individuals: Randy and Sonia
25   Sandquist, Shannon Sandquist and Cody and Cheri Sunderland. This email will be included in
26   the packet.
27
28   Joe Gardner said that he doesn’t want to redo the zoning. He said they were just coming back
29   for clarification and believes that the market will demand second story.
30
31   Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments there being none, he closed the Public
32   Hearing.
33
34   Councilmember Stewart said that most homes are rambler or split entry and not two story.
35
36   Councilmember Wells said he wanted to clarify why this was brought back to the City Council.
37   He said that initially the developer came to Council meeting and was focused on the zoning
38   change and not the conceptual plans. He said he doesn’t believe the developer was given the
39   opportunity to present concept plans and ask for specifics on whether the homes should be
40   single level or two story.
41
42   Councilmember Buroker said the approval for this development became confused with a senior
43   housing development approval.
