Fiscal Year 2023-2027 Capital Improvement Plan

Council Deputy Director Symes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide a summary of Council staff's
review of the proposed Fiscal Year 2023-2027 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP). The purpose of the CIP is to strategically fund
projects to ensure adequate resources are committed to address pressing City construction and maintenance needs for
infrastructure city-wide. Capital improvement projects are those exceeding $30,000 adding to the City’s “physical plant”, projects
exceeding $50,000 for major rehabilitation or reconstruction of the physical plant, projects resulting in the acquisition or long-
term lease of land, and/or feasibility studies and evaluations. CIP funding sources include the General Fund, federal and state
grants, B&C road funds, utility enterprise funds, bonds, and other miscellaneous sources (grants, partnerships, and fundraising
proceeds). An ordinance was adopted in January of 2021 that changed the process for submitting and acting upon the CIP; it also
changed projects amounts and separated projects into three types: one-time, perpetual, and RAMP funded. The CIP and annual
budget will now be reviewed together, allowing better alignment with what is planned and what is proposed for funding in year
one of the CIP. The CIP will still stand as a long-term planning document and CIP projects will be reviewed with each of the
Department’s proposed budget through the budget review process. Mr. Symes reviewed the schedule for consideration of the CIP,
after which he provided project breakdown; there are 58 total projects, 17 of which are new and 22 of which have been amended
from prior years. He provided a funding breakdown by Department, concluding the total FY23 funding proposed for CIP projects
is $164,354,955.

Proposed City Boundary Line Adjustment between the Cities of Ogden
and North Ogden (Continued from April 12, 2022)

Planning Manager Montgomery reported a developer is proposing to build a 142-unit apartment complex near Ogden’s
border with North Ogden City, at approximately 1514 North Washington Boulevard. The developer would like to build on
properties on both sides of the city’s boundaries but doing so would result in an inconsistent development. Developing in two
different cities would mean having to gain separate approvals for site plans, permits, two different utility providers, and separate
fire/police services. It would also mean having two different design standards within a single development. In order to maintain
consistency across the development, North Ogden City has requested to adjust city boundaries with Ogden and absorb
approximately 1.6 acres from Ogden. If the Council desires to adjust municipal boundaries, then a resolution must be passed
stating an intent to act. After passing a resolution, and following a 60-day protest period, the Council may adopt an ordinance
approving the boundary adjustment. North Ogden City must follow through with the same process in order to finalize the
boundary adjustment. He noted the Planning Commission recommended approval of the boundary adjustment with a vote of 4-1.
Commissioner Southwick voted against the proposal, stating that it is a bad precedent and bad public policy to exchange
boundaries. In addition to the recommendation to approve, the Planning Commission recommends the following condition: The
development should have a 10’ landscaped setback along the north property line of the Washington Mobile Estates development.
He then indicated the Council met in a work session on April 12, 2022 to receive a presentation from Planning staff. There was a
concern about lost property tax if the boundary was adjusted. Planning staff was asked to come back and present information on
how much tax revenue could be lost in the case of a boundary adjustment. He noted that he researched the tax implications of the
matter and found that the maximum property tax revenue lost per year would be $22,675; additionally, the City could also lose
one-time permitting fees of $77,225.

Council member Hyer stated that since the Council’s last discussion of this issue he has heard from many residents as
well as a Planning Commissioner who expressed concern about the proposed boundary line adjustment. The Planning
Commissioner who contacted him indicated he had proposed a property trade as an alternative to the boundary line adjustment. He
also asked if there is an opportunity to share the property tax revenue between both cities. Mr. Montgomery stated he would need
to confer with legal counsel about whether revenue sharing is allowed. He also noted that the land trade would be an option, but
the City would also need to follow a lengthy process that would involve other property owners.

Discussion among the Council and Mr. Montgomery then centered on the long-term viability of the existing mobile home
park adjacent to the property subject to the boundary adjustment and whether the use could be relocated, which would render the
concern about isolating an existing neighborhood moot; there was also discussion about the development opportunities for the
property if it were to remain in Ogden City under its current zoning. The Council concluded to proceed to consideration of the
intent resolution on May 10 but indicated that at this point they still have concerns about the implications of the boundary
adjustment, and they are leaning towards denial of the resolution.

Acting Chair Lopez invited input from North Ogden Planner Brandon Bell and the applicant, Rick Scadden. Mr. Bell
noted that he feels some of the concerns expressed by the Ogden City Planning Commission and Council can be addressed
through a development agreement for the project. Mr. Scadden noted that he is one of the partners for the project; he understands
the concerns expressed by Ogden City but noted that he feels that the proposed boundary adjustment makes sense given that the
majority of the subject property is already located within North Ogden. He stated that he will provide amenities in the project as
whole; open space exists in the project, but it would be split between city boundaries. He stated he wants to be a good neighbor to
Ogden City and if he is unable to develop or secure sufficient access to his project, the property may continue to sit vacant for a
long time into the future. This led to discussion among the Council and Mr. Scadden regarding the components of the project that
would not comply with Ogden City land use ordinances were the property to remain in Ogden City. Mr. Scadden indicated he is
willing to leave his property within Ogden City so long as it is possible to work with Planning staff to address the issues that have
been raised. The Council asked Mr. Scadden to meet with Planning staff to discuss options for the property further before
presenting the matter to the Council again.

Proposed Zone Text Amendment — Allow Food Manufacturing in the
Business Exchange Industrial (BEI) Zone

Planning Manager Montgomery reported a petitioner has requested to change requirements in the BEI zone (Business
Exchange Industrial) to allow for manufacturing of food for human consumption. The purpose of the change is to allow an ice
cream manufacturing facility. The BEI zone does not currently allow food manufacturing because the zone was originally
intended to be an area for manufacturing, research, and development of outdoor recreation products. The Planning Commission
recommended approval, and they felt that food manufacturing should be permissible because there is a food manufacturer, Ogden
Distillery, across the street.

Discussion among the Council centered on the applicant’s development plans and the availability of utility services in the
area to handle changes in land use. The Council indicated they are comfortable considering the proposed text amendment in a
future meeting.
