PUBLIC HEARING:

PC 26-004 Brickyard 4 (Continued from the February 26th meeting) [Design Review &
Subdivision] Jake Thompson/Direct Homes Inc, authorized agent/owner is requesting to subdivide
.80 acres into 20 lots and construct 18 townhome units and one three story mixed use building at
approximately 50 West 800 North in the Mixed Use (MU) Zone; TIN 05-048-0018, in the Bridger
Neighborhood.

Staff: Aimee Egbert, City Planner, reviewed the request, which includes, a 7,361 square foot, three-
story, mixed use building fronting 800 North with 2,255 square feet of ground-floor commercial space,
645 square feet of second-floor commercial space, and two two-story residential units. The request
also includes three, three-story townhome buildings containing 18 units, each with two-car garage
parking on the main level.

The mixed-use building generally meets material and fenestration standards; however, the north
ground-floor frontage provides only 32% transparency (below the required 50%). The primary
entrance faces the parking lot rather than the street, and the street-facing façade lacks architectural
interest.

The townhome buildings do not fully meet façade variation, street orientation, or transparency
standards. The middle building is too similar to the adjacent buildings, the west-end units have 5-
15% transparency (below the required 20 – 50%), and the entrances do not face the street.

As conditioned increasing the north street-facing transparency and architectural interest on the
mixed-use building would bring the building into compliance. Reorienting the west end townhome
entrances to face the street, increasing window area and architectural details, and modifying the
middle townhome building to ensure required façade variation would bring the townhome buildings
into compliance.


Proponent: Jake Thompson stated that the townhome units will be for sale. In response to
Commissioner Maughan, he clarified that the mixed-use building will be condominiumized and the
residential parking stalls will be designated within the commercial parking lot.

Public: Stephanie Wildman expressed concerns regarding parking, building height, project density,
and felt the proposal was not held to the same standards as the apartments she owns across the
street.

Commission: The Commission agreed to the recommended conditions. They reviewed the
proposed setbacks and determined the east setbacks were acceptable (7.25-7.5 feet for the mixed-
use building and 8.75-9 feet for the townhome buildings).

R. Holley responded to the public comment, explaining that the apartments across the street are
located in the MR-12 zone, which regulates setbacks and building heights differently than the MU
zone.

PC 26-013 Logan 2045 General Plan

Staff/Proponent: Russ Holley, Community Development Director, reviewed the Future Land Use
Plan (FLUP), noting that the updated plan uses fewer, broader land use categories to allow greater
flexibility when applying zoning districts. He highlighted the planned upzoning of the 400 North
corridor, which is intended to support future growth and increased residential density within the City.

R. Holley reviewed the Action Matrix, which outlines implementation strategies, policies, and
recommendations contained in the 2045 General Plan. The matric serves as a prioritization tool for
staff as they schedule tasks and projects over the life of the plan.

R. Holley also presented a comparison of existing land use acreage with the acreage proposed in
the FLUP.

R. Holley then reviewed the Zoning Audit Memo, an internal document created to assist staff, the
Planning Commission, and City Council as they begin updating the Land Development Code
following adoption of the General Plan. The memo evaluates the current Code for consistency with
the General Plan’s policies, recommendations, and implementation actions.

Commissioner Maughan asked about priority annexation areas. R. Holley explained that Cache
County lacks sufficient infrastructure and services to support significant new development and
therefore anticipates that some unincorporated areas will eventually annex into neighboring cities. R.
Holley also explained that annexations must be initiated by property owners and are reviewed
individually by the City Council.

Public: Lisa Stoner, a Logan resident, requested that the General Plan include a goal to protect
Logan’s dark skies.

Commission: The Commission discussed additional topics they would like the City Council to
consider adding to the goals section, including dark sikes initiative, recreation center, senior center,
animal control, and the summer citizens program.

Commissioner Maughan suggested making the agriculture technology (Ag-Tech) focus in the
Economic Development chapter, less specific or expanding it to encompass agriculture and other
industries. R. Holley noted that Ag-Tech centers on technologies that improve agriculture efficiency
and that, as water conservation continues to evolve, it will remain a viable economic sector.

Commissioner Duncan added that the USU’s Agriculture Department is far more diverse than
traditional crops and dairies, the department includes biotechnology, medical research, food
production and food science.
