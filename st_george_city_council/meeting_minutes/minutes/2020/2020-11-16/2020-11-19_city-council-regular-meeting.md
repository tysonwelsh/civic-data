     Mayor Pike called the meeting to order and welcomed all in attendance.

      Mayor Pike read a determination he wrote regarding conducting the meeting with an
      anchor location presents a substantial risk to the health and safety of those who may
      be present at the location based on the following facts:

      1. Across the country Covid-19 levels have continued to grow.
      2. On Nov 11, 2020, Governor Herbert ordered everyone to wear face masks. In
         addition, he asked everyone to telework if at all possible and to use extra
         precautions. He even instructed individuals to not gather socially with anyone
         they do not live with.
      3. The Covid-19 levels in the state of Utah have continued at high rates with
         Saturday, November 14 reaching an all-time high of 4,986.
      4. The hospitals and ICU units are operating at near capacity.
      5. Washington County Covid-19 numbers have continued to rise, with 160 new
         cases, for example, on November 14, 2020.
      6. On November 16, 2020, the City Manager, Adam Lenhard, ordered all city
         employees to telework if it is possible for them to do so.

ZONE CHANGE/ORDINANCE:
     Consider approval of an ordinance changing the zone from R-1-10 (single-
     family residential, 10,000 sq. ft. minimum lot size) to PD-R (Planned
     Development Residential) on approximately 24 acres generally located on
     the west side of the intersection of Canyon View Drive and Gap Canyon
     Drive.

      Planner Dan Boles presented the request to change the zone from R-1-10 (single-
      family residential, 10,000 sq. ft. minimum lot size) to PD-R (Planned Development
      Residential) on approximately 24 acres generally located on the west side of the
      intersection of Canyon View Drive and Gap Canyon Drive. He provided a PowerPoint
      presentation covering the following topics: zoning map; general plan map;
      conceptual site plan; landscape plan; elevations; renderings; and materials. The
      Planning Commission recommended approval. A number of neighbors attended the
      Planning Commission meeting and voiced concerns, mainly with traffic.

      Assistant Public Works Director Wes Jenkins provided a background of the traffic
      studies and roads in the area. A traffic study was performed for the entire
      development around 2005/2006. The study concluded with 7-8 recommendations of
      improvements, most of which pertained to Dixie Drive. Other improvements
      included competition of Canyon View Drive and the extension of Plantations Drive
      (Gap Canyon Drive) down to the Tonaquint area. As part of their agreement with
      the City, there is a per lot cost the developer would pay to the City as each
      subdivision platted to assist with the cost of the improvements along Dixie Drive.
      The developer has already paid for a portion of the extension of Canyon View Drive.
      In 2013, the traffic study was updated specifically for this PA area, the completion of
      Canyon View Drive was recommended. In 2018, the City did a traffic analysis at
      Dixie Drive and Canyon View Drive to see if a traffic signal was warranted when a
      commercial subdivision was being proposed. In both traffic studies for 2013 and
      2018, the traffic along Canyon View drive was almost identical; traffic on Dixie Drive
      increased. When the proposed development is complete, the traffic on Canyon View
      Drive will go from approximately 2,400 vehicles to 4,500 vehicles per day which is
      under the capacity of about 6,000 vehicles. When the project is complete, there will
      be five (5) connection points between this area and Dixie Drive. He spoke about the
      capacity and completion time frames of the other roads in the area.

      Mayor Pike inquired about what the City can do to help get the roads completed. He
      is concerned that too much is developed prior to the roads being built.

      Councilmember Larkin commented that she is worried about the traffic in this area.

      Mr. Jenkins noted staff will have to keep that in mind and follow up with this issue as
      the south side progresses. The 2005/2006 traffic study says that if the City feels like
      traffic has increased, the City Engineer may require additions to the traffic study to
      explain how traffic will flow out of the subdivision to Dixie Drive.

      Applicant Jonathan Jensen explained they are interested in building a project for his
      company; they are trying to minimize the total impact on the total acreage. Their
      hope is to provide a sufficient amount of amenities to help residents and visitors to
      the property. Their goal is to be a long-term stake holder in the St. George area.
      He understands there are challenges with this property – topography, adding
      improvements, and utilities; he believes they have been able to work on all of these
      issues. They would like to build a high-quality project and hope this is a good
      addition to what St. George is looking for. He recognized that people are stressed
      with change and they do not discount but rather appreciate the concerns of the
      neighbors. They hope to create a continuation of enjoyment for the number of
      people who use the private property currently as the start of their bike rides or
      hikes.

      After being ask by Councilmember McArthur, Mr. Jensen stated there are currently
      no plans to allow access or a staging area, nor have they created a plan around this
      opportunity. When the project began, they did not anticipate this continuing, but
      they are happy to work something out. He would like to use the site plan approval
      process to give an opportunity to add such components.

      Rick Rosenberg explained this project is part of the larger Divario project and part of
      the development agreement approved several years ago. He noted there is a large
      recreation area near this property that is proposed to be part of a public park per the
      development agreement. That area can be available for the continued use of
      recreation; a number of people currently park and start their rides or hikes there.
      Development of this proposed project does not prohibit the recreation use that is
      happening now. There are plans as a total, to maintain the recreational biking. He
      showed the trail corridors on the project master plan. The community park will be
      about 25+ acres and can provide good access. He added this project contributed to
      the construction Sunbrook Drive, the extension of Canyon View Drive, and are
      setting aside funds to pay for other improvements as required. The entire traffic
      plan in this area were all master plan issues that were made as a result of the
      planning of this property over the past 30 years.

      Mr. Boles suggested that if approved, the applicant work with staff to work on a
      conservation easement or something to lock in the open space on PA3.

      Mayor Pike commented that he appreciated Mr. Jensen for saying that they would be
      willing to help facilitate trail access. He would like to see this as a stipulation as part
      of the motion.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman agreed. He would like to see a conservation easement
      in favor of the City so everyone knows they have to come to the City to do anything
      with the property.

      Councilmember McArthur asked if the Council wants higher density with open space
      or medium density on this parcel.

      Councilmember Larkin noted she is not against higher density development in certain
      areas of this development; however, Gap Canyon is iconic and highly used. She is

      concerned about higher density near and the view from Gap Canyon; she would like
      the buildings to be lower. Further, she is concerned with the parking from a high-
      density project. She would like to see a lower impact, medium density project in this
      particular spot.

      Councilmembers Randall and Councilmember Smethurst agreed.

      Mayor Pike noted this property is currently zoned R-1-10 and on the general plan
      was planned for medium density residential. He asked how much of the property is
      developable and how many homes would be built if it remained R-1-10 and would
      that work.

      Mr. Rosenberg replied he believes most of the property is developable and
      approximately 75 homes could be built if the property remained R-1-10. He outlined
      options for the site.

      Mr. Boles put a map on the screen and outlined the planning area.

      Mr. Jensen noted the majority of what they do not plan to build on is developable.
      They prefer not to move mountains. He spoke about the height of the proposed
      project. The plan shows the buildings far from the neighbors to the east, maybe
      they should move back towards the road.

      After being asked by Councilmember McArthur, City Attorney Shawn Guzman
      explained the development agreement has a map for this pod. He asked Mr. Boles
      what the map says. Mr. Boles showed the map and stated it shows medium density
      residential, up to nine (9) units per acre. Mr. Guzman stated there is topography
      and infrastructure to take into account. Parking was brought up as a concern, he
      believes this is a valid concern as Gap Canyon Drive will be an arterial road and was
      not designed to allow for parking. If there is a concern with the number of stalls for
      this project, the Council can take that into consideration and may reduce the amount
      of overall units on the parcel.

      Councilmember McArthur commented he project could be a higher density than R-1-
      10; however, what has been proposed is not what he believes the Council wants to
      see on this property based on tonight’s discussion.

      MOTION:
          A motion was made by Councilmember McArthur to deny the

      in one area.

      Councilmember McArthur noted the density is approved at up to nine (9) units per
      acres and per the plans, it feels like the developer is trying get all nine (9); he

      Councilmember Larkin agreed. She believes this is one of the most valuable areas of

      concerns of the Council and noted they are conscience of the elements in the area.

      Mayor Pike read a chat from Mark Teepen.

      Mr. Teepen explained they have worked over the years to ensure the canyons remain
      open to the public. This project, as it is clustered, provides for a better viewshed.

      Mayor Pike commented he appreciates what their team is trying to do overall. The

      presented. The Council is not saying they don’t want anything built, they have
      something else in mind.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman advised this is part of a development agreement with a
      master plan; therefore, he asked for clarification on why the item was denied so the
      developer understands what the Council may consider.

      Councilmember McArthur outlined the reasons for denial; the clustered aspect of the
      project - the Council would like to see it spread-out a bit more; parking issues; and
      access to the trails for those that want to continue to utilize the area.

      Councilmember Larkin added height is a big deal, as well as pitched roofs – the
      Council would like a low visual impact.

      Mayor Pike noted the height would be under 35 feet; a single-family home could be
      that tall.

      Mr. Willis stated the proposed height is 39 ½ feet.

      Councilmember Larkin commented the spirit of the message it to have a low visual
      impact. What was proposed was a higher visual impact. She appreciates all of the
      open space being preserved. The open space will be what sells the property and
      valued generation after generation.

      Councilmembers Randall and McArthur agreed adding these notes to the motion.
