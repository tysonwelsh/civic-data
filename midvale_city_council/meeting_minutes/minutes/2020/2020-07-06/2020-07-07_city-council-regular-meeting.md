A. DISCUSS SINGLE FAMILY ZONE
Nate Rockwood said the current General Plan was adopted in 2016. The Plan is
developed with City Council and the community to be the foundational document outlining
the goals and vision of the City. It is also used as the guiding document in developing the
City’s planning and zoning ordinances and in making planning and development
decisions by City Council.
In general, major city planning objectives are set with the adoption of the General Plan.
These objectives become set into the planning and zoning ordinances, which are used to
regulate development. It is common and understandable for the public to not have a full
understanding of the process. It is more likely for citizens and property owners to only
become involved with the planning process when a new development is affecting their
neighborhood or property, than it is for them to become involved in the creation and
adoption of the General Plan and have a complete understanding of how the new General
Plan might affect them. Too often when the citizens come to complain about a new
development or planning process, it is already too late for their concerns to be accounted
for and City Council is locked into allowing a development, which meets all the zoning
ordinance requirements, to move forward. Some recent new development projects in
Midvale have highlighted this issue.

A. CONSIDER REQUEST TO VACATE CITY-OWNED RIGHTS-OF-WAY
LOCATED WEST OF BINGHAM JUNCTION BOULEVARD (IVY DRIVE
AND BINARY WAY)
Alex Murphy said as part of the Jordan Bluffs Lot 201 Amended subdivision that was
recorded in April, the City received dedication of rights-of-way for the extension of Ivy
Drive west of Bingham Junction Boulevard and a new street, Binary Way. The property
owner at the time, Gardner Jordan Bluffs LC, was required to dedicate the rights-of-way
to create a parcel to sell to Zions Bank prior to completing a Large Scale Master Plan
(LSMP) for the Zions Bank project. The Zions LSMP proposes public streets in a different
location than originally identified on the recorded plat, so Zions and Gardner have
requested a vacation of the original rights-of-way to allow new ones to be dedicated. An
updated plat has been prepared and will include dedication of new public rights-of-way
according to the updated Jordan Bluffs concept Gardner has developed.
The City’s Municipal Code dictates the process vacation requests must go through. Per
§12.16.050, this request was forwarded to other affected City departments to determine
potential impacts to the City. The various City departments concluded vacation of the
right-of-way was appropriate and, because of how recent the rights-of-way were
dedicated and the fact that no construction work had begun on them, did not express any

concerns with the proposal. All vacation requests require a review and recommendation

from the Planning Commission and approval from the City Council with public hearings

approve the request.

The requested vacation is also a prerequisite for a subdivision plat under consideration

FISCAL IMPACT:

No immediate impact. This vacation will allow for the proposed public roads to be

to City policy.

Alex Murphy said in 2019, the City adopted a zoning ordinance for Subareas 1-3 of the

Jordan Bluffs zone. This ordinance established goals for the district, review process

requirements, and standards for commercial, residential, and mixed-use development

based on a preliminary “pod” concept put together by the property owner, Gardner

Company. To date, one project has been approved under this ordinance (Holmes Homes’

Jordan Bluffs Condo project).

Following an agreement between Zions and Gardner to develop the northern pods, D&E,

Gardner developed a new concept for the Jordan Bluffs area that would, over time and

subject to market conditions, develop an urbanized core of the project area with Zions

serving as the northern anchor and future development incrementally contributing to the
vision.
) To implement the new concept, certain requirements of the zoning ordinance and the

2019 Amended and Restated Master Development Agreement for Jordan Bluffs Project

(‘MDA’) require modification. Staff is still working with Gardner to solidify the terms of the

MDA but wanted to present the initial proposals and development concept to the Council

for discussion.

Additionally, the Planning Commission has conditionally approved a Large Scale Master

Plan for the Zions Bank project. This approval is contingent on the Council approving

changes to the zoning ordinance and MDA and requires approval of a site-specific

Development Agreement for Zions Bank. The draft Zions Agreement is also included in

this report and discussion.

PROPOSED ZONING ORDINANCE AMENDMENTS:

The first piece of the proposal is the zoning ordinance amendment. To allow the new

Jordan Bluffs concept to work, changes to following sections of the ordinance are

required: Purpose, Development Requirements, Parking and Access Standards, Fencing

and Screening Standards, Residential Developments, Commercial Developments, and

Signage.

17-7-10.12.1 Purpose.

The new concept requires a new layout of the overall pod map. The changes results in a
reduction in the overall number of pods (14 to 12) and new boundaries for the pods. This
change also includes a revision to the central linear park location and will require

17-7-10.12.5 Development requirements.

Proposed changes to this section include a new road layout, new road standards, revised
landscape, building, and parking lot setbacks for the core, and increased building heights
in certain pods.

The new concept proposes a new street system on the west side of Bingham Junction
Boulevard. The new street system provides a central boulevard-type street as a primary
street serviced by secondary and tertiary streets with standards designed based on
expected traffic volumes. On-street parking is required for the primary boulevard and an
expanded bike network is provided on the primary and secondary streets. Staff and the
applicant have provided updated figures for this section and recommend one additional
change to the Public Road Classification figure to add the bike network prior to adoption.
In conjunction with the new road layout, building and landscape setback along streets in
the new core have been modified. For Pods E-K, which include all pods west of Bingham
Junction Boulevard, minimum and maximum setbacks are determined by street type
(primary, secondary, or tertiary) and priority. Outside of those pods, landscape setbacks
for minor roads have been decreased from 15’ to 10’. Major roads (Bingham Junction,
East Ivy, Center Street) remain as originally adopted.

In response to marketing efforts for the site, Gardner is also requesting the building height
east of Bingham Junction Boulevard be increased from 80’ to 95’ and that it be measured
from finished floor elevation to top of exterior wall (excluding parapets) instead of to the
parapet. This results in a maximum building height increase of approximately 20’.
Following the Planning Commission’s public hearing, a provision was added to this
section clarifying that the new frontage requirements only apply to the first street of each
type for a development. This provision is necessary to provide flexibility in the timing of
buildout in the core area.

17-7-10.12.6 Parking and access standards.

The proposed changes to parking standards include a reduction in the minimum
requirement for Office uses, a modification to access requirements, and the addition of
on-street loading areas.
The current language requires a minimum of 3 parking stalls per 1000 SF of net leasable
floor area for general office uses or 5 stalls per 1000 SF for intensive office uses. This
Standard does not account for proximity to TRAX stations or shared parking opportunities
that may eventually become in the project area, so a modification to establish a minimum
of 3 stalls per 1000 SF and maximum of 6 stalls per 1000 SF is proposed.
The City Engineer expressed some concern about mandating minimum access
separation requirements in the zoning ordinance, specifically calling out that it limited his
ability to consider traffic patterns and street layout when reviewing development

Proposed changes to this section include removing the duplication of building height

Because overall building height requirements are defined in §17-7-10.12.5, this text

amendment proposes removing them from this section.

New parking garage screening requirements are provided in this text amendment, based

on a newly defined “within clear sight of’ standard. Parking garage elevations within clear

sight of major roads (Bingham Junction, Center, new primary, or new secondary roads)

or of a public park are required to cover at least 30% of the elevation with materials that

match the primary building on the site. Elevations that are not within clear sight of major

roads or a public park are required to be screened with landscape material. For the

purposes of these requirements, elevations are reviewed individually and considered

“within clear sight of” if they are (1) approximately parallel to a street or park, (2) within

300’ of one of a street or park, and (3) not otherwise obstructed by a building or elevation

change. Parking garages in Pods E-K are also required to comply with the landscape

setback requirements of §17-7-10.12.5(L).

17-7-10.12.11 Commercial developments.

Proposed changes to this section match the building height and parking garage changed

3.3 Project Amenities.

The biggest change to with the proposed MDA amendments is to the central community

park. As part of the new concept, the park area has been relocated from the central linear

park configuration to the top of the cap slope. This change strengthens the connection

between the new park and the Jordan River while maintaining the area of the original

proposal. The required width in the MDA (125’) is not provided throughout the park nor

does this proposal have the same level of design as originally contemplated. However,

the requirement to provide park amenities in terms of acreage and cost are still within the

MDA and will be required with this new development concept.

A minor change to the trail connections from the cap to the Jordan River Parkway Trail is

proposed that would expand the allowable types of lighting. Instead of requiring only

bollards, the lighting would need to be “pedestrian-scaled,” which can include bollards,

short poles, or other configurations. The other design requirements remain as-is and the

City is still required to accept responsibility for the trail connection.

Revisions to the wetland and slope maintenance standards are also proposed. This

language is still under discussion, but generally seeks to establish minimum expectations

for the maintenance and improvement of those areas to reinforce their position as

amenities for the Jordan Bluffs area as natural wetlands and open space.
