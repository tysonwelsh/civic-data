   6.   Public Hearing: Draper Academy Conditional Use Permit Request (Administrative
        Action)

On the request of Christine Kremer and Bret Steel for a Conditional Use Permit to operate a
Private School on 0.41 acres of property located at 12390 South 800 East in the TC (Town
Center) zone. Application USE-960-2020. Staff contact is Jennifer Jastremsky, 801-576-6328,
jennifer.jastremsky@draper.ut.us.

Ms. Jastremsky began her presentation and detailed the location of the Draper Academy. The
property consisted of two homes converted to office space. It was located in the Town Center
designation and TC Zoning designation. The applicant proposed a private school in the existing
brick building onsite in the corner of the lot. The school would hold fifteen students and three
teachers. The Town Center Code allowed for a private school with a Conditional Use Permit and
a limit of fifteen students. There were not proposed changes to the site. The City Staff conducted
a parking study and the total required parking spaces were eighteen. The property currently had
twenty-one spaces, complying with the requirement. The Town Center Code did not allow street
parking to count toward the required spaces, however, at the time that this property came to the
City for Site Plan approval that was not the case. The twenty-one spaces included street parking.
Additionally, there would be three classrooms. She displayed images of the property.

Chairman Adams asked if the parking would be an issue with the other building on site.

Ms. Jastremsky noted the parking would be sufficient and referred to page three of the packet
provided to the Commission for the parking breakdown. She detailed the buildings on site. There
were ten onsite spaces and eleven offsite.

Commissioner Van Hoff was also concerned with the offsite parking. He asked what ADA
loading, on the site plan, referenced.

Ms. Jastremsky replied there was an ADA space in that location. She wanted to clarify that the
area next to that space was marked as a loading area and not an additional parking stall.

Applicant Christine Kremer detailed the school as an environment for kids who struggle in a
traditional classroom. Upon student entry at the school, they would be tested, and customized
curriculum for each student would be made. The school would have one teacher per five
students. The Granite School District calendar would be followed.

Commissioner Van Hoff asked if the school was for grades one through twelve and to help them
integrate into a traditional school.

Ms. Kremer noted it would depend on the child. The school only taught up to grade eight.
Students in the past had been caught up and then went to public schools. Most students were
retained up to eighth grade.

Chairperson Adams opened the public hearing.

There were no comments, and the public hearing was closed.

Motion:        Commissioner Squire moved to approve the request of Christine Kremer and Bret
               Steel for Draper Academy, Conditional Use Permit to operate a Private School on
               0.41 acres of property located at 12390 South 800 East in the TC (Town Center)
               zone, Application USE-960-2020, based on the findings and subject to the
               conditions of the Staff Report dated August 6, 2020. Commissioner Van Hoff
               seconded the motion.

   7.   Public Hearing: Draper Wingate Hotel Site Plan Request (Administrative Action)

On the request of Alan Melchior, for a Site Plan approval to allow the property at 12361 South
Minuteman Dr. be developed as a hotel. The property is 2.4 acres in size and located within the
CSD-DP (Draper Peaks Commercial Special District) zone. Application SPR-979-2020. Staff
contact is Jennifer Jastremsky, 801-576-6328, jennifer.jastremsky@draper.ut.us.

Ms. Jastremsky presented. The Draper Crossing development predated the CSD zoning and had
design standards that development would follow. The Draper Wingate Hotel would include a
single, ninety-eight room hotel building where the existing restaurant was located. Parking would
be to the South and East of the hotel. The access points would remain as they currently were. The
site would have shared cross access with all of the neighboring properties. There were 137
parking spaces onsite that conformed to the requirements for parking. There were 39 additional
spaces from the minimum required based on hotel rooms.

Tree diamonds were required in the CSD and were found in the parking lot. There was 12%
overall landscaping and complied with the standards. The site would be heavily landscaped
around the entries and along Minuteman Way and Pioneer Road.

The architecture was a four-story building at fifty-five feet tall. There was a text amendment to
the Draper Peak CSD to increase building height, and this building did comply with the fifty-
five-foot height limit. The building materials would be EIFS and stone. The CSD required
architectural theming that including the stone and hyped roofline features which the building
would have. She presented pictures of the existing site.

Commissioner Van Hoff noted that the report indicated it was two lots and would be
consolidated. He asked if the property had to be consolidated if the parking was on one parcel
and the building on the other. He asked how the Commission could ensure that the parking
would be sufficient.

Ms. Jastremsky responded that many times a commercial property would contain multiple
parcels. When there was cross access and parking agreements, the parking did not need to be on
the specific property. There was a shared parking agreement with Draper Crossing. The City
Staff felt there was plenty of parking and access to parking. The applicant was currently applying
for a lot consolidation and would be complete soon.

Chairman Adams asked for clarification on Commissioner Van Hoff’s comment that the lot
could not be built without the parcel to the South being developed with parking stalls as shown.

include the parking, and the applicant would not be able to build the hotel without the parking.

their site, they would not meet their parking requirements.

Chairman Adams noted that the applicants Allen Summerhays and Alan Melchoir were on the

A request by David Smith for approval of an Amended Plat in the RA2 (Residential
Agricultural) zone amending the Crossgrove House Subdivision for the development of lot 2 for
a single-family home. The property was located at 12726 South Boulter St. Staff Contact: Travis
Van Ekelenburg, 801-576-6522, travis.vanekelenburg@draper.ut.us.

City Planner Travis Van Ekelenburg presented. The Crossgrove House Subdivision was
subdivided in February of 2018 for the future development of a single-family home. The
property was currently zoned RA2, Residential Agricultural, with a 20,000 square foot minimum
lot size. The property met the minimum requirements for the zone. The Land Use Map of the
General Plan called for low to medium density land use designation for the property. RA2 was
listed as a compatible zone for the land use designation. The applicant proposed two changes to
the original plot. They suggested removing note five from the plan stipulating no lots would have
access to Tanburhan Lane and no private lanes.

Additionally, they would add asphalt width of Tanburhan Lane. The lane would be twenty-one
feet in width and comply with the standards. The setbacks of the lot would be thirty feet from
Tanburhan Lane and Boulter Street, and have12-foot setback from the South and West property
lines.

The applicant had submitted a request to deviate from street standards for Tanburhan Lane.
Specifically, the applicant requested to eliminate curb and gutter requirements for private lanes
found in 17-5-030(D) stating “curbs, gutters, sidewalks, shall be installed on existing and
proposed streets by the subdivider in all subdivisions. The Planning Commission may, after
receiving a recommendation from City Staff modify or waive the street design requirements.”

The Draper Engineering Division recommended that the applicant be granted a waiver deviating
from the street design requirements per section 17-5-030(D).

He provided pictures of the site. There were two letters of support sent to the Commission, via
emails, supporting the application and deviation request.

The Staff recommended approval based on the conditions listed in the Staff Report. There were
also model motions for the Commissions consideration.

Commissioner Kent Player asked if the lot access would be on Tanburhan Lane along the
Eastern edge.

Mr. Van Ekelenburg replied, yes, but that the location was undetermined. He imagined it would
be toward the Easter Edge.

Applicant David Smith, 1128 E Baldwin Lane, he noted they live three blocks West of the lot
and felt it was a unique location. The access point was moving to Tanburhan Lane to the West
end of the lot. It would eliminate a 200-foot lane being constructed from Boulter Lane and
destroying the front yard area and historical trees over 100 years old in the middle of the lot. The
wanted to preserve the historical aspects by waiving the curb and gutter requirements to keep a

Commissioner Squire asked the Staff if they did not grant the deviation for curbs and gutters,
would it only be that section of road that would be required to have curb and gutter.

Mr. Van Ekelenburg stated that was correct.

Commissioner Squire asked if Tanburhan Lane was a private lane and if there was a requirement
that private lanes have a curb and gutter. Additionally, she asked if private lanes were not
required to have the width of a public road.

Mr. Van Ekelenburg noted it was a private lane and that private lanes were required to upgrade
the access. The width of private lanes was determined by the number of lots and what the
improvements would need to be.

Commissioner Player commented that he was glad that Mr. Smith worked with his neighbors and

Commissioner Squire agreed with Commissioner Player.
