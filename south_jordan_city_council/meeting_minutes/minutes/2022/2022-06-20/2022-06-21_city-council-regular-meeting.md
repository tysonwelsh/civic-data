to a short-term rental. She frankly doesn’t know how it will go. It might be fine, it might not be

Mayor Ramsey noted that usually this isn’t a time that they answer back, but she asked City
Attorney Ryan Loose to fill Ms. Stowe in on what happened legislatively a couple of years ago.

City Attorney Ryan Loose said it might be best to talk after, as there is kind of a lengthy history
to this. Generally, with short-term rentals, we have the ability to regulate some things about
them. However, we have no ability to enforce that regulation because about four years,
Representative Knotwell, who represented part of South Jordan and Herriman, ran and passed
legislation saying we couldn’t use booking a short-term rental as evidence they are breaking our
rules. This has created a problem where many cities have different rules and requirements on the
books, but actually enforcing that is near impossible since you can’t bring any actual evidence
into a court because we are preempted. There are discussions at legislature of possibly changing
some of that, as short-term rentals are eating into the market for affordable housing, and that has
legislators concerned. We are looking at some regulations, currently we have none in South
Jordan, and we haven’t had any real problems with them until recently when we have started
seeing the same things being seen elsewhere. Things like a chartered bus coming in, parking in
the driveway and then 30 people going into the home, obviously that’s not what it’s for. Up until
now we have had a few rented out, that we were aware of, that were reported as rented out for
like a Thanksgiving Day party where they park up and down the street, but so does everyone
else’s guests. Everyone is annoyed that a short-term rental is bringing in cars, but someone
would be there anyways and there is nothing illegal about parking on a public street. We are
looking at what regulations we can do, and how we can enforce it. That has been assigned to an
attorney in his office, and they are going through that currently. If there are specific issues with a
short-term rental, that is a neighbor of yours, contact code enforcement at the city; unless it is a
police action, then obviously dial the police. If we can address it, we will, if we can’t address it,
we won’t. In regards to business licensing, even that in the past four or five years has seen the
city’s ability to license home occupation business severely curtailed. The majority of them we
don’t even license anymore unless they have either customers coming there, or some other
clients.

Director of City Commerce Brian Preece reviewed background information from this
meeting’s packet of supporting documents. He noted that with the cost of land and
construction, he has been discussing with Tom Buckley how the city could help. They
discussed doing an agreement similar to Riverton Chevrolet, the Nissan Dealership of Tim
Dahle, and Southtowne Volkswagen. Basically, there is a sales tax rebate; this is not any
kind of property tax rebate, only a sales tax rebate. This is usually about 30%, with an
annual cap and then a total cap. This means it is all post-performance, we don’t put out any
money until we actually receive it, and it’s of the half a percent. Those deals have worked
South Jordan City                                                                                  8

out very well. Most years, if we didn’t have the cap, the 30% would have equaled two to
two and a half times what we are actually giving them. As everyone knows, House Bill 151
was passed last legislative session, which prohibits cities after July 1 of this year from
entering into these types of agreements in the future. He has conferred with our City
Attorney Ryan Loose and RDA Attorney Craig Smith, along with other cities, and it has
been understood that per the law, if you already have an agreement started you vest under
the previous ordinances. We are here tonight, to see if we can get the board to approve a
memorandum of understanding. It does not obligate either side to anything, but it does give
us the ability to start negotiating details. With the others, he has looked at their pro formas
and whatnot, and we have tailored it to the pro forma and what makes sense. This way it’s
good for them, but we also aren’t giving away too much.

Chair Ramsey added that House Bill 151 doesn’t allow this unless there is a significant
housing component that goes along with it, which for a car dealership there would not be.

Director Preece said there are a few things, if infrastructure is involved or you are in a rural
