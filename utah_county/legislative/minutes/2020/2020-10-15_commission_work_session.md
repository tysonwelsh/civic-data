HISTORICALLY, THE COUNTY SURVEYOR’S OFFICE ORIGINATED IN 1898 —
THAT’S AS FAR BACK WE CAN TRACE IT ANYWAY - BUT THE LAST 75 YEARS
FROM ABOUT THE MID 1940’S TO THE PRESENT DAY, THE OFFICE OF THE
COUNTY SURVEYORS HAS BEEN BASICALLY 3 COUNTY SURVEYORS. THE ONE
COUNTY SURVEYOR THAT WAS HERE FOREVER AND HE DID SOME AMAZING
THINGS THAT WAS REALLY PROGRESSIVE IN NATURE FOR THAT TIME FRAME
AND IT’S AMAZING THAT PEOPLE STILL TODAY THAT BRAG ABOUT HOW GREAT
THIS OFFICE IS DON’T REALIZE THE HISTORY OF HOW THAT ALL CAME ABOUT.
IT CAME ABOUT STARTING CLEAR BACK IN THE 40’S, 50’S AND 60’S. WHAT I’M
GETTING AT HERE, THE DEPUTY SURVEYOR UNDERNEATH THAT COUNTY
SURVEYOR WHEN HE RETIRED WAS THE COUNTY SURVEYOR THAT I FIRST
STARTED WORKING FOR WHEN I STARTED IN 1980. AND HE IN TURN WAS THE
DEPUTY SURVEYOR TO THAT ORIGINAL SURVEYOR THAT I’M BRAGGING ABOUT
ALITTLE BIT AND THAT SURVEYOR I STARTED WORKING FOR IN 1980. HE WAS
VERY PROGRESSIVELY MINDED. I STARTED AGAIN IN 1980 AND IN 1990 HE SENT
ME BACK TO SUNNYVALE, CALIFORNIA HEADQUARTERS OF TRIMBLE
NAVIGATION AND TRIMBLE NAVIGATION WAS THE FIRST MAJOR COMPANY OR
CORPORATION THAT GOT PERMISSION FROM THE FEDERAL GOVERNMENT
BASICALLY FROM THE DEPARTMENT OF DEFENSE, TO UTILIZE SATELLITE
TECHNOLOGY FOR SURVEYING PURPOSES AND UTAH COUNTY WAS THE FIRST
GOVERNMENT ENTITY IN THE STATE OF UTAH TO ACQUIRE GPS TECHNOLOGY.
THIS IS IN 1990. THERE WAS ONE PRIVATE ENTITY DOWN ON THE SOUTHERN
END OF THE STATE THAT HAD IT, BUT UTAH COUNTY WAS THE VERY FIRST
GOVERNMENT ENTITY. AND BACK IN THAT DAY THE COUNTY INVESTED ABOUT
$110,000 IN THIS GPS EQUIPMENT. IT AMOUNTED TO THREE LARGE BOXES AND
THEY WERE HEAVY, BULKY, AWKWARD. YOU PUT THEM ON YOUR BACK AND
YOU HIKED UP THE HILL OR WHEREVER YOU’RE GOING AND THEY WERE
BASICALLY SATELLITE RECEIVERS. IN ADDITION TO THE RECEIVERS THEY HAD
ANTENNAS. YOU HAD A TRIPOD TO PACK. THERE WAS A LOT TO BE ABLE TO
GATHER THE GPS DATA AND WE DID THAT FOR A NUMBER OF YEARS. IT WASN’T
JUST ON THE BACK. IT WASN’T ON YOUR PHONE OR YOUR WI-FI - THIS
OBVIOUSLY PREDATED THE PHONE, THE CAR GPS, OR BOAT GPS. WE WERE
LITERALLY PIONEERING THE TECHNOLOGY. WE WERE EXPERIMENTING WITH
THINGS THAT TRIMBLE NAVIGATION WASN’T EVEN SURE THAT IT WOULD DO.
WE WERE TRYING TO BE PROGRESSIVE THAT WAY AND THESE THINGS I’M
TALKING ABOUT ARE THINGS THAT WERE LATER ABLE TO CREATE THE
TECHNOLOGY TO DO BUT BECAME AN UPGRADE TO THESE BIG BULKY
RECEIVERS.

I’LL BE HONEST WITH YOU, WHEN I WAS BACK THERE IN 1990 TO SUNNYVALE,
CALIFORNIA TO LEARN ABOUT THIS, I WENT KICKING AND SCREAMING.
SHORTLY AFTER THAT TIME FRAME WE BECAME A LITTLE MORE
PROGRESSIVE WHEN SOME OF THE OTHER GPS TECHNOLOGY CAME OUT AND
WE FOUND OUT WE COULD BE MORE MOBILE AND NOT HAVE TO PACK THESE
BIG BULKY RECEIVERS AROUND. IT WAS AROUND THAT TIME THAT I WAS
FORTUNATE TO HIRE A PERSON BY THE NAME OF ANTHONY CANTO. MR. CANTO
IS A VERY INTELLIGENT MANS. HE’LL BE A GREAT COUNTY SURVEYOR. HE
ALWAYS HAS BEEN PROGRESSIVELY MINDED. I WOULD ASK THE COMMISSION
TO CONTINUE AS MUCH AS YOU POSSIBLY CAN TO GIVE HIM THE SUPPORT AND
THE VENUES WHEN IT COMES TO TECHNOLOGICAL ADVANCES. I GUESS IN

Factors to consider:

+ Admissible evidence

* Prior criminal history

+ Extraneous facts of the case, which may or not be admissible, but which give
context to the question of whether a criminal charge is brought

+ The Defendant's constitutional rights

+ The overall circumstances of the defendant, including whether the defendant's
prospect of success In society will be benefitted by participation in the criminal
Justice system

+ The criminal justice systems ability to handle the case effectively when viewing
the totality of other cases currently in the system

* The balancing of the need for community protection vs. need for punishment

How has it changed our practices

Specific case examples:

Retail theft case: defendant stole a Chapstick {valued at $2.69) from local
store and because of prior convictions for theft, he was arrested for felony
theft, Incarcerated on $5000.00 bail.

Under the traditional system, Defendant was charged with a felony in the
district court,
Under the new system, the case was filed as a class B misdemeanor theft
charges in the Utah County Justice Court pursuant to HB 300,

How has it changed our practices

Specific case examples:

Protective Order Violation: father was charged with violating protective
order when he learned that his son was in the emergency room and he
called his ex-wife to find his son which violated the protective order.

Under the traditional system, Defendant would have been charged in the
district court with a fefony resulting in a criminal record for the father.

Under the new system, the screening division declined charges because
there was no criminal intent to violate the statute or protective order,
Although it was a technical violation of the statute.

How has it changed our practices

Specific case examples:

Drug possession: defendant was in a room with three peaple when officers
entered and found several glass pipes. Defendant admitted that one of the
pipes was hers and since ithad residue of methamphetamine, she

was arrested on a third degree felony because she had prior convictions
several years old.

Under the traditional system, Defendant would have been charged in the
district court with a felony.

Under the new system, Because she did not have any actual meth in her
possession and had already received services in the district court, the
screening team did not file the charges,
