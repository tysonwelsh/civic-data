Proposed Ordinance 2023-7 amending the zoning ordinance to establish
standards for converting hotels and motels to multiple-family dwellings

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed zone text amendment
relating to hotel and motel conversions to multi-family residences came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated
Administration is proposing an amendment to the zoning ordinance which would establish standards for converting a hotel or
motel to a multiple-family dwelling. Ogden has several older hotels and motels that are losing their suitability as short-term
lodging facilities. The City has received some proposals to convert these motels into multiple family dwellings. Administrative
staff is concerned that, if these conversions are not done carefully, the resulting housing could result in undesirable living
conditions for residents and create various crime and social issues. To encourage housing that is safe and livable, staff has
proposed the following, with added input from the Planning Commission:

|. Encourage more mix of incomes and household types, require a certain percentage of units to be one and two-
bedroom units.

2. Require specific elements, such as stoves, ovens, refrigerators, counters, and cabinets, to be included in motel-
specific conversions, which otherwise typically do not have kitchens, or only have small kitchenettes.

3. Require facilities with sixteen or more rooms to provide 24-hour on-site management, to manage potential
crime and social issues.

4. Encourage attractive and livable facilities, set standards for open space, amenities, and architectural design.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
purpose behind the proposed zone text amendment. He referenced previous discussions with the Council regarding the proposal
and highlighted their concerns regarding the use of gas appliances in hotel units converted to multi-family units. He noted this is a
policy decision of the Council to make but offered information regarding the pros and cons of the use of natural gas appliances
and a pie chart highlighting the popularity of natural gas appliances in industrial, residential, and commercial kitchens. He
concluded the Planning Commission reviewed this item on November 2, 2022, and recommended that the Council amend the
zoning ordinance to establish standards for converting hotels and motels to multiple-family dwellings. The Commission found that
the amendments were needed to create safe and livable housing if hotels and motels are converted into apartments. The
Commission voted seven to one in favor of the amendment. Commissioner Southwick was the lone no-vote, stating that the
requirement to provide 24-hour on-site management was too burdensome, and he otherwise supported the amendment.

Council member White stated that she it is not her goal to support a ban on natural gas appliances, but she would like to
promote the use of appliances that have a higher energy efficiency rating; she has provided documentation supporting this request
to City Administration and asked that the ordinance language be adjusted to incorporate this recommendation. The Council offers
support for tabling this action to give Council staff and Planning staff time to work together to amend the ordinance to incorporate
language in support of the use of energy efficient appliances.

Proposed Ordinance 2023-8 amending the General Plan by amending the
Housing Element to meet the State of Utah requirements for moderate :
income housing :
A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed General Plan amendment
for the Moderate-Income Housing Implementation Plan came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Planning
Division has been working to meet the requirements of Utah state law with regard to a moderate-income housing plan. The
requirements, commonly referred to as requirements of HB 462, require each city to develop and submit a moderate-income
housing plan and update the housing section of the City’s General Plan with specifics regarding how the City will address
moderate income housing. Ogden City has been in compliance with the requirements of HB 462 in preparing and submitting a
moderate-income housing report for the last several years; however, the City did not meet the October 2022 deadline for updating :
the General Plan. This proposal would bring the City into compliance with that requirement.
Senior Planner Rypien summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to define terms relating to
the subject matter. Moderate income housing is defined as 80 percent of the County’s area median income (AMI); Weber
County’s AMI is $74,345 and 80 percent of that number is $59,476. Cost burdened is when a household spends more than 30
percent of their gross income on housing. He presented a formula indicating that $59,476 divided by 12 is $4,956.33 per month;
30 percent of that number is $1,487. Moderate income households can afford a $277,000 home, based on a 30-year mortgage with
a five percent interest rate. The State of Utah has adopted legislation aimed at increasing access to affordable or moderate-income
housing; in order to be in compliance with this legislation, City Administration recommends the adoption of a number of
strategies aimed at ensuring an appropriate amount of moderate-income housing in the community. The Council’s role in this
\ process is to adopt a General Plan amendment with the proposed changes to the Housing Element regarding Moderate-Income
| Housing with its strategies and implementation plan. Mr. Rypien concluded the Planning Commission reviewed this item on
December 7, 2022 and voted to recommend approval of the General Plan Amendment based on the findings that it is consistent
with the Utah State Code 10-9a-403 meeting all minimum requirements specific to moderate-income housing.
