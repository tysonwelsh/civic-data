1.     PUBLIC MEETING
       An Appeal for the Variance Application Denial on the Lot Size in the Commercial
       Corridor Land Use District for a Drive-Up Window, located at the Corner of 3300 South
       200 East.
       ACTION ITEM
       Applicant: Wendy’s, Represented by Dominion Engineering
       Address:    203 East 3300 South

City Planner, Jed Shum, presented the Staff Report and reviewed the appeal process. He explained
that the original application was denied by the Community Development Director and this application
was an appeal of that denial. Mr. Shum reviewed the variance and appeal sections from Title 17
involving the review process and authority of the Planning Commission as well as the Appeal
Authority determination.

With regard to Appeals:

An appeal from the Community Development Director's decision regarding an interpretation of this
Title, or on any Land Use Application, shall be made to the Planning Commission by any person
aggrieved by the interpretation or decision within 10 days of the Community Development
Director's written Land Use Decision, stating each fact and every theory of relief on appeal.

       1.     The Planning Commission conducts each appeal de novo.

       2.     The Planning Commission shall act as a quasi-judicial body and shall afford due
              process to the parties on appeal.

       3.     Each party may prepare [and] call such witnesses and present such evidence as it
              deems appropriate.

       4.     Only witnesses called by a party may testify.

       5.     After hearing all evidence and legal arguments presented by the parties, the Planning
              Commission shall apply the plain language of the Code and issue written finding of
              facts, conclusions of law, and a decision on the merits of all theories of relief the
              appellant has raised in the appeal.

       6.     If a Land Use Regulation does not plainly restrict a Land Use Application, the Planning
              Commission shall interpret and apply the Land Use Regulation to favor the Land Use
              Application.

       7.     The Planning Commission shall reverse the decision of the Community Development
              Director only if the Director's decision is not supported by substantial evidence in the
              record or is otherwise arbitrary, capricious, or illegal.

       8.      The Final Order of the Planning Commission acting as an appeal authority is a final
               decision, appealable to the district court.

       9.      Unless otherwise stated in the Planning Commission's final decision, an order
               following a de novo review vacates any official determination made by the land use
               authority.

       10.     No further administrative appeals are permitted from a final order of an appellate
               authority and any subsequent review is to be made by the district court.

       11.     Appeals to district court shall be made within 30 days of the final decision.

When granting variances, the Community Development Director may grant a variance only if:

       1.      Literal enforcement of the Land Use Regulations would cause an unreasonable
               hardship for the Applicant that is not necessary to carry out the general purpose of the
               Land Use Regulations.

       2.      There are special circumstances attached to the property that do not generally apply to
               other properties in the same district.

       3.      Granting the variance is essential to the enjoyment of a substantial property right
               possessed by other property in the same zoning district.

       4.      The variance will not substantially affect the general plan and will not be contrary to
               the public interest.

       5.      The spirit of the land use ordinance is observed, and substantial justice is done.

Mr. Shum clarified that the appellant has met all five conditions listed above.

In determining whether or not enforcement of the land use ordinance would cause unreasonable
hardship under Subsection (B)(1), the appeal authority may not find an unreasonable hardship unless
the alleged hardship:

       1.      Is located on or associated with the property for which the variance is sought.

       2.      Comes from circumstances peculiar to the property, not from conditions that are
               general to the neighborhood.

       3.      In determining whether or not enforcement of the land use ordinance would cause
               unreasonable hardship under Subsection (B)(1), the appeal authority may not find an
               unreasonable hardship if the hardship is self-imposed or economic.

       4.      In determining whether or not there are special circumstances attached to the property
               under Subsection (B)(1), the appeal authority may find that special circumstances exist
               only if the special circumstances:

               a.     Relate to the hardship complained of; and
               b.     Deprive the property of privileges granted to other properties in the same
                      zoning district.

Mr. Shum reported that the request is to combine four lots for a new drive-up window development
on the corner of 3300 South 200 East. This is a permitted use in the Commercial Corridor District
according to the matrix. The original variance application was to request an exception to the Minimum
Lot Size standard as set forth in the Commercial Corridor Land Use District, which has a minimum
lot size of one acre. There are two existing structures at the subject location including Speedy Auto
Repair, which is still in operation, and the abandoned Amber Restaurant. Mr. Shum presented the
ALTA survey submitted by the applicant showing the property lines. The lot size is 0.804 acres, which
is less than the required minimum. The proposed site plan for a Wendy’s restaurant was presented.

Mr. Shum reviewed the Conclusions of Law set forth in the Staff Report as follows:

       1.      Without a variance, the applicant’s use as proposed, does not meet the required
               minimum lot size requirements in the Commercial Corridor Land Use District. The
               minimum requirement is 1 acre and, the proposed lot size is 0.8 acre.

       2.      The applicant has not provided sufficient evidence to show an unreasonable hardship
               associated with this property. An unreasonable hardship refers to difficulty in
               complying with a zoning ordinance because of circumstances unique to the property
               and not to conditions general to the area. A hardship may not be self-imposed or solely
               economic. Smaller parcel sizes are not a unique circumstance or characteristic of
               property in South Salt Lake or that particular area. The proposed newly combined
               property is a self-imposed, economic hardship where compliance with the Code may
               be achieved through acquiring more properties.

       3.      The applicant has not provided sufficient evidence to show any special circumstances
               attached to the property. Special circumstances refer to physical conditions unique to
               the property, which relate to the hardship, and make compliance difficult or
               impossible. The adjacent lots are developable, there are not any circumstances that
               additional acreage, site planning, and development cannot overcome, therefore
               circumstances are not of difficulty or impossibility, but rather economic.

       4.      The applicant has not shown that granting a variance for the lot is essential to the
               enjoyment of a substantial property right possessed by other property in the same
               zoning district. The properties have existing, permitted uses as an auto repair and
               service and a restaurant that may continue, similar to nearby properties.

       5.      The applicant has shown that the variance would not violate the goals of the General
               Plan, however, the variance would be contrary to the public’s interest as other future
               new developments within the Commercial Corridor that have sufficient property or

               have acquired sufficient property to meet the minimum one-acre lot size requirements.
               If this variance is granted, the minimum lot size requirement may become an unfair
               burden on other future developments that are combining lots.

Mr. Shum explained that based on the information presented, staff recommends that the Planning
Commission move to deny the Appellant’s request based on the Findings of Fact and Conclusions of
Law set forth in the Staff Report.

E. Farley Eskelson and Andrew Horne from Dominion Engineering were present representing the
Appellant who is requesting that a Wendy’s Restaurant be built on the site. Mr. Eskelson reported
that four parcels are to be combined on .85 acres, which does not meet the minimum standard for the

back property to the north. Both are owned by SYFY Properties, Inc. and utilize a prescriptive
easement. The owners were contacted and are not interested in selling either the driveway or the
property. The site plan contemplated the removal of both the Speedy Auto Repair and Amber
Restaurant to construct a Wendy’s Restaurant. The easement and the owned property equate to 1.04
acres, which meets the minimum. He noted that the driveway to the east will always be a driveway
and cannot be anything else because of the easement and the access to 3300 South. The two buildings
on the property were to be removed.

Mr. Eskelson explained that they will keep and use the driveway to the east. The parking near the
