removal of three units from the project. He highlighted the existing property uses and said

1.2 Consideration of CU-19-006, Conditional Use Permit for a Mixed-Use Development

Consisting of 3800 Square Feet of Commercial Space and 176 Dwellings Location: 3999

South Main Street Applicant: JF Capital Planner: Blaine Gehring

Blaine Gehring said this property was recently rezoned to M-D for the purpose of allowing a

new mixed-use project. Under the M-D Zone, mixed-use projects are a conditional use. The

applicant, JF Glenwood, LLC, was requesting a conditional use permit to develop a mixed-
use development consisting of 3,800 square feet of commercial space and 176 residential

units. The project would be developed on three existing parcels between 3975 and 3999 S.

Main Street and the property consists of 1.89 acres. Nearby multi-family residential uses are

the Meadowbrook Apartments being developed on the southwest corner of 3900 South and

Main and the Bud Bailey apartments on West Temple. The site is within one-half mile from

the Meadowbrook TRAX Station. The project consists of 1 mixed-use building with 2 levels

of parking and 4 levels of apartments. The building has 3,800 square feet of commercial
space and 117 2-bedroom and 59 1-bedroom apartments for a total of 176 units and a height
of 66 feet 6 inches. The commercial space has been reduced by 2,200 square feet from the
original proposal to allow for additional parking. The parking has been modified to meet the
required 1.7 spaces per unit as recommended by the community council and Planning

Commission in April. The site falls within the Meadowbrook Center area in the new General

Plan which is an area of a greater mix of uses including more higher density residential.

Development in the Meadowbrook center is intended to be mid-rise in scale. At their meeting

on July 2, 2019, the Millcreek Community Council voted to send a recommendation for

approval of the conditional use permit CU-19-006 by a vote of 7 in favor and one abstain.

Commissioner LaMar asked about the 1.72 parking ratio. Gehring said it was originally a

1.56 ratio and the approved development agreement said 1.7. He said the guest parking was

included in that figure. Commissioner LaMar said that 1.7 should be the ratio for residential,

not total parking.

Jake Wood, JF Capital, said he has tried to be as collaborative as possible.

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 17 July 2019 Page 5 of 12
Amber Measom, Millcreek Community Council, said the developer met the 1.7 ratio. She
said traffic is a concern in the area, but the project was great.

Vice Chair Stephens opened the public hearing.
Dave Hogan, 4035 S. Main Street, asked about the commercial space decreasing in size and
not the residential. He said the traffic in the area is a huge problem; there is no right turn lane
from Main Street to 3900 South. He asked why there was not any concern to add that from
the Meadowbrook side. Lilly said Millcreek got a grant to improve the cross section of Main
Street which includes a parking shoulder, two traffic lanes, a bike lane, and a turn lane. Lilly
said the existing right-of-way encompassed the space to accommodate those improvements.
Ella Loveland, 728 E. Roosevelt Avenue, asked about the irrigation ditch. She said the ditch
must be kept intact and she brought up the responsibility of the culvert. She said the culvert
crosses Main Street but was not sure if it crossed the site property. Vice Chair Stephens said
the project would go through a technical review process after the preliminary approval. John
Brems said the irrigation ditch would be taken care of either through the road expansion or as
part of the development.
Vice Chair Stephens closed the public hearing.
Commissioner LaMar said there were only nine proposed parking spots for the commercial
property. He said the project was 20% under code requirements. He said the 1.72 ratio can be
misleading based on the project. He said it should have 294 residential spots and 54 guest
parking spots. Vice Chair Stephens said the Planning Commission should have been more
precise when coming up with the parking ratio, so it did not come at the expense of the
commercial parking. Commissioner LaMar said the bedroom unit makeup was the variable.
Commissioner Reid offered that a consideration for reduced parking be allowed when within
close proximity to public transit. Commissioner LaMar said he was in favor of the proposal
except the parking. Vice Chair Stephens brought up that there should be no additional charge
for residential parking. Commissioner Claerhout asked about reducing the units instead of the
commercial space to increase parking. Gehring said the applicant met the 1.7-unit ratio.
Wood said the project was based on code in maintaining 50% commercial frontage. The
developers originally proposed 100% frontage but had since reduced it. He said apartments
are more predictable economically and he has been trying to make the best project possible
while meeting code. Commissioner Booth asked about reconciling the parking discrepancy.
Gehring said the Planning Commission asked for the 1.7 ratio in the development agreement
and the Council approved it.

Commissioner Booth moved to approve the conditional use CU-19-006 located between

3975 and 3999 S. Main subject to the conditions in the development agreement adopted by

the City Council. Commissioner Reid seconded. Commissioner Booth said the Commission

went through the development agreement process which had a lot of requirements. Vice Chair

Stephens called for the vote. Commissioners Claerhout, Reid, Booth, Stephens, and Allen

voted yes. Commissioner LaMar abstained. The motion passed.

1.3 Consideration of ZM-19-013, Rezone from A-1 to RM Subject to a Development
Agreement for a Senior Housing Condominium Development Location: 4046 S. 700 W.
Applicant: Rick Whiting Planner: Blaine Gehring
Blaine Gehring said Rick Whiting was requesting to rezone property located at 4046 South
and 700 West from A-1 to R-M to facilitate the development of a 23-unit 55+ senior
condominium development. The three-story condominiums would be no higher than 30 feet
in height and would have 8-1,280 square foot units, 10-1,200 square foot units and 5
penthouse units. Covered parking is included with 38 underground parking spaces and 7
uncovered guest parking spaces. An earlier rezone request was made by Whiting to rezone
the property to build an 11-unit townhome Planned Unit Development. That project received
a recommendation of denial from the Millcreek Community Council after a neighborhood
meeting in which the neighbors were opposed to the density and height of the project. At that
time, both the neighborhood and the community council were favorable towards a 55+ senior
housing project on the site at a height no greater than 28 feet. Gehring said that Whiting has
tried to meet those recommendations. Currently the subject property is zoned A-1 and
consists of approximately 1.06 acres with approximately 117 feet of frontage along 700
West, a public road. The property has an existing single-family dwelling located on the
premises which will remain. The property is mostly vacant and has served as horse pasture
for many years. The adjacent zoning is A-1 to the south, north and west with the Sunnyvale
Park in A-2 along with M to the east and R-M to the north. Millcreek’s Future Land Use Map
indicates the area to be designated as “Neighborhood 2 & 3” to accommodate a mix of
different higher density housing types. Such density would allow for a range of 6-35 units per
acre with heights up to 2-4 stories. At their meeting on July 2, 2019, the Millcreek
Community Council voted to recommend denial of the zone change by a unanimous vote of
7 members.
Commissioner Allen asked about the history of the proposals. Gehring said it was previously
an 11-unit townhome project that was recommended for approval to the City Council.
Commissioner Reid asked about a staging plan for the area for greater density. Gehring said
the General Plan outlines specific land uses recommendations for areas, developments have
to apply for the use project by project. Vice Chair Stephens asked about the conditions in the
staff report and read them to the Commission. The conditions were:

1. Limited to 23 55+ senior condominiums units as indicated in site plan;

2. Limited to a maximum of 30 feet in height;

3. Minimum off-street parking requirements specified under Chapter 19.80 of the Millcreek Code
for a 23 55+ senior condominium unit development, including 7 additional guest parking stalls
required by the Planning Commission;

4. Dark sky compliant street lighting only;

5. Secured boundaries and gated entry required;

6. Upgraded fencing with landscaping and shrubs on the perimeter of the project as per site plan;

7. Tree lined driveway with landscaping and shrubs as indicated in site plan;

8. Security System (Including a temporary surveillance camera for Loveland’s property during
construction);

9. Development must have CC&R’s approved by Millcreek City Attorney and at minimum include:
a. Pets must be leashed at all times;

b. Homeowners and guests must acknowledge and accept the neighboring agricultural uses
including odors and the natural and potentially offensive behaviors of farm animals;

11. No guest parking allowed along 700 West;

12. Rentals of units are prohibited;
