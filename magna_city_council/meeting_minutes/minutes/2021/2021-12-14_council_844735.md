	Chief Del Craig reported that Magna Precinct’s largest increase this month has been burglaries and vandalisms, but calls were down in November by 150 calls. Resolution 21-12-04

	J.D. Kesler, Attorney, Parsons Behle & Latimer, reviewed Resolution 21-12-04 authorizing the condemnation of real property owned by Anderson Properties, Inc., to extend 4100 South west beyond 8400 West to provide access to the new high school as well as residential development.  It has been designed to be a straight road for safety reasons, in part where there can be a traffic light.  However, that will require using some of the Anderson Properties property – a .626 acre parcel, a little over 27,000 square feet.  Otherwise, the road would have to veer to the south. 

	Dave Anderson, Anderson Properties Inc., stated the property being taken from Anderson Properties is a family business that has been in place for a long time, and it has already been struggling.  Taking this property by eminent domain is going to crush it.  His family initially received an offer of $70,000 to purchase the property.  Then, 20 days later, it got another offer of $270,000, after it was appraised for that amount.  His family rejected that because it did not cover expected costs.  He suggested an offer closer to $400,000 be considered, and that a compromise be reached without his family being threatened, as it has been.  

	Paul Ashton stated the Magna Metro Township did everything it could to try to avoid condemnation.  Initially, it designed the road to curve into the subdivision, but West Valley City, which owns half the road, refused to grant permission to do that.  The reality is the high school is being built, and the road has to go in.  He recommended approving the resolution as well as approaching West Valley City again, since it has had a change in administration.  

	Council Member Prokopis suggested tabling this until the Magna Metro Township has exhausted every other possibility.  It sounded like Anderson Properties wanted to compromise.  He would be skeptical too if an offer went from $70,000 to $270,000 in a couple weeks.  He was worried that once the Council approved this, the property would be condemned before the Council even got a chance to talk to West Valley City.

	Council Member Pierce stated if this were put in place, negotiations would be off the table.  She would rather explore other options.    

	Mr. Kesler stated he was not aware of the $70,000, nor has Anderson Properties counter-offered, so this is the first time he has heard about the $400,000.   The parties can continue to negotiate even if the resolution is passed.  The landowners also have the right to invoke the ombudsman process, which is remediation for additional appraisals, done by an appraiser of their choice that the condemner has to pay for.  He explained the process that would happen after the resolution was approved.  Tabling the approval of the resolution would cause delays that have effects moving forward. 	

	Council Member Peel stated this resolution does not handcuff the property owner from negotiating what they want.  

	Council Member Prokopis stated with condemnation hanging overhead, he thought it did affect them.  He was open to condemning the property, but was not there yet.  He would like to see another month or two of negotiations with the property owner.    

	A Representative from D.R. Horton, stated D.R. Horton has been working with West Valley City.  It submitted at least four designs in the past two years.  There was some leeway on one design that affected this property, but it would require a big sweeping turn, which would affect other parcels on the west side of 8400 West.  It did not make sense to curve a road and create more hazards than if it were straight.

	Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to approve the following resolution authorizing the condemnation of real property owned by Anderson Properties, Inc., with the acknowledgment that it did not prevent Anderson Properties from negotiating the price.  The motion passed 3 to 2, with Council Members Pierce and Prokopis voting in opposition.  

RESOLUTION NO. 21-12-04 	 DATE: December 14, 2021 
 	 
A RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING THE CONDEMNATION OF REAL PROPERTY OWNED BY ANDERSON PROPERTIES, INC. FOR EXTENSION OF 4100 SOUTH  
 
WHEREAS, Magna Metro Township desires to facilitate the extension of 4100 South beyond 8400 West; and  
 
WHEREAS, Township officials or their agents have as yet been unable to acquire the Property from the owner through reasonable negotiation for the estimated market value; and 

WHEREAS, although negotiations are ongoing and may continue, it is necessary to commence condemnation action on the Property in order to proceed with the project without further delay,

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, by the Magna Metro Township Council, Utah, as follows:

Use of the Property for the extension of 4100 South, for access to adjacent properties including a high school, is hereby declared to be a public purpose and necessary for the best interests of Magna Metro Township and its residents.

	Action by the Council and its attorneys to acquire immediate occupancy and ownership of the Property by condemnation is hereby approved.

		PASSED AND APPROVED by the Magna Metro Township Council this 14th day of December, 2021.

Set Public Hearing for Ordinance

		Paul Ashton stated the Salt Lake County Health Department has noise regulations in place, but nothing in statute would preclude the Magna Metro Township from having its own ordinance.
