          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to the Adams
Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) came before the Board for consideration. The memo stated the proposal is for the Board
to approve a budget amendment to the Adams CRA budget from $10,811,794 to $28,520,773. Of the total $28,520,773,
$21,348,418 would be available for development and infrastructure. The remaining funds would be set aside for housing and
administration.
          An additional memo from the Department regarding proposed amendments to interlocal agreements with Ogden City,
Weber County, and Ogden School District pertaining to the Adams CRA came before the Board for consideration. The memo
stated the proposal is for each of the taxing entities to continue participating at 100 percent until 2027 when participation rates
would be reduced from 100 percent to 75 percent for the County and School District and 90 percent for Ogden City. Also, the
proposal includes a request that the school district extend its participation through the end of the term to accommodate the
reduction in participation. The School District’s participation currently ends in 2037. Approval of updated interlocal agreements
will be considered in separate but related actions. RDA staff are currently in the process of negotiating updated interlocal
agreements with the other taxing entities.
           Economic Development Director Meess summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound
on both proposals impacting the Adams CRA; she provided historical information regarding the creation of the Adams CRA plan
and budget in 2018. She also summarized the vision and objectives of the Adams CRA and reported on projects that have been
completed within the CRA to date. The original budget for the CRA was fairly conservative and was capped at $10.8 million, with
$8 million available for development activities. The proposed budget amendment includes the following:
               • Projected investment increases from $95.6M to $133.4M.
               • Maintain project area term (through 2045).
               • Increase total budget from $10.8M to $30.5M.
               • Maintain current participation of 100% through Year 10, then decrease to 75% for County and OSD, 90% for
                    City.
           Ms. Meess then expressed a willingness to address some of the comments made during tonight’s City Council meeting
regarding the development of an apartment complex and other projects within the Adams CRA; staff has considered
redevelopment tools as a means of supporting the long-term financial sustainability of the City and tax increment financing (TIF)
is a vital funding source for redevelopment projects that align with the City’s objectives. She provided a detailed explanation of
how TIF is generated and the impact it has on personal property taxes as well as the City’s property tax revenues.
           The Board engaged in discussion with Ms. Meess regarding the potential for projects completed with the CRA to impact
property values in the property area; Ms. Meess emphasized that the CRA will not directly influence any increase of property tax
rates in the property area, but increased property values could result in higher property taxes for home and business owners in the
project area.

           Board member Richey wished to clarify the manner in which property taxes are determined; if property values increase, a
resident’s property tax bill will not automatically increase because the State of Utah’s taxation laws require the property tax rate to
be adjusted to ensure the City receives the same amount of tax revenue each year. A property owners tax bill will only increase if
the City holds or increases the tax rate year after year.
           A final memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed participation and
incentive agreement with Alpha-Adams Group came before the Board for consideration. The memo stated the proposal is to enter
into a participation and incentive agreement with Alpha-Adams, LLC to build a 101-unit market-rate multi-family housing project
at 2637 Adams Avenue to be known as the Malakite Apartments. The incentive will also include the renovation of an adjacent
commercial building at 457 26th Street. The project area consists of three parcels which include a single-family residential
building and a large surface parking area. The proposed incentive for the project is $4.7 million to be paid annually post-
performance through 2044. With the TIF incentive, the project is proposed to include structured parking, plazas and walkways,
and enhanced exterior finishes. The proposed project fits within the goals and objectives of the Adams CRA as well as the Nine
Rails Creative District.
           Ms. Meess summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the participation and
incentive agreement for Adams Avenue Apartments; the project is being referred to as the Alpha Malakite project, a market rate
multifamily housing at Adams Avenue and 26th Street. It will be a mixed-use setting with adjacent commercial renovation, contain
101 units that will be highly amenitized, 65 parking stalls, outdoor plazas and walkways to enhance the Nine Rails Creative
District, and high-quality exterior finishes to complement existing buildings. She presented a concept plan for the project and
compared that plan with photos to display the existing conditions of the project site. She summarized the terms of the participation
and incentive agreement as follows:
              •    Estimated private investment of $35M in Malakite apartments
              •    Estimated to generate $4.7M in TIF by 2045
                        o Includes TIF from adjacent commercial renovation
                        o TIF required to make project financially feasible
                        o TIF will contribute to the cost of:
                                  ▪ $3.25M: Structured parking
                                  ▪ $1M: Plazas, walkways, and landscaping to enhance Nine Rails Creative District
                                  ▪ $1.5M: High-quality exterior finishes
          Board member Choberka stated she likes the design of the building, but she shares Mr. Pate’s concerns about the building

and incentive agreement is subject to the project complying with all planning and permitting guidelines for Ogden City. Staff will
ensure the building complies with all development requirements for the zone. Board member Choberka asked if that includes
setbacks, to which Ms. Meess answered yes.

Proposed Resolution 2025-11 adopting the budget for the Redevelopment
Agency for Fiscal Year 2025-2026
          A memo from the Management Services Department regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2026
Redevelopment Agency (RDA) Budget came before the Board for consideration. The memo stated each year, the Mayor, serving
as Executive Director of the RDA, presents the RDA budget at a meeting in May as required by state law. State law also requires
that the annual budget be adopted no later than June 30. The Agency is staffed and managed by Community and Economic
Development Department. The RDA Board is comprised of all members of the Ogden City Council as Board members. The Chair
of the Council serves as the Chair of the Board.
          Finance Director Sorensen summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to offer a comparison of
the proposed budget with the adopted budget for FY 2024-2025; the increment budget will decrease by approximately $600,000
due to the expiration of two districts – Fairmount and Lincoln. The next areas to expire include the CBD Mall in FY2029, Ogden
River in FY2030, and Flagship in FY2031. He presented a chart illustrating transfers into the RDA budget for RDA development
and housing projects, totaling $4.8 million. He also discussed the bond debt service paid by RDA funds, the balances of which is
$69 million on June 30, 2025. The RDA also has two Section 108 loans outstanding with a total of $2.4 million and a Goldenwest
Credit Union Loan and DL Evans Loan, which have a total balance of $580,175. Administration is working on a formal proposal
to repay debt earlier than scheduled. For a copy of Mr. Sorensen’s presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the
meeting.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2025-11, entitled:
                   “A resolution of the Board of Directors of the Ogden City Redevelopment Agency adopting the annual budget
                   for Fiscal Year 2025-2026 as required by Section 17C-1-601.5, Utah Code Annotated, 1953.”
