    2.   General Plan Updates and Process. (Mark McGrath/Community Development). Mr. McGrath said that the
         City of Taylorsville is taking a unique approach in that they are updating the General Plan and the Land Use
         Code at the same time. He said that he is taking the lead on the General Plan update and Jim Spung is doing
         the Land Use Code. Mr. McGrath said his purpose before the Planning Commission this evening was to
         initiate a discussion to make the Commission aware of what will transpire after the new year with regard to
         the General Plan. He advised the current General Plan is now obsolete and the decision was made to start
         over from scratch on a new version. He went over the content chapters of the present Plan as being:
         Introduction; Community Character; Land Use; Transportation; Economic Development; Parks, Open Space,
         Recreation, and Trails; Neighborhoods and Housing; Implementation. Under the new Plan Affordable
         Housing; and Environment and Sensitive Lands would be added. He explained that the Implementation
         Chapter shows what has been done over the past few years. He also defined some discussion points for this
         evening and discussed what is envisioned for each: Core Principles; Big Ideas; Areas of Change/Areas of
         Stability; Land Use; The New Normal; Community Character/Urban Design; Transportation; Economic
         Development; Park, Open Space, Recreation, and Trails; Neighborhoods; Affordable Housing; Environment
         and Sensitive Lands; and Small Area Planning.

         2.1.1     Mr. McGrath said the Big Ideas should be brought forth with an eye on the need to be forward
                   thinking and bearing in mind Taylorsville is an aging community. He mentioned two such projects
                   implemented recently as being the Performing Arts Center on the City Center property (which is in
                   its’ final construction phase), and initiation of a Bus Rapid Transit System, which is has been set back
                   by about eight months due to funding. Commissioner Burggraaf asked who did the estimate of eight

         2.1.2     Mr. McGrath continued on to say that another “Big Idea”, the Redwood Road streetscape project is
                   in final review and has run into some funding problems in that it will be much more expensive than
                   anticipated. They do have enough funding to place the utilities underground, which has been a
                   major portion of this project.      There will be new decorative lighting included along Redwood
                   Road and fixing of damaged sidewalks along it included.

         2.1.3     He suggested that some of the next “Big Ideas” could be anything from an affordable housing plan,
                   neighborhood plan, to redevelopment projects. He wants to focus on sense of community and
                   quality of life.

         2.1.4     He showed some examples of buildings that address the street, in ways both incorrectly and
                   correctly.

         2.1.5     Under the “New Normal”, he showed a table of different uses and how the Covid 19 disease has
                   affected people. That working from home seems to be where it’s at right now and traditional retail is
                   definitely taking an economic hit. But traditional retail will eventually do what it does best and that is
                   “change”. The constant about retail is that it is never constant. Offices will also re-emerge while
                   some people will continue to work from home, many will return to a physical presence at work. The
                   optimum plan is for retail centers to become more diverse places for offices, services, gathering and
                   even residences. Offices will not disappear but what is going to be built will be different. There will
                   be less of open design concept in offices but rather more closed off spaces. That the “Amazon Proof”
                   retail will expand rather than shrink. Commissioner Quigley asked for a definition of “Amazon Proof”
                   and Mr. McGrath said that a lot of retailers are struggling right now. Amazon has the capability to
                   sell a product at a lesser amount and have it delivered. “Amazon Proof” is those types of retailers
                   that Amazon cannot accommodate, i.e., a restaurant or bar.

         2.1.6     There currently is a shortage of residential living space and change will have to come in that area. In
                   order to get that change there is also the need to retain the residential quality of life, for instance,
                   neighbors do not want an apartment complex right in the middle of a residential neighborhood.

         2.1.7     Mr. McGrath added there are many places in Taylorsville that are in need of change, especially along
                   the BRT route and older shopping centers. The area of 48th South and Redwood Road is an example
                   of an underutilized area. Regarding parks and recreation, there is a need for park renovations, new
                   community parks, new neighborhood parks, bike paths, trails, and event areas.

    3.   Land Development Code Update Process. (Jim Spung/Senior Planner)                       Mr. Spung said that Mr.
         McGrath has outlined in his General Plan presentation the larger policy and vision of the City for the next five
         to fifteen years. That the Land Development Code (LDC) is the rule book for how the City gets there. This
         outlines the procedures for people wanting to develop here to follow. The primary goal is to align the zoning
         and development code with that vision. He stated the Land Development Code is the regulations for how
         land can be used and developed in the City – location and size of buildings, what types of things can be done
         on a specific property, the quality of development as far as architectural standards, landscaping, how much
         parking is necessary, storm water run off requirements, etc. It also contains the rules for how to obtain
         approvals as well as what criteria the decision-makers are going to look at to either approve or disapprove.

         3.3.1     The reason for updating the Code is to align the code of the laws with the vision that is established in
                   the General Plan.

         3.3.2     It is also a very effective economic development tool and when the Code is clear, predictable and
                   efficient it means that property owners, business owners, residents and developers can come in and
                   know exactly the expectation of the City for developments and how to get there.

         3.3.3     An updated Code will improve the user experience and increase Staff efficiency. There are many
                   things in the Code today that don’t make sense or make everyone’s jobs more difficult because there
                   are steps to be taken that are not necessary.

         3.3.4     The original County-based rendition was changed in 2004 and then updated in 2012. It is time to do
                   another update now. Goals for that update are:
                   3.3.4.1 Streamline development review procedures and increase predictability of land use
                            decisions.
                   3.3.4.2 Consider the needs of the City both now and in the future.
                   3.3.4.3 Align the LDC with Utah State Statutes.
                   3.3.4.4 Enable great design and a diverse mix of land uses.
                   3.3.4.5 Improve user-friendliness through logical structure, graphics, tables and illustrations.

         3.3.5     The First Task to be completed is Project Orientation. City Staff will review background ordinances,
                   plans, and related materials to prepare for initial meetings. Those meetings will help shed light on
                   where the current regulations are working well and where they can be improved.

         3.3.6     The Second Task is LDC Assessment and Outline. Staff will evaluate the current LDC and prepare a
                   report that outlines the major issues to be addressed. This action will serve as a roadmap for the
                   remainder of the project.

         3.3.7     The Third Task is Prepare the Draft LDC, which will be done in three installments: (1) Zoning Districts
                   and Uses; (2) Development and Design Standards; and (3) Administration and Procedures. For each
                   of the three installments, Staff will prepare a public draft and post those materials on the project
                   website and present the materials to the Advisory Committee, Planning Commission, City Council and
                   to the general public. Staff will collect and consolidate comments received during each of the
                   instalments and prepare a single Consolidated Draft for another round of review prior to beginning
                   the adoption process.

         3.3.8     The Fourth Task is LDC Adoption. After receiving input on the Consolidated Draft, Staff will create an
                   Adoption Draft that will be considered for recommendation by the Planning Commission and
                   approval by the City Council. As a companion guide to the new LDC, Staff will prepare an Executive
                   Summary that describes the project, compares the existing and proposed regulations, and
                   summarizes all major changes. That material will be presented by Staff during a series of public
                   hearings to inform and educate decision-making bodies and to solicit additional public input.

         3.3.9     Mr. Spung said this whole process is anticipated to take about a year to complete. This will create a
                   document that is much simpler and easier to use by adding work-flow charts that show step by step
                   how to accomplish what is to be done and adding more tables/graphics. He added that footnotes
                   will be utilized to show changes between the older version and the new proposed one.

         3.3.10    Mr. Spung showed Commissioners how to access the project web site and encouraged all
                   Commissioners to take the survey contained therein. He explained the first applicable meeting on
                   this will be in January 2021.

         3.3.11    All Commissioners present expressed their support for this project and thanked Staff for their
                   presentations.

     This item was for discussion purposes only, with no motion required.
