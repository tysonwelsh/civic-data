Proposed Ordinance 2023-48 amending Section 7-2-15 of the Ogden
Municipal Code to amend provisions pertaining to camping and related
activities

A memo from the Public Services Department regarding proposed amendments to the City Code pertaining to camping
and related activities came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Administration is proposing an ordinance
revision which would amend the definition of camping and prohibit the storage of personal property for more than 15 minutes on
public property.

Parks and Cemetery Manager Stewart summarized the memo and explained that as mandated by recent State legislation,
in order to receive Homeless Shelter Cities Mitigation Restricted Account funds (Utah Code 35A-16-403(2)(g)), cities must
enforce an ordinance that prohibits camping. Camping prohibitions are common in cities where unauthorized camping leads to
public spaces being rendered unavailable for their intended use. These affected areas cause unsanitary conditions, healthrisksto | }
campers and the surrounding community, environmental damage, fire hazards, criminal activity, and conflict with residents and
businesses. The proposed amendments create a more specific definition of camping and will be easier to enforce. Amendments
also clarify that the camping ordinance will not be enforced when the City’s shelters are full. Additionally, storing personal
property is a similar problem that often accompanies camping, and the proposed ordinance gives officers an additional tool to
combat the problem. Storing personal property in public areas obstructs pedestrian and vehicular traffic and negatively affects the
aesthetics of public spaces.

Chair Choberka inquired if the language in the proposed ordinance is explicitly required in order for the City to receive
Homeless Shelter Cities Mitigation Restricted Funds. Mr. Stewart answered yes and indicated it is his understanding the City
receives $1.7 million annually from the funding source.

Council member Hyer asked ifthe City can choose not to enforce the ordinance in times when the homeless shelter in the
community is full, to which Mr. Stewart answered yes; he added that enforcement of the ordinance would also be suspended
during “Code Blue” conditions during the winter months of each year.

Council member Blair asked how often the shelters in the community are full. Chair Choberka stated that depends on the
shelter as different services are offered at different shelters. Mayor Caldwell stated that is correct; during the winter months, the
Lantern House is able to open areas that are not typically used for sleeping so that people can find shelter out of the cold. Capacity
at the different shelters can be expanded under the approval of the Fire Chief, but those offerings are not always accepted by the
City’s unsheltered population.

Chair Choberka stated she has some concerns about some of the prohibitions included in the proposed ordinance; she is
concerned that someone cannot leave items on a public street for longer than 15 minutes and they do not need to be warned before
the items are removed. The previous ordinance required the Police Department to issue a notice before items could be removed.
Mayor Caldwell stated that enforcement of these types of rules will always be subjective; he assured the Council that the City’s
Police Department, which includes homeless advocates, will be flexible and fair in their enforcement of the ordinance.

Chair Choberka introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2023-48, entitled:

Large Neighbor Up Grant Program: Policy Analyst Hallmark used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize
Council staff’ s recommendations regarding the Large Neighbor Up Grant Program. The Neighbor Up Grant Program is designed
to help neighbors better connect with each other by strengthening a sense of community and improving the quality of life in
Ogden’s neighborhoods. The program was established in 2018 and since then, the City has received requests totaling $20,571. The
amount awarded to date is $18,487 and the current program awards up to $575 per event. The proposed Large Grant program
would increase the amount per event to up to $1,500 and is intended to cover gatherings of 100 people or more on public property.
The Large Grant amount is contingent upon size of expected gathering and a match requirement; the applicant is required to match
the dollar amount provided by the Neighbor Up program, either monetarily or via in-kind services. Mr. Hallmark concluded the
application and office processes will be the same for the Large Grant program as the current program.
Discussion among staff and the Council centered on insurance requirements for large events on public property versus
\ smaller events on private property, with the Council ultimately concluding to support the proposed addition of the Large Grant
program.
