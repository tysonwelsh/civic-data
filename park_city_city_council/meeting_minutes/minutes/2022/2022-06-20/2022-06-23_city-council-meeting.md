FY22 Final Adjusted Budget and FY23 Final Budget Discussion:
Jed Briggs and Erik Daenitz, Budget Department, presented this item.

Water Fund: Daenitz stated the fund was stable and met the debt service obligation.
The main source of revenue was from water service. The largest water consumers,
largest to smallest, were residential, commercial, multi-family, and irrigation. He noted
costs were increasing for personnel and the 3Kings Water Treatment Plant. He
displayed the year-over-year expense change. He indicated the fund covered debt,
operating costs, and capital projects.

Clint McAffee, Public Utilities Director, stated he requested an additional $1.2 million for
operating expenses and indicated the costs went to ensure a high-quality water supply,
having a reliable water service, water leak reduction, and leasing surplus water to
Weber Basin. Going forward, there would be a water master plan update and an asset
management program. He stated there would be no lead pipe service lines in the City
limits. Council Member Toly asked if that was throughout the City, to which McAffee
stated that requirement was for the service lines. He also discussed an upcoming PFAS
regulation the City would need to comply with when it was issued. Council Member

Doilney asked if there were discussions within the State on PFAS or if this was a ski
resort problem. McAffee didn't have the answer, but offered a few possible solutions
being considered. The wells made up 30% of the City's water supply and they were the
only water sources where PFAS was detected.

McAffee updated Council on the 3Kings Water Treatment plant and noted construction
was on schedule. Water would begin to run through the plant this year and it would be
in full production by this time next year. He also stated the water from Judge would be
completely treated by 2024 and the water from Spiro would be partially treated
beginning 2024. Council Member Doilney praised the Water team for being on schedule
and on budget during the pandemic and stated it was an example of accomplishing big
things and having positive results.

Council Member Toly asked if the Water Department would be able to find quality
applicants to fill the additional personnel needs. McAffee stated an employee would be
promoted to superintendent and four operators would be hired, and all positions but one
were recently filled. Council Member Rubell stated many of the increased costs were
due to the startup of 3Kings, and he asked if those costs would decrease in time.
McAffee stated the costs would stay the same and would increase again in 2028 and
2033. Council Member Rubell asked to have a future discussion on leasing surplus
water. McAffee reviewed the City had an interlocal agreement to share water with
Weber Basin. Snyderville Basin would need water in the future and that would have
very high costs. The entities agreed the City would lease water to Weber Basin who
would then lease it to Summit Water. When the demand exceeded the City's excess
supply, Snyderville Basin would build a new plant. The lease was a rolling five-year
agreement and each year, the City would review its needs and available surplus.
Council Member Doilney asked if the rates were locked in for the five-years, to which
McAffee affirmed. Council Member Doilney asked if the lease could be terminated if the
City's other water sources were somehow deemed unreliable. McAffee stated a clause
was added last year to decrease the supply in the case of drought or other catastrophic
failure of the City’s water source. He noted the City had a very flexible water system, so
an unreliable water source could be diverted for irrigation or snowmaking. Council
Member Gerber indicated the City had surplus water without using the water from the
Judge Tunnel. She asked if the additional water would be sold as well when that water
was treated. McAffee indicated the water leased would increase with time and the water
from the Judge Tunnel was scheduled to be leased by 2026.

Council Member Rubell asked about water incentives. McAffee indicated he would
return on July 28th to discuss Empire Creek and a water conservation rebate program
proposal. Mayor Worel asked to see the chart on the budget used to date for the 3Kings
project.

Flagstaff Transfer Fees: Briggs stated an agreement was signed in 1999 and amended
in 2000, and the fees were split between the City and the master owners association
(MOA). On average, the City received $600,000 per year and the funds needed to be
split between Transit and Open Space in a way that would benefit the Flagstaff area.
Deters reviewed the largest uses of the funds were for wildfire mitigation, noxious weed
mitigation, open space easements and acquisitions, conservation easement costs,
recreational improvements and maintenance, and equipment.

Council Member Rubell asked if the Flagstaff funding was audited to determine if the
$600,000 was an accurate amount. Deters stated real estate sales were checked to
verify percentages. Briggs indicated the MOA had their own transit and other services.
The City never checked to see if they were using their portion of the funds appropriately.
Margaret Plane, City Attorney, noted the money belonged to Flagstaff. Council Member
Gerber asked if any of the money should be used to maintain the mine shafts in that
area. Deters stated there was an agreement with the Empire Pass HOA, and Friends of
Ski Mining History were allotted some funds to raise the Daly West Headframe. There
was also a project list that the Planning Department put together. Council Member Toly
stated they were looking at two projects to apply for grants. Council Member Gerber
asked what the remainder funds would go to. Deters stated the funds fluctuated and it
had been flush since the real estate market had been robust in the past few years. But
the inventory was dwindling now and not as many funds were coming in. Briggs didn't
recommend using all the funds at this point, but that was at Council’s discretion.

Robbie Smoot reported transportation service ran consistently to Flagstaff since 2019.
Julia Collins stated Transportation looked at access, safety, bus stops, roads that led to
developments, and traffic signal transit hardware. Council Member Rubell asked if this
was money in the bank to be used, to which Briggs affirmed. Council Member Rubell
asked if this should be a route for full-size buses since other solutions were being
looked at around town, and asked for a future discussion on that. He asked if the use for
Richardson Flat had to be for transit to the Empire area. Briggs stated there was
flexibility with the funds, but it did need to benefit the Flagstaff area.

516 Marsac Avenue (Old Peace House) Redevelopment Overview and Project
Update:
Browne Sebright, Affordable Housing Program Manager, presented this item and
reviewed the property was acquired in 2017 with Transit Funding to be used as Transit
housing. A feasibility study was conducted and the Housing team proposed three
options for this property's use: transitional housing, City employee housing, or deed
restricted housing for purchase or rentals.

Council Member Gerber asked if renovating the garage area to interior space had been
considered. Glidden affirmed the four-car garage had been considered for renovation
but there were parking requirements. Council Member Gerber stated it was a good
location to under-park the parcel. Glidden indicated the R1 Zone was not an accepting
zone for an affordable master planned development (AMPD), which was one reason for
the option to rezone the property. A variance could also be requested from the Planning
Commission to under-park the parcel.

Council Member Doilney asked how long it would take to complete Option 1B, maintain
the existing building envelope and construct an addition. Sebright estimated 24 months
for completion. There was a plat note so a plat amendment would be needed before the
addition could be constructed.

Council Member Toly asked if maintaining the existing building envelope and not
extending, Option 1A, would take the same amount of time. Glidden stated the plat note
limited the size of the structure to 2,600 square feet. Constructing a new building in the
existing envelope would still require a plat amendment if the square footage increased,
and that would take an estimated 1.5 years. Council Member Toly noted Option 4 was
only a remodel with no other changes.

Council Member Dickey asked if the plat note would have to go to the HOA. Sebright
indicated there was no HOA. Council Member Dickey favored maximizing unit count. He
asked what the probability would be to rezone the property. Sebright stated a successful
outcome was unknown. This zone had stood for a long time. Glidden discussed this with
the Planning Director and the City would have to get approval from every property
owner which would take a long time. Low Density was there for a reason, but there
could be another zone that could make sense.

Council Member Rubell asked if there was a cost estimate for Option 4, to remodel.
Glidden didn't have an estimate but felt it would be under $1 million. He stated this
would gut the building to make three separate condos/apartments. Council Member
Rubell asked what the value of the property was, Option 5, to which Sebright estimated

recommended Council restrict fireworks and open flames this year, given the dry
conditions.

the authority to the fire code official, to which Thacker stated the authority had to remain

                       BACKGROUND
What is Fractional Ownership?
• Percentage ownership in an asset.
• Common investment structure for expensive assets
  such as vacation homes, luxury cars, aircraft, etc.
How is it structured?
• Real Estate typically involves a management company                   Image Source: The New York Times

• Ownership through LLC
• Management company oversees regular upkeep and operations
Why Fractional Ownership?
• Affordability - shared ownership/title, shareholders split benefits
• Shared responsibilities/costs – furnishings, upkeep, investments
• Management - maintenance, cleaning, scheduling
• Flexibility – scheduling, ability to sell fraction at any time
                    BACKGROUND
                                 Timeshares
Utah Code Section 57-19-2(26) defines Timeshares as a small, undivided fractional
fee interest in real property by which the purchaser does not receive any right to use
an accommodation except as provided by contract, declaration, or other instrument
defining a legal right, and in Utah Code Section 57-19-2(27) as a right to occupy
fixed or variable accommodations during three or more separate fixed or variable
time periods over a period of at least three years, including renewal options,
whether or not coupled with an estate in land.

The LMC requires a CUP and evaluation of Timeshare instruments, rules and
regulations, management, utility service payments, minimum duration of stay
intervals, and parking considerations.
                   BACKGROUND
                      Private Residence Clubs
Ownership of a condominium unit is shared by no less than four and no more than
12 owners and is operated by a property management service that provides
reservations and registration.

                     Fractional Ownership/Use
Shared ownership of a Single-Family Dwelling in a primary residential neighborhood.

• The proposed amendments update the language to match current State
  law

• The amendments also remove the delegation of authority, so the City
  Council, instead of the Fire Code Official, would ultimately decide on the
  issuance of a restriction.
