3.    Wasatch Sheds; 8895 South Redwood Road; Temporary Use Permit (150 days); SC-2
      Zone; Trinnium, LLC/Brandon Slater (applicant) [#22457; parcel 27-03-251-084]

Larry Gardner gave an overview of the application for temporary shed sales on the property until such
time the applicant can apply for full site plan development as a permanent use. The temporary use
permit is only good for one period of 150 days. The conditions of approval include benchmarks that
need to be met. If the Commission approves the use, staff will not issue the permit until all conditions
of approval are met. After issuance of the temporary use permit, the applicant has 90 days in which to
apply for permanent site plan approval. It is not the city’s intent to ever keep a temporary use
permanently. The property has to develop through site plan approval for it to be a permanent use.

7.     Foundations Cove; 8925 South 2700 West; Rezone 1 acre from P-O (Professional Office)
       Zone to P-O (SHO) (Professional Office – Senior Housing Overlay) Zone and Master
       Development Plan; Cerebral Palsy of Utah/Shelly Petty (applicant) [#22284; parcel 27-04-
       251-012]

Shelly Petty, Cerebral Palsy, said they have been in the West Jordan area since 1985 with two group
homes. They have a day training facility on 90th South north of Reams where they would like to add
some accessible/affordable housing. The plan is to redo the day training program with a new building
and add the apartment to the east with a view of the golf course.

Justin Heppler, AJC Architects, said this site is compatible with Beehive Homes senior housing across
the street, and will serve as a nice transition from the commercial to the south and residential to the
north. The site plan has a conceptual design that is representative of what they could fit on the site with
the new zoning. There is not enough property for a van turnaround, so a van stop on 2700 with an
accessible pathway to the day center building would be installed. Twenty-five, one and two-bedroom
apartment units will be on the east with the tenant amenity portion overlooking the golf course and
Wasatch mountains. Conceptual elevations respect the zoning requirements for durable materials and
character of architecture. Windows are on the scale of a single-family residential home. They are
pulling back from the single-family to the north and tucking more to the commercial property.

Ray McCandless said the application is for the Planning Commission to decide if the Senior Housing
Overlay should be applied to the existing P-O zoning. The zoning ordinance does not allow
multifamily housing unless it is located in specifically exempted areas or meets other criteria, such as
senior housing. The land use designation is for neighborhood commercial, so staff would recommend
that be amended to the professional office designation. Building heights and setbacks are already
established in the P-O zone. The overlay allows development of senior housing and for persons with
disabilities, which they feel fits within the assisted living facility definition in the zoning code.
Building design and placement would come at a later date. Staff recommended a positive
recommendation be forwarded to the City Council.

Matt Quinney asked if the Senior Housing Overlay allows for persons of all ages.

Ray McCandless said assisted living centers and similar uses can be approved by the Planning
Commission with a conditional use permit. The overlay says occupants have to be 55 or older.

Matt Quinney asked if the conditional use permit will let them circumvent the age requirements of the
SHO for persons of all ages with cerebral palsy. He asked if this question needs to be addressed before
it gets to the City Council to make sure the applicant is going to be able to do what they intend.

Larry Gardner said there is an exemption under the Cap and Grade ordinance that allows for non-profit
housing of this type that could be occupied by all ages.

Matt Quinney wanted to include a condition that this be verified before it goes to the City Council.

Kent Shelton understood that the building is not being approved at this time, but he asked if a building
of three stories is allowed with the proposed zoning.

Ray McCandless said the P-O zone allows for a building height of 40 feet, and the Senior Housing
Overlay doesn’t affect that. The height can increase with a conditional use permit, but three stories
should be less than 40 feet in height.

Matt Quinney opened the public hearing.

Tanner Morrill said this is a great idea. Rents are increasing and it is hard for people with disabilities to
find affordable housing. He suggested that the apartments be located closer to the road.

Barry Bessey, representing Reams Food Stores, would welcome something like this next to their store
but he wouldn’t want it to impact their operations. They have deliveries at night and some of the
motors behind the building are noisy. Moving the building closer to 2700 West would help with the
noise.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Matt Quinney thought the proposal was a great idea. He thought that moving the parking lot to the
back would be better aesthetically.

Ray McCandless said Salt Lake County Flood Control called the office to say that any disturbance
within 20 feet of the canal top of bank needs a flood control permit.

Kent Shelton said moving the building may provide room for a van turnaround that is close to the
building.

9.    Text Amendment – Interchange Overlay Zone (IOZ) - Amend the 2009 City Code Section
      13-6K (IOZ) Interchange Overlay Zone Design and Development Standards relating to
      road standards and other requirements; City-wide applicability; City of West Jordan
      (applicant) [#22707]

Ray McCandless said the amendment came about following a review by the Engineering department
on a project in the Interchange Overlay Zone (IOZ). There was a concern with the width and
functionality of the street standards that were approved as a part of the IOZ text. The proposal is to
remove the existing road standards from the IOZ and apply the engineering standards. This change is
important for other developments in the IOZ to alleviate some of the width problems. This amendment
will also fix the setback requirements for commercial buildings that may not front on a street. The
Planning Commission will still see all site plans for buildings in the IOZ, so they can address any
issues. Another section says at least one functional door or window needs to be installed on larger
buildings to break up the wall plane. There are other ways to accomplish the purposes of that
Planning & Zoning Commission Meeting Minutes
November 17, 2020
Page 11

requirement, so the statement of ‘or other significant architectural feature as approved by the planning
commission’ be added. That requirement is specific to this overlay, but there are standards in other
sections of the code that require buildings to be broken up, which is 50 feet in commercial zones.

Jay Thomas asked if the zero side line is to allow for San Francisco style buildings.

Ray McCandless said that will allow buildings right up against the side property line.

Kent Shelton asked for clarification of real-world impacts that these amendments will make.

Ray McCandless said it won’t make a significant change. Regarding the roads, it will improve safety
