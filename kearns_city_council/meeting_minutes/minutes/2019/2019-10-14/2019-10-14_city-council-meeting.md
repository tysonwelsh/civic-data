Greater Salt Lake Municipal Service District – Contracted Staff Reports

UPD Station Feasibility Study Presentation - EDA Architects

                 Tom Brennan, EDA Architects, stated EDA Architects was hired to look into the
feasibility of developing a new police substation for the Kearns area. The goal is to determine
what is the ideal facility that will meet the current and growing needs of the police department as
well as the community. The purpose of the meeting tonight is to introduce themselves and to
listen to concerns of the Council as well as the public. It a couple of months, he hopes to bring
back a preliminary feasibility study for the Council’s review and input.

               Council Member Snow asked if property had been selected for the police
substation.

               Mayor Bush stated one property that is being considered is a surplus property
that the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) has that is located at 5600 West and just
north of 6200 South.

                 Mr. Brennan stated EDA is also looking at the possibility of staying in the same
location or close by the current facility. A selection format is being developed that will evaluate
the properties based on the location, size of property, and other various elements. The goal is to
provide a facility that has a prominent presence as a civil entity within a community, that is easily
accessible, and is visible from public transit or private vehicle.

               Mayor Bush stated Kearns needs a facility that it can grow into and not grow out
of.

               Mr. Brennan stated the goal is to plan a facility that will function for a minimum of
20 years. Anything beyond that is a guess. That is why a parcel needed to be selected that
allowed for growth in the future.

Resolution – Proposed Tax Increase to Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area of 3
percent

                                                 2
DATE           MONDAY                            OCTOBER                                 14, 2019


                Council Member Peterson stated on September19, 2019, the Board of Directors
for the Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Area (SLVLESA) considered a resolution stating its
intent to levy a 3 percent tax increase for calendar year 2020. The board voted 7 to 1 in favor of
the resolution.

              Frank Nakamura, Legal Counsel, Salt Lake Law Enforcement Services Area,
stated SLVLESA made a policy decision a couple of years ago to have small incremental tax
increases rather than impose a large tax increase. So, in 2017 there was a 9.5 percent increase,
2018 - 5 percent, 2019 - 3 percent and another 3 percent for this year. Some of the increase will
go towards compensation so deputies can be recruited and retained. A huge portion (85 percent)
of UPD budget is tied to compensation.

              Mayor Bush stated the tax increases equates to a 12.5 percent increase. This is
huge. The Unified Fire Authority (UFA) did not raise taxes for nine years, then it asked for a 9
percent increase, which will sustain it for another eight years. She wondered when these small
increases would end.

              Mr. Nakamura stated he did not know the answer. The increases will last if they
are needed in order to be competitive in the marketplace with compensation.

                Mayor Bush stated the Council needed to look at the situation and decide if it is
in the best interest of the citizens to stay with SLVLESA or pay directly into UPD for services it
would like.

                Mr. Nakamura stated before the Council decided, he would ask that it does its due
diligence. If the Council looks at the revenue coming in versus cost, it will find that it is on the
positive side due to the involvement of Salt Lake County.

                Council Member Peterson stated UPD cannot be compared to UFA, they are two
different pies. There are three options: do small increases now, a large increase in a couple of
years, or cut services.

               Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Peterson moved to approve
the resolution. Roll was called showed the vote to be: Council Member Peterson “Aye,” Council
Member Brown “Aye,” Mayor Bush “Nay,” Council Member Schaeffer “Nay,” and Council Member
Snow “Nay.” The motion failed 3 to 2.

Ordinance Repealing and Replacing Title 2 of the Kearns Metro Township Code

             The Council reviewed the following Ordinance repealing and replacing Title 2 of
the Kearns Metro Township code regarding council administration:

ORDINANCE NO. 19-10-03                                                     Date: October 14, 2019

       AN ORDINANCE OF THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL REPEALING
       AND REPLACING TITLE 2 OF THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP CODE
       REGARDING COUNCIL ADMINISTRATION

                                           RECITALS

       WHEREAS, Title 2 of the Kearns Metro Township Code (the “Code”) addresses the
administration of the Kearns Metro Township Council (the “Council”) and related matters.




                                                18
DATE           MONDAY                           OCTOBER                                 14, 2019

        WHEREAS, Salt Lake County (the “County”) originally adopted Title 2 prior to Kearns’
incorporation and the title is specific to County Council rather than the Council and does account
for the metro township form of municipal governance;

       WHEREAS, Title 2, as currently constituted, became part of the Code when Kearns
incorporated pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-2a-414(3), which specifies that each County
ordinance in effect on the day a metro township incorporates remains in effect as a metro township
ordinance until the metro township amends or repeals the ordinance; and

       WHEREAS, the Council desires to repeal and replace Title 2 to more specifically address
the needs of Kearns, to better reflect the Council’s actual administration, and to comport with the
metro township form of municipal government; and

       WHEREAS, the Council has determined that the attached, revised version of Title 2 will
serve the best interests of the Kearns Metro Township and the general public.

        NOW, THEREFORE, IT IS HEREBY RESOLVED by the Kearns Metro Township Council
that:

    1. Title 2 of the Code is repealed and replaced in its entirety with the ordinance attached
       hereto; and

    2. The ordinance will become effective 20 days after publication pursuant to Utah Code Ann.
       § 10-3-711(1) and Utah Code Ann. § 10-3-712.

        APPROVED and ADOPTED this 14th day of October, 2019.
