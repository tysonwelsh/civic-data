In accordance with the requirements of Utah Code Section 52-4-203, the County Clerk records in the minutes the names of all persons who speak at a County Commission meeting and the substance “in brief”
of their comments. Such statements may include opinion or purported facts. The county does not verify the accuracy or truth of any statement but includes it as part of the record pursuant to State law.

       favorite of his quotes that I think about every year is “Darkness cannot drive out darkness. Only light can do
       that. Hate cannot drive out hate. Only love can do that.” It just reminds me that if we want to change anything,
       we need to make a change. If we don’t make a change, things will remain the same.

F. RECOGNITIONS:

     1. Recognition of Ricky Hatch for being named the Utah Association of Counties Clerk of the Year.
        Commissioner Harvey: For a lot of years, you have done not just a good job but just an exceptional job.
        You represent not only Weber County but the state and the country as you testify before Congress. Weber
        County is exceptionally blessed to have you. Your team upstairs is wonderful and your election team is top
        notch. And more importantly you exemplify the word good man.
        Commissioner Bolos: One of the things I like about Ricky is his openness. With the controversies that swirl
        about us we are fortunate to have you in Weber County.
        Commissioner Froerer: I don’t know if most appreciate what Ricky has done in our elections and showing
        integrity. You lead in the state of Utah.
        Ricky Hatch: It has to flow through me to my staff. I couldn’t testify before Congress or participate at the
        state level without things being right at home in our office. The team we have is phenomenal. They are hard
        workers and understand public service.

     2. Recognition of John Ulibarri for being named the Utah Association of the Counties Assessor of the Year.
        Commissioner Harvey: I thought I knew John pretty good when they moved down here six years ago. There
        are a lot of questions regarding the assessment process and John has been incredibly patient with me as I’ve
        learned this process. He has always been good to me and concerned citizens. He is a great leader. The staff
        is incredibly helpful and great at their job. He’s a good man and a good friend.
        Commissioner Bolos: He helped us navigate West Haven’s first property tax when I was mayor. We invited
        John down to our open houses and he took all the bullets. I am thrilled to continue to be working with the
        best assessor in the state.
        Commissioner Froerer: I’ve known John for a number of years. He used to come to the Legislature. He
        always has a calm demeanor. He understands the business of appraisal very well. Which helps answer the
        very difficult questions from citizens in a very diplomatic way. Congratulations!
        John Ulibarri: It’s easy to lead when you have a great team behind you. It enables a certain amount of
        freedom to do work to promote the interests of Weber County at the state level. It’s really the Weber County
        Assessor’s office that deserves the recognition. I really want to defer any applause to this team.

     3. Recognition of Tucker Weight for receiving his Professional Engineering License.
        Gary Myers (Engineering): I get an opportunity to brag on one of my guys. We are super excited and proud
        of Tucker. He recently passed his Professional Engineer exam. This is not an easy feat that requires months

        Commissioner Harvey:
        Commissioner Bolos: Thank you for taking the initiative to do this. It makes you an even more valuable

    4. Recognition of Kelly Janis for receiving the International Code Council 2022 Trailblazer Award.
       Stan Berniche (Building Inspection): International Code Council develops building codes worldwode. This
       award is for anyone willing to step up and promote the next generation of code officials. Kelly has been to
       local junior high and high schools promoting what we do and why we do it. In 2022, Kelly also completed
       her Masters degree in Public Administration.
       Kelly Janis: I think it’s important for our younger generation to know there are alternatives to going to
       college and they can have a solid career in government and learning the trades. One thing children don’t
       know is receiving a national certificate can help you get college credit for prior learning assessment and
       helps them save on college tuition.
       Commissioner Harvey: Your road has not always been easy but you have always chosen the high path and
       made things work. But to care enough to perpetuate what you love doing, I don’t know if I can pay a higher
       compliment to anyone that gives back like that. Congratulations on your choice doing what you’ve done.
       Commissioner Bolos: Kelly, you speak to me when you say you go into the high schools and junior highs
       and talk to the students there. I think there are so many that graduate high school and feel like if they don’t
       want to go to college there is no place for them. It means a lot for those students who sees someone
       succeeding in a career they may not have known even existed.
       Chair Froerer: I appreciate your passion you have for your job and the service you provide for Weber
       County. It takes a fine diplomat to work with code enforcement. I am very impressed with what you have
       been able to accomplish and for what you do.

         Chair Froerer: I talk on different occasions on winning in Weber. You have seen this morning four perfect
         examples of why we continue to win in Weber. I am very proud to be associated with this group of people
         that helps us accomplish what we aim to do which is winning in Weber.

   1. Presentation of a check to the Weber Community Foundation/COE from Rocky Mountain Power,
      (Pacificorp).
      Melissa Freigang (Executive Director, Weber Prosperity Center of Excellence): We are presenting today on
      the Weber Community Foundation. Huge kudos to Chris Crockett for the original idea to have a
      governmental non-profit. It allows the Center of Excellence to do the deep dive work required to build the
      social services delivery system. Every championship team I have been a part of had great athletes with great
      talent, work really hard, and have great ethics. But they also had great coaches and support staff. It’s great
      to have wonderful partners Rocky Mountain Power who helped us put on reentry simulation.
      Commissioner Froerer: Melissa we want to give thanks for being the driving force behind the Weber
      Community Foundation. We appreciate your work. As we look in the future this is something that will help
      the citizens.
      Kirk Nigro (Regional Business Manager, Rocky Mountain Power): Rocky Mountain Power is humbled to be
      partnering with Weber County in the capacity of social issues. The Commissioners have fully supported
      tackling not only the easy decisions but some of the most difficult decisions with intergenerational poverty.
      These difficult solutions require the entire community. I’m completely blown away with the CoE is working
      on synergies between all these groups bringing a multiplied effect. We are excited to be the first corporate
      donation to the foundation.
