 1   Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, explained that he sent the benefit analysis

 4   and then considered other areas in the policy that potentially needed to be updated.

 6   Mr. Hicks said that the City has FMLA leave which is unpaid for up to 12 weeks. This can be
 7   supplemented with leave time and the City also has short term disability that can be used for the
 8   birth of a child. He said that his staff collected data from 8 different cities nearby to compare.
 9   He reported that half of the cities that responded had a caregiver leave benefit and said that this
10   benefit would help with the COVID-19 issue. He explained that during 2020 the City was able to
11   use relief funds to pay employees for time off due to COVID-19. These relief funds are now
12   expired and he believes adopting a caregiver leave benefit could help with parental leave as
13   well as sickness related to COVID-19. He said it is difficult to identify a cost to this benefit,
14   however, in analyzing the last five years and how many employees had events that would
15   qualify, he estimates it would be about $7,000.
16
17   Mr. Hicks explained that his staff provided an analysis of the leave time. He said that the City
18   currently has a use or lose personal leave time and they recommend leaving personal leave as
19   personal leave but convert any unused personal time to sick time at the end of the year. He
20   said he wanted to receive guidance from the Councilmembers as to where they would like the
21   City to be in comparison to other cities on vacation, sick time and personal leave.
22
23   In response to Councilmember McCay’s job sharing questions from a previous council meeting,

27   not recognize Veterans Day and his staff recommends that the City adopt Veterans Day as a

25            Ordinance No. 21-28-Amending Section 18.45, RM-6, amending the RM-6 zone to
26            individual lot development only, amendments proposed by Riverton City
27
28   Mr. Lethbridge explained that Riverton City currently has in its code the RM-6 Zone, which
29   allows for multi-family development at a maximum density of six units per acre. That code
30   allows for both attached units within commonly held open space, as is more typical with multi-
31   family, or subdivision of property into individual lots. In its application of this Zone, Riverton City
32   has repeatedly only approved it with a Specific Development designation limiting development
33   to individual lots. As such, staff is proposing the zone itself be amended to only allow
34   development utilizing individual lots. The Council clarified the previous proposed draft to also
35   limit the zone to detached units. This is reflected in the proposed amendment.
36
37   Mr. Lethbridge said that the standards for individual dwelling lots are already contained in this
38   section, and so the amendment is primarily deletion of the sections for attached units and
39   common open space developments.
40
41   Councilmember Buroker asked for clarification on whether this ordinance would negatively
42   affect any current applications. Mr. Lethbridge said that no current applications will be
43   negatively affected.
44
45   Councilmember Wells MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 21-28-
46   Amending Section 18.45, RM-6, amending the RM-6 zone to individual lot development
47   only. Councilmember Buroker SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on
48   the motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-
49   yes, McCay-yes, Stewart-yes, McDougal-yes, and Wells-yes. The motion passed unanimously.
