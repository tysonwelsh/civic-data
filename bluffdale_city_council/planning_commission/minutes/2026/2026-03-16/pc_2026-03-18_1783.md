5. CONSIDERATION AND VOTE on a Proposed Plat Amendment for the 1780 West
Phase 2 Subdivision Relocating Lot Lines to Accommodate the Construction of an
Automotive Service Establishment. (Application 2025-44). Applicants, City of Bluffdale
and S-Devcorp, Inc., Represented by Nicole Stangl. Staff Presenter, Caitlyn Tubbs.

Planning Manager, Caitlyn Tubbs, presented the Staff Report and indicated that the subject property

is at the southwest corner of the intersection of Bangerter Highway and Redwood Road. The original

and amended Subdivision Plats were reviewed. S-Devcorp, Inc. was currently under contract to
purchase Lots 6 and 7 of the 1780 West Subdivision from the City, and the Plat Amendment would
move the lot line to accommodate a future minor automotive service establishment on Lot 7. An
easement would also be dedicated on Lot 7 for the City to install a “Welcome to Bluffdale” sign.

Staff recommended approval of the proposed Plat Amendment.

Mike Stangl was present on behalf of the applicant. Chair Cragun stated that she was pleased to see

the sign easement included in the proposal as the City has discussed installing a sign for many years.

Community and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell stated that the location had been

secured, but the project was not yet funded.

Commissioner Griffis moved to APPROVE the proposed 1780 West Phase 2 Plat Amendment,

based on the findings presented in the Staff Report dated March 13, 2026. The motion was

seconded by Commissioner Luker. Vote on motion: Ulises Flynn-Yes, Tina Griffis-Yes, Kori

Luker-Yes, Chair Cragun-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

DISCUSSION ITEMS

6. Discussion of Non-Residential Land Use Tables.

Ms. Tubbs provided background on the item. In 2025, the Planning Commission requested that Staff

review the Non-Residential Land Use Tables to determine if any uses should be added. They also

expressed interest in re-examining Permitted, Conditional, and Permitted Uses by zone.

Staff determined that the following uses should be added:
® Medical Transportation

A satellite ambulance service had expressed interest in locating in the southwest Salt Lake

Valley, but Bluffdale did not allow this use. The business would have a sleeping area for on-

call staff and City Code currently treated such facilities as domiciles.

e Fulfillment Center

The Land Use Tables currently had only retail, wholesale, and warehousing uses. Retail uses

focus on point of sale and warehouse uses focus on storage of items to be sold elsewhere.

There had been numerous inquiries for businesses that would assemble or fulfill online orders

and have a small storefront or allow customers to pick up orders, which did not fit any of those

categories.
Ms. Tubbs reported that Home Occupation and Residential uses were not reviewed. Mr. Crowell
added that there are hundreds of allowed Home Occupations, and the code was substantially revised
in 2020. However, some uses may need to be reviewed and updated.
The Land Use Tables were reviewed. Mr. Crowell reported that some zones listed in the table were
not active, and some active zones were not included in the table. For example, the Professional Office
(“PO-1”) Zone was inherited and had not been applied to any properties in the City, and the
Destination Retail (“DR”) Zone was created for a specific purpose. A micro-flex project near 2950
West and 13900 South is in the General Commercial (“GC-1”) Zone, but during negotiations some
uses were added or subtracted. Ms. Tubbs had created a Geographic Information System (“GIS”)
layer for Staff to track changes made through Development Agreements. .
In response to a question, Ms. Tubbs reported that the review was initiated during discussion of a new
State public noticing requirement. At the time, the Planning Commission discussed Conditional Uses
and whether they should be included in the Land Use Tables. State law no longer requires a public
hearing for Conditional Use Permits (“CUP”), but it is required by Bluffdale City Code. There had
been no CUP requests in several years. Mr. Crowell added that the State requires approval of all
CUPs unless negative impacts cannot be mitigated with reasonable conditions.

Mr. Crowell reported that some mining uses had been preempted. Staff originally determined that a
CUP may be necessary for the Bridgesource plant, but changes to State law preempted the CUP. Site
Plan approval was still required. Things change over time. For example, the changing nature of retail
has required some interpretation. Bringhurst Station requires all businesses to have a retail presence,
but the percentage of retail required is open to interpretation.
Chair Cragun asked if it would be better to first complete a Text Amendment to add the two
recommended uses to the Non-Residential Land Use Tables or if the Planning Commission should
discuss all Land Use Tables and any changes be made in conjunction. She preferred that they be
addressed individually. Ms. Tubbs stated that Staff had no preference, and the next step was at the
Commission’s discretion. Chair Cragun stated that it would be reasonable to add Medical
Transportation and Fulfillment Centers to the Non-Residential Land Use Tables.
In response to a question raised by Chair Cragun, Mr. Crowell stated that there are Fulfillment Centers
in the City, but they were individually classified as each fulfillment center is unique. The Planning
Commission may also want to consider splitting the Construction Sales and Service category. All
contractor services currently fall under this category. However, some of those uses are very
impactful. For example, a property maintenance contractor yard will have bulk materials, grinding
dust, multiple crews, etc. A sales office for a heating company has little impact, but it falls under the
same category. The category could be split by businesses with yards versus those without, which
may allow the latter use in the General Commercial (““GC-1”’) Zone.

Mr. Crowell reported that Staff had not yet determined what changes would be necessary in response
to bills passed in the latest Legislative Session, but those would likely be presented to the Planning
Commission in batches rather than all at once.
Chair Cragun asked Staff to return with a Text Amendment to add the two new uses and split the
Construction Sales and Service category. Staff can then consider each section in detail during their
legislative review.
Commissioner Flynn asked how many Medical Transportation or Fulfillment Center applications had
been received. Ms. Tubbs reported that three inquiries for Medical Transportation were received in
2025, and approximately eight were received for Fulfillment Centers. Chair Cragun stated that the
changes would clarify the City’s requirements for both Staff and the applicant.
Mr. Crowell stated that the GC-1 Zone is changing, particularly in the area of 2700 West where more
flex space is moving in. As the City has allowed uses like Fulfillment Centers through Development
Agreements, it may be time to codify them in the Land Use Table.
In response to a question raised by Commissioner Luker, Ms. Tubbs reported that Associate City
Planner, Ellen Oakman, reviewed other cities’ code and determined that some cities have created
Fulfillment Center definitions. Mr. Crowell remarked that scale needed to be considered in defining
fulfillment centers versus warehouses.
Mr. Crowell stated that many contracting businesses are seeking open industrial space for contractor
yards, and they are seeing it push into more residential areas. Staff will keep an eye on how
permissive the community should be regarding allowing heavier commercial uses in residential areas.
The opportunity for training on related topics was discussed. Mr. Crowell stated that climate
resilience, food insecurity, and housing were major topics across the country. The City was also
currently working on an accessory dwelling unit program with Salt Lake County. He encouraged the
Commission to contact Staff with any ideas for training.
