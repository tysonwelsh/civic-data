6. CRS America & Oquirrh Tire & Service; 5970 West Dannon Way; Revocation of
Conditional Use Permits; M-1 Zone; The City of West Jordan (applicant) [#29189; parcel 26-
02-400-019]
Community Development Director Scott Langford left the meeting at 6:07 p.m.
Mark Forsythe gave the background history of the item. In 1995 the only part of the property that was
developed was the building, drive aisles, and parking in front, which has remained unchanged. Some gravel
was installed on the site at some point and some metal fencing was in approximately 2016. In 2016 the first
Administrative Conditional Use Permit was approved for CRS America for motor vehicle sales. One of the
conditions of approval at that time was that all vehicles were to be parked on a paved asphalt or concrete
surface. In 2017 Oquirth Tire and Service received a Conditional Use Permit for motor vehicle repair in the
Drinking Water Source Protection Overlay, with a similar condition of approval. The main trigger for this
review process was when staff received a business license application for Oquirrh Mountain Moving. It is
customary for staff to review the conditions of the property to make sure all conditions are being met. On
September 21, 2022 an inspection was made, and photos showed a number of vehicles that were parked on
unpaved surfaces. Notices were sent to the property owner shortly after that by email, certified mail, and
letters over the next few months. Mr. Forsythe did not receive a response from the business owner until
today around noon. In order for a Conditional Use Permit to be revoked there are five findings for the
Planning Commission to consider prior to making a recommendation to the Community Development
Director for final decision. Also, the condition for parking on a paved surface reenforces City Code that says
any parking area needs to be paved with asphalt or concrete.
Two criteria specifically triggered the revocation review. Criteria A) The conditional use is a nuisance or is
detrimental to public health, safety, or welfare. Mr. Forsythe showed the drinking water source protection
overlay map. The southeast corner of the property crosses Zone 2, which is in the 250-day time of travel.
The code states that if the property crosses any boundary of one of those zones, then the stricter zone
applies. In this zone, it would take 250 days’ time for any chemical that infiltrate the ground to travel to a
water well, which is located across the street. The water department is very concerned about this situation
because there is no vehicle that is completely perfect as to not leak fluids. Anything that is not an
impervious surface has a risk for contamination. If that were to happen it could trigger a superfund status
and EPA could become involved. Criteria E) The conditions of the permit have not been complied with.
From what has been observed on the site, vehicles are parked on a gravel surface, which is not paved and is
in violation of the conditions. Staff found that Criteria B, C, and D were not necessarily in violation.
Business licenses have been renewed every year.
Ammon Allen asked if there would have been a site plan provided when the conditional use permit for car
repair was first approved that showed how it would comply with code.
Mark Forsythe said there was a simple aerial site plan provided. As he reviewed some of the minutes and
history, it was the understanding at staff’s level was that most of the vehicles would be parked inside or
around the warehouse.
Ryan Fuller, subject business owner, said he would like this decision to be tabled. He was only informed on
Friday after businesses hours and Monday was a holiday. Today was his first opportunity to have a

8. Review of Balanced Housing Report for 2022

Scott Langford returned to the meeting at 6:57 p.m.

Mark Forsythe explained that the Balanced Housing Ordinance caps any new multifamily housing until
the ratio of 77% single family to 23% multifamily is met. Each year the ratio is calculated based on
building permits. The current ratio is 73% single family and 27% multifamily, which is the same as the
previous year. In 2022 there were only 138 single-family permits and 26 multifamily permits. In order
to get to the break-even point there will have to be 6,431 more single-family homes and no additional
multifamily units.

There was a discussion on how the ratio was decided based on the goals in the general plan at that time
and if it could be revisited. Exceptions to the rules have been added throughout the years. The
ordinance allows the city council and planning commission opportunity to place multifamily in correct
places. If there are any new exceptions added, it would have to follow the code amendment process.
Jay Thomas thought that the ordinance helps to manage the ratio.

Ammon Allen asked why we need the ordinance when the approval of multifamily is already managed
through zoning and land use maps.

Scott Langford said most communities with traditional Euclidian zoning manage it through the general
plan. Our City Council at the time it was adopted felt that this additional tool was a way to manage the
growth and it gave a clear indication to the development community of what they wanted to see in the
city.

Ammon Allen asked if there is even enough land outside of the excepted areas to get to the desired
ratio.

Mark Forsythe said it is difficult to know because they are dealing with the current and proposed
changes to the future land use map.

Duncan Murray said a lot of the discussion interplays with the availability of infrastructure. Various
utilities master plans have been updated and coordination needs to be done between zoning and utilities
to make sure development does not outpace the infrastructure for the given ERC’s on each property. So
multiple types of planning methods are important.

Matt Quinney asked what the point of the ratio is when we are not in compliance and likely never will
be based on the amount of undeveloped land.

Scott Langford said there may be areas from a planning standpoint that could be adjusted, and from his
experience the goal could be accomplished using other planning tools. However, the city council sees it
as a benefit, and it will have served its purpose if it helps to push multifamily development to locations
where it makes sense.
