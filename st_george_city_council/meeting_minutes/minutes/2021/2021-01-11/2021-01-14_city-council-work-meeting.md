DISCUSSION REGARDING IMPACT FEES FOR PARKS AND RECREATION AND STORM
DRAIN:
     City Manager Adam Lenhard explained there have been discussing impact fees for
     several months. The meeting tonight is to address questions from the Southern Utah
     Homebuilders Association (SUHBA) regarding Parks & Recreation Impact Fees as well as
     items related to Storm Drain Impact Fees. He mentioned a letter staff wrote to SUHBA;
     the letter is included in the agenda packet.

      Fred Philpot with Lewis Young Robertson and Burningham reviewed the letter, outlining
      questions from SUHBA and staff’s answers as included in the agenda packet. He
      explained park impact fees are designed to be based on a level investment approach.
      The level is expressed in value per capita. This allows flexibility for the City to meet the
      needs of the residents as it relates to parks, trails, recreation, etc. After being asked by
      City Manager Adam Lenhard, Mr. Philpot explained how the level of investment was
      determined. The per capita investment was slightly over 1,500. He explained impact
      fees will fluctuate and values will change over time. There are three factors influencing
      this change: 1) looking at better data, more robust data; 2) inflation area impacts; and
      3) the City has invested general fund monies and other revenues, in addition to impact
      fee revenues, to expand the level of service or level of investment over and above what
      was shown in 2007 and 2014.

      Mari Krashowetz, Executive Director of SUHBA, thanked the City for the extra time and
      involving them in the process. She commented costs have increased over the years;
      however, as proposed, the Parks and Recreation Impact Fees would be a
      120% increase. She compared other Utah cities to the City’s proposed fees. More
      residents are utilizing outdoor recreation such as trails more often than they would a

      Their methodology is similar to what is used across the state – looking at average
      household size, single-family and multi-family. Some entities have asked these types

DISCUSSION REGARDING THE DIVARIO DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT:
     Mark Teepen provided a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Project
     history; Divario; Gap Canyon; Divario location; Introduction; Master plan; and Land use
     plan. After being asked by City Manager Adam Lenhard, Mr. Teepen pointed out on the
     land use plan exhibit where PA3 is.

      Community Development Director John Willis outlined the Master Plan further and
      explained the table. City Manager Adam Lenhard noted the Master Plan allows for
      density to move from one planning area to another. Mr. Willis stated, density can
      move; however, the overall max density cannot increase.

      Mr. Teepen continued with his PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
      Mountain bike trails map; Trail arch underpass; True Grit Epic event; Typical weekend;
      Bear Claw Poppy Trailhead and event area; Proposed keyhole wah trailhead and
      proposed Bear Claw Poppy Trail Head and events area Developer contributions Power
      substations & transmission; Development status – Phase 1; Phase 1; Phase 1 view;
      Development agreement; Master plan use plan; Land use summary; Parks & open
      space; Hillside review slope map; Traffic impact study; and Conclusion. The first traffic
      study was done in 2007 with an update done in 2015.

      Community Development Director John Willis pointed out the General Plan has
      limitations. The development agreement mentions the conceptual units and the
      development will still need to go through the zone change process. Additionally, the
      development is subject to zoning ordinance, hillside, and open space. The Council has
      the ability to determine which density they feel is appropriate, as well as looking at the
      open space and other items associated with the project.

      After being asked by Councilmember Larkin about doing this project as a Planned
      Development, Mr. Teepen explained they first started the project just selling large areas
      to homebuilders. The development agreement was used to layout the project.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard noted it may help if staff can do a better job in providing
      context when bringing the plats to the Council for approval.

      Planning Commission Member Kemp commented the view sheds were a sticking point.
      He suggested taking pictures and impose the elevations to show the project.

      Councilmember McArthur stated seeing an overall master plan for a project helps the

      Rick Rosenberg noted the traffic study was performed based upon the land use areas
      and take into account peak periods. He explained the City has a transportation model

PRESENTATION OF THE GENERAL OVERVIEW OF THE DIVARIO PROJECT, AND
SPECIFICALLY DISCUSS PA-3:
     Jon Jensen presented a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
     Rilassante at Divario; Photos of the property; Aerial map of the project; and
     Renderings. The total number of units is a maximum of 216; the density would be 9
     units per acre.

      Councilmember Larkin commented that she is concerned with maxing out the density in
      this particular location. There is already quite a bit of density in this area. She
      understands density is needed but doesn’t feel it needs to be all in one area.

      Mr. Jensen outlined the Divario landscape plan that was previously presented for
      approval.

      Mr. Teepen explained when the general plan was emended in 2006, this particular
      parcel was restricted on the number of units; they agreed to develop 9 units per acre
      and now they are being asked to go less. How much less does the Council want them
      to go?

      Mr. Jensen noted the plans have always included three-story buildings. In response to
      trying to bring down the heights and spread out the density would be to layout
      townhomes.

      Thad Seegmiller, attorney for the applicant, explained both concepts were styled to put
      the density towards the thoroughfares and away from the trailheads and public access
      points, which is another challenge with a development agreement that has guaranteed
      density.

      Mr. Jensen showed some 2-story renderings for the project. If they come down on
      heights, they will lose units. They would like to make an application that the Planning
      Commission and City Council would feel comfortable with. He mentioned a 3rd option
      but hasn’t presented it yet. After being asked by Planning Commission Member Kemp,
      Mr. Jensen outlined access for the project.

      Councilmember Smethurst commented there is already high density in this area. He
      feels the proposal fits in the area if the traffic can be worked out.

      Councilmember Larkin mentioned if apartments are clustered together, issues can
      happen. She would rather see them further apart and not so clustered.


      Mr. Seegmiller asked the Council about the view scape. In comparing the two concepts,
      he asked is there one concept that indicates better effort to preserve views.

      Councilmember Larkin commented she believes it is possible to provide density and
      preserve the view.

      Mr. Teepen noted this is not an apartment site, it is a medium density site. They are
      not adding more apartments, they are adding medium density.

DISCUSSION REGARDING THE GRAND VIEWS AT GREEN VALLEY ZONE CHANGE:
     Community Development Director John Willis explained this item was recently heard by
     the Planning Commission. It is a fairly large project; therefore, staff felt it would be
     good to discuss in a work meeting.

       Bob Hermandson presented a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
       Area exhibit. He explained R-1-10 is the holding zone; their intent is to transition from
       commercial areas (assisted living center). He outlined each area on the map. Mr.
       Hermandson showed an area zoning plan and programming concept exhibit after which
       he outlined the overall density – approximately 7.5 dwelling units per acre. He
       continued with his PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: Architectural
       plans – Grand Views at Green Valley Architectural Drawings; Programming concept
       including access points; Examples of tiny homes; Example twin homes; and Example
       townhomes. He is trying to put enough detail and teeth into the project in the
       entitlement so that the developer can build value and the City can what is proposed in
       the future. After being asked by Councilmember McArthur, Mr. Hermandson showed
       and outlined the tiny homes pod on a map. All parking requirements are being met,
       they are not asking for any exceptions. He outlined the topography and elevation
       changes. The apartment complex will be rental units, the remainder of the units will be
       for sale. After being asked by Councilmember Mcarthur, Mr. Hermandson showed and
       outlined the amenities site plan.

       speak with planning staff early on to make sure the parking is adequate.
