Background: 
 
The Canyons Specially Planned Area (SPA) was originally approved in 1998.  This approval 
involved 6,388 acres  and five (5) different property owners.  With that approval there were 
entitlements granted for some of the developments at the Canyons known as Ski 98.  This 
included developments such as the Grand Summit and Sundial Lodge, the Colony and the 
Silverado.   It was anticipated that there would be a comprehensive amendment to this plan 
that would expand the entitlements and property involved. 
4
3 
 
  
In November of 1999, that amendment to the SPA was completed.  After numerous public 
meetings and hearings, the SPA was extended to include approximately 7,768 acres and 37 
different property owners to establish the current Development Agreement (DA).  The guiding 
principles for the DA were the Global Principles: 
 
• Comfortable Carrying Capacity 
• Allowable Density 
• Unit Configurations & Occupancy 
• Resort Support and Mountain Recreation 
• Viewshed 
• Environment 
• Employee Housing 
• Economic Base 
• Transportation 
• Highway 224 & Resort Entry 
• Benchmark Assessments 
• Development Design Character 
• Master Community and Resort Facility, Amenity, Recreation, Cultural Arts, and 
Marketing Plan 
• Community Integration 
• Infrastructure Maintenance and Management 
• Construction Mitigation and Management 
• Development Pattern 
• Development Phasing 
 
The key components to the Development Agreement (DA) were to establish development  
planning areas, create a detailed Land Use & Zoning Chart to allocate maximum heights, 
maximum square footages, uses for each parcel, create Comprehensive Design Guidelines, set 
benchmarks for a Canyons Transportation Master, the Canyons Workforce Housing Plan, Trials 
Master Plan, Natural Resources Management Plan and Open Space and Viewshed Protection 
Plan, to name a few.   
 
There are twelve (12) distinct development areas within the SPA. Each development area has 
specific planning standards based on its location, including allowable uses, density, and 
configuration, as outlined in the Development Agreement and its accompanying Exhibits.  
 
Due to the location and phasing requirements outlined in the Development Agreement, the 
mid-mountain development has not commenced and cannot begin until specific  thresholds are 
met. As part of the long -range planning for the Red Pine Village, Red Pine Lake, and Tombstone 
development areas, the applicant is working to address access and traffic concerns for these 
areas. 
 
5
4 
 
Per their application statement, TCFC has vetted Red Pine traffic -related matters with County 
personnel and the Fire Marshal, including access for service vehicles and emergency services . 
From those discussions, it has become clear that if the "general" limitation under Section 3.15 
were to be applied in such a manner as to strictly prohibit all vehicular access not only for the 
general public but for residents and guests in particular, such a strict prohibition would 
eliminate a crucial means of evacuation that must be available in the event of a wildfire or 
other life-threatening emergency. By letter of March 17, 2023 , the Fire Marshal has concluded 
that (i) the use of a gondola is not a sufficient means of egress for occupants of the 
development and does not meet the requirements of the Park City Fire District or the 
International Fire Code, and likewise, (ii) the use of shuttles is not a sufficient option for 
emergency egress. The Fire Marshal has further concluded that, given the constraints of the 
development area, personal automobiles need to be available, as a combination of shuttles and 
lifts or a gondola would not provide a successful means of emergency evacuation required for 
the numbers and locations of people on the mountain. A copy of (i) the proposed 11-
word redline amendment, (ii) a letter stating that TCFC would be willing to provide, agreeing to 
the imposition of a requirement that the roadway be non-public, gate-controlled access, and 
(iii) the Life Safety letter from the Park City District Fire Marshall EXHIBIT A. 
 
TCFC believes this critical life -safety issue can be adequately addressed and reconciled with the 
general traffic limitation by adding a clarifying phrase in Section 3.15. This phrase would restrict 
vehicular traffic to residents and guests only via a gate -controlled non -public access road. 
Emergency and resort service vehicles would, of course, still be allowed. This approach would 
achieve the dual County goals of (i) reducing traffic and (ii) ensuring life safety by providing the 
only practical means of emergency evacuation.

OVERVIEW OF APPLICATION FOR DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT AMENDMENT 
As part of TCFC's long-range planning for its Red Pine project (entitled under the Canyons 
SPA DA), TCFC is undertaking to proactively address traffic-related items associated with that 
future project. This Application relates to Section 3.15 of the DA, which reads: 
As required in Section 3.3.S{a) of this Amended Agreement, a remote parking and 
management plan, to include check-in, concierge service and transportation 
between parking and accommodation, must be included in the Transportation 
Plan. This aspect of the Plan shall be adopted and implemented in conjunction 
with hotel/lodging unit development in Red Pine Village, Tombstone, and Red Pine 
Lake Development Areas. In general, automobile access to these areas, will be 
prohibited. Access will be provided and maintained for service vehicles 
and emergency services. (underscore added) 
Of particular note, the highlighted vehicular limitation is "In general," not absolute, and access 
related to emergency services must be provided. 
TCFC has vetted Red Pine traffic-related matters with County personnel and the Fire 
Marshal, including access for service vehicles and emergency services. From those discussions, 
it has become clear that if the "general" limitation under Section 3.15 were to be applied in such 
a manner as to strictly prohibit all vehicular access not only for the general public but for residents 
and guests in particular, such a strict prohibition would eliminate a crucial means of evacuation 
that must be available in the event of a wildfire or other life-threatening emergency. By letter of 
March 17, 2023 (copy attached), the Fire Marshal has concluded that (i) the use of a gondola is 
not a sufficient means of egress for occupants of the development and does not meet the 
requirements of the Park City Fire District or the International Fire Code, and likewise, (ii) the use 
of shuttles is not a sufficient option for emergency egress. The Fire Marshall has further 
concluded that given the constraints of the development area, personal automobiles need to be 
available, as a combination of shuttles and lifts or a gondola would not provide a successful 
means of emergency evacuation required for the numbers and locations of people on-mountain. 
TCFC believes this major life-safety issue can be properly addressed and reconciled in 
harmony with the general traffic limitation through the insertion of a clarifying phrase in Section 
3.15 that would allow vehicular traffic only for residents and guests via a gate-controlled non­
public access road. Access for emergency and resort service vehicles would, of course, still be 
allowed. This approach would serve the dual County goals of (i) mitigating traffic and (ii) meeting 
life-safety demands by providing the only practical means of emergency evacuation. 
Attached is a March 30, 2023 Traffic Study Update from Hales Engineering that is specific 
to Red Pine. This focused Study Update concludes that the only potential additional traffic 
mitigation measure associated with future Red Pine development would be at the High Mountain 
Road / Escala Court intersection. This Study Update also notes that the 2018 Transportation 
Master Plan Study, which was utilized in connection with the 2018 SPA amendment, took into 
account projected traffic data associated with the Red Pine development (including vehicular 
traffic for residents and guests) and substantiated that existing traffic infrastructure and 
improvements have already been sized to accommodate Red Pine-related traffic impacts.
