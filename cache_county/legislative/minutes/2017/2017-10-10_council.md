To: Mayor Craig Petersen
From: Kymber Housley!
Date: September 22, 2017
Subject: Use of RDA Funds for Construction of New Library

Pursuant to your request for a legal opinion regarding the use of Redevelopment Agency funds !
for the construction of a new library within the Downtown Project Area, I submit the following:

The new library is proposed to be built within the Downtown Project Area, but the available |
funds that could be used to help fund the construction have been mostly generated by other
project areas within the Agency’s boundaries. The Community Reinvestment Agency Act as set
forth in Utah Code Ann. §17C-1-409 (1)(a)(iii)(E) provides for the use of Agency funds for all or .
part of the cost of the installation of publicly owned infrastructure and improvements outside the
project area from which the project area funds are collected if the Redevelopment Agency and
City Council, (which in the City of Logan are made up of the same members), determine by
resolution that the publicly owned infrastructure and improvements benefit the project area.

A question to be answered is does the construction of a new library constitute publicly owned
infrastructure and improvements. Under the definitions of the Act in UCA §17C-1-102, “project
area development” specifically includes public buildings and the definition of “publically owned
infrastructure and improvements” includes facilities, infrastructure, and improvements benefiting
the public and to be publicly owned or publicly maintained or operated. Under the Act’s
definitions, if the library was considered a “local government building” it would require
additional approval from the taxing entities, however, the Act’s definition of “local government
building” specifically excludes public buildings the primary purpose of which is cultural in
nature. It is my opinion that a library is cultural in nature.

My conclusion is the construction of a new library does meet the definitions of both “project area
development” and “publicly owned infrastructure and improvements” and therefore it would be
permissible to use Agency funds generated outside the Downtown Project Area as an additional
funding source, conditioned upon the Agency and the City Council passing resolutions that the
library would benefit the project area from where the funds are generated.

SERVICE PROVISION ON COUNTY ROADS

A RESOLUTION OUTLINING THE COUNTY COUNCIL’S POLICY REGARDING THE EXPANSION OR
CONTINUATION OF SERVICES ON COUNTY ROADS

Whereas, Cache County has reviewed the impact of new development along unimproved
roadways on the safety and welfare of citizens of Cache County, and;

Whereas, Cache County has determined that within the existing budget constraints
funding is not sufficient for the existing network of roadways to be properly maintained
and that additional road length is a net cost to the county even considering existing Class
B allocations from the State and property tax rates, and;

Whereas, it is not in the best interest for the safety and/or welfare of existing or future
residents of Cache County to diminish services on existing roadways to maintain new
roads or to provide . substandard service on new roadways, and;

Whereas, there are nearly 200 miles of existing paved road in Cache County that can
accommodate development with little to no impact on the County’s ability to serve said
development.

Now, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the County Council of Cache County, Utah, in a
regular meeting, lawful notice of which has been given, finds that it is in the best interests
of the citizens of Cache County to hereby adopt the following resolution:

1) There shall be no further expansion of road services on substandard roads that do
not already serve existing homes including: .

a. No expansion of winter maintenance activities (snow plowing).

b. No gravel roads be paved or “Chip Sealed”.

c. No acceptance of new roadways, gravel or paved.

2) The County Council may grant exceptions to this policy if proposed road
improvements improve the health and/or safety of existing subdivisions, homes,
_or businesses.

On behalf of Mendon City we would like to comment on the request for rezone:

1. Mountain Heights Rezone

What: Request to rezone approximately 99.84 acres from Forest Recreation (FR40) Zone to
Agricultural (A10) Zone.

Where: 7571 West 1000 South (Tax ID: 11-095-0004, -0011).

The requested rezone is located in a secondary aquifer recharge area as outlined in the US Geologic
Survey’s “Hydrogeology of recharge areas and water quality of the principal aquifers along the
Wasatch Front and adjacent areas, Utah (Cache Valley); Plate 1”. Mendon City is concerned that
any change in zoning designation associated with a new A10 designation would allow for additional
homes to be developed along with their associated water needs. Any wells could impact down slope
springs including one of Mendon City’s culinary water sources located to the south and east.
Current Utah Division of Water Rights policy has required wells in the area to be at sufficient depth
(elevation 4420 or valley floor) to insure no impact to surrounding water sources. Mendon City
would request that the County seriously consider the impact to limited water sources additional
homes could have.

Mendon City is further concerned with water quality issues that might arise with septic systems being
located in the recharge area and possible pollution to downstream water sources. Mendon is
currently dealing with a contaminated well due to nitrates that may have been impacted by septic
systems located above the well site.

Finally, Mendon is concerned with the narrow, gravel road accessing the location and the additional
traffic that would occur if approximately 10 homes were built at the location. The road is steep and
runoff from the road is negatively impacting the road surface along with concentrated water flows
washing gravel and debris on to down slope properties. Significant improvements would be needed
to accommodate any development.
