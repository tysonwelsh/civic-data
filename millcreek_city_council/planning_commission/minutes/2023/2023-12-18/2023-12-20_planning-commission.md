2.1 Consideration of CU-23-007, Request for a Conditional Use Permit to Accommodate
a Drive-Thru Coffee Shop in the C-2 Zone Location: 877 E 4500 S Applicant: Jenny
Sanders Planner: Carlos Estudillo

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 20 December 2023 Page 2 of 8
Planner Carlos Estudillo explained the applicant is seeking a conditional use permit in the C-
2 Zone for a drive-thru coffee shop at 877 E 4500 S. This is in the Smiths parking lot. There
are established businesses here and even another drive through coffee shop. The Community
Council did not see the site plan favorably due to flow of traffic issues. Planners then worked
with the applicant to create a new site plan that mitigates parking and driving issues. Mr.
Bagshaw, Development Review Engineer explained that the traffic flow and site orientation
has been changed to an L shape instead of a U shape. Two parking spaces were lost in the
redesign. The new site egress is a single lane for safe exit on the west. Mr. Bagshaw,
Development Review Engineer, said that engineering is very satisfied with these changes.
The layout of the building at the front of the property would be considered an accessory
structure and will not be allowed at the front of the property. Mitigation of this could include
moving the building to the side of the property or connecting it to the main building. There
would be a shared agreement between Smiths and the coffee shop to allocate parking. There
is a proposed monument sign and plans consider how the sign could be moved to not block
traffic line of sight. Mr. Estudillo gave a recommendation that based on the findings in his
report that staff recommends that the Planning commission take comments at the public
meeting and approve the Conditional Use Permit application CU-23-007 with the condition
that the applicant must install streetlights on their right of way facing 4500 S.

Chair LaMar said that the parking lot would allow turning into the property from the east.
Leaving the parking lot is problematic and could require restriping the parking lot. Mr.
Bagshaw said a right-hand only sign be installed to help prevent stacking problems on 4500
S. Commissioner Lofgren likes the current proposed site plan because it prevents issues with
parking. Commissioner Vance asked about restriping the parking lot. Commissioner
Anderson asked about what direction to restripe the parking lot. Mr. Bagshaw said there are
several options for restriping, but Smiths must agree. Chair LaMar asked if the applicant, Ms.
Sanders, had anything to add. Ms. Sanders replied that Mr. Estudillo gave a good summary,
and they are willing to make any changes needed. Chair LaMar asked if the Millcreek
Community Council is aware of the changes that have been made. Mr. Estudillo said the
Millcreek Community Council is aware of the changes and they did not have new comments
on the new plan.

Chair LaMar opened the public hearing.

A gentleman who did not give his name gave public comment and said he came here to hear
about the first item on the agenda.

Ryan Corbin, owner of Java Joes— Mr. Corbin stated this is a better site plan than before and
that he is an expert in drive through coffee. Mr. Corbin asked for a bypass lane that would
help deal with a large order. This would move cars out of the way. Otherwise, a large order
creates a cork that stops traffic from moving. Mr. Corbin asked where the traffic data came
from. His traffic data for his coffee shop came from his other Java Joes location.
Implementing a bypass lane will help everyone in that parking lot. Mr. Corbin expressed
frustration at this process.

Chair LaMar closed the public hearing.

1.1 Consideration of ZT-23-012, Consolidation of C-1, C-2, and C-3 Zones and the
Creation of a New Commercial C Zone Planner: Brad Sanderson
Mr. Sanderson gave the presentation for this item. The guiding principles were established as

part of the Millcreek Together Code Update, ensure the code is user friendly, consolidated,
and united, include language that is clearly defined and uniform, makes development easy,
encourage community health. The Code update primary effects new development and will
not rezone any properties as part of the residential mixed (RM) code update. All properties
will remain within their respective zone. This will not affect existing uses and or structures so
long as they were legally permitted at the time they were established. This does not change
any existing height transition requirements. One change made has been to refine uses that are
in the RM Zone. Some dwellings have been changed to permitted use rather than conditional
use. Multiple household developments which have more than 6 units would be conditional

use. Some commercial uses would be allowed under a conditional use permit or a mixed-use
development. Mixed use development is required for properties along street frontages located
within 500 feet of major intersections. Commercial uses such as restaurant, retail, and
personal services are permitted as part of a mixed-use development. Staff will add affordable
housing and ownership density incentives. Additional design standards are being proposed.
Staff will propose code changes to alter location and screening criteria for dumpsters and
utilities, recreational amenity requirements have been revised. Mr. Sanderson asked
rhetorical questions for now, but these are items to consider by the Planning Commission.

1.2 Consideration of ZT-23-015, Updates to the RM Zone Planner: Brad Sanderson

Mr. Sanderson spoke about commercial zones. C-1 Zones are rare, C-2 Zones are far more
common, C-3 are also rare. The general overview of guiding principles is the same. This
code update will not rezone any properties as part of the commercial zone update. There are
far more uses in the Commercial zone than the RM Zone. The commercial zone has
affordable housing requirements. This code change seeks to consolidate the Commercial
zones into one zone. The update seeks to change certain uses from a permitted use to a
conditional use and vice versa. Mixed-use continues to remain as an allowed use within the
commercial zone. One conversation that Mr. Sanderson had with an RV dealer that borders
an RM Zone and hoped it would not affect the expansion of their business. Mr. Sanderson
said that perhaps for properties on State Street there could be exceptions made in the code to
accommodate their businesses and the 150-foot setback. There are more design standard
changes in the commercial zone code update. Staff would like to screen and fence residential
areas from commercial areas. 80% lot coverage would be required. Staff is proposing a build-
to area within 300 feet of a major intersection. There are pros and cons to the build to
requirements. 75% of a building’s face needs to be near the street for properties. The design
standards would maintain existing material requirements for general commercial use, update
specific design standards for hotels and prohibits stucco, a mixed-use development requires a
minimum of 100 feet of street frontage, new requirements for dumpster placement and
screening. Consolidation of the three commercial zones would make the code less
cumbersome but are there any foreseeable or unintended detriments resulting from the zone
consolidation. Should commercial zones be reserved strictly for retail type uses. Mr.
Sanderson reviewed many other considerations for the Commissioners. Mr. Lilly said that the
Canyon Rim and East Millcreek Community Councils had concerns. Mr. Sanderson spoke
about limiting drive through windows to streets not facing residential areas. The Millcreek
Community Council suggested restrictions on new storage units on east of 700 East, and
wanted to reduce the percentage of affordable housing that can be built in the C Zone. 100%
is too high. The Mount Olympus Community Council was okay with this. The East Millcreek
Community Council gave a positive recommendation. Commissioner Vance asked the goal
behind having a buffer between uses that are the same nearby each other. Mr. Sanderson said
it prevents oversaturation of the same type of businesses right next to each other. If the same
type of businesses is next to each other and do not generate sales tax that is a problem.
Commissioner Vance said a limit is necessary but isn’t sure that the ratio should be.

Chair LaMar opened the public hearing.

A man who did not give his name encouraged that this be continued for another time. He was
worried about height restrictions and limiting development. Height restrictions may dissuade
developers.

Chair LaMar closed the public hearing.
