TIP Amendment – FrontRunner Commuter Rail Strategic Enhancements – Bob Allen, MAG As a result of the Utah 2021Legislative session, $100M of one-time funding will come through the TTIF program and $200M of transportation bond funding was approved for strategic enhancements of the UTA FrontRunner Commuter Rail. As part of the total request to improve the Commuter Rail reliability, enhance speed, frequency, and capacity, UTA has identified three necessary components: ● Double-tracking in strategic sections ● Station Improvements ● Additional Vehicles By double-tracking at strategic locations of the Commuter Rail along with station improvements and the purchase of additional vehicles, FrontRunner will increase peak hour frequency to 15 minutes at key stations between Ogden and Provo, reduce travel time on express trains, and MPO Technical Advisory Committee March 22, 2021 Page 3 increase seating capacity on peak hour trains from 8 cars to 14. The Total estimated project cost is $350M. This project is new to the UTA Capital Plan and requires early action to be amended into the TIP to enable potential federal funding for the remaining funds to make these FrontRunner Commuter Rail Strategic Enhancements. Brad Kenison asked if UTA knows where the double-tracking sections will be planned for. Laura Hanson, UTA said the double-tracking sections have not been determined. UTA finished the Future of FrontRunner Study a few years ago and are doing some additional operational analysis to make that determination. MAG Staff recommend the TIP be modified to add the FrontRunner Commuter Rail Strategic Enhancements project. Steve Mumford moved that MPO TAC recommend the Frontrunner Commuter Rail Strategic Enhancements project be added to the TIP. Aaron Wilson seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. UTA 5 Year Service Plan – Laura Hanson, UTA Laura Hanson, UTA reported that our region is growing rapidly, bringing both challenges and opportunity for transit. UTA has reorganized and has a new Office of Planning and Engagement to work on strengthening their coordination with local governments as they plan for the future. UTA’s Planning Process starts with the development of the Regional Transportation Plan (RTP) with MAG and Wasatch Front Regional Council. UTA is proposing a complimentary document to accompany the RTP that will focus on local transportation needs called the UTA Long-Range Transit Plan (LRTP). UTA’s LRTP has a 30-year horizon and will identify the additional funding and resources needed to achieve their regional vision. The Five-Year Service Plan is one piece of UTA’s planning process and serves as a concrete plan of projected needs based on the information and resources UTA has available to them. The Five-Year Plan is organized around a series of guiding principles. UTA’s focus in the Five-Year Plan includes system wide planning, core network investment, all-day service, expanded hours, new technologies, and new projects. UTA’s Five-Year Service Plan can be found at http://rideuta.com/serviceplan UTA is asking stakeholders for their input to help develop a cohesive, system-wide vision for public transit to best serve our communities’ needs, meet the demands of growth, and attract additional resources. MPO Technical Advisory Committee March 22, 2021 Page 4 Point of the Mountain Transit Study Update – Patti Garver, UTA Patti Garver, UTA gave an overview of the Point of the Mountain (POM) Transit Study. The purpose of the project is to study high-capacity transit connection between southern Salt Lake County and northern Utah County with connections to existing and emerging development areas. The process to update agencies and begin environmental analysis began January 2021 and will go until Fall 2022, or longer. Benefits and similarities of Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) and Light Rail Transit (LRT) were discussed. The Locally Preferred Alternative (LPA) recommended by the Steering Committee is a Common Ground Segment from Draper FrontRunner to Lehi (Traverse Mountain) that will cover 8 miles with up to 7 station. Two of the stations to be located at the POM with an option to extend to include two additional stations and transit way. To implement the Common Ground Segment, an LPA adoption is needed by project stakeholders. Next steps are to present the LPA to City Council meetings, adoption by the MPO and UTA Board of Trustees, begin the environmental study, and secure additional funds for the study with TTIF, UTA funds, STBG funds, and legislative appropriation. Kim Struthers moved to give the vote of support from TAC on the Point of the Mountain Transit Study with the addition that UTA and the Consultant Team consider the southern terminus at 2100 North area to capture the Lehi TOD planned at 2100 North. Chris Trusty seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. UDOT, TIF, TTIF-Active Process Update – Bob Allen, MAG Bob Allen reported that UDOT’s TIF and TTIF prioritization processes guides the State Transportation Commission in allocating funds. UDOT prioritization process and nomination requirements were discussed. Every project that is in Phase 1 of the RTP automatically goes through the UDOT prioritization process and is not required to be nominated. Local governments, cities or the county may nominate projects for UDOT’s consideration. UDOT and MAG cannot nominate projects. It must be done a local level. UDOT is creating a list of projects that have already gone through the prioritization process and have been ranked. Over the next few months UDOT will work to identify the outcome of the legislative session and the funds they have available through TIF. At that point, they will begin to make decisions on the projects. The deadline for 2022 nominations is March 31, 2021. MPO Technical Advisory Committee March 22, 2021 Page 5 UDOT will go through the 2022 nominations and select projects to be funded 3-4 years out. Those who are interested in nominating a project may contact MAG Staff or UDOT Region 3 Staff for assistance with the nomination process. Projects eligible for nomination include road, active transportation, transit, and first mile-last mile projects.
