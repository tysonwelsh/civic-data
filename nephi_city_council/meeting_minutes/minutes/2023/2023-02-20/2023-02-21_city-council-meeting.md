SCHOOL RESOURCE OFFICER BETWEE JUAB SCHOOL DISTRICT AND NEPHI CITY APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that with the hiring of Sarah Robison as the new school
resource officer for the elementary schools, new state regulations require the City to adopt an
agreement with the Juab School District. The agreement will help to clarify the role of the SRO and
outline the scope of work. It also stipulates the interaction between school administrative staff and the
SRO. If there is a disagreement between the administration and the SRO, there is a conflict resolution
process with the Chief of Police having the final say. The agreement was prepared by the City Attorney
and reviewed by the Chief of Police and City Administrator. The agreement had also been approved by
the School District at their most recent meeting.
   Councilor Parady moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the SRO agreement with the additions of the
numbers of $44,000 and $21,050 be inserted in the blanks on page 2 of the agreement. Councilor
Callaway seconded the motion. The motion carried on a unanimous vote.

WRIGHT DIRECTION, LLC REIMBURSEMENT AGREEMENT APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson informed the council that from time to time, developers will install utilities that may
benefit other property owners if they decide to also develop. If the original developer is to be
reimbursed for the costs associated with the installation of utilities, they must institute something called
a reimbursement or pioneering agreement. Reimbursement agreements are designed to provide
reimbursement payments to developers who install infrastructure beyond their proposed development.
The agreements typically outline the costs associated with the infrastructure installation, a time frame
to be reimbursed, the methodology for reimbursement, and an area that is benefitted by the
infrastructure installation.
   Mr. Atkinson stated that The Wright Direction, LLC has installed a power line to provide electricity to
the future meat packing plant. This line had to be extended quite a distance to the west and has the
potential to benefit several properties along the way. The attached reimbursement agreement specifies
a cost of $598,041.97 and the developer is responsible for $91,699.77 leaving a balance of $506,342.20
that could potentially be reimbursed. The reimbursement amounts are based on the number of amps
used by future developments on the power line that has a capacity of 600 amps delivered at a primary
voltage of 12,470.
   The agreement outlines a reimbursement timeframe of 20 years. Mr. Atkinson stated that this is a
long time to administer and track a reimbursement agreement. One argument for the extended time
frame, however, is that the size of the benefitted area is quite extensive. This time frame would allow
for the majority or full cost of the improvements to be reimbursed. As with other reimbursement
agreements, the city will use its best efforts to collect the reimbursement fee on developing properties
in the benefit area but is not liable for failure to make such a collection.
   Mike Sperry who was in attendance at the meeting and who works for Juab Title made the suggestion
that the exhibit showing the parcel number in the reimbursement district should also have the legal
descriptions attached. Councilor Worwood moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the reimbursement
agreement pursuant to adding the legal descriptions to each parcel number. Travis Worwood
representing Wright Direction LLC said he would have that done. Councilor Callaway seconded the
motion. The motion carried unanimously.

OUT-OF-STATE TRAVEL APPROVED FOR ZACH LARSEN FROM THE GAS DEPARTMENT:

  Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that Zach Larsen from the Natural Gas Department had
requested to attend two different Cathodic Protection classes in Houston, Texas. There are 4 steps to
achieving a NACE (National Association of Corrosion Engineers) certificate and two of the four steps will
be held this year. The first class is Cathodic Protector Tester (cp1) and takes place April 3-7 and the
second class is Cathodic Protection Technician (cp2) and takes place August 28-September 1.
  Upon completion of the two classes, he would be able to better resolve problems with the system’s
cathodic protection that otherwise would have to be hired out. There are funds in the training budget
to cover the costs of the training. Councilor Callaway moved to approve the out-of-state travel for Zach
Larsen. Councilor Parady seconded the motion. The motion carried unanimously.
