ORDINANCE 23-07: ENACT PART 3-10-600P OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY
MUNICIPAL CODE TO CREATE THE HEALTHY WEST VALLEY
COMMITTEE
Craig Thomas, CED, presented proposed Resolution 23-07 that would enact Part 3-10-600P
of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Create the Healthy West Valley Committee.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       This action will establish a consistent level of staff support and efficiencies needed
       to coordinate the groups meetings and activities. Additionally, this action will
       formalize their position within the community and open opportunities to seek
       funding through various grants. Official recognition as a Committee will also
       create an expectation to meet desired goals and outcomes as directed by the City.

       The Healthy West Valley Coalition began meeting in the fall of 2018 as a group of
       concerned residents, government representatives and health sector partners. The
       group consistently meets monthly to network, discuss resident needs, share
       resources, and formulate ways to help create a healthier community. The Coalition
       has shown that they are a valuable asset to the residents of West Valley City and
       desire to continue working together as an official Committee of West Valley City.
       This action will also help provide staffing support needed to help facilitate their
       continued growth and to allow them to seek funding through various grant
       opportunities.

       RESOLUTION 23-34: AUTHORIZE THE PURCHASE OF A VARIABLE WIDTH
       SPREADER BOX FROM BERGKAMP INC
       Dan Johnson, Public Works Director, presented proposed Resolution 23-34 that would
       authorize the Purchase of a Variable Width Spreader Box from Bergkamp Inc
       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
       follows:

              For over 25-years, the Operations Division of the Public Works Department has
              self-performed application of slurry seal as a pavement preservation tool on
              subdivision roads throughout the City using a fixed width spreader box that can
              only be used with slurry seal.
              The Department is adding microsurfacing to the in-house pavement preservation
              toolbox. Microsurfacing is similar to slurry seal, in that they are both aggregate
              and asphalt emulsion mixtures. Slurry seal oils rely in evaporation for the material
              to set up taking anywhere from 3-5 hours, while microsurface oils “break”
              chemically, setting up and ready for traffic in about one hour. Slurry seal is placed
              at a one-stone thickness, while microsurfacing can be placed at multiple stone
              thicknesses, giving it the ability to fill minor rutting and larger cracks.
              Microsurfacing cannot be applied using the existing fixed width slurry box. The
              variable width spreader box is equipped with augers to move the material around
              and attaches to the existing equipment used to apply slurry seal and can be used on
              both slurry seal and microsurfacing to spread the material evenly across the
              pavement surface.
              The microsurface treatment will be suitable for placement on main roads and will
              provide the Department with additional pavement preservation methods to prolong
              the life of our roads.

       Councilmember Harmon verified that the City already has the trucks and won’t need
       anything in addition to utilize this equipment. Dan replied that is correct. Councilmember
       Harmon stated he recalls leasing equipment similar to this for the parking lot at the Maverik

B.    FORM OF GOVERNMENT DISCUSSION
      Mayor Pro Tem Huynh stated the Council has discussed the form of government
      and would like to see a resolution on the agenda next week to allow for a ballot
      proposition in November that would change the current Council/Manager form of
      government to a 7 person Council with a strong Mayor with a 2 year term limit.

      Councilmember Nordfelt stated that West Jordan City went through a similar
      process in 2017. He indicated that residents formed a committee to research the
      different forms of government and potential impacts the change might incur. He
      noted that the residents recommended against the change but West Jordan chose to
      place the item on the ballot regardless. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that he feels
      it’s important to thoroughly study this issue so that the Council and the residents
      can make an informed decision. Councilmember Christensen agreed and noted that
      he spoke to the Mayor of West Jordan and stated that he still has a CAO (Chief
      Acting Officer) who essentially functions as the City Manager.

      Mayor Pro Tem Huynh stated that the Council has had a lot of time to study
      documents and information emailed by staff. He indicated that West Jordan is doing
      well as a City and briefly discussed their process. Mayor Pro Tem Huynh stated
      that he believes this change is best for the City and added that he would like to see
      a 2 year term limit for the Mayor so that no person can serve for too long a time.
      He indicated that this has been discussed by the Council and he would like to see it
      move forward. Councilmember Christensen stated that none of these decisions have
      been made and indicated that he has not been a part of any discussion that led to a
      specific decision like this being placed on the agenda. Councilmember Fitisemanu
      agreed that he doesn’t recall discussing the selection of a specific form of
      government, a term limit, or agreeing to place this item on the agenda for Council’s
      consideration.

      Councilmember Fitisemanu stated that this is not something the council should
      jump into quickly and noted that he likes the idea of involving residents or creating

 a committee to research the issue independently. He indicated that he isn’t
 comfortable making this type of decision based on his own personal opinion and
 noted that he was elected to represent the residents in his district and he would like
 the opportunity to hear their input. Councilmember Fitisemanu stated that he likes
 the idea of having town hall type events and asked if there is a timeline. Eric
 Bunderson, City Attorney, replied that the resolution doesn’t need to be approved
 until August at the latest. He noted that if the Council passes a resolution they are
 required to hold two public hearings within 45 days and the resolution can also be
 rescinded up to 60 days after approval.

 Mayor Pro Tem Huynh discussed West Jordan’s experience and stated he would
 like to give citizens the opportunity to vote for their form of government.
 Councilmember Nordfelt stated that passing a resolution like this is a big decision.
 Councilmember Fitisemanu agreed and stated that there are pros and cons to every
 form of government. He stated that most citizens don’t understand the different
 forms of government and he wants to ensure that the council does their due
 diligence in ensuring voters are properly educated and understand the different
 implications this change can bring about.

 Councilmember Nordfelt stated that the current form of government has been
 working very well for West Valley City. He indicated that 7 elected officials can
 fire a City Manager when that person is no longer serving the City well. He
 indicated that this process allows someone who is qualified, properly educated, and
 legally trained to effectively manage a municipality. Councilmember Nordfelt
 stated that this is a benefit that cannot be provided by a strong Mayor form of
 government. He stated that the City may get lucky once or twice with an elected
 Mayor but for the most part, it is unlikely. Councilmember Nordfelt referenced the
 last two presidential elections and the disgruntled nature of citizens with their
 outcome. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that the Council would lose a lot of
 authority with a strong Mayor and noted that it’s more equal and balanced when
 there are 7 elected officials who consider policies and can fire a City Manager at
 any time if changes need to be made quickly. He indicated that a Mayor is elected
 and if that person is not serving the City well, citizens have to wait until the next
 election. Councilmember Nordfelt indicated that an elected Mayor could hire an
 administrator to run the City but then residents will be paying a full time Mayor in
 addition to a full time “City Manager” type position. He indicated that the closest
 City in size to West Valley City is Salt Lake City and questioned if the Council
 knew how many people work in the Mayor’s office. He stated that he would suspect
 that it’s 20 times more employees than it is for the current City Manager’s office.
 Councilmember Nordfelt indicated that the council would transfer a lot of its
 authority to the Mayor in this form of government and indicated that 7 heads are

 better than one. He indicated that a Mayor can be politically motivated and can
 make decisions based on what is best for them politically as opposed to a City
 Manager who makes decisions based on what is best for the City and not based on
 what will get them more votes. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that a lot of
 residents don’t understand the difference in forms of governments and he fears that
 residents will choose to change it before fully understanding the implications. He
 indicated that he doesn’t feel this proposed change is best for West Valley City.

 Councilmember Christensen stated that it’s difficult to keep positive momentum
 going when the leader of the City changes constantly and has different goals and
 priorities. Councilmember Christensen stated that West Valley City began with a
 different form of government when it was first incorporated. Councilmember
 Christensen stated that the current form of government has served the citizens of
 West Valley well and he believes “if it isn’t broke, don’t fix it”. Councilmember
 Christensen stated that the City has been fortunate to have a qualified City Manager
 for over 20 years who has kept staff going, learned from mistakes, and kept
 momentum moving forward. He noted that a new Mayor with a 2-term limit means
 that every 8 years, there could be a new administration which means new
 employees could be politically motivated and not serving the residents of the City.
 Councilmember Nordfelt indicated that residents elect the Council to study issues
 and make decisions that is best for the City long-term. He noted that he believes
 that changing the form of government to strong Mayor is a tool to please those
 seeking power.

 Mayor Pro-Tem Huynh stated that Salt lake City and Sandy are very different than
 West Jordan, Taylorsville, and Murray. He indicated that he believes the Mayor
 would serve as a check and balance to the Council. Mayor Pro Tem Huynh stated
 that the Mayor would live in the City and would want to fix issues in the
 community. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that West Valley City is fortunate to
 have one City Manager who has carried out a consistent policy in the last 20 years
 while other municipalities have had multiple different leaders with different goals
 in that same time frame.

 Councilmember Harmon stated that he is in favor of going through with this process
 and added that he likes the idea of having a town hall meeting. He indicated that he
 would like to approve a resolution and gain public input with the knowledge that
 the resolution can be rescinded. Councilmember Harmon stated that he believes the
 citizens should have the option to vote on the form of government, especially since
 they haven’t had that option in 40 years.
