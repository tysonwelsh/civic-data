5.1 Unified Police Department
Detective Dawn Larsen presented the UPD report for June 2024. This included some suspicious
vehicles, a burglary into a private residence where charges were filed but the DA declined to
prosecute. There was a search and rescue for a group looking for the “treasure,” where they
ended up at lookout peak. There were a handful of suspicious circumstances reported, with a
total of 50 calls for service with 19 reports written. She asked the council about the new bathroom
built, noting the east side door doesn’t shut or lock, and in fact doesn’t fit in the frame. The
council discussed that issue and will look into it. She asked if road construction would be
completed before a bicycle race scheduled for August 25, 2024. If the construction will not be
completed by that date, the race will need to hire two additional officers to sit at the construction
zone.
Mayor Smolka responded that the lower construction site should be completed, and the upper
site will be close to completion.
Council Member Hawkes added that the application is for a bicycle race coming down the
canyon. They are stating there will not be any lane closures.
Detective Larsen responded they really need to close those lanes for the race, it is a safety issue
doing time trials and cyclists not wanting to slow down for cars.
Council Member Hawkes responded that, according to Michelle, they have no plans to close any
lanes for this race, using the westbound lane for the route. She would like to discuss approval
of this race. They discussed the trial starts being staggered, noting the eastbound lane is closed
for construction.
Detective Larsen feels it would be unsafe to have uphill traffic through that area during the race.
Council Member Hawkes noted that they have always closed the uphill lane for races in the
canyon, and residents are accustomed to the eastbound lane being closed for race events. She
expressed concern for resident reaction if something different were done, with the construction
zone being the main area of concern.
Mayor Smolka agreed, he does not like the idea of closing the westbound lane.
Detective Larsen would prefer to stay with their normal procedures and will have an officer
stationed at the construction zone, and she will try to get more information about the race
tomorrow for the council as this is their first race.
Council Member Hawkes also noted that the organization is asking for their permit fees to be
waived, they are a veteran based organization.

5.2 Unified Fire Authority
Division Chief Bryan Case reported call volume for Station 119, and said Station 119 response
times were around 12 minutes. Chief Case reported a finalized UFA budget was available for
review on the website which also provided an update on recruitment efforts. He reported Salt
Lake County Emergency Management was working to create evacuation zones in the canyon
with maps, signs, and sirens. He said Station 119 crews are working to be an example by cutting
down some trees close to the station, and they will be participating in emergency management
home assessments and leaving tags on front doors to get information to the public.
Council Member Hawkes discussed the abilities of the CodeRed system for those who might be
stuck or stranded in an emergency.
Chief Case also noted that installing sirens throughout the canyon would only be useful if they
are tested and residents know and understand what they mean. He also discussed some of the
other items on the emergency maps he was discussing like potential helicopter landing areas.
He talked about new pieces of equipment and asked the council to remind residents to be
hypervigilant about wildfires this time of year.

5.3. Salt Lake County Animal Services

6.1 Reappointment of Emigration Canyon Planning Commission
Polly McLean presented the following names for reappointment to the Emigration Canyon
Planning Commission: Dale Berreth will have a one-year term, expiring March 1, 2025; Jim
Karkut and Jody Geroux will have the two-year terms; Tim Harpst and Andrew Wallace will have
the three-year terms. All of the appointees are eligible for reappointment at the end of their term
if the council desires. Council Member Pinon suggested aligning the terms with the half-year
point, rather than having the term expire in March, since Planning Commission Chairs are
elected at the first meeting each year. Ms. McLean said term effective dates could be
reconsidered and they will work to offset those dates.

6.2 Consideration of a Resolution Restricting Open Fires and Ignition Sources
Ms. McLean said this agenda item was an ordinance prohibiting fire ignition sources, not a
resolution as published. She advised the Council to discuss and vote on the ordinance that
evening since the substance remained the same. The Council reviewed proposed Ordinance
No. 2024-07-01.

ignition sources as presented. Council Member Hawkes seconded the motion; vote was
4-0, unanimous in favor. Council Member Harris was absent from the vote.

Mayor Smolka met with some legislators on Saturday, discussing funding for re-writing the code,

6.5 Ongoing Projects
Mayor Smolka reported the Lower Finn wall was almost complete. He and other members of the
community have been working on choosing a color for the Red Hill art sculpture, they are hoping
to match the hill somewhat with their final choice. All paving is complete on public sideroads in
the main canyon. The other project he is looking into is at approximately 1100 Pinecrest, where
the road is sloughing into the creek. He met with the state stream moderation staff, as well as
the Army Corp of Engineers, and they have permission to move ahead. A contractor visited the
site yesterday, coming back with a quote of $64,000 to work on the area where the road is
undercut. He is working with the private property owner on getting permission for some of the
work, which may entail filling in some of the failed open culvert area.
Council Member Hawkes asked about aftermath of flooding in multiple areas and what the future
plans were for those.
Mayor Smolka said the city did received some State grant funding for specifically the Cedar Loft
and Skycrest culvert repairs. They are also working on the area of Pinecrest where the road
washed out previously, but took out a bunch of trees in the process.

6.6 Budget Items
Mayor Smolka said a quarterly financial report would be ready for Council review at the next
Council meeting. He also noted a refund check received on Worker’s Comp for around $63, and
an insurance bill for around $10,000.

6.7 Records Management
Nicole Smedley reported options for long-term records management, after discussion with the
State Archives, and recommended using Google Does. She said she believed Google Docs
would be searchable for archival use, but she is also willing to look into other options at higher
costs.
Ms. McLean suggested the Council invite Michael Duncan with the MSD to provide instruction
on options.

(a) Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD)

issue with limited access to their homes. The illegal dumping was getting out of hand, and at
one point he saw a truck engine sitting inside of the area. He went down there today and it was
pristine, clean.
