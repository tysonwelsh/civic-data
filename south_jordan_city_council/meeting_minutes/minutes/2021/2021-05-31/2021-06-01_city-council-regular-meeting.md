         G. Presentation Item: Report from Southwest Salt Lake County Visioning Projecton
            Transportation Analysis. (By Fehr & Peers, Natalia Brown, WFRC, Jory Johner, and UDOT,
            Grant Farnsworth)


Natalia Brown reviewed prepared presentation (Attachment A).
Jory Johner reviewed prepared presentation (Attachment A). He said they are the only state that works
together through the planning process on long term regional transportation plans with all the metropolitan
planning organizations. There are four in Utah, Logan, St. George, Utah County and Wasatch Front
Regional Council. They also work with the rural parts of the state, which UDOT does the planning for.
Since 2007 they have completed four, this will be the fifth, unified transportation plan.
Grant Farnsworth said the Regional Transportation Plan was compared to the large amount of investment
on the west and southwest side, especially with east/west Mountain View Corridor and Bangerter
interchanges. We can see during the current construction, with travel restricted east/west, travel is
extremely constricted and hopefully we will feel an alleviation when that is opened back up again. Their
research was also done to see what other efforts are being missed in the plan, both big and small. They
wanted to see what happened when money wasn’t an issue and things were allowed to be looked at with
no restrictions. The two projects mentioned are the 12600 connection and 11400 South. They have been
working with staff and it was brought to his attention as we updated our Master Transportation Plan that
they take counts every few years to get traffic amounts and 11400 South was presented as 50% greater
than what they were showing. That really showed a need to widen the road and South Jordan was recently
awarded a Technical Planning Assistant Grant to allow us to say “we recognize the volumes are much
higher, what are our options besides widening.” They understand volumes can adjust with Mountain
View and Bangerter Highway and they have already started with City Engineer Brad Klavano and other
staff. Regarding 12600 South, that is going to be a major impact. They want to look at that a little bit
further as this is very high level, not very much engineering just basic concepts. They want to get an idea
of what this could be before it gets on the plan. First they have to financially constrain it, and with an
$800 million planning level cost that is a lot to push other projects out of the way, but they want to make
sure everyone is comfortable with it. They will be speaking to Riverton and South Jordan as this moves
along.
Ms. Brown reviewed prepared presentation (Attachment A).
Council Member Shelton asked about the bonding bill, if that moved projects up.
Mr. Farnsworth said they were appropriated to 2024-2025 with their transportation fund, which is just
capacity funding, and they programmed out to 2029-2030. They didn’t bond, they just programmed
further out to say “these are the projects that are coming out for the next five years.” They did receive the

additional one time funding and there was bonding for the Transportation Investment Funds (TIF) related
to the Frontrunner project.
Council Member Shelton asked to clarify if this moved some projects up so they’ll happen sooner?
Mr. Johner said the projects have now been scheduled, they were not scheduled previously. As they
developed these plans they assumed WFRC’s area would get a certain portion of the TIF money. After the
list of projects came out they realized they had to move a few projects, which included moving a project
back and out of the plan (a system interchange in Weber County) to help balance the budget. A lot of the
change also came from cleaning up the project costs or they got more revenue than what they had planned
for when they adopted the plan a few years ago. That’s where the Mountain View Corridor project moved
from Phase 2 to Phase 1, Old Bingham down to the county line. They also moved up a project from Salt
Lake City to Farmington that was in a later phase, Phase 3, the I-15 reconstruction and widening project
that is going to get funded. They waited until the Transportation Commission acted upon and make draft
recommendations at their April meeting. Their amendment will still go out for public comment on the
larger projects, the ones that impacted air quality conformity and freeway projects, this summer and then
those will get finalized as part of their amendment package in August.
Mayor Ramsey said with the TIF funding the 9800 South and Bangerter project was funded, this is slated
to be started in 2023.
Mr. Farnsworth said the approvals for 9800 South, 4700 South and 13400 South have come through;
9800 South was made a priority.
Council Member Shelton asked to confirm that the freeway on 12600 South connecting Mountain View
and Bangerter was not on any long range plan at this point. He also asked about the 9000 South freeway
scenario and said he assumed it didn’t do well when researched.
Mr. Farnsworth said 12600 South is just part of the preferred scenario, it is not part of a plan yet.
Ms. Brown said the 9000 South freeway scenario did not perform well and was a large investment with
little benefit for that amount.
Mr. Johner said if you make improvements to all arterials versus one freeway connection the costs would
be similar, but not as effective. He suggested looking more at local street connectivity and the benefits of
not all roads funneling into this small one mile space. There is a lot of difference between here and the
east side of Salt Lake County where there is a tighter grid that is spread out, they don’t need a multi-lane
roadway in some of those areas because they have a tighter grid rather than roads spaced out with cul-de-
sacs. He pointed out that not everyone needs to get onto the freeway to get off in one exit, it makes more
sense to use city streets to move locally.
Mayor Ramsey said she has heard from people and read social media posts saying “there was no
planning, everyone was idiots and this should never have happened with traffic right now at the Bangerter
interchanges” and she wants it to be heard that there was a lot of planning. The best planners are working
on this at every level, there is so much planning taking place. It is such a collaborative effort and they
know traffic is challenging right now with those two interchanges being done, but it is going to be so
much better when it’s done and she appreciates the planning taking place. She wanted to thank everyone
here working on this.
