A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to notification
requirements came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated based on recent state law changes, cities are no longer
required to provide public notice using the newspaper for land use applications. Instead, state law generally requires posting
notices on the City’s website, on the Utah Public Notice website, posting in at least three public places, and, if applicable, mailing
to affected entities as required by both state and city code. As such, the proposed amendments remove the newspaper noticing
requirements for subdivision code section changes, street and alley vacations, zoning ordinance changes and rezones, general plan
amendments, and designations to the local historic register. The Council extended consideration of the proposed amendments at
the March 21, 2023 meeting. Council members voted to extend consideration with the direction that information regarding
noticing costs and newspaper subscribership and circulation be presented. City staff presented the information requested by the
Council at the work session on July 11,2023. The proposal was presented to the Council for consideration at the August 15, 2023
meeting. At that meeting, Council members expressed concerns about costs and archiving of notices. The Council tabled the
consideration of the item to the August 29, 2023 meeting.
Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
proposed amendments to notification requirements. Senate Bill 201 eliminates the requirements to post notices in newspapers but
does require notices be posted on the Utah Public Notice Website and the City website. The proposed ordinance amendment

Mr. Brierley noted that during the August 15 meeting, the Council asked several questions about the manner in which the
Utah Public Notice Website archives the notices that are published there. He provided a summary of the benefits of using the
Website, and emphasized language taken from the Website indicating that past notices are archived and are publicly accessible.
Users can search for current, past, and future notices of public bodies. He noted he performed a search of Ogden City public
notices and the search yielded 31 pages of archived notices; he also performed an audit to determine if all required notices had
also been published on the website and found that all planning notices for the past two years were properly posted and archived.
He then discussed the process for subscribing to a public body; the website provides options for subscribing to individual public
bodies or to all public bodies for a government entity. The same option is available through the City’s website. He then presented
a graph illustrating the most effective noticing method of land use items; Planning staff has polled meeting participants to
determine how they learned of certain land use item and less than five of the 45 people polled indicated they read a public notice
in the newspaper. The most effective noticing method is direct mail, which will still be provided for certain land use applications.
He discussed the amount of staff work and non-monetary resources dedicated to public noticing and added there is also a customer
service cost associated with requiring newspaper noticing. If there is a requirement for a 14-day public notice for a land use item,
the notice must be sent to the newspaper three days before the 14-day window, meaning that more lead time is needed and there is
. a potential for applications to be delayed. The Planning Commission has recommended that the Council adopt the proposed
ordinance amendments to replace the requirement for posting notices in the newspaper with a requirement to post it on the Utah
Public Notice Website. Staff proposed that the effective date of the new requirements be January 1, 2024, to give staff additional
time to advertise the change in noticing procedures. '
Council member White asked if the noticing changes will apply to all notices, not just land use notices. Mr. Brierley
stated the State law amendments extend to all types of notices, but this particular amendment only applies to land use matters.
Other Departments may come forward with proposed amendments for the types of notices they are responsible for. Chief )
Administrative Officer Brown stated that she will need to research noticing requirements for other types of decisions; however,
she knows for certain that requests for proposals (RFP) or other public solicitation notices are still published in the newspaper.
Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated that notices that are placed in the newspaper are not of the same format of
the notices that are included on the City website and the Utah Public Notice Website; this means that newspaper publishing is not
just a matter of sending the same notice to one additional email address, but staff is required to create a notice in an entirely
different format.
Council member White asked if the Planning Division will still send postcards to landowners/residents affected by a
myriad of different types of land use applications, to which Mr. Brierley answered yes.
Discussion among the Council and staff centered on the manner in which staff will communicate with the public about
the noticing changes until January of 2024.

wise to evolve and provide broader access to information; he is not sure the newspaper can satisfy that requirement any longer.

Chair Choberka stated she has mixed feelings about this issue; she agreed there is a number of people in Ogden that do
not use the internet and she is not ready to vote to stop newspaper advertising and eliminate their source of information.

Vice Chair Richey acknowledged that communication methods are changing, but if there are still some that are being
served by newspaper noticing, he wants to preserve that tool for at least longer than six months. He noted he does sympathize with
the amount of staff time and resources that are spent on newspaper notices, however. Council member Nadolski agreed. He knows
staff spends a great deal of time on noticing, but the time they spend is worth preserving the trust of the public. Chair Choberka
agreed and stated that there are so many challenges in informing people and keeping them engaged and she is not comfortable
eliminating any method of communication at this point.

Council member White stated she was concerned by the lack of a plan for rolling out the changes.

There was brief discussion among the Council regarding whether it may be appropriate to eliminate the requirement for
newspaper noticing at some point in the future, after which the majority of the Council present concluded to deny the proposed
ordinance at this time.
