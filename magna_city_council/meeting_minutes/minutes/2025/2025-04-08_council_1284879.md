                F. Discuss Recommendations from Subcommittees Regarding Amending Magna City
                   Code, Specifically Section 11.20, Stopping, Standing and Parking

        Dan Torres presented proposed amendments to Title 11, Chapter 20 of the municipal code,
        focusing on improving parking enforcement. The revisions aim to better align city policies with
        current Unified Police Department (UPD) practices and establish a more enforceable and
        effective framework for addressing parking violations in residential areas. The proposed
        updates emerged from a collaborative process involving a staff working group, UPD input, and
        a subcommittee comprised of Council Members Trish Hull and Mick Sudbury. The primary
        objectives were to strengthen enforcement powers, introduce clearer parking regulations, and
        revise outdated fine structures.

        Key revisions include:

            -   Explicit no-parking zones near mailboxes, crosswalks, and intersections, consistent with
                U.S. Postal Service guidelines
            -   A reaffirmed 24-hour on-street parking limit in residential areas
            -   Enhanced restrictions on commercial vehicle parking in residential neighborhoods
            -   Updated and increased fine amounts for specific violations, with the intent of promoting
                compliance rather than generating revenue

        Council Member Hull explained that many fines were raised from the previously low $25 level,
        particularly for more egregious violations such as parking large commercial trucks in residential
        areas. The subcommittee aimed to make penalties meaningful enough to discourage repeat
        offenses and address persistent complaints from residents.

        Council Member Audrey Pierce raised questions about the scope of the 24-hour rule and
        whether it applied to both marked and unmarked parking areas.

        Dan Torres clarified that the restriction is specifically intended for residential streets. He also
        acknowledged that current code language referencing a permitting process for extended on-
        street parking had been removed, as no such permit process had ever been implemented. The
        need for clearer signage and differentiation between public and private roads, such as those
        governed by HOAs, was also discussed.

        Council Member Hull raised a separate concern about an existing ordinance requiring cars to

                I. Discussion Regarding Adoption of Engineering Standards and Specifications

        Tamaran Woodland and Lizel Allen presented an informational update regarding the Magna
        City Engineering Standard Drawings, as required by House Bill 368, which mandates
        municipalities to adopt their own engineering standards. This initiative aims to modernize and
        formalize the city’s engineering standards for public infrastructure and private development
        projects. They explained that the city will adopt a package of engineering standards
        incorporating industry norms, specifically those established by APWA (American Public Works
        Association), AASHTO (American Association of State Highway and Transportation Officials),
        and MUTCD (Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices). For APWA, the proposal
        recommends adopting the most current Utah-specific standards automatically, allowing the city
        to remain up to date without requiring new council action every time an update is issued. One
        significant focus of the presentation was on APWA Standard Drawing 255, which governs
        street repair procedures following road excavation. This standard became controversial after
        the 2017 update introduced a two-inch mill and overlay requirement extending four feet beyond
        trench patches. Magna’s current ordinance is more stringent, requiring even larger overlay
        areas for new or recently resurfaced roads (0–3 years)—specifically 15 feet beyond the trench
        or to the road’s centerline. Council Member Trish Hull recalled that this enhanced repair
        requirement was implemented to maintain road aesthetics and protect investment in newly
        paved streets. The engineering team recommended a practical exception to the overlay
        requirement: waiving the mill and overlay for excavations under 300 linear feet in roads older
        than three years. This exception is designed to ease the burden on utility providers like Magna
        Water or Enbridge, who often need to make small-scale but urgent repairs. They clarified that
        the city’s existing, stricter requirements would continue to apply to newer streets. The
        discussion included how other cities handle similar situations. For example, West Valley City
        has its own version of Standard 255 without the overlay requirement, while Salt Lake City
        mandates mill and overlay repairs for seven years after reconstruction and three years after
        repaving. The proposed approach for Magna aims to balance maintenance quality with
        practical flexibility for utility work.

        Council Member Steve Prokopis voiced concern over whether quality enforcement, rather than
        just the patch size, might be the more important factor in preserving road longevity. In
        response, staff reported that a new construction inspector has been assigned to oversee
        excavation permits in Magna, ensuring that repairs—regardless of scale—are done to proper
        standards.

        Additional discussion included updates to lighting standards, with the city planning to adopt a
        new arterial light fixture that matches the community aesthetic but provides sufficient
        illumination for wider roads. This change was made in response to prior feedback that existing
        fixtures, such as those along certain corridors like in front of D.R. Horton’s office, were
        insufficient.
