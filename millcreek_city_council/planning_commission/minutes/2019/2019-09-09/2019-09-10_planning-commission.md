1. Discussion
1.1 City Center Overlay Zone
Francis Lilly gave the Commission an update on the community engagement for the city
center overlay zone. Staff took the overlay zone to the community councils last week and
three of the four requested more time to review the ordinance proposal. He suggested hearing
the item at the Planning Commission meeting the next week but recommended a continuance
of the item until the community councils could provide their recommendations. The Mt.
Olympus Community Council liked the plan and recommended that buildings that are in
excess of 40 feet that are on the north side of 3300 South on the corners of Highland Drive
and 1300 East have no more than 25% be housing. They want those corners to have more
commercial use. He said on Highland Drive there is a federally designated opportunity zone
that allows money to be reinvested in the site in a tax deferred manner which has advantages
for certain investors. Mike Winder said the proposed 25% restriction made sense from an
economic development standpoint.

Lilly said the height sensitivity map in the city center master plan shows color-coded heights
from 1-6 stories. He highlighted the map for the Commission. The idea is that 6+ story
buildings could be tolerated along the proposed open space and along 3300 S. and 1300 E.
That map did not tie to existing parcels, specify height in feet, or take account of the projects
being built on Gunn Avenue. Lilly said staff entered into an agreement for the Mountair
streetscape redevelopment. That property was purchased from the developers on Gunn
Avenue. He showed the Commission an updated proposed height sensitivity map with
changes to the height translated to feet (12-74’) instead of stories, tied to existing parcels
boundaries, and accommodating approved developments. Lilly said that the Millcreek
Community Council recommended that a lower height be allowed to the south of the city
center open space to allow for a view, and the proposed map reflected that, too. He pointed
out that greater height could be tolerated geographically down the slope towards 1300 E. and
there would be a height transition to the west from Highland Drive. He said Mountair
residents are 215 feet away from the possibility of a 50-foot tall building across the street on
Highland Drive. He then highlighted a map that showed the existing parcels under the newly
proposed color-coded height transitions from the comments provided at the Commission’s
last work meeting. The map showed greater height allowances on the south side of 3300
South. Winder pointed out that Salt Lake City could put in greater building height on their
property (west side of 1300 E.) next to what Millcreek is doing. He suggested allowing more
height on the north west end of the city center along 3300 South. John Janson pointed out
there would also be landscaping and stepbacks on buildings on Highland Drive so it should
not impact the Mountair residents.

Lilly asked the Commission to consider things about the city center in comparison to Sugar
House. He said the community had voiced their opinions through the General Plan and City
Center Master Plan engagement and through other means for years that they want good open
space, affordable housing, improvements to utilities, less density, fixing roads like Highland
Drive, increasing the walkability of Highland Drive and Richmond Street (1300 E.), storm
drainage, and undergrounding powerlines. Millcreek could facilitate these changes by
creating a community reinvestment area that will allow tax increment to be reinvested into
the neighborhood for public projects. To get that tax increment, the City needs to create
conditions that enhance the viability and developability of parcels. That is why staff is
making the case for greater height, because it increases the potential increment over the 20-
year life of the community reinvestment area which gives the City additional funds to give
the community the things that it wants. The community reinvestment area must set aside 20%
of the increment for affordable housing throughout the City. Winder said if there was ever a
situation that a project contemplated more than 6 stories, the Planning Commission would
review it. He went through a memo that he previously emailed to the Commission regarding
height, the city center open space bond, and the tax base. He said the County Geologist said
having the open space be within close proximity to the fault line was low risk compared to
the rest of the valley.

Francis Lilly showed the Commission pictures of buildings from Sugar House that were
examples of setbacks, heights, and lengths. John Janson pointed out that stepbacks are
required on Richmond St., Highland Dr., and around the open space. Lilly showed good and
bad examples of windows and asked the Commission for their opinion. Commissioner
Stephens requested larger sidewalks. Janson requested good materials, windows, and greater
indentations in the buildings. Commissioner Stephens mentioned that more stepbacks

provided better view corridors. Winder mentioned that Gunn Avenue would be renamed to
Villa Vista Avenue. He then mentioned that a particular piece of property would like to
redevelop with a mixed use if they are granted greater height. Lilly mentioned that all mixed
use and multifamily developments would be a conditional use in the zone. Chair Healey
asked about the Mt. Olympus Community Council recommendation of 25% housing
restriction because he would like to see more offices. Lilly said the intent is to not have a
residential village. Chair Healey asked about the Highland Drive transition from a 4-lane to a
3-lane road. Lilly said the businesses on Highland Drive preferred the 3-lane road and want
cars to slow down, so they are drawn to the businesses. The traffic study for the city center
will be done in the next few weeks. Staff has talked to the Utah Department of

cross streets.

Chair Healey asked about Millcreek’s attempt at acquiring the Brickyard Plaza. Lilly said the
Council made a good faith commitment not to bring the Brickyard up in negotiations. Winder
said no council can bind a future council. Lilly said a property owner in Brickyard Plaza
expressed interest in annexing into Millcreek. He said he did not entertain the discussion, but
the conversation let him know what the property owners there might be thinking. Lilly felt
that more rooftops would support the commercial in the city center, but he did not want to
put in rooftops who would then shop in Brickyard. Council Member Marchant said he toured
Sugar House and Holladay and said that Holladay officials said they did not have enough
rooftops to support the commercial that got built in their city center. He said the city center
needed a good balance between commercial and residential. Commissioner Stephens said he
wanted staff to fine tune architectural standards because the Planning Commission would not
see permitted uses. Chair Healey offered that the greatest height allowance should be 76 feet
instead of 74. Blaine Gehring said he has worked in other cities where there have been site
plan review committees to enforce the building standards. Lilly said that topic had come up
and he would consider it.

Lilly said the next ordinance revision would be done after the Planning Commission meeting
on September 18"". Chair Healey suggested looking at Farmington Station as a model
because it attracts shoppers. Commissioner Stephens brought up arcades versus awnings
along the open space and said that what gets developed should be consistent with specific
dimension and column requirements. Chair Healey said he would like the intersection corner
buildings to not have residential in them. Chair Healy recommended changing the 2-inch
caliper tree requirement to 3 or 4 inches. Commissioner Stephens suggested not using a
color-coded height sensitivity map, to instead use patterns for designations. Winder requested
having an exception mechanism in code for the City to be able to approve an amazing project
that differed from the default of the code. Lilly said that State code says that land use
ordinances have to be interpreted through plain language which eliminates the use of
administrative discretion in interpreting the code. Lilly said the code would need to be
changed through the appropriate process should an exception need to be made.
Commissioner Stephens recommended not allowing commercial recreation in the city center
until the commercial recreation definition in code is amended. Commissioner Sieber asked
about the color of street lighting. Lilly said he could add a provision prohibiting white lights.
Commissioner Sieber asked for an updated map to include the federal opportunity zone. A
side conversation took place about opportunity zones. Winder asked if the Tuxedo Junction
