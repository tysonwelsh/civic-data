and campaign business, explaining that it was not appropriate to attend a city-sponsored event( he used the
example of

        1. 2    Mayor' s Report


 EnMayor Overson mentioned a recent ribbon- cutting at Wasatch Pediatric Dentistry. As always, she
loved to celebrate new businesses in the community.

An Evening ofArt had been held recently at Centennial Plaza. Renowned artist Ben Hammond had been
an invited guest, as had teacher/artist Justin Wheatley. The mayor stated it was a very enriching and
enlightening evening and she hoped to host a similar event sometime in the Fall.

Starry Nights on the Plaza was becoming more popular each week. She pointed out how exciting it was to
watch the community interaction.

     She wished to thank the Parks and Recreation Committee for organizing the recent Ride Roll&        Stroll
event along the Jordan River. Those that participated in the event enjoyed it.

Let' s Talk Taylorsville was held the previous week. In-home childcare and neighborhood safety were both
discussed, and it was nice to have an opportunity for back-and-forth dialogue with residents.

Mayor Overson explained that she recently had the opportunity to meet with State Representative Brett
Garner who had filled the vacancy left when Rep. Karen Kwan replaced Sen. Karen Mayne. Rep. Garner
had asked to meet in order to share ideas of interest and concern in Taylorsville. She appreciated the
opportunity to discuss topics of mutual interest.

An open house had been held the previous evening to discuss the City' s General Plan and proposed updates
to it. The event was a great opportunity to discuss the vision of the city from the residents' perspective and
to hear their concerns and ideas. The elected officials were present not so much to comment but to listen to
residents and hear what they had to say.

The Mayor and Chief Cottam both had the opportunity to speak at Arcadia Elementary' s 5th grade
graduation the previous week. She said there was a lot of great energy and excitement in the room, and she
really appreciated the opportunity to interact with the kids.

Speaking of schools, Granite School District' s Summer Lunch Program would begin the following Monday
at Freemont Elementary and Vista Park. Children could attend with a parent and eat a meal on site. This
program helped feed children who might not get enough to eat at home when school was not in session.


K-9 Ghost was the newest member of TVPD' s K-9 team. He was deployed shortly after becoming certified.
The Utah Peace Officers Association recently held their K-9 trials. Officer Moore and Turbo took first place
and were named the Overall Top Narcotics Dog. They also took first place in the Open Area Narcotics
competition and in Patrol Building Search for Humans. Sgt. Palmer and Koda took second in Patrol
Building Search for Humans and first in Closest Call Off. Mayor Overson stated that TVPD' s K-9 team
were" Top Dogs."

The police department recently hosted a lunch to honor all the school crossing guards who helped protect
children as they made their way to and from school. In particular, she wished to acknowledge Ladd Tanner
who was retiring. Mr. Tanner was an icon at Fox Hollow Elementary School and would be missed.

Mayor Overson mentioned that Taylorsville Cemetery had looked beautiful over the Memorial Day
weekend. She gave a shout out to Suzie Smith and the cemetery team.

Pickleball courts at Vista Park had been painted and would be ready for play the following weekend. A
great deal of work still needed to be done around the courts, but the courts themselves were nearly done.
Progress was being made on the courts at the Senior Center as well. The mayor took a moment to
acknowledged the TRCC funding( Tourism Recreation Culture & Convention) that had made various city

projects possible, including more pickleball courts.

3.        REPORTS


          3. 1    Historic Preservation Committee Report– Susan Yadeskie


         Committee Chair Susan Yadeskie expressed her appreciation to the other members of the Historic
Preservation Committee. She pointed out that most were senior citizens, but they still did a phenomenal
amount of work each year.




There were some unspent funds for FY22- 23 only because there had been a challenge securing a contractor
to do some work. Grant funds had been secured to do some work on the Jones Dairy. The museum had been
added to the National Historic Registry— only the second such building in Taylorsville.



Ms. Yadeskie looked forward to speaking to the public about the community' s history during Taylorsville
Dayzz. Additionally, the Fall festival was scheduled for September 23rd. The festival would include a
pumpkin painting contest, games for kids, refreshments, etc.

There would be volunteer opportunities at the museum during the annual Day of Service event in
September. As always, volunteers were critical to the museum' s success.


Ms. Yadeskie expressed concern about recent thefts and vandalism that had taken place at the museum. She
asked those gathered to contact the committee with any ideas on combatting the crime.

The committee was seeking younger volunteers who could commit to taking part in educating the public
on the city' s history. Plenty of people were willing to volunteer for one or two events but they really needed
people willing to commit longer term.

     J       Mark Chalk, General Manager and CEO of Taylorsville- Bennion Improvement District greeted the
