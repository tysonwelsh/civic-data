2.     2021 Development Updates.

City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, described what had been done in
the recent past. Over the past few weeks, he, Senior City Planner Jennifer Robison, and Associate
City Planner Ellen Oakman compiled data for commercial projects and housing figures. All relate
to conversations pertaining to the General Plan. Information was also received from other sources
including the Kem C. Gardner Institute regarding trends in the City.

Mr. Crowell referenced the Rockwell Ridge Business Park, which was once part oflndependence.
It is located on the southeast corner of Porter Rockwell Boulevard and 14600 South. The
intersection is plaimed to be massive. In the past, the City generated an estimate of the value of
the building to calculate Building Permit Fees. The value wi ll translate into assessed value, which
is revenue at some level. The Economic Development Area ("EDA") takes the assessed value and
puts it into the tax increment availability to the use of the project.

Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner, stated that it goes back to all of the taxing
entities of which the City is one. Ultimately it is a windfall for the City because the various EDAs
are charging five to 10 percent as a Management Fee. The prope1iy tax percentage as a ratio is
higher than that. Once it comes back, there will be an income revenue benefit to the City.
Mr. Crowell stated that although it got off to a slow start, the Eastern Bluffdale EDA has been very
successful. Other things taking place in the same EDA were identified including the public garden
and other buildings and infrastructure. Mr. Crowell stated that the plans are moving forward as
expected and set the stage for good things to come.

Potential prope1iy sales and future office buildings were discussed. Mr. Crowell stated that many
had to be h1rned away from the buildings on the Redwood Road side because the use table that the
Council approved does not allow a number of uses. The second phase was ready to proceed. The
hope was that the market will remain robust.

Mrs. Robison described SilverOnyx and stated that they provide herbal supplements. They have
a business currently in the Rockwell Ridge Business Park and will maintain two sites. There will
be offices upstairs and in front with the manufacturing component behind. They w ill have a great
view of the entire valley from the office window. The owners are very happy to be in Bluffdale.

Mr. Crowell commented on the Porter Rockwell Business District and stated that the City has
entered into an Access Agreement w ith the Utah Department of Transportation ("UDOT") about
what to expect in terms of access in the area. He recalled that a portion of the Geneva gravel pit
that was put on the no11h side of Po1ier Rockwell on the Independence side, seems to be
disconnected from any other application. When Geneva exhausts its existing mining rights they
can choose how to proceed. Mr. Crowell explained that the corridor also goes out to the Redwood
Road intersection. He noted that the main intersection on Redwood Road and Poiter Rockwell
Boulevard is controlled by one specific landowner.

A question on timing was raised . Mrs. Robison stated that they want to open when the bridge is
open. They are currently working on their site plan and hope to have a permit by the end of the
year to begin construction by January. Mayor Timothy had spoken to Wadsworth who informed
him that the girders will be delivered on October 11 for the bridge. They plan to place the girders
and pour the deck next spring.

Mr. Crowell stated that families shop, spend money, and need places to work so the number of
housing starts and permits relate to economic development. Certain businesses see ce1tain
attributes and require a certain number of people and demographics. There were several approvals
issued in 2019, however, not all will come to fruition. Over the next five years, significant changes
can occur. He noted that commercial starts evaluations are up. The Council should determine the
fiscal impact of various land use types.

Benj Becker from Zions Public Finance provided numbers earlier in the year. In response to a
question raised, Mr. Kartchner stated that currently, the commercial fees are keeping up with the
reduction in housing starts. If everything comes through that they expect to, it will likely exceed
it for one or two years. From a purely tax standpoint, the apmtments are commercial and do not
receive the residential exemption. It was noted that the City has the same tax benefit. City
Manager, Mark Reid, stated that because of the numbers they have to be very careful when starting
work on park projects and ensure that they have enough money collected to complete the project.
Projects need to be phased based on the available funding.

Mayor Timothy asked if the goal is for an 1-15 type of interchange. If so, he asked what needs to
be done to get one. He explained that if one is desired, specific things must be done immediately
to promote it so that approval is granted. He explained that there is a lot of competition with the
interchange that is to be constructed in Lehi. The problem is that once it is approved it diminishes
the need for one in Bluffdale. Mayor Timothy stated that a determination should be made as to
the impact of an interchange on the traffic in smrnunding areas. He beli eved that an interchange
would be of benefit to the City. If they were strategic, he felt they could do the proper density. He
suggested consideration be given to what it would bring and the potential for certain parts of the
City to attract necessities that benefit all residents that they do not have currently. He explained
that certain businesses will not come otherwise. The Mayor stated that if they had something
tangible such as a Master Plan it would go a long way.

Mr. Reid commented on The Point Project where unlimited height and density are proposed. He
commented that what they are showing, however, seems to be realistic and similar to what already
exists. It is similar to the office complexes in Lehi and noted that 30,000 residents are expected to
live in the area. They are also expecting 40,000 jobs to be created there, which will create the need
for the C ity to create close proximity residential housing.
