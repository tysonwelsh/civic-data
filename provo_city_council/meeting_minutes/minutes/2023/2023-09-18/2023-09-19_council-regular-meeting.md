the cost of the repairs after a $1,000 deductible is paid. He requested assistance from the city.

Chair MacKay requested Brian Jones to meet Mr. Meininger at the door to collect his contact
information.

With no other comments, Chair MacKay closed the public comment period.

5.         A resolution approving the first amendment to an interlocal agreement for the Provo
           Medical School Community Reinvestment Project Area. (23-045) 36:10

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-42, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Keith Morey, Assistant Director of Development Services, presented. He said after talking about this
item numerous times, he felt like this version accurately captured all the feedback provided.

Councilor Fillmore asked if there had been anything that was not addressed that was requested in
previous conversations.

Mr. Morey said he believed everything had been addressed and updated.

Mr. Jones clarified the amount with Mr. Morey.

Councilor Shipley asked for context for how the numbers and ratios were determined.

Mr. Morey discussed the implementation of two different criteria in a business context. One criterion
involved the next phase of the commercial department structure, which had to be completed within five
years, and the other criterion was the first phase of residential development, to be completed within ten
years. The main goal was to establish a mechanism for recouping funds in case either of these criteria
was not met. He said the specific ratio for the claw back was open to adjustment, with the focus being
on ensuring penalties were in place for both scenarios, and the penalties would accumulate if either
condition was not achieved.

Councilor Shipley expressed concern about the financial impact of including or not including a building in
a project. The overall project value increased from around a million dollars on the residential side to
approximately 31 million in total when the building was factored in. Councilor Shipley emphasized that
the primary goal should be to incentivize additional work, rather than rewarding work that has already
been completed. The key issue was determining the right balance for the claw back and making sure it
strongly motivates additional work. Councilor Shipley acknowledged the challenge of finding the ideal
number for this incentive and sought a rationale for its determination.

Mr. Morey discussed the challenge of dividing the penalty between two aspects of a project. He
mentioned that there was not a specific scientific formula for the split, and it could have been either
50/50 or 40/60, for instance. The decision could have depended on the developer's perspective on
which option would be more impactful or hurtful to them. He also suggested considering input from the
developer, as they had previously indicated the use of this arrangement for financing in a previous
meeting.

Councilor Shipley mentioned that there was a previous allusion to using this project as a financing tool
to support its continuation and growth. He asked whether the claw backs outlined in the contract might
potentially jeopardize the project's financing.

Greg Stewart, of GT Medical Holdings, said he did not believe the claw back would jeopardize financing.

Mr. Morey stated from his perspective, their previous discussions on the development demonstrated
good faith. They had expressed concerns about the developer's commitment to their promises and were
evaluating whether penalties had been included in the contract. In some cases, there had been a lack of
foresight because they had placed trust in the developer and expected them to follow through on their
commitments. He said he saw the inclusion of penalties as a positive step, indicating that they were
thinking ahead and were prepared to enforce consequences if the developer did not fulfill their
obligations.

Chair MacKay opened public comment. With none, she closed public comment and brought the
discussion back to the council.

Chair MacKay shared her excitement for this development.

Councilor Ellsworth asked if there was any interest in continuing the item to review alternative numbers.

Councilor Shipley said he would love to see a resolution tonight, assuming the number is comfortable.

Councilor Hoban asked what number would be comfortable.

Councilor Handley said unless there is a substitute motion made by a councilor, they should vote on the
item as is.

Councilor Fillmore agreed that this item needed to be voted on.

Mr. Jones reminded the council that unless there was a change, no new motion was needed due to
there already being an implied motion on the table to vote on.

Mr. Stewart explained that the two structures under discussion represented roughly 20% of the total
project cost. He said to determine the penalty amount for not building these structures, they simply
took approximately 20% of the previously approved increment and reduced it by that amount. He added
that the decision was not based on any specific scientific formula but was a straightforward way to
account for the potential impact on the project if these buildings were not constructed.

Chair MacKay called for a vote on the implied motion.
