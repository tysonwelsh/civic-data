Healthy Orem Update Presenter: Heather Cox, Management Analyst and Debi D'Amico,
Community Connection Supervisor

Ms. Cox introduced two guests from the Utah County Health Department, Carrie Bennett
and Kate Jenkins. They are here to contribute and support the wellbeing action plan. Ms. Cox
gave a review of the process so far. The process began in March of 2024 with the Utah healthy
community designation. Last October, USU presented the results of their survey to the City
Council. Ms. Cox introduced the members of the Orem Health Coalition which includes City
staff and community members. She explained that there are four general pillars of community
and personal health. These pillars are physical environment, social and economic factors, health

D’ Amico introduced the second priority. The second priority is free health classes at City venues.
These classes will mainly be at the library and recreation center, with some classes at the senior
center. The third priority is to create an annual health fair. There are plans to have the annual

1600 North Nighttime Construction Exemption Presentation Presenter: Chris Tschirki, Public
Works Director, Dan Young, WCG Engineering and Diego Carroll, Utah Department of

Transportation (UDOT) Project Manager

Mr. Tschirki introduced Mr. Carol and Mr. Young. They are working on the 1600 North
project. Mr. Tschirki explained that UDOT will give an update on the project and they will make
a formal request of the City Council. Mr. Carrol and Mr. Dan Young reported that almost all of
the 33 homes have been purchased and some work has started on 1600 North. By April Ist,
UDOT would like to have all the funds in place to be able to bid this project. Mr. Tschirki
explained the financials for this project.

Mr. Tschirki spoke about the formal request for UDOT. He explained the City code
restricts construction work near residential zone properties from 10:30 PM to 7:00 AM. The
request is that this project is allowed to work through the night. This accelerates the project in a
safer manner. This has been done on previous projects. UDOT representatives predict that
working in the night hours will save at least a year on the completion of the project. There are
certain types of construction work that will still be restricted and those are written in the
resolution. This resolution will be a consent item at tonight's City Council meeting.

EPA Brownfields Grant Report Presenter: Matt Taylor, Senior Planner

Mr. Clark invited Mr. Taylor to come and present on the EPA grant. Mr. Taylor has been
running that program and it has now reached its completion. Mr. Taylor started with an
explanation of a few terms. EPA is the Environmental Protection Agency and they protect human
health and the environment by enforcing regulations based on laws passed by Congress.
Brownfield is a property that may be difficult to expand, redevelop, or reuse because of actual or
suspected environmental pollution. Environmental Site Assessment (ESA) is a study that
evaluates a property for pollution or environmental hazards, often before it's sold or developed.
Remediation is the process of cleaning up pollution typically in accordance with the Phase III
Environmental Assessment Remediation Plan.

Mr. Taylor spoke about what the Brownfield grant did. It provides preliminary
assessment of targeted areas, it provides phase I and phase II studies for willing property owners,
it provides asbestos and lead contaminated studies, it provides market analysis of contaminated
properties examining local economic conditions to determine the best new uses for previously
contaminated properties, and provides area-wide studies. Orem received the 2018 EPA planning
grant for Geneva Road area, the 2019 EPA brownfield grant and the 2024 grant closeout. The
grant provided Orem with qualified environmental professionals, the Brownfield Redevelopment
Solutions. Brownfield Redevelopment Solutions assisted in public outreach and education,

City Council Conflict of Interest Disclosures Presentation Presenter: Jon Ditto, Assistant City
Attorney

Mr. Ditto informed the Council that this presentation is for elected officials. He explained
that in the last legislative session, the House passed H.B. 80 related to conflict of interest
disclosure requirements. H.B. 80 requires local elected officials to annually file a conflict of
interest disclosure statement. This disclosure shall be filled with the City Recorder no sooner
than January | and no later than January 31. This disclosure must be responsive to each item
listed in Utah Code 20A-11-1604(6). Mr. Ditto gave each of the City Council members a copy of
the conflict of disclosure file. The disclosure needs to contain general information of the elected
official, employment information, any business entity ownership/interests, and some other items,
such as spouse’s employment and optional disclosures.

The City Council expressed concerns about the amount of information required for this
conflict disclosure. They recommended the City speak with the Utah League of Cities and Towns
about this new bill. Mr. Ditto explained that the City Recorder has 10 business days, once the
form is submitted to post an electronic copy on the City website, provide the link to the Lt.
governor’s office and keep the disclosure posted until the elected official leaves office. The
failure to timely report or accurately report results in the following: 1) City Recorder is required
to report any violations to the Utah Attorney General, the statute provides that any violation is a
Class B misdemeanor and 2) the City Recorder must impose a civil fine of $100 against the
elected official.

changing the mini threshold from $4.999 to $5,000, the mid-major threshold from $9.999 to

two grant sizes? The second question is how do new groups get consideration for the Major
category and how would the Council like to review the request? The City Council discussed
these questions. Mr. Nelson asked if it would be appropriate to have an applicant give their
presentation to become a major grant applicant in a work session. Mayor Young suggested that
the Strategies and Innovations team look into these questions. Mr. Bybee suggests defining the
major requirements more clearly. Mr. Nelson suggests only having two categories: a mid
category and a major category.

Emergency Preparedness and City Drill Report Presenter: Heath Stevenson, Emergency
Manager and Lon Myers, Assistant Fire Chief

Mr. Stevenson spoke about emergency preparedness and gave a report on the 2024 City
Drill. He explained that there are monthly neighborhood preparedness courses and ham radio
classes are taught each quarter. In 2025, Orem and Provo will combine their efforts for
preparedness classes. This year, the emergency management team participated in city, county and
state exercises. He presented a list of partners in the emergency preparedness team. Mr.
Stevenson spoke about Everbridge, this is a mass notification system. He encourages the
community to sign up for this notification system.

Mr. Stevenson reported that the Orem City Drill had just over 44,000 people who
participated in, were accounted for during the drill. This included residents from 17 of the 21
areas throughout the City of Orem. All reports arrived to the City EOC (Emergency Operation
Center) within 2.5 hours. Mr. Stevenson expressed his gratitude for the new EOC that is being
put into the City building.

Chief Myers wanted to recognize Mr. Stevenson and the great job he is doing in this
position. He has built a vast network of colleagues and the City is very fortunate to have him
working here. City Council members gave praise to Mr. Stevenson and the fire department for
their efforts in emergency preparedness and for being willing to send fire fighters to California to
help.

Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) Legislative Priorities Presentation Presenter:

Karson Eilers, ULCT Representative

Mr. Eilers works for the ULCT as the policy director and he has joined the meeting by
Zoom to discuss the league's legislative priorities for 2025. This year there are currently 239 bills
to be put before the legislature. The budget for this year will be really tight for the state. The
ULCT will be watching for costs that could be pushed from the state to the municipalities. One
of the big issues this year will be homelessness. The next biggest issue is public safety,
specifically related to immigration. Another issue worth mentioning has to do with sales tax.
Housing is an issue many municipalities are interested in. He recognized Councilmembers
Macdonald and Millett for serving on housing committees. He spoke about the State housing
plan that will be released in the upcoming days. Mr. Eilers spoke about external accessory
dwelling units (ADU) which have been a concern for Orem. There is a bill that would be a carte
blanche legalization for every urban city on external ADUs. The ULCT plans to push back on
this bill.

Mr. Bybee gave Mr. Eilers some of the City Council priorities. These priorities include
growth management, transportation and trail efforts, short term rentals, and the utility want
transportation fee. Ms. Millett expressed her concern with external ADU’s and she would like
these types of ADU’s to maintain the setbacks for surrounding properties/neighborhoods. Mayor
Young asked Mr. Eilers to look at the H.B. 80 bill from last year concerning conflict of interest
statements for elected officials.
