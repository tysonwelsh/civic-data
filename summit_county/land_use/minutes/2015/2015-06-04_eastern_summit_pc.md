WORK SESSION 
 
1. Status updates regarding the possible changes to the Development Code, Zoning 
Maps, and General Plan- Planning Department Staff  
 
Director Putt said it seems that everybody is becoming frustrated that they can’t get 
lines drawn on the map.  In the interest of doing this efficiently, there are a couple of 
routes that could be taken.  He recommended Staff put together a proposed map based 
on their best judgment and the recommendations of the Commission and the public.  
This will not be a final map.  A citizen’s group has presented a map that will be taken 
into consideration as well.   
 
Director Putt said each Commissioner will have homework to produce a map.  Staff will 
also produce a map and will explain the “why” behind the proposal.  He asked they do 
the same thing.  Each Commissioner should draw zoning lines on a map and briefly 
explain why they would like to see it zoned that way.  Examples of the reasoning behind 
the zoning might be that it fits the existing development pattern or the existing lot sizes.  
Director Putt asked that they give the reasoning with each zone they put on the map.   
 
Staff will bring their map and each Commissioner will bring theirs.  They will go 
through the maps together.  There will be obvious areas where they are in agreement.  
Those areas will be set aside as “mission accomplished.”  In the areas where there are 
varying perspectives, they will know the reasons why they vary.  The maps will be 
shared, along with the reasoning, with the Commission as a whole.  A decision will then 
be made concerning where the lines will be adjusted.   
 
Director Putt said when there are six or seven people drawing the map, they seem to 
fight over control of the pencil.  It is time to put the pencil down and analize the 
reasoning.  He thinks that will be more efficient.   
 
Director Putt said there are other ways they could go about this; however, he doesn’t 
recommend they go these routes.  He described the other methods.   
 
1. They could keep the map the way it is.   
2. There is the option they can develop an efficient rezoning process for people to 
adjust the map.    
3. The subcommittee could continue creating the map, but that hasn’t been as efficient 
as they thought it would be.    
4. They could take the map that was presented by the citizen’s group and discuss that 
for data.  There are areas of agreement, but that would probably short-circuit the 
Commission’s input.

Weighing all of the options, he thinks the best way to proceed is to allow Staff to 
hammer out a base map for next time.  During the next two weeks he would like each 
Commissioner to do the same thing.  It would be appreciated if they could get their 
maps to Staff in advance of the meeting.   
 
Commissioner Willoughby said a map was presented at the last meeting.  Was that the 
citizen’s map?  Director Putt said it was.  Chair Ure said since that time, there have been 
a couple of maps presented.  He gave a map with his suggestions to Administrator 
Barnes.  Another map came from the citizen’s group.  These maps are starting points to 
go forward with.   
 
Commissioner Willoughby said she would like to repeat what she heard Director Putt 
propose to be certain she understands.  He suggested that Staff will send each 
Commissioner a map to use for placing the zones where they would like to see them 
located and why.  He would like each Commissioner to have this ready for the next 
meeting.  They will take all of the maps and overlay them for the County Council.   
 
Director Putt responded there is an interim step before they take it to the Council.  He 
said Staff will produce a map.  Each Commissioner will do the same thing.  They will set 
down and discuss, as a group, where Staff’s map should be modified or where it should 
stay the same.  Along with the maps, each will outline their reasons behind the zoning.  
Beyond creating a map to take to the community and ultimately the County Council, 
they will have the explanations behind the zones.   
 
Director Putt said the map that comes out of their group discussion will then be taken 
to the public, where changes will be made.  Each time a change is made the reasoning 
will be noted.  Everybody will know the why behind the where.  Why the lines are being 
proposed where they are at.  They will also be able to go back to see the development of 
the map and the comments that got them there.  There will be a record of what was 
created and why.   
 
Commissioner Henrie said he likes this process better than the way they have been 
going about it.  At first, an idea was presented that all County Class D roads should be 
zoned AG-1.  This was to be a starting point.  That idea was not supported by any 
reasoning.  He thinks the approach that Director Putt has presented will help with the 
Commission’s and the public’s expectations.  
 
Commissioner Hanson said she thought what they have done in the past was just very 
generic with the purpose to look at all possibilities.  Chair Ure agreed with 
Commissioner Hanson.  At the same meeting, different ideas were also presented.     
 
Commissioner Vernon said what he is hearing is the same conversation they had six 
months ago.  They are going to present a map to the public.  That has been done.  They 
have received the public’s input.  They had a plan.  There were motions made.  Some of

At this time, Chair Ure invited the gentleman who wanted to speak earlier to give his 
input.  Christian Saddler said he has 100 acres on the west side of Rockport Ranches 
that he believes is in the AG-100 zone.  He is wondering what the zoning will be in the 
future.  Will it be AG-6?  He has two lots of record because the lots were divided prior to 
1976.  His property has been chopped up by roads, which makes it fairly unusable for 
building their home.  His property was pointed out on the map.   
 
Mr. Saddler said his property spans Rockport Ranches and Rockport Estates.  Mr. 
Saddler said he doesn’t want to have the kind of density that comes with one acre lots.  
Instead of one or two large estates, he would like to put more houses in this area so it 
would be affordable for people to live in Summit County. 
 
Chair Ure said that currently they are proposing the AG-80 replace the AG-100.  
Administrator Barnes said a tool Mr. Saddler could use would be the option to apply for 
one of the new zones the Commission is going to create.     
 
Administrator Barnes said a number of individuals have come to his office to talk about 
their properties.  He is taking their input and applying it to the map.  He doesn’t know 
how Mr. Saddler’s property will eventually be zoned, but the fact that he brought their 
attention to his property means it is something they will consider.  In the new Code, 
there will be the option to rezone to another zone.     
 
Commissioner Vernon said what he is hearing from Mr. Saddler is that he would like to 
have a more lucrative zoning plan than a restrictive zone.  Mr. Saddler said that is 
correct.  He would like to see the AG-6 zone applied to his property.   
 
Mr. Saddler said he thinks the AG-6 makes sense because the 100 acres have been 
chopped up by roads.  Commissioner Henrie asked how this was originally developed.  
Mr. Saddler said it was developed before 1976.  That is why he has two lots of record.

maps as well.  Commissioner Vernon said he thinks they are getting too many zones.   
 
Chair Ure said it was pointed out in a subcommittee meeting that if there is land in the 
AP-40 zone with more than 10 contiguous acres, the land is eligible for two units of 
density.  If that language is applied, property located in the AP-40 zone would have two 
units of density.  If the land was in an AG-20 zone, it would still have two units of 
density.   
 
Director Putt requested they include a 20-acre zone when they draw their maps

split would remain.  Director Putt said this would most likely take its place.

another way to feather out the density.  Commissioner Willoughby said, as has been

Commissioner Vernon said this would not make sense if it would only be used for one 
area.  It might be a good idea if it could be used throughout Eastern Summit County.  
Chair Ure said he thinks this will be very beneficial in the foothills.  He thinks it would 
benefit Marion and Henefer among other locations.     
Administrator Barnes said there are different types of maps.  Some have the current 
zoning, some without any zoning.  There are large maps that have less detail, but show 
the entirety of Eastern Summit County.  There are smaller maps with the major 
population centers.  These maps have more detail.  He said whatever type of map the 
Commissioners would like to use, he can get to them.  He personally prefers to use the 
maps that show the geography.  The map they were given tonight has everything except 
the last area they talked about.   
 
Commissioner Hanson noted there is an area on the Henefer map that has a “possible 
AG-6” designation.  She wanted to know what the citizens of Henefer said about that 
area.  Administrator Barnes said they met with the mayor and planner of Henefer.  At 
first, they loved the idea of the increased density of the AG-1 zone, but then they did an 
about-face.  Even though they would like more density, they realized they only have the 
capacity for 20 more sewer and water connections.   
 
Administrator Barnes said in the end, they decided they like the AG-1 on the east side.  
The west side has steep hills and wetlands.  They thought the AG-6 would make better 
sense there.  Commissioner Willoughby said even though the city doesn’t have sewer, 
the citizens could dig wells and have septic tanks.     
 
Commissioner Henrie said the Commission has not had any in-depth discussion on the 
rezones being proposed.  It has been said if someone doesn’t like their zoning, they can 
apply to be rezoned.  That seems like there really will be no zoning.  It gives everyone 
the opportunity to zone for 
1/3 acre lots.  They need to be careful when they create the 
rezoning criteria.     
 
Director Putt said there are two ways they can go about this.  The first is what 
Commissioner Henrie has said.  They can have very clear predictable standards.  
Another way is through the General Plan.  The General Plan can speak to the broader 
concepts of when it is appropriate to rezone.  He said they have a little bit of that 
language in there now, but it could be beefed up.  They need to keep in mind the 
General Plan is not regulatory, only advisory.  Broader criteria can be put in concerning 
the “how” and the “when” it is appropriate to rezone.   
 
Commissioner Henrie said he thinks it is time to start getting some specifics about the 
new zones and the rezoning process.  There are a lot of considerations to take into 
account.  Commissioner Vernon said there would have to be a need to rezone.  
Commissioner Willoughby asked if applications for rezoning would come to the 
Commission.  Commissioner Vernon said if the landowner meets the requirements they 
set forth, then the Commission shouldn’t have anything to say about it.

Chair Ure said the subcommittee has started working on this.  It is in Chapter 4.  
Director Putt said the zone districts and the Code are about 90% done.  They only have 
the last 10% to work on.  They just need to clean it up.  More than likely the dense 3-to-

Chair Ure said they all have homework to do.  Director Putt said they will get the maps

Commissioner Vernon said he would like to get more information about the circle that 
has been drawn around the town of Wanship.  Would it be wise to draw the whole town 
as AG-1 in order to allow for expansion?  Director Putt answered his initial reaction is 
that Wanship is an area where they would like to see it develop back into its own town 
center.  They need to decide if it is appropriate to zone it that way now or see it come 
back for a more comprehensive way of rezoning.  He thinks the latter will be the better 
choice because this may be more than just a residential zone.   
 
Director Putt said when he and Administrator Barnes drew that circle it was nothing 
more than to say there is a big opportunity to re-create a small community.  There are 
crossroads and an interstate highway.  Staff would like to receive public input 
concerning Wanship.
