Commissioner Franklin said it is his observation that the original SPA agreement 
didn’t have a definition for height.  The applicant is laying out what the height of the 
building is from sea level.  This is a responsible position to take.   
 
Commissioner Harte said this was a useful and informative field trip.  He said a key 
point is they are working on an amendment to the SPA.  The way buildings are 
measured can be changed, but they don’t have to be.  Another key element is the 
finished grade, which means that fill could be brought in resulting in a taller building.     
 
Commissioner Harte said a poignant comment made at the public hearing was from a 
woman who was a realtor.  When she purchased the unit on the seventh floor, she had 
no fears that a future building next to her with five floors would be taller than hers or 
would block her view.   Commissioner Harte said that a floor number doesn’t actually 
relate to the height of the building.  Visiting the site offered a good perspective.   
 
Commissioner Harte said there are trade-offs when measuring with elevations or 
stories.  With elevations, it is known exactly what the height of the building will be.  He 
would like to know how the Commission feels about the way a building is measured.   
 
Commissioner Cooke said he feels that defining elevation seems to make sense. He 
said there were some loosely defined regulations on height.  He questioned why the 
regulations were there to begin with?  He believes it was because the preservation of 
view shed was paramount.  He is struggling with the best way to do that.   
 
Commissioner Cooke said they are talking about amending the SPA.  In the future, 
there could be another amendment.  They have to do their best to get this right, as if 
there won’t be another amendment.

Chair Peck said graphics and the site plan has helped to answer some of her questions;

Commissioner Klingenstein said he likes having predictable information.  He said he 
asked Attorney Brackin to research the difference between design guidelines and 
design standards.  The SPA references design guidelines.  That means there was not a 
hard and fast standard about the height of the stories.  It isn’t firm that 10 feet is a story.  
People have stated they were relying on the guidelines.   
 
Commissioner Klingenstein they all know the original intent of the SPA was to be 
intense and vertical.  He noted the ground floor of Vintage on the Strand is 14 feet.  The 
second story is 12 feet.  He said it was interesting that the DRC has massaged the design 
guidelines to meet good resort principals.  He does not believe that a 10 foot story was 
ever intended to be a hard and fast number.  Having a view was never assured.  
Everybody knew that something was going to be built.   
 
Commissioner Klingenstein said the other variable that has been bantered about is 
the grade.  Are they talking about natural grade, existing grade, or final grade?  It is 
important that everybody understands they are talking about final grade.  
Commissioner Harte said final grade could lend itself to being abused.  If they decide 
to hold to the original rules, the building could still be tall.  Holding to the original rules 
doesn’t necessarily accomplish the desired goal. 
 
Chair Peck asked Commissioner Cooke if his questions were answered by the site 
visit.  Commissioner Cooke answered it was very helpful to actually stand there and 
visualize the height.  He asked which building has the highest elevation in the Resort 
Core.  He was told the Hyatt Hotel has an elevation of 7058 feet, but the Grand Summit 
is the tallest building.

Chair Peck checked with Commissioner Cooke and Commissioner Klingenstein that 
the questions they submitted had been answered.  Both Commissioners indicated their

Chair Peck said that Commissioner DeFord’s questions should be answered.  Mr. 
Raymond said they have responded to a number of his questions.  Height has been 
addressed.  He believes there is confusion with the buildings that have a mixed number 
of stories.  Only Building 17 has the designation of 5-6.  There is no interpretation of 
this anywhere in the SPA.  They used the 5 story height in determining the building’s 
height.   
 
Mr. Raymond said they could have gone much higher on RC22.  They could have put on 
a 16’ roof.  The building has been stepped to meet the slope.  They have 3, 4, 5, and 6 
stories in the building.   
 
Spencer White said the design guidelines were established in 1999-2000.  The LUZ for 
RC 17 & 18 was amended in 2009.  When that occurred, the site-specific design 
guidelines for RC 17 were not amended.  He said that Summit County Attorneys have 
told them it is the amended LUZ that supersedes the design guidelines.   
 
Mr. Raymond said they found some photos that show RC 17 has been filled over the 
years.  All of the material is going to come out to build the parking structure.  A 
discussion took place about the different areas that have had fill placed.  Some of these 
areas will need excavation to level the land.  Another discussion took place about the 
entry point for the Canyons and other roads.  Mr. Raymond said if there is an 
established road that becomes the entry for the development.         
 
The design guidelines of the Lower Village were discussed.  Mr. Raymond said instead

Commissioner Cooke said there are a number of trails within the Canyons easements 
that have loose ends.  Planner Northrup-Robinson told the Commission that they will be 
receiving an application for a project located in Lower Village.  Vail would like to 
construct a team building.  They have received comments from the Snyderville Basin 
Recreation District that they will not sign the plat until the loose ends are finalized.    
 
Attorney Brackin said there is a master trails agreement as well as the resort village 
management agreement.  The master trails agreement was signed pre-Vail.  Both 
agreements require everyone to sign the documents.  They seem to be unable to get Vail 
to sign the documents.  All other parties have signed and are deeded.  The County will 
have an opportunity to hold their feet to the fire when they come in with an application 
for Red Pine or the Lower Village.   
 
Commissioner Harte said he is concerned about Red Pine Village.  Once they lock in 
the Resort Core, options may be removed for Red Pine Village.  He is worried that by 
pushing this part of the development down the road, the options will be limited.   
 
Attorney Brackin said that while she understands his concern, she is not concerned 
taking into account what Red Pine Village is and is not.  She said that Red Pine Village 
was always intended to be the last thing built.  It is a ski-in and ski-out facility.  There 
will only be a service road that is not opened to the public.  There are two ways for 
resort guests to get there.  One is through the tram and the other is the shuttle.   
 
Commissioner Harte said there will be hotel lodging, restaurants, and condos in Red 
Pine.  He is concerned that the area Replay has designated a gathering area where the 
service road is located.  It seems that they are ignoring some of the impacts.   These 
need to be talked about.  Commissioner Klingenstein pointed out that the LUZ table 
included Red Pine.  That gives ideas of what has been talked about.  Administrator 
Barnes said Red Pine is on their radar screen.

Attorney Brackin said that Vail wants to allow cars and have parking at Red Pine.  
Commissioner Klingenstein said the minutes should express the concern of the

Administrator Barnes said the reason they are not talking about Red Pine is because
