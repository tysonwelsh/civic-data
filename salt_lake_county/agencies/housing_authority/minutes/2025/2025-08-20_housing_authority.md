DRAFT: 2026 ANNUAL PHA PLAN W/MTW SUPPLEMENT to ANNUAL PHA PLAN & AGENCY SPECIFIC WAIVER REQUEST 
and the 5-Year Plan (2026-2030) 
• COO Marni Timmerman presented the Agency’s proposed PHA Plan for 2026 and the 5 -Year PHA Plan for years 
2026-2030. She began, every five years as a housing authority, we have the expectation with HUD to put together 
a five -year plan. It's to outline our mission, our long -term goals, and strategies for serving the needs of low -
income, very low -income, and extremely low- income families in our jurisdiction over a five -year period. What 
you're seeing on the screen now is  our proposed goals for our 5 -year plan for years 2026 to 2030.  These goals 
align with our overall goals as an agency to increase affordable housing, continue acquiring affordable housing, 
and to continue to increase rental subsidies. In addition, we plan to reposition and secure long- term viability of 
184 Public Housing units using HUD’s various repositioning tools as well as focus and execute the Resident Services 
Strategic Plan. And as we want to diversify our funding, CEO Kimball added, as we transition out of public housing, 
we received slots where we could acquire new public housing or convert to a RAD project. This is 244 units and 
we want to ensure, before we transition all of our public housing, we deploy these as it’s one of the few areas that 
will allow, or programs that will really focus on the 30% AMI (Average Median Income) and below. We’re going to 
try and keep that in play as long as we can. We’re also looking into if we are in a financial position to become rated 
to be able to issue bonds 
• Commissioner Akerlow asked, for clarification, how many units would be LIHTC (Low -Income Housing Tax 
Credits)? And how many projects can you have going at one time? CEO Kimball answered, we can have four open 
projects at a time, and that really limits what we can do with each application. It’s usually between 50 -70 units 
you can build. We are open to increasing that to be aligned with Salt Lake County . COO Timmerman continued, 
for our last five-year goal, we increased affordable rental subsidies by nearly 1,000. Commissioner Johnson added, 
funding is a significant component to that. COO Timmerman answered, right. The number of vouchers awarded is 
one thing. How far the money goes that is attached to those vouchers is another. 
Commissioner Akerlow added, is another way to look at this, rather than saying we’re going to build 320 units, is 
it more we want to serve more households? And those 1,000 households could be made up of new units, new 
vouchers? There are different ways to get to the 1,000 units. Commissioner Litvack added we don’t want the goals 
to be under-reflective of the need; we want it to be evident we have a very high need. CEO Kimball summarized 
we are to come back with a goal of 1,00 units over the next five years. 
• COO Timmerman added, the other component of our annual plan is the MTW (Moving- To-Work) Supplement, 
which is the mechanism where we request waivers from HUD to execute our plan to better suit our local 
population. The first waiver we’re requesting for 2026  is an agency -specific waver, for acceptance of local 
certificate of occupancy for PBV (Project -Based Vouchers) Inspections , where we need to provide backup and 
documentation and rationale of how this will best serve our population. This would mean that at the inception of 
the project, we need to en sure the architects and contractors know all the specs and requirements so they're 
building to the NSPIRE standards; this would have saved a lot of time with New City Plaza. Our inspectors were 
going out and inspecting every unit, which is good. If HUD approves this waiver, in circumstances like that going 
forward, we would be able to use our certificate of occupancy to say all of these units pass for the initial inspection. 
And then on an annual or biannual basis, those units would be inspected as people have r eexaminations. 
Anecdotally, this would have saved the Agency significant costs, which meets a goal under the MTW. We want to 
use our funding effectively.  
The next waiver we are requesting is an alternative project-based voucher selection process; we want to be able 
to award PBVs to properties that are not owned by a PHA, but by nonprofits, for -profits, or the agency that are 
not Public Housing. We want to be able to give vouchers and be nimble and do it quickly and flexibly.  We have 
criteria outlined where we would weigh these publicly funded projects selected within the last 10 years through 
a competitive process, and they are mission-aligned properties, and need PBV support for affordability or strategic 
opportunities. This could be properties that were not competitively selected, but that serve special populations 
like seniors, homeless, disabled, or expanding our access to housing in under -resourced areas. This would still 
have to come before the board, but it would allow us to not have to go through a cumbersome application process 
to be able to award vouchers. It gives us the freedom to do it in a way meeting the community needs.

HOUSING CHOICE VOUCHERS (HCV) & ELIGIBILITY QUARTERLY REPORT 
• HCV Director Brady presented. She shared the HCV program, as of August 2025, is 92.9% leased with 3,612-unit 
months leased, and they are actively leasing up special vouchers including 52 Fostering Youth to Independence 
(FYI) vouchers and 75 Family Unification Program (FUP) vouchers. The team has successfully absorbed the  West 
Valley City Housing Authority  (WVCHA) and its vouchers in time for processing August payments,  and we are 
currently spending time addressing fin al discrepancies and organizing files for on going operations. We also 
recently pulled 500 applicants from the waiting list from the HCV program, and those packets are in process. We 
only have two waiting lists currently open, Project-Based Vouchers (PBVs) and New City Plaza, and New City Plaza 
was one we had been actively notifying, but that has slowed down as New City Plaza is nearly fully leased. 
• CEO Kimball added, HUD will transition the money from the WVCHA as needed; and to get all the files entered 
and everything involved with transferring the WVCHA data to our database; we did use a contractor for it, but it 
was a heavy lift for HCV Director Brady and her team, working with IT Administrator Slusser, to get all the data in, 
and ensure it was accurate. Our great thanks to them for this.

MONTHLY FINANCIAL REPORT 
• CFO Aniban introduced the monthly financial report . Reviewing the agency's financial position, we have Total 
Assets at $291 million, which is good for an Agency our size. The balance sheet shows a one-to-one liquidity ratio 
with $12 million in cash, though there is a $2.9 million receivable in supportive housing services due to fronting 
non-federal dollars for reimbursement-based programs. CFO Aniban ran the report as of August 19th, and it drops 
to $1.1 million, which means Housing Connect has collected around $1.8 million since June 30, 2025.  
• While the Central Office Cost Center (COCC) shows a deficit, this was anticipated in the budget  and is offset by 
Business Activities and B lended Component Units. The board also discusse d HUD's decision not to transfer $3 
million in budget authority from the absorbed West Valley City Housing Authority, which changes our projected 
reserves from $3 million to $600,000 by year -end. We will continue to monitor this situation and appeal to HUD 
if necessary.  
Further financial information can be found in the materials for today’s meeting, including the 2nd Quarter, 2025, 
RAD report. 
  
CEO REPORT 
• CEO Kimball mentioned the August Advocacy with NAHRO. 
We are gearing up for the budget and probably be starting that later this month. One of the things we are thinking 
about is staff retention and how do we recruit and maintain good staff. What the Property team has done with 
bonuses mirrors what’s done in the market, and we believe that helps.  
CEO Kimball discussed with the board concerns about potential policy changes in housing assistance, including 
work requirements and time limits for vouchers, which could negatively impact vulnerable populations. CEO 
Kimball reports the administration may implement work requirements without Congressional action as early as 
next year, while time limits might come by 2027. The discussion highlights the disconnect between wages and 
housing costs, with statistics showing minimum wage workers cannot afford even modest housing in Salt Lake 
County.

a chance to recognize Chair Bernal for his leadership and service at the National NAHRO Conference in Phoenix in 
late September.  
We also received a  2025 Community Innovation  Award of Merit  in the  Project Design category under 
Modernization/Rehabilitation for “Granger: Moderniz ing Affordable Housing Opportunit ies.” Thank you to
