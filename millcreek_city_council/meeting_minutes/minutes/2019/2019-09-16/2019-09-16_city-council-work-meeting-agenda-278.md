1.1 Purchase of Tyler Technologies “EnerGov” Software Discussion; Laurie Johnson,
HR-Finance Director
Laurie Johnson stated that the City would like to amend the contract with Tyler
Technologies by adding EnerGov Software. She said the City has reached a point that it
needs more functionality for building permitting, business licensing, and engineering
functions. Mayor Silvestrini asked if Millcreek currently uses iWorQ for those functions.
Johnson confirmed. Jim Hardy gave the Council a handout which listed what iWorQ does
not do well and what EnerGov does do well. Hardy stated that iWorQ was easy to learn
and helped the City start out but explained that iWorQ has less workflow capability than
EnerGoy. He said iWorQ is a manual system requiring staff to do more work. EnerGov is
more customizable for the City and each step of the process from application to fees to
reviews works in sequence. The scheduling with iWorQ is unusable because it lets

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 16 September 2019 Page 2 of 8

applicants schedule dates in the past and on weekends, so currently applicants email
inspection requests which in turn takes up the permit tech’s time. EnerGov is more
adaptable to city scheduling through its customization. Plan reviews are conducted
through BlueBeam and iWorQ is not compatible with that software. EnerGov has a bridge
to BlueBeam. Hardy continued stating that there is a contactor portal in EnerGov with a
password protected login unlike iWorQ where the portal is not private.

Mayor Silvestrini asked if a contactor can see all their projects in EnerGov. Hardy
answered yes, and that no one can access information without the password. Laurie
Johnson stated iWorQ is labor intensive and will free up staff time in doing less data entry.
She also stated things are less likely to get lost in the cracks. Mayor Silvestrini stated that
contractors have issues with iWorQ, and this will make all jobs easier. Hardy stated that
the building department currently sends an email manually through iWorQ, but in
EnerGov it is done automatically. Mayor Silvestrini asked about the cost of EnerGov.
Johnson stated that it will cost more than iWorQ, but it is fully integrated and will save
staff time. EnerGov will initially cost $130,000. She stated that there will a time
commitment and investment to get EnerGov working correctly. Johnson continued stating
that the yearly subscription is $95,000 a year with EnerGov compared to iWorQ at
$32,000 - $35,000. Council Member Marchant asked about the initial purchase price of
iWorQ. Johnson explained that iWorQ costed $15,000 and is small in comparison.
EnerGov is more integrated and will also talk to the financial software. Currently the
Finance Department must manually move financial records from program to program but
EnerGov will do this automatically. Council Member Marchant asked if EnerGov was the
best software. Johnson answered that for a city the size of Millcreek, this software would
be the most robust.

Council Member Marchant complimented the City Manager on hiring an effective staff.
Council Member Marchant stated that the City has saved money up to this point since it
was not needed early on. Mayor Silvestrini asked if EnerGov will manage bonds. Johnson
described that it would not track bonds, but the City will have better control because of the
cross-functionality with the financial software. Council Member Catten asked how many
employees currently use iWorQ and would use EnerGov in the future. Kurt Hansen stated
that 29 employees, three quarters of staff, use iWorQ. Johnson said she built the new
software cost into the budget amendment. Council Member Marchant asked how long the
City would use EnerGov. Johnson stated potentially a decade. Mayor Silvestrini stated he
was supportive of the software update. He said most contractors are happy with what the
Building Department is doing, but Millcreek’s software is behind the curve. Hardy stated
that the contactor portal will save the contractor time in bringing information to the
Building Department because contractors, architects, and designers all work online.
Johnson said she would create a resolution to amend the budget. Johnson stated that the
software cost of $95,000 a year is locked in for five years, so there will be no increases in
the yearly operating cost. Mayor Silvestrini asked what other programs were considered.
Johnson replied there were no others that had the capacity to meet Millcreek’s needs.
Council Member Marchant asked what cities were using EnerGov. Kurt Hansen stated that
Sandy, Draper, Ogden, and Taylorsville use EnerGov. Council Member Marchant asked
what program Salt Lake City and West Valley use. Hansen said they use Excella.

1.2 Review FY 2019-20 Budget Amendment #1; Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director

Laurie Johnson gave a copy of the budget amendment to the Council. She said the
amendment would increase the General Fund budget by $677,568. Johnson went over the
highlights of the budget amendment. She said Class C Road Fund revenue and expenses
increased due to updated information and the use of fund balance would increase to
purchase the EnerGov software. The budget was reorganized to reflect the reorganization
of city departments. The amended budget would include the new financial management
software. The General Fund Revenue increased by $250,000 on Class C Road Funds
based on what has been received this fiscal year. Promise Program donations received
showed $826 since the fiscal year. The use of General Fund Balance increased from
$326,000 to $752,000 to cover the purchase of EnerGov Software and the Unified Police
Department (UPD) contract. The City collected more than needed for the UPD contract.
Johnson said after paying $687,638, there will still be $877,000 to pay for next year’s
UPD contract.

Johnson went over the amendments with each expense budget. The Mayor and Council’s
budget did not change, and the City Manager budget had a minor change to cover
personnel costs. The Justice Court budget was increased by $210,000 to cover actual costs
and the caseload with Salt Lake County. The Legal Services budget decreased due to
moving an employee to the Legislative Services budget and increased due cover Salt Lake
County contract costs, change of $93,000. The Public Safety budget costs were estimated
at $10.4 million and the contract ended being $10.7 million. The increase covers the UPD
contract and alcohol tax grant increase. Mayor Silvestrini stated that Millcreek is sending
alcohol related fund to UPD for alcohol related enforcement. John Geilmann stated the
money is earmarked for the Millcreek precinct. The Recorder budget reduced by $65,000
due to no primary election this year.

Building Services is a new department and came out of what used to be City Services. The
Communication and Programs budget increased due to the Communication Outreach
Specialist being moved from the Human Resources budget since that employee is no
longer doing risk management. Johnson said the Emergency Management position may be
evolving due to a vacancy. The Information Center budget increased due to personnel
costs with duty changes. EnerGov is a line item and increased the IT Management budget
by $250,000. Professional IT services reduced by $25,000 and equipment costs were
reduced by $10,000. Mayor Silvestrini asked how IT professional services was reduced.
Johnson stated the Information Center Manager could do the day-to-day duties thereby
reducing the cost of the professional contract. The Economic Development budget
increased slightly under miscellaneous and personnel expenses. The Business License
Administration budget increased slightly due to salaries and personnel, and the fee study
was moved to this fiscal year from last year. The Finance Department budget experienced
no change. The Human Resources budget experienced a change with an employee moving
to a different department. The Workers Comp Insurance was increased to cover costs.
Legislative Policy is a new department but still covers some of the previous city services.
The Salt Lake County Parks contract was moved to the Public Works budget. No change
was made to the Facilities or Fleet budget. The Planning and Zoning Department was
renamed from Community Development. The Promise Program donations were added to
that budget by $826; the total Promise Program budget is $100,000 plus the donations.
The Public Works budget increased by $16,000.

1.3 Review “Tax Exempt Bond Continuing Disclosure Requirements” Document; Laurie
Johnson, HR- Finance Director
Mayor Silvestrini stated that the City must disclose certain information as an issuer of
bonds. The obligation to disclose bonds is ongoing, and part of disclosure is disclosing
that the City is not part of ongoing or aware of major litigation. Mayor Silvestrini said that
the bond council states that the City should confer with them concerning new disclosures
that can affect revenues and other major changes. The investment market would be more
comfortable if the City had a set policy to set compliance disclosure procedures. The
disclosure procedures are uniform and there are people trained throughout the City who
will be responsible for these requirements. The bond council and financial advisory firm
have given their input on the best practices for disclosure. The document has been
reviewed by the City Attorney and Laurie Johnson. Mayor Silvestrini said that once the
policy has been adopted, it will apply to any future and current bonds. He said Johnson
has been reviewing the procedures for disclosure of bonds. City Attorney John Brems will
prepare a resolution to bring before the City Council. Mayor Silvestrini stated there is
room for collaboration among the City Council on the disclosure agreement and the
requirement for disclosure is not on the shoulders of one person; it is a check and balance.
This will help the City with future bond issuance if the City has the bond disclosure
procedure. Johnson explained there is a yearly checklist to verify.

Council Member Marchant asked if disclosure would be an annual event. Johnson
explained it would be a yearly report. Mayor Silvestrini stated if an event arises, the event
has to be reported right away; for example, an economic downturn in tax revenue, or other
material changes like a lawsuit. Council Member Marchant asked if there was an expense
regarding filing. Johnson stated no. Mayor Silvestrini stated that disclosures are filed
electronically. Johnson stated that she has done the filing before. Brems explained that city

1.4 State of the Promise Program; Francis Lilly, Planning and Zoning Director
Mayor Silvestrini greeted the members of the audience who were there to talk about the
Eagle Project they did with the Promise Program. Francis Lilly turned the time to Ashley
Cleveland to discuss the state and progress of the Promise Program. Cleveland explained
the promises to Millcreek residents as education, health and safety, and economic well-
being. The Promise Program has been working with the United Way on the Promise
Committee and five other subcommittees; Health, Safety, Education, Economic
Wellbeing, and Promise Youth. Cleveland explained the community goals and targets.
Promise has a goal for all Millcreek students to work at or above their grade-level. There
is a suicide prevention goal in zip codes 84109 and 84124. In 84106, the goals for
economic wellbeing is to have all Millcreek families being financially capable to provide
for their families. Cleveland is looking for a second chair for the Safety Subcommittee.
The target area for the Safety Committee is in zip codes 84106, 84107, 84123 and partially
84124 to have access to resources and opportunities to keep families safe and improve
quality of life. The Promise Youth Committee is doing well with chairs of Yessi Timoteo
and Jannica Davis. The Business Council-Promise Support Committee is managed by
Nicole Chidester and chaired by Carrie Coppola. The Millcreek Youth Council is chaired
by Jordan Hatch and Cheri Jackson with the Youth Mayor as Arushi Verma. The
Millcreek Youth Council is targeting Millcreek at large with no focus on a specific area
other than volunteerism.

The Promise Leadership Committee has 35 individuals who have grown and advised over
the first year and now wish to stabilize, advise, and seek interventions. The Education
Subcommittee has 12 members and they wish to help the Read With Kids! Volunteer
Program. They have 25 consistent volunteers and want to get 25 more. They are working
with getting training in the Iready! Volunteer Program. The Safety Subcommittee has 8
members and their goals are safe routes and where bus routes will go for Millcreek
Elementary. Roosevelt Elementary is relocating and Mill Creek Elementary will be
absorbing 10-20% of its students. Council Member Marchant asked about the timeline on
Roosevelt school moving. Francis Lilly stated the new school is under construction and
may be ready in 2021. Mayor Silvestrini said he has been in discussion with Granite
School District about potentially leasing the property to the City if the City finds a public
use for it. Council Member Marchant asked about the Youthlinc program. Mayor
Silvestrini stated Youthlinc has a new location in Murray and Millcreek keeps in touch.
Cleveland stated the City still has a good relationship with Youthlinc. Council Member
Marchant noted he was at a breakfast on Labor Day and Roosevelt was recruiting students.
Mayor Silvestrini explained it was for the dual immersion program. Cleveland said the
Economic Wellbeing Subcommittee will focus on wages, affordable housing, job training,
and ESL education. She said they raised $4,000 for small libraries. Mayor Silvestrini

and Towns conference and it passed. Mayor Silvestrini acknowledged the Governor’s
proclamation on anti-idling and would like John Brems to draft an anti-idling ordinance
for the City. Council Member Uipi asked about idling enforcement and what it looks like.

Mayor Silvestrini stated enforcement is difficult due to exceptions in the State legislation,
but UPD would enforce the ordinance. He said there would be exceptions to idling such as
during low and high temperatures as well as having children and elderly people in the
vehicle. Mayor Silvestrini stated the ordinance is cautionary and education will be more
effective than enforcement. Kurt Hansen asked which cities have similar ordinances.
Mayor Silvestrini stated that Sandy and Draper, as well as eight other cities in Utah had
them and Draper just recently enacted their ordinance on idling.

Mayor Silvestrini informed the Council that the following evening at Churchill Junior
High there would be a presentation on the new FEMA floodplain maps. Mayor Silvestrini
stated it was unclear if the City must enforce the floodplain before the new flood map is
enacted. He also noted that the maps will determine if homes built in that area will be
allowed to have basements due to flooding concerns. Council Member Uipi asked if
residents are willing to build above the flood area. Mayor Silvestrini said the floodplain
designation makes it hard for people to build due to the Residential Compatibility Overlay
Zone (RCOZ) height restrictions. John Miller stated that staff has made a request to the
State for a written opinion on how to interpret the federal code. Miller is in the process of
getting a written statement on which map to use. Jim Hardy explained that some families
have applied for permits and need an answer on how to develop their property. Rita Lund
said she FEMA will be at the meeting to clarify the requirements.

Rita Lund told the Council that the Emergency Manager quit. She said that Unified Fire
Authority was currently working on contracts with Holladay, Midvale, and some of the
metro townships to staff emergency managers in those jurisdictions. The United Fire
Authority (UFA) would send an emergency manager to the City to fulfill tasks set by the
City. Lund and Johnson think one day a week for the UFA emergency manager would
work well. The cost is $18,000 annually for the one day a week schedule. Council
Member Uipi asked if Millcreek would be paying proportionally for its size. Mayor
Silvestrini stated yes. The City would pay the UFA to use the emergency manager and this
person should have better training and emergency management education. Mayor
Silvestrini explained the downside is the person would not always be available. Mayor
Silvestrini stated that Millcreek having its own emergency manager is more responsive,
yet the contract basis is more affordable. He continued by saying that someone local
working on the CERT organization was also a good idea. Council Member Uipi stated that
finding a good fit for the position meant there would need to be clear expectations for who
is hired. Lund stated that the job description for the UFA emergency manager has been
sent out and they are interviewing. Council Member Uipi is favorable towards using the
UFA manager. Council Member Catten and Mayor Silvestrini wanted to know what the
City would be signing on for and what the job requirements would be for the new
emergency manager. Lund stated that if the City hires an individual for communications,
instead of emergency management, that person could also help with committees like the
Arts Council and Youth Council. Council Member Catten stated it seems like the City
needed a communication person that can help with CERT. Rita Lund noted that the
previous employee was doing a lot of IT work and website work as a catch-all. John
Geilmann stated the City needs the right hire for the position since the City is capped at 38
staff members.
