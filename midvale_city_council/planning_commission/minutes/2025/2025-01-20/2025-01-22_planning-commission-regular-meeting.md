REGULAR MEETING

Chair Erickson called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. She
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

   1. Joshua Smith requests Conditional Use Permit approval for a 224-unit townhome
      project located at 7309 S 180 W in the Transit Oriented Development (TOD) zone.

      conditional use permit in the TOD zone.

      use permit as follows (Staff responses in bold):

      law;

      2. The structures associated with the use are compatible with surrounding structures in

      Response: Yes, see attached plans.
      3. The use is not detrimental to the public health, safety and welfare;
      Response: Correct.
      4. The use is consistent with the general plan, as amended;
      Response: Yes.

DISCUSSION:

Commissioner Liedke asks how many extra parking spaces will be provided in this development.

Elizabeth Arnold, Senior Planner, responds that nearly 100 additional parking spots were provided
in the approved site plan. The requirement was 336 parking stalls, and the developer provided 448
stalls.

Commissioner Tippetts inquires about the type of units there will be, rental versus for sale, and the
cost of the units.

Mr. Smith replies that all units will be only rentals, no low income or federal assistance will be
accepted. However, with the square footage, these will be more affordable than commonly priced
rental townhomes.

Commissioner Tippetts questions the location of the entrance and exit for the community. As it
appears on the map, California Avenue would not have any additional traffic impact as the
entrance/exit is located elsewhere. Commissioner Tippetts clarifies with the developer that the plan
is to tie it to a different sewage connection.

Mr. Smith confirms that this is the resubmittal of the plans and that the developers have worked
with Midvale Engineering to ensure that the new connection will be taken care of.

Commissioner Liedtke thanks the developer for the additional parking. He then asks whether or
not the amenities will be available to the residents immediately or if there are many phases that
will affect the quality of life for the first phase of residents.

Mr. Smith explains the different setups of each unit, ultimately stating that all units have a two-car
garage, either side by side or tandem. The units are going to be professionally managed and secure
as they will be a fully gated community.

Commissioner Edwards inquires about the construction timeline.

Mr. Smith states that the goal is 26 months in total.

Commissioner Liedtke questions about the route for the entrance and exit.

Ms. Arnold clarifies that it does go through the Remington Apartment Complex and has access to
State Street.

Chair Erickson comments on the location and ideal type of development in the area. The proximity
to transit will be perfect for the high-density project.

Commissioner Liedtke would like clarification on the ownership of the plot and the State’s
intention of the property prior to this development proposal.

Wendelin Knobloch, Planning Director, states that this lot was not considered for a Homeless
Shelter and that it is privately owned.

Vice Chair Anderson voices her dislike for the exterior shape and architectural design. The
proportions are all the same and it appears terribly similar throughout the community.

Dan Teed, the project architect, explains the approach for the balance of density, price, and
available rooms per unit created in an affordable way. These units are unique for the limited space
but have a homey, community feel. The variety makes it less cookie-cutter and has more
personality. This is supposed to be a middle ground between apartments and single-family homes.

Chair Erickson approves of the small green spaces all through the project and the different exterior
designs to create a more appealing image in the community.

Commissioner Erickson voices his concern about the possible doubled increase in density
throughout the egress of Remington Apartments. Acknowledging that staff have examined this, he
did want to mention the possible traffic congestion.

Commissioner Tippetts voices that this project fits the area and the intended Master Plan.

Daniel Van Beuge, Deputy City Attorney, states that this is an administrative decision that is up
to the Commission. The project must be approved if all requirements are met.
