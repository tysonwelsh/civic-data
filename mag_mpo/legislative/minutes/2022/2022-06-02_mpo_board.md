Public Comment (7:45) None. Station Area Planning – LaNiece Davenport, MAG (8:08) HB462 (Housing Affordability Amendments, Rep. Waldrip) requires cities with fixed rail transit stations to adopt station area plans for those stations and update general plan and zoning to implement the station area plan. MAG, in partnership with the Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC), Utah Transit Authority (UTA), and the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity (GOEO), can provide technical assistance to help communities complete or update their SAPs. SAP provisions have an effective date of June 1, 2022. The first application deadline for these technical assistance funds is July 15, 2022. Overview Bipartisan Infrastructure Law - Jennifer Elsken, FHWA (28:42) On November 15, 2021, President Biden signed the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA), also known as the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law, into law. This is the largest long-term investment in our infrastructure and economy in our Nation’s history. It provides $550 billion over fiscal years 2022 through 2026 in new Federal investment in roads, bridges, transit, as well as water infrastructure, resilience, and broadband. FHWA provided an overview of the highlights of BIL, new provisions, and upcoming funding opportunities. TIP Project Priority List – Bob Allen, MAG (42:50) MAG selects projects every two years to be funded using certain Federal, State, and County funds. There are approximately $100m in funds available this cycle. Most of these funds are available in 2025 and 2026. Projects are scored and ranked through an adopted selection process. TAC visited each project via a bus and virtual tour. MAG staff has scored each project based on approved criteria. TAC scored each project in the prioritization meeting. These scores were combined with the MAG staff score to create an initial prioritized list. The TAC score and staff score each constituted 50% of the final score. TAC recommends the prioritized list be adopted by the MPO Regional Planning Committee. Funding and final adoption into the TIP will occur in August. Mayor Kaufusi moved to approve the Project Prioritization List as presented. Mayor Andersen seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Federal performance Measures-Statewide Target Adjustments – Bob Allen, MAG (1:00:45) MPO Regional Planning Committee June 2, 2022 Page 5 Federal Transportation law requires all States and MPOs to set targets for and track specific transportation performance measures. In 2018, UDOT set and MAG ratified, targets for safety, infrastructure, and mobility. Incrementally, the State and MPOs can change those targets. UDOT would like to make minor adjustments to the previously set targets. MAG must adopt those new targets or determine its own targets and how it will achieve them. Mayor Olson moved to adopt the adjusted statewide targets for the required federal performance measures as presented. Mayor Larson seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. FY23 Unified Planning Work Program Approval – Shawn Eliot, MAG (1:32:30) The Unified Planning Work Program (Work Program) outlines the work that MAG, UDOT, and UTA will perform within the Mountainland MPO planning area. The Work Program is updated annually and contains budget for MPO programs and staff. The FY23 Work Program has a total budget of $3.52m, $1.76m for staff and admin costs and $1.76m for studies, programs, and contracts. Revenue for this $3.52m budget is approximately 40% federal funds while 60% is state and local funds form UTA, UDOT, MAG state funds, and local governments. Staff costs are down this year by $442k due to a restructuring in the Regional Planning Department allocating more staff time for rural planning outside the MPO. Studies and contracts are up $1.11m due to larger regional studies, MAG staff managing local studies funded through the TIP selection process, and the creation of a new grant program called the Transportation to Land Use Connection (TLC). Mayor Andersen moved to approve the FY23 MAG Unified Planning and Work Program as presented. Mayor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
