Mahogany Ridge Development Parking Issues

                Council Member Barney stated the Council has had complaints from residents in
the Gabler’s Grove community around parking regarding obstructing traffic and safety issues for
pedestrians. The Council has addressed its own code setbacks on residences to 25 feet minimum
in front to allow for full sized vehicles to park in the driveway without obstructing the sidewalk.
However, the setback in the Gabler’s Grove and Mahogany Ridge development agreements is
22 feet, and that is too short for a full-sized vehicle. The concern with that is when a resident
parks their car in their driveway and obstructs the sidewalk, it pushes pedestrians onto the street.
Another issue in Gabler’s Grove are the narrow streets, which are not wide enough to
accommodate two cars passing in the street when there are cars parked on both sides of the
street. In the Mahogany Ridge development agreement, street widths are slated at 25 feet. The
average width of a car is six feet, so when there are four cars on the street, there are only three
remaining inches between them. He understood the value in having narrower neighborhood
streets because they slow traffic, but there had to be a balance. Most people have multiple

Redevelopment of Magna Mantle Park and Ownership of all Magna Parks

Magna Mantle Park

                Adrian West, Municipal Economic Development Manager, Salt Lake County’s
Economic Development Division, stated the redevelopment of Magna Mantle Park has been a
collaborative effort between the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD), and Salt
Lake County’s Economic Development Division and its Redevelopment Agency (RDA). The
Magna Mantle Park borders the Empress Theatre, which has complained about a section of the
grass being used as a place for dogs to relieve themselves, and that waste gets tracked onto the
sidewalk and into that building. Matt Starley, Long-Range Planner, MSD, and students from Utah
State University have put together different renderings of what the redevelopment could look like
to solve this problem. The County and the MSD are hoping to get the project done in a couple of
months. They are in the material selection phase now, i.e. brick and furniture selection. They will

              Matt Starley, Long-Range Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(MSD), delivered a PowerPoint presentation on the Magna Historic District’s Magna Mantle Park
Design Project, reviewing the project location, resources, and partners; project challenges; project

Ownership of Magna Parks

               Mr. Paul Ashton stated currently, Salt Lake County owns all the parks in the
Magna Metro Township; however, it has turned two parks to the Redevelopment Agency – one
on 9033 West and another on 9045 West. The County would like to formally transfer other parks
to the Magna Metro Township, which should have been done when the Magna Metro Township
was created. The agreements deeding these parks to Magna have a provision in them that say
the parks must remain as parks, otherwise, the property would revert back to the County. Once
the parks are transferred to the Magna Metro Township, it will have liability and have to maintain
the parks.

               Council Member Prokopis asked if language could be added to the agreements
stating the parks must be in proper order when the County transfers them.

              Council Member Hull stated the contracts say the Magna Metro Township is
taking them as is.

               Brian Tucker, Planning Manager, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(MSD) stated the MSD received an application asking it to vacate an alley in the Magna Metro

or using the alleys as parking, particularly those alleys near Main Street. That could be part of
the conversation of how to revitalize Main Street.

different. The parcels along Magna Main Street could have great utility. He had talked to Salt
Lake County’s Economic Development Division about cleaning some of those up and getting

Capital Projects and Assigning Designee to Smart Sheet

Capital Projects

                Steven Kuhlmeier, Public Works Engineering Project Manager, Salt Lake
County’s Public Works Engineering Division, stated Granite School District contacted the County
about the absence of sidewalks at the rail crossing on 8400 West by the Little Valley Gateway
development. This has created a gap in Granite School District’s safe route to school for students.
There are a lot of new students in Elk Run Elementary School, and it is expected a lot of new
students will enroll in the new high school as well coming from Little Valley Gateway. Granite
School District is asking the County to connect the sidewalk across the rail crossing. It is a priority
project for the district.

Union Pacific has a right of way there and it considers any improvement to be a widening of a rail
crossing, and it fights very hard against widening any at grade crossings because they are the
highest risk locations of derailments. All rail crossings are controlled by the State, so the County
is working with it on options. It is a long process to get Union Pacific to the table on options and
there is typically a cost for that, so if the County does get Union Pacific to come to the table, it
may need some funding. If Union Pacific does not approve the sidewalk, the Magna Metro
Township may need to consider a bridge or a tunnel.

               Council Member Hull stated sidewalks will be needed on both sides of that rail
crossing with the number of new students coming from new developments.

              Council Member Barney stated it might be easier to do a tunnel or bridge rather
than spending time, effort, and money trying to get sidewalks.

               Paul Ashton stated the Council could have the County start looking into
constructing sidewalks on the other side of the rail crossing.

              Rori Andreason stated Ben Horsley, Chief of Staff, Granite School District, will be
at the next meeting to talk about capital improvement projects, so the Council could discuss this
then.

2023 Municipal Municipal Election Process and Agreement with Salt Lake County for Election
Services

               Rori Andreason reviewed the 2023 election costs for the Magna Metro Township.
This year, the County is going to invoice it for costs, rather than an amount not to exceed as it
has done in prior years. The Magna Metro Township will have two options – a traditional election
with a primary, which will cost $16,823, or ranked choice voting, which will cost $10,935 for the
general election plus the ranked choice voting cost that is dependent on the number of entities
doing ranked choice voting. The more entities choosing ranked choice voting, the lower the cost
because a ranked choice voting machine costs $25,000 to rent, and that cost is broken up, as is
the administrative cost. The Council needs to make a decision on which option it wants to do by
the end of April.

                 Council Member Pierce stated candidates have to file in the next month and a
half for a traditional election, so it might be easier to accommodate candidates by doing ranked
choice voting.

              Ms. Andreason stated candidate filling for a traditional election is June 1-7, 2023,
and for ranked choice voting, it is August 8-15, 2023. She will put the resolution authorizing the
agreement with the County on the March 28, 2023, meeting agenda.

Change of Form of Government Process

                Rori Andreason stated once the Council approves a resolution to move forward
with changing the form of government, it has forty-five days from that date to hold two public
hearings, so if the Council adopted the resolution May 23rd, it would need to hold two public
hearings in June. After that, it has 60 days to let the County know whether it will need to have
that on the ballot. The deadline for that is September 1st, so the Council could move off a decision
on the resolution until June. It is important to strategize the timing of that to allow time to educate
the public and make sure they understand that prior to the election, but close enough to the
election date, so the information is still fresh on their minds.

               Mayor Peay stated he wanted to do the resolution in May to ensure there was
ample time.

              Council Member Barney stated that would give the Council July through October
to educate the public. The Council will have to justify and explain to the voters why this is the
right move. Then, the change of government would be on the November election, and the election
of a mayor on a future election.
