Study Session

1.0   Presentation: Convention Tourism Assessment Area (CTAA)

1.1   Clay Partain, Executive Director of Sports Salt Lake (under the umbrella of Visit
      Salt Lake), gave a presentation regarding creation and potential benefits of a
      Convention Tourism Assessment Area in Salt Lake County, and encouraged
      the Council to vote in favor of participation.

2.0   Report: Annual Fire Report

2.1   Fire Chief Clint Smith presented a 2022 Annual Fire Department Report, and
      answered questions from the Council.

3.0   Report: Traffic

3.1   Chief of Police Rich Ferguson provided a traffic report for the month of March
      2023.

4.0   Update: Draper Days

4.1   Kellie Challburg and David Wilks updated the Council regarding plans for
      Draper Days. Assistant City Manager Bret Millburn reported on donations
      received.


Draper City Council Approved Minutes – April 18, 2023
5.0   Update: Trails Plan

5.1   Staff requested feedback from the Council regarding a proposed Trails Plan
      that would include a list of all existing and proposed trails. Parks and
      Recreation Director Rhett Ogden emphasized a desire to establish a
      framework with the City Council for future trail development.

5.2   Suggestions were made to: include statistics regarding trail usage; reach out
      to specific property owners regarding potential land acquisition for trail
      access; mitigate user conflict when possible; identify the values for which the
      City was aiming; create a plan for Corner Canyon; and include the Parks and
      Trails Committee in the process.

6.0   Council/Manager Reports

6.1   No reports

Business Session

1.0   Call to Order: Mayor Pro Tempore Cal Roberts

2.0   Thought/Prayer and Pledge of Allegiance

2.1   Carolynn Jennings from the Corner Canyon 4th Ward, Corner Canyon Stake,
      offered a prayer. Network Administrator Jake Sorensen led the Pledge of
      Allegiance.

3.0   Proclamation: Child Abuse Prevention Month

3.1   Mayor Pro Tempore Roberts read aloud a proclamation recognizing April 2023
      as Child Abuse Prevention Month.

3.2   Anna Roberts with the Family Support Center thanked the Council for taking a
      stand against child abuse, and spoke of the mission of the Family Support
      Center.

4.0   Public Comments

4.1   David Shurtliff, Draper resident, referred to the City’s mission statement and
      said in recent weeks it had begun to feel like Draper City versus Draper
      citizens, with neighbors helping neighbors with flooding, but elected officials
      and City staff hiding from responsibility.

4.2   Paul McCarty, Draper resident, spoke of a request for insulation and
      connection of the Willow Springs Canal to the East Jordan Canal, and request
      for sandbags and sand in that area, spoke of City responsibility, and expressed
      a desire to work together with Draper City to be proactive rather than reactive.

4.3   Ron Smith, Draper resident, spoke of past experience as the Draper City
      Engineer, flooding in 1983 and 1984, and his understanding that all canals in
      Draper would be connected to the East Jordan Canal. He asked why the Willow
      Springs Canal was not built with a connection to East Jordan Canal.

4.4   Curtis Carter, Draper resident, read aloud a request from residents of Willow
      Springs Lane and adjacent neighborhoods for the City Council to authorize an
      emergency connection of Willow Springs Canal to the East Jordan Canal to
      prevent imminent flooding of homes along Willow Creek.

4.5   Jeff Johns, Draper resident, said he appreciated City efforts when his home
      flooded in February for the first time in his 28 years of residence, said he was
      doing the best he could to prevent further flooding but was scared of what
      was coming, and asked the City to please help.

4.6   Ben Bringhurst, Draper resident, said this was the first year his home flooded
      in his 28 years of residence, spoke of past experience with flooding, said he
      had learned to never underestimate the power of water, and spoke of work
      that needed to be done to keep water in the creek bed and prevent flooding.

4.7   John Watson, Draper resident, spoke of the importance of planning for the
      future and mitigating issues, referred to a water table graph, and spoke of the
      need to plan for potential spring flooding as record snow levels melted.

4.8   Rob Urry, Draper resident, spoke of flooding in 1983, and requested the City
      take necessary measures and provide sandbags to prevent property damage.

4.9   Mike Mainzer, Draper resident, spoke of property damage from ground water,
      referred to the City’s message that HOAs were supposed to take care of

       drainage issues, said his property was not in an HOA, and said he was not
       aware of the un-mapped in-ground drainage running through an easement on
       his property when he purchased. Mr. Mainzer said the problem went beyond
       the capability of HOAs, and the City was needed to step in for a long-term
       solution.

4.10   Dave Davis, Draper resident, said he was an attorney, said his backyard was
       adjacent to Willow Springs Canal, spoke of liability, referred to the many pleas
       for help from the City, spoke of needed improvements to infrastructure
       regardless of the precedent that might be set, and said he knew the people
       would come together to help.

4.11   Devin Stoker, Draper resident, said his property had already experienced
       significant flooding from ground water, and asked the Council for public relief
       and help.

4.12   Craig Vielstich, Draper resident, expressed concern about collecting debris,
       and asked for City help and support.

4.13   Aubrey McMillan, Draper resident, spoke of several issues on her property that
       caused health problems, commented that someone at the City approved the
       way her home was originally built, and said the ground water problem was
       never disclosed to her by the City.

4.14   Louise Popp, Draper resident, said many of her neighbors’ properties had
       flooded, said no one knew where the culvert running through their
       neighborhood was, said she and her neighbors were willing to help fund
       efforts to find the culvert, and asked for City help.

4.15   Elizabeth Bringhurst, Draper resident, thanked the Council for their service,
       said her home already flooded that year along with at least 125 other homes
       in the area, spoke of the cost to repair and replace, and spoke of a desire to
       know if the City had a plan in place.

4.16   City Manager David Dobbins described current City efforts for flood
       prevention, and spoke of communication with Salt Lake County regarding
       County plans.
