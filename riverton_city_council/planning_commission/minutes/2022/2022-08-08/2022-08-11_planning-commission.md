      A.     “ANNA’S LET’S PLAY MUSIC STUDIO,” A HOME OCCUPATION
             OFFERING MUSIC AND MOVEMENT LESSONS TO CHILDREN AT 13594
             SOUTH FIRETIP COURT. APPLICANT – ANNA COLES.

Chair Pro Tem Rushton opened the public hearing. City Planner, Lisa Halverson presented
the staff report and stated that the applicant plans to offer music and movement classes to
children aged four through six. She plans to hold classes on Tuesday afternoons at
4:15 p.m. and 5:15 p.m. Each class will have five to seven students. The request was
before the Planning Commission because bi-monthly the parents are invited to the classes,
which results in more parking being needed during those times. The applicant has room for
four cars in her driveway and would need permission for three cars to be parked on the
street for two hours every two weeks.

One comment was received from a neighbor who was concerned about excessive noise if
there are several children playing in the backyard at the same time. He was also concerned
about the parking and trash. It was noted that the home is located on a court.

The applicant had two proposals for parents dropping their children off. One would be to
drive into the court and drop them off in front of the house. The second was to drop them
off at the sidewalk before entering the court, which would be next to the house. She was
amenable to either option.

Chair Pro Tem Rushton asked if the applicant would provide the days and times that the

       C.     HOME OCCUPATION – “DG TREE LLC,” A TREE SERVICE COMPANY TO
              BE LOCATED AT 12403 S. REDWOOD ROAD. APPLICANT – DANIEL
              GILSTER.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above application
originally came before the Planning Commission on June 9, 2022, at which time the
applicant testified that he did not live in the home but had renters there. He purchased the
property intending to run the business out of the back of the property while keeping tenants
in the home. After speaking with him at the meeting, the Commission wanted to give the
applicant time to bring the property into compliance with the fixed standards and
qualifications for a home occupation, and the matter was continued. No additional notices
were sent.

The applicant has four employees and several work trucks that he parks on the property.
Mr. Prestwich presented photos from the home inspection as well as those provided by the
applicant. The property is zoned RR-22 and the General Plan calls for it to be higher
residential density rather than commercial.

There are fixed standards in place that the City cannot vary from. Those in question included
number one which specifies that a bona fide resident may be in the home 185 days per year.
Number 3 indicates that the home occupation must be an accessory use on the property
and incidental to the primary use of the dwelling unit. It was noted that there is no threshold
to make such a determination. As a result, the Commission would have to exercise
judgment with regard to what is secondary and incidental. Number 4 states that an
allowance for more than one employee is allowed per Staff/Commission approval. The
conditions are that the vehicles should be screened and out of view to preserve the
residential integrity of the property and shall not displace any required front yard
landscaping. Number 10 specifies that the outdoor storage space shall not involve the use
of any unscreened or unenclosed yard space. Outdoor space can be used if it is approved
by the Planning Commission but the area must be screened.

At the last meeting, the zoning was discussed with regard to what was allowed in the zone.
The current zoning is RR-22 and has probably not changed since the City was incorporated.
The General Plan, which was updated by the City in 2020, calls for the area to be medium
to high-density, which would allow five to eight dwelling units per acre. A Historic
Preservation Overlay would also be included with the General Plan designation. The intent
was to increase the density slightly while preserving the historic character of the area.
Mr. Prestwich presented a diagram of the General Plan.

The zoning and table of uses was discussed. Mr. Prestwich reported that some uses are
allowed that may apply to this application. Landscaping Services are permitted in the
Commercial Regional Zone but not in the Commercial Gateway zone. Construction Services
were similar. The City anticipated that additional development would come after changing
the General Plan in 2020 and there are applications currently in the process in the area.

Mr. Prestwich addressed the sample motions included in the packet including a motion to
deny in the event the applicant has not complied with the standards or if the Commission
finds that any of the conditions are on the prohibited list. Mr. Prestwich explained that the
City is looking for ways to add more conditions to address potential impacts and protect the
applicant and the City. Per City ordinance, if a home occupation is denied due to non-
compliance an applicant cannot apply again for one year. Mr. Prestwich asked that the
Commission be cautious when choosing to deny an application.

If the Commission were to table the application, it would be put on hold until the applicant is
ready to proceed. At that time the City would send out new public notices. In this case,
since the matter has already been continued, if the Commission is not ready to approve it,
tabling it would be the next best option. The applicant indicated that he could come into
compliance within the next two weeks, however, tabling it would give him additional time.

City Attorney, Ryan Carter reported that the applicant has evicted the tenants and it does
appear that he is trying to comply with the conditions. He noted that it is not uncommon for
Code Enforcement to allow additional time for applicants to come into compliance if they can
show material progress. Because the applicant has shown that that is the case, he agreed
that tabling the matter for at least one month would be the best course of action.

Commissioner Park asked for clarification on the allowed uses. Mr. Prestwich explained
that the permitted home occupation uses differ from the commercial uses. The Table of
Uses are specific to commercial uses. The prohibited home occupation uses were listed
elsewhere in the ordinance. After reviewing the list, he determined that there is nothing that
is expressly prohibited that would be similar to this type of use.

Commissioner Gilchrist asked if moving to deny due to non-compliance would preclude the
applicant from coming back in the future to reapply. Mr. Prestwich clarified that it is
prohibitive in that they would not be able to apply again for one year. Because the applicant
is trying to come into compliance, tabling the matter would be the better option.

Mr. Carter explained that denial would force an appeal on the applicant’s part, which he
would be entitled to. If the applicant were to come into compliance with an appeal pending,
it would be a moot point to maintain the decision of denial.

              BASED OUT OF A HOME AT 13752 SOUTH 4170 WEST. APPLICANT-

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the matter was also on the agenda
and heard at the June 19, 2022 meeting. It was continued to the meeting in July but was
continued further to this meeting due to a conflict on the part of the applicant. He described
the location of the property at 13752 South 4170 West in the RR-22 zone which allows one-
half acre lots. The applicant has a three-car garage with an extra wide driveway. The
backyard is fenced, which is important with short-term rentals. Mr. Prestwich presented
photos of the property and the surrounding area. There were concerns expressed by
neighbors regarding on-street parking and the applicant indicated that most of the cars on
the street do not belong to her.

The City Council adopted an ordinance for evaluating and operating short-term rentals.
There are 10 steps including proof of ownership, proof of off-street parking, and the business
licensing process. There are also rules for short-term rentals including no more than eight
renters, no more than one rental contract at a time, providing off-street parking, and the
property must be maintained according to Riverton City ordinance. Conditions were added
to include maintenance of the property according to the Riverton City ordinance and to let
the City know how and where the applicant is advertising the property.

Mr. Carter reported that condition number 5 should be a standard condition for any VRBO
going forward. This one is unique because there was business activity prior to a business
license application. As a result, condition number 4 was added. There was also a concern
with respect to the maximum number of days a property can be used for short-term rentals,
which is difficult to enforce. Staff has looked at that and there may be a solution in the
private sector where they can determine how many days during the year a property is
reserved. The City would have to subscribe to that service.

Commissioner Park asked if the term “regularly “ in condition number 5 can be defined.
Mr. Carter responded that they use “regularly” because it is unknown how often updates will
occur. He stated that perhaps at least a quarterly report could be provided. Applications for
Business Licenses are annual and a semi-annual report may be reasonable.

recalled from the previous discussion that there was a concern about parties taking place
on the premises. He asked if there were limits on the number of visitors the renters can
have. Mr. Carter stated that the Code specifies no more than eight renters so guests of
renters equate to more than eight people.

Discussion continued regarding the language in the ordinance and the maximum number of

Riverton Ranch Estates Subdivision under a Development Agreement. One of the elements
is different from a typical application in that it is treated the same as a rezone. The final land
use authority is the City Council. Therefore, the action would be a recommendation to the
City Council who will make the final decision.

Mr. Carter reported that the Development Agreement states that they will have no more units
than are allowed. The City granted the zoning to consolidate a parcel that was left by the
Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”). There was a subsequent application for a
Conditional Use Permit for a private lane for the area. Mr. Carter stated that it seemed that
the applicant failed to follow through with the conditions, which led to a denial by the Planning
Commission. That denial was an appeal, which was still pending. Because of the
uniqueness of this parcel, the idea of creating a Development Agreement came into play.
The Development Agreement would create an exception to the City’s existing Code under

the private lane ordinance that would allow it to be tailored to this project and resolve the

The City Council needs to make the decision and it is the responsibility of the Planning
Commission to move it forward to the Council. It is a legislative document because it creates
an exception to the private lane ordinance in that it bypasses the appeal, whereas the
administrative application bears out evidence that it did not meet the criteria at the time.

The Development Agreement expresses the view that the decision rendered by the Planning

The Development Agreement allows the private lane to go forward as it applies to the project

amendment to the Code as it applies only to this project.

The Legislature has empowered cities to use Development Agreements to make
arrangements that are uniquely tailored to an individual piece of property; however, they
need to go through the same process that they would ordinarily if it were a rezone.
