SCHEDULES ITEMS

       RESOLUTION – Authorizing the purchase of the former Hillcrest Elementary School
       property from Alpine School District

Mayor Brunst said the property of the former Hillcrest Elementary School they were discussion
was approximately 9.33 acres and had been appraised for $5.6 million. The school building was
still in place on the property. He mentioned that Utah Code specified that the land could be used
for certain purposes only, such as a town hall, park, community center, the creation of private or
public jobs, office space, and others. Mayor Brunst said some of the allowed uses to not match city
zoning designations. He added that if the City were to resell the property in the future, the proceeds
of the sale would be split between the City and the Alpine School District.

Mayor Brunst said two appraisals of the property had been completed, one ordered by Alpine
School District and a second ordered by the City. The appraisals said the highest and best use of
the property would be for commercial office space, though one mentioned residential
development. He said they City would need to change the zoning if residential development were
to go in on this property and the City was not proposing or even supporting that kind of
development in this area.

Mr. Davidson said that years ago the City had entered into an agreement with the Alpine School
District to lease the property for the purpose of a city park. As part of that agreement, it was
outlined that the City would have first right of refusal to purchase the acreage the city park was on
if the Alpine School District were to sell. He said he understood the District was not interested in
splitting the land into parcels though the City may wish to separate pieces of the property.

Mayor Brunst said he saw a few options: 1) to purchase the whole 9.33 acres and section off a city
park and find other use for the remaining property, or 2) purchase only the 1.61 acres of the park

alone. He thought homeowners whose homes abutted the property may wish to sell property to the
City, allowing for a park of approximately 2.5 acres. Mayor Brunst said the City would be
responsible for asbestos testing and abatement on the building if the purchase was for 9.33 acres.
The school was built in 1954 with an addition built in 2003, and the older part of the school

liability issues, as well as additional costs the City would incur as part of their due diligence. Mayor
Brunst said he personally was in favor of putting in a nice park on the property, but was not in
favor of purchasing the full 9.33 acres.

Mr. Sumner asked for clarifications on the two appraisals. Mayor Brunst said the appraisal
conducted by the Alpine School District put the price of the building at $3 million but the one the
City conducted assigned no value to the building because of its state.

Mrs. Lauret said the newer addition built in 2003 was a gymnasium and cafeteria, and wondered
if that portion of the building could be used well by the City. Mayor Brunst said it would require
a new HVAC system, but was in good shape otherwise.

Mr. Lambson asked about the acreage of other parks throughout the city compared to the
approximately 2.5 acres the Mayor talked about. Mr. Davidson the City’s webpage had a parks
guide that detailed the acreage of each park. 2.5 acres for a park would be on the small side
comparatively, with most if not all city parks in excess of 3 acres. Mr. Peterson said it was difficult
to do apples to apples comparisons without taking into account the additional acreage next to each
park that was owned by Alpine School District. So 2.5 acres for a park was not enough for the
residents in the area to have a neighborhood park.

In response to Mr. Spencer’s concerns about liability and the due diligence conditions, Mayor
Brunst said the City would assume the liabilities of the asbestos and environmental studies
outcomes for the whole building and property. Mr. Davidson said there was high potential for an
asbestos issue in that building, and the mitigation of asbestos was factored into the valuation and
appraisal of the property. Mr. Stephens said if they found the results of the due diligence testing to
be unacceptable, the City had the right to cancel the contract.

Mrs. Lauret asked Mr. Davidson to clarify the funding mechanism for the purchase. Mr. Davidson
shared a chart and said the money for the purchase would come from two sources. The first was
park impact fees, the current balance of which was $1.8 million. The second source was the City’s
Redevelopment Agency, specifically the RDA 85-03B and RDA 85-03A areas, the latter covering
the property they would purchase. Mr. Davidson said the purchase arrangement would be between
the City and the Alpine School District, so the proposal was to borrow the money from the RDA
85-03B and add it to contributions from RDA 85-03A and combine with park impact fees to reach
the $5.605 million cost. Over time, they could repay the loan from the RDA by entering into a
long-term lease to a permitted user or selling a portion of the property and using proceeds of the
sale or using future park impact fee dollars.

Mr. Lambson asked if any CDBG dollars would be available for this particular project. Mr.
Davidson said that parts of the Hillcrest neighborhood did qualify for CDBG money so that would
be available. In response to a question from Mayor Brunst, Mr. Davidson said he did not plan to
use COVID related CARES money for this project.

Mr. Peterson agreed with Ms. McCann when she said residents in south Orem felt that the City
spent money disproportionately in north Orem. The people in the southeast area needed a local
park to go to, and he felt they could build a beautiful park for this community, and that it was more
than just a matter of dollars and cents.

Mayor Brunst said there were many beautiful parks throughout the city that all residents could
enjoy, including residents in the southeast part of Orem. He felt they could built a beautiful park
on 2.5 acres. Mayor Brunst wanted to emphasize and clarify that the property would not be used
for high density housing, and that it did not work well for commercial development either as it
could cause disruptions along the corridor.

Mr. Sumner said he agreed with the sentiments shared by Mr. Peterson, and said the City Council
should not deprive the southern residents of their own community park. He felt it should be equal
to other parks in the City. Mr. Lambson agreed that green space was what the area needed, not
more concrete. Mrs. Lauret commented the existing gym on the property could be a good
opportunity to expand youth basketball programming.

Mr. Macdonald said he appreciated the discussion with the Council, and felt they could more
toward a decision. He said it seemed they had two scenarios before them: to purchase some of the
property, or to purchase the full 9.33 acres. Mr. Davidson added that the City had the opportunity
to go through the due diligence and potentially back out of the sale if they saw fit.

Mr. Macdonald offered to have Mr. Peterson make the motion for the resolution. There was some
clarification needed, so Mr. Macdonald moved, by resolution, to authorize the purchase of the
former Hillcrest Elementary School property from Alpine School District, indicating that the
purchase would be of the approximately 9.33 acres available. Mr. Peterson seconded the motion.
Those voting aye: Jeff Lambson, Debby Lauret, Tom Macdonald, Terry Peterson, David Spencer,
and Brent Sumner. Those voting nay: Richard F. Brunst. The motion passed, 6-1.
