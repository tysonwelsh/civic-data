    2. PRESENTATION OF 80 ARMORED PLATES FOR DEPUTIES TO WEAR THAT PROTECT AGAINST RIFLE FIRE.
         Lydia Hebdon started Blue Line Ladies, a nonprofit organization, with Aly Ward and Anna McEwen
         (officers’ wives committed to improving law enforcement officer safety), with the goal to get steel plated
         vests for every officer in the State. Many law enforcement departments do not have the funding to provide
         all the necessary gear for their officers. Ms. Hebron said that last year there were many officer deaths and
         she felt compelled to do something to help protect her husband and other officers. Blue Line Ladies
         discovered that the County Sheriff’s Office needed 80 vests and started raising money. They have raised
         enough for 44 vests so far and still need donations to purchase the others. She said that they will not stop
         until they can furnish vests for officers in the entire State. Weber County Sheriff Terry Thompson
         expounded on the significant work that they are doing. He and the commissioners expressed sincere
         appreciation and applauded them for their very needed service. Ms. Hebdon asked the Commission to
         spread awareness about this need and of the nonprofit’s work to purchase vests to help keep officers safe.
    3. RECOGNITION OF RON BULLOCK AWARD RECIPIENTS CRAIG BROWNE, OUTSTANDING BUILDING
       OFFICIAL, AND MICHAEL BOSCH, OUTSTANDING PLAN REVIEWER/INSPECTOR.
         Sean Wilkinson, County Community Development Director, stated that Mr. Browne and Mr. Bosch received awards
         from the Bonneville Chapter of the International Code Council. He said that the awards are a big deal and are not
         given out frequently. He said that his staff do an amazing job and that he was thrilled that these two were recognized.
         Mr. Bosch was not able to be present. Mr. Browne said that this is the first time in 23 years that two awards have
         been awarded at one time, that they have to be nominated by their peers and voted on by past winners of the awards.
         Mr. Browne said that he and Mr. Bosch have done a lot of work for the Chapter. He said that they are the first
         preventers and it is their job to ensure that a building is safe. The commissioners expressed thanks for their hard work
         and dedication, noting that Mr. Browne has been a leader in his field.

    4. PRESENTATION OF THE OGDEN-WEBER CONVENTION VISITORS BUREAU ANNUAL MARKETING PLAN.
         Sara Toliver, CVB President/CEO, thanked her team for the great work that they do. She presented the 2017 annual
         report and the 2018 marketing plan stating that it had been another amazing year. The highlights included that there
         was a 6.2% year-over-year increase in travel and tourism-related taxable sales compared to a 4.3% year-over-year
         increase in non-TMPF or other taxable sales in Weber County in 2016, which shows that our economy is strong. Other
         highlights included that travel spending resulted in more than $250 million in our county last year with $7.9 million
         collected in local tax receipts, the county’s Transient Room Tax (TRT) increased 10%, and their goal was to increase
         the economic impact by half a million dollars but by 10/1/2017 it had already increased by $4.2 million. The 2018
         goals include economic impact greater than $10 million, grow revenue and increase frequency of city-wides and
         annual group business, and 10% increases for both OECC revenue and room nights.
