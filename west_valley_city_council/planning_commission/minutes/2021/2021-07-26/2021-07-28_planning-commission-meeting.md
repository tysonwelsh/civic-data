GPZ-13-2021
Brian Pitcher
4270 S 4060 W and 4312 S 4000 W General Plan change from low density residential to
light industrial and zone change from R-1-8 to M
1.81 acres

Brian Pitcher, the owner of the subject parcels, has submitted a General Plan/zone change
application for two parcels totaling 1.81 acres at 4270 S 4060 W and 4312 S 4000 W. The
property is currently zoned R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000
square feet) with a General Plan designation of low density residential (3 to 4 units/acre). The
proposed zone is M (manufacturing) and the proposed General Plan designation is light
industrial.
The two parcels are surrounded by R-1-8 zoning on all sides. Surrounding land uses include
single unit dwellings to the north, single unit dwellings and a mobile home park to the west, a
mobile home park to the south, and an auto repair business and single unit dwellings to the east.
The subject property includes a triplex, a 12-unit mobile home park, and 12 storage units.

According to County Assessor records, the triplex was built in 1950, the mobile home park was
built in 1960, and the storage units were built in 1962. Zoning was first adopted in this area in
1965, so no zoning was in place at the time the existing uses were constructed. None of the
existing uses conform with the existing R-1-8 zone.

The applicant is requesting the M zone to redevelop the property into storage units, which are
referred to as self-storage facilities in the ordinance. The attached concept plan and application
materials from the applicant provide details on and support for the proposed project. To
accommodate a concept similar to what the applicant is proposing, a development agreement
would be needed to eliminate the required 20’ setback required between buildings in the M zone
and adjoining residential uses. The City’s Board of Adjustment has granted variances for several
storage unit projects so that the buildings could be built on the property line of adjoining
residential properties. Examples of these storage unit projects are located at 1656 W Whitlock
Avenue, 4667 S 4800 W, and 5443 S 6055 W. The idea behind these variances was to prevent
the creation of spaces that are difficult to maintain and that attract inappropriate activity since
they are largely hidden from view.

The large building shown in the concept plan on the west end of the property is problematic in
that it does not have appropriate fire access. The applicant is working on an alternative concept
plan to address this issue.

Normally, staff would not recommend new M zoning that is surrounded by R-1-8 zoning.
However, in this case, staff believes M zoning may be appropriate here for the following
reasons:
     As pointed out by the applicant, the existing uses are run down. The M zone would
        facilitate redevelopment. Based on the assessed value of existing storage unit projects
        within the City, the resulting storage unit project would likely double the value of the
        property.
     Redevelopment of the property into single unit dwellings that would comply with the
        current zoning is, in staff’s opinion, unlikely. This is due to the irregular shape of the
        property and the high value of the land relative to vacant property that would typically be
        purchased by a developer to build homes.
     Of the 26 storage unit developments in the City, 14 of them are adjacent to residential.
        Hence, it is not unusual to have storage units next to residential. A map of the existing
        storage unit developments within the City is included for reference.
     While the M zone allows for uses that could be problematic next to residential, a
        development agreement could be used to prohibit all uses except storage units at this
        location.
     A few years ago, storage units were allowed in the C-2, C-3, LI, and M zones. However,
        a City initiated ordinance revision adopted by the Council in 2019 limited storage units
        to the M zone only. In other words, the M zone is now the only option for storage units.
       Given the neighboring residential uses, a conditional use permit would be required for a
        storage unit project should the zoning be changed.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This application should be approved subject a development agreement that
      incorporates the following standards:
          a. Only a self-storage facility shall be allowed on the property.
          b. Assuming fire code standards can be met, storage unit buildings may be built on
             the property lines adjoining residential uses. All of other ordinance requirements
             shall be met.

   2. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the
      public hearing.

   3. Denial. Manufacturing zoning should not be placed adjacent to residential uses.

                requesting the zone be changed to M (manufacturing) because that is the only
                zone that allows storage units. He explained other uses permitted within the
                manufacturing zone could be limited through a development agreement.
                Steve stated that if the zone change is approved, a conditional use permit will

                property and the auto repair shop. He wants to work with the City and his
                neighbors to create a fair project for everyone.

                wall would be about fifteen feet. Mr. Pitcher noted that he believes that is
                correct. He explained that his architect had proposed a fifteen footwall to the

                favors this project and would like to see a tall storage building developed on

property on 4000 West that her heirs want to preserve. She stated that she
would like the area to be void of manufacturing businesses and increased
traffic to keep their current way of life.

proposal. Mr. Webber stated that there is a space between his property and the
mobile homes currently located on the subject property. He explained that
people are driving in and out all the time, and he also believes there is
potential for unwanted behavior. Mr. Webber stated that moving the mobile
homes to abut his property line would eliminate issues with the irrigation and
the unwanted traffic.

Steven Cable, a neighbor, wanted to address the concern with irrigation. He
explained that there has already been damaged to the irrigation ditch by large
trucks driving over it. Mr. Cable would like to see this issue addressed.

Brian, the applicant, started to ask members of the public questions to help
ease their concerns.

at the meeting.

C-15-2021
Malone Powersports
Use Expansion and Landscape Exception
1379 West 3300 South
M Zone (5.16 Acres)

The applicant, Malone Powersports, is requesting a conditional use for expansion of a
recreational vehicle sales use (ATV/UTV) at 1379 West 3300 South, as well as an exception to
section 7-10 of the WVC Municipal Code, Standards for Landscaping Along High Image
Arterial Streets due to site constraints and an outdoor display request per 7-7-118.

This item was continued from the July 14th hearing to allow more time for the applicant to update
the site plan and provide additional information.

This property is zoned M, Manufacturing. The West Valley City General Plan designates this
area as General Commercial. The surrounding zone is C-2 to the north and east and M to the
west and south. The surrounding uses include Jones Heating to the north, Questar Gas and GHID
facilities to the east, Light Industrial/warehouse uses to the south, Dura Crete to the west.

This property is also located within the Jordan River Overlay Zone (JROZ). The use is
considered “Motorized Outdoor Recreation Equipment” and is currently prohibited in that
overlay zone. However, per 7-14-203, existing businesses may continue and can expand with
approval by the Planning Commission.

Malone Powersports acquired Plaza Cycle in 2019. They have also now acquired the Highland
Golf site to the east, which was approved for an expansion in 2019 (C-15-19), and the
Volkswagon auto service center to the west. They will use the Highland Golf area for
service/offices and the existing Malone Powersports building for sales/parts. The auto service
building is not within the overlay zone and does not have an intended use at this point. The
current lease expires this month so that area will be cleaned out and used for storage and
additional parking for the time being.

Now that the parcels are all consolidated Malone Powersports, in coordination with West Valley
City RDA and a Cares Act Grant, is looking to cover/abandon the canal to provide better access
and circulation internally and parking for larger trucks and trailers. The final canal design and
process is being coordinated with the Canal Company. The back southern portion of the
Highland Golf property will also be improved and paved for additional storage and parking. This
is the use expansion portion of the application.

Since the site has frontage on 3500 South, which is a High Image Arterial, the requirements set
forth in 7-10 are triggered for this project. The ordinance requires a 15’ landscaped berm, a 10’
sidewalk and a 5’ strip of additional landscaping and street lighting. Based off the existing site
layout, access and circulation concerns and a proposal for display areas, the applicant is
requesting an exception to the streetscape as follows:

Segment 1:

Opt A: A 10’ landscaped area with a 10’ sidewalk and a 10’ display area.

Opt B: Comply with the 7-10 standards of a 15’ landscaped area a 10’ sidewalk and a 5’
landscaped area and provide display behind the streetscape to accommodate additional parking.


Segment 2:

Opt A: A 10’ landscaped area with a 5’ sidewalk.

Opt B: A 10’ landscaped area with a 10’ sidewalk.
Opt C: Comply with the 7-10 standards of a 15’ landscaped area a 10’ sidewalk and a 5’
landscaped area. The parking stalls would need to be relocated and no display would be
provided.


Segment 3:

Opt A: A 10’ landscaped area with a 10’ sidewalk and a 10’ display area.

Opt B: Comply with the 7-10 standards of a 15’ landscaped area a 10’ sidewalk and a 5’
landscaped area and provide display behind the streetscape to accommodate additional parking.

UDOT has indicated if an internal connection is provided that they may require an access to be
closed. This would impact the design of the site so the applicant is continuing to coordinate that
review to ensure the design is completed as required.

As discussed in the Study Meeting if the exception to 7-10 is not granted at this time then the
Highland Golf site would need to comply with the original approval from C-15-19 which has the
streetscape design as required by Ordinance with no exceptions. The canal parcel would be
consolidated with this lot and covered and the streetscape would continue along the extents of
this parcel. The back portion of that site could be expanded as indicated on their plan and
improved with asphalt for outside storage and parking.

The remaining portions of the site would need to be maintained. Display would not permitted in
the landscaped areas and all landscaping would need to be re-installed and repaired as originally
required. See C-59-86 and the WVC Municipal Code.

The building façade is also in need of repair and maintenance. There is currently a pole sign on
the property for Plaza Cycle. The applicant intends to keep this sign on site and is working on
coordinating the Malone Powersports name with Plaza Cycle so the sign does not become off
premise. The Highland Golf pole sign will need to remain multi-tenant and display tenants that
are currently operating on site or it will be considered abandoned and will need to be removed.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as the
following:

   1. The existing business, Malone Powersports, may expand to the east on the canal and the
      original Highland Golf site.

   2. Segment 1 shall be designed as follows:

           a. A 10’ landscaped area with a 10’ sidewalk and a 10’ display area.
              b. Comply with the 7-10 standards of a 15’ landscaped area a 10’ sidewalk and a 5’
                 landscaped area and provide display behind the streetscape.

    3. Segment 2 shall be designed as follows:

              a. A 10’ landscaped area with a 5’ sidewalk.

              b. A 10’ landscaped area with a 10’ sidewalk.

              c. Comply with the 7-10 standards of a 15’ landscaped area a 10’ sidewalk and a 5’
                 landscaped area. The parking stalls would need to be relocated and no display
                 shall be provided.

    4. Segment 3 shall be designed as follows:

              a. A 10’ landscaped area with a 10’ sidewalk and a 10’ display area.

              b. Comply with the 7-10 standards of a 15’ landscaped area a 10’ sidewalk and a 5’
                 landscaped area and provide display behind the streetscape.

    5. All existing landscaped areas must be improved per the plans or repaired and replaced
       with live plant material per the City Code.

    6. All buildings must be maintained.

    7. Signage shall meet the standards set forth in Title 11 of the WVC Municipal Code.

    8. Must meet the requirements of all affected departments and agencies.

    9. Subject to review upon valid complaint.

Continuance, to allow for the resolutions of any issues raised at the public hearing and to allow
the applicant additional time to update and revise the site plans and request.

Denial, of the request for an exception to the Standards for Landscaping Along High Image
Arterial Streets (7-10). The criteria in 7-10-104 have not been met.
