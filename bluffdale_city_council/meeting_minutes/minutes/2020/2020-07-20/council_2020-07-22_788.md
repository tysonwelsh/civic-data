	•	Welcome.

Mayor Timothy called the meeting to order at approximately 4:34 p.m. and welcomed those present.  All City Council Members were present with the exception of Wendy Aston.  There were no Planning Commissioners present.  

	•	Continued Discussion Regarding the 2020 Bluffdale Economic Development Strategic Plan.

City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell presented the staff report and informed the Council that Planning Commission Member Debbie Cragun would join the meeting as soon as she is available.  

It was reported that at the previous Economic Development Meeting, the Strategic Plan for Economic Development was discussed.  The Council previously asked staff to review and determine realistic time frames and costs for tasks identified in the Strategic Plan.  Mr. Crowell reported that staff has met and reviewed the ideas and resources.  A spreadsheet of projects and tasks for the Strategic Plan was displayed. 

The details of obtaining a grocery store were addressed and the grocery store study had been completed.  There was not a completion date for the project because it is an ongoing effort and staff had not come up with a list of priorities.  

Mr. Crowell informed the Council that the project to complete 14600 South is a high priority because of the significant amount of discussion that has taken place over the last few years.  To complete the project, assistance from all departments will be required, including all utility providers, as well as other cities and entities.  Mr. Crowell explained that to complete a plan from Redwood Road to I-15, a Project Manager is required at an anticipated cost of $100,000.  The expected completion date was anticipated to be May of 2021, however, staff projected January of 2022.  After a plan is determined, it will cost millions of dollars to implement.  

Staff was currently focusing their attention on sign ordinances.  A consultant can be hired if the Council desires more than what staff is doing.  The Committee projected it to be completed in November of 2020, however, the City has a moratorium that requires it be completed by September of 2020.  This was to be reviewed on August 19, 2020 by the Planning Commission as well as a repeal and replace of the existing Sign Code.  

Training items, such as form-based codes and RDA tools can be discussed and initiated this year.  The Planning Department can introduce form-based codes, but a consultant can be hired at a cost of $30,000 to $50,000.  By the end of this year, form-based codes can be determined at an Economic Development Meeting.  Concerning the RDA tasks, training needs to be completed which can be accomplished by April of 2021.

Mr. Crowell reviewed Strategy 2, the Multi-Modal Transportation Plan.  A number of tasks listed are currently in staff’s plans.  If the City would like to integrate the Transportation Plan with its Parks and Trails Plan, the Parks and Trails Plan will need to be updated and will cost a significant amount of money.  This has the potential to be broad and includes a significant number of tasks.  The projected date to complete this is further in the future because of the need to update the Parks and Trails Plan.  

Strategy 3 incorporates the Regional Transportation Plan.  Staff asked for an audit, but City cannot compel the company to adapt its plans.  However, staff can incorporate earlier plans and report the status of the regional planning efforts.

The development of corridors and gateway and commercial corridors was reviewed.  Mr. Crowell informed the Council that the City does not currently employ an architect, but depending on the level of expertise desired, one can be hired.  This will require a significant amount of ordinance work and could be completed by next year.  

Mr. Crowell reported that the risk of not completing the Sign Code by the time the moratorium expires is that the City will revert to the current ordinance.  

Zoning along key corridors was discussed.  The General Plan may direct the Council on what to do with this project.  If so, the zoning will follow the General Plan.  However, if a task is required to be completed immediately, staff needs to know so they can act before the General Plan is determined.  

Marketing key streets, specifically Bluffdale Boulevard, is a topic that Mayor Timothy and Dave Kallas emailed to Mr. Crowell.  A commemorative street name was in the process of being approved, but staff would like to know when to implement the process.  

Dave Kallas reported that in the Strategic Plan goals, the name change is to be implemented in conjunction with the completion of the new intersection.  September 1, 2020 is when the intersection is projected to be completed.  Staff Engineer, Dan Tracer responded that there will be two names, 14600 South and Bluffdale Boulevard.  

Mr. Crowell reported that the timeline is currently January.  The second portion of this project is to fund the street sign changes.  

Mayor Timothy asked if the Council desires to wait until the road was improved before adding the new commemorative sign.  Council Member Kallas stated that the name is aspirational and that if putting the sign up is delayed for completion of construction, it could take a significant number of years.  The original idea of putting up the sign was to promote Bluffdale City and create awareness.  The name change can define what the Council hopes the City will aspire to in the future.  Traci Crockett agreed.  Mr. Crowell stated that staff would continue to work on this.  

Mayor Timothy commented that he has been and is still in favor of changing the name of Camp Williams Road, especially now that Camp Williams is in favor of it.  Dave Kallas agreed and stated that with the commemorative name change on 14600 South, he hoped UDOT would include the name on the exit ramp of I-15.  City Manager, Mark Reid responded that this is a possibility.  Mr. Crowell agreed to include this in the evaluation and will inquire as to how much will be charged for the betterment.  Mayor Timothy stated that because the street names are different on both sides of I-15, UDOT may default to using 14600 South, but the City can request it.  

Mr. Crowell reviewed commercial signage.  He recommended the projection date of July 1, 2021  be included in the budget.  An Electronic Message Center was recommended to be considered and will cost tens of thousands of dollars.  

The Minor Robust Communication Program may be a budgetary issue for July of next year because of the desire for more available resources in the Communication Department.  Economic Development can retain the same projection date listed.  The Council asked that staff review and report on the department, its relationships with other departments and if there is a need for additional funding.  If completed by next spring, the plan would be prepared if there is funding.  Mr. Crowell stated that the five problematic or priority ordinances had yet been identified.  

Mr. Crowell recommended various projects that could be initiated.  He reported that the spreadsheet is the methodology of how staff approached the Strategic Plan.  Dave Kallas commented that staff completed what the Council requested and he appreciated the realistic costs and time frames for the projects.  He desired to go forward and recommended that the Council discuss the tasks that are the highest priority at Economic Development Meetings.  

Mayor Timothy desired to have a Gantt chart to visualize progress that consists of the strategies and the breaking down of tasks with time frames for the projects.  This will help promote progress and assist staff.  Council Member Kallas agreed.  Mayor Timothy stated that after staff has put the information in the Gantt chart, the Council can review and fine-tune it.  Dave Kallas agreed and recommended that the Council write up the agendas using the tasks from the chart for Economic Development Meetings.  Mayor Timothy agreed that the chart will help accomplish that.  It will assist the structure of the meetings, the structure of the effort, and the structure of staff going forward.  Mayor Timothy asked Mr. Reid to develop a Gantt chart with as much detail as possible, along with time frames and priorities.  

	•	Development Updates – Commercial Flex Space and “Warehousing”.

Mr. Crowell reported that he was asked to join a meeting with DAI, Holiday Oil, and Dave Kallas.  They discussed the eastern portion of the Independence Master Plan.  There is currently pending zoning that has never been acted on from September of last year when it was tabled.  

A map was displayed of the original idea the Council reviewed, which is considered industrial flex space also known as “warehousing”.  Holiday Oil is interested in the site as well as other sites in West Jordan for a corporate office and distribution center in conjunction with a convenience store.  Other areas of the project were also be reserved for retail or professional space.  The discussion also involved what the warehouses should look like.  

Types of warehouse architectural plans were displayed and reviewed.  Mr. Crowell reviewed reasons of why warehouses are not appealing to look at.  Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison, had coordinated with architects to plan appealing buildings.  Currently, the architect working with the City was conforming to the desires of the City, however, the City will have issues if architects vary from these desires.  A change in the Use Table will be required. 

Dave Kallas stated that there has been frustration within the community about warehouse uses.  He asked if it was possible to build warehouses that accommodate the City’s economic development goals but do not have a warehouse feel.  Creating design standards will give a warehouse district a community ambiance.  He explained that his concern stems from listening to residents who do not want to live in a community with too many warehouse uses.  

Holiday Oil’s proposal was considered warehousing.  The City has high expectations for the Porter Rockwell area and the projects that are being submitted are apartments and warehouses.  This is frustrating when this area is the hope of the City.  In the meeting, Dave Kallas recommended that Holiday Oil locate their headquarters in Bluffdale City.  He approves of their commercial hub on the frontage of Porter Rockwell because of the desire to have a more retail component.  The Council desired to see retail businesses in the front with a warehouse in the back consisting of office and warehouse uses.  Council Member Kallas reported that Holiday Oil stated that they would present renderings at a future Work Session.  

Mr. Crowell reported that John Linton from Holiday Oil is preparing renderings for the real estate portion of the project.  This would be Holiday Oil’s corporate headquarters and bring 100 jobs.  They are trying to vertically integrate the distribution into their business.  70%-80% of their distribution is for Holiday Oil.  Holiday Oil will not lease their pads but will find partners to work with.  

Mrs. Robison requested that the Council share ideas on their expectations and desires for warehouses to be more appealing.  Bluffdale City does not have a main street which makes it difficult to incorporate this in the City.  

Mayor Timothy requested that staff create a portfolio of what the City should look like and state that until a proposal is created, it is difficult to know where to begin.  

Mrs. Robison used Bringhurst Station as an example of retail looking warehousing and reviewed elements that give it a retail appearance.  It is still a big box and has the appearance of a warehouse.

Jeff Gaston gave examples of well-planned areas with appealing design standards.  He commented that better design brings better businesses into the area.  Mrs. Robison stated that the areas pointed out by Council Member Gaston are current retail areas that may not be obtainable for the City.  

In response to a question, Jeff Gaston stated that businesses will come to the 2700 West area.  He would not want to settle for flex office space.  There are a few areas where the City can capitalize on commercial.  It was noted that office space needs to be on the frontage road to be successful.  The City has a small percentage of land to capitalize on.  Council Member Gaston gave an example of the need for retailers rather than office space and warehouses in the Independence area.

Mr. Crowell reported that properties on 2700 West have received the most flexible zoning with the intent being to allow a variety of businesses.  Mr. Crowell recommended that the City employ an architect immediately to assist with design standards.  

Dave Kallas asked if the City currently controls the construction and type of a flex building built on commercial property.  Mr. Crowell explained that the City does not.  Council Member Kallas stated that it would be worthwhile to review the Use Table.  If the City allows warehouse flex construction, a design standard should be required.  

Mrs. Robison reported that the City does have standards.  It may be beneficial to determine the standards.  Staff was trying to determine the type of construction, use, etc. to accomplish the City’s goals.  Mr. Crowell commented that many applicants desire flexible spaces.  If the City is in an emergency situation, the Code needs to be reviewed to address this crisis quickly.

Dave Kallas recommended that staff learn how other cities have approached warehousing.  If a significant number of applicants are approaching the City, a design standard should be put in place.  The Use Table should not be a high priority.  Mr. Crowell explained that with a form-based code, the Use Table is not useful.  Examples of form-based codes were given.  Dave Kallas explained that a form-based code is what the City should enact.  Examples of warehouses in various cities were given.  Mrs. Robison stated that this is a top priority and needs to be put in place to direct applicants.  She stated that staff would come prepared at a future meeting with ideas.  

Mr. Crowell identified the benefits of hiring an architect but stated that staff will borrow from other cities if it is not possible to hire one. 

Mr. Reid reviewed the study with Zions Bank and recommended that the City review the study again.  

Planning Commissioner, Debbie Cragun, stated that when an application comes before the Commission, applicants have already completed what is required and it is too late to control the outcome of the plans.  The Commission desired to understand the Council’s long-term vision to be of assistance in accomplishing the City’s goals.  Commissioner Cragun stated that this is a crisis and should be a top priority.  

Mayor Timothy recommended that staff draft a small portion of the design standard to show what it will look like in the Code.  He also desired to see the standard and results of using a form-based code.

He asked if the Council would be interested in receiving more information.  He stated that it could be offered as a Franchise Agreement.  The Council was interested in getting additional information.
