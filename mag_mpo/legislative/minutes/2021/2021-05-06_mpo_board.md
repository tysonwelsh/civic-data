FY22 DRAFT Unified Planning Work Program (UPWP) – Shawn Seager, MAG The July 1, 2021 to June 30, 2022 (FY22) DRAFT MAG Unified Planning Work Program (Work Program) describes the transportation planning work undertaken by Mountainland Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO) Staff, Utah Department of Transportation Staff, and the Utah Transit Authority Staff within the Mountainland MPO planning area. The Work Program is an annual budget that illustrates to the members, Federal Highway Administration, Federal Transit Administration, and the general public where the planning resources are allocated. It describes the planned work, budget, and funding sources. This Work Programs $2,851,656 budget increased 8% compared to the FY21 budget of $2,571,482. This is due to an increase in new studies / contracts and staff cost in COLA, merit, and equipment cost. Revenue for this $2.8M budget is approximately 93% federal funds from the United States Department of Transportation while 7% is local funds from UTA, UDOT and our Local Governments. This budget proposes to spend $651,500 or 23% on new special studies and contracts including: 1. Joint Planning Studies with Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC), UDOT, and UTA 2. Travel Demand Modeling 3. Planning Opportunities Contingency 4. Project Management / Development Engineer Consultant 5. Household Travel Survey / Street Light Data MAG Staff: Shawn Seager Chad Eccles Andrew Jackson Michelle Carroll Robert Allen Shawn Eliot Claudia Arteaga Andrew Wooley Tanner Fullmer Chad Eccles MPO Regional Planning Committee May 6, 2021 Page 3 Mayor Kafusi moved to approve the FY22 MAG Unified Planning and Work Program as presented. Mayor Miller seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. TIP Amendment-Frontrunner Extension Environmental Study – Chad Eccles, MAG MAG, UDOT, and UTA in partnership with the cities in the south part of Utah County are currently participating in the South County Transit Study to assess and plan for future transit to extend from the south terminus of the Frontrunner Commuter Rail line. The partners involved realize that the next step in moving this project forward is to conduct an environmental study. At the March 2021 MPO Regional Planning Committee, some of the mayors requested that MAG prepare a funding strategy to conduct the environmental study. The current federal administration is preparing a large infrastructure funding program. To be prepared, MAG recommends that this project be shovel ready in the event funding becomes available. It would be beneficial to move forward with the environmental work and have funding set aside to help the partners fund this important next step. Payson City has since offered to sponsor this project on behalf of the other cities in the south county. MAG has determined that it would have the capability to set aside $2M for an estimated $4M study with the rest of the cost to be shared. This amendment would preserve $2M for this study. MAG Staff and TAC recommends that the MPO Regional Planning Committee amend the TIP to include $2M for the environmental study for a Frontrunner extension into south Utah County. Mayor Brunst moved that the MAG TIP be amended to include $2 million to be put toward an environmental study for a Frontrunner extension into southern Utah County. Mayor Stout seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. TIP Amendment-SR92/North County Blvd Property Acquisition - Bob Allen, MAG The intersection of SR92 and North County Blvd continues to be very busy. The eastbound right turn to southbound movement is the biggest issue for the intersection. Future modifications to the intersection may be needed. The parcel at the southwest corner of the intersection is up for sale and UDOT and Highland both agree that it should be acquired to allow future intersection improvements. The Canal Blvd project within Highland and Cedar Hills is nearing completion and has MPO Regional Planning Committee May 6, 2021 Page 4 excess funds allocated to it. Highland would propose that $250k of those excess funds be used to acquire the SR92/North County Blvd parcel. MAG Staff and TAC recommend the TIP be modified to move $250k from the Canal Blvd project to purchase the SR92/North County Blvd property. Mayor Stout moved that $250k be moved from the Canal Blvd project to purchase the SR92/North County Blvd property. Mayor Mann seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. UDOT STIP Workshop Preliminary Results – Commissioner Jim Evans and Rob Clayton, UDOT Commissioner Jim Evans presented a timeline of the Statewide Transportation Improvement Program (STIP) beginning January 2021 through December 2021. The Transportation Commission has been evaluating data in terms of programs that money can be spent for and projects. Planning areas and cities have been giving their input. In June the Transportation Commission will make their recommendation that will go to public comment in July. Rob Clayton explained that the next step for the Transportation Commission is to finalize the list and get the STIP updated so they can move forward. During the legislative session, Utah received one-time money to fund transportation projects. UDOT’s Region 3 that primarily covers Utah County came out well largely in part to the organized effort and communication from MAG, the Utah Valley Chamber of Commerce, and all who have supported the efforts. The Transportation Commission is going to act on the list of projects that came from the legislative session. They do not have the ability to change the projects but will assign the scope and budget for the projects. Many of the projects on the list are familiar and are scheduled to begin Spring 2022 through Spring 2024. For additional information and links to the transportation projects visit http://udot.utah.gov Shawn Seager reported that the Mountainview Corridor project was not scoring as high as some of the other projects it was competing against. UDOT and MPO staff were paying attention to it and got Commissioner Evans involved. He explained that Commissioner Evans showed a lot of interest in the project and helped guide it through the UDOT approval process. Recognition was given to Commissioner Evans as the project will change the nature of the northwest commute into Salt Lake County and will have a positive impact on Saratoga Springs and Eagle Mountain.
