PC 24-001 UT1 Woodruff -Continued from the January 25th meeting. [Conditional Use Permit]
Troy Benson-Verizon/Logan City is requesting a design review permit for a new Verizon Wireless
Communication Tower located near the West Willow Park batting cages at 565 West 700 South in
the Recreation (REC) zone; TIN 02-058-0043 (Woodruff Neighborhood).


Russ Akina, Parks and Recreation Director said that Logan City has a lease with the batting cages,
who pay an annual fee to conduct their business on City property. It would be the same situation
with the Verizon Cell Tower.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Peterson said the reason the project was continued was so the
applicant could look into alternate locations for the tower that were not near the playground.


R. Holley said that the tower that was recently approved on County-owned property is a Cache
County emergency communication tower for radio communication between other county towers and
not a private cell tower.

Commissioner Guth asked why there is a setback for a cell tower from the street but not from a public
playground or pavilion. Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director said that the setback
from the street is for safety, if the tower fails there is a two-to-one distance on the height from the
street.

Commissioner Guth stated that the Land Development Code Chapter 17.42.050.C states that the
Planning Commission may approve a conditional use permit if the use is compatible with surrounding
land uses and will not interfere with the use and enjoyment of adjoining properties.

This location for a cell tower is not compatible with the surrounding land use for the following reasons:

The cell tower is an industrial use and the park is for outdoor recreational use. The cell tower is an
unattractive, large metal imposing structure that detracts from the natural setting. The sole purpose
of the park is for public enjoyment of the outdoors.

The presence of the cell tower in the center of the park and directly adjacent to a playground, picnic
area, and fishing pond has direct negative impacts by degrading the quality of those experiences.
The stealth tower is not a good alternative, it does not disguise the tower at close proximity, it is more
appropriate in disguising towers at a distance, and it makes the tower taller and bulkier.

It is not good precedence to allow a for-profit entity to use public land in a way that devalues the land
for the public. If one company wants a tower there will be many others that will also request one.

Would a cell tower be considered in other parks. Willow Park is in a low-income area with under-
represented populations. What would the public response be to a cell tower in any other park such
as Lundstrom or Hyrum Gibbons, next to the playgrounds and pavilions. There would likely be a
large public outcry.

This is poor planning, once a decision is made there is no undoing it. There are likely better locations
for a cell tower in this part of the City. The City, County, and State have a large contiguous area in
this zone and need to collaborate to find the best location for a cell tower. The planning should
include space for other providers to minimize the impact and reduce the need for other towers.

Mohamed Abdullahi, City Attorney, stated that Conditional Uses are similar to permitted uses in that
they must be approved if conditions can be placed on the project mitigating for any identified issues
or detrimental effects of that project. If the Commission cannot find any reasonable conditions to
mitigate potential impacts, and the recourse is to deny a project, the Planning Commission needs to
clearly articulate those un-mitigatable issues. Commissioner Guth stated that the primary concern
was location, and in its current location, the detrimental effects of the town could not be mitigated.


R. Holley said that cell towers are permitted in the Industrial and Commercial zones and conditionally
permitted in the Public and Recreation zones. Cell towers have to be allowed in different parts of
town to provide coverage. Commissioner Guth agreed that a cell tower is necessary in this area, but
does not think that a tower should be placed in full view or adjacent to a heavily used playground. R.
Holley discussed the different elements of a tower that can be considered by the Commission in their
review of the Conditional Use Permit, e.g., height, dimensions, design, location, etc. Commissioner
Guth stated that the tower, in the specific proposed location, is not compatible with the land uses in
the park, and short of creating an invisible tower or placing it in a different location where it is not
visible, is the only way to mitigate for this specific issue.

There was discussion about placing the tower in the parking lot instead of adjacent to the playground.
Russ Akina said that they did look at moving the tower to the southwest side of the batting cages
and away from the playground, however, they encountered problems with meeting minimum
setbacks and screening the tower from the homes to the west. Russ Akina also expressed concerns
about access to existing infrastructure necessary to service the tower, and the negative impacts that
may have on the park.

Commissioner Guth asked about some alternate locations near the golf course driving range or the
Willow Park Zoo, on other City-owned properties. There was discussion about the primary reason
why the tower was limited in where it could be located in the park, and that was due to the underlying
grant used to purchase the park property. The conditions of the grant used to acquire the property
prohibited cell towers.

R. Holley said that the zoning allows cell towers in the proposed zone and the Commission is required
to review the request on a specific property. The Commission has the authority to require the cell
tower be located elsewhere on a specific property, but the Commission cannot require an applicant
to move an allowed use to a completely different property.

The Commission discussed alternate locations on the broader site encompassing City owned
properties, and not just in Willow Park. There was additional discussion about the County Fair
Grounds and the potential to share their new 70’ tower. Mohamed Abdullahi, City Attorney, reminded
the Commission that because the Conditional Use permit is similar to a permitted use, the
Commission has to articulate the detrimental impacts of the project and then identify reasonable
conditions that would mitigate for those detrimental impacts. The Planning Commission agreed that
the specific location of the cell tower is not a compatible land use with the adjacent playground or
this general part of the park. The current location, with its current detrimental impacts are un-
mitigatable short of moving the tower to a different location.

The Commission discussed an alternative site on the same property where the tower could go and
determined that the southeast corner of the specific parcel, across the parking lot from the batting
cages and playground, would be a better location that is further away from the playground. The
Commission also discussed having a solid wall enclosure around the tower.

R. Akina said he would like to evaluate the alternate location to see if a tower would affect the traffic
or pedestrian flow through the parking lot, or if there would be any safety concerns with the people
playing baseball on the adjacent field where there are not fences, or kids walking between the fields
and the batting cages.

MOTION: Commissioner Peterson made a motion to Continue a Conditional Use Permit for PC 24-
001 to the February 22nd meeting to see the proposed tower at the alternate location disused.
Commissioner Heare seconded the motion.

PC 24-004 Land Development Code Amendment – Engineering Design Standards, Water and
Sewer, Chapter 17.29 -Continued from the January 25th meeting. [Code Amendment] Darren
Farar/Logan City is requesting an amendment to Chapter 17.29 to adopt the City of Logan Public
Works Design Standards for sanitary sewer, culinary & secondary water, and storm drain systems.

STAFF/PROPONENT: Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director reviewed the proposal to
adopt by reference the City of Logan Public Works Design Standards for sewer and water
improvements. Land Development Code Chapter 17.29 currently references these specific sewer
and water technical standards for new development.

The proposed amendment updates the Public Works Design Standards, the City’s approved
Materials List (2024), and Public Works Details (Manual of Standard Plans 2007 edition)

Darren Farar said they are trying to provide a comprehensive document that follows State and
Federal codes.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Lewis said it was good to see the detailed explanation of everything
that goes underground to make infrastructure durable.

MOTION: Commissioner Heare made a motion to forward a recommendation of approval to the
Municipal Council on a code amendment for PC 24-004 with the findings for approval as listed below.
Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion.

FINDING FOR APPROVAL
1. Utah State Law authorizes local Planning Commission to recommend ordinance changes to the
   legislative body (Municipal Council).
2. The Code Amendments are done in conformance with the requirements of Title 17.51 of the
   Logan Municipal Code.
3. The proposed Code Amendments are consistent with the Logan City General Plan.
4. The proposed Code Amendments will clarify the minimum design standards acceptable for new
   sewer & water infrastructure in Logan City.
5. No public comment has been received regarding the proposed amendments.
