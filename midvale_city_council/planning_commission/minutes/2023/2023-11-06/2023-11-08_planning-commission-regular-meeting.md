GENERAL SESSION

Chair Liedtke called the Planning & Zoning Commission meeting to order at 6:00 p.m. He
explained how the meeting would proceed. First, the Planning Department would brief the
Commission; then the applicant would speak to the Commission; the item will be opened to the
public for their comments on public hearing agenda items; the Midvale Deputy City Attorney
will then brief the Commission on their role on the given item; and then the Commission would
deliberate and decide.

PUBLIC HEARINGS

1.   Erica Sanford requests Conditional Use Permit approval for a daycare located at 202
     E Fort Union Blvd in the Single-Family Residential-1/Duplex Overlay/7200 S
     (SF1/DO/7200 S) zone.

      The applicant requests conditional use permit approval to allow for a daycare facility
      located at 202 E Fort Union Blvd in the SF-1/DO/7200 S zone. The daycare facility
      would allow up to twelve children to attend.

      This proposal has been reviewed by Planning Staff, the City Engineer, and the Unified
      Fire Authority for compliance with the respective guidelines, policies, standards, and
      codes. Staff finds the proposal complies with requirements outlined in Midvale City
      Municipal Code 17-7-1.

      Public notice has been sent to property owners within five hundred feet of the subject
      parcel. One objection has been received as of the writing of this report and has been
      included in the attachments.

      Staff recommends approval.

APPLICANT:

Erica Sanford, applicant, introduces herself and explains her stance on the objection submitted.
The fence will be fixed, and the gate and carport will be secured during daycare hours.
The gap in the fence is temporarily blocked off with plywood between fence and wall.

Commissioner Snow asked about liability because she does not own the property. The Deputy
City Attorney ensures it would be civil and dependent on the situation.

Ms. Sanford explains that the basement tenant no longer resides there with their six children. The
family that moved had the children that were causing most of the disturbances to the neighbors.

Ms. Sanford now rents both units.

Chad Burst, neighbor, has concerns with the fence and the liability with renter being the business
owner. The legal stress would be overwhelming if there was not already someone meant to be in
place. He asks to clarify if the amount of daycare children allowed is twelve, including her children
and their friends or not. He is concerned with the parking and the busy road. Safety for the children
and lack of space outside of driveways for pick-up and drop-off. Has the site inspection occurred
yet?

No site inspection has occurred yet, state and city license follow through with that process.

Ms. Sanford responds, the state limit including her own children is twelve maxima. During the
school year, her children will be at school and only the 5 or 6 children will be there for the daycare
during those day hours. The concern of a car parked outside in a prohibited area, the car has been
moved and is not stationary there.

Snow asks if the basement unit could be rented out separately and allow her family to reside there
plus operate a business.

The applicant responds, the state does not allow for the basement unit to be rented out while
running a daycare business for the risk of more children living at the property.

Wendelin Knobloch, Planning Director, verifies that this is more of a concern for the state licensure
rather than the Planning Commission criteria.

2.    Midvale City initiated request to amend Section 17-7-8.4(A)(9)(c) of the Transit
      Oriented Development (TOD) zone relating to townhome development in the

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION NOVEMBER 8, 2023.
      Midvale City Municipal Code.

       Midvale City requests an amendment to the above-listed chapter of the Midvale City
       Municipal Code. This section regulates the development of townhouses in medium and
       high-density residential developments. The proposed amendment removes language that
       is cumbersome and difficult to implement in practice. The amendment will make the
       development process smoother and easier for both applicants and city staff.

       Public notice has been sent to affected entities as required in 17-3-9. B of the Municipal
       Code. No comments have been received as of the writing of this report.

       Staff recommends approval.
