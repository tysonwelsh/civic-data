Cardell Andrews, Aligned Data Centers senior director of the technical program, said that this is their

5" building in West Jordan. He showed the alignment of the transmission line, which leads to a new

on-site substation and will go south to Old Bingham Highway. One of the key components with the

construct, and service the line, but their company will draw power from it. He explained that the
buildings are used as data centers housing servers that connect to the grid to provide the types of data
used throughout the world.

Megan Jensen stated that the application for power lines is considered a major utility and is a

conditional use. She reviewed landscaping in the area. Aligned Data Centers will remove 59 trees and

plant 80 new trees. The Public Services Department has reviewed and is okay with the plan.

4. Residential Overlay; 6676 and 6686 South Redwood Road; Recommendation to the City
Council to Amend the General Plan Future Land Use Map on 6.23 acres of property
adding the future land use designation of “Potential Residential Overlay District (ROD)”;
City of West Jordan (applicant) [parcels 21-22-327-003, 008]
Larry Gardner said Strategy 1g in the Moderate-Income Housing plan is to update the future land use
map for each potential ROD (Residential Overlay District) area to reflect a future rezone to ROD. The
city received a letter of non-compliance from the State of Utah because the benchmark for that strategy

was July 31, 2024. This action is to become compliant. He emphasized that this action is not a rezone

of property but a marker on the future land use map that could reflect a future zone. He named the

three areas that were at one time identified for the ROD, including this property. Since that time, two

of them no longer are being considered. A developer has been involved with this property in the past,

but there is no current application. The recommendation to the City Council does not carry any

entitlement. Entitlements only come with a proposal and application that meets constraints for utilities,

traffic, and the ROD ordinance.

Based on the information and Criteria set forth in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning

Commission forward appositive recommendation to the City Council for this application.

George Winn opened the public hearing.

Gary Cannon, representing the South Jordan Canal, stated that there is some concern about what an

end product would look like as it pertains to safety and reducing liability on the South Jordan canal.

There are challenges with Canal Road, and they want to be involved with discussion on the access if

the property changes to residential.

Michael Hansen, West Jordan resident, stated that he lives on Canal Road and is also speaking on

behalf of his grandmother. They are concerned about the potential for high-density apartments because

of the narrow width of Canal Road, which is currently closed from access at Redwood Road. High-

density housing would be an issue for those who still live on Canal Road. He referred to a meeting in

the past for a proposed 1200-resident apartment complex, which is not a good idea. He was against any

type of high-density housing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

George Winn asked for comments on potential traffic issues with this application.

Larry Gardner said this application does not give entitlement. For the property to develop under the

ROD, a zone change is required and then a site plan approval. Concerns about access to Canal Road

will be vetted at that time. He asked the Fire Marshal if access on Canal Road was needed for fire

safety.

Paul Brockbank stated that the development would need two access/egress points. On a past iteration,

one of the suggestions was a limited access to the shopping center to the south with an easement,

which is one solution to satisfy the fire code. The developer of that plan was trying hard not to open

Canal Road for access.

George Winn said to be clear, the application is only for a recommendation to the council for a future

land use map amendment. All access and safety issues would be addressed later as part of an

application to develop the property.

8. Text Amendment — City Center Zones; Recommendation to the City Council to Amend

the West Jordan City Code Section 13-5I-5: Residential Density. Adding the requirement
for moderate income housing and amending maximum residential density; City of West
Jordan (applicant)
Larry Gardner stated that the amendment is to bring the City into compliance with the State of Utah
Moderate Income Housing Plan Strategy to integrate moderate income housing into the City Center

Planning & Zoning Commission Meeting Minutes

November 19, 2024

Page 7

Core zone. The original intent was to base the amendment to the City Center zone on the adopted 3-

Station Area Plan. The City Center zone was approved in 2007 and multi-family residential was never

listed on the use table in the City Center Core, but it was mentioned in the text of the ordinance. He

believed that this was an oversight, so an addition to the use table will correct that. Section 13-5I-5,

Residential Density, will state that all residential uses shall contain a minimum of 10% moderate

income housing based on the total unit count for each building and/or project as approved by the city

council through a master development agreement. The MDA will make the developers aware that they
are responsible for paying for the moderate-income housing, and not the city. Another change in this
amendment adds mixed-use commercial to the use table with a minimum density of 14 dwelling units

per acre and a maximum of 80. The density ranges in the table reflect the recommendation in the 3-

Station Area Plan.

9. Text Amendment — Alcohol Beverage Control; Recommendation to the City Council to

Amend the West Jordan City Code Title 17 — Alcohol Beverage Control and Uses; City-
wide applicability; City of West Jordan (applicant)

Alan Anderson, Council Director, said the proposed amendment will simplify Title 17 and reduce the

pages from 33 to 8. Repetitive State requirements have been omitted, the process for obtaining a city-

issued permit has been simplified so it is more business friendly, and current practices of the Business

Licensing Division are accurately reflected. The City Council also adopted an administrative

enforcement hearing program that uses an administrative law judge, which is referenced in this code.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation for the changes

to Title 17 of West Jordan City Code, as it relates to Alcohol Beverage Control and Uses.
