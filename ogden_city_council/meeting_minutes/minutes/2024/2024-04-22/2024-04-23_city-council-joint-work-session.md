         The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agenda for the Special City Council, Special Redevelopment
Agency (RDA), and Municipal Building Authority (MBA) meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; receive the annual report
from My Hometown; discuss proposed Short Term Rental Ordinance amendments; receive an update regarding General Plan
revisions; discuss proposed Utility Revenue Bond parameters – 36 inch Canyon Waterline project; discuss Council business,
including Financial Principals; and hear Council/RDA/MBA comments.

Agenda Review
         Chair Richey and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the Special City Council,
Special RDA, and Special MBA meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

My Hometown Annual Report.
           My Hometown Ogden (MHO) is a volunteer-based program supported by Ogden City. There are eight volunteers from
the community that make up the executive board, who manage thousands of volunteers from within the Ogden community.
Volunteers not only help people with various home projects, but they build relationships and help create a sense of community in
many neighborhoods. John Watson, President of MHO Leadership, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to present the annual
report, which highlighted MHO’s mission, vision, operations, accomplishments, and goals for 2024. For a copy of the presentation
in its entirety, see the meeting information packet.
           Mayor Nadolski and Council members thanked Mr. Watson for the information provided tonight and the valuable service
MHO provides to the Ogden community.

Proposed Short Term Rental Ordinance Amendments (continued from
March 12, 2024);
          Council Policy Analyst Burton explained the current proposal is to amend the Ogden Municipal Code to require vacation
rental guests to park on-site, prohibit outside visitors from being on-site during a rental period, require applicants of owner-
occupied rentals to submit three forms of evidence to show proof of residency, to require the applicant of a non-owner-occupied
rental to have owned the unit for three years, and to permit short-term rentals in the C-ENT and H25 zones. The City Council has
discussed the proposal in work sessions. Much of the discussion was about the parking issue that the proposal is trying to address.
At the request of the council, the Planning Division presented other options to address parking, including requiring the owner to
be present during the duration of a rental period. Several council members expressed interest in receiving community feedback
about the issues that the ordinance will address. A FlashVote survey was conducted during the second week of April 2024 to
receive community feedback about issues like parking and parties.
           Kevin Lyons, representing FlashVote, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the results of the
FlashVote survey; he concluded the takeaways are that there are a high number of nuisances related to parking at short term rental
properties and that would be the top issue for the City to address through ordinance amendments and enforcement. There was brief
discussion among the Council and Mr. Lyons regarding the number of responses to the survey and the scientific nature of the
survey. The Council also heard from Planning Manager Brierley and Building Services Manager Tremea regarding the difficulty
in addressing complaints that have been lodged regarding existing parking issues at existing short term rental properties; Planning
staff is seeking better defined parking regulations in order to take enforcement action. The Council Chair explained that the item is
on an upcoming agenda.

General Plan Revisions Update
          Senior Planner Rypien explained the purpose of this agenda item is an opportunity for a kick-off discussion with the
Council regarding the General Plan update.
          Christie Oostema and Megan Moore, representatives of the City’s consulting firm Logan Simpson, led the discussion
with the Council; they used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide a project overview, background on the consultant
team, community engagement plan, and other project background. The General Plan update process is anticipated to take
approximately two years with an estimated completion date of September 2026. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see
the meeting information packet.
          Discussion among the Council and the consultants, as well as Planning staff, centered on the appropriate messaging
surrounding the General Plan update project and the community stakeholders and partners that are key to the success of the
project.
          Vice Chair White disclosed that she is employed with the Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC), which is the grantor
for the project, but she does not have a conflict of interest and can participate in the General Plan update project in an unbiased
manner.

Proposed Utility Revenue Bond Parameters – 36” Canyon Waterline
          Laura Lewis, Lewis Robertson Burningham (LRB), discussed the proposal for the Council to adopt a bond parameters
resolution for the financing of the replacement of the 36-inch Canyon water line. The project will involve several funding sources
and will include proposed water rate increases to pay for the debt service. The project, Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) Project
Number WU003, is estimated to cost approximately $100 million and includes not only the water line in the Ogden Canyon but
the 36-inch concrete lined steel pipe running under Pineview Reservoir from the City’s wellfields as well. The proposed funding
sources include $34.3 million from a State Revolving Loan Fund, $42.6 million from a federal Water Infrastructure Finance and
Innovation Act (WIFIA) loan, $10 million from State appropriations, and $12 million from City American Rescue Plan Act
(ARPA) funding. The bond parameters resolution is required as the City is incurring debt and must comply with state bonding
requirements.
              • Bond Parameters Summary:
                        o Discount – 3 percent
                        o Term – 40 Years
                        o Rates – Not to exceed 6 percent
                                  ▪ State Revolving Loan Fund – 1 percent
                                  ▪ WIFIA Loan – 5 percent
                        o Maximum Par Amount - $82,100,000
                        o Total Estimated Debt Service
                                  ▪ State Loan - $39,965,000
                                  ▪ WIFIA Loan - $75,060,000
          Ms. Lewis concluded that if the Council adopts the parameters resolution, a public hearing will be set for June 11, 2024,
with closing on the bond scheduled for late June or early July.

Council Business
         Financial Principles: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss
the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2025 financial principles; the presentation focused on the substantive changes to the financial
principles document when compared to the FY2024 document:
             • Changing the consideration of a property tax increase from every other year to an option of every year.
             • A statement that a Five-Year Financial Strategic Plan will be completed in FY25.
             • Changing the language regarding minimum operating reserves from setting a goal of 20 percent to maintaining
                  20 percent.
             • Minor changes in grammar and punctuation.
         For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Discussion among the Council and Council staff centered on the State-defined process the City must follow when
considering a property tax increase.
