      Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas informed the Council of the need to update the noise
      ordinance. City Manager Nathan Cherpeski expressed confusion over the current
      enforcement mechanisms, noting that police officers struggle to cite noise violations

      updates, given changes made by the county over the years without City Council input. City
      Manager Cherpeski proposed developing a more tailored ordinance that the City Council
      could adjust based on their preferences, allowing for stricter or more lenient regulations as

      2. Administrative Reports
          2.1. Discussion Regarding Proposed Policy Concerning Acceptance of Transfer of
                Private Infrastructure to Public – Bryce Terry, City Engineer
      City Engineer Bryce Terry acknowledged the longstanding deliberations on instances where
      HOAs or resident groups petitioned the City Council to convert private roads or other
      infrastructure to public ownership. Based on previous Council discussions and community
      input, City Engineer Terry outlined a draft policy intended to formalize the criteria and
      process for such transfers. The policy sought to balance the City's fiscal responsibility and
      liability concerns with the community's desire for improved services and infrastructure.

      City Engineer Terry emphasized the necessity for residents or HOAs to demonstrate majority
      support for the transfer, either through HOA governance mechanisms or unanimous consent
      in non-HOA scenarios. He indicated this initial step was crucial as it set the groundwork for
      subsequent assessments and considerations. The policy further required applicants to
      convey the infrastructure to the City either through dedication or easement, depending on
      the type of infrastructure involved.

      The core of the policy lay in the third point, where a detailed condition assessment would
      identify deficiencies compared to city standards. This assessment was pivotal, especially for
      infrastructure like roads lacking in sidewalks, proper width, or curb and gutter. City Engineer
May 22, 2024 – City Council Minutes                                                   Page 3 of 10


      Terry said that bringing such infrastructure up to city standards could be financially onerous,
      necessitating comprehensive planning and community consensus.

      Moreover, the policy mandated that applicants bear the responsibility for rectifying identified
      deficiencies or providing funding for city-led improvements. Concerns were raised about
      scenarios where HOAs might dissolve or face financial insolvency, leaving unresolved
      questions about ongoing maintenance responsibilities and city obligations.

      Councilmember Ohrn asked what would happen if an HOA disbands. City Manager Cherpeski
      responded that staff are actively researching the City’s obligations. The Council
      acknowledged the complexity of balancing community desires for enhanced services with the
      financial and logistical burdens on the City.

          2.2. Discussion of the proposed fiscal year 2025 and 2026 capital improvement plan
               (CIP) and proposed capital equipment projects – Kyle Maurer, Director of Finance
               and Administrative Services
      Finance Director Kyle Maurer emphasized the financial flexibility and liquidity of the City’s
      current situation, particularly in relation to ongoing projects. He offered a detailed overview
      of the proposed fiscal year 2025 to 2026 capital improvement plan, highlighting both capital
      equipment and projects. Director Maurer provided an extensive breakdown, starting with
      distributing handouts outlining projects proposed for the next two fiscal years, along with a
      detailed packet concerning bond projects and their financial obligations and options.

      Director Maurer outlined the goals for the session, which include reviewing and discussing
      the proposed project list, specifically addressing the inclusion of a capital projects bond in
      the tentative budget. Maurer encouraged discussion on Council support, potential issues, and
      additional information needed. He indicated the direction of future steps concerning capital
      projects would be contingent on the outcome of this discussion. The overview revealed a
      total of approximately $72 million dollars in projects, with clarification that some projects
      would be funded by grants while others would rely on the proposed bond.

      Director Maurer proceeded to delve into specific projects slated for fiscal year 2025, seeking
      input from Engineering and Community Development staff present to address any queries.
      He highlighted projects including road widenings, traffic signals, and various infrastructure
      improvements, with emphasis on the time-sensitive nature of certain projects due to pending
      agreements with developers. He noted the funding mechanisms, such as impact fees and
      reimbursement agreements, for each project to illustrate the financial strategy behind the
      proposed capital plan.

      Director Maurer continued the discussion with a comprehensive review of park and
      recreation projects, encompassing everything from park expansions and trail improvements
      to playground replacements and open space enhancements. He stressed the necessity of
May 22, 2024 – City Council Minutes                                                    Page 4 of 10


      updating the parks and trails master plan due to the high growth rate, with specific allocations
      detailed for each initiative. Director Maurer invited questions from the Council,
      acknowledging the volume of information presented and allowing time for general comments
      before final decisions are made on the proposed projects.

      Councilmember Hodges asked if the traffic signal at the intersection of Mountain View
      Corridor and Real Vista would receive financial relief through state participation. City
      Engineer Bryce Terry responded in the affirmative; however, noted the City would have to
      cover the cost to widen and stripe the road.

      Director Maurer stressed the need to prioritize projects given current financial constraints.
      He emphasized the forthcoming recommendations to streamline the Capital Improvement
      Plan (CIP) to align with available funding and staff capacity. The Council shared concerns
      about specific projects, including the overdue upgrades for 5600 West, which had been
      postponed due to impending infrastructure work by Jordan Valley Water Conservancy
      District. City Engineer Terry clarified that the reconstruction of the road would coincide with
      the water line installation to optimize resources and minimize disruption.

      Councilmember Ohrn raised another persistent issue regarding Gina Road, advocating for its
      upgrade, citing its frequent need for maintenance. City Manager Nathan Cherpeski
      acknowledged the challenges and the financial limitations for improvements and highlighted
      the City's commitment to evaluate and possibly defer projects to manage financial strains.
      He stressed the need for a realistic approach, possibly scaling down project scopes to fit
      within allocated budgets. The Council discussed fiscal implications and expressed concern
      about the sustainability of relying on impact fees amidst unpredictable revenue trends.
      Director Maurer interjected with financial insights, detailing the bonding capacity and annual
      debt service obligations. He projected a manageable annual bond payment of approximately
      $2.1 million over 20 years, leveraging impact fees to cover costs where applicable. The
      Council acknowledged the need for strategic financial planning, especially with pending
      infrastructure needs like the public works and public safety buildings.

      Director Maurer offered an overview of fund balances across various city projects, showing
      the use of reserves to fund capital projects. He invited feedback from the Council on the
      proposed projects and financial strategies. Councilmember Ohrn expressed frustration over
      perpetual funding challenges for essential infrastructure like roads, despite the city's growth
      and development needs. City Manager Cherpeski reassured the Council of ongoing efforts to
      seek alternative funding sources and optimize project timelines. He reaffirmed the City's
      commitment to prioritizing essential projects while managing financial prudence. Director
      Maurer echoed the sentiment, highlighting the upcoming revision of the CIP to align with
      available resources and project priorities. He relayed his desire to publish the CIP plan to the
      residents to offer a more realistic timeline when projects would commence.

         2.3. Discussion on the fiscal year 2025 and 2026 budgets – Kyle Maurer, Director of
              Finance and Administrative Services
      Finance Director offered a detailed review of the proposed budgets for fiscal years 2025 and
      2026, alongside considerations for market adjustments. Councilmember Ohrn expressed
      appreciation for the efficiency of staff, particularly Finance Director Maurer, for his swift
      response to resident inquiries earlier.

      Councilmember Hodges raised concerns about the proposed salary increases for the Council.
      He expressed reservations about the timing of these adjustments, citing ongoing budgetary
      constraints and recent cuts to city events as reasons for caution. He noted while he
      understood the rationale behind market-driven salary adjustments, he believed it might be
      prudent to delay such increases until a more financially stable period, potentially after the
      next election cycle. The Council shared varying perspectives on balancing fiscal
      responsibility with the need to attract and retain qualified personnel.

      The Council acknowledged the demands of the job, including significant time commitments
      beyond what their compensation might reflect. Councilmember Hodges indicated this was
      the reasoning to take a more cautious approach to budgetary decisions, ensuring that any
      adjustments aligned with both fiscal prudence and equitable compensation practices.

      Director Maurer outlined the timeline for the public hearing on the proposed budget
      adjustments.

      8. Public Hearing
         8.1. Public Hearing to Receive Public Comment on the Tentative Fiscal Years 2025 and
              2026 City of Herriman Operating and Capital Budgets – Kyle Maurer, Director of
              Finance and Administrative Services
      Finance Director Maurer offered an overview of the fiscal year budgets for 2025 and 2026,
      encompassing bother operational and capital bond budgets for the City. He acknowledged
      the intricate nature of the bond issuance processes and highlighted the reliance on expert
      guidance to navigate complexities effectively.

      Director Maurer transitioned to a detailed presentation focusing specifically on the general
      fund aspects of the budget. He emphasized that while fiscal year 2024 had seen some
      unexpected revenue increases, particularly in class C road funds, projections for 2025
      anticipated relatively stable revenues with a conservative estimate of a 2% increase in taxes.
      He observed the volatility of sales tax revenues in recent months and the City's cautious
      approach to budgeting to avoid potential future shortfalls.

      Regarding expenditures, Director Maurer outlined that a significant portion—41%—of the
      budget was allocated to personnel costs, reflecting the City's labor-intensive operations. He
      explained the planned transfers between funds, including adjustments due to the completion
      of debt payments related to a future public works property.

      Looking ahead to fiscal year 2026, Director Maurer highlighted anticipated challenges such
      as potential insurance rate increases and adjustments in retirement funds. He emphasized
      the City's commitment to infrastructure investment, particularly in road maintenance and
      pavement management, as evidenced by the planned increases in funding for public works
      and parks departments. He asked if there were any questions. There were none.

      10. Discussion and Action Items
          10.1. Discussion and consideration of a request to amend the Herriman City Official
                Zoning Map for ±2.74 acres of property located at 12650 S 6000 West and 12664
                S 6000 West from A-1-43 Single Family Agricultural Zone to A-1-10 Single Family
                Agricultural Zone. (File No. Z2024-019) – Michael Maloy, City Planner
      City Planner Michael Maloy presented the request to amend the Herriman City Official
      Zoning Map for 2.74 acres of property located at 12650 South 6000 West and 12664 South
      6000 West. The proposal sought to change the zoning designation from A-1-43 single-family
      agricultural to A-1-10 single-family agricultural, potentially allowing for the subdivision of the
      two parcels into up to five lots. City Planner Maloy outlined that the zoning amendment aligns
      with the neighborhood one designation of the general plan, which anticipates a maximum
      density of 2.5 units per acre. He indicated that the Planning Commission had thoroughly
      reviewed the request and found it compliant with all relevant standards, with unanimous
      support for approval.

      agricultural property, emphasizing that vinyl fencing might not be suitable for agricultural
