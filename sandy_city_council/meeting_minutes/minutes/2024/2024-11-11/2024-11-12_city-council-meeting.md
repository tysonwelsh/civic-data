1.         24-398                 City Council receiving an update from the Wasatch Front Waste and
                                  Recycling District
                   Attachments:   Presentation

                                  Pam Roberts with Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD), provided a
                                  report and review of the organization. WFWRD mission is to provide sustainable quality
                                  integrated waste and recycling collection services for the health and safety of the
                                  community. She reviewed employee satisfaction and engagement, and the organization's
                                  employee leadership development program. WFWRD has implemented an apprentice
                                  program to train new drivers for their CDL. They recently added ten new trucks to their
                                  fleet. She reviewed the services provided to Sandy residents in 2024 (through
                                  September), environmental stewardship, and curbside recycling. Ms. Roberts reviewed
                                  the district's annual budget history and financial projections for future years. She spoke
                                  about the impact of higher costs which has necessitated the need for an increase in fees
                                  charged to homeowners. The WFWRD Board held a special meeting in October to review
                                  fee increases and on October 28th tentatively adopted the tentative budget which
                                  included a fee increase of $6.50 per month. There will be a Public Hearing held on
                                  November 18th at 6:00 pm to allow for public comment on the Tentative 2025 budget at
                                  the WFWRD offices located at 604 West 6960 South in Midvale. Council questions and
                                  comments followed.

2.         24-401                 Fire Department requesting a discussion on the staffing of a 4th
                                  ambulance
                   Attachments:   Fourth Ambulance presentation - Updated 11.7.24

                                  Fire Chief Ryan McConaghie spoke about a proposed staffing increase to run a fourth
                                  ambulance. The department is requesting three additional people to staff the fourth
                                  ambulance. A total of 6 staff people are required to run the additional ambulance service.
                                  The city received a grant which covers the cost of three staff, and an additional three are
                                  needed to properly staff the fourth ambulance. Call volume has increased by
                                  approximately 38% to 52%, which has necessitated the request for to staff and run a
                                  fourth ambulance. Ambulance transports increased in July 2024 by 42% compared to the
                                  average transports from 2023. He spoke about the impact of the MVP Shelter on
                                  ambulance service. Chief McConaghie reviewed the costs associated with adding three
                                  new employees and the additional fleet costs of running a fourth ambulance. He also
                                  reviewed the recommendation form the PCG Audit report which included adding a fourth
                                  full-time staffed ambulance. Council questions and comments followed.

                                  Council moved to General Citizen Comment on the Agenda.

3.         CA08222024 Amendments to Title 15 and Title 21 of the Municipal Code related to Short
           -0006816   Term Rentals
           (CC WS)
                   Attachments:   Staff Report and Exhibits
                                  Business Licensing Exhibits
                                  Planning Commission Draft Minutes 10-17-2024
                                  STR Presentation

                                  Melissa Anderson, Community Development, presented proposed amendments to Title
                                  15 and 21 of the Sandy Municipal Code related to Short Term Rentals. She provided an
                                  overview of the changes and the financial impact of the code changes. Changes include
                                  requiring both the owner and the renter to sign an agreement to adhere to the code
                                  standards and also requires the owners to complete a STR Management Certification
                                  class prior to applying for a STR permit. Parking plan standards were clarified. Additional
                                  requirements included improved noise control standards, maintaining an adequate
                                  number of operational smoke and carbon monoxide detectors, and implementing a
                                  parking plan. The Planning Commission forwarded a positive recommendation with some
                                  suggestions and code clarifications. Council questions and comments followed. Ms.
                                  Anderson provided additional clarifications on the code and impact of the changes. Legal
                                  office will follow up on questions from the Council. James Sorensen and Ms. Anderson
                                  responded to Council questions. Dustin Fratto and Tracy Cowdell provided some
                                  background information on prior year's Council discussions on short term rentals.

4.         24-397                 Community Development Department presenting Section 7 (Targeted
                                  Growth Plan) of the draft Pace of Progress: Sandy General Plan 2050.
                   Attachments:   Council Presentation-General Plan Section 7
                                  Section 7-Targeted Growth Plan (10/21/24 draft)
                                  Planning Commission Staff Report (10/17/2024)
                                  Exhibit H (revised)-Other Substantive Changes (10.18.24).pdf

                                  Council discussed the option to push back this evening's presentation on Section 7 of the
                                  draft Pace of Progress: Sandy General Plan 2050 to allow Council Members more time to
                                  review the plan or to proceed with the presentation. After the discussion, Council
                                  proceeded with the presentation.

                                  Jake Warner, Community Development, introduced Erika Chmielewski with GSBS
                                  Architects who led the presentation of the draft general plan. The focus on this evening's
                                  presentation is Section 7: Targeted Growth Plan which included a review of Land Use
                                  and Future Land Use Map and Annexation Policy Plan. She reviewed the Utah State
                                  requirements of the General Plan and the Future Land Use Map (FLUM) with the Council.
                                  The FLUM provides a vision for the long-term use of land, often looking decades ahead
                                  and reflects the city's goals for development, density, and overall growth patterns, aiming
                                  to inform decision-making over time. Ms. Chmielewski reviewed the two-year process of
                                  gathering feedback, analysis and community engagement which resulted in the
                                  development of the FLUM. The FLUM can be considered as an executive summary to the
                                  General Plan. She reviewed FLUM methodology, specifically areas of stability and of
                                  potential future change. She provided information detailing current and proposed
                                  structures for each of the various designated areas on the FLUM. Estimated future
                                  population of Sandy is 103,596 which could result in the addition of over 6,100 new units
                                  of housing in the city. Land Use Designation are broad categories that describe the
                                  intended use and character of areas within the city and sets general expectations for the
                                  types of land use, density, and overall character the city aims to achieve in the future in
                                  these areas. Ms. Chmielewski reviewed the density standards, priorities and goals for
                                  low, moderate, and high density neighborhoods, commerce areas, and transition
                                  corridors. Council questions occurred throughout the presentation. Staff responded to
                                  questions regarding concerns related to zoning needs.

                                  Ms. Chmielewski continued the presentation and provided an overview of the Annexation
                                  Policy contained in the draft General Plan. In 2024, HB 330 was passed by the
                                  legislature which will result in the automatic annexation of unincorporated islands within
                                  or adjacent to larger municipalities by July 2027. She spoke about the impact to Sandy
                                  as a result of these annexations. She reviewed with the Council annexation decision
                                  making criteria and provided a map detailing the unincorporated areas within or adjacent
                                  to Sandy City. Council discussion, comments, and questions followed.

                                  Jake Warner and the consultant are available for questions. Please reach out to them any
                                  time with questions or comments. The review of the draft general plan will continue at
                                  next week's council meeting.
