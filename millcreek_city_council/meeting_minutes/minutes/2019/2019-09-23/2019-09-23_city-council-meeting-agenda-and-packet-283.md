1. Discussion on Proposed Millcreek Code Chapter 9.65 Regarding Shopping Carts
Kurt Hansen said the need for this ordinance came from code enforcement because of the
abandoned shopping carts throughout the City. Hansen went through the recommended code
changes that said if someone was using a shopping cart outside of the owner’s property,
written permission would be required. The shopping cars would also have identification on
them. There are exceptions to the ordinance which would entail security measures to ensure
the carts are not stolen or the owner may have a retrieval service collecting the abandoned
shopping carts every day. Mayor Silvestrini said the ordinance was modeled after South
Jordan’s ordinance. Hansen said code enforcement would be allowed to pick up abandoned

shopping carts and charge the owner a fee in addition to other measures. Council Member
Marchant asked how the stores (shopping cart owners) felt about the proposed ordinance.
Mayor Silvestrini said he would like the ordinance to be vetted through the Millcreek
Business Council and he would like the grocery stores’ input. Mike Winder said he could
reach out to the individual businesses who use shopping carts. Council Member Jackson asked
if there was a particular area with a problem. Hansen said a lot of carts end up on the west side
of the City. Mayor Silvestrini said most carts have identification on them and wondered if
code enforcement could report the missing carts to the stores. He said he was interested in
working out a plan with the stores instead of regulating carts. Council Member Catten said she
did not want to punish the stores when the customers were removing them from the property.
She would also like input from the grocery stores. The Council suggested that Hansen look for
an alternative solution before they would pass an ordinance regulating shopping carts.

2. Discussion on Proposed Millcreek Code Chapter 14.50 Dedication of Private Streets to
Public Ownership
Kurt Hansen said instances have come up about dedication of private streets to public
ownership and the City does not have a code regulating it. If a resident has a private street and
they do not want to maintain it anymore, this ordinance provides a process for the City to take
on that maintenance. Hansen went through the process. Mayor Silvestrini questioned the fee
of $115 being adequate. Francis Lilly said any application that goes to the City Council costs
the applicant $115. Mayor Silvestrini said the street would have to meet city standards before
the City took ownership, though it may make sense to accept a street that does not meet
standards. Hansen said the code offered the Mayor discretion on the requirements. Lilly said
the Transportation Master Plan offers variability in street requirements. Council Member
Jackson inquired about an assessment of roads in relation to snow plowing and garbage truck
access. Lilly said new developments require 24 feet of asphalt plus curb, gutter, and sidewalk.
Council Member Uipi asked about an inventory of private roads in the City. Lilly said there
was not one, but there are hundreds of private streets in the City. Hansen said he would build
some flexibility into the proposed ordinance and bring it back to the Council. Council
Member Uipi asked about a designated fund to support the applications. Mayor Silvestrini
said it would create a budgetary impact, but the Council did not have to accept the roads.

Mayor Silvestrini welcomed the students of Jean Massieu School of the Deaf to City

A PROCLAMATION RECOGNIZING THE 207! ANNIVERSARY OF THE
JEAN MASSIEU SCHOOL OF THE DEAF

WHEREAS, the Jean Massieu School of the Deaf was founded in 1999 as a charter school by the local
Deaf Community in the Salt Lake City area; and

WHEREAS, in 2010 moved to its current Millcreek location, in order to better support students in
American Sign Language and English; and

WHEREAS, the Jean Massieu School merged with the Utah Schools for the Deaf and Blind to align both
organization's missions to best support the needs of deaf/hard-of-hearing children throughout all of
Utah; and

WHEREAS, the Jean Massieu School is committed to promoting and fostering a fully accessible
learning environment with the goal of being proficient in both languages - American Sign Language
and English; and

WHEREAS, the Jean Massieu School serves deaf/hard-of-hearing students preschool through post-high
school. The Jean Massieu School is a fully accessible setting that strives to develop a sense of identity
in which students feel they can be proud of who they are as Deaf/Hard-of-hearing individuals.

BE IT KNOWN, that the Mayor and City Council of Millcreek do hereby recognize the Jean Massieu
School of the Deaf as a Valued Community Partner this, Twenty-Third Day of September Two
Thousand Nineteen, and declare this Jean Massieu School of the Deaf Day.

IN WITNESS THEREOF, I have set my hand and caused to be affixed the Great Seal of Millcreek this
23" day of September 2019.

3. Financial Matters
3.1 Public Hearing on Opening and Amending the 2019-2020 Fiscal Year Budget
Laurie Johnson went over general fund revenues which included an increase in Class C
Road funds based on what was received this year and Promise Program donations. There
was an increase in the use of fund balance for the UPD contract and EnerGov software.
The total amount of change in the General Fund budget was an increase of $677,568.
Johnson then went through the individual expense budget changes which reflected the
organizational changes with departments.

The Mayor and Council’s budget did not change, and the City Manager budget had a
minor change to cover personnel costs. The Justice Court budget was increased by
$210,000 to cover actual costs. The Legal Services budget decreased some due to moving
an employee to the Legislative Services budget and increased some to cover Salt Lake
County contract costs. The Public Safety budget costs were estimated at $10.4 million
and the contract ended being $10.7 million. The increase covers the UPD contract and
alcohol tax grant increase. Mayor Silvestrini stated that Millcreek is sending alcohol
related fund to UPD for alcohol related enforcement. John Geilmann stated the money is
earmarked for the Millcreek precinct. The Recorder budget reduced by $65,000 due to no
primary election this year. Building Services is a new department and came out of what
used to be City Services. The Communication and Programs budget increased due to the
Communication Outreach Specialist being moved from the Human Resources budget
since that employee is no longer doing risk management. Johnson said the Emergency
Management position may be evolving due to a vacancy. The Information Center budget
increased due to personnel costs with position title changes. EnerGov is a line item and
increased the IT Management budget by $250,000. Professional IT services reduced by
$25,000 and equipment costs were reduced by $10,000. Mayor Silvestrini asked how IT
professional services was reduced. Johnson stated the Information Center Manager could
do the day-to-day duties thereby reducing the cost of the professional contract. The
Economic Development budget increased slightly under miscellaneous and personnel
expenses. The Business License Administration budget increased slightly due to salaries
and personnel, and the fee study was moved to this fiscal year from last year. The
Finance Department budget experienced no change. The Human Resources budget
experienced a change with an employee moving to a different department. The Workers
Comp Insurance was increased to cover costs. Legislative Policy is a new department but
still covers some of the previous city services. The Salt Lake County Parks contract was
moved to the Public Works budget. No change was made to the Facilities or Fleet budget.
The Planning and Zoning Department was renamed from Community Development. The
Promise Program donations were added to that budget by $826; the total Promise

3.3 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-31, Adopting Tax-Exempt Bonds
and Continuing Disclosure Compliance Procedures

Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek has a reporting obligation for the municipal bond that
Millcreek issued. It requires Millcreek to report to the Securities Exchange Commission
about any material changes in Millcreek’s finances that could affect the City’s ability to
repay the bond. In order to guide Council and staff, the underwriter and bond counsel
recommended that Millcreek adopt compliance procedures for reporting. The compliance
officer will be the Finance Director. He said staff and Council Members should report
anything they see that could affect the bond.

Council Member Marchant moved to approve Resolution 19-31, Adopting Tax-Exempt
Bonds and Continuing Disclosure Compliance Procedures. Council Member Uipi seconded.
The City Recorder called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.

4.4 Unified Police Department Report

Detective Christian Thompson said there were 2,906 calls for service in August 2019 and
those calls generated 1,163 cases, 462 citations, and 62 booking arrests. The crime report
for August included: 49 assaults, 25 burglaries, 25 drug offenses, 79 family offenses, 1
homicide, 0 kidnappings, 123 larcenies, 1 robbery, 4 sexual offenses, and 23 stolen
vehicles. He said the Patrol Unit had Sergeant Clay Hansen transfer to Millcreek Precinct
from Taylorsville. Officer Graf located a suspicious vehicle and an arrest was made on a
felony fugitive. The Street Crimes Unit had detectives conduct a controlled drug buy in
which three suspects were arrested and a drug house was shut down. The Community
Oriented Policing Unit did a bar check and all bars were in compliance. They have also
been working on Eliminating Alcohol Sale to Youth (EASY) operations, working with
local businesses and schools on dealing with shoplifters and active shooters, and spoke to
students at the Fairland Preschool about law enforcement. He said officers had been
patrolling school zones since school had started. Council Member Marchant said crossing
guards have expressed appreciation for UPD patrolling the school zones.
