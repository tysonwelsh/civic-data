2.     PUBLIC HEARINGS

       A.      'RIVERTON COVE AMENDED SUBDIVISION,' PLZ-25-1004, AN
               APPLICATION TO AMEND RIVERTON COVE SUBDIVISION BY
               CHANGING SEVEN (7) REAR LOT LINES, AMENDING LOTS 1-3, 24-29,
               PARCEL B, AND PARCEL G. APPLICANT - ISABEL JONES.

City Planner Tim Prestwich, presented the Staff Report and stated that Riverton Cove is
an infill subdivision located west of City Hall. It was the first infill subdivision to utilize the
RM-6 Zone. Mr. Prestwich reported that the applicant, Isabel Jones, obtained affidavits
from all impacted property owners and the Homeowners Association (“HOA”) to pursue
the application on their behalf. Notices were also sent to all property owners within 300
feet of the subject property and posted on the City website, and no comments were
received.

The amendment will change the rear lot lines to increase the size of Lots 1, 2, 3, 24, 25,
26, 27, 28, 29, and decrease the size of HOA-owned Parcels B and G. It would not affect
the shared community parking spaces, storm drain ponds, or fencing. All proposed lot
sizes meet City ordinance, and Staff recommended approval. If approved, the next step
would be to obtain the notarized signatures of all lot owners on one mylar and then submit
the Amended Plat for recordation.

Chair Park asked if the modified Parcel G would still meet all requirements for stormwater
detention. Mr. Prestwich stated that Staff worked with the applicant’s engineer to obtain
drawings showing the contours to ensure that there would be no changes to the pond

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Rushton, Mr. Prestwich confirmed that

Commissioner Matheson asked if the property owners were purchasing the land from the

owners.

Ms. Jones displayed photographs of Parcels B and G and read from her statement, which
was included in the Meeting Packet. She lives on Lot 24 and looks forward to seeing a
beautifully landscaped yard instead of weeds.

Chair Park opened the public hearing.

Curtis Hansen reported that he lives to the south of Ms. Jones. His family has lived in the
home for two years, and in that time, the land has been neglected. He and his neighbors
look forward to the opportunity to beautify it and make better use of their backyards.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Rushton asked if the condition of the parcels was discussed during the
handoff from the developer to the HOA. Mr. Carter explained that any interaction between
the developer and homeowners is outside the City’s purview. The community’s
Covenants, Conditions, and Restrictions (“CC&Rs”) usually state that the developer’s
involvement in management of the HOA ends when a certain percentage of lots have
been sold. If the developer pledged to make improvements to the parcels, it would have
been included in the sales documents. Verbal agreements are unenforceable and, as in
this case, homeowners must decide how to proceed. HOAs have an ongoing
maintenance obligation on leftover open parcels, and all homeowners in the subdivision
were obligated to share those maintenance costs even though many of them did not have
access to the property. He believed the amendment would resolve that issue.

Commissioner Cluff moved that the Planning Commission APPROVE PLZ-25-1004,

      B.      ‘PALATERIA LA ROSA,’ PLZ- 25-2039, AN APPLICATION FOR A
              CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR A FOOD TRAILER WHICH WILL
              SELL   FROZEN   TREATS    AT   OTHER   LOCATIONS,   AND
              OCCASIONALLY PARK BEHIND A BACK YARD GATE WHILE AT THE
              HOME, LOCATED AT 13259 SOUTH 2480 WEST. APPLICANT -
              CHRISTIAN ALTAMIRANO.

Planner Lisa Halversen, reported that the applicant purchased a food truck to sell
popsicles and frozen treats. Because they wish to store the truck at their home, a CUP
will be required. City Code prohibits selling in the City for more than 90 consecutive days
and forbids selling in residential areas, so the CUP would only allow parking. The
application could not move forward because a letter of permission from the property
owner had not been received. Staff requested that the item be continued to the next
meeting.

Commissioner Keele moved that the Planning Commission CONTINUE PLZ-25-
2039, “PALATERIA LA ROSA”, to the September 25, 2025, Planning Commission
meeting. Commissioner Cluff seconded the motion. The motion passed with the
unanimous consent of the Commission.

3.    DECISION ITEMS

      A.      'MOUNTAIN RIDGE PHASE 28,' PLZ-25-8011, AN APPLICATION FOR A
              FINAL/DETAIL  PLAN   FOR     53    RESIDENTIAL    UNITS  ON
              APPROXIMATELY 5.96-ACRES LOCATED AT 13620 SOUTH 4395
              WEST. APPLICANT - BRANDON PARR, EDGE HOMES.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and reviewed the Phasing Plan. The subject
property is on the west side of Riverton, south of 13400 South and east of Mountain View.
Mountain Ridge East is the block-scale plan on the east side of Sentinel Ridge Boulevard.
The west side is built out. Permits have been issued for Mountain Ridge Apartments and
construction is scheduled to begin. The Planning Commission previously considered
Phases 26A, 26B, and 27. The project was specifically phased to provide adequate
circulation and limit the number of required temporary cul-de-sacs.

The park was in process. The City Council had approved some amenities, and the plans
were in Staff review prior to being presented to the Planning Commission. Mr. Prestwich
noted that completion of the park was not required during a specific phase of
development, but the developer was obligated to complete it.

The Site Plan and Subdivision Plat were reviewed. Phase 28 will include 44 townhomes
and nine single-family lots. The townhome units will face the public streets and have
front-loading garages. Some units in a previous phase had rear-loading garages. Staff
reviewed the application and all departments recommended approval.

In response to a question from Chair Park, Mr. Prestwich clarified that the developer can
choose to construct Phases 29 through 31 prior to completing the park, but he did not
believe that was their intention because completed amenities help home sales. It will be
a public park, and the master developer is contractually obligated to complete it.

Brandon Watson spoke on behalf of Edge Homes. The park will be 10 acres and have
many amenities, including Edge Homes’ first splash pad. Design is underway, and they
plan to begin construction as soon as possible. The Master Development Agreement
obligates them to construct the park, and Impact Fees will be utilized to cover the costs.
They are in the process of constructing Greenford Lane, which will contain all utilities for
both the park and Phase 28. Their goal is for it to be paved before winter.

Chair Park asked about the occupancy rate for the completed portions of the
development. Mr. Watson stated that development is beginning on the east side of
Sentinel Ridge Boulevard, and they are pleased with the commercial development on the
west side of the road. He did not have specific numbers but estimated that the homes on
the west side are at 90% to 95% occupancy.
