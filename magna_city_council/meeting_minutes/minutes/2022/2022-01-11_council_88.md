Ordinance

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to consider proposed
amendments to Magna Municipal Code regarding a Noise Pollution Control Ordinance.

                Paul Ashton reviewed the ordinance, whereby input was sought from different
cities in putting it together, and it was submitted to Chief Del Craig and the police to get their
comments. The ordinance addresses an issue the Magna Metro Township has had for many
years.

               Council Member Prokopis, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to open the
public hearing. No vote was taken.

               No one spoke in favor of or in opposition to the ordinance.

               Council Member Prokopis, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to close the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

               Chief Del Craig stated the Magna Precinct has been dealing with noise situations
in the community where it has responded multiple times, but either could not get a decibel meter
or could not meet the noise level with a decibel meter, so it was unable to take action. The Magna
Precinct needs a new noise ordinance that is manageable, workable, and enforceable, and that
has standards the police can assess and gives citizens guidance.

The precinct did reach out to other communities to look at what they were crafting and found a
model it liked. That has been incorporated in this draft. He worked with Mr. Ashton to make sure
it was reasonable under the law and constitutional, as well as enforceable. Then, the ordinance
was distributed to police officers, and they have made some suggestions. He asked that this not
be approved for a couple of weeks to allow time for more review and to go over the concerns of
the police officers with Mr. Ashton.

              Council Member Barney stated he wanted to make sure the Council was not
creating an ordinance based off an individual circumstance and instead was creating a more
                                              3
DATE            TUESDAY                               JANUARY                               11, 2022


universal ordinance. One resident was concerned with the timeframes for the noise, such as
using a snow blower early in the morning.

                Chief Craig stated that was one of the concerns raised by the police officers. This
gives a certain time frame on snow removal, with an exception for commercial snow removal that
is 800 feet from a dwelling. The general noise ordinance applies from 10:00 PM to 7:00 AM, and
this ordinance covers impulsive noises.

               Council Member Prokopis stated this does address a deficiency in the ordinance
a citizen brought to light, but it was not created just to address that situation. It addresses a need
throughout the community.

              Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Pierce, moved to table action
on the ordinance until the next meeting. The motion passed unanimously.

Community Structure Plan for the Mahogany Ridge Development

                 Melissa Anderson, Planning Manager, Municipal Services District (MSD),
reviewed the Community Plan for Mahogany Ridge Development located on approximately 79
acres of land at approximately 8230 West on the north side of 4100 South. The Magna Metro
Township recently approved this area as a Planned Community (PC) Zone. This next step of the
process is to approve a more detailed plan, which must be consistent with the original approved
plan. The original plan had a range of residential units between 408 to 1,816, and the developer
is now proposing 860 residential units and 250,000 square feet of commercial and industrial
space. All the required elements necessary were submitted to the Magna Planning Commission
for review. The land uses are consistent with the original PC zoning, and the transportation
system and infrastructure were adequately addressed. The Magna Planning Commission has
recommended approval of the Community Structure Plan. The Municipal Services District staff
also recommends approval with two conditions: 1) that the applicant resolve a remaining technical
issue, including reviewer comments prior to or with each subdivision submittal; and 2) that in the
future, the traffic input study be changed if needed.

               Mayor Peay asked about the restrictions in this area.

             Paul Ashton stated the designs will have to comply with the Magna Metro
Township’s Overpressure Zone ordinance.

            Council Member Hull asked if there was a solution for the road going out onto
8400 West. People can only make left turns onto that road unless a barrier is put in down the
middle.

                 Nick Jeppson, Representative, Ivory Development, stated the road through the
project is called Cordero Drive, and it will tie in where Little Valley ties into 8400 West. The
intention is for that to become a new signal location. In addition to having to comply with the PC
Zone and overpressure zones, Ivory Development will also have to comply with a development
agreement.

                Council Member Barney stated the original plan specified 10,000 square foot lots
for the single-family residences. He asked if that was maintained in this new plan.

feet, and all the structural elements are in the development agreement. There are two different

Fee Waiver Request for the Salt Lake Track Club Winter Series

                Paul Ashton reviewed a request from the Salt Lake Track Club to waive the fees
for the Winter Series races out by Saltair. The fee is $500 per event, and there will be three races,
so the total waiver request is $1,500. These events bring many people into the community.

              Council Member Pierce asked what safety considerations were being required
due to the ongoing pandemic.

               A representative of Salt Lake Track Club stated normal events that are held
inside the building after the races, i.e., the award ceremony, raffles will not be held inside this
year. Everything will be posted outside the building instead. The building will only be open for
use of the restrooms. Masks will be required for anyone who goes inside. Masks will also be
required by the runners at the start and finish of each race.

               Council Member Pierce recommended bystanders be required to wear masks
outside too.

               Council Member Barney asked what the $500 paid for, and if the Council was
charging a fee that was unnecessary if it waives this every year.

                Mr. Ashton stated the Council adopts a consolidated fee schedule every year, and
this falls under “Special Event Fee.” It is used by the Municipal Services District (MSD) for
administrative costs. Just because the Council has waived the fee in the past, does not mean it
has to waive it again this year. There are a number of special events the Council does not waive
the fee for. However, it is easier administratively to impose the fee on everyone except those
entities that come and petition for a waiver, and they have to justify a waiver.

              Council Member Barney stated by picking and choosing who to waive fees for,
the Council may run into favoritism and discrimination.

              Mr. Ashton stated the Council has not waived that many fees. The fees it has
waived are almost always for non-profits.

                Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Barney, moved to waive the
fees for the Salt Lake Track Club for its Winter Series in the amount of $1,500. The motion passed
unanimously.
