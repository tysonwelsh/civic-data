Unified Police Department

                Chief Del Craig stated most recently, there was a drive-by shooting into an elderly
person’s house. The shot was gang-related and it was intended for someone other than the
person whose house was shot. The UPD served search warrants on two residences in Midvale
City and one in West Valley City with the help of the Metro Gang Unit and other officers. The
operation involved about 50-60 officers who hit the houses simultaneously. The goal was to
recover the firearms in the shooting, and the officers did recover the firearm used in the first
shooting, but not the firearm used in a second shooting. The UPD is seeing an increase in gang
activity, primarily coming off the 7200 West corridor. That activity is tied throughout the valley.
Young people are recruited by well-established gangs that have a network in the valley and
sometimes beyond. Most gang crime is gang on gang, but gang members are also involved in
drugs and other issues, which the young recruitments get involved with.

Tomorrow, the UPD will be doing a traffic blitz in the Magna Metro Township primarily focused on
8400 West and will have the motor squad here. This has been done in the past, and last time,
about 60-80 citations were issued. The goal is to get people to comply with the law. The UPD
will be doing some enforcement on Magna Main as well. If there are any areas residents would
like to have the UPD work on, he asked that they give him a call.

                Council Member Barney asked if the Council could take action to address some
of the parking issues in Magna so they could be enforced.

             Chief Craig stated parking is under criminal law, and under criminal law, law
enforcement must prove intent and prove upon reasonable doubt that the person driving the car

parked it illegally. The UPD can put a ticket on the window of a vehicle, but if that person goes to
court to argue the ticket, the court will dismiss it. The solution would be to enforce parking under
civil law, which presumes the registered owner of the vehicle parked it, and the ticket would go to
the registered owner of the car. To put parking under civil law, the Magna Metro Township would
need to have a civil law procedure or civil judge and pass some ordinances making illegal parking
a civil violation. The UPD can currently address violators parking in disabled parking spots
because different provisions apply to that.

Financial Report

                Dave Sanderson delivered a presentation on the budget ending April 30, 2023.
Revenues are good. The highest percentage of revenue is from building permits, at 67 percent.

Action/Discussion Items

Resolution 2023-05-02



                                               4
DATE            TUESDAY                               MAY                                  23, 2023

                Paul Ashton reviewed the resolution to change the form of government under
which the Magna Metro Township would operate from a metro township council form of municipal
government to a six-member council form of municipal government, and which sets two public
hearings and the election for this purpose. The statute requires the public hearings be held within
45 days after the Council approves the resolution. Then, the Council would decide whether or
not to place it on the ballot this fall. If this were to be placed on the ballot and pass, the Magna
Metro Township would have to wait another two years to elect officers, and those officers would
start in January following the election.

Proposing legislation to the Utah State Legislature relating to the metro townships’ form of
government

                  Mr. Ashton also reviewed the proposal by metro township attorneys asking metro
township councils to authorize their attorney and mayor to work with State legislators to start the
process of drafting legislation to convert the metro townships to cities and towns. The metro
townships’ population would determine whether it became a town or a city, and which class of
city. The Magna Metro Township has a population of just over 30,000, which would make it a city
of the third class. Representative Jordan Teuscher has said he would sponsor a bill and present
it to the Political Subdivisions Interim Committee this summer. Once a bill becomes a committee
bill, it gets top priority at the Legislature. The Legislature would then vote on the bill next year.
He asked if the Council would authorize him and Mayor Peay to work with the other metro
township attorneys to start that process. If the Council authorized that, he would recommend
deferring approval of the resolution until after the Legislature made a decision. Then, if the
Legislature did not pass a bill to convert the metro townships to cities and towns, the resolution
could still be approved next year, and an election held that November.

               Council Member Prokopis asked if the Legislature making the metro townships
cities circumvented the will of the people.

               Council Member Barney stated the risk in not passing a resolution now is the
Legislature could determine the form of city. He would like the legislation crafted so that decision
was up to the communities.

                 Mr. Ashton stated if the Legislature specified a form of government for the new
cities, it would be a five-member council form of government.

               Council Member Prokopis asked if the Legislature did approve a five-member
council form of government, whether the Council could change that to a six-member council.

             Mr. Ashton stated he thought that could be worked into the legislation, and it would
be dependent on the class of the city.

               Council Member Prokopis asked if the Council did not vote on the resolution until
its next meeting, whether it would still meet the 45-day timeline to hold the public hearings.
