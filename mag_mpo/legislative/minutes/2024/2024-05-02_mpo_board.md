TIP 2024 Selection Process - Project Scoring Bob Allen stated there were about 22 TIP project concepts proposed. Those projects were broken into 4 categories. The projects have been scored by MAG staff as well as by the MPO TAC. During the MPO TAC meeting each concept was presented with a virtual tour of the project area. Each project score is divided into four categories that include: Mode Specific (Roads Transit, AT), Environmental Quality, Page | 2 Safety, and Other Considerations. The MPO TAC scores, along with the MAG staff scores are normalized to create a max scoring number. Bob Allen outlined the timeline moving forward starting on June 6th the MPO Board will have the option to adopt a final ranked list of the projects. On July 1st the draft TIP selection list will go out to the public for comment. On July 22nd, the MPO TAC will make a recommendation for the final TIP selection list. The 2025 TIP Finalized List will be on the MPO Board agenda for August 1st for adoption. There is no action for the Board on this matter today. TIP Modifications - Action A. Safe Streets 4 All (SS4A) Safety Action Plan - Action Calvin Clark stated over the last several months MAG has been working on this process based on a federal grant that was awarded. The grant focuses on safety planning. Calvin Clark turned the time over to Kai Tohinaka, with Parametrix. Kai Tohinaka introduced himself and stated with the grant there is a guide for commitment and goal setting that must be incorporated into the plan. The commitment options are 1) Setting a target date Page | 3 for achieving zero roadway fatalities and serious injuries; or 2) Setting an ambitious percentage reduction of roadway fatalities and serious injuries by a specific date with an eventual goal of eliminating roadway fatalities and serious injuries. The proposal before the Board incorporates both of these commitment options. Kai Tohinaka stated there are eight elements that are incorporated into the project. He spoke to how these elements have been incorporated into the plan and the data that has been collected up to this point. These include: Leadership Commitment and Goal Setting; Planning Structure; Safety Analysis; Engagement and Collaboration; Equity; Policy and Process Changes; Strategy and Project Selections; and Progress and Transparency. The recommendation for the Leadership Commitment is to reduce fatal and serious injury crashes 50% by 2050 with an eventual goal to eliminate all fatal and serious injury crashes. The tag will be “Fifty in 50”. Kai Tohinaka explained that to achieve the Leadership Commitment there are common countermeasures that can be done for strategic project selections that include improvements for active transportation, shoulder treatments, signal timing changes, and speed management to name a few. With this there are recommendations for policy and process changes that include:● Improve data collection and reporting,● Foster regional, state and interdisciplinary partnerships,● Increase funding and resources,● Improve Multimodal connectivity, and● Increase community and political support. Calvin Clark stated this goal of Fifty in 50 is what the committee felt was best. The goal is inline with UDOT’s goal of reduction in fatality. Mayor Frost asked if there is any effort to educate drivers. He stated you can build all the improvements, but these accidents seem to be driver error. Kai Tohinaka responded that a lot of the recommendations are centered on specific improvements. There are also recommendations within the plan for education and enforcement. Mayor David Young asked about the percent goal and how it is measured. Calvin Clark stated the goal is by 2050 the serious injury and fatalities will have dropped by 50% from what they are today. That is about an average of a 2% reduction each year. Page | 4 Mayor Carla Merrill moved for MAG to commit to the goal to reduce fatal and serious injury crashes 50% by 2050, with an eventual goal to eliminate all fatal and serious injury crashes. Mayor Mark Johnson seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Public Participation Plan/Title VI Shawn Eliot presented information on the Public Participation Plan (PPP). The PPP is a federal requirement of MPOs to lead a transportation planning process that creates opportunities for public involvement. Components of the PPP include: Public participation with the MPO and core products, Public interactions with decision makers, Stakeholder involvement, and Facilitate public feedback. In addition, Title VI prohibits recipients of federal funds from discriminating on the basis of race, color, or national origin. MAG will consider structure and procedures to protect Title VI Groups by identifying protected groups through data analysis, limited english proficiency, and how MAG addresses Title VI complaints. Both the PPP and Title VI plan were last adopted in July 2019. These plans should be updated about every 3 years. Shawn Eliot presented the proposed timeline for both the PPP and Title VI adoption. The beginning is now, Spring 2024, for kickoff and introduction to the MPO TAC, the MPO Board, the Wasatch Choice Community Advisory Committee and the MAG Executive Council. During early summer there will be public outreach and coordination with MAG, Federal, and State partners. In late summer and early fall the draft PPP and Title VI will be presented with a 45 day public comment period. The process will conclude in fall with the adoption of the final PPP and Title VI plans. Ideally this will be on the MPO Board agenda for August 5th.
