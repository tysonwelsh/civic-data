about this interfund loan. They were hoping the Airport would be in a position to make these

maintenance) are the main revenue sources. Councilor Katrice MacKay said she would like to
see a report on airport revenue and Mr. Borget indicated that the airport could cover this in their

        Alternatives. (23-028) (4:38:05)

Dave Decker, Public Works Director, presented. Prior to a presentation in March on updated
flood plain maps, Public Works staff wanted to present an update to the Council. Ben Rood, CRS
Engineers, presented. The levees along the Provo River were built following flooding in the
1980s. Following Hurricane Katrina in 2007, FEMA released updated guidelines on levees. The
levees along the Provo River are not currently FEMA-certified. Councilor David Shipley
recalled a conversation about appealing the denial; Mr. Rood indicated that FEMA denied the
appeal which Provo had submitted. There was a potential question of seeking congressional
support for the appeal, but that route was not pursued as FEMA was unlikely to change their

                                                                                                    9
https://documents.provo.org/onbaseagendaonline
Elizabeth VanDerwerken – Executive Assistant
determination. It would take quite a process to overturn FEMA’s process on levees nationally.
There is no subsequent action that can be taken since the appeal was denied. Mr. Rood shared
information about the floodplain in Provo; FEMA’s official floodplain maps were going to be
released soon and would likely reflect much of this information. The map denotes areas where
homeowners with federally backed loans would be required to obtain flood insurance. There is a
six-month adoption period for those maps.

Councilor Katrice MacKay asked at what point homeowners have to obtain flood insurance; Mr.
Rood indicated that at potential flood depths of over 1’ (determined by FEMA) that this
requirement is introduced. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked if they had any sense of what
river activity would be this spring. Mr. Rood said it was difficult to predict; they have been
looking at the indicators for the watershed downstream of Jordanelle Reservoir and identifying
the usual averages versus this year’s figures. Without being able to predict the weather patterns
which will impact the snowmelt, it is challenging to say. They are working with other local
officials to ensure storage in the reservoirs can be utilized to help mitigate flood impacts.

In CRS Engineers’ study on the levees, they divided the levees into several sections to identify
the purpose of each section of the levees (along the lakeshore versus along various sections of
the Provo River). The study includes several methods by which the levees could be rehabilitated.
Mr. Rood outlined the alternatives they have identified, including:
      Rehabilitating the existing levees (which would require removing trees and vegetation)
      Building levees setback behind existing levees (to preserve existing river corridor)
Any alternative will have impacts on homeowners, as property acquisition will be needed in
order to construct setback levees. Councilor David Shipley asked if the cost figures referred only
to a couple levees or all of them. Mr. Rood indicated that in several of the areas where it is not
developed, new construction is required to build above the floodplain levels. Councilor George
Handley clarified that all the alternatives shoulder the costs on the City’s side, rather than putting
the burden on homeowners. Mr. Rood confirmed that was the case; the “do nothing” alternative
would place the costs on homeowners to purchase flood insurance. Mr. Handley asked what the
costs for that would be. Mr. Rood said FEMA has recently updated their insurance program; it
could cost between $600-$6000 per flood insurance policy per year. Councilor Rachel Whipple
shared comments on these potential impacts. She asked if the consultant has talked to Parks and
Recreation about a linear riverfront park. Mr. Rood indicated they have not, but it would be a
good conversation to pursue. Their main priority right now is figuring out how they will fund the
cost of the levees. Ms. Whipple thought creating a citywide asset with broader benefit would
make a large, costly project like this more palatable to more of the population.

Mr. Rood outlined their process for implementing the Provo levee plan, including a pilot study
for levee reconstruction, grant applications, and flood flow evaluation and stakeholder meetings.
