     A. CONSIDER A TEXT AMENDMENT TO THE MIDVALE MUNICIPAL
             CODE REGARDING CHAPTER 13.16 STORM WATER ORDINANCE
Keith Ludwig said since 2003 the city has been under the requirements of Phase ll of the
Clean Water Act. This required that we get a discharge permit from Utah Department of
Environmental Quality (UDEQ) with all its requirements, including creating a storm water
management plan.

Each year the State (UDEQ) conducts audits of various citles' and counties' storm water
programs to evaluate how well they are preforming their storm water duties.

Proceedings of l\4idvale City Council

Early last March staff had the opportunity to spend two days being interviewed by the
Division of Water Quality personnel. These meetings were held virtually. Prior to those
interviews, staff sent them records, reports, and other paperwork regarding our storm
water program.

They also came out and did site inspections at our Public Works facility and the cemetery.
There were also two construction sites inspected. The Moda Union project on Cottonwood
and Millenium Way, and the Holmes project at 700 West and Bingham Junction Blvd.

On May 12th of this year, the city received their report of our audit outlining the
deficiencies that needed to be corrected. We then had 30 days to respond with a
corrective action plan and a timeline in which to complete them. Most of the items had to
do with creating standard operating procedures (SOP) for our activities, documenting our
activities, or doing more inspections. There were several that dealt with our storm water
ordinance and changes or additions that needed to be made.

After reviewing their report, staff worked up a corrective action plan with some items being
taken care of by August 30th and others by December 1st.

All items required by the August 30th deadline were submitted well before that date and
have been acknowledged as being complete by the State. Eight of the remaining twelve
items deal with the storm water ordinance.

The following deficiencies have been addressed with the indicated section in the
ordinance that was added or changed to deal with each one:

1        Update the ordinance to restrict non-stormwater discharges. Section 13.16.120
         Dumping and lllicit Discharge was changed to more explicitly define allowed and
         prohibited discharges into the storm drain system.

2        Ordinance must be updated to include the requirement for the use of sediment an
         erosion control practices for construction. Section 13.16.100 C was added to
         require this.

         Ordinance did not include sanctions to ensure compliance with erosion and
         sediment control measures. With the additions made to Section 13.16.100
         enforcement can now be done through the ordinance.

4        Ordinance did not apply to construction projects greater than 1 acre or projects
         less than one acre that were part of a larger common plan of development. Section
         13.'16.100 C now defines these construction projects.

A
         Ordinance did not require construction operators to prepare a Storm Water
         Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP). Section 13.16.100 D now requires a SWppp
         to be prepared.

        A.   DISCUSS STREET CUT MORATORIUM


                                             10
Proceedings of lvlidvale City Council
Meeting October '19, 2021


Nate Rockwood said he and Keith Ludwig have worked on this and feel it would be a
good idea. He said over the last several years the city has seen an uptake in the amount
of road cuts. These can have a detrimental effect on the longevity of the lifetime of the
pavement. lt is especially frustrating when these cuts happen in recently rebuilt, overlaid
or slurry sealed roads.

Staff has d rafted and is recommending implementation of a road cut moratorium policy.
This will help keep our roads not only looking good but will also help with the longevity.
Road cut moratoriums are common and have been implemented in several surrounding
communities. The road cut moratorium policy will be ongoing.

The proposed moratorium is summarized below:

    a    For new and reconstructed streets and overlaid streets, no road cut permits will be
         allowed for three years after the date of completion.

    a    For those streets recently slurry sealed, no road cuts will be allowed for one year
         after the date of construction.

         For an emergency situation the City Engineer will allow road cuts, but the special
         restoration standard will apply.

         The special restoration standard will be in effect following the moratorium

              o   For new street, reconstructed streets, and overlaid streets, this standard will
                  be in effect for three years after the moratorium ends.

              o For streets that have been slurry sealed, this standard will be in effect for
                  one year after the moratorium ends.

              o   This special restoration standard will require the asphalt surface to be
                  restored by milling out and then overlaying the area that was impacted. This
