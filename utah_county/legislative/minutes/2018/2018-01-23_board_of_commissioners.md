3.     ADOPT (OR DENY) AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND THE OFFICIAL UTAH COUNTY ZONE MAP
       FROM THE MINING AND GRAZING (M&G-1) ZONE TO THE GRAZING (G-1) ZONE FOR
       PROPERTY LOCATED IN SECTIONS 10, 11, 14, 15, 16, 20, 21, 22, 23, 26, 27, 28, 32, 33, AND 34, T8S
       R1E, AND SECTIONS 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 11, 14, 15, 16, 22, AND 23, T9S R1E, WEST MOUNTAIN
       AREA OF UTAH COUNTY (Continued from the January 9 and 16, 2018 Commission meetings)
(Track A, 10:45)
Bryce Armstrong of the Utah County Community Development Office mentioned the new Grazing (G-1) Zone which
was adopted by the Board at the previous week’s meeting and applies to contiguous agricultural properties. Several
property owners have either submitted requests to not be included within the G-1 Zone, or must give written consent
specifically to be included within the zone – the proposed ordinance today addresses those exceptions. Mr. Armstrong
and the commissioners reviewed noticing requirements and discussions with a Todd Cusick of CMC Rock.

(Track A, 20:20)
Commissioner Ivie opened the floor to public comment for this agenda item, and West Mountain resident Nita Burch
came forward with a presentation about the historic Native American petroglyphs found in the area. She referenced
published work ‘Southwestern American Indian Rock Art and the Book of Mormon’ by James Roy Harris, Sr., and the
magazine ‘Ancient American’ which featured pieces of significant art found on West Mountain. Ms. Burch showed
pictures of tombs and carvings dating back thousands of years. She cited federal laws intended to protect antiquities
and asked the Board to re-zone West Mountain back to grazing for the sake of history.

(Track A, 25:25)
Shauna Phelps commented on those who might object to being included in the Grazing Zone, pointing out they have
had ample time to bring concerns to the Board of Commissioners. Commissioner Graves noted the Board has been
sent letters and other forms of communication expressing opposition to the zoning changes – not all have been present
at Commission meetings. Commissioners Lee and Ivie added they have been in contact with representatives of the
Bureau of Land Management (BLM). Lana Creer-Harris presented photographs of rock art from the mountain (copies
of which can be found attached to these minutes). At least one mining company owner has admitted directly to Ms.
Creer-Harris about disrupting artifacts. Ms. Creer-Harris spoke of the environmental and historic importance of West
Mountain.

(Track A, 35:40)
Condie Construction representative McKell Condie acknowledged the dilemma of balancing agriculture preservation
and material production. Mr. Condie presented an aerial map to explain his company would be limited to expand
boundaries due to the new zoning even though they do not operate on grazing land. This will adversely give other
companies the opportunity to infringe on residents while simultaneously removing the ability for Condie Construction
to work with their neighbors. The commissioners discussed the potential problems with Mr. Condie and Utah County
Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore.

(Track A, 44:50)
Provo resident Robert Stevens questioned the need to rush this decision. He suggested extending this decision for six
months to a year, asking whether there is underlying motivation to adopt the changes now because mining rights will
soon expire for certain parties. Mr. Stevens pointed out the county may be able to use drainage water from nearby
subdivisions to suppress dust caused by earth extraction. Julie Sainsbury of South Utah County Community Voice
expressed appreciation for the legislative process, and responded to the assumed question, ‘Why do we need to act
now?’ Ms. Sainsbury explained how the cumulative effect of mining hurts the community over many years. A change
to zoning can give time to evaluate the impact and preventive measures that neither the state Division of Air Quality
or Utah Division of Oil, Gas and Mining (DOGM) can address due to lack of funding or regulations. Ms. Sainsbury
reminded the Board they are the only resource for the county residents.

(Track A, 55:15)
There were no additional public comments and Commissioner Ivie returned the discussion to the bench. The
commissioners outlined the properties which have protested being included in the G-1 Zone and reviewed legal
descriptions of the parcels with Bryce Armstrong of the Utah County Community Development Office and Utah
County Deputy Attorney Robert J. Moore. Commissioners Lee and Graves debated the point in creating an ordinance
with the intention of returning to amend it, talking about balance and accommodations.

           Commissioner Lee made the motion to adopt the ordinance amending the Official
           Utah County Zone Map from the Mining and Grazing (M&G-1) Zone to the
           Grazing (G-1) Zone for property located in the sections detailed in Regular
           Agenda Item No. 3, and excluding property which must legally be omitted per
           agricultural protection status, as well as Tax Parcel Serial Nos. 28:020:0008,
           28:020:0009, 28:020:0005, 28:020:0026, 28:020:0020, and 29:019:0017. The
           motion was seconded by Commissioner Graves and carried with the following
