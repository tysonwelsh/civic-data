3.   Public hearing and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit for a pavilion 
and amphitheater at the Boy Scout Camp; East Fork of the Bear Camp; SS-2326-X, SS-2327-
X and SS-2328-X; Boy Scouts of America, Applicant. – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner presented the background of the application. The application is for an 
amphitheater and pavilion to be placed near the Thomas Monson Lodge. In the AG-160 
Zone, these types of uses are Conditional Use. A Conditional Use Permit was granted for the 
Monson Lodge about three years ago. The pavilion will be a basic roof structure over a 
concrete pad. The amphitheater will have places for the scouts to sit while working on 
scouting items. Staff recommends the Planning Commission review this application, 
conduct a Public Hearing and approve the Conditional Use. 
  
The public hearing was opened. There was no public comment. The public hearing was closed. 
 
Commissioner Sargent stated there are stricter fire procedures than when a fire occurred 
in the area of the scout camp in 2002, and hopes that a fire like that never occurs again. The 
scout camp is a fabulous facility. 
Findings of Fact:  
1. The applicant has submitted a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) application for a 2,583-square 
foot pavilion, and an amphitheater with a maximum occupancy of 753 people on the East Fork of 
the Bear Scout Camp.  
2. The Camp is located in the AG-160 zone.  
Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
August 17, 2017 
Page 3 of 14 
 
 
3. Institutional structures in the AG-160 zone are conditional uses.  
4. In August of 2014, the Planning Commission approved a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) 
application for 22,467 square foot lodge and 180,000-gallon water tank on site.  
5. The use of the camp will remain the same, with scouting activities on site.  
6. The camp is approximately 361 acres, with access via Highway 150 (the Mirror Lake 
Highway) and then a gravel Forest Service road.  
7. Parking, internal circulation and access on site have been determined to be adequate by the 
Summit County Engineering Department.  
8. Service Providers have reviewed the application and provided comment, or have not stated 
concern with the application.  
9. Staff has reviewed the application for compliance with the applicable development standards 
in Chapter 2 of the Development Code.  
10. Staff has not found areas of noncompliance with the applicable development standards in 
Chapter 2 of the Development Code.  
11. Both the amphitheater and pavilion will be smaller in scale and mass than the lodge.  
12. The pavilion will have a maximum height of 12 feet above finished grade, in a zone where 
the maximum height is 32 feet.  
13. The top of the amphitheater begins at existing grade and with the stage 10 feet below. 
Conclusions of Law:  
1. The application complies with applicable requirements in Chapter 11-2 of the Development 
Code.  
2. The application complies with the Conditional Use Permit criteria in Section 11-4-12 of the 
Development Code.

3. The application complies with the Eastern Summit County General Plan.  
Conditions of Approval:  
1. Prior to construction, the applicants shall submit a construction mitigation plan to Summit 
County for review and approval.  
2. All service provider requirements shall be met. 
 
Commissioner Clyde made a motion to approve the Conditional Use Permit according 
to the Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law and Conditions of Approval in the Staff

1. Discussion regarding a Sustainability Certification for Mountain Valley Stone – Ray 
Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Planner Milliner introduced Mountain Valley Stone, who is working on getting ANSI 
certification. There is a requirement to give a presentation in a public forum, and Mountain 
Valley Stone has chosen the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission meeting as their 
public forum.

voluntary standard to measure and improve sustainable practices. One piece of the 
standard is reclamation. The plan for reclamation was presented, including what has been 
done so far and what Mountain Valley Stone has planned for reclamation in the future. 
Sustainable practices often save money and are a good sale point for stone. The point of the 
certification is to be good neighbors, identify the best practices for the business and being 
the best Mountain Valley Stone can be in sustainability and conservation. Local excavators
