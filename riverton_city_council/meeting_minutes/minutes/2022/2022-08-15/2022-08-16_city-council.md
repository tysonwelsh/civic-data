Discussion Items

       Code Enforcement and Law Enforcement Discussion

Mayor Staggs asked David Brickey, City Manager, to lead the discussion.

Mr. Brickey explained the difference in civil code enforcement issues versus criminal. He said
that civil cases seek compliance rather than imposing a fine or a sentence. The City currently
has only two officers taking care of civil issues. One officer is handling animal cases and one
officer is handling code cases. He may ask for an additional officer to help with civil issues. Mr.
Brickey asked Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, to discuss the code
enforcement side and Ryan Carter, City Attorney, to discuss the criminal side.

Councilmember Wells said that at least two residents are in attendance that are concerned
about parking issues in the City. He said the prosecutor and the judge are throwing out parking

cases. Mr. Wells talked to code enforcement in Draper and Draper’s code enforcement is much
different. He would like the City to adopt an ordinance that can be enforced.

Mr. Brickey explained that issuing parking tickets is difficult because of property rights. He said
prior councils have had strong opinions about strict liability crimes and did not want citations
issued unless police saw the crime in progress. If that is no longer the position of the Council,
he asked the Council to make this known.

Councilmember Wells said the specific issue he is concerned about is a bailer attached to a
vehicle that is parked across the sidewalk and no citation has been issued. The City needs to
do something because citizens have a right to use the sidewalk. He would like the ordinance to
be changed so the City can enforce parking problems.

Mayor Staggs agreed that code enforcement might need an additional officer to help with code
and animal cases.

Councilmember Stewart asked what prior council gave the direction about strict liability crimes.
Mr. Brickey said this was a generalization that was shared with him. Councilmember Stewart
said he thought parking problems were being enforced on public streets, however, the judicial
branch refused to enforce.

Mayor Staggs said that in years past, code enforcement has been “complaint driven.” The
Council would like certain violations enforced swiftly.

Councilmember McDougal asked the departments to explain the current code before the
Council gets into specific cases.

Mr. Carter explained there are a variety of issues to consider with a parked vehicle. He believes
that a tractor is considered a motor vehicle and is subject to impound. It is not required that an
officer observe a vehicle for 48 hours but the officer could mark the tires to know whether the
vehicle is there for more than 48 hours. There are instances where no parking signs are
displayed and if the officer does not observe who parked the vehicle in the no parking zone, the
case is thrown out because they cannot prove “beyond a reasonable doubt” who parked the
vehicle. Salt Lake City steps outside the criminal area of the law and uses a different court
system to enforce parking. The City prosecutor’s office in years past has put together
ordinances that make it a civil penalty to park and these ordinances have never been adopted.
These have not been pushed forward since Mayor Staggs was elected Mayor.

Mayor Staggs asked if civil cases would be heard in justice court. Mr. Carter said it would be
heard in the justice court but set at different times and days from the criminal cases. Mr. Carter
said the City needs to determine how much they would like to invest in these types of cases. In
regards to parking tickets in the justice court, some citizens pay these tickets, however, if they
decide to fight it then the City dismisses it because they cannot prove beyond a reasonable
doubt that the owner parked the vehicle.

Mayor Staggs said that maintenance of property and animal bites are also civil issues. Mr.
Brickey said that dog bites are criminal matters whereas a dog at large would be a civil issue.
He explained that an animal license cannot be taken until there are three incidents. He believes
that after one bite, the animal license should be revoked. Mayor Staggs, Councilmember
McDougal, and Councilmember Buroker all agreed with Mr. Brickey. Councilmember McCay
said she agrees if it is severe enough.

Mr. Lethbridge said the three occurrences is a general standard. The dog could be impounded
or quarantined regardless of how many occurrences. Mr. Lethbridge said that he relies on the
good staff that he has in working with the animals and owners. He would like the policies and
practices to be formalized.

Councilmember McDougal clarified that he would like the code written and enforced to say that
the license is removed if there is one bite of another animal or person that breaks the skin.
Mayor Staggs asked if the license is taken away or if the animal is impounded and quarantined.
Mr. Lethbridge said that currently the animal is impounded and quarantined and asked the
Council to clarify whether they would like the license removed after one bite.

Mr. Brickey said there are property rights and the court process requires due process even
though there may be a one strike rule ordinance. Taking a license may take as long as 90 days
to a year to prosecute. Councilmember McCay said she would like the code enforcement officer
to have discretion about whether to take the license or not. Mr. Brickey does not want to take
away officer discretion and have code that says shall or must. Mr. Lethbridge clarified that
animals are rarely euthanized.

Mayor Staggs clarified the Council would like to have the ordinance read that if the animal bites
one time and breaks the skin then the animal is impounded or quarantined. The officer can then
decide whether the license should be removed or not.

Councilmember Stewart said these ordinances get dicey because sometimes it is a one-time
situation. He believes that enforcement should be based on the propensity to continue.

Mr. Lethbridge said the Council will need to have future discussions about space for these
animals. Mayor Staggs said that in the past the City contributed $50,000 to expand Stoneridge,
which might be a future option.

Councilmember Buroker said she supports the language that says “breaks the skin” and
supports language that says the officer “can” take the animal. Councilmember McDougal said
he would like to get the language in the books, then deal with enforcement and educating
residents about the process.

Councilmember Wells agrees with impounding an animal after one bite. He says that what the
City is doing right now is not working for violations regarding residences in his district. He said
the City does just enough and then backs off and the problem continues.

Mayor Staggs asked if the Council would like to move towards a Salt Lake model for parking
issues which would make these offenses a civil matter instead of a criminal matter.
Councilmembers Wells and McDougal would like parking to be strongly enforced.

Mr. Brickey said staff will put together language for the ordinance and include the budget
impacts related to salary and benefits for an additional code officer. Councilmember Stewart
expressed concern about seasonal street parking being separated from other parking issues the
City faces. Councilmember Buroker said the Council must be careful in drafting an ordinance
regarding parking because residents all over Riverton park on the streets.

Mr. Carter said the ordinance addresses vehicles parked in one location for more than 48 hours.
He said this ordinance is enforceable but is also tricky. Mr. Lethbridge said the City must be
careful of unintended consequences when drafting parking ordinances.

         VECC Update

Scott Ruf, Executive Director of VECC Agencies, was hired by VECC two and a half years ago.
He said that staffing and the job market has made service very difficult.

Mayor Staggs explained that VECC stands for Valley Emergency Communications Center
which dispatches incoming emergency calls for both police and fire. Mayor Staggs expressed
concern about Riverton’s participation jumping from $80,000 to $260,000. He asked Mr. Ruf
what the City can expect going forward.

Mr. Ruf said he looked at the budget as soon as he became the Executive Director of VECC
and realized there was a $3 million shortfall. He immediately made a proposal to the Board of
Trustees. In fiscal 2020, the Board approved a 12% increase to all agencies except for Unified
Police which was -5%.

Mr. Ruf said in preparation for fiscal 2021, they met with chiefs and the board, created a finance
and audit committee, and looked at the member assessments. In looking at the assessment
models, they realized the UPD was being charged at a higher rate than Riverton or Herriman.
In order to settle the budget, there were large increases. In fiscal year 2022 with the model in
place, there were increases because of compensation, health insurance, changes to Versaterm,
and dispatch systems. For fiscal year 2022, there was an 8.8% increase overall, including
pass-through for shared service agreements. For fiscal year 2023, there is a 5% increase
overall.

Mr. Ruf explained the legislature had a giant spotlight on VECC for their performance and they
have been able to turn that around and focus on emergency services. They have made
significant improvements, have a positive balance and hope to have a 10% reserve by 2025.

Mayor Staggs said he would like to see the formula that VECC uses to determine fees. He
expressed concern over VECC using the number of police officers to determine the cost and
would prefer that the number of calls be used to determine the cost.

Councilmember Stewart would also like to see the formula. He doesn’t believe the numbers
add up and feels like all of the other agencies are subsidizing Unified Police Department. He
believes Riverton is one of the lowest call volumes in the valley and one of the lower crime
areas in the valley. He doesn’t understand why Riverton had a 300% increase.

Chief Hutson said there has been a lot of debate about the formula. However, a dispatcher
must sit at the phone no matter how many calls come in. The formula is a 50/50 split between
sworn officers and calls for service. When Mr. Ruf said there was a 12% increase, he was
referring to the entire VECC budget. If you take the assessment portion and apply the formula
then some agencies go up and some go down.

Councilmember Stewart does not like the model and feels that paying $265,000 for VECC
seems out of balance.

Mayor Staggs would like to receive the model to understand better how the fees are assessed.
He expressed concern over dispatch times and feels like Riverton is forced by statute to be part
of VECC. He is encouraged that VECC is taking steps to ensure there are no significant
increases in the future.

Mr. Ruf said that every month a performance and progress report is created. On the fire and

City is proposing approval of a Development Agreement establishing requirements, standards,

and parameters for development of approximately 5.5 acres located at 13265 S Lovers Lane,
allowing for subdivision of that property at lot sizes consistent with the current zoning. Utah
State Code allows for the adoption of a Development Agreement which allows for and facilitates
development of property, provided such an agreement is adopted in a manner consistent with a
zoning or ordinance amendment. The property is currently zoned RR-22, a single-family
residential zone, with half-acre lots and animal rights. The General Plan designation for the
area is Estate Density Residential.

Mr. Carter explained that a development agreement can be used to design a property in the
same way that a comprehensive set of ordinances would apply to this specific property. The
legislature has said that if the development agreement creates distinctions from the way the
code would apply to the property then the City must go through the same process as it would for
a change to the ordinance. The unique standards that apply to this property are “not much.”
The private lane width standards are smaller in some areas. The developer would also like to
develop in phases. Because phase 1 is only adding one more lot then he will not be required to
asphalt the private road. When phase 2 is developed, he will need to asphalt the private lane
completely.

Councilmember Wells asked about access roads in and around the development and about any
litigation claims with Mr. Olson. Mr. Carter explained the different access roads that could be
potentially in and around the development and said this development agreement will resolve the
litigation matter.
