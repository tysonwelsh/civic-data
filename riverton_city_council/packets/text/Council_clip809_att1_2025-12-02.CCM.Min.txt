37          Animal Shelter Discussion
38
39   Nate Slack, Public Relations Specialist, provided an update on animal control operations and
40   recent improvements to manage the number of animals in the shelter and improve response
41   and posting times for lost pets. He shared that data showed a significant decrease in adoptable
42   animals at the start of the year due to transition efforts with Stone Ridge, and the shelter has
43   maintained fewer than ten animals at a time since then. He outlined several strategies, including
44   coordinating with neighboring cities, featuring adoptable animals in the City newsletter, creating
45   short videos to showcase behavior, and holding adoption events or shelter open-house activities
46   to raise visibility.

48          Update on HB 48 Wildland Urban Interface
49
50   Fire Marshal Wade Watkins presented an overview of House Bill 48 and its relevance to
51   Riverton City, noting that the City’s 2010 adoption of the Wildland Urban Interface (WUI) Code

 1   already met the bill’s requirements. He explained that Riverton’s structure exposure scores,
 2   based on fire likelihood, intensity, and ember load, were low, with none exceeding five and well
 3   below the state’s high-risk threshold of seven. Because of this, Riverton did not need to
 4   establish a WUI boundary, and no local properties were expected to be subject to the state-

 8   establishing a boundary would provide any benefit. In response, Fire Marshal Watkins advised
 9   that boundaries are typically adopted only when justified by higher exposure scores and noted
10   that adopting one unnecessarily could influence insurance costs.

12            Homeowners Association (HOA) Water
13
14   Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, reported on efforts to address Riverton’s unusually high
15   water usage and water loss rates, and to identify causes and reduce losses. He prioritized rapid
16   leak repairs and investigated HOAs after discovering that many communities were receiving
17   unmetered water through redundant connection points equipped with weighted check valves.
18   Testing confirmed that isolating these valves significantly increased metered flow, indicating
19   long-term unbilled usage. After completing a full billing cycle, he found that several HOAs, about
20   25 communities, will see substantial increases on their upcoming bills, with one large account
21   showing an estimated $16,000 increase in a single month. He also noted that this particular
22   HOA had been repeatedly warned about leaks over the years but had declined to repair them,
23   benefiting from unmetered water use. Correcting this issue is expected to significantly improve
24   the City’s water-loss reporting and financial accuracy.
25
26   Councilmembers discussed strategies for communicating upcoming water bill adjustments to
27   HOAs and residents following the identification and repair of long-standing leaks. Concerns
28   were raised about ensuring residents understand the historical context, that these bills reflect
29   usage that should have been metered and billed for years. Councilmembers emphasized direct
30   communication both to the HOAs and to individual units to reduce confusion and prevent
31   misattribution of costs. Staff confirmed that letters were already being drafted and that all 24
32   affected accounts would be placed on a watch list to monitor usage and ensure accurate
33   reporting to the state. Councilmembers requested to see the letter before distribution and to
34   receive a list of HOAs within their districts so they can provide outreach as well.
35
36   Councilmembers reached consensus to implement a phased billing approach for all 24 HOAs,
37   applied equitably and based on a percentage discount rather than fixed dollar amounts. They
38   agreed on a three-month transition period, no longer than six months, after which all affected
39   accounts will move to full, real-time billing.

41   City Manager Report
42
43   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, provided an update regarding the South Valley Aqueduct project,
44   noting that residents recently received mailers implying the contractor had City approval for
45   night work. He clarified that the City did not issue such approval and that the authorization
46   referenced in the mailer was granted by another governmental entity. He explained that, under
47   the City’s noise ordinance, waivers may only be granted for specific events, such as a one-time
48   overnight concrete pour, not for blanket or open-ended after-hours construction. Any work
49   conducted between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m. remains subject to the ordinance, and this has been
50   communicated to the project representatives.

27            Resolution No. 25-61 – Approving the Riverton City Skate Park be Closed for Use
28            by the Public
29
30   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, provided an update on the skate park, clarifying that the
31   discussion was not related to any adjacent development or land transactions. He reviewed the
32   timeline, noting that the skate park had been assessed during strategic planning nearly a year
33   prior, with further evaluations conducted by multiple vendors and the City’s new insurance
34   carrier, Utah Risk Management Agency. The inspections indicated that the park could not be
35   safely repaired and needed full replacement. He emphasized that the decision to close the
36   skate park was solely for safety reasons and discussed plans to install a temporary fence to
37   prevent future use.
38
39   Mayor Staggs explained that the decision to close the skate park could have been made
40   administratively based on the safety report, but he chose to bring it to the Council for
41   discussion due to prior conversations regarding the facility. He clarified that any future
42   decisions regarding rebuilding or relocating the skate park would be addressed by the Council
43   at a later time.
44
45   Councilmember Haymond asked about the legal process for closing the skate park, how it
46   would be enforced, and the type of fencing needed to mitigate liability without excessive cost.
47   Staff explained that a six-foot chain link fence could be rented and locked, with signage
48   indicating closure for safety, at a cost of approximately $1,500 per month. Ryan Carter, City
49   Attorney, confirmed that fencing the park in this manner is the minimum required to protect the
50   City legally, noting that temporary or lightweight fencing, such as orange hazard fencing,
51   would not be sufficient. He emphasized that prior experiences with inadequate fencing have
     Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     December 2, 2025



 1   exposed the City to legal liability, and the chain link fence is the preferred method to prevent
 2   unauthorized access.
 3
 4   Councilmember McCay noted that the letter regarding the skate park stated it needed to be
 5   made safe and properly repaired, rather than explicitly torn out. She asked whether additional
 6   contractor options could be explored to repair the park, given the limited history of emergency
 7   incidents and examples of other City areas with “use at your own risk” signage. In response,
 8   Mr. Hicks responded that exploring additional contractors was possible, but cautioned that
 9   even if repairs were made, the park would still need to meet safety inspections by specialists.
10   He suggested that the Council first decide whether the skate park should remain at its current
11   location or be replaced, as that would guide next steps for repair or reconstruction.
12
13   Councilmember Pierucci asked about the legal effectiveness of posting cautionary signs at the
14   skate park. Mr. Carter explained that because the park is designed for children, who are not
15   legally capable of fully understanding risk, warning signs alone do not protect the City from
16   liability. Councilmember Pierucci emphasized the importance of finding a long-term solution
17   and suggested educating residents about skate park facilities in nearby cities while the park
18   remains closed, potentially using signage with QR codes linking to alternative locations.
19
20   Councilmember Buroker emphasized that the discussion and decisions were focused on
21   resident safety and fiscal responsibility, and stressed the importance of communicating to
22   residents that the action being taken was to ensure the City remains safe.
23
24   Councilmember McDougal said that the City has a fiduciary and safety responsibility to
25   residents. He noted that professionals had identified risks in the skate park, supporting the
26   need to close it. He supported communicating alternative facilities to residents and stressed
27   that the closure was a prudent measure for both public safety and City liability, while allowing
28   for thoughtful consideration of the park’s future.
29
30   Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 25-61 –
31   Approving the Riverton City Skate Park be Closed for Use by the Public. Councilmember
32   Buroker SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion. Mayor
33   Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes,
34   McCay-no, McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed with a 4 to 1 vote.
