 Action Agenda

 1           A presentation of the tentative budget for Provo City for the fiscal year beginning July 1,
             2026, and ending June 30, 2027. (26-001) 7:12

Mayor Judkins presented the proposed FY 2027 tentative budget, emphasizing that it is a balanced
budget with no proposed property tax increase. She stated that the administration worked closely with
finance staff and department directors to identify efficiencies, responsibly manage supplemental
requests, and prioritize long-term investments that improve quality of life for Provo residents. Mayor
Judkins highlighted investments in public safety, cybersecurity, permitting efficiency, and employee
support, including a 2% cost-of-living adjustment and expanded parental leave. She also noted efforts to
reduce Fire Department overtime costs, maintain low property taxes and utility rates through utility
fund transfers, and support Council priorities such as home ownership, economic development, and
code enforcement. She thanked staff and the Council for their collaboration throughout the budget
process.

Kelsey Zarbock, Budget Officer, expressed appreciation to the Council, department directors, staff, and
city leadership for their extensive work and collaboration in preparing the FY 2027 tentative budget. She
specifically recognized Administrative Services Director Dan Follett and Budget Analyst Andrea Wright
for their significant contributions to the budget process. Ms. Zarbock explained the timeline for the
budget adoption process, noting that the tentative budget was being delivered to the Council, with
tentative adoption scheduled for May 12, public hearings on June 9 and June 23, and final adoption

Councilor Garrett asked for clarification regarding updated sales tax and revenue projections discussed
during the previous work session. He referenced an earlier estimate showing approximately $6.2 million
in increased sales tax and other revenues and asked whether the updated figures reflected a reduction
to $3.6 million in sales tax revenue alone or if the original estimate had included additional revenue
sources.

Ms. Zarbock explained that the sales tax revenue projection for FY 2027 was ultimately adjusted to a
more conservative estimate based on updated information. She stated that the proposed budget
reflects an approximately $3.6 million increase in sales tax revenue compared to the FY 2026 budget.
Ms. Zarbock noted that the previously discussed $6.2 million figure may have referred to a broader
overall revenue estimate rather than sales tax alone but confirmed that sales tax revenues are still
projected to increase significantly in the FY 2027 budget.

Councilor Garrett asked whether it was fair to conclude that the majority of the increased FY 2027
budget was allocated toward adding new staffing positions, based on the summary of proposed
positions included in the budget document.

Ms. Zarbock responded that personnel costs are a significant component of the FY 2027 budget and are
outlined in the personnel section of the budget highlights. She noted that several new full-time positions
were added but explained that many of those additions were offset by reductions or savings in other
areas, such as overtime reductions associated with new firefighter positions. She stated that staff could
provide a more detailed breakdown of the overall personnel cost impacts if needed.

Councilor Garrett stated that he was seeking additional context as he reviewed the proposed budget
and wanted to better understand where the increased revenues were being allocated. He noted
references in the Mayor’s budget letter to new staffing positions and cost-of-living adjustments and
expressed interest in identifying how those increased revenues were reflected throughout the budget.

Ms. Zarbock explained that each department section in the budget document includes tables showing
historical actual expenditures, the FY 2026 adopted budget, adjusted FY 2026 figures reflecting
carryovers or mid-year appropriations, and the proposed FY 2027 budget. She stated that comparing the
FY 2026 and FY 2027 figures could help identify where revenues and expenditures had increased and
offered to provide an additional summary to assist with the review process.

Councilor Garrett thanked staff and the administration for their work on the FY 2027 budget. He
expressed appreciation for the focus on Council priorities and for presenting a balanced budget that
does not include a property tax increase.

Councilor Bogdin asked when the Council would receive the consolidated fee schedule.

 Stormwater Service District

 3           A presentation of the tentative budget for the Provo City Stormwater Service District for
             the Fiscal Year beginning July 1, 2026, and ending June 30, 2027. (26-001) 24:32

Ms. Zarbock explained that the Storm Water Service District budget is included near the end of the
Provo City budget document on page 142. She stated that the budget was being presented for Storm
Water Board review and again noted that final adoption would not occur until the second meeting in
June.

Chair MacKay asked for clarification regarding the ongoing purpose of the Storm Water Service District,
noting that the district had originally been created for a specific project that has since been completed.
She asked whether the City intends to maintain the district indefinitely or if there are future plans
related to its continuation.

Ms. Zarbock explained that the Storm Water Service District was originally created to support bond
financing for stormwater-related projects and noted that the associated bonds were paid off several
years ago. She stated that the district currently functions as a separate entity primarily because of the
historical bond structure and suggested that legal staff would be best suited to determine whether the
entity is still needed. Ms. Zarbock added that the district now oversees stormwater division operations
and maintenance within the Public Works Department, including storm drain system maintenance.

Board Member Bogdin asked Assistant City Attorney Gary Millward whether he had any additional
insight regarding the continued existence and purpose of the Storm Water Service District.

Mr. Millward stated that he did not have specific insight at that time regarding the Storm Water Service
District but noted that legal staff could review the requirements for dissolving a special service district
and consult with Public Works staff. He indicated that, following that review, legal staff could provide
guidance on whether and when the district could potentially be dissolved.

Board Member Whitlock summarized his understanding that the Storm Water Service District was
originally created to facilitate bond financing, that the bonds have since been paid off, and that the
district now appears to function primarily as an administrative artifact rather than a separate
operational entity.

Mr. Millward agreed that the Storm Water Service District appeared to primarily remain in place
following the payoff of the related bonds. He stated that decisions regarding how the district’s funds are
currently budgeted would be a matter for the Finance Department, while legal staff would need to
review the legal requirements and process necessary to dissolve the district if that were pursued.

Chair MacKay clarified for the public that the proposed budget presentation marked the beginning of
the Council’s review process and that no final decisions had been made. She stated that the Council
would carefully study the budget over the coming weeks to ensure it aligns with Council priorities and
emphasized that the budget process would continue for approximately seven weeks before final
adoption.

 Adjournment

The meeting was adjourned by unanimous consent at approximately 5:53 PM.
