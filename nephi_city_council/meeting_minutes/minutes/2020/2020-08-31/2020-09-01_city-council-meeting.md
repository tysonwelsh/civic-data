Mayor Glade R. Nielson called the meeting to order and welcomed all present and then Councilor Larry
Ostler offered a prayer.

PLEDGE OF ALLEGIANCE:
  The Pledge of Allegiance was led by Chris Baird, publisher and CEO of the newspaper Serve Daily.

PAR TAX ART’S GRANT PRESENTATION:

   Mayor Nielson presented the Juab Fine Arts Council (JFAC) representatives, Michelle Harris and David
Whipple. with a grant check for $3,911.00. The funds were made available from the PAR Tax collected
in fiscal year 2020. A certain percentage of the tax collected is allocated to the arts. The Juab Fine Arts
Council were the only organization that applied for the grant. Michelle Harris gave a brief review of the
JFAC’s future planned events that includes a Murder Mystery dinner theatre, Nutcracker ballet, Hocus
Pocus dance recital, and their annual Christmas Festival.

CONSENT AGENDA APPROVED:

  Councilor Worwood moved to approve the consent agenda which included the approval of the
minutes of the regular council meeting held on 8-18-20, the work session minutes 8-18-20, regular
council meeting 8-25-20, the work session 8-25-20, and a list of claims on the warrant register dated
9-1-20 Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

PUBLIC COMMENT

   Ryan Crum said that he had a son who had been involved in a recent ATV/vehicle accident. He said
that he had met with City Administrator Seth Atkinson and told him that there needed to be proper
signage on the city streets. He said at the intersection where his son’s accident occurred there was not
a yield sign or a stop sign. He said that one of the charms of the community is that youth can drive
ATVs within the city. He said that the laws that are in place needed to be enforced. He questioned if
anyone had been issued a ticket in the past year for not having a safety certificate.
   Kurt Lainhart addressed the council and said that he is very concerned about the youth driving golf
carts on city streets. He stated that there had already been a death of a young person involved in an
ATV accident within our city and it happened right in front of his house. He said he had recently seen a
9-year-old driving a golf cart plow through an intersection.
   Ruth Bonzo said that she has a neighbor with young children who are driving a motorized vehicle
that is very dangerous. She thought that their needed to be rules to protect the young drivers from
harm.
   Robbie Fowkes who came later in the meeting spoke to council about his concerns with the golf cart
ordinance. He said he was representing the adults who drive golf carts on the streets. He was worried
about the part of the ordinance that talks about driving at night. He said that sometimes when he is
returning from the golf course it is after dark. He stated that his daughter drives his golf cart on the

OHV REGULATIONS ORDINANCE DRAFT AND GOLF CART REGULATIONS ORDINANCE DRAFT DISCUSSED:

  Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council drafts of two ordinances regulating the driving of off highway
vehicles and golf carts on city roads. The council agreed to bring back the final draft of the ordinance
with the suggested changes from the council and the public for possible approval at the September 15,
2020 regular city council meeting.

ED MALMGREN LEVEL CHANGE REQUEST APPROVED:

  Councilor Memmott moved to change from level 2 to level 1 on the annexation area map for the Ed
Malmgren property located south of the golf course. Councilor Seely seconded the motion. The motion
passed on a unanimous vote.

JAY FORSCUTT PROPERTY AGREEMENT APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson updated the council that Mr. Jay Forscutt owns property at 153 South 100 West and
would like to construct a new home on the property. There is an existing home there that he would like
to stay in while constructing the new home. In reviewing the request, he stated that the staff felt that
this could be accomplished provided that the old home would be demolished soon after the new home
was completed. City Attorney Kasey Wright had prepared an agreement stating that the existing home
would be removed within 90 days of the new home being completed. In order to protect the city and
adhere to the code, the city may withhold a certificate of occupancy on the new home and take legal
action. The agreement also states that a certificate of occupancy may be issued if a bond for 1.5 times
the cost of removal is furnished by Mr. Forscutt. Councilor Worwood moved to authorize the mayor to
sign the Forscutt agreement. Councilor Seely seconded the motion, which passed on a unanimous vote.

CITY ADMINISTRATOR UPDATES:

   Mr. Atkinson updated the council that the construction company of the water project is beginning to
patch and pave the roads that have been torn up around town.
   Mr. Atkinson stated that on September 9th the city will close out the temporary loans for the water
project with Zions Bank and sign for the USDA loans. When the USDA loans are exhausted the city can
draw on the 2-million-dollar grant from the USDA. He stated that Karl Larsen from the USDA was able
to get a 1.5% interest rate on all three loans that were originally at a much higher interest rate. He said
that the city will save approximately $80,000 per year in debt service with the lower interest rate.
  Mr. Atkinson asked the council who would like to be on the hiring committee for the new Gas
Department Utility Tech. Councilor Ostler volunteered to be on the committee.
