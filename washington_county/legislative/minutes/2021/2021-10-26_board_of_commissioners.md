WASHINGTON COUNTY COMMISSION
WORK MEETING MINUTES
OCTOBER 26, 2021

The Work Meeting of the Board of the Washington County Commission was called to
order by Commissioner Iverson at 12:00 p.m. on October 26, 2021, in the Washington
County Administration Building Chambers, 197 East Tabernacle, St. George, Utah. Those
in attendance were: Commissioner Victor Iverson, Commissioner Adam Snow, Deputy
County Attorney Devin Snow, County Administrator Nicholle Felshaw and Deputy Clerk
Sarah Lloyd.

Others in attendance included: Representatives from Enterprise, North Central Fire Special
Service District, Gunlock Special Service District, Pine Valley Special Service District,
Gold Cross Ambulance, Dammeron Valley Special Service District, Diamond Valley
Special Service District, and Northwestern Special Service District.

REGULAR AGENDA ITEMS FOR CONSIDERATION:

DISCUSSION OF CONSOLIDATION OF FIRE SPECIAL SERVICE DISTRICTS
ALONG THE SR-18 CORRIDOR

Commissioner Victor Iverson welcomed all in attendance and stated that over the past
decade changes have developed in public safety and fire safety. Because of these changes,
discussions of consolidation and changes to the districts have been ongoing.

Commissioner Snow asked everyone to introduce themselves and which district they
represented. He explained the reason for this meeting was to ensure that fire and EMS
services along the SR-18 corridor provide the best service to the residents.

County Deputy Attorney Devin Snow explained that North Central and Pine Valley Special
Service Districts (SSDs) would be consolidated into the proposed Mountain Fire SSD with
Baker Dam Road being the dividing line. The second SSD called Western Fire SSD would
include Dammeron Valley SSD, Diamond Valley SSD and Northwestern SSD. The County
would adopt a Resolution next week to initiate the process of creating the new SSDs. In
December, there will be a hearing on the creation of the new districts with a sixty (60) day
protest period. The goal is to have these new districts ready by March of 2022.

Commissioner Iverson stated that the Commission would appoint the boards with each
board having a Captain or a Chief. The Mountain Fire SSD would have a close relationship
with Enterprise. Chief Moody in Enterprise and Chief Barnes in Dammeron Valley would
remain the fire chiefs of the new boards. The County has agreed to infuse some money into
Dammeron Valley SSD to facilitate a transport license for EMS. There is a total debt of
$276,000 that the County will look to meet in order to create the new SSDs.

Duncan Reed from North Central Fire SSD stated that they currently work well with
Enterprise and do not see any need for change. Commissioner Iverson stated the goal is to
provide better response times for citizens. He further clarified the SSDs are already

functioning in collaboration as a district; the only real change is the boards. Commissioner
Iverson stated that the process would include deciding if the SSDs will charge an
assessment or a mill levy with possible Truth in Taxation as well.

Stuart Bowler from Northwestern SSD stated his concern is the fees associated with
combining districts. Northwestern raised their fee last year to the current one hundred and
thirty dollars ($130) a year to meet the cost of maintenance.

Mayor Brandon Humphries of Enterprise pointed out that Pinto is not included because the
response times are not reachable and there is no water system to support firefighting. Pinto
residents will not be charged for services they cannot use.

Frank Bilostofo of Northwestern SSD explained that a number of firefighters are volunteers
and many may choose not to volunteer under restructured SSDs. He inquired how the
staffing of the fire departments would be addressed. Commissioner Snow questioned why
these volunteers would leave when they would have the same Chief. Mr. Bowler stated that
the board is worried about being replaced by someone out of St. George who doesn’t
understand their community. Commissioner Snow explained that the goal of the
restructuring is to have a minimum level of service while still relying on volunteers and
the existing structure. By consolidating the SSDs there would be better integration, more
unified training and the ability to improve the level of service.

Joseph Stevenson with North Central Fire SSD questioned why money would be spent on
board members instead of firefighters. He further inquired why districts that run efficiently
need to be changed. Commissioner Snow explained that board members are not paid; any
money collected goes into the safety of the community and the safety of firefighters.
Commissioner Iverson explained that the state legislature passed a bill in the last session
that Emergency Medical Service (EMS) is now an essential service that is the responsibility
of the local government. Washington County currently has Gold Cross, Dammeron Valley
that will be requesting a transport license and Chief Moody out of Enterprise. The concept
behind this restructuring is for the volunteer firefighters to continue to cultivate and provide
training. A station would be manned twenty-four (24) hours a day in Dammeron Valley
with two people at all time that are cross-trained in fire and medical transport to
immediately respond to the Western Fire SSD; with a similar setup in Enterprise for the
Mountain Fire SSD. Winchester Hills was left out of the SSDs for geographic reasons; St.
George could provide better service due to close proximity. Commissioner Snow stated
that Winchester Hills could be reevaluated if necessary.

Mary Esther Putnam from Pine Valley SSD questioned how much information from the
Zion’s Public Finance evaluation is being used to make these decisions on restructuring.
She stated that Pine Valley works well with Central and Enterprise and does not see any
reason for the change. Mayor Humphries clarified that Enterprise will be surrounded by
the Mountain Fire SSD but will remain separate from it. Mayor Humphries expressed the
opinion that board members need to be elected instead of appointed. Commissioner Snow
explained that the reason for appointments versus elections are to have people from each

area. Commissioner Iverson further explained that the cost of elections are high and there
is an added burden with an election. He asked that there be an appointed board to start with
the option to change to an elected board if deemed necessary. Devin Snow explained that
appointed boards are easier in general to manage, but in the future, an elected board is
always an option. Currently only Northwestern, Southwest and Diamond Valley have
elected boards. Bob Dalley from Pine Valley SSD stated there have never been issues with
appointed boards and their appointed board there works well and is appointed by the

Mr. Dalley stated that Pine Valley SSD has zero debt and wants to ensure that they stay
fiscally responsible.

Mr. Bilostofo questioned if there would be paid and volunteer firefighters in the same
department and the same station. He stated that structural firefighting and wildland
firefighting are completely different. Chief Chet Barnes from Dammeron Valley SSD
stated that legally he and Chief Moody have to provide EMS. He explained that they have
the ability to train for wildland, structural and EMS in house currently. There is a fire
academy currently happening in Dammeron Valley with twenty-six (26) people currently
enrolled.

Commissioner Iverson stated that this is a process and he appreciates all the countless hours
that have been put into ensuring the communities safety. Commissioner Snow added that
this is a multi-month process and asked everyone to contact the Commissioners if they
have any questions or concerns.

Mack Sorensen from Dammeron Valley SSD praised Chief Barnes hard work and effort in
training and serving the public. He explained that to Chief Barnes it is more than an
appointment and he works very hard to ensure the community is safe. Mr. Sorensen
explained that EMS service in Dammeron Valley is being done and done well currently.

John Berry with Gold Cross Ambulance stated he has worked in Pine Valley and Enterprise
for years and they do a great job. He explained that the national standard for response times
is eight minutes and fifty-nine seconds (8.59) for lights and sirens and eleven minutes and
fifty-nine seconds (11.59) for other events. He questioned the willingness to take his
livelihood away on the SR-18 corridor but the willingness to believe the EMS licensure
alone would be adequate experience responding from Enterprise to Pine Valley. Mr. Berry
questioned why the licensure would be pulled from an agency in Washington County for
the same response times with no tax cost. John Cazier from Diamond Valley SSD
responded that Gold Cross is not providing first responder services effectively. He stated
that the times that Gold Cross has come to Diamond Valley it has taken forty-five to fifty
(45-50) minutes, not even close to the eleven (11) minute standard. Mr. Cazier remarked
that a man in their area had a stroke and needed to be taken to the hospital which was an
eight (8) minute drive and it took Gold Cross forty-eight (48) minutes to arrive and then
wanted a full assessment before they would load him and take him to the hospital.
Commissioner Iverson stated that currently Dammeron Valley has certified first responders

who rely on Gold Cross to transport. He explained that transport is a way to recoup a little

Kathy Vause with the Northwestern SSD stated she has been in Central for twenty-eight
(28) years and believes much of this restructuring is based on the weaknesses within
Central. She remarked that after speaking with her neighbors, most are very excited about
being with Enterprise and the strength they can provide.

Charlie Guthrie with Gunlock SSD questioned what the next steps were. Mr. Snow stated
that on November 1* a Resolution would be passed to start the process. A hearing would
be held in December on proposed creation of the new districts, at which point there is a
sixty (60) day waiting period or protest period. The end date for the process would be
March of 2022. Mr. Snow also reiterated that many of the questions on the day-to-day
decisions would be answered by the boards.

Mr. Bilostofo questioned the ability to receive grants after this restructuring. Commissioner
Snow stated that if anything it would make it much easier to receive the grants and there
would be a broader distribution of resources. Mr. Bilostofo asked that the County help with
grant writing. Commissioner Snow explained that for federal grants, the federal delegation
has the ability to help facilitate those grants.

Having no other business, the meeting was adjourned at 1:20 PM.
