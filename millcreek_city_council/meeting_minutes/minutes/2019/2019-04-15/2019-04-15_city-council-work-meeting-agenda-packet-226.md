Minutes of the
Millcreek City Council
April 15, 2019
5:30 p.m.

Work Meeting

The City Council of Millcreek, Utah, met in a public work meeting on April 15, 2019 at City
Hall, located at 3330 S. 1300 E., Millcreek, UT 84106. Notice for this meeting was posted on
Millcreek’s website, Millcreek’s e-newsletter, and at City Hall but was inadvertently not posted
on the Utah Public Notice Website.

1. Discussion Items

1.1 Review FY 2018-19 Tentative Community Reinvestment Agency Fund Revenue &
Expense Budget; Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director
Mayor Silvestrini said there is almost $1 million in the Community Reinvestment Agency
fund transferred from Salt Lake County for and the property tax generated from the West
Millcreek Community Reinvestment Area in 2018. Laurie Johnson said at the beginning
of the fiscal year there were not any funds to recognize, so this fiscal year and next fiscal
year’s budget will be established at the same time. She went over the revenue budget and
said the previous collections carried forward from the West Millcreek Community
Reinvestment Area was $486,321 and the increment from tax year 2018 was $490,748,
which adds up to the $977,069 reflected in the budget. She said she put a $5,000 place
holder in for interest. Council Member Marchant asked about property tax revenues future
receipt estimates. Johnson said this budget is based on the increments for the community
reinvestment area, not the property taxes collected in the General Fund. Mike Winder said

1.2 Review FY 2019-20 Tentative Community Reinvestment Agency Fund Revenue &
Expense Budget; Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director
Laurie Johnson projected the West Millcreek Community Reinvestment Area property tax
will be $500,000. She said the City can amend the budget every quarter based on actuals.
Mayor Silvestrini asked if the County Assessor/Treasurer assesses work in progress. John
Brems said they take a “snapshot” of work done on a project on January 1 of the
applicable year and then they determine the value.

1.3 Review FY 2019-20 Capital Improvement Project Fund Revenue & Expense Budget;
Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director
Johnson went over the capital improvement fund (CIP) revenue budget and said the
percent change from the previous year decreased because of the increase in the public
works contract. John Miller said he met with Salt Lake County last week and they
committed to try and give Millcreek the same level of service with a $1 million increase
instead of the requested $1.7 million increase. Miller said Millcreek would pay $5 million
for the same level of service provided last year that costed $4 million. Johnson went over
the expense budget and the percentage changes from the previous year; a 39% decrease in
pavement preservation, an 82% decrease in sidewalk projects, a 300% increase in traffic
calming, a 250% increase in storm drain, and a 200% increase in active transportation.
Johnson said projects in the CIP fund this fiscal year could get carried over into the next
fiscal year as opposed to projects in the general fund budget.

Council Member Marchant asked about traffic calming. John Miller said he went over the
draft traffic calming policy previously with the Council. He said the community councils
are responsible in large part for the traffic calming project recommendations. The
community councils’ recommendations go to the City Engineer for a budgetary estimate
and then the estimates go back to the community council for prioritization. Miller said the
traffic calming budget line item went up from $25,000 to $100,000. Council Member Uipi
asked about the old sidewalk request list. Miller said he gave it to the community councils

1.4 Review FY 2019-20 Municipal Fee Schedule; Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director
Laurie Johnson reviewed the proposed fee schedule with the Council. She said feedback
was based on the department directors’ input, and it will be updated about twice a year
moving forward. Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek adopted the fee schedule from Salt Lake
County. He said he has heard complaints from the public that Millcreek charges high fees.
Mayor Silvestrini pointed out references in the business licensing fee section about the
sheriff and said he would talk to Unified Police Department about updating the language.
Elyse Greiner brought up election fees and asked the Council if they wanted to implement
a candidate filing fee. Greiner said in 2017, the candidates paid $50 to file though that
amount was not in city code. The Council decided that a $50 filing fee was reasonable.
They also decided that nomination petitions should not have a filing fee but should require
50 signatures. Kurt Hansen described the International Code Council table in the fee
schedule. He said the fee schedule would refer to the table which gets updated every six
months. Greiner mentioned that the GRAMA fees in the proposed fee schedule differed
from what the Council approved in 2017.

Johnson brought up the budget calendar changes and said a public hearing on the tentative
FY 2019-20 budgets will be held May 13, 2019. She provided the Council with a schedule
of contracts based on budget expenses. Kurt Hansen said in reference to the Salt Lake
County Parks contract, there are no budgetary items for new equipment, repairs, etc. The
budget only addresses the maintenance of parks. Mayor Silvestrini said the City needs to
do master planning on parks.

1.5 Strategic Planning; Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director
Laurie Johnson said she created a spreadsheet for the next 5 fiscal years with the visioning
themes and the related goals and strategies. She said the Council would prioritize the goals
and strategies. She recommended working through one or two vision themes a month. The
Council would determine if the theme strategy was a budgetary or legislative item. She
said it keeps the focus on pertinent themes that are prioritized every year. She said the
budget would then be framed on the strategic plan. John Geilmann said the General Plan
took in a lot of public input over a couple years. He said cities can put their general plans
on the shelves or use them and by incorporating Millcreek’s plan into the budget, it keeps
it alive. He said tying the budget to the General Plan was innovative.

1.7 Mayor and Council’s Discretion on Discussion Topic
Mayor Silvestrini said in the last two weeks, staff noticed that Salt Lake City crews were
cutting into, milling, and patching 1300 E. at and north of Miller Avenue and south of
Elgin Avenue. The crews said they intended to overlay the street to 3300 S. later this year.
The Mayor said staff was wondering why they were doing that when the road is in
Millcreek. Mayor Silvestrini said 1300 E. used to be a state highway and when UDOT
(Utah Department of Transportation) deleted 1300 E. from the state highway system, they
signed a letter delegating to Salt Lake City the responsibility for maintenance and
jurisdictional responsibility from 3300 S. north to University Parkway. The Mayor said
this is a topic to discuss with Salt Lake City and Millcreek staff is meeting with their
representatives this Friday at 3:30 p.m. at the Salt Lake City offices. He said he was not
sure UDOT had the right to convey ownership because state law mandates that when
UDOT deletes a street from the state highway system, they have to give the road to the
jurisdiction in which it is located. Mayor Silvestrini said this happened 12 years ago.
Council Member Jackson asked if Salt Lake City did anything to the road since 2007.
Mayor Silvestrini said he did not have any record of work done since then, but staff is
looking into it. He said Millcreek’s municipal services funds, when Millcreek was
unincorporated, were used to resurface 1100 E., Miller Avenue, and Woodland Avenue.
He said he has asked Salt Lake County for those records. The roads that service the
Brickyard Plaza are 1100 E., 1300 E., Woodland Avenue, Miller Avenue, and 3300 S.,
which is a state highway. He said the taxpayers of Millcreek, through Salt Lake County,
did the last treatments to the streets other than 1300 E. He said Salt Lake City has claimed
in a letter to Millcreek that they have done the lion’s share of maintenance for the roads
surrounding the Brickyard Plaza.

Mayor Silvestrini said he wanted to discuss with Salt Lake City Public Utilities about
effecting repairs in Millcreek. He said they should get an excavation permit to cut into
Millcreek streets and they have not been. He said he will encourage and insist they do it to
ensure the streets are patched to Millcreek’s standards. Council Member Jackson said the
Council needs to know what is going on with city streets because they address the
constituents’ concerns.
