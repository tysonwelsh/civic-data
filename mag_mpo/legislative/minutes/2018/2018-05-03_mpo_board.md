Adopt FY19 Annual Unified Planning Work Program Executive Summary – Shawn Seager The July 1, 2018 to June 30, 2019 (FY 19) Unified Planning Work Program (Work Program) is an annual budget that describes the transportation planning work undertaken by Mountainland Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO) Staff, Utah Department of Transportation Staff and the Utah Transit Authority Staff within the Mountainland MPO planning area. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee May 3, 2018 Page 3 The Work Program illustrates to our members, Federal Highway Administration, Federal Transit Administration, and the general public where our planning resources are allocated, and describes the planned work, budget, and funding sources. The Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) reviewed the FY19 Annual Unified Planning Work Program Executive Summary on April 22, 2018 and recommended that the Regional Planning Committee (RPC) consider approving the Work Program. Upon approval, the Work Program will be included in the Mountainland AOG FY 18-19 Annual Budget and presented to the MAG Executive Council for approval on May 24, 2018. Transportation Improvement Project - MPO Draft Project Priority List – Bob Allen The MPO funds projects in the Transportation Improvement Project (TIP) every two years. The TIP is a capital improvement plan for regional transportation projects that includes: MPO funded projects, UDOT projects, UTA projects and county funded projects. TAC reviewed each submitted concept report and created a ranked project list following the process established by RPC. That process included: • Reviewing submitted concept reports – February 12-15, 2018 • MPO staff scoring each project – March 29, 2018 • Visiting each project for a field review – April 9, 2018 • Voting to create the ranked priority list of projects April 23, 2018 • RPC review Concept Reports Ranking – May 3, 2018 • RPC final project list approval – June 7, 2018 • Create a funding scenario according to approved list - August 2, 2018 There was a general discussion about project priorities, funding sources and allocations, and BRT zero fare funded by CMAQ, in addition to security measures that will be taken when BRT opens. There are approximately $40 million available TIP funds available in 2021-2022. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee May 3, 2018 Page 4 Orem City Corridor Preservation Request – Jim Price Orem City has received a request to purchase a home from an owner at 885 W 1600 N due to medical hardship that is located within the 1600 N corridor, #17 on the Eligible Corridors Map. Because Orem is actively seeking to widen 1600 N, it is anticipated that this property will need to be purchased for that future project. If purchased for the project under eminent domain, the city is required to pay for all relocation costs which would include wheelchair access upgrades in addition to all other costs to acquire and move the owners. In the shorter term, it is also anticipated that the owner will have increased difficulty in selling the home on the open market because of the threat of the future project. This purchase can be classified as a medical hardship mitigation and because this is a willing seller, reduces the future costs associated with forced relocation. Total estimated costs of acquisition: • Appraised property value: $263,000.00 • Estimated costs for appraisal, acquisition agent, closing fees and administrative adjustments: $18,650.00 • Total request: $281,650.00 Estimated Fund balance after approval: $6,918,000 The Technical Advisory Committee voted on April 23, 2019 to recommend to Regional Planning that this request be approved. Mayor Johnson moved that the Regional Planning Committee approve the Orem City Corridor Preservation request for $281,650.00. Council Member Shellie Baertch seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. HCR14 Combining Metropolitan Statistical Area – Andrew Jackson HCR 14, Recognizing Utah’s Robust Economic and Urbanization passed in the last legislative session. It asks the federal government to combine the Provo-Orem, Salt Lake and Ogden metropolitan areas into one single Metropolitan Statistical Area. Due to air quality and other issues, it is recommended that MPO members take a stand to oppose the combination of the three areas into one. Other Business Air Quality Update – Susan Hardy Utah was designated as being marginal in the ozone nonattainment area. That designation is important because it does not require Utah to come up with a state implementation plan, which is a difficult process that creates limits on emissions. Because of this, Utah will be given a reprieve for approximately three years of observations. If, within the three years, Utah does not violate the ozone standards, the state will automatically become a maintenance area. In addition, it has been determined that the future modeling of ambiance emission in Utah shows that a substantial reduction.
