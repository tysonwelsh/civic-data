 1.     A discussion reviewing the progress of Fiscal Year 2024 Council Priorities (24-015)
        (00:13:00)
Justin Harrison, City Council Executive Director, started off the discussion of reviewing the
Council’s 2024 budget priorities. Mr. Harrison showed a list of the budget priorities that were
adopted last year. He said that the goal of this meeting is to determine the goals of 2025, and long-
term goals that fit separately (like CIP projects) and that would not be accomplished in one year.

One of the priorities of the Council was the funding of long-range plans. This includes the General
Plan, the Downtown master plan update, the conservation and resiliency plan, the Gateways plan,
the Canyons and hillsides plan, and the river and lakeshore plan. These long-range plans have been
funded and are ongoing.

The next priority was a Provo-specific housing audit. There was no further action taken after the
Council meetings in January and February 2024. The council staff is monitoring the progress of
the State and the Gardner Institute database.

There was a goal for a five-year staffing plan for Police and Fire. The administration came forth
with what a staffing plan would look like in the next five years. With that, three additional Fire
Captains were funded, two police officers were funded, and one additional police officer was
funded after that. Becky Bogdin asked if these hires were specific to the airport. Mr. Harrison and

Mr. Harrison shared that IS and Cyber Security enhancements were funded last year. Some were
not funded, but most were. The ones that were funded included email security gateway, airport/pw
analyst position, and a CityView update. He said that in the supplemental material, there was an
obnoxious large spreadsheet for all these specific funding sources and projects. He said it has been
updated to reflect that. Ms. MacKay asked if they knew what the unfunded things were. Mr.
Harrison said they were management tools, threat protections, a cybersecurity analyst, a Cayenta
analyst, server hardware support, and an admin assistant. Mr. Harrison assumes the IS department
will request the unfunded things again this year.

Ms. Bogdin said that the city has several positions that are unfunded and how important those
positions are. Mr. Harrison said those are great questions and that this meeting will give context
for when the departments give their individual presentations and what their needs are. Ms. MacKay

understand the needs of the IS department. Mr. Henderson added that there will be cybersecurity
discussions at every budget priority decision meeting. Ms. MacKay said that things from last year's
list that were unfunded should be labeled as critical/non-critical. Mr. Harrison said that Council
staff will report on the current needs of unfunded projects. Ms. Bogdin said that it is hard to see if
this is a priority and won’t until they meet with IS.

Mr. Harrison then talked about employee retention. In FY2024, there was a 4% cost of living
increase. There was funding to match and mitigate the increase in cost of living in Provo.

Mr. Harrison then moved on to City Debt Reduction. That has been a priority of the Council. He
said there is a spreadsheet where all the city’s debt was laid out, and its current financial position.
Mr. Harrison said the City is in a great position considering the amount of debt and the
accompanying interest rates. He made note of some of the long-term debts that will be paid off in
the next few years.
Mr. Harrison then talked about the Provo App and website updates. He said that departments have
been updating and purging the website of old information to move to a new city website more

The second-to-last topic discussed was the Southeast Grocery Store. Supermarkets depend on
market research, and it's a private market decision for businesses to choose a relocation location.
Mr. Keith Morey added that it is a difficult situation because grocers have specific standards and
criteria they evaluate. Even in areas where all the necessary elements were present, they found that
it was not the right time for grocers to move. He emphasized the importance of supporting existing
businesses like Days Market. Ms. Bogdin mentioned hearing a rumor about a plan for a small
office building in the south of Provo. Mr. Morey mentioned uncertainty about what would happen
to the rest of the property as no one has stepped up for it yet. One significant challenge is that
Provo has the lowest average income in Utah County, so grocers often look elsewhere. Mr.
Handley expressed confusion about the logic of not putting a market in Southeast Provo since
people need groceries regardless. Mr. Morey explained that the type of grocer matters because
they have different criteria for choosing locations, including a loyalty element. Ms. MacKay shared
feedback from citizens in the Southeast, noting that they want Walmart prices rather than the higher
prices often found in smaller local grocery stores. Mr. Handley emphasized considering different
scales of grocery stores and believes that while some may still go to Walmart for certain items, the
convenience of a local grocery store will attract business. Mr. Morey mentioned that grocery stores
seek locations where people shop frequently. Ms. Bogdin asked about how Target at the mall might
help, and Mr. Morey said it would have a positive impact.

 2.      A discussion regarding Council Priorities for Fiscal Year 2025 (24-015) (01:51:56)
Justin Harrison opened the discussion to the Council’s priorities for FY2025. There were 29 topics
submitted by the councilors and staff, split into five categories. The first was Citizen Engagement
which includes language accessibility, matching grants, and social service education. The second
category was employee retention which includes cost of living adjustment (COLA) and third-party
compensation analysis. Ms. MacKay said that the exact same COLA is going to occur this year
too. The third category was housing. This includes Land Trust, Lodging for Victims of Domestic
Abuse, Missing Middle Housing, Owner Occupancy, Pocket Neighborhoods, RDA, Rental
Dwelling License Study, Starter Homes, and Zoning Updates. Fourth category was Parks and Open
Space which includes bathrooms, dog parks, open space and trail improvements, and Slate Canyon.
The fifth category is Public Safety which includes animal control, code enforcement, flock
cameras, IS and cyber security, and police and fire staffing plans. Mr. Harrison opened the meeting
to discussion.
