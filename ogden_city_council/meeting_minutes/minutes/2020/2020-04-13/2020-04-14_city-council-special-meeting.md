Proposed Ordinance 2020-15 adopting the Capital Improvement Plan for
Fiscal Years 2021-2025
          A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Capital hnprovement Plan (CIP) for Fiscal Years (FY) 2021-2025
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated as part of the CIP process, the City Council discusses and considers
projects proposed in the plan, incorporates changes as desired, receives public input, then adopts the plan. The CIP is intended to
prioritize capital project funding for the FY2021 budget. Council Staff will review the proposed FY2021-2025 CIP.
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the
Council staffs overview of the FY2021-2025 CIP Plan. The objectives of the Plan include scheduling, financing and constructing
City capital improvements in the upcoming fiscal year and projected for the following four fiscal years. CIP projects are those
exceeding $30,000 adding go the City's "physical plant", projects resulting in the acquisition or long-term lease ofland, and/or
feasibility studies and evaluations. CIP funding sources include: the General Fund, federal and state grants, the Utah Department
of Transportation Class B&C road funds, utility enterprise funds, bonds, and other miscellaneous sources. The CIP Plan identifies
City needs, establishes long-term goals for investment, ensures expenditures are consistent with the General Plan, and informs the
budget to set priorities. Like the budget itself, the CIP is a policy document that reflects the values and goals of the City (both the
Administration and City Council). Ms. Mabey reviewed the timeline for considering the FY2021-2025 CIP, noting that the
schedule has been adjusted in response to the COVID-19 pandemic. The current CIP proposal for consideration includes 45 total
projects; 19 new projects are added, 20 have been removed, and three existing projects have beeri significantly increased (defmed
as greater than 20 percent in total project cost). The total for all projects included is $106,507,997. She provided a CIP project
breakdown by department and fund, after which she identified Administration's top ten projects, which total $13,489,000:
               1. 23 rd and 25 th Street Bus Rapid Transit - EN007
               2. Fire Station #4 - F1032
               3. Marshall White Center - RG00l
               4. General Facilities - FL007

              Project highlights in the proposed CIP include removing six airport projects and adding three; Fire Station 4 update and
    Fire Training Facility replacement; majority of Lester Park funding planned for FY2022; Marshall White Center general
    improvements; $20,000 decrease in funding for sidewalks, curb and gutter; and the addition of Water and Sanitary Sewer Master
    Plan projects. The next steps in the process include additional Council dialogue, the Council's ranking of Administration's top ten
    projects, holding a public hearing on April 28, 2020, and adoption of the Plan to inform the FY2020-2021 budget. Ms. Mabey
    concluded by referencing Council staff documentation that provides inqividual project smmnaries to accompany the project briefs
    provided by City Administration.
              Council member White asked ifthere is a City ordinance prescribing a deadline for Departments to submit proposed CIP
    project briefs or if they can be submitted at any point throughout the year. Ms. Mabey stated the project information briefs are
    included in the CIP docmnent that is recommended to the City Council by Administration and the Planning Commission.
    Including a new project in the CIP requires a formal CIP amendment. Council member White inquired as to the process for
    pursuing a CIP amendment. Ms. Mabey stated that Administration or the City Council can suggest an amendment to the CIP, but a
    vote must be taken in a public meeting to allow for the amendment.
              Planning Manager Montgomery then referred to discussion held during the April 7, 2020 work session meeting regarding
    the Planning Commission's recommendation relative to the CIP; he reiterated that it is the Planning Commission's role to evaluate
    the Plan's conformance with the City's General Plan. They found that projects AR002 and AR008 are not consistent with the
    General Plan until they are included in the Airp01t Master Plan; a formal action to include those projects in the Airport Master
    Plan will take place during the Planning Commission's April 15, 2020 meeting. They also recommended a change to the status of
    the CIP project GF032, but they did not have the authority to make that recommendation. He concluded the Commission
    ultimately determined to recommend approval of Administration's proposed CIP based on its consistency with the General Plan.

Public Comments
          Chair Choberka summarized the public comments that were provided in writing and that were included in the packet for
the meeting at the following link: https://www.ogdencity.com/DocumentCenter/View/12314/4-14-20-5a-Written-Public-
Comments. She invited Council members to address any of the public comments if they so desire.
          Council member Hyer stated that he does not want to respond to any specific public comments, but noted that the
Council is reviewing all public comments that are being submitted.
          Chair Choberka acknowledged Ms. Lawrence's comments about the fact that the Pandemic is impacting people of color
at a greater rate than other races. Chair Choberka noted this is not unique to Ogden, but is a nationwide issue. She also invited a
response to Ms. Umbel's comments about spring clean-up services offered in other communities and whether that is something
Ogden City could offer. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith stated that Ms. Umbel has been referred to the City's Public
Services Department to get more information about the Dial-a-Dumpster program, which replaced the City's spring cleanup
service several years ago.
          Chair Choberka then referenced comments made by the Mayor during tonight's work session about the personal
protective equipment the City has made available to public safety employees and essential employees in other City Departments.
Finally, she referred to Ms. Castillo's comments about the misunderstanding relative to the Planning Commission's authority to
recommend an amendment to the CIP; she noted Ms. Castillo will have an opportunity to provide public comments at the public
hearing scheduled for consideration of adoption of the CIP.

Mayor Comments
         Mayor Caldwell stated that it is National Dispatcher's Week and it is important to recognize dispatchers for the key role
they plan in public safety response. Dispatchers work around the clock all year and he believes that the City's dispatch team is one
of the best in the State of Utah and the entire nation. The City will be recognizing dispatchers and other medical workers during a
ceremony at 4:00 p.m. tomorrow. He then noted he received a call from his brother, who is an emergency room doctor for
Intermountain Health Care, about his assignment to report to a New York City hospital to aid in COVID response; he is proud of
his brother and all medical workers who are on the front lines. He then noted he appreciates the residents of Ogden City for their
patience and support; this is an unprecedented time and it is important for everyone to work together to 'flatten the curve'.

Council member Comments
         Chair Choberka thanked the Council, City Administration, City staff members, and residents for their dedication and all
they are doing to help keep the community safe. She stated that it can be tempting to resume normal life given the nice spring
weather, but it is important to keep in mind that any activity that takes someone outside of their home could essentially endanger
someone else's life.
