29   2) Presentation of updated Starter Home Attainable Residential (SHAR) ordinance.
30
31   Brittney Harris presented the current iteration of SHAR (previously AHOZ, CHOZ).
32
33   The major changes to the ordinance since the last time the Planning Commission saw a
34   presentation include: the ordinance only being permitted in zones with half-acre lots or smaller;
35   requirements for the lots must be under 6000 square feet and the homes under 1200 square feet;
36   and deed restrictions for owner occupancy long-term. This current version has been through
37   multiple rewrites with input from engineering, the Lehi City Council, the Mayor, and others. The
38   city also would be able to use this version of SHAR as an alternative to ADUs by focusing on
39   adding density in detached single-family home neighborhoods that provide affordable ownership
40   instead of rental units.
41
42   Commissioner Eyre asked if deed restrictions ever fall off the properties. They do not.
43
44   Extra density for these developments is determined by taking the normal maximum allowed units
45   for a developer, multiplied by 1.5 and rounded up to the nearest whole number. Lot specifics
46   have also been determined, including side yard setbacks that accommodate for future garages;
47   unfinished basements; and maximum allowed square footage. The city is aiming to develop
48   homes that cost less than $400,000.

50   Commissioner Eyre asked if developers could charge more for finishing a basement. Harris
51   explained that finishing a basement likely wouldn’t be allowed under the development’s terms.
52   Commissioner Eyre asked if lot sizes can equal 6000 square feet. Lot sizes must be under 6000
53   square feet as that is the minimum lot size for an ADU.
54
55   Attainable homes in developments will be required to be constructed with similar design and
56   materials as other homes in the subdivision, to maintain high quality construction. A reduced
57   public right of way is a possibility that is being explored.
58
59   The process of approval for subdivisions will be as follows: developers create a concept plan,
60   which is gone over by the DRC. After that it goes to the City Council, who can write a
61   development agreement. The development agreement goes back to DRC, and then before the
62   Planning Commission, after which it goes back to City Council for final approval.
63
64   Commissioner Kunze asked how the concept functions as an ordinance as opposed to the overlay
65   zone it was previously proposed as. Harris explained that any units allotted by zoning could
66   follow normal code, and the SHAR requirements only applied to the bonus density granted by
67   the ordinance. Commissioner Jackson asked if there were any invested rights in the ordinance.
68   Harris explained that there are not, as the ordinance would be a legislative item, not an
69   administrative one, as the overlay zone previously was. The Planning Commission discussed
70   their role in the approval process. Kim Struthers explained that the process in whole is
71   discretionary at the level of the City Council. The Planning Commission wouldn’t have the
72   ability to stop an applicant or force it through. Struthers explained that he sees this as a tool that
73   developers can ask for, and that it must be voluntary. As such, it’s a development agreement with
74   no invested rights.
75
76   Commissioner Eyre asked to see a zoning map of the city so the Commission could see where
77   the ordinance could be implemented. He also asked how lot size was determined. Harris
78   explained that engineering suggested testing the ordinance on smaller zoning. Commissioner
79   Jackson asked for more distinction between this and an overlay zone. Harris explained that an
80   overlay zone would allow for by-right development, whereas an ordinance is discretionary.
81
82   Commissioner Eyre asked how the $400,000 figure was determined. Commissioner Jackson
83   asked if the figure was adjustable for inflation. Harris explained that the figure can be adjusted
84   through the City Council, so long as justification was provided. The figure was selected as it is
85   closer to an ‘attainable’ home than other prices, while still being possible to achieve by
86   developers. Commissioner Eyre suggested using a percentage of the AMI. Harris explained that
87   there are many factors that go into that percentage, and as such it’s difficult to determine.
88   Commissioner Jackson shared that he sees the price as more of a starting point for a conversation
89   with developers than a hard and fast rule. Kim Struthers explained that impact fees are variable,
90   but usually around $17,000-$18,000. Commissioner Lockhart asked if the city is offering a
91   discount on impact fees for the applicable lots. The city will not be offering any discounts.
92
93   Commissioner Jackson asked if there are any limits on how large unfinished basements were
94   allowed to be. Harris explained that enlarging basements would increase the price of the finished
95   project, so the limiting factor would be the $400,000 price point. Kim Struthers mentioned that

 97   Commissioner Eyre worries about forcing homes to be ramblers if this policy is implemented.
 98
 99   Brittney Harris clarified that the two concern points for the city were home price and utility
100   capacity.
101
102   Commissioner Lockhart asked who would manage the deed restriction. Harris explained that the
103   city could pursue legal action if renters are reported. Commissioner Lockhart asked if there were
104   any other owner-occupied deed restrictions. The city has not issued any, but they have been
105   implemented by HOAs or by individual owners. Commissioner Jackson shared that while he
106   likes requiring the home to be purchased by an occupant, he doesn’t like the idea of setting up
107   deed restrictions in perpetuity. Commissioner Lockhart asked if the city had any other tools to
108   achieve the same means. Harris explained that owner-occupancy could be written into the
109   ordinance, but that a deed restriction would give the city more teeth to pursue legal action if
110   needed. Commissioner Jackson shared fears of creating a nanny state by having perpetual deed
111   restrictions. Harris explained the purpose of the deed restrictions was to maintain the homes as
112   starter homes for more than one occupant. Commissioner Lockhart shared frustration that this
113   would minimize the value of the home as an investment. Harris explained that the city’s goal is
114   to develop homes as shelters rather than as assets. Commissioner Jackson was worried about
115   legislating for populations down the line.
116
117   Kim Struthers compared property rights to a bundle of sticks. Not all properties have all property
118   rights. A deed restriction is one aspect of property rights, and these homes would be purchased
119   with the awareness of that deed restriction. Commissioner Jackson shared fears over the current
120   state of the market, and that potential buyers are not as able to pick and choose properties as they
121   used to be. Commissioner Eyre shared his agreement with Struthers, and that he likes that the
122   deed restriction will keep the market moving. He sees purchasing the house with the deed
123   restriction in the same vein as buying a home for the view—it’s not a right the owner ever
124   possessed. Commissioner Lockhart feels like the ordinance is a major deviation from the General
125   Plan. Commissioner Roberts also worries about deviating from the General Plan. Harris
126   explained that density increases are happening anyway due to ADUs, and that the ordinance will
127   be better for development, as density will be planned for in utilities design. She also explained
128   that the General Plan is a snapshot of the city based off a certain point in time, and that changes
129   to the world can affect it. Commissioner Roberts worries that this is equivalent to buying
130   property in one zone and attempting to change it to another. Struthers and Harris explained that
131   there are rights and pathways to request and achieve zone changes.
132
133   Harris finished her presentation by explaining that cities are adjusted to a much slower rate of
134   development than Lehi is currently experiencing. Trying to keep neighborhoods at similar
135   densities has resulted in massive housing pressure in the city and the construction of density-high
136   housing in other areas. Commissioner Roberts asked if the ordinance would be an adjustment for
137   the master plan. Harris explained that while the density is increased, it will be accounted for in
138   regard to utilities and infrastructure.
