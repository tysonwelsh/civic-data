       Presenter. Jennifer Robison.

Mrs. Robison presented the staff report and noted that the matter was continued from the
December 7, 2022, meeting. The public hearing was opened and left open for this meeting.
Mrs. Robison reviewed the location of the subject property and the surrounding area. She
explained that the General Plan identifies the subject property as Mixed Use. The other Geneva
Rock property is currently zoned Sand and Gravel (SG-1) where the mining continues to take
place. The subject property is across the street on Porter Rockwell Boulevard and isolated from
the rest of the Geneva Rock property. Therefore, they were seeking to rezone the property for a
commercial and residential use that fits within the Mixed-Use category. The subject property is
currently zoned SG-1 and needs to be changed for the subject property only to allow for
development. Mrs. Robison identified the location of a future traffic light proposed on a major
collector road. The traffic light will also allow for nearby commercial opportunities.

Mrs. Robison next reviewed the proposed Conceptual Plan for the subject property. One of the
possible adjustments to the proposal, based on feedback provided at the last Planning Commission
meeting, was to consider the viability of making the property all commercial with no residential.
The original document specified up to 65,000 square feet of commercial. The proposed text now
specifies a minimum of62,000 square feet ofcommercial with the allowance for the expansion of
square footage for commercial development. The residential cap remained at 50 townhome units.

Mrs. Robison noted that at the previous meeting,there was discussion regarding densities and how
they work when looking at residential and commercial development. She asked Jared Morgan
from GWC Capital to show how the densities were being conceptualized for the entire property.
The residential use, if there were 50 townhome units on 7.51 acres, would have a density of
approximately 6.66 units per acre. That density was consistent with the overall density of the
Independence project. As a point of reference, the density for the Banner Hill Project that was
approved at the last Planning Commission Meeting was 7.25 units per acre. She noted that the

remainder would be landscaped and passive open spaces. She clarified that overall density was

In response to Commissioner Flynn's question regarding whether on-street parking would be

change to the construction timeline to specify that commercial pads and residential units need to

location would be determined in connection with the site plan approval. She stated that traffic
studies would be a required element of the site plan review and approval process, as well. Final
widths and designs of streets would be determined by the City Engineer based on the traffic
analysis during the site plan process.

5.        PUBLIC HEARING. CONSIDERATION. AND RECOMMENDATION on
          Amendments to the Moderate-Income Housing Element of the City of Bluffdale
          General Plan.(Application 2022-36). Staff Presenter. Ellen Oakman.

Associate City Plarmer, Ellen Oakman,presented the staffreport and stated that the City submitted
its Moderate-Income Housing Report per HB-462. The State Code had more robust and specific
requirements than the previous Code. The City submitted its report by the October 1, 2022,
                      BLUFFDALE CITY PLANNING COMMISSION
                                      MEETING MINUTES
                                  Wednesday,January 4,2023

deadline. The report included the City's strategies and implementation plans that were approved
by the City Council. However,in November,the City, along with about 60 other municipalities,
received a letter stating that a few revisions needed to be made to meet the State Code. Bluffdale
City is required to provide more specific timelines for the implementation ofone ofthe strategies.
The changes resulted in a melding of the five previous strategies into three solid strategies. The
City Council must adopt the revised document by February 16,2023. The City will need to provide
another report in 2023 with even more detail on the implementation. Mr. Crowell commented that
the State thinks this is a very important topic, so the requirements are more rigorous and specific.
The State is scrutinizing each city's plan very carefully. Some cities are being singled out as good
or bad examples. This issue is something that Bluffdale needs to monitor carefully.
Ms.Oakman read the revised strategies from page 45 ofthe packet. Each ofthe strategies includes
action items and timelines for accomplishment ofthe strategies, which Ms.Oakman next reviewed.
Mr. Crowell noted that budget adjustments have been made to achieve the objectives.

Ms. Oakman reviewed the actions that have been accomplished, including the Senior Housing
Zone,The Centrum SD-X zone, and the ongoing planning for an assisted living facility.

Mr. Crowell reported that one idea that was considered for the future is a Community Land Trust,
which is not very common in Utah. However, that idea seemed to confuse the reviewers. For a
city the size of Bluffdale there must be at least three strategies, so the City opted to streamline its
strategies.

Chair Cragun requested that the Planning Commission be kept apprised of any requirements that
are implemented by the State in addition to what is already occurring with the City Code.

Ms. Oakman commented that the State wants more specificity with target dates and dates of
accomplishments, so the report includes more dates. Tlie report includes construction goals for
the year, which will make the report more tangible. Mr. Crowell stated that he has attended a
variety of meetings to get additional training and to share with other municipal leaders to discuss
compliance with HB 462.

Chair Cragun noted that Bluffdale only has a certain amount ofspace to continue building, so she
wanted to know ifthe State Law provides space limitations. Mr. Crowell stated that construction
could go taller. For example, a building could be razed and replaced with a taller building. He
reported that in Bluffdale about45% ofthe housing is multi-family, which is a high percentage for
Utah. He stated that there are a variety of options. The looming question was where Bluffdale
could place more housing if it was required. The State Legislature indicated that a key
consideration would be the location oftransportation facilities, such as state routes, arterial roads,
transit hubs, Redwood Road, Porter Rockwell Boulevard, 14600 South, and Bangerter Highway.
The City still has funds from Economic Development Areas("EDA")and Redevelopment Areas
("RDA")but those funds will eventually expire and stop accumulating. The proposed plan will
be reviewed by the City Council at least annually.
