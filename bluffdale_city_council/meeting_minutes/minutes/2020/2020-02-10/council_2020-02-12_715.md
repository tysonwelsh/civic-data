Johnny Loumi s Jr. stated that everything revo lves around funding . He recommended that they be
vigilant and aware of federal funding that is available.

Mayor Timothy reported that Bangerter Highway and Mountain View Corridor are the State's top
priorities to address congestion. The State is focused on regional proj ects, which will cost a
significant amount of money. To receive funding from the State, the C ity needs to include a
regionally based project when proposing individual City projects.

Traci Crockett commented that zoning and the General Plan for 14600 South should be addressed
since they will drive economic development into the C ity. Mayor Timothy stated that 14600 South
is the largest drawback to bringing economic development. To be financially stable as a City,
economic development is critical. It was noted that choices regarding economic development are
significant now and in the future.

Mr. Loumi s remarked that the gravel pit and the land around and to the south of Po1ier Rockwell
Boulevard is an area of potential economic developm ent and a priority. Dave Kallas commented
that the gravel pit is on the list and the project is 50% complete. Commissioner Loumis stated that
revenue was never discussed.

Traci Crockett asked if the trestle bridge would be considered part of the 14600 South priority.
Mr. Crowell responded that it was one component. Council Member Crockett commented that
getting from the rooftops to retail businesses will be very difficult without widening 14600 South.
City Manager, Mark Reid explained that extending 14600 South to Redwood Road is staff's top
priority. Now that it is in the plans to be completed, the next priority is to determine whether
widening 14600 South is a priority over completing the trestle bridge.

Mayor Timothy stated that the Legislature does not see value in the road and trestle bridge. In
order to get funding to complete the trestle, there needs to be progress on the completion of 14600
South. The plans need to be analyzed to determine what can be done now to accomplish the long-
term goal for 14600 South while continuing to work on the broader plan. The study on the trestle
will take time and be much easier to get funding for if the environmental document is complete.
Improvement costs were briefly discussed.

Mr. Crowell confirmed that staff was purs uing an environmental document to receive federal
funding. Mayor Timothy commented that it is difficult to know what federal funds will be
awarded, which makes it a challenge to plan for. His preference was to prepare the environmental
document on the trestle and in the meantime improve 14600 South with City funds. He noted that
environmental documents use the most extreme cases to determine what is to be done, which does
not always have a desirable outcome. For this reason, he did not want to conduct an environmental
study on 14600 South but acknowledged that it would be useful for the trestle.

Mr. Crowell addressed the studies and plans for a wider road on 14600 South and the history of
residents desiring to keep the trestle. Assistant City Engineer, Trevor Andra, addressed the
envirom11ental documents and funding for 14600 South and the trestle. He explained that
separation of the area is possible to keep federa l money concentrated on the trestle. Mayor
Timothy remarked that the State may help with funding to improve it.

Jeff Gaston reported that Draper is currently petitioning for another lane for a freeway exit. As a
result, it would be wise for Bluffdale to also petition for an additional lane on the opposite side of
the exit. He was not sure if they were petitioning for State or Federal funding. Mr. Crowell stressed
the importance of coordinating and persuading UDOT to understand the needs of the City. Mayor
Timothy commented that State funding would be a piece of it, and Federal funding would cover
the trestle.

Mark Hales remarked that a very aggressive plan is needed for 14600 South to avoid wasting time.
Mark Hales stated that focusing on the entry points could be completed this year. His priority was
the recreation issue, social media marketing, and community involvement, all of which would be
considered sho1t-term. Mayor Timothy commented that work needs to begin inunediately on
whatever proj ect is decided on.

Dave Kallas' priority was to update the General Plan and the permitted uses; however, he was
willing to postpone them for a year to ensure that the resources are included in a market study for
the grocery store. He felt that could be completed this year. Another priority would be to transition
the Economic Development position to full-time. This would increase staffs capacity to complete
other projects. Traci Crockett remarked that zoning for development in the City would be a greater
priority than the market study. Dave Kallas agreed with the impo1tance of the zoning but felt that
the market study could impact decisions on the General Plan. Council Member Crockett agreed
but questioned whether the market study would provide the desired information.

M r. Reid commented that even with an increased amount of economic development in the City,
there still was not a grocery store in Bluffdale. Traci Crockett was unsure that should be the focus
because if there are not enough rooftops, retail businesses will not come. Commissioner Loumis
stated that the City is in a difficult situation. He stressed the importance of bringing in a grocery
store and increasing the number of rooftops where possible. There was discussion about rooftops
and one-acre zoning. The supermarket study would be more specific to understand what Bluffdale
needs to do to appeal to grocery store developers. Mayor Timothy stated that if the study is a
blueprint for a plan, that should be the priority.

In response to a comment, Mr. Crowell reported that he placed a copy of a Supermarket Strategic
Analysis in Dropbox. He reported that vendors approached the City and described the process he
has gone through to choose a company for the supermarket study. The cost is $50,000 and could
be completed in 90 days or Jess. Mr. Crowell addressed the study g uidelines, reviewed possible
locations for a grocery store, and described the benefits of moving forward w ith the proposal. A
