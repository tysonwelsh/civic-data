       Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained the current home occupation
ordinance and exceptions.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said he had received an email that was sent from Commissioner
Cluff to Mayor Staggs. He said that a conditional use permit or home occupation is something
that can be done outside of the dwelling of the home. This must be designated and must go
through the approval process. He doesn’t see any part of the ordinance that would prohibit any
type of agricultural type home occupation.

Mr. Carter explained there is an application fee for a home occupation. This is a one-time fee,
not an annual fee. His biggest concern is short term traffic coming in and out of neighborhoods

for a home occupation. He doesn’t believe anything in our City code is prohibitive or difficult to
overcome.

Mayor Staggs said that the examples cited show that home occupation permits are allowed but
must go through an application process. He asked the Council if they had heard from
constituents that this process is too burdensome.

Councilmember McDougal said he had heard just the opposite from his constituents and that
residents are using their homes as a retail business. Mr. Lethbridge said this is why the
permitting process is essential because the City can establish the conditions upfront.
Councilmember McDougal would like these permits regulated so that they do not expand
beyond the control of the City.

Mr. Carter said there are a couple of ways to approach the problem and asked the Council if
they would like more or less latitude for agricultural uses. He also asked if the City has set the
bar too high or too low for home occupations without distinguishing between the two types of
uses.

Councilmember McCay said she hadn’t had any complaints in her district. She asked why the
business licensing fee was zero and the home occupation fee was $175. She would support
decreasing the home occupation fee from $175 to $100.

Councilmember Stewart asked if there is a difference in the application process for different
production levels for agricultural uses. Councilmember Buroker agrees with Councilmember
McCay about the fee for $175 for conditional use permits being reduced.

Mayor Staggs said the fee for the home occupation covers staff time, time in front of the
Planning Commission and mailing and noticing. Mr. Carter agreed that the City has out-of-
pocket costs for these home occupation permits, especially staff time and mailings. He believes
the City may be losing money on these applications. Mr. Lethbridge said the $175 application
fee is for conditional use permits that must come before the Planning Commission. A significant
number of home business license applications are not charged a fee.

         Discussion of Eliminating Legal Nonconforming Use Rights Under Utah Code 10-
         9a-511(2)(b) and Other Fee Corrections

Mayor Staggs explained that the Council has determined that pawn shops and title loan
businesses can only have one location per 10,000 residents and must be in commercial
regional zones. Some of these businesses are not in commercial regional zones but have been
grandfathered in, even though ownership has been transferred multiple times. Mayor Staggs
asked Mr. Carter to look into this problem and let the Council know if there are any options for
eliminating nonconforming use rights.

Mr. Carter explained the legislature had created a pathway to which cities can consider the
termination of legal nonconforming use rights. He gave the example of Provo City and its failure
to require owner occupancy for home rentals. Provo City created a formula to allow owners to
amortize out the properties that were not owner-occupied.

Mr. Carter further explained that the formula for eliminating legal nonconforming use must be
sound and the calculation must give the benefit of the doubt to the property owner.

Mayor Staggs asked the Council to determine what uses and locations they would like to
amortize out. Mr. Carter recommended targeting nonconforming uses in the downtown area.
All Councilmembers agreed with this recommendation.

Mayor/Council/Manager Reports

David Brickey, City Manager, said the City had purchased a generator to be used for City Hall.
Since the purchase of the generator, installation costs have gone up considerably.

Mr. Hicks said this would be a rollover and a budget amendment. The City has gone out to bid
to have the generator installed in two different areas. He asked the Council to determine if they
would like to install the generator or sell it and what location to install it, by the police generator
or behind the court area.

Mayor Staggs said putting the generator behind the court area is cheaper. Councilmember
Stewart said that separate locations are better and voted to install the generator behind the
court area. All Councilmembers agreed to install the generator behind the court area.

         Water Supply Issues – Report by Sherrie L. Ohrn of a proposed property tax
         Increase by Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District for Water Service Purposes

Sherrie L. Ohrn, Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, reported that Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy will be having a tax increase this year and will have a truth in taxation hearing on
August 10 at 6 PM at the district offices. They will use the additional tax money to build new
infrastructure to accommodate the growth in the valley.

Ms. Ohrn said that residents need to start conserving water. She said the park strips and big
lawns need to go away. She asked that cities look at ordinances and standards to encourage
water conservation.

Mayor Staggs told Ms. Ohrn that Riverton City had adopted the water conservancy standards.
He also wanted the conservancy district to know that 100% of Riverton residents are connected
to secondary water and that the City has already installed 1/3 of the secondary water meters.

Councilmember Wells asked whether car washes recycle their water. Ms. Ohrn said that she
believes they do and that cities need to ensure their ordinances include water conservancy
standards for these types of uses.

Councilmember McDougal expressed frustration for the water conservancy messaging. He said
there is water for the existing population but not for the increased growth. He believes the
infrastructure costs should be passed on to the new growth. Ms. Ohrn said she will pass this
information on to the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District.
