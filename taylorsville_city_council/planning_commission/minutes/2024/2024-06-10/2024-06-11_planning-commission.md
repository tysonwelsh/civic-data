                                   OTHER MATTERS

 3.       Planning Commission Review and Discussion of the Taylorsville General
          Plan Update – Chapter 3: Land Use (Mark McGrath, AICP / Long-Range
          Planner)


Long-Range Planner Mark McGrath explained that the proposed updated Land Use Map
was now available. He gave a brief overview of some of the changes.

He described the new land use categories or “place types.” One of the goals of the update
was to attempt to find ways to accommodate additional housing which was greatly
needed. The plan was to funnel 90% of the future growth into 3% of Taylorsville’s
geographic footprint. This would require some high intensity development.

Mr. McGrath reviewed the eight site-specific areas which were deserving of more detailed
analysis within the plan. He asked for feedback regarding potentially joining Taylor’s
Landing with the adjacent property in some fashion, assuming the property owners were
interested. He requested input from the planning commissioners on that possibility.
Commissioner Wright expressed interest but wished to know how residents would feel
about it. Commissioner Wendel pointed out that it would not necessarily affect current
residents since it could happen as many as twenty-five years down the road.
Commissioner Quigley expressed excitement about the possibilities.

Regarding the 4800 South Historic District, the possibility of having a community for active
seniors was discussed. Commissioner Wendel discussed the need for redevelopment of
the southwest quadrant of 5400 South Redwood Road shopping area, specifically
challenges with accessing the various businesses.

Mr. McGrath sought the Commission’s input regarding the best land use for the area of
Redwood Road south of I-215. Commissioner Wright pointed out how challenging the site
was unless an additional access point were made available. He suggested that low-
density residential might be the most appropriate use. Although Mr. McGrath recognized
the appeal of that, he felt it was unlikely due to the proximity to the freeway. He
encouraged them to continue to contemplate the possibilities of that area.

Regarding the area of 5400 South Bangerter Highway, the Volta project was still planned,
although the developers had run into financing challenges. Kearns planned their own
town center near there and Mr. McGrath expressed the desire to make this a more
attractive area. In fact, Kearns had recently approached him about the possibility of
creating a transportation land use plan for that portion of 5400 South.

As for the old Westbrook Elementary site, Mr. McGrath explained that
Community/Economic Development Director Wayne Harper suggested an affordable
housing development of some sort—perhaps low- or medium-density housing. One
possibility was to earmark the space specifically for housing of educators within Granite
School District. Commissioner Wendel felt strongly that Westbrook should continue to
remain available for public use. Commissioner Quigley agreed.
