Monthly Financial Report 
• Chair Bernal introduced the monthly financial report. The core of this meeting today is our next item, and 
it’s more than the standard monthly financial report. CFO Aniban started the presentation. This is a new 

2 
 
format we’re presenting to the Board; we’ll take a bit more time and explain it, item by item. The objective 
is to have a report that is more useful to you; this report also goes to investors and lenders as they normally 
ask for financial reports, so we’re trying to develop reports that fulfill both purposes. And we are looking for 
feedback from you to improve this as we go along. This is not just a financial report, this is a finance report 
for the month covering the financial report and other aspects of the finance operations.  
The first report on the screen is the Finance report which covers the financial statement, balance sheet, and 
income statement. These are combined. Then comes the Cash Reserve Benchmark, as these are important 
for us to monitor our cash balances as well, and which funds are reserved, and which are available to use. 
We added, as new to you, the Grant Portfolio, which is a snapshot taken out of the HUD website and 
included in the report. And the last part is our Housing Choice Voucher program, which is one of the most 
important programs to our agency, as this captures around fifty percent of our agency’s operations.  
• We need to ensure we’re tracking our lease-up regularly and the money that comes to us. We have to look 
at it from a strategic point of view so in case the worst happens, we can communicate right away if we have 
to one: pull vouchers, and two: we can manage our cash position to ensure the impact is minimal as far as 
the program operations. 
• If you look at Public Housing, Housing Choice Voucher program, up through Supportive Housing & Resident 
Services, this is a legal entity, this is what you practically call Housing Connect. The ownership of this 
program, including assets within the program, are legally owned by Housing Connect. The blended 
component units are legally an entity in itself, and we own them, but they’re also an independent LLC, so 
they are presented differently. And then the Discretely Presented Component are all the LLCs currently 
owned by investors; this includes Kelly Benson Apartments, Bud Bailey Apartments, The Hub, Bodhi, Hunter 
Hollow, East 72, and Granger. We control them as a managing member, but presently they are owned by tax 
credit entities, still within the 15-year compliance period. The terms Blended Component Units and 
Discretely Presented Component units are audit terms. 
• Central Office Cost Center is unique for HUD or PHAs; for reporting purposes, as we are an independent 
agency, we’re allowed to charge management fees, which go into the Central Office. Also, our LIHTC and 
other managed units pay management fees which also go into the Central Office cost center. Central Office 
Cost Center is also used for our cash flow administration as we have a central disbursing account. Business 
Activities is where we want to grow; we can use it without attachment or you can use it to support 
affordable housing programs, or to develop more properties and expand our portfolio without HUD 
assistance. 
• CEO Kimball commented when CFO Aniban reviewed the Reserve Statement, he went over how much we 
need and reserve in each area. We’re getting close but still need more to feel comfortable. Commissioner 
Litvack asked what is our Unrestricted amount target? CFO Aniban answered five to six million. The Board 
thanked CFO Aniban and his team for their hard work and the new format which makes it clear to everyone 
what the numbers represent and how it’s laid out in a very generic, yet specific, easier format to 
understand. For additional details, please refer to the financial information in the June Board book.

PROPERTY OF THE MONTH 
• Villa Charmant will be presented by CAO Kienast. 
o Villa Charmant  is this month’s property. I ’ll focus a little bit more on the property metrics and some 
demographic data around the community, along with recent capital repairs we’ve completed there. Right 
now, our net income is surpassing our budget, and that’s a good sign. We had a number of New City Plaza 
residents we had transferred to Villa Charmont, and now they’ve been moved back , we’ve got to have 
Villa Charmant’s units ready and released for lease-up. 
o We hired a new property manager, and she started today, I believe. She’s dug in and they’re getting going 
over there. I looked at the AMI  (Area Median Income)  for Salt Lake City and I had no idea the  AMI is 
~$120,000. In the last three (3) years, it’s gone up ~$40 -$60 grand. It perfectly aligns with the metrics 
around Villa Charmont since we’re servicing residents at 54% and 57% median income. And the median 
range in that area is about $66,000 for income, so I think we’re servicing the exact right population there. 
We were happy to get the capital improvements made for the property; it cleared up some safety 
concerns for us.  
 
REAL ESTATE DEVELOPMENT REPORT 
Director Moorhead presented:  
• There’s a good possibility we may close on some additional loans that have been committed to the deal for New 
City Plaza. It’s basically one loan for $1.4mm from Salt Lake County, and another loan for $895,000. 
o We were able to get the waivers into HUD for Build America, Buy America;  under The Infrastructure 
Investment and Jobs Act, we finally got affordable housing to be treated as infrastructure. Unfortunately, 
this comes with rules, as the materials need to be sourced from the US. As we closed this deal long before 
the bill was passed, we needed to apply for a waiver.  
o HVAC at New City Plaza: the air conditioning is working there, but not great. We are working with the 
contractor and the design team  to get what we paid for in accordance with what was designed. We are 
still having issues with ventilation and cooking smoke and fir e alarms, and we are working to get that 
system tested and balanced within the one-year warranty period. 
o CAO Kienast went on to discuss the insurance policy: we’ve been under a number of policies during the 
development phase. We transferred off all those policies and went on to the Utah Trust to i nsure the 
building. We have come to find out because of the HUD Loan on New City Plaza, they will not accept the 
trust as their insurer as it’s not AAA rated, and they’re a self-funded entity reinsured through AAAs. We’ve 
gone around with HUD trying to get a waiver on this and they’re just not rated period. Vice-Chair Nguyen 
asked who is in charge of that? How come they are not rated? Director Moorhead answered, it’s most 
likely because the trust only ensures (essentially) municipalities within the State of Utah, or in our case, 
Public Housing Authorities in the State of Utah, as it’s not open to the public. And the issue now is we 
need an AAA-rated policy to close. CEO Kimball stated we will not do this type of HUD financing again. 
• Granger: we’re going through the conversion process right now. Director Moorhead stated he is working on 
getting all the due diligence over to the equity investors and the lender so we can receive the remaining developer 
fee. We’ve made our final payment to the general contractor.
