DISCUSSION ITEM
5. Overview and Status of Title 13: Land Development Code (LDC) Update. (Jim Spung/AICP/Senior
Planner)

Mr. Spung presented this item.

5.1 He outlined what the Land Development Code is as being: (1) Location and size of buildings; (2) Allowable
land uses; (3) Quality and layout of development; and (4) Procedures for evaluating applications — and for
subdividing land. He discussed the reasons why the LDC needed to be up upgraded as: (1) It is a very
effective economic development tool (clear, predictable, and efficient); (2) Improves the user experience
and increases staff efficiency; and (3) Acomprehensive update tailored specifically for Taylorsville has never
been completed. The last update was in 2012 and is already outdated. To achieve these goals there needs
to be (1) Streamlined development review procedures and increased predictability of land use decisions;
(2) Consideration of the needs of the City both now and in the future; (3) Alignment of the LDC with Utah
State Statutes; (4) Enabling great design and a diverse mix of land uses; and (5) Improvement of user-
friendliness through logical structure, graphics, tables, and illustrations.

5.2 Commissioner Quigley asked if Kent Burggraaf (previous member of the Planning Commission) should be
replaced on the committee working on this update since he has moved out of the City and Mr. Spung said
that at this stage, he did not think that would be necessary but would clear that with Mr. Wayne Harper,
Economic/Community Development Director and let him know.

5.3 Mr. Spung explained the timeline for adoption as: Fall 2020 — Project orientation; Winter 2020 — LDC
assessment and outline completion; Spring 2021 through Summer of 2022 — Prepare the draft LDC (which
includes Administration and procedures, zoning districts and uses; and Development/design standards);
then there would be internal and public review and consolidation of the draft; and finally, in the Fall of 2022
there would be adoption of the LDC.

5.4 Mr. Spung said there will be ample opportunities to provide comment and input. During the Project
Orientation, Staff will (1) Review adopted plans, policies, and planning efforts; (2) Organize and meet with
the Advisory Committee; (3) Identify and meet with community stakeholders; and (4) Meet with appointed
and elected officials.

5.5 Mr. Spung said one important element to change is the structure of the LDC to organize it down from 41
chapters into 7 chapters with improved page layout to make it more user-friendly. The first draft will include
a lot of commentary and footnotes identifying the changes from the current standards and add graphics
and tables to make it easier to follow.

5.6 Mr. Spung explained the final process of LDC adoption: (1) Adoption draft incorporates review comments
from the consolidated draft; (2) Executive summary will show major changes; (3) Planning Commission
public hearing and recommendation will take place; (4) City Councill will hold the public hearing and
provide adoption decision; (5) After adoption, a final draft will be prepared reflecting edits approved during
the adoption hearing. Mr. Spung advised the first draft will probably be available in a couple of weeks.

5.7 Commissioner Wendel asked Mr. Spung how Staff had come up with the vision for this document.
Mr. Spung said how it came about with this vision was the past-history of his work experience saying that
he graduated in 2013 and worked for North Salt Lake City as a Planner for a few years, coming to Taylorsville
in 2015. He stayed here for a couple of years and realized that the LDC was in disarray so was looking at
solutions to say how it could be fixed. That was paused when he was hired by a consulting firm in Denver
that strictly updates Codes. He accepted a position with them as a consultant and their process was
essentially being hired by cities and counties across the nation to update their Land Development Codes
comprehensively. He did that for about three years and worked in several communities across the nation
and help draft four or five Codes in other parts of the country. That experience really helped him
understand the mechanics of what helps the Code work, what makes it user-friendly, what types of things
do not work, what type of language is necessary for legal protection, etc. He found it was different in every
community. He felt all gave him a good handle on what Taylorsville can use as far as improvements in this
Code. As far as the mechanics of actually drafting the ordinance is something that his years of experience
in that field helps. It is going to be a lot of work but with an understanding of the process it will make it
easier. Commissioner Wendel said that it is difficult for her to go from all the words in the old document
to the beautiful charts, graphs and very user-friendly experience as being proposed. She thanked him for
doing such a good job in this endeavor.

5.8 Mr. Spung commented that the finished product will not be considered light reading by any means but will
be more understandable and more intuitive for Staff, the development community, and for city residents.
His goal is to try to eliminate and simplify a lot of the legal jargon and planning nomenclature to make it
easier to be understood. However, that since it is a legal document, it will have to have the language to be
of help in court if something is challenged.

5.9 Mr. Spung said as far as the adoption goes, when the draft does come before the Planning Commission it
will contain an executive summary highlighting some of the major changes. For example, a new procedure
will be introduced to review minor plats or plats that are relatively small but maybe do not require the same
level of scrutiny as other projects. There is an authority change for the flood plain administrator from the
Director of Community Development to the City Engineer. Something like that would be highlighted in a
memorandum. There is no way to capture every single change made and edit. Staff will try to do that as
much as possible in the footnotes, but the executive summary will also be very helpful regarding policy
changes.

5.10 The Commission will be hearing this item several times for feedback but there will only be a formal public
hearing at the very end along with the recommendation to the City Council. Upon receiving approval from
the Planning Commission, the City Council will hear it in a formal public hearing setting and ultimately adopt
or request changes to the draft. Once it is adopted there may or may not be additional changes that come
about as part of those hearings, so staff will then implement any additional comments or revisions and have
it codified and available on the website. Normally there is a lag time of a few months between the adoption
dates and the effective dates. That gives Staff time to update all internal procedures to align with the new
Code. Also, to help train and educate everyone on the new rules and standards. From the adoption date,
usually it will be about six months to when the Code will actually be effective and become the law for the
City.

5.11 Mr. Spung felt this will be a good training experience not only for the new commissioners but also for the
existing ones who may have questions on proper procedures. It demonstrates how the zoning code works
and what the role of the Planning Commission is. The first part will basically summarize all of the Planning
Commission’s responsibilities, which issues they have authority to decide, and which issues they can
approve, along with how the review process works. For instance, on a subdivision process, what the types
of things are to look for in order to approve or deny an application.

5.12 Commissioner Russell said Mr. Spung had mentioned that before his arrival in Taylorsville that the City had
patterned the vision after different municipalities as to what they were doing for Codes, etc. and asked that
now after a few years have passed he wondered if Mr. Spung had gone back and looked at other
municipalities and if so, had that been helpful. Mr. Spung said yes, it is always helpful, and Staff has no
intention to reinvent the wheel. Generally, Taylorsville’s Code is pretty solid and serves the City well. The
downfall is that it is really disorganized and even reorganizing the existing content into the new framework
would be a major improvement. He advised the Commissioners that they can expect the first draft
very soon.
