1. City Center Open Space Open House

Francis Lilly said this was the first open house for the City Center open space and its working
title was “Millcreek Common.” Lilly introduced John Kelsh as part of the design team and said
he would talk about public spaces in a brief presentation and the public could then have a
conversation in an open house format. He said Council Members were in attendance though the
event was not a formal Council meeting. He encouraged the audience to participate in the
citizens poll on the City Center found on the City’s website.

John Kelsh said he wanted to give the audience a foundation for why the open space was being
planned. He said survey results that he would present were from 650 respondents, though more
had participated since he created the presentation. He said he titled his presentation, “Millcreek
Common; the City’s Living Room,” because the idea of living rooms for communities had been
around for hundreds of years. He would point out plazas, places, and piazzas; the city living
rooms of Europe. He highlighted spaces from Barcelona, Sienna, Rome, Montpelier, and Lille.
He said the concepts that made those spaces popular were because they had the following
qualities: places to sit for people watching; surrounded by beautiful architecture; boutique
shopping; activities after 6 p.m.; street entertainers and musicians; annual spectacular events;
adjacent buildings with viewing balconies; different elevations around but a flat open space;
pageantry and music; local crafts and food for sale; people can see and be seen; water and

sculptures; evening dining; something to do in all weather; events of all types, every day;
whimsical attractions for kids and adults; iconic sculptures that invite photography; architectural
statements; gardening activities; water features; plaza space that is large, flat and open like a city
stage; and urban green spaces. He then highlighted popular public spaces in America; Detroit,
Boulder, Pittsburgh, and Rapid City. Kelsh went over amenity examples that public spaces
commonly offer with pictures illustrating the concepts.

Kelsh went over the preliminary results of the poll with results in a bar graph format for
amenities and a point system for preferred style of park landscape design. Those designs
included mid-century modern, modern curvilinear, modern angular, formal, pastoral, organic,
and traditional.

Dave Harris, EPG Design, said the design team was gathering input from the public at that stage
in the planning process. He said the City Center open space public outreach schedule included
two more events. On January 14, 2020, the design team would show preliminary designs of the
open space to the public and the next event after that would be on March 17, 2020. He said in
March the project would go out to bid. An audience member asked about the surrounding
building use and height in the City Center. Harris said the buildings would be high density
mixed-use development with retail and office space on the first floor and residential on top. Mike
Winder said City staff or the EPG Design team could answer questions during the open house
component of the evening. Harris explained the open house process which involved putting
green and red stickers on posters indicating favor and disfavor for proposed amenities and open
space ideas.

The remainder of the meeting was conducted in an open house format with multiple
conversations taking place simultaneously.

The meeting adjourned at 6:49 p.m. due to loss of quorum.
