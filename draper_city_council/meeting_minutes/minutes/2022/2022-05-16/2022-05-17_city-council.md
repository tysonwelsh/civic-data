Study Session

1.0   Discussion: Parking and Emergency Access – South Mountain – Scott Cooley

1.1    Mr. Cooley began by noting enforcement was their biggest issue. He indicated
       that citizens were parking on both sides of the street, so they added “No
       Parking” signs to one side of the street. He noted these created issues for
       residents. Mr. Cooley said they have since limited the parking, though they
       are not enforcing it as strictly. In the alternative being proposed, Mr. Cooley
       stated that they would require “addressing” on each of the units on either side
       of the alley to aid in emergency response. Mr. Cooley said their main options
       were to enforce signs more strictly or create greater emergency access points.
       He expressed hopefulness and suggested they send out a survey to the
       neighborhood. Mr. Cooley noted that, no matter what they do, action would
       need to be taken. He opened the floor for questions.

      Councilmember F. Lowry asked how they would best ensure the alleyways
      “would have numbers on them.” Mr. Cooley said they could help provide the
      extra signage - which would not be too costly. He said they did not need to add
      fire hydrants to meet spacing requirements, though it would be beneficial to
      the space. He clarified that all that is required is changing the Code. He said
      changing the numbers would be a cheaper option than adding fire hydrants.

2.0   Update: Capital Improvement Projects – Rhett Ogden

      Mr. Ogden presented this portion of the meeting. He noted that they finished
      some parking lot projects including the Rotary Park parking lot. He added that
      they put up signs along the Peak View Interpretive Path. He said these signs
      included the history of the area. Councilmember F. Lowry asked about
      replacing the signs along the trail at the Mehraban Wetlands Park and if they
      could include this in the project, and Mr. Ogden confirmed that was correct.

Mr. Ogden went on to discuss some plantings added along trails on the north
side of town which should be finished by August. He said these same areas
were having some other landscaping changes that would be finished by late
July.

Mr. Ogden overviewed their conservation changes to certain parks which
included increased trees being planted. He noted costs totaled around 50,000
dollars though revenue should not be an issue. Mr. Ogden said residents in
the area would be supported on how to best care for the new plants and lower
water usage in their yards.

Mr. Ogden said the money spent on these projects had been allocated for these
specific needs ahead of time. He opened the floor for questions. The Council
felt these updates all looked great.

Councilmember F. Lowry asked about how they can implement their water plan
with the changes made to the splash pad. Mr. Ogden replied that they were
currently open 10 AM to 6 PM six days a week, and lost roughly 7,000 gallons a
day on the splash pads. He said this was based on how much the wind pushes
the water and how much kids splash excess water. He added that they have to
flush the tank for sanitation purposes once or twice a week. He noted that
between 1800 and 2400 gallons are used in addition to what is lost. Mr. Ogden
said splash pads utilized less water than the amount used to fill the swimming
pools. Councilmember Vawdrey suggested cutting down hours of operating to
even two days a week to meet the City’s conservation standards. She suggested
cutting down the hours from 2 PM to 8 PM as she did not anticipate kids would
be using the splash pads as early as 10 AM.             Councilmember T. Lowery
expressed concern that people would use their sprinkler water in higher
volumes if they cut down on the splash pads usage. The Council agreed that
keeping the splash pads open two days a week was the responsible thing to do.
Councilmember T. Lowery pointed out that they need to address how much
water they use in all their parks. Mr. Ogden said they have mechanisms in place
to note when it rains so they do not use excess water. He noted the main issue
was that there was not much spot control maintenance - they required more
“manual” upkeep. He said it is likely they have been notified of a park strip
unnecessarily being watered as many still do not have “smart timers.” He said
they water most City properties four days a week. He added that he did not
think it was in the City’s best interest to keep using as much water and stressed
that they are attempting to cut down water usage by 25 percent.

6.0   Action Item: Resolution #22-25, consideration for adoption of a resolution
      authorizing the issuance and sale of not more than $7,500,000 aggregate
      principal amount of Sales Tax Revenue Bonds; and related matters.

6.1   Mr. Dobbins said that this bond would allow them to utilize property outside of
      City Hall. He said this resolution would allow them to start the process of
      issuing these bonds. He stated no final decision was made - the process was
      simply beginning. Mr. Dobbins then introduced Mr. Robertson. Mr. Robertson
      briefly spoke on the process. He said the general process would allow the City
      to issue bonds to buy the property directly adjacent to government buildings.
      He said there were a variety of components largely involving City fund
      allocation and the buying/selling procedures. He went through a visual aid
      going through sales tax revenue bonds and how they compare to other states.
      He said the main goal was getting the lowest borrowing cost. Ultimately, Mr.
      Robertson said they decided the most efficient way to do this was funding
      “capitalized interest” so they would not have any payment for the first four
      years and there would be no “hiccup” to the City’s budget. He added that the
      City would contribute three million. He said tonight began a two-step process.
      He asked the City to enact “bond parameters” which included a public hearing
      request for June 7th. He stressed that these were simply parameters that
      would establish max loan requests and max interest rates. Mr. Robertson said
      they would adopt these parameters tonight with public input at a later date. He
      noted the goal was to finish the entire process at the end of July. He went on
      to discuss where interest rates have been going. He expressed hope that
      inflation would gradually calm down.
