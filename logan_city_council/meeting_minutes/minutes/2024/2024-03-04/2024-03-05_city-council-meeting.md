Change to Citywide Spring Cleanup Program – Mayor Daines (34:12)
Announcement on Changes to Spring Cleanup, March 5, 2024
In order to better manage and utilize city resources, we are making some major changes
to our annual spring cleanup, where crews have historically gone street to street citywide
for green waste pickup. We can’t find any other city in the entire state that provides this
service, and we are asking for the public’s help as we make this change.
To give you perspective, this event takes most of our streets department employees four
weeks to cover the entire city. They spend one week downtown washing roads, and
sidewalks and clearing storm pipes and inlet boxes. The other three weeks are dedicated
to covering every single street in Logan to pick up green waste from individual properties.
This is significant as these are the same individuals working to keep our underground
utilities functioning properly and our streets and sidewalks fixed and maintained.
With the number of staff and equipment involved, the cost is about $64,000 a week, or
almost $200,000. Last year because of potential flooding, we canceled street-by-street
spring cleanup and created green waste drop sites at four parks throughout the city, which
seemed to work quite well. Going forward we will make last year's spring cleanup process
permanent.
To incentivize residents, we are lowering the price of a monthly green can from $5 a month
to $4 a month. If you don’t have a can, please sign up for one. They are picked up weekly
at your curb from mid-April to mid-November. Many of the piles our crews pick up
curbside could easily fit in a green can over a week or two, and you’ll have that service all
summer long.
Please don’t put branches and green waste curbside. If you have a large load that won’t
fit in your green can over several weeks, you’re welcome to take it to the green waste drop
sites, or even better, take it directly to the landfill. That saves our crews time. You can also
rent a dumpster for green waste if you have a large amount. Tomorrow, we’ll be posting
and sending out info on our website about where the green waste sites will be located, how
to sign up for a green can, or who to call to order a dumpster.
Crews will still spend one week cleaning up downtown. However, instead of going house
to house, we will set up and attend to the four green waste drop sites to give individuals a
convenient location to deposit their yard waste. This will give our crews an additional three
weeks to fix potholes and repair damaged roadways, clean our storm drain system, sweep
and clean leftover winter debris, address safety hazards along our sidewalks, do weed
control, and other needed projects.
We understand that this is a change from what's been done in Logan City historically. With
our community growing, we are continually trying to stretch our resources and be as
efficient as possible. There may need to be flood prevention efforts again this spring.
Although we have had less snow in the valley this year, with this last storm, our watershed
is now at 130% of normal, so we will be watching that closely as well.
As we approach the end of the snow season and look forward to warmer temperatures and
spring rains, we want to thank our citizens for the work they do to keep our community
tidy. By not putting green waste curbside or in the road, your efforts will help keep our

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a recommendation of

located at 181 North 200 East (TIN# 06-066-0023) from Traditional Neighborhood
Residential (NR-6) to Town Center 2 (TC-2).

 North:     NR-6: Residential Uses             East:      NR-6: Religious Uses

PROJECT
The proponent is requesting to rezone the 0.13-acre property at 181 North 200 East from
NR-6 to TC-2. The applicant is planning a small 3-room boutique hotel and sculpture
walk for the property. If the rezone is successful, a Conditional Use Permit will need to
be obtained to legally establish the commercial use. The property is currently a single-
family home, built in 1909, with a detached garage in the rear yard. An open-ditch
irrigation canal traverses the property flowing northward and essentially bisects the
parcel with the home on the front section and the garage on the rear section.

GENERAL PLAN
The Future Land Use Plan (FLUP), adopted in 2008, identifies this property as Town
Center (TC). The General Plan, a nonregulatory visioning plan, describes TC areas as
being a hub of the City and Valley and reflects a vibrant downtown, with a variety of
commercial, mixed-use, and institutionally uses and activities. The TC designation
covers land from 200 South to 500 North and staggers between 100 East and 200 East
along the eastern side of Downtown Logan. The TC designation staggers between 100
and 150 West along the west side of Downtown.

Land Development Code (LDC) - Zoning
The TC-2, which encourages dense, walkable commercial and residential development in
the downtown area with unique and interesting building designs. This zone allows a
density of up of to 30 units per acre along with a mixture of commercial uses. The City
went through a deliberate and robust process in 2018 to redefine the Town Center zoning
districts which culminated in the adoption of Ordinance Number 18-07. This year-long
zoning process established TC-2 in the outer-ring areas of downtown as a way to buffer
or transition the residential neighborhoods further east and west of Downtown. The goal
of the zoning strategy adopted in 2018 was to further implement the visions expressed in
the General Plan through a methodical transition of uses and structure.

Site
The proposed 3-room hotel would be within the current walls of the home and essentially
convert the existing bedrooms to hotel rooms. The conversion will require numerous
building updates to comply with current commercial building codes. The site will need to
be redeveloped to accommodate the required parking stalls associated with the
commercial uses and the required open space and usable outdoor space.

Parking Requirements
The LDC requires 1.0 parking stalls per hotel room plus 4 additional stalls. The
additional stalls are likely for employee’s and additional guests. The gallery or outdoor
sculpture garden land use is not specifically addressed in the parking cade and thus would
be required to go through the alternative parking plan process. The applicant has
submitted an alternative parking plan stamped by Michael Taylor that shows up to 5 stalls
on-site in the rear yard and identified surrounding public parking lots within 600 feet of
the site. The LDC also has code language that waives parking requirements in the TC
zone when a project renovates within the existing walls of the building. This code section
typically applies to existing downtown commercial building that convert from retail to
restaurant or similar scenarios, to avoid the need to demolish a portion of the building to
create the uptick in parking requirements. The submitted plan shows a parking stall
perpendicular to the drive and difficult to access. As conditioned to meet LDC parking
requirements, the project complies with code regulations.

Useable Outdoor Space and Landscaping
The LDC 17.10.070 requires 10% landscaping and an additional 10% useable outdoor
space in the TC-2 zone. The LDC 17.32 generally describes useable outdoor space as
outdoor areas that are designed and used in conjunction with a primary use for the benefit
and enjoyment of the residents and their guests of the development. Useable outdoor
space may include public gathering areas, patios, decks, walkways, plaza's, active
recreational areas, and natural open space areas but do not include parking lots,
driveways, and other similar vehicular-oriented areas. Landscaping is defined as planted
and natural areas contained trees, shrubs, grasses, mulches, and other similar materials.

The LDC requires a minimum of 20 trees and a combination of 50 shrubs, flowers and
ornamental grasses per acre of land for multi-family residential projects. The LDC also
requires minimum perimeter and interior parking lot landscaping to reduce the visual and
environmental impacts of asphalt parking lots. At 0.13 acres, 1,132 SF of open space, 2
trees and 6 shrubs/flowers/grasses are required.

SUMMARY
Given the FLUP designation of TC and the current commercial land uses on the block,
the request to up-zone this parcel to TC-2 would be a predictable and expected zoning
change. With the LDS Temple located to the east of the block with extensive setbacks,
TC-2 development and land uses on the subject property and the remaining 200 East
block face would be compatible and pose minimal impacts to the residential areas further
east. Being a core development proposal, infrastructure and utilities can be more
efficiently utilized as compared to outer fringe areas. The hotel use will have parking and
traffic impacts and possibly noises during late hours as guest come and go. Being semi-
residential in nature, hotel uses should be quiet once guest are settled into their rooms for
nearby residential uses. Parking can be entirely waive based on TC zoning allowances or
the alternative parking plan can be accepted which uses a combination of on-site and off-
site public lots within 600 feet to satisfy parking demands.

This is an update of the Subdivisions, Boundary Line Adjustment, Plat Vacations, and

SUMMARY OF PROPOSAL
The changes required by Senate Bill 174 are centered around four general areas:

Procedural
• Cities need to establish the Administrative Land Use Authority for the different types
of land divisions. The language in SB174 emphasizes that the review of a preliminary
plat is an administrative function and not a legislative function. Final plat approval cannot
be reviewed by either the City Council or the Planning Commission. (Already in current
LDC)
• Cities cannot mandate a conceptual plan review. (Logan does not mandate conceptual
plan review)
• Cities need to define what a "complete application" consists of. (Logan will have new
"complete applications 11 for all elements covered under SB174)

Timing of Review Process
• Initial review of a complete preliminary plat application shall be completed within 15
business days. (In proposed code language - City's predevelopment review process will
be conducted within 15 days of complete application whereas "approval11 of a
preliminary plat still resides with Planning Commission in a public hearing)
• Final plat review completed within 20 days of receiving a complete final plat
application.
(In proposed code language)
• Capped review cycle - no more than 4 review cycles for the final plat. (In proposed
code language)
• Applicant required to respond to changes. All disagreements must be in writing. (In
proposed code language)
• Modified lot line adjustment provisions to remove requirement to record an amendment
plat. (In proposed code language)

Engineering Standards
• Cities need to have codified engineering standards. (Road standards already codified,
water/sewer standards in process with PC 24-004)
• No bonding for landscaping on private property permitted anymore. (No longer
requiring landscape bonds - this does affect applicants trying to get into buildings during
winter, early, etc.)
• New road standards - limit pavement widths on residential roads (25 mph) to no greater
than 32'. (Existing road standards consistent with this requirement)

              Logan Municipal Council Minutes ~ Logan, Utah ~ March 5, 2024
Appeals Processes
• SB 174 creates two distinct appeals processes after the four review cycles have been
exhausted and 20 days have passed. (In proposed code language) Disputes related to

engineers to function as the review authority on the specific disputed standard. Other
disputes follow existing appeals process & appeals authority.

Councilmember Johnson requested confirmation on whether the Logan City Planning
Commission had any concerns.

Mr. Holley stated that the Planning Commission did not list any concerns. The Planning
Commission voted unanimously in favor of the code amendment.

Chair A. Anderson requested confirmation that the amendments to the code are to ensure
the City code is up to date.

Mr. Holley confirmed that is correct, the amendments to the code are to ensure that the
code is up to date.

The proposed ordinance will be an action item and public hearing at the March 19, 2024,
Council meeting.

OTHER CONSIDERATIONS:

The Council expressed their delight at the library opening and their appreciation for the
library staff and gratitude to all of the library donors, who were also recognized at the
opening of the library.

Mayor Daines remarked that Karen Clark, Library Director had previously commented
that she has taken more library card applications from citizens outside of Logan than she
has during her whole tenure as library director.

Councilmember Johnson said the intention of the Transportation Master Plan is to mesh
the bike and pedestrian moveability as part of the Plan. The hope is to continue the
discussion with staff along with UDOT as they provide input for their plans for Main
Street.

Chair A. Anderson stated that on Friday, March 8, 2024, the Cache County Chamber of
Commerce will be recognizing the City for the new Carol and Jim Laub Plaza along with
the success of the Giant Pumpkin Festival hosted for the first time last year.

No further items were discussed.

ADJOURNED. There being no further business, the Logan Municipal Council adjourned
at 7:37 p.m.
Esli Morales, Deputy City Recorder
