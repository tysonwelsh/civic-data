4.      Potential Emergency Dispatch Center Consolidation. (22-047) (0:56:36)

Wayne Parker, CAO, and Fire Chief Jim Miguel, presented. Chief Miguel said there were several
emergency dispatch operations throughout Utah County and this as an opportunity to work with
Orem on consolidation of the Provo & Orem dispatch centers into one. There are three major
problems they are still trying to solve and issues that would facilitate a consolidation:
       1. How cell phone calls hit a signal tower and where they are directed. Near city
               boundaries, calls sometimes get routed to the other city, resulting in a delay in
               emergency response as the dispatchers have to relay details to the other city’s
               dispatch.
       2. Coordinating between different communications channels in the two different cities.
       3. Extraordinary or major police and fire incidents—Provo and Orem already provide
               mutual support to the other city.

Mr. Parker and Chief Miguel highlighted the positive aspects of consolidation, including
improved service levels and response times, cost savings and efficiencies for both cities,
encouragement from State officials and agencies, facility opportunities, attracting and retaining
employees, history of working together and high trust levels, and more opportunities for
collaboration. The basic concept is that the consolidation would start with a service contract and
eventually move toward an interlocal agency setup. Provo will operate the center and Orem
employees would all become Provo employees. Councilor George Handley asked how it would
work for Orem employees and also about the service contract arrangement. Chief Miguel
clarified that Orem would pay, with no increase to Provo’s costs. Mr. Parker would address the
contractual arrangement in more detail. The first phase feasibility analysis is complete, and the
second phase of due diligence is beginning. The target completion is this fall, with a contract
ready for approval in October.

Councilor Katrice MacKay asked about the cross-use of public safety personnel. Chief Miguel
responded and said this may increase some due to more efficient communications, but Provo
does not see this as a decrease to its public safety employees. Ms. MacKay relayed a question
from Councilor Shannon Ellsworth, who wondered about the downsides. Chief Miguel felt that
as long as Provo is able to meet the needs of the employees as we come together, and provide a
positive work environment, he does not see a downside.

Councilor Rachel Whipple had some questions about the contractual relationship. She wondered
what the odds were that Orem would choose not to consolidate. Chief Miguel said the Orem City
Council has been informed and are having a briefing next week. If Provo serves them properly
and accomplishes the goals, he can’t see why they would not want to undo this consolidation
which has great advantages for both cities. Ms. Whipple just wants to make sure that funding is
appropriately secured. She also asked which cities rely on Orem for public safety. Orem provides
police to Lindon, and fire protection to Lindon and Vineyard.

Wayne Parker, CAO, presented on additional aspects of the consolidation. They plan to utilize an
interlocal agreement or agency in the future. A management committee would include 3
members from each city and a quorum vote would need 4/6. There would also be a technical
committee, with 2 members from each city, plus the dispatch operations center manager. They
would make recommendations about operational aspects like equipment. Orem could terminate
the contract with 12 months’ notice that could fall no later than June 1 each year, ensuring at
least a full fiscal year from the time of intent to when any termination would take place.

Ms. MacKay asked how employees are feeling about the change. Chief Miguel has met with
dispatchers in both cities—although this is not going without some trepidation from some staff,
the vast majority see this as the next logical step. Currently, Orem’s dispatch center does not
have a communications director. Heather Perkins is Provo’s Emergency Communications
Manager; she has really improved morale, operations, and communications in the center and
many people are excited about the leadership on display in Provo.

Mr. Parker said they proposed using Provo’s emergency communications fund as the accounting
entity under the contract. The funds would not go back to general fund, which would allow funds

6.      A presentation regarding utility rates, property tax rates, and fees. (22-016)
        (1:52:26)

John Magness, Interim Council Executive Director, presented. Mr. Magness briefly addressed
private goods and resources versus public goods and resources. Provo charges some fees for
services and some for commodities (such as utilities). Councilor Katrice MacKay asked if Provo
has specific reserve accounts for depreciation and capital improvements. Mr. Magness said the
City has them for capital improvements for most departments and equipment, but not everything.
Mr. Magness outlined a model for total cost recovery, which includes capturing both the direct
and indirect costs in how the fees are structured. He also outlined a cost structure for
commodities fees. Councilor David Shipley cited parking as an example for where the city was
trying to apply cost-recovery. He thought Mr. Magness was explaining the concept well by
highlighting the other real costs to a department with management overhead in addition to hourly
employees performing the work. Mr. Magness explained that true cost recovery needs to include
all those costs. Costs not recovered through fees are a policy decision. Councilor George
Handley noted that when building more infrastructure, there is a similar problem because there
are so many indirect costs or long-term costs with maintenance.

Mr. Magness reviewed the fees for the last five years. There have not been any adjustments in
the last five years for administrative costs (blueprints and records), business licensing, city hall
rentals, community development, engineering (although there are two fee adjustments in
FY2023), fire department, general building inspection, library (although there are room rental fee
adjustments for FY2023), parks and recreation (Covey Center, Golf Course, Recreation Center,
youth sports, sport field rentals, and the Peaks Ice Arena; although most of these have changes
planned in FY2023), permit parking areas, Police, commercial sanitation fees, and justice court
(which are primarily set by the state). In the last few years, fees have been adjusted for the
airport, cemetery, parks and recreation (pavilion rentals, Rec Center day pass, shooting park,
adult sports), some culinary water, some sanitation fees, waterwater, and impact fees (which
were set by the last impact fee study).

Dan Follett, Finance Division Director, presented. State law requires that the City notify utility
rate payers of any transfers they plan to make which come from the payment of utility fees. They
do this with a mailing that goes out in utility bills annually. Mr. Follett outlined the transfers that
take place with the transfer amounts for FY2023:
     General Fund:
            o 10% transfer
            o 1% road transfer
            o Administrative overhead
     Other Funds:
            o Telecom Debt Service
            o Provo 360
            o Water
Mr. Follett gave additional context for each of these transfers and when several of the transfers
would be dropping off. The total transfers for FY2023 are $19,122,626. Councilor David Shipley
asked about the end date of the telecom debt service; Mr. Follett explained that the end was
targeted for 2026, but if there were extra funds in the account they could pay this off sooner. Mr.

was sufficient for the City’s pressing needs. Mr. Follett thought that was a question which should
be deferred to Public Works. He did note this was not the entire road maintenance budget—there
are other revenue sources for that, but this amount was designated 100% to road repairs (rather
than to overhead, compensation, etc. in the Streets division) only. Wayne Parker, CAO, shared
additional clarification on this 1% figure and the impacts to it over time. Presentation only.
