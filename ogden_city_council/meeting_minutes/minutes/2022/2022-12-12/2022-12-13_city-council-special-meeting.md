Proposed Resolution 2022-26 approving the acquisition of ***declaring the
intent to purchase*** surplus property from the Ogden School District

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
approving the acquisition of surplus property from the Ogden School District (OSD). The memo stated Administration has
forwarded a proposal to the Council for the purchase of surplus property from OSD. The District has deemed the subject parcels
as surplus, and Ogden City is a eligible entity entitled to submit a notice of intent to purchase the surplus property. The notice of
intent must be submitted within 90 days of the receipt of the declaration of surplus, which the City received on September 21,
2022. The Administration is proposing the use of Quality Neighborhoods Initiative funding for the purchase of three of the four
properties deemed surplus by the District.

Community Development Deputy Manager Smith summarized the memo and explained Administration has forwarded a
resolution to the Council for the approval of the purchase of surplus property from Ogden School District. The District has
deemed the subject parcels as surplus, and Ogden City is a eligible entity entitled to submit a notice of intent to purchase the
surplus property. The notice of intent must be submitted within 90 days of the receipt of the declaration of surplus, which the City
received on September 21, 2022. The Administration is proposing the use of Quality Neighborhoods Initiative funding for the
purchase of three of the four properties deemed surplus by the District. The four properties included in the determination were as
follows:

Parcel #05-065-0001, 3875 Jackson Avenue — former Grandview Elementary School site, 5.15 acres

Parcel #12-084-0051, 625 Grant Avenue — Former Lynn Elementary School site, 5.87 acres.

Parcel #1 1-071-0038, 605 North Jackson Avenue — vacant property, 6.55 acres

Parcel #11-044-0037, 685 North Jackson Avenue — existing single-family home adjacent to the 605 North
Jackson Avenue property, 0.56 acres

Mr. Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide aerial images of each of the properties to orient the
Council to the impact that improvements and/or infill development of the properties would have on the surrounding
neighborhoods. Administration is proposing the City purchase three of the four properties, recommending forgoing the purchase
of the 3875 Jackson Avenue (Grandview Elementary) site. Administration is anticipating the development of single-family homes
on the two larger properties with the rehabilitation and sale of the home at 685 North Jackson Avenue.

Council member Blair inquired as to the appraisal process for these types of properties. Mr. Smith stated that the School
District and the City both commissioned independent appraisals. The results of the two appraisals are averaged together to
determine the final appraised value.

Chair Nadolski referenced the slide in Mr. Smith’s presentation regarding the Grandview Elementary site; Council
member Hyer has noted a separation between the City park space and the old school site, but there is no barrier between the two
properties and the area has been used as open space with no limitations on the uses. This could be cause for any confusion about
ownership of the space or allowed uses.

Council Deputy Director Symes then noted that Mr. Smith’s presentation represents Administration’ s proposal regarding
the surplus properties; however, Council members have engaged in conversation about what can be done with the properties and
potential funding sources for improvements on the properties. Council staff has developed an alternative resolution that
communicates the intent to purchase all four pieces of surplus property to give the Council an additional 90 days to discuss the
highest and best uses of the properties and viable funding sources for desired improvements; if after the 90 day period has lapsed
and the Council decides not to pursue purchase of one or all of the properties, the City will lose its option of first right of refusal.
If the City does decide to purchase the properties, it will be necessary to deposit earnest money for the sale; ifthat requirement is
not satisfied, the Schoo] District can market the properties as available for public sale.

Chair Nadolski stated that the Council felt they needed the additional 90 days to evaluate options for using the property
in the future; since the Council’s last discussion of this item, he and Vice Chair Lopez have been approached by community
partners who are anxious to engage with the City regarding long range open space planning and investment and there are some
potential partners who may offer financial support for such projects.

Council member White asked if adoption of the proposed resolution entails communication of a dedicated funding source
for future improvement projects at the sites or if the City has an additional 90 days to identify funding sources. Mr. Symes stated
this resolution will give the Council an additional 90 days to contemplate funding sources.

Council member Choberka thanked Council staff and Council leadership for working to develop the alternate resolution
to give the Council additional time to evaluate this matter.

Council member Hyer stated that he has spoken with School District Board members who indicated they are not
concerned giving the City an additional 90 days to consider this issue. Council member Blair stated that he also reached out to the
School District and they enlightened him about their process to declare properties as surplus; they also indicated that the request
for additional time to evaluate the matter is not concerning. He thanked Council staff and leadership for their attention this matter
and also thanked City Administration for their efforts in evaluating the opportunity to purchase the properties.

Council just saved the City from a result in which some of the property offered for sale by the School District would have been
developed for residential use. If the Council had not acted, the Grandview property would have been purchased by a developer
who would have built a subdivision with the maximum density allowed by Ogden City Code. With all the plans identifying the

organizations with ambiguous interpretations of the ordinances that they are entrusted to carry out. He asked who is protecting the
little people from the executive branch of Ogden’s form of government. He stated that he has often been asked to run for Mayor
and was even offered substantial funding to explore a bid for the office, but he is not interested in running for that position. He
asked that City officials stop spreading that rumor and cease undermining his motives and his integrity.

Al Jones stated he understands the job performed by City Council members is very difficult and he applauded them for
their service. He loves Ogden and so do many other people; he loves to see so many people getting involved in their local
government and will always encourage people to stay involved. Ogden is very unique and has more potential than any other place
he has visited; it takes leadership to maintain that uniqueness and residents do not want Ogden to be like other towns. It is not
possible to solve today’s problems with yesterday’s solutions; new solutions need to be explored. There is a great deal of work to
be done responsive to many projects being considered in the community and he suggested that City government slow down and
carefully examine reasonable solutions. He referenced projects such as development of the Stock Exchange area, Union Station,
WonderBlock, Nine Rails, and more. He applauded the Council for the work they do and hopes that they can work with all other
parties to come up with the best solutions moving forward.

Scott Richards stated he and his wife, Tina, are the directors of the Ogden FamilySearch Center in Ogden; he has
concerns about the paid parking initiative and how it will impact his business. They have a small parking lot with 13 spaces and
they routinely run into problems with people parking in his lot though he has erected signage prohibiting parking by those not
affiliated with the Center. When there is an event in downtown Ogden, many people use his parking spaces and he worries that
problem will only worsen when other facilitates become part of the paid parking system. All services provided at the Center are
free of charge and handled by volunteers, all of which park in public parking areas rather than the lot so that spaces are reserved
for patrons. He asked if provisions can be made to protect his small parking lot and he asked that the Council consider this issue as
discussion of the paid parking system moves forward.

Angel Castillo also spoke to the difficulty of the job performed by City Council members; she thanked them for their
service and noted that it must be more difficult to perform that service when the body is dealing with a lack of community trust.
She applauded the Council for having difficult conversations and for being transparent. She then referenced tonight’s Council
discussion regarding the proposed conversion of certain hotel and motel properties to multi-family units and suggested that any
decision on that issue be based on a valid market study. She ran into situations as a Planning Commissioner where proposals were
not based upon market studies, but it is important to understand that market studies should be relied upon to make such important
decisions. She stated that the same is true for the WonderBlock project; the City should not give away tax increment financing
(TIF) so easily without a valid market study supporting it.

Travis Pate stated that tonight’s discussion about the Ogden School District properties is a clear example of the many
proposals and recommendations that are made to the City Council without the presence of a valid argument. The same is true for
the recent action taken regarding the acquisition of Union Station property. Many other properties in the City have been gifted to
developers with no competition; he suggested the City utilize the request for qualifications (RFQ) or request for proposals (RFP)
processes to ensure qualified developers are selected for redevelopment projects in the community. He discussed the
Redevelopment Agency’s history of property acquisition and noted that they have a poor track record of redevelopment success.
This causes lack of trust in the community.

Teresa Bramwell stated she has been attending City Council meetings for the last several weeks to plead for her private
property rights to be upheld; she began telling the Council the story of the history of her property in the December 6 meeting and
she continued that story tonight. Her family’s farm became the ugliest fourplexes ever built in the history of the world; Ogden
City facilitated that project, and it would have never happened if the developer had not been handed her family’s property on a
silver platter. The City’s Code Enforcement Division was heavily involved in the transaction and their efforts started with citations
regarding the illegal keeping of horses on the property. The City demanded that her family sell their horses and their rabbits and
remove outbuildings from their property. Eventually they were told that they had not obtained a permit to enclose their front porch
and they were required to remove those improvements. She concluded that her family was harassed by Code Enforcement, which
ultimately led to the property being acquired by a developer; essentially, her family’s property was taken by force and sold to a
developer who built fourplexes.

Margaret Bejarski stated she and her husband moved to Ogden in the past year; they relocated for employment and had
the opportunity to pick a residence anywhere between Salt Lake and Logan and they chose Ogden because of the incredible
community they observed, the unique infrastructure and town, and the passion of the residents. She addressed the paid parking
initiative and noted that while she understands the City stands to gain revenue associated with the program, accessibility to the
community will be lost. The Council heard from a certain demographic of the community: young families with kids who want
access to open space and sports facilities. She is not part of that demographic, but she is part of the demographic that attends many
events and venues in Ogden’s downtown; she lives in the area surrounding 25" Street and she loves it, but asking people to pay
for parking in downtown Ogden will reduce accessibility for the demographic she is part of. She stated she understands that
foregoing the program will result in a potential loss of revenue, but it is important to value the City’s culture and understand the
potential costs of implementing the program.

Stephanie Vaughan stated she grew up in Ogden and she and her husband bought their first home here; when she
observes the new development occurring in the area, she becomes concerned about future planning of the City and whether future
generations of Ogden residents are being thought of in those planning efforts. She cares deeply about sustainable urban growth
and mixed-use walkable growth. She stated that for the WonderBlock project she would love to see economic development that
offers opportunities for people to gather and enjoy the space in a ‘third space’, such as a coffee shop, bakery, or bookstore. She
stated that ‘third spaces’ have been lost over the last several decades resulting in society becoming more isolated from one another
and creating opportunities for people to come together is very important. Walkability is also very important because it supports the
health of the community and decreases traffic congestion. There is already a problem with too many cars in the City and
increasing walkability will reduce the parking problems in the future. She knows that there is a study that advocates for mixed-use
development, but she wished to express her support for ensuring walkability of those mixed-use developments.

Heath Satow stated he could hear the anger in Mr. Van Wagoner’s voice tonight; he stated he has also spoken very
angrily in the past and that anger comes from the fact that citizens often feel their representatives are more concerned about
getting along with City Administration and getting their pet projects taken care of than they are with being an actual representative
of the people who voted for them. He agreed with Mr. Jones about the unique nature of Ogden City and the need to preserve that
nature; residents do not want the City to be developed in a way that makes it reflect Salt Lake or even Denver. The Mayor has not
connected with the people of Ogden in any meaningful way since he last campaigned. He only saw one elected official, Chair

leadership’ and he asked that those leaders genuinely listen to the people they represent. Finally hearing some recognition that the
