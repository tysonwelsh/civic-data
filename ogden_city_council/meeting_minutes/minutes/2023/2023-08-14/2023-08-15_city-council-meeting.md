Proposed Ordinance 2023-49 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to
remove newspaper noticing requirements aligning with state notification
laws
A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed amendments to notification
requirements came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated based on recent state law changes, cities are no longer
required to provide public notice using the newspaper for land use applications. Instead, State law generally requires posting .
notices on the City’s website, on the Utah Public Notice website, posting in at least three public places, and, ifapplicable, mailing
to affected entities as required by both state and city code. As such, the proposed amendments remove the newspaper noticing
requirements for subdivision code section changes, street and alley vacations, zoning ordinance changes and rezones, general plan
amendments, and designations to the local historic register. The Council extended consideration of the proposed amendments at
the March 21, 2023 meeting. Council members voted to extend consideration with the direction that information regarding
noticing costs and newspaper subscribership and circulation be presented. City staff presented the information requested by the
Council at the work session on July 11, 2023. :
Deputy Planning Manager Simpson summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize
the proposed amendments to notification requirements. Senate Bill 201 eliminates the requirements to post notices in newspapers
but does require notices be posted on the Utah Public Notice Website and the City website. The proposed ordinance amendment
addresses notices for the following types of applications:
e Street name changes
e Subdivision ordinance amendments
e Street and alley vacations
e Zone changes
e Zoning ordinance amendments
\ e General plan amendments
/ e Historic resource designations
If the proposed amendment is approved, the requirement to publish notices in the newspaper will be replaced with a
requirement to post the notice on the State Public Notice Website. During the March 21, 2023 meeting, the Council asked
questions about newspaper circulation in the area and staff found that subscription to the paper has decreased by over 30,000 in
the last 30 years; current circulation is just 22,000. Since the Council tabled the proposed ordinance on March 21, Planning staff
has conducted their own survey to determine how people who attended Planning Commission meetings had learned of certain
agenda items; they found that just one person out of the 40 polled learned of an application through the newspaper, but they had
read a headline and article about the application, not the actual legal notice. The Council also asked about the cost associated with
newspaper noticing; each notice has a cost of approximately $200, for an annual average cost of $2,400. Mr. Simpson concluded
the Planning Commission has recommended that the Council adopt the proposed ordinance amendments to replace the
requirement for posting notices in the newspaper with a requirement to post it on the State Public Notice Website.
Vice Chair Richey asked if residents could sign up for notifications on the City’s website and the Utah Public Notice
Website. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith answered yes; residents can subscribe to a specific public body.
Council member Hyer stated his remaining concern is historical preservation of notices; he asked if the Utah Public
Notice Website retains or archives notices to be able to determine the date that certain notices were posted. Mr. Simpson stated he
imagines the State of Utah does archive public notices, but he cannot say for certain. Council member White stated that she had
that same question the first time the Council discussed this issue; she is concerned that the questions that were initially asked by
the Council have not been answered and that it is not possible for City staff to fully explain the current noticing process or the
process that will be in place when the amendments are approved. Mr. Simpson stated the only change is advertising on the Utah
Public Meeting Notice website versus the newspaper, and he is not sure if the newspaper archives their public notices. Council
member White stated she understands the proposal and does not fault Mr. Simpson for not being able to address the Council’s
questions tonight; however, if the matter is voted on tonight, she will vote in opposition.
Chair Choberka stated that she is worried that not all residents have digital capabilities to search and find notices they
may be interested in. Mr. Simpson stated he understands that concern, but noted staff has weighed whether the small number of
people who obtain notices via the newspaper warrants the City’s annual expense on hard copy notices.
Council member Blair asked when the changes would go into effect if approved. Mr. Simpson stated that it would be
) — effective based upon the effective date of the ordinance; however, staff is willing to work to phase out the current practice and
- transition to the new practice and will work with the City Council to spread the word about updated noticing procedures.
Council member Hyer stated he is not as concerned about reducing access to notices by eliminating newspaper notices,
but he is not sure that the Utah Public Notice Website is being fully used by public bodies, not just Ogden City; he has searched
for notices from several entities and has been unable to find them. He stated he would recommend a three-month transition period
a

and work to make changes to the City’s website to provide an archiving mechanism. Additionally, newspaper notices should .
include language informing the reader that the City will cease providing newspaper notifications and will eventually be providing }
all notices on the City website and Utah Public Notice website. The Council engaged in discussion about Council member Hyer’s
recommendation, after which Council Executive Director Eller-Smith suggested that the Council table this item until the August
29 meeting to give Planning staff time to address the concerns expressed by the Council. She would also recommend that the
transition period be six months rather than three months, which will give the Council staff sufficient time to activate a
communications plan regarding the matter.

Chair Choberka then called for public input regarding the proposed ordinance.

Travis Pate stated that it may be that just one person sees a newspaper notice, but in his case, he has informed up to 30
people of a notice that he has read. He stated the input of these residents matters. He then noted that it may be possible to view an
archive of notices that have been posted on the Utah Public Meeting Notice Website, but it is not possible to know how many
notices were not posted on the website, even if there was a requirement for them to be posted. The accuracy of the noticing is
difficult to determine. He added that the $2,400 spent on noticing is a tiny fraction of the City’s total budget. Also, it would be
difficult to manipulate the records of an independent source — the newspaper; the same cannot be said for the two websites that are
managed by government entities. He emphasized the historic value and independent source value of newspaper noticing is
immeasurable.

Teresa Bramwell stated she does not trust government; she has been taken advantage of several times in her life
specifically by Ogden City and she does not like anything that makes it easier for the government to manipulate taxpayers. If
passing the proposed ordinance only means that it will be convenient for City staff to post and find notices, but not necessarily
convenient for a resident to find, she does not trust the position and feels that the City is trying to undermine the people.

Dave Timmerman agreed with Mr. Pate’s assessment that the $2,400 spent on public notices is a very small portion of
the City’s budget. i

Angel Castillo stated there are still many people who receive the newspaper; additionally, senior citizens who frequent
local senior centers often obtain a newspaper while there and read through it. She was recently at the Golden Hours Center where
there were three editions of the newspaper, but at least 12 people read them in the short time she was there. There is a small part of
the population for which the newspaper is still their primary source of news and information. She agreed with Mr. Pate’s concerns
about the importance of being able to archive notices and information provided in the newspaper; the same benefit is not available
for electronic notices. She finds it hard to believe that the City would not want to be intentional when trying to reach out to all
residents of the community. She agreed that it is necessary for the Council to be able to evaluate all the unintended consequences
of this proposal. Providing adequate notice is worth the $2,400 spent by the City each year.

Council member White stated it is important to point out that this is not just a $2,400 hard expense, but also a significant
amount of staff time and resources on current noticing practices. Council member Nadolski agreed and stated that the workload
imbalance is an issue that he is considering.
