Washington County Planning Commission Meeting
                                March 09, 2021
The Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting was held on Tuesday, March 09, 2021 in
the Council Chambers of the Washington County Administration Building located at 197 E.
Tabernacle, St. George, UT., also via electronic means through Zoom. Chairman Brandon
Anderson opened the meeting at 1:30 p.m. He led the Pledge of Allegiance and then explained
meeting protocol.

Commissioners present: Kevin Jones, Todd Jones, Slade Hughes, Frank Leuck, Reed Scow, and
Mark Owens.

Staff present: Scott Messel, Community Development Director; Victoria Hales, Deputy County
Attorney; Doreen Bowers-Irons, CDD Administrative Assistant; Kurt Gardner, County Building
Official; Ron Whitehead, Public Works Director.


1.     PUBLIC HEARING FOR THE RESOURCE MANAGEMENT PLAN ELEMENT
OF THE WASHINGTON COUNTY GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT REVIEW. Review,
discuss, and hold the public hearing for the Resource Management Plan Element of the
Washington County General Plan then make a recommendation to the County Commission for
approval.

Victoria Hales provided a background on the Resource Management Plan history which, is
required by State Code. She then briefed the commission on the updates to the Resource
Management Plan Element of the Washington County General Plan. She pointed out that the
changes are more generalized to grammatical changes and not substantive.

Mr. Messel explained how the county accomplished the Resource Management Plan when the
State Code requirement was enacted.

The Resource Management Plan Element was discussed in detail. Commissioner Owens asked if
under the Cultural Section, Item E, Detailed Explanations, Number 3, it would be beneficial to add
Pine Valley to that portion as it is historical. Ms. Hales answered that section is basically tied to
grants which are generally given to cities and towns for development. The county doesn’t compete
with cities and towns for those grants for development purposes.

Commissioner Kevin Jones asked staff if they were comfortable with the wording of “will” in the
draft. Mr. Messel answer in the affirmative. He explained the document is a recommending
document and subject to changes, updates, etc., to the Mandatory General Plan. Commissioner
Owens asked that the Pine Valley Chapel be added to the list of historical buildings section. Ms.
Hales indicted they would amend that section to include it.

Chairman Anderson declared the public hearing open.

There was no one in the audience nor via Zoom who wished to comment.

Chairman Anderson the declared the public hearing closed.

2.      PUBLIC HEARING FOR ORDINANCE AMENDMENTS. Review, discuss, and hold
a public hearing and recommend approval of the proposed amendments to the Washington County
Ordinances: Title 10, Chapter 4: Definitions; Title 10, Chapter 8, Residential Zones; and Title 10,
Chapter 9, Planned Development Zones, Planned Development Rural Recreation Grounds and
Facilities.

Mr. Messel and Ms. Hales reported on the proposed ordinance amendments. He gave a background
of how the rural recreation grounds and facilities use began. Rural recreation grounds and facilities
has now grown and expanded over time in several ways and not as originally intended. A
moratorium has been put in place on Rural Recreation Grounds and Facilities use. The proposed
amendments to the ordinance proposes to strike the use from the code in order to do a rewrite and
develop a better code to ensure that the rural recreation grounds and facilities and their
requirements are well thought out and established. We want to be proactive instead of reactive
with the code going forward. We will be looking into other jurisdictions nationwide and start to
develop a feasible and well thought out code. He discussed the time frame in getting the ordinance
done which may take up to two years.

Commissioner Hughes asked if there were any permanent rural recreation grounds and facilities
in the county that have been a problem. Mr. Messel answered some are still in the construction
phase, which he pointed out. He spoke about a facility in Virgin that have had some issues.

A lengthy discussion regarding Rural Recreation Grounds and Facilities and the areas where they
could be located, the densities, and several other concerns ensued. The discussion included the
time frame for the implementation of an ordinance to allow Rural Recreation Grounds and
Facilities, which would not be immediate and could take up to two years before it is passed. The
planning commission determined the ordinance amendment rewrite should be a priority.

Commission Hughes motioned to table the item until there is something to allow this type of use
so that it is not a 10 year process. He was advised the public hearing was needed to take place.

Chairman Anderson declared the public hearing open.

Brian Thiriot stated he was supportive of the rural recreation use and sad to learn that it is going
to go away. His family has 160 plus acres of property out by Goldstrike that would fit well for
rural recreation grounds and facilities. He asked that the ordinance not be too restrictive.
There was no one in the audience nor via Zoom who wished to comment on the item.

3.     PLAT AMENDMENT. Review an amendment to the existing plat of the Topaz Estates
Subdivision to adjust the lot lines for Parcels, TE-7-DA and TE-8-DA, which are located at 8624
N. Topaz Court and 8627 N. Topaz Court in Diamond Valley. The applicants are Eric & Dee Jones
and GST Investments. Benn Willets is the agent.

Mr. Messel provided the proposed plat, site location, and map of the property. He reported the
proposed lots have been adjusted a couple of times and now the applicants would like to make
another adjustment to realign Lots 7 and 8 of the Topaz Estates Subdivision. He cited State Code
addressing lot line adjustments, which requires a public meeting, not a public hearing. Staff has
reviewed the proposed lot line adjustment and found no issues with the proposal and would
recommend the planning commission approve the amended plat to adjust those lot lines. No
easements will be moved and both lots are owned by the applicants.

Chairman Anderson asked if there were any utility easements along the lot lines. Ron Whitehead,
Public Works Director answered there are none along the lot lines they are changing to but there
are existing easements across the lot that are changing. The easements were pointed out.

No one was present nor via Zoom to represent this item.

4.     JOINT DEVELOPMENT STAFF MEETING REVIEW. Report of the Land Use
Authority Joint Development Staff Meeting held on March 02, 2021.

Mr. Messel reported on the Joint Development Staff Meeting held March 02, 2021. Staff reviewed
a proposed zone change for Parcel 3005-K-NS in the New Harmony area from the OSC-20 (Open
Space Conservation 20 acre minimum lot size) zone to the A-20 (Agricultural 20 acre minimum
lot size) zone. A proposed zone change and a 6-lot preliminary subdivision plat of Parcel 8183-B-
NW from the A-10 (Agricultural 10 acre minimum lot size) zone to the RA-5 (Residential
Agricultural 5 acre minimum lot size) zone, located at approximately to 500 West 200 North in
Veyo; the applicant is Lemual Leavitt. The previously reviewed Planned Development for a
campground facility on Parcels 1143-A-NP-HV and 1148-A-NP-HV, located on Dalton Wash; the
applicant is Kyle Chen. A zone change and potential 7–lot subdivision in Brookside. A PD in New
Harmony at Exit 40. Those items will come before the commission when the projects have all the
documents and required information.
