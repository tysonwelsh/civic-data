Presentation by Councilor Katrice MacKay: Addressing Provo's Housing Challenges
00:22:27

Council Chair Katrice MacKay opened the Provo City Housing Summit by expressing
appreciation to the State Legislators for their recent efforts in addressing housing issues. She
highlighted several key pieces of legislation, such as HB 572, SB 206, SB 268, SB 168, and HB
13, which have contributed to housing affordability in Utah. She also acknowledged the role of
the Ken C. Gardner Housing Affordability Dashboard, funded through HB 462, in supporting the
Summit’s goals.

Councilor MacKay outlined the objectives of the summit and Provo’s initiatives aimed at
addressing housing challenges. She emphasized the City’s goal to create more attainable
homeownership opportunities by leveraging state support. She noted that the recent economic
difficulties, including rising interest rates, material and infrastructure costs, and challenges with
builder financing, have all contributed to a shortage of affordable, owner-occupied housing in
Provo. As a result, many families are being forced to relocate, and rental properties are replacing
owner-occupied homes, causing instability in neighborhoods and schools.

    1. Housing Goals:
          o Provo's primary housing goal is to increase the number of attainable, owner-
             occupied homes. This includes single-family homes, townhomes, and
             condominiums at prices affordable to moderate- and middle-income households.
          o The current housing market has led to a rapid increase in prices, making
             homeownership unattainable for many, with 61% of housing units in Provo being
             rented, compared to the statewide average of 30%.
    2. Actions Taken:
          o The City has been proactive in ensuring that new developments prioritize owner-
             occupied homes. Development agreements are being used to guarantee owner-
             occupancy, which are recorded on the property title.
          o Leveraging City-owned land for housing developments is another strategy to
             reduce costs and promote homeownership. For example, the Buckley Draw

Presentation by Councilor George Handley: Key Demographic and Economic Housing
00:35:57

Councilor George Handley addressed the key demographic and economic factors that are
shaping Provo’s housing challenges, noting that the high percentage of rental properties, the
affordability gap, and rising median home prices are making it increasingly difficult for residents
to afford homes in Provo.

Key Issues Impacting Homeownership:

    1. High Rental Dominance:
          o Councilor Handley highlighted that 62% of housing units in Provo are rentals,
             including 22% of single-family homes and 58% of attached single-family homes.
             This high rental rate, driven by investors purchasing properties and converting
             them into rentals, has reduced the number of homes available for purchase.
    2. Affordability Gap:
          o He stressed that the gap between income levels and home prices is one of the
             largest barriers to homeownership. Housing costs for homeowners with a
             mortgage take up around 37% of household income, which exceeds the

    1. Illegal Rental Units:
           o Investors converting single-family homes into duplexes or rentals have further

    2. Developer Preference for Rentals:
           o Many developers prefer building rental units over for-sale homes, limiting the
               options for families like the Smiths.
    3. Infrastructure Constraints:
           o Infrastructure issues, particularly in West Provo and near Provo Canyon, such as
               sewer limitations, are delaying the construction of over 1,400 entitled housing
               units, including apartments, townhomes, and single-family homes.
    4. Limited Resources for Buyers:
           o Programs like Home Purchase Plus are restricted by income, credit, and liquid

could see growing income inequality and displacement as families are priced out of
homeownership. The high cost of housing, illegal rentals, and infrastructure delays affect not
only individual families but also the entire community. Councilor Handley called for strategic
interventions to ensure that Provo remains a viable place for residents of all income levels to live
and thrive.

Presentation by Councilor Rachel Whipple: Proposed Solutions for Housing Affordability

Councilor Rachel Whipple introduced a series of legislative solutions designed to make housing
more accessible and affordable for Provo residents. These solutions address illegal rental units,
infrastructure development, and assistance for renters aspiring to become homeowners. She
explained that these proposals would help families struggling to purchase homes while also
addressing systemic issues in Provo's housing market.

Council Vice-Chair Whipple began by discussing the need for more homes built for sale rather
than rent. One pressing issue is the rise of investors purchasing single-family homes and
converting them into illegal rental units. These conversions often lead to over-occupancy and
violations of renter's rights, and the current $1,000 fine is insufficient to deter such practices.

Legislative Ask: Amend Utah Code 10-3-703 to allow cities like Provo to impose stricter
penalties for property use violations, raising the penalty cap. This would give Provo greater
control over illegal conversions, ensuring more homes remain available for families who wish to
buy.

Next, Council Vice-Chair Whipple proposed that legislation be drafted to limit the number of
housing units a single investor can own. In new developments, investors purchasing multiple
properties often prevent local families from buying homes.

Legislative Ask: Draft legislation to limit or disincentivize the number of housing units a single
investor can own in designated residential areas, particularly in new developments. A statewide
limit could also help reduce investor dominance in the housing market.

Council Vice-Chair Whipple then touched on development agreements, which are one of the few
tools Provo must guarantee owner-occupied homes in new projects. However, Provo cannot
propose development agreements; they must be initiated by developers.

Legislative Ask: Allow cities like Provo to request development agreements to ensure a better
balance between rental and for-sale units in new housing developments. This would help Provo
fulfill its general plan goals and moderate-income housing strategies.

Shifting focus to renters who want to transition to homeownership, Council Vice-Chair Whipple
discussed the Law Enforcement First-Time Homebuyer Program. While this program is helpful,
she argued that it should be expanded to include other essential community workers.

Legislative Ask: Expand the Law Enforcement First-Time Homebuyer Program to include
firefighters, teachers, and healthcare workers, providing more essential personnel with
opportunities to purchase homes.

She also addressed the need to expand the first-time homeownership program, especially in
legacy cities like Provo, where most land is already developed. Provo needs programs that help
residents finance the purchase of existing homes, not just new builds.

Legislative Ask: Expand the first-time homeownership program to cover existing homes in
legacy cities such as Provo, and increase the maximum purchase price to reflect current market
conditions.

Finally, Council Vice-Chair Whipple proposed a partnership with the state to convert existing
rental properties into owner-occupied homes. Many single-family homes in Provo are being
converted into rentals, undermining efforts to create stable, long-term neighborhoods.

Legislative Ask: Create an owner-occupancy overlay zone where homes sold must go to owner-
occupiers who will live in the home for at least one year. If rental properties are sold, current
tenants would have a 90-day notice and the right of first refusal to purchase the property.
Additionally, landlords selling properties in these zones could receive a capital gains tax rebate
after the home has been owner-occupied for a designated number of years.

Conclusion:
Council Vice-Chair Whipple emphasized that these legislative changes are crucial for ensuring
that more families in Provo have the opportunity to achieve homeownership. Expanding first-
time homebuyer programs, tightening enforcement against illegal rentals, and improving
infrastructure for new developments will create a balanced and affordable housing market. She
urged state legislators to support these initiatives and help Provo’s residents realize their dreams
of owning a home. Provo’s staff will be following up to discuss next steps with policymakers.
She thanked everyone for their time and support in these efforts.

Opened the Floor for Comments 01:43:47

Someone asked about the current state of the shape of the available housing state.
Council Chair McKay said we have older stock and that Provo is interested in making necessary
upgrades to make some older homes including pioneer homes to be reusable.

Nick Fritz from IHC asked about how many units are in the land trust. Director Sarah Van Cleve
replied, that there is much land available to develop affordable housing units, however the only
current project includes six affordable housing units.

Director of Utah Housing Coalition, Tara Riddle asked whether the new housing units will include
features that allow for aging in place. Specifically, she inquired about ensuring wide entrances,
accessible bathrooms on the main floor, and a bedroom on the main floor as well. She emphasized
that these features are important for allowing people to stay in their homes as they age, pointing
out that many existing homes have too many stairs and design elements that make them less
suitable for older residents. She suggested that it is a good idea to focus on including such features
in future developments. Council Chair McKay said that she will keep that in mind because she is
on that board.

Director Riddle then inquired about unrelated people living in a home and requested an exemption
for seniors. Council Chair Katrice MacKay explained that the age requirement for the exemption
was recently lowered from 65 to 60. She added that there is a process for having a legal Accessory
Dwelling Unit (ADU) in a home.

Steve Waldrip then asked what resources the city could dedicate to a matching program for state
rehabilitation and the conversion of single-family rental homes that are in poor condition. He
specifically asked if the city has access to Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) funding
or RDA’s 10% fund to assist in such efforts.

Council Chair MacKay confirmed that Provo does have Redevelopment Agency (RDA) resources,
with some already allocated to the six townhomes previously mentioned. She also noted that the
city receives CDBG funds annually, but the amount has been significantly reduced recently.

A Planning Commissioner asked about the Provo City General Plan, which emphasizes infill
development as a key housing strategy. He noted that greenfield development is often more
expensive than redevelopment and asked how infill fits into the city’s overall housing strategy.

Council Chair MacKay responded that infill development has been a focus for Provo, with several
projects completed in pioneer neighborhoods that have larger lots. She affirmed that infill
development remains a viable option for the city moving forward.
