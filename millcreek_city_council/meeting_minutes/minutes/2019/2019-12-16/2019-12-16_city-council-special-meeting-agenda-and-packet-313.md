’ about shopping carts not staying on site. Staff brought forth a proposed ordinance to regulate

would be interested in exploring options to deal with plastic bags. Dave Fenton said Romac
Industries does specialty projects and services for retailers including repairing shopping carts and
installing cart retention systems which sends out a signal and locks the shopping cart wheels so
they cannot be removed from the site. Romac Industries also provides shopping cart retrieval

2. Ordinance Discussions; Kurt Hansen, Legislative Policy Director
a. Code Section 11.20.130 Parking Prohibited When

Kurt Hansen said Millcreek did not give citations for illegal street parking in the winter.
Staff puts warnings on cars and emails Unified Police Department (UPD) about the cars.
Hansen proposed an ordinance that restricted street parking from November 16" through
March 15" instead of the existing ordinance which read, “No person shall park a vehicle
on any city street when it is snowing or snow is on the street during the months of
November, December, January, February, March and April.” Hansen said other cities
restrict the time of day in addition to the month. Mayor Silvestrini said some residents
need street parking for their homes. He said that snowplows would not plow streets with
cars on it. Council Member Marchant asked about the process for citations. Mayor
Silvestrini said the process was putting a warning on the car, if it was not removed within
a certain amount of time a ticket would be issued, and if it was not moved within a
certain amount of time after that then it would be towed. Council Member Uipi said it
was a hazard for residents if roads were not plowed. She said the City did not have the
manpower to enforce the ordinance. Mayor Silvestrini said it was appropriate to prohibit
parking during snow times because the streets needed to be plowed. The Council decided
the existing ordinance sufficiently met the City’s needs. Council Member Catten asked
why code enforcement could not issue citations. Mayor Silvestrini said there would need
to be an enforcement court to hear ticket appeals. John Brems said criminal violations
were done through law enforcement. Council Member Jackson said an enforcement

b. Anti-Idling Ordinance
Hansen showed the Council a Compendium of Idling Regulations from the American
Transportation Research Association. Hansen felt that an outdoor temperature of 40
degrees at the low end and 80 degrees at the high end was allowable for idling. Mayor
Silvestrini said the ordinance would be complaint driven with one warning and UPD
would do the enforcement. He said it would be important to have trained law
enforcement dealing with the matter because it would involve confrontation. Council
Member Marchant said it was a safety issue with pick-up and drop-off areas at schools.
Council Member Catten said staff could ask the schools to remind parents to not idle.
Mayor Silvestrini would like to require signage in the City where there were
opportunities for idling. Hansen said there should be public engagement on the matter.
Council Member Jackson suggested taking the proposed ordinance to the community
council meetings in January.

c. Code Section 14.50 Dedication of Private Streets to Public Ownership
Hansen said private streets would need to meet certain criteria before the City would take
ownership of it. He said Millcreek counsel built some flexibility with Council
determination into the proposed ordinance. He showed the Council the proposed
language. Mayor Silvestrini said each situation could have different factors. John Brems
said he added the phrase, “‘...unless there is a compelling public interest...” was an
escape hatch but the Council would have to establish a factual basis for the decision.
Francis Lilly said the General Plan gave the Council a lot to go on when the issue came
up. The Council decided to have that ordinance be brought as a first reading to the

3.1 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution 19-40, Approving and Authorizing
Execution of an Interlocal Cooperative Agreement to Revise and Restate the
Creation and Governance of the Unified Fire Authority
Mayor Silvestrini said the agreement rewrote the interlocal that created the Unified Fire
Authority (UFA). It was necessary because the termination provisions were called into
question because of when Herriman and Riverton left UPD, so it would ensure nobody
left the fire service without adequate notice. He said with an influx of new members, the
members became concerned about voting power being diluted and that a smaller
jurisdiction could affect the budget though they represented a small service area. The
agreement added a weighted voting mechanism that would only come into play with
budgetary matters and when at least two members called for it. He said the UFA Board
looked at population and financial contribution to UFA made by the entities for the
weight. Mayor Silvestrini passed out a handout on those city weights. He said cities that
contract with UFA but were not members of the Unified Fire Service Area (Alta,
Holladay, and Cottonwood Heights) had a less relevant taxable value. He pointed out that
Millcreek’s taxable value was higher than Taylorsville. The other changes in the
agreement would modernize the agreement because the original interlocal was drafted to
create the UFA when it was born out of County Fire, so a lot of the verbiage was no
longer applicable. He mentioned that Brighton would incorporate in January and would
be invited to join UPA.

Mayor Silvestrini asked for public comment. There were no comments.

Council Member Uipi moved to approve Item 3.2, Resolution 19-40. Council Member
Jackson seconded. The Recorder called for the vote. All Council Members voted yes. The
motion passed unanimously.
