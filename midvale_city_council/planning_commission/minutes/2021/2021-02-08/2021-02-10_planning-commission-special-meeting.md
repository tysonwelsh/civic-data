The submitted elevations show the adaptation of two shipping containers painted two different
colors and accented with fiber cement panels and perforated stainless-steel panels. In this subarea,
the ordinance requires retail storefronts and facades be designed with integrated architectural
features to create visually interesting frontages, quality and attention to detail, and reflect regional

The final landscape plans for WinCo were approved by the City on January 26, 2009. The approved
landscape plans show 83,980 ft2, which exceeds the required 13% of the lot area, and this project

maintained since WinCo opened. Staff recommends requiring the applicant replant the missing
landscaping to match the original approval or submit updated plans showing the overall site meets
applicable ordinance requirements as part of the Final Site Plan. The proposed landscape plan
shows only the changes for the immediate area surrounding the new building.
Staff recommends including these requirements as a condition of approval.

There are shared parking agreements within the Riverwalk Shopping Center that will need to be
provided by the applicant as part of the final site plan approval. Staff recommends requiring proof
of this parking agreement as a condition of approval.

Compliance with Title 17 of the Midvale City Municipal Code will require some minor revisions
to the site plan, as indicated throughout this report. Once these revisions have been addressed, no
other conflicts with this Title or State/Federal Law have been identified. Separate applications and

4.     RESIDENTIAL PLANNED UNIT DEVELOPMENTS; ALEX MURPHY,
       PLANNING DIRECTOR (STAFF).

Mr. Murphy stated that for many years, the City has had standards for master planned
developments in the residential (SF and RM) zones. This language predates the current version
of the ordinance adopted in 2001. It has seen minor updates over time but has remained largely
unchanged for many years.

Recently, the City has started receiving more complaints regarding the impacts of master planned
developments on existing neighborhoods. These complaints have included topics such as
parking, open space, density, building heights, setbacks, and others. In response to these

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION FEBRUARY 10, 2021
complaints, the City Council directed Staff to propose changes to master planned development
standards. The City Council adopted a moratorium temporarily prohibiting multifamily master
planned developments in the SF zones in July 2020, which Staff has proposed to make
pennanent as a separate agenda item.

Staff spent several months meeting with Councilmembers to obtain input on what issues they see
around master planned developments and best practices for allowing this style of development.
The attached language is a first draft of standards for a new development type, a "residential
planned unit development" or "RPUD," that could replace the old master planned development
language. This new language includes the following:

      •   New density standards, with density increasing as project size and unit mix increase.
      •   Allowed and prohibited uses within RPUDs. All projects may use single family
          detached or two-family dwellings. As project size increases, additional unit types are
          allowed: single-family attached (townhomes) can only be constructed in projects at
          least 2 acres in size and multifamily projects (between 4-8 units per building) are not
          allowed until a project reaches 5 acres.
      •   Nondiscretionary density bonuses for preserving existing housing stock, providing
          affordable units, and providing for-sale products.
      •   Minimum lot size and setback requirements. Side setbacks are increased above
          underlying zone requirements, providing a better buffer along the edges of the project,
          and a maximum number of units per building is established, prohibiting overly large
          buildings. Housing mix requirements. To receive the highest allowed density in any
          project, an RPUD must provide a variety of housing types based on overall project
          size. Projects under 5 acres must provide at least 2 housing types, with each housing
          type constituting at least 25% of the total unit count. Projects over 5 acres must provide
          at least 3 housing types, with each type constituting at least 15% of the total unit count
          and not exceeding a limit based on unit type (ranging from 30-60%). Projects would be
          allowed to provide fewer unit types but would not be eligible for the highest density
          numbers as a result.
     •    Building design standards, including requirements for porches on certain units,
          minimum roof pitch, garage design, window coverage, and other standards. Existing
          units that are preserved for RPUDs are encouraged, but not required, to meet these
          standards. The State Legislature is currently considering a bill that would prohibit
          cities from implementing building design standards. Staff is monitoring the debate and
          may pull these requirements from consideration if such a prohibition is adopted.
     •    Minimum parking ratios. Per unit calculations are based on bedroom count with a
          reduction provided for projects providing affordable housing at or below 30% AMI.
          Guest parking is based on project size and unit count. Overflow parking is required for
          stalls located in a private garage; this is intended to provide additional parking
          opportunities when garages are converted to storage areas. On-street parking may be
          used for a portion of the guest and overflow requirements and must be evenly
          distributed throughout the project.
     •    Design standards for parking areas, including minimum stall size and aisle width,
          garage location and orientation, and screening requirements.
     •    Open space and primary park space requirements. A primary park must be provided
          centrally located in the project and include a variety and type of amenities that
          increases based on project size. The park may not be used as an above-ground

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION FEBRUARY 10, 2021
          stonnwater management facility. At least 40% of the project must be open space.
      •   General landscape standards, including maximum turf coverage, minimum plant
          coverage, minimwn tree count, and other standards meant to bring landscape design up
          to par with existing requirements.
      •   Ownership and maintenance of common facilities, with options to create an HOA,
          dedicate property to the City (following Public Works and City Council approval), or
          transfer to another organization for long-term conservation.
      •   A deed restriction on common open space, prohibiting future development of
          commonly owned areas.

Staff would like to discuss this language with the Planning Commission and obtain feedback on
what provisions should be pursued or removed. This language is intended as a starting point for
the discussion and Staff expects it will be revised before adoption is considered.

6.        [PREVIOUSLY AGENDA ITEM 3) TXT-20-08; ZONING ORDINANCE TEXT
          AMENDMENT; CITYWIDE; TEXT AMENDMENT MODIFYING MASTER
          PLANNED DEVELOPMENT STANDARDS IN SF-1 AND SF-2 ZONES; CITY
          STAFF (APPLICANT); ALEX MURPHY, PLANNING DIRECTOR (STAFF).

Mr. Alex Murphy stated that in 200 1, following the City's annexation of the Fort Union area,
the City's zoning ordinance was rewritten to fold the City and County zoning ordinances
together. As part of this effort, the Single Family Residential (SF-1 and SF-2) Zones were
created and given the following purposes:



     A.    Allow neighborhood commercial and recreational activities that are compatible
           with residential neighborhoods;
     B.    Provide opportunities for variations in architectural design and housing types;
     C.    Provide opportunities for duplex (two-family) uses in specific duplex overlay districts;
     D.    Provide opportunities for agricultural uses in discrete agricultural overlay districts; and
     E.    Promote pedestrian connections within developments and between
           adjacent neighborhoods.



     A.    Allow for single family development that is compatible with existing development;
     B.    Maintain the character of neighborhoods with compatible residential design;
     C.    Allow for neighborhood commercial uses that are compatible with
           residential neighborhoods; and
     D.    Require streetscape design that minimizes the impacts of both new development
           and the automobile on existing residents.

A map of these zones is attached.

When these zones were created, they included standards for master planned
developments, streamlining the standards that had been part of the City's earlier zoning
ordinance. These standards included development requirements, including open space
and trail networks, and development incentives, including a density bonus, opportunity
to building multifamily residential uses instead of single family, and others. These

PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION FEBRUARY 10, 2021
standards have seen only minor modifications since 2001.

In recent years, City residents have begun expressing greater concerns with the impacts
master planned developments have on existing neighborhoods, particularly when
constructed as multifamily residential projects. To address the issue, the City Council
adopted a temporary moratorium, prohibiting multifamily residential master planned
developments in the SF- I and SF-2 zones, in July 2020 (Ordinance No. 2020-0-11 , see
attached) and directed Staff to begin working on amendments to the zoning ordinance to
address these concerns.

Staff has spent the last several months researching best practices and interviewing
Councilmembers to develop new standards for master planned developments. The new
language is not yet ready for adoption but the moratorium on multifamily residential master
planned developments expired in January 2021 and a permanent fix is needed to ensure the
negative impacts of these projects does not resume. Consequently, Staff is proposing an
ordinance amendment that would permanently repeal the option to construct multifamily
master planned developments in the SF- I and SF-2 zones. Master planned developments
proposing single family detached units or, in applicable areas, duplex/twinhome units, would
still be permitted and no other changes are proposed. This amendment would allow Staff to
continue improving the master planned development language while also ensuring property
owners are able to take advantage of their right to develop in the meantime.

STAFF RECOMMENDATIONS:

Staff recommends forwarding a positive recommendation to the City Council to adopt
the proposed text amendment.
