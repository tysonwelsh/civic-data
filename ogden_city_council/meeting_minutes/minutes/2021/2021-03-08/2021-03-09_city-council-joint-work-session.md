Ogden Nature Center 2020 Annual Report
          Ogden Nature Center Executive Director McKinley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the Center's
Annual Report. She stated that the COVID-19 pandemic created a great deal of uncertainty at the beginning of 2020 and the
Executive Board of the Center was very concerned about the impact the pandemic would have on the Center; however, once the
Center reopened to the public on June 1, visitation slowly began to resume. Staffmg was not reduced, but volunteer numbers
decreased drastically, which had a negative impact on various programs at the Center. However, she is hopeful that volunteerism
will increase throughout 2021 as school field trip activities will return to normal in the 2021-2022 school year. She then reported
on plans for land related projects in 2020, including the storm water pond project. She displayed a photograph of the pond area
from 2019, after which she shared a 2020 photo illustrating the drastic improvement to the pond area. The storm water detention
pond not only increases the beauty of the Center but serves as a wildlife habitat as well. She reported that some special events
were cancelled in 2020 but will be held again in 2021. Finally, the 2020 operating budget was $944,549 and the 2021 operating
budget will be $923,240; in 2020 the Center received a paycheck protection program loan of$115,900 (which will be forgiven)
and $259,000 in CARES grant funds. This increased the total operating income to $1,095,850. She concluded that the Center
survived the 2020 COVID-19 pandemic but thrived thanks to a resilient and creative staff and Board of Directors, along with
additional funding. She expects the Center will be a stronger organization once the pandemic is over.
          Council member Choberka thanked Ms. McKinley for her report and expressed her gratitude to the staff of the Center for
implementing changes to meet the needs of the community amidst the COVID-19 pandemic.
         ·Council member Stephens asked if any programming is taking place at the Center's property on Mountain Road. Ms.
McKinley stated that most activities occurring at that site include land management activities and habitat improvements.
Programming is limited, but the facility is open as a nature park for hiking and community enjoyment.

Proposed Rezone at 1752 Pacific Avenue
         Sam Hunter has submitted a petition to rezone three parcels at approximately 1752 Pacific Avenue from R-1-5 toM-1 for
the construction of several small manufacturing shops. The properties were included in the 20 16 Gibson Community Plan update
and were rezoned from M-1 to R-1-5 in 2019 in accordance with that plan. The Planning Commission has forwarded a
recommendation of denial of the proposed rezone with a vote of six to two.

              Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the purpose of the rezone
    application, The applicant seeks to rezone three parcels to allow for the construction of a light manufacturing shop, This would
    include the demolition of the existing single-family dwelling, combining all three parcels into one, and developing a 50 foot by
    13 5-foot building that will be divided into two to four shop spaces. He discussed the history of the property dating back to 20 16

    Commission's consideration of the application; they considered whether the proposed rezone creates a defmite edge for the
    development and providers buffering between types of uses. The subject property is along the boundary of an M-2 zone to the
    west, an M -1 zone to the south, and a R-1-5 zone to the east. It fronts Pacific A venue to the west and provides a defmite edge and
    buffer between the two zones. There are a mix of single-family dwellings and manufacturing uses to the south. In 2019, the
    Commission found Pacific A venue could act as a transition area with manufacturing zones on the west and residential to the east.
    The proposed M-1 zone would connect with properties to the south and no buffer was provided for the R-1-5 zone to the east.
    Further Commission considerations for the current application include whether the proposed use reflects the character of the
    overall district; districts determined to be in transition may be given special considerations. The area has historically been zoned
    for manufacturing uses, but residential uses are scattered throughout the manufacturing areas. The Gibson Community Plan
    spurred the rezone of existing single-family homes from manufacturing to R-1-5 and many of the properties on the east side of
    Pacific A venue are single family homes. The area is part of a larger future redevelopment area, the primary objective of which is

    direction of the Gibson Community Plan. The Commission considered whether the Community Plan supports the current
    application; the Plan identifies development objectives for the area, including strengthening the residential character of the area
    south of 17111 Street near the river. He reviewed the zoning map to orient the Council to zoning changes; there is light
    manufacturing called for to the west of Pacific Avenue, adjacent to the railroad, with residential uses to the east. Pacific A venue

    property's surroundings, after which he discussed the development possibilities for the property; under the requested zoning

    street widening, a lot depth of 115 feet with a 20-foot rear and front setback. An additional42 feet would be set aside for parking
    area. The Commission considered the fact that the area is in transition as well as the appropriate boundary line for the property.
    They ultimately voted six to two to recommend denial of the application to rezone the property from R-1-5 to M-1, based on the

Business Depot Ogden Lease Revenue Ordinance Amendments
         The City's Community and Economic Development Department has forwarded a recommendation to renew the Quality
Neighborhood Initiative funding for an additional five years. The proposal would continue the appropriation of $1,000,000 per
year from Business Depot Ogden (BDO) lease revenue to be administered by the Redevelopment Agency. The proposed
ordinance amendment renewing the Quality Neighborhood Initiative funding also comes with the elimination of the $200,000 per
year appropriation for the HELP loan program, which was approved for a five-year period beginning with the fiscal year 2017
budget. In addition to these changes, the proposed ordinance amendments also include a $1,000,000 per year appropriation for a
project or projects benefiting the Marshall N. White Center and a potential recreation center which may or may not be integrated
into the Marshall N. White Center. The proposed ordinance amendment would establish budgetary requirements for the use of
BDO lease revenue for the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative and the Marshall N. White Center for a period of five years
beginning with Fiscal Year (FY) 2022, which begins July 1, 2021.
         Comptroller Stout used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide additional information about the BDO proceeds
ordinance; the current ordinance directs priority of use for proceeds received at BDO as the Mayor's proposed budget is prepared.
Proceeds are limited to use for one-time projects or funding. In the past, proceeds have funded the HELP program and the Quality
Neighborhoods Initiative, with sunset for both programs in FY2021. The HELP program made available approximately $1.2
million to lend to applicants; there is approximately $845,000, or 75 loans outstanding. Repayment is estimated annually at
approximately $100,000. During FY20, 26 new HELP loans were issued, with an average amount of $13,500. Administration is
proposing that the ordinance be adjusted to allow for allocation priority as follows:

Quality Neighborhood Initiative Program Renewal: and
Synchrony Bank Line of Credit Renewal
          The City's Community and Economic Development Department is proposing the City Council, Mayor and
Redevelopment Agency renew the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative. The Quality Neighborhoods Initiative was first adopted in
November 2015 as a way to coordinate various community and economic development programs and strategies into a
consolidated vision designed to help stabilize and revitalize neighborhoods in Ogden. The Initiative uses several programs,
including the city's five-year Consolidated Plan and Annual Action Plans, the City's Redevelopment Agency, and other city and
state programs, to establish a coordinated pattern of public investment into the overall health of the community. The program uses
several funding sources, which include Community Development Bock Grants, BDO lease revenue, state and other local funding
sources. An update to the BDO lease revenue appropriation is addressed in a separate but related Council item.
          Community Development Manager Ogden used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the proposal to
renew the Quality Neighborhood Initiative. He discussed the evolution of the Initiative relative to funding sources and
neighborhood focused strategies. The purpose of the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative is to create healthy neighborhoods with a
broad range ofhousing options reflecting the demographic, ownership, and value ratios of the community. The philosophy of the
initiative is that a strong core builds on strength and enhances the City's image. The five guiding principles of the Initiative
include:
                1. Concentrate resources and target interventions to establish and encourage investment in the components that
                    make up a Quality Neighborhood.
                2. Focus on creating communities of choice by promoting and supporting an environment of unique homes with
                    general market demand.
                3. Identify and build on strengths by focusing on areas exhibiting a higher degree of Quality Neighborhood
                    characteristics, by investing in historic structures, and preserving and promoting architecture that contributes to
                    the unique character and value of legacy neighborhoods.
                4. Build confidence in the market by maintaining critical momentum through appropriate velocity and scale of
                    investments.
                5. Overcome impediments to success by establishing a strong declared intent to the public that the City will take
                    steps necessary to assure quality projects are supported.
          Mr. Ogden highlighted the characteristics of healthy neighborhoods relative to housing, demographics, social and
enviromnental indicators, and facilities/services. The Initiative aims to build on core strengths currently in place in the
community, pursuing physical improvements that result in lasting changes, and promoting affordable and accessible housing
concepts. Targeting in the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative area results in maximized impact and lasting community benefits that
will snowball to incent private investment. Mr. Ogden presented graphs providing a comparison of demographics and income in
the County, the entire City, and the Neighborhood Revitalization Strategy Area (NRSA); healthy neighborhood indicators include
housing values and a viable rent versus ownership status ofhousing units. By comparison, the City has a relatively high number of
affordable housing units. Historical accomplishments in the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative area include development ofinfill
housing units, rehab/resale of dilapidated units, issuance of HELP and Own in Ogden loans, unit reduction, and public
improvements; the total investment for these accomplishments is $64,800,000. He provided a breakdown of Quality
Neighborhoods funds and Synchrony Bank line of credit funds, after which he presented photographs of several of the projects
that have been completed with these funding sources. He then summarized Administration's recmmnended updates to the Quality
Neighborhoods Initiative; while selected properties should be primarily in target neighborhoods in the East Central area, a
significant portion of City funds are available for use citywide, i.e., purchase/rehab/resell, Own in Ogden, HELP, Emergency
Home Repair, and Code Enforcement. Additionally, Administration would like to focus on restoring historic properties where
possible, as well as develop new and renovate housing primarily in target neighborhoods which demonstrates best practices in

Administration would like to renew the Quality Neighborhoods Initiative for a five-year period, beginning in FY22. This would
