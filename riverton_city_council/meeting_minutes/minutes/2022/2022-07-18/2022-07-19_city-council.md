Discussion Items

       Garbage Collection RFP Discussion

Craig Calvert, Contract Administrator and Purchasing Manager, explained the RFP for municipal
waste will go out the first week of August. He recommends the City include a bid option to have
the hauler service and maintain the cans as part of the contract and compare that cost to the
cost of the City maintaining the cans.

Mr. Calvert asked the Council whether they would like to continue to have recycling every other
week, weekly recycling or eliminate recycling. Councilmember McDougal said that he does not
recommend weekly recycling. He said that most of the recycling is going to the landfill. He
reported that the state passed a law which requires every waste hauler to divulge what
Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting

percentage is going to recycling on a quarterly basis. He would like the bid to include recycling
every other week.

Councilmember Buroker said the current glass recycling program does not cost the City any
money. Mayor Staggs asked which companies can provide recycling. Mr. Calvert responded
that Waste Management and Rocky Mountain Recycling are the two providers for recycling.
Councilmembers agreed the bid should include a recycling option and then they can make a
decision once they compare.

Mr. Calvert asked if the Council would like to include a green waste program. He said if the
Council would like a green waste program then the City would need to purchase cans, $45 for
each resident for a total of $500,000. Councilmember McDougal said the green waste program
is going away at the Transjordan landfill, all green waste will go directly to the landfill.
Councilmembers agreed that they do not want an option for a green waste program.

Mr. Calvert asked if the Council would like to have multiple containers for glass recycling.
Councilmember Buroker said she would like one additional glass recycling container. Mayor
Staggs asked if there would be an additional cost for another location and Mr. Calvert did not
know. Councilmembers agreed to include an option for 1 or 2 glass recycling containers and
then decide based on the price.

Mr. Calvert explained the previous bid included a Christmas tree pickup option, electronic waste
recycling and document destruction. Council agreed that none of these options would be
included in the current bid process.


         TRCC Grant Application

Sheril Garn, Director of Events and Operations, said that the City has submitted the TRCC
Grant application and received confirmation. The applications will go to an internal committee
for review and then to the Board for review. After the Board reviews the application, then the
City will be asked to present to the Board.

Ms. Garn and Casey Saxton, Communications Director, presented a slide show of the grant
application. This is included in the packet.

Mayor Staggs said that Ms. Garn has asked him to make the presentation to the TRCC Board.

Councilmember Stewart asked if the application process is more favorable for cities in the west.
Councilmember McCay responded that the TRCC Board has more money overall to award and
will be giving priority to accessibility parks and regional projects.

Ms. Garn asked for clarification on whether the Council agreed with getting construction
documents completed now so that construction could begin in January 2023 if the City was
awarded the TRCC funds. Council agreed that construction documents could be completed
now.

         Green Well Exterior Design and Time-Line

Mr. Calvert explained that in 2020 the City did a RFP for a water treatment facility at the Green
Well. This contract was awarded to Total Water Management. Total Water Management
Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting
July 19, 2022




completed Phase 1, permit to discharge into the Jordan River. Total Water Management would
now like to proceed with Phase 2, construction of a containerized treatment facility. If the
Council does not want a containerized facility, there is money in the budget for construction of a
building.

Will Richardson, Total Water Management, congratulated the City on securing the discharge
permit. He presented two different proposal ideas for the treatment system: a containerized
treatment system that would sit next to the building or an expansion of the building on site.

Mayor Staggs explained the RFP was $2.8 million which included the treatment plant with the
containerized treatment system. When he presented to the Salt Lake County Council, he
presented a building expansion option. He said the City has invested $2.1 million in this project
so far and would contribute another $500,000 so that the system would blend in with the
existing housing system. He asked which option the Council would prefer: building expansion
or containerized treatment system.

Councilmember Stewart asked if having a fixed building reports better than having a portable
container. Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, said that a building would be better for
assets.

Total Water Management said they will be very involved in the implementation of the system
and will work with City contracted engineers to complete the project.

Councilmember McCay asked what the price difference will be between the building and the
containerized system. Mr. Hicks said the price difference is not known until the City receives
the engineering drawings and paperwork but estimates it to be $100,000 to $300,000 difference.

Councilmember Buroker said she would definitely like to expand the building because it looks
much nicer and would like the landscaping to be more natural. She would also encourage staff
to explore an education opportunity for residents on water treatment.

Councilmember McDougal asked if the building expansion allows for future upgrades. Total
Water Management recommends having a conversation with engineers to determine if there
should be space saved for future use.

Mayor Staggs said the discharge permit allows the City to pull 2,000 gallons per minute which is
near capacity and the building will maximize the output of the well today. Total Water
Management said if technology were developed in the future where there would not be
discharge, he recommended a bigger building that could house this new technology.

Stacie Olson, Water Manager, said they have been focusing on training for this well. They have
also hired a water quality operator conservation specialist who will be managing this well.

Council agreed the City should expand the building instead of using a containerized system.
Council would also like to build the building to meet future needs. Councilmember McCay
agreed with Councilmember Buroker that she would like to provide an educational opportunity.

Casey Saxton, Communications Director, said he would like to have outdoor signage near the
Green Well that describes the importance of culinary and secondary water and describes how
the water is cleaned. Councilmember Stewart suggested the City provide a QR code that links
to a video explaining the process of how Riverton City receives its water.

Public Hearings

         Ordinance No. 22-19 – Hughes and Hubbard Rezone, Rezone of 2.61 Acres
         Located at 12984 South 4490 West From RR-22 to R-3 (Single-family Residential
         with 14,000 sf Minimum Lot Size)

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained this was a public hearing for an
application to rezone 2.61-acres of land located at 12984 South 4490 West from RR-22 to R-3.
The property is addressed off an existing private lane but has frontage on the east side of 4570
West Street (4570 West is also known as Eagles Flight Road). The rezone includes all, or
portions of, parcels 27-31-200-038,-039, & -040.

Mr. Lethbridge said the property is currently zoned RR-22, which is a single family residential
zone with animal rights. Most of the properties between Sunday Drive and 4570 West north of
Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting
July 19, 2022




the SLR property north to Dutchman Drive are similarly zoned. In the last 3-4 years there have
been three (3) rezones in the area consistent with the General Plan. Two of the rezones
changed properties to R-3, and one facing Dutchman Lance changed zoning to R-2 SD.

Devin Hubbard, Applicant, commented that he has worked with the City to make sure everything
is in place for this development.

Councilmember Stewart asked about connectivity on Sunday Drive. Mr. Lethbridge said there is
nothing specific to this rezone.

Councilmember McCay asked about access. Mr. Lethbridge said there is no connection into the
SLR project because that project is private. The primary connection point would be from 4570
West. Councilmember McCay also asked if the applicant plans to remove the existing house
and the applicant said yes.

Councilmember McDougal asked about the property to the south and whether it is incorporated
into this rezone. Mr. Lethbridge said it is not tied to this rezone.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember McCay MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 22-19 –
Hughes and Hubbard Rezone, Rezone of 2.61 Acres Located at 12984 South 4490 West
From RR-22 to R-3 (Single-family Residential with 14,000 sf Minimum Lot Size).
Councilmember Stewart SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the
motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes,
McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Stewart-yes. The motion passed unanimously.
