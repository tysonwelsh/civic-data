2.      REVIEW OF BUSINESS MEETING AGENDA – Mayor Mike Weichers.

The Business Meeting Agenda was reviewed.

Mayor Weichers stated that a Proclamation would be presented honoring the life of
Don J. Antczak.

The first legislative item involved consideration of Ordinance 379-A or 379-D for a General Plan
Amendment for the .95-acre real estate located at 3526 East Fort Union Boulevard from residential
office to residential medium-density. Mayor Weichers reported that public input had been
extremely beneficial in understanding the importance of this request to the residents. The Council
considered the 2019 land use change and questioned whether a zone change was premature while
input was still being received. Growth and development patterns and affordable housing had also
been taken into consideration.

City Attorney, Shane Topham reported that although Work Session discussion is beneficial,
similar discussion would need to be on the record during the Business Meeting.

Council Member Bracken stated that he served as a Council Member present during the 2019
General Plan Amendment. He considered the current ordinance to be a lighter intensity request
and found it favorable.

Council Member Birrell commented that the current General Plan Amendment included a number
of items residents expressed concern with and believed the Residential Office Zone was more
favorable to the desired setbacks. District 4 constituents prefer R-1-8 (single family) for properties
along Ft Union Blvd east of 2700 East.

Council Member Newell shared the concerns of those expressed by residents. The aesthetics and
access along Fort Union Boulevard is a priority and he encouraged the Council to consider all of
the variables when vetting the Ordinance to ensure minimal issues in the future.

The next agenda item was consideration of Ordinance 380-A or Ordinance 380-D approving the
rezone of .95-Acre of real property located at 3526 East Fort Union Boulevard from R-1-8
(Residential Single-Family) to R-2-8 (Residential Multi-Family) and amending the zoning map.
Mr. Topham explained that the zoning classification must be based on the appropriate land use
classification. The proposed Ordinance 380-A would not be supported by the current land use and
would only be considered after Ordinance 379-A is approved. He clarified that zoning conditions
may only be used for conditional permit land uses, dwelling unit density, building heights, or
building square footage.

The next agenda item was consideration of Ordinance 381 amending Code Chapter 11.22
concerning the Permit Parking Program. Community and Economic Development Director, Mike
Johnson, stated that there was previous discussion regarding removing the portion of the Ordinance
that allowed for the creation of new permit parking areas. He confirmed that the change would
prohibit any new permit parking.

The next agenda item involved consideration of Ordinance 382 and Section 14.12.090 of the City
Code addressing roadway design. The City Council previously expressed concern with road
design speeds. After researching the Northeast Association of State Transportation Officials
(“NASHTO”), they discovered a way to change the design speed. Functional and contextual
classifications will be utilized to allow the City to design at different speeds. He reported that
classification will be included in the forthcoming Transportation Master Plan. Mr. Johnson
clarified that changing the design speed, as it exists, does not change posted speed limits. The
proposed Ordinance was to take effect with new or redesigned roads.

In response to a concern regarding traffic calming, Public Works Director, Matt Shipp confirmed
that Staff will be present the City Council with a new five-year plan during the next budget
discussion. Traffic calming may be reviewed with the consideration of finite funding. Mayor
Weichers remarked that a study of road condition labeling was conducted that gives each road a
Pavement Condition Index (“PCI”) grade for each stretch of road. If a road meets a specific
minimum, a slurry seal will be applied, however, if the grade is below the acceptable standard,
replacement will be considered.

Mr. Shipp stated that the forthcoming five-year plan requires less heavy maintenance and is geared
more toward reconstruction. Arterial roadways were reviewed. He confirmed that the item
involves consideration of new roads and new design. Council Member Bracken expressed concern
with the maximum speed limit being 30 MPH on a core arterial. Council Member Birrell
questioned a redesign at speeds higher than those listed in the proposed Ordinance. She was
frustrated by the weight it carries if the Council can override a road with this type of designation.

Mayor Weichers explained that the Ordinance was intended to address road design that includes
traffic calming and lower speeds. It was confirmed that the proposed standard takes into account
what currently exists in the City and the potential for future redesign.

        6.1      Consideration of Ordinance 379-A Approving a General Plan Amendment for
                 .95 Acre of Realty at 3526 East Fort Union Boulevard from Residential Office

                 OR
                 Consideration of Ordinance 379-D Denying a General Plan Amendment for
                 .95 Acre of Realty at 3526 East Fort Union Boulevard from Residential Office
                 to Residential Medium Density.

Mayor Weichers reported that the City has received numerous emails and phone calls from
residents regarding this issue. He encouraged the Council Members to consider the input received.
In 2019, there was a land use change that indicated that the eastern portion of Fort Union Boulevard
is a transitional area that does not have the same character as the lower western portion. The City
was in the process of updating the General Plan and was considering whether it would be expedient
to change the zoning at the same time. He stated that dramatic growth and development have
warranted caution when making land use changes. He reported that the developer presented his
plans at a previous meeting. There were concerns that the proposal did not include plans for
affordable housing.

Council Member Birrell had multiple discussions and received numerous emails from her
constituents regarding the proposal. Many of their concerns pertained to safety, traffic, quality of
life, and aesthetics. Setbacks were an issue, particularly along the back and sides of the homes.
She believed that a Residential Office zoning would allow for greater setbacks than Medium-
Density Residential.

Council Member Newell stated the City Council conducted an extensive investigation to determine
which outcome would be most beneficial to the City. Input was taken into consideration by both
City officials and members of the community.

Council Member Petersen stated that this issue is not unlike many other decisions made by the
Council which takes all comments into consideration. Responsible growth of the community is a
priority, and he appreciated the public input received.

Council Member Bracken stated that he served on the City Council in 2019 when the General Plan
was amended. It was called for by residents who believed the proposed location needed to be
reviewed. Medium-Density, Residential, and Residential Office were considered at that time. In
reviewing the request, Residential Office was determined to have greater setbacks but allows for
more intense uses. When reviewing building envelopes in the R-1 and R-2 zones, he found there
was not a significant difference. Traffic from three additional units equates to 30 additional cars.
He stated that he would typically support a change from Residential Office to Medium-Density
and maintained that the 2019 process was appropriate.

Council Member Bracken stated that the City originally contracted Public Works services through
the County, which worked well from incorporation until 2015. There was a time when accidents
and complaints exceeded the County’s ability to respond. At that time, the City determined that a
change was needed. A private service provider was contracted but unable to meet the City’s needs.
The contract was terminated. While negotiating services through Salt Lake County, the City hired
Public Works Director, Matt Shipp as well as a Supervisor. Questions were raised concerning the
City’s plows and equipment being left out and unprotected and the potential cost to the City. It
was determined that a Public Works Yard would be constructed to allow for the proper care and
storage of the equipment. He expressed his support for the Resolution.

Council Member Birrell reported she has lived near the foothills for 22 years and was pleased with
the services provided by Salt Lake County. Her concerns were with what is proposed being a
complex and providing meeting rooms, office space, and overnight accommodations. She believed
the City has obligated itself financially to own more equipment. Prior to obligating the City to
bond for millions, she would prefer one more meeting with Salt Lake County to produce a mutually
satisfactory contract and discuss conditions for snow plowing services. A Sustainability Plan was
suggested and further due diligence was preferred.

Council Member Petersen stated that when visiting the public works buildings of neighboring
cities, each one included a housing unit for their Public Works Department. With regard to
accommodations, there were two cots proposed to be located in a small office space that would
allow for workers to rest between shifts. This has been studied at great length and the City has
been in touch with Salt Lake County to determine the services they can provide. He believed the
City has done its due diligence and the proposed structure is needed. He expressed his support for
the proposed Resolution.

Council Member Newell reported that he retired from an industry that supplied salt, tools for
repair, and other items necessary to maintain Public Works-type vehicles. He emphasized the need
to mitigate costs and believed the Public Works Building was an investment with a return that will
come back to the City based on the fact they will be able to maintain, house, and protect equipment
