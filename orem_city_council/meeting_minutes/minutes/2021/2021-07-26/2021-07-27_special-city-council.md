       PUBLIC HEARING / ORDINANCE - Amending portions of Section 22-24-5(F) of the
       Orem City Code Pertaining to the overall height standards within the North Village
       District (NVD) zones and Portions of section 22-24-5(H) pertaining to parking standards
       in the North Village District (NVD) Zones.

Mr. Bench gave background on the status of the high limit ordinance. Mr. Bench stated that a 48
foot core 35 foot edge, with 2.25 stalls per unit will be voted on during this meeting.
Mr. Bench stated that the planning commission voted against recommending this ordinance.

Mrs. Lauret asked why the Planning Commission recommended against this ordinance. Mr.
Mickey Cochran, the chairman of the Orem City Planning Commission informed the City
Council on why the Planning Commission recommended against this ordinance. He stated that
the city has already changed things enough and the current recommendation of 2 stalls per unit
with a 60 foot height limit was up to state and city standards.

Mayor Brunst expressed he feels more restriction on the core height of the NVD zone could stunt
the overall growth of State Street. Mayor Brunst then stated the city in the past has strategically
placed high density housing developments throughout the city, stating this area is a zone where
high density housing should be allowed. He stated he would prefer to see the core height
maximum be 60 feet. Mayor Brunst concluded if the city does not allow high density in this
zone, those sorts of developments may be pushed into more unfavourable areas.

Mayor Brunst opened the public hearing at 6:21 pm

Kevin Hale expressed concern with the traffic in the NVD zone. Mr. Hale stated this
development would force traffic into surrounding residential areas.

John Barrick expressed thanks to the city council for hosting this special meeting. Mr. Barrick
stated the NVD zone is for small scale development and this high density project is not small
scale.

Alice Gold expressed concern with traffic in the area. She stated traffic on 400 west has been bad
for years and a high-density housing development in the area will compound upon that. Mrs.
Gold then expressed support for the 48 foot height limit and 2.25 parking stalls per unit.

Jonathan Guyman stated his concern with the traffic entering a development in this area. Mr.
Guyman also expressed concern with the current 2 parking stall standards.

Genelle Hale expressed her concern regarding the traffic in the area, as well as how residents
would be entering the proposed development.

Cindy Ogle stated her concern with how surrounding neighborhoods would be affected by a rise
in traffic within the NVD zone.

Jill Barrick stated the effects of a high density housing project do not belong in neighborhoods.

Will Frieden expressed he feels the proposed development would be too far set back from state
street to be 60 feet tall.

Carol Wilson expressed concern regarding traffic and parking issues that already exist in
surrounding areas and could get worse with a high density development.

Steve LeFranca stated he has worked in the area for around 25 years. He stated he has adjusted
his work hours just to avoid the current traffic in the area. He expressed his concern with how it
could get worse.

Kathrine Chritiansen stated her family owns five acres of land in the area. She then expressed her
concern with traffic in the area if a high-density development were to be built in the NVD zone.

Stephanie Visnaw stated a 60 foot tall high-density development would negatively impact the
traffic in the area. Mrs. Visnaw also expressed she would rather have a 60 foot commercial
building in the area instead of a 60 foot tall high-density development.

Lisa Hale stated no one in her neighborhood goes to Oak Canyon Junior High anymore because
1600 North is too dangerous. She expressed that a high-density housing development in the area
would make the traffic and safety problem much worse.

LaNae Millet stated that according to the state street master plan, the NVD zone is supposed to
be focused on residential housing and small scale development. Mrs. Millet expressed her
support for the 45 foot height maximum in the core.

Summer Davies expressed her concern with the traffic in the area, and stated a high-density
housing development in the area would make the current problem worse.

Sheran Mitchell expressed how she feels the traffic in the area is terrible and would get worse
with a high-density housing development in the area.

Wayne Barney expressed support and agreement with those who spoke before him. Mr. Barney
stated he feels this development is not really a state street development and falls more towards
400 west. Mr. Barney expressed his experiences and concern with bad traffic in the area.

Brett Seal expressed his support for lower height limits.

Lillian Simmons stated she lives in an area where high density housing was built, and she feels
traffic would not change very much if a high-density housing development were to be built in the
area.

DeAnn Jacobsen expressed her concern with the traffic and parking in the area.

Mayor Brunst closed the public hearing at 7:31 PM and brought the item back to the Council for
further discussion.

Mr. Kelly informed the council on traffic accidents at the 1600 N and State as well as the 1600 N
and 400 W intersections. Mr.Kelly stated there have been 500 accidents in the past 10 years at
these intersections.

Mr. Lambson expressed thanks to all those who came out to voice their concerns and opinions on
this project. Mr. Lambson stated he agrees with the state street master plan and the NVD zone
should stay small scale. Mr. Lambson stated he plans on voting in support of smaller height
limits.

Mrs. Lauret stated she will be voting in favor of shorter height limits.

Mr. Sumner expressed his understanding with both sides. He then stated that change is going to
happen, but because no commercial property owner has strongly spoken out, he will be voting in
favor of smaller scale developments.

Mr. Spencer expressed that he will vote in favor of lower height limits because 60 foot tall
developments are not small scale.

Mr. Macdonald expressed his support for smaller scale developments and lower height limits.

Mr. Peterson stated the Lindon Temple will also add more traffic to the area. He then expressed
his support for the citizens who came to voice their concerns tonight and stated he plans to vote
for lower height limits.

Mayor Brunst expressed his thanks to the citizens and staff for navigating this issue. He stated
the council tries their best to support the citizens by keeping high density housing out of
residential areas. He then stated Orem does need apartments and high density housing at the
same time safety and traffic are large concerns within the city. Mayor Brunst stated he would
rather see a 60 foot height maximum, but because of the overwhelming amount of opposition to
the project, he plans to vote for the proposed amendments.

Mr. Spencer moved to approve by Ordinance Amending portions of Section 22-24-5(F) of the
Orem City Code Pertaining to the overall height standards within the North Village District
(NVD) zones and Portions of section 22-24-5(H) pertaining to parking standards in the North
