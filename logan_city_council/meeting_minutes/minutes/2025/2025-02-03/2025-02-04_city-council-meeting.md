Logan Municipal Council                Logan, Utah                     February 4, 2025

Minutes of the meeting of the Logan Municipal Council convened in regular session on
Tuesday, February 4, 2025, in the Logan Municipal Council Chambers located at 290
North 100 West, Logan, Utah 84321 at 5:30 pm. Logan Municipal Council Meetings are
televised live as a public service on Channel 17 and the City of Logan YouTube channel
at: https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCFLPAOK5eawKS_RDBU0stRQ

Councilmembers present at the beginning of the meeting: Chair Jeannie F. Simmonds,
Vice Chair Mike Johnson, Councilmember Amy Z. Anderson, Councilmember Mark A.
Anderson and Councilmember Ernesto López. Administration present: Mayor Holly H.
Daines, City Attorney Craig Carlston, Finance Director Richard Anderson, and City

Chair Simmonds explained that any person wishing to comment on any item not
otherwise on the agenda may address the City Council at this point by stepping to the
microphone and giving his or her name and address for the record. Comments should
be limited to not more than three (3) minutes unless additional time is authorized by
the Council Chair. Citizen groups will be asked to appoint a spokesperson. This is the
time and place for any person who wishes to comment on non-agenda items and items
that are germane or relevant to the authority of the City Council. Items brought
forward to the attention of the City Council will be turned over to staff to respond to
outside of the City Council meeting.
Ethan Payne, a resident of Logan expressed concerns regarding intolerance in the
community. He stated that in the past two weeks, two friends of his from the LGBTQ+
community are considering moving due to direct harassment. He also said that Latino
students at Logan High School are specifically being harassed. He feels that this is not okay
and something must be done. What he expects is for elected officials to act, whether it is
via a public statement or public education.
Matthew Fatuesi, a resident of Cache Valley spoke in regard of alternative tax/bond
methods. He expressed concern regarding the negative impact that ICE will have on the
community and how neighborhoods interact.
Tom Jensen, a resident of Logan and former Logan City Councilmember, expressed his
gratitude to the Council and Mayor for all that they do. He feels safe in Logan City and
feels we have a great City. He referred to areas on 200 East (300 South and 700 North) that
need improvements for better traffic flow and access to 200 East. He also suggested that a
continual walkway in the downtown area West of the plaza would make a difference. He
said that progress is being made, but he would like to keep things going.
Mayor Daines interjected that there is currently a midblock walkway through the
downtown area. A new pedestrian crossing at 200 North midblock has been added and next
summer a pedestrian crosswalk with bulb outs will be added on 100 North.
Gail Hanson, a resident of Logan read a portion of the preamble of the Constitution of the
United States. She reminded the Council of the importance of promoting general welfare
in order to obtain domestic tranquility. She is grateful to the Council and community, who
are committed to the process.

PUBLIC HEARING – Public hearing with respect to (a) the issuance of not to
exceed $60,000,000 of water and sewer revenue bonds and (b) the potential
economic impact that the improvements to be financed with the proceeds of said
bonds will have on the private sector – Richard Anderson, Finance Director (35:35)

Finance Director Richard Anderson addressed the Council regarding the proposed bonds.
He said this is a public hearing and no action is required by the Council at this time
although they can provide direction. On January 7, 2025 the Council approved a
Parameters Resolution which establishes the parameters in which the City is authorized to
issue the bonds. What has been established is an amount not greater than $60,000,000, no
greater than 6% and no greater in length than 31 years. In the future the City will come
back to the Council with a budget to expend those bonds toward a specific project. At this
time the Council is not approving a project, they are not approving a route and not
selecting an option. We are simply fulfilling the required process to authorize the City at
some future date to issue bonds for a specific project. The reason this is being done now
is that part of the Parameters Resolution is something that also includes a Reimbursement
Resolution and with this resolution, the City would not be able to reimburse itself for
costs that have already been incurred up to this point.

Chair Simmonds opened the meeting to a public hearing.

Matthew Fatuesi, a resident of Cache Valley asked how much we aren’t able to currently
reimburse if from previous bonds that were issued.

Utah Municipal Code 10-2-419 governs the adjustment of common municipal
boundaries. Procedurally, (1) each municipal shall adopt a resolution indicating their
intent to adjust the common boundaries; (2) hold a hearing no less than 60 days after the
adoption of the resolution; (3) publish a public notice for three weeks in the newspaper
and on the Utah Public Notice Website; (4) hold a public hearing; (5) adopt an ordinance;
and (6) file with the County and the State. While each jurisdiction will hold their own
hearings, we are coordinating with Millville City on hearing dates and fulfilling
minimum noticing requirements. The proposed hearing date for this Resolution is
February 18, 2025 and the proposed workshop & hearing dates on an adoption Ordinance
are April 15, 2025 and May 6, 2025.
