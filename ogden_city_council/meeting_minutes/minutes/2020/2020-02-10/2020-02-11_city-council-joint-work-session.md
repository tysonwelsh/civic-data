Proposed Southeast Ogden Community Plan
          Council Deputy Director Symes explained Administration has proposed an update to the Southeast Ogden Community
Plan. The Plan covers the area generally south of 36th Street and east of Gramercy A venue. The Plan sets forth vision statements
for land use, housing, parks and open space, and transportation. The Plan prescribes vision strategies for topics and issues within
each of the vision statement areas. The Southeast Ogden Community Plan update would be the first major rewrite of the Plan for
the area since 1987.
          Deputy Planning Manager Spencer reported Planning staff has been working on this plan over the past year and he is
excited to present the draft document that represents the input from the Southeast Ogden Community and Planning Commission.
He made a presentation to orient the Council to the content of the Plan document. He presented an aerial image of Ogden City and
highlighted the area identified as the Southeast Ogden Community. It contains 2,157 acres, is equal to approximately 12 percent of
the City's total area, and has a population of9,674, which is 11 percent of the City's total population. He discussed the history of
the Plan dating back to 1987 when it was adopted and stated it has been adjusted over the years as properties have been developed
and zoning designations have changed. A 2017 petition to amend the Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) regulations in the
Southeast Ogden Community Plan area spurred this Plan update. A community plan is essentially a vision statement for future
development of the area to occur. It is tailored to meet the needs of a specific area and is a unique plan for the area that is also
incorporated in the City's General Plan. Issues identified throughout the plan review process relate to ADUs, student housing,
traffic, open space, and future land use considerations. Mr. Spencer briefly reviewed the accomplishments of the existing Plan,
including:

              Since the Plan's creation in 1987, there are 11.24 new miles ofroad, 119 new subdivisions, 941 new buildings (893 of
    those being homes), and a population increase of 3,182. The community involvement process for this Plan update included
    meetings with Weber State University (WSU) students in March 2018 to discuss student housing, launching the Southeast Ogden
    Community Plan website in 2018 (with 2,455 total views to date), and the first open house meeting held in January of2019. This
    meeting was advertised via 2,500 mailed notices and on social media; there were over 100 people in attendance. This meeting was
    followed by steering committee meetings to discuss land use, transportation, housing, and open space. An additional meeting was
    held with WSU to discuss the future of student housing to accommodate student population growth for the next five to 10 years.
    Finally, a second open house meeting was held September of2019 and over 60 residents were in attendance.

              Mr. Spencer stated the vision statement for the housing section of the proposed Plan is "preserving existing single-family
    neighborhoods, while providing additional housing options for the future growth anticipated in the Southeast Ogden Cmmnunity
    over the next 10-15 years". The Plan contemplates the difference between traditional rental properties and ADU s. In a traditional
    rental or duplex scenario, the first unit can contain a family unit and one related adult, and the second unit can contain the same or
    up to three unrelated adults. For an owner-occupied ADU, the first unit can contain a family unit with one related adult, but the
    ADU can only have the family unit. Educating the residents of the Southeast Ogden Community Plan area about the difference
    between rentals and ADUs was necessary in order to proceed with identifying the neighborhoods in the Plan area that could
    accommodate ADUs. He presented a map to identify the areas that have been deemed appropriate for ADUs. This included the
    northwest neighborhood, College Heights, Forest Green, and the southern area. Upon adoption of the Plan and approval of zoning
    ordinances, it will be possible to detennine the limitations on ADUs in each area. The Housing Steering Committee emphasized
    the importance of implementing better enforcement of ADUs in the Plan area. This would include analysis of the existing ADU
    ordinance to detennine whether more enforceable regulations could be implemented. Mr. Spencer noted that staff will create an
    online and searchable database of legal ADUs and provide residents with a reporting option when an ADU is being operated
    without a license. City staff would also record a certificate with Weber County for properties that have ADUs specifying those
    properties are single-family homes. The City cmTently has three approved AD Us and two others in the review process. There was
    discussion among the Council and Administration staff regarding the City's ability to enforce the current ADU ordinance.
              Mr. Spencer moved to student housing issues stating the Housing Steering Committee discussed the need to incmporate
    quality student housing and other high-density housing types in the cmmnunity without impacting single-family neighborhoods.
    They recommended providing for, and supporting, student housing options and other multi-family development along Harrison
    Boulevard and TOD adjacent to the BRT route. They wish to implement architectural controls for new multi-family development
    and require new developments to provide certain amenities on site that will enhance the residents' quality of life within their
    individual developments.

         The Plan identifies and creates 'pocket parks' in the community using waterwise design. College Park is identified as a
pocket park for which waterwise practices can be used. Improvements and monument signage are called for at this park. The Plan
also identifies the detention pond on Shadow Valley Drive as a pocket park. Improvements require funding, 0-1 zoning
designation, and monument signage. The Plan also cites the need to improve trailheads in the community. The City should work

         Mr. Spencer discussed the final strategy of the Plan dealing with transportation. The vision statement for this component
is "Southeast Ogden is a community where all people can enjoy transportation choices that fit their needs and an infrastructure
that supports safety, accessibility, and connected neighborhoods". The Plan calls for the integration of Bicycle Master Plan
objectives in the Southeast Ogden Community by improving prevalence ofbicycle parking infrastructure at commercial locations
and creating physically projected bike lanes along Harrison Boulevard. The City's Bicycle Master Plan could also be amended in
response to additions that are needed based upon physical conditions in the Southeast Ogden community. This could include
consideration of ways to implement bike lanes on downhill slopes of Shadow Valley Drive or other means to protect downhill
slopes, facilitating connectivity of a bike lane from Shadow Valley Elementary School to 4600 South, and pursuing creative
means of dealing with physical limitations on Country Hills Drive from Beus Pond north to provide bike protection on downhill
slope. The Plan also calls for improved traffic flow through the community and specifically in and out of the WSU campus.
Objectives related to this matter include encouraging WSU to create a connective road system to the Dee Event Center connecting
4600 South with Country Hills Drive as the BRT develops; exploring safe and legal routes as BRT develops to create a designated
route from Country Hills Drive to Village Drive on WSU campus behind commercial businesses in that area. Additionally, it is
necessary to address missing connections within neighborhoods to disperse traffic in the following areas:

          Mr. Spencer cited a list of "spot" issues throughout the Southeast Ogden Community to address such as speeding, on
street parking, traffic congestion, and pedestrian access and safety. Safe routes to school are a high priority in relation to
pedestrian safety and access and it is also important to uphold Americans with Disabilities Act standards throughout the
community. Transportation infrastructure must be maintained in good repair and one of the topics that was raised throughout
discussions of the transportation component of the Plan was the ability for residents to communicate transportation deficiencies to
the City. The suggestion was made to create a user-friendly reporting mechanism that could help the City to prioritize and address
maintenance issues. Finally, in relation to transportation, the Plan calls for integration of the BRT project in the community. This
includes implementation of TOD zoning around BRT stations, coordination with the Utah Transit Authority to notify residents
about bus changes, exploring the installation of protected bike lanes during the BRT construction phase, creating a park and ride
lot at the Dee Events Center, and creating first and last mile solutions.
          Mr. Spencer concluded by summarizing the remaining steps in the process for consideration of the Plan document. The
Planning Commission has provided a rec01mnendation to approve the Plan on a six to zero vote. The next action lies with the City
Council and once the Plan is adopted, staff can begin work to implement plan goals as early as this smmner. There was discussion
among the Council to solicit feedback regarding various components of the Plan or suggested edits. There was a focus on
appropriate regulations for ADUs and the locations in the Plan area where the land use may be appropriate. Council member Hyer
suggested the Council hold a joint session with the Planning Commission to continue deliberation of the component of the Plan
relating to ADUs. The Council discussed the idea. Council member Nadolski noted that he is comfortable with the current
recommendation and is pleased with the robust process undertaken by City staff to ensure the residents in the Southeast Ogden
Community Plan area could participate in the process. Other Council members expressed concern that the Plan's direction
regarding AD Us is not specific enough and it would be difficult to implement or enforce. The Council concluded to hold a joint
work session with the Planning Commission focused on the regulations relating to ADUs than the content.of the entire Plan
document.
