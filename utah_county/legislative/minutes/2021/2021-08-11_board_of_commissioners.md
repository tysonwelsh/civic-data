COMMISSIONER GARDNER - ANNOUNCED THE EMPLOYEE OF THE MONTH
FOR AUGUST 2021 - JENNIFER FLOYD (READ NOMINATION):

Jennifer has worked with the Health Department for ten years. She started as a WIC
Health Aide and has worked her way up to Senior WIC Health Technician with
responsibilities of WIC Outreach Coordinator. The Outreach tasks came about as she
saw a need and proposed a solution to help the WIC program grow and progress.

For the past year, Jennifer Floyd has developed a presence on Social Media for Utah
County WIC on Facebook and Instagram. Our followers on Facebook have increased
from less than 100 to 720. Under Jennifer's planning, we have increased our
Facebook post frequency. She has produced various videos to promote WIC, including
Facebook live videos, a video that showcases what clients can do with an extra $50
by using WIC services, and a white board drawing video in English and Spanish. She is
very creative and was the mastermind behind the WIC “Cereal Girl” music video
which had 7.4 thousand views, involved WIC staff, and promoted changes to the WIC
food package. She has a very strong presence on the camera and is excellent at
communicating the most important information to the public.

Jennifer has created new outreach materials that use more infographics than words
to help get our message out. She has created outreach materials for Pediatrician
offices to use as they write down a child’s measurement to give to mom. She created
a “What is WIC” presentation for medical offices for training their staff to be aware of
and refer to WIC. She has provided outreach materials to the Mom/Baby units in the
hospital to give in their welcome packets. With help from other staff, she has given
presentations at medical providers all over the county, including 38 medical offices,
three hospitals and 16 community resource programs last year.

Jennifer has helped us collaborate with many other community partners by organizing
and participating in a variety of local fairs and community events. A sampling includes
the 2019 South County Healthy Family Days (even procuring a fire engine from the
Mayor), Teen Health Summit, BYU International Student Orientation, Alpine School
District Mental Health Night, and the Centro Hispano Family Fun Day. If she does not
attend the events personally, which many times she does, she will prepare materials to
make sure staff have adequate resources. Jennifer is an active member of multiple
committees in the community which allows her to network with other programs and
build crucial relationships.

She is a talented trainer and presents on many topics in staff meeting from customer
service to required Civil Rights training, which she always makes more interesting and
personal. She inspires us to continue learning and progressing at work and in our
personal lives as well. She cares about our WIC staff, shares donuts and homemade jam
with them, and has a go-to attitude that helps foster growth.

Jennifer works well with the women, infants, and children we serve. An example of
this was the time Jennifer noticed a mom with a new baby and three older kids
having a hard time talking with the dietitian in the room. She gladly got permission
and took the older kids out of the room to pick out stickers, they all sat on a bench
together to read a story, and then checked out our sticker collection some more. The
mom received some advice and resources in a quiet room with no distractions.

She lives in Payson with her contractor husband and has four talented children.
Jennifer enjoys being in the outdoors camping, hiking and is always up for another
marathon race. Jennifer is a firecracker! She is dependable, responsible, and dedicated

31. DENY (OR APPROVE) AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND THE FOLLOWING SECTION
14.12.C.9 OF THE UTAH COUNTY LAND USE ORDINANCE; A REQUEST TO REMOVE THE
REQUIREMENT FOR PLANNED SUBDIVISIONS TO PROVIDE A CERTIFICATION THAT
LANDS BEING SUBDIVIDED ARE FREE FROM INVASIVE NOXIOUS WEEDS OR HAVE
UNDERGONE APPROPRIATE NOXIOUS WEED CONTROL TREATMENT.

-Greg Robinson, Community Development

GREG ROBINSON, COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT PLANNER - WE DID GO THROUGH
OUR PLANNING COMMISSION OF 7 MEMBERS AND IT WAS A 7/0 TO RECOMMEND
DENIAL OF THE APPLICATION. THE ORIGINAL INTENT, WHEN THIS ORDINANCE
WAS PUT IN PLACE IN 1996, THE AREA THAT MOST OF THE DEVELOPMENT IS
HAPPENING IN THE FARMLAND WAS 40-ACRE MINIMUMS FOR DWELLINGS. THAT
WAS CHANGED TO A 5-ACRE MINIMUM AND THEN THIS STATUTE WAS PUT IN
PLACE TO PROVIDE ASSURANCES TO ADJACENT FARMLAND TO PREVENT THE
SPREAD OF NOXIOUS WEEDS. SINCE THAT TIME, THERE HAVE BEEN A COUPLE OF
CHANGES TO THAT AS IDENTIFIED IN THE STAFF REPORT. THE BIGGEST THING
TODAY IS THAT THIS DOES GIVE US AN OPPORTUNITY TO LOOK AT THE
PROPERTY AT THE TIME THAT THIS CHANGES FROM MAINLY AGRICULTURAL
USE TO MORE RESIDENTIAL USE AND ALSO AT THE TIME OF THE INSPECTION, IT
GIVES US AN OPPORTUNITY TO EDUCATE THOSE PEOPLE THAT ARE TAKING
OVER THE PROPERTY ABOUT THE NOXIOUS WEEDS THAT MAY BE PRESENT AND
HOW TO MITIGATE THOSE RESPONSIBLY.

COMMISSIONER GARDNER - RIGHT NOW DOES IT SAYS THAT IS HAS TO BE
COMPLETELY FREE OF ALL OBNOXIOUS WEEDS?

GREG ROBINSON - IT DOES NOT. IT’S JUST A NOXIOUS WEIGHT CERTIFICATION.
INITIALLY WHEN THIS WAS INSTITUTED IT WAS DONE THROUGH PRIVATE
AGENCIES THAT WERE APPROVED BY THE WEED BOARD TO GO OUT AND DO
THESE INSPECTIONS BECAUSE THERE WEREN’T QUALIFIED OR CERTIFIED
INSPECTORS. THE COUNTY HAS NOW TAKEN OVER THE WEED CONTROL IN
PUBLIC WORKS AND ARE DOING THOSE INSPECTIONS. THEY HAVE A STANDARD
AND THERE ARE CERTAIN WEEDS THAT IF THERE ARE ANY PRESENT THEN THEY
WILL FAIL BUT THOSE ARE CLASSIFICATIONS THAT THE STATE IS VERY
CONCERNED ABOUT. THERE ARE CERTAIN CLASSIFICATIONS OR CERTAIN TYPES
OF WEEDS THAT IF THERE ARE SOME PRESENTS THE INSPECTOR WILL EDUCATE
THEM AND ASK THEM TO TRY AND MITIGATE THOSE BUT STILL WILL PASS THAT
INSPECTION.

COMMISSIONER GARDNER - MY ISSUE WITH THE CODE AS IT’S WRITTEN
CURRENTLY IS WITH THOSE REQUIREMENTS OF 100%. I HAVE A REAL PROBLEM
WITH WE AS A GOVERNMENT ENTITY REQUIRING PEOPLE TO USE CHEMICALS
