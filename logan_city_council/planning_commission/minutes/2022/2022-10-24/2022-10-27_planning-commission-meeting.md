PC 22-058 The Watermark [Zone Change & Design Review Permit] Joshua Low/Watermark Project
LLC, authorized agent/owner are requesting a zone change of 11.13 acres from Commercial (COM)
to Mixed Residential High (MR-30) to construct 28 townhomes and 204 multi-family condominiums
for a total of 232 residential units located at approximately 1262 West 2200 South in the Commercial
(COM) zone; TIN 03-007-0010; -0011; -0023 (Woodruff Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Senior Planner, reviewed the request for a Rezone of approximately 11.13-
acres (three parcels) from Commercial (COM) to Mixed Residential High (MR-30), and a Design
Review Permit to develop a new multi-family housing project containing 232 units. The Design
Review Permit is contingent on the outcome of the zone change.

R. Holley reviewed the surrounding zones and housing types. Nibley City borders this project to the
south and east. Thomas Edison Charter school is located directly to the south in Nibley City.

The property is identified on the Future Land Use Plan (FLUP) as COM and considering its proximity
to the highway, commercial land use could be considered a viable long-term solution. The South
Highway Overlay anticipated this area to be developed commercial with larger setbacks and in a
nodal manner associated with the new 1000 West traffic light. There will only be two additional traffic
lights installed along the highway, one near 3200 south and one possibly near 2600 south. The FLUP


has already identified numerous acres in this area as MR that are currently in the process of being
developed into multi-family and townhome developments. Commercial use would be ideal for this
site, whereas living near a high-speed highway is not ideal (noise, pollution, and safety). The rezone
request is not consistent with the FLUP and has not been anticipated by both the surrounding Logan
and Nibley neighborhoods. Staff is recommending denial of the zone change.

There have been 85 comments received on this project. The main concern addressed in all of the
comments was the nearby elementary school and the safety of the children who attend.

Commissioner Croshaw asked what the density difference is between COM and MR-30 zones. R.
Holley stated the maximum height is lower in the COM zone, but the density is the same.

Commissioner Doutre asked if utilities are available for this property. R. Holley said that utilities are
available water is available across the highway and sewer is available to the north and the west.

PROPONENT: Josh Low talked about his client and an alternate plan they will be proposing, that
includes commercial space if the current proposal does not get approved.

PUBLIC: Wayne McKay spoke about his concern for the safety of the children at the neighboring
elementary school. With a four-story apartment building next to the school, there is no way to know
who is living in these units that can watch over the schoolyard. He also spoke about issues with the
traffic getting onto 1000 West.

Alyssha Jenson spoke about the noticing for this project, the safety of her children that attend the

J. Bingham said that there is no room for any more students at Ridgeline or Logan High School.

Brent Speth owns storage sheds across 1000 West. Spoke about how the area next to the highway
should remain commercial and residential should be back off the highway.

Frank Scheabel a retired geologist spoke about the wetlands in this area, the high water table in this
area, and where all the water is going to go if the area is developed.

Mark Oakey talked about the open space proposition on the ballets and how this is the exact
opposite, and the animals that live in the open spaces.

Stephanie Winkler talked about the school being a commuter school that already brings in additional
traffic. The children do not need four-story buildings right next to where they play outside

Dr. Christopher L. spoke about this project changing the culture, nature, and environment of the
valley negatively.

Holly Weston said that the public notice should be mailed to a larger area than 300 feet around the
project property. She read a portion of the comment she submitted.


Luke Dickey asked if there were any plans to widen the roads.

Mickey Duke spoke about how there is already too much density in this area and is amazed that this
project would even be considered.

Jordan Schvaneveldt spoke about the school having enough challenges as is and there should not
be any added.

Tyler Cady spoke about the traffic concerns, stormwater, and the water table.

COMMISSION: Lucero asked Russ Holley to address the public about the public noticing. R. Holley
stated that Utah’s notification requirements were followed for this project. Public Notices were mailed
to all property owners that are within a 300-foot radius of the property. A notice was published in the
Herald Journal on October 10th and 13th. Notice was posted on the City’s website as well as the
Utah State Public Notice site on October 17th. There is also a notice posted on the City’s Facebook
Page.

Commissioner Lucero asked Russ to address the comments made regarding the stormwater and
the roads. R. Holley said that projects are submitted for review at about 30% complete. A lot of the
technical engineering is completed during the building permit phase. All building plans submitted for
review have to meet state and local code requirements to be issued a building permit. A portion of
the frontage street (center of the road to the project site) is required to be developed along with the
property.

Commissioner Peterson said he does not see a need for the rezone. Commissioner Guth agreed
and asked what the neighboring zones are in Nibley and that it would be nice to have a transition
between the neighboring properties and this development. R. Holley said it is a mixture of single-
family homes to the southeast, townhomes to the south, just past the school, and some commercial
and warehouses to the west and southwest.

The Commissioners agreed that this is a nice project that offers different housing types, this is just
not the right location.

PC 22-061 Fairway Lofts [Zone Change, Design Review, Conditional Use Permit] Tony
Johnson/Alliance Acquisitions LLC are requesting a Zone Change of a 0.26-acre parcel from Mixed
Residential Low (MR-12) to Neighborhood Center (NC) and a Design Review and Conditional Use
Permit to construct approximately 1,000 SF of commercial space, one residential unit, and one short
term rental, located at 391 South 400 West in the Mixed Residential (MR-12) zone; TIN 02-038-0001
(Woodruff Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Senior Planner reviewed the request for a Zone Change, Design Review, and
Conditional Use Permit to remodel a vacant corner store on the property into an operational
commercial service building, remodel the existing home and add a second residential unit. The first
step in this project is the zone change from Mixed Residential Low (MR-12) to Neighborhood Center
(NC). The NC zone is the smallest scale commercial zone in the city, similar to the Island Market
and Herms Inn, it is designed to be neighborhood and pedestrian-oriented, where the commercial
space is less than 3,000 square feet. This is a good location as there are not any NC zones on the
west side of town. Where This is a historic corner store, Fairway Market, built in 1951, and the west
side of town is underserved, staff is recommending approval of the zone change.

The proposed site plan includes renovating the corner store into a drive-through, a new residential
tiny home, and the renovation of the existing Victorian home for short-term rental use. Staff’s
concern is with the drive-through and it not being appropriate to the character of the neighborhood.
there are also six driveways being proposed, the Land Development Code (LDC) allows two
driveways. Staff is recommending denial of the drive-through and a redesign submitted for two
driveways.

Commissioner Doutre asked if there is any other zone that would allow this development. R. Holley
said that the Commercial Center (CC) zone allows for a cluster of commercial uses and doesn’t put
a cap on the building size. The NC zone is the least intensive zone that would allow for what the
applicant is proposing.

PROPONENT: Tony Johnson, the developer on this project said their main focus is on the
preservation of existing buildings. With this project, they are trying to make sure the corner
commercial space is a viable location. Most of the businesses they have spoken to about using the
space have requested the drive-through. Tony gave several operating options they could incorporate
to keep the drive-through. He also suggested that they try the drive-through for a year and then
revisit to see if there are any issues and/or neighborhood complaints.

PUBLIC: Jeannie Simmonds asked if residential space is included in the total square footage
allowed in the NC zone. R. Holley said that only the commercial space is limited to 3,000 square
feet. The number of residential units is based on the size of the lot, which in this case is .25 acre,
they are allowed two residential units.


COMMISSION: Commissioner Doutre said this project makes sense, this is a great area for the
proposed zone and use. The proponent has done a great job of cleaning up this property.

Commissioner Lucero asked if the drive-through concern is a neighborhood compatibility issue, or is
it a restriction in the LDC. R. Holley said that it is a neighborhood compatibility issue.

Commissioner Doutre asked how the six driveways are determined and asked if the proposed drive-
through entrance, garage entrance, and parking entrance on the east side should be considered one
driveway cut.

Darren Farar, Logan City Engineer stated that Logan City allows one driveway per lot, or per 300
feet of frontage. There is approximately 120 feet of frontage total for this development, so they are
allowed one driveway. The Public Works director can approve a second driveway. After looking at
this site it was determined that it is a good location for a second driveway. Additional driveways
would cause issues with the intersection, especially if there is a drive-through.

Commissioner Croshaw asked what the driveway cuts can be for this location. R. Holley said that
driveway cuts typically mirror the adjacent street, this project would be for a two-lane, 24-foot wide
access. The parking lots need to have at least a 24-foot wide driveway cut to allow for cars to enter
and exit at the same time.

Commissioner Guth stated that a drive-through is not appropriate in a residential area it would create
a real safety concern. R. Holley stated that a drive-through is not prohibited in the LDC for the NC
zone, but staff does feel that it is not compatible with the neighborhood zones, and this will be
addressed in a future code amendment.

There was a discussion on the commission needing to see a redesign of the drive-through and
driveways. Commissioner Peterson stated that these concerns are covered in the conditions, so
conditional approval would be sufficient. The Design Review Permit is contingent upon the City
Council approving the rezone request.

PC 22-057 The Logan Silos [Design Review Permit] Tim Rodebush/Logan Silos LLC, authorized
agent/owner are requesting a Design Review Permit to repurpose the existing site and structures.
Phase one includes outdoor event space and two, two-story mixed-use buildings that will contain
approximately 7,000 square feet of commercial space on the main floors and residential units on the
2nd floors located at 280 West 300 South in the Mixed Use (MU) zone; TIN 02-050-0012; -0007
(Woodruff Neighborhood).

STAFF: Tanya Rice, City Planner reviewed the request for a Design Review Permit to repurpose the
structures at the vacant IFA Mill site, approximately 2 acres located at 280 West 300 South. Phase
one includes the repurposing of the two existing one-story buildings. The ‘Farmhouse’ (A on the site
plan) on the west side of the property will be remodeled to have two floors with commercial use on
the main floor and two residential units on the 2nd floor. The ‘Barn’ (E on the site plan) on the east
side of the property will be remodeled to have two floors with commercial use on the main floor and
six residential units on the 2nd floor. There are also two existing and two proposed train cars (T) that
will be repurposed for a mix of commercial and residential use which has not been designated yet.
Outdoor event space (G on the site plan) is being proposed surrounding the buildings and parking
areas.

Using the max density allowed for this side, 44 parking stalls are required and 52 are proposed. The
site plan conforms with frontage and access, the existing structures do not meet the current setbacks
but are considered to be grandfathered structures as long as the footprints of the buildings are not
expanded with the development. Any new structures will have to follow current setbacks.

The proposed site layout does not show pedestrian circulation and needs further development to
show pedestrian connections throughout the development site, connection to future phases of
development and pedestrian access points to 300
South and the city sidewalk. Bike parking stalls will
need to be added to the site plan as well.

The project site does not have existing landscaping,
nor was there a landscape plan provided with the
proposal. The site plan identifies ‘Outdoor Event
Space’ (G on the site plan) which is shown in a drive
aisle and parking area, and as such, does not count
towards meeting the minimum open space
requirements. Staff is recommending that an overall
performance landscape plan is submitted prior to


the first building permit specifically identifying open space, landscape plantings, usable outdoor
areas, and public gathering areas.

The north and east facades of the single-story portion of the Farmhouse are visible from 300 South.
These façades show about 10% transparency. As this is intended for retail space, staff recommends
that the ground floor frontage transparency meet the 50% minimum requirement for the north
elevation and 20% on the east.

There is little variation in color or materials throughout the exterior of the Barn building except for the
floor-to-ceiling windows covering the former open bays. Additional windows are added on the north
elevation at about 25% transparency. As this is intended for commercial uses and the north side of
the Barn is proposed as outdoor event space, staff recommends that the ground floor frontage
transparency meet the 50% minimum requirement. The east-facing ground level is proposed as a
100-foot solid wall that can be seen from 300 South. Staff recommends an additional 20%
fenestration as required for exposed side elevations and to break up the 100-foot wall.

PROPONENT: Tim Rodebush said that all parking stalls could be located in the empty lot (phase 2)
to the east of the proposed site so the open space could be located where the parking is currently
proposed. He is still waiting on engineering to determine if they can build on top of the existing
structures.

PUBLIC: Jeanie Simmonds asked about the proposed barn being a fertilizer shed and if there are
any environmental concerns. Asked if you can legally expand a non-conforming building and have
it maintain its legal non-conforming status. Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director said
that a non-conforming structure can grow inward or upward without increasing the degree of non-
conformity and therefore remain a legally existing non-conforming structure.

COMMISSION: Lucero said she is excited about this project and it’s nice to see this historic site be
repurposed.

The commission discussed continuing the project to see a pedestrian pathway plan.
