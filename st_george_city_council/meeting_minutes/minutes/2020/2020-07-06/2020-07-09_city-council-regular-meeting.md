OPENING:
     Mayor Pike called the meeting to order and welcomed all in attendance. The
     invocation was offered by Luigi Persichetti with the Unity Church and the Pledge of

COMMENTS FROM THE PUBLIC:
    Resident Duane Monette addressed the Council regarding the Divario project using
    Sunbrook Drive. Nearby residents were not aware that the area was going to be
    developed. He asked the Council to install a stoplight in the area and for the second
    access road to be installed.

      Public Works Director Cameron Cutler explained Sunbrook Drive does not warrant a
      traffic signal; if including the homes that are going to be built, it will. A traffic signal
      can be installed based on projected number. He plans to start the design sooner
      rather than later.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard stated Sunbrook and Divario were approved at about
      the same time; Sunbrook Drive is a public street. It is not anywhere near capacity
      on traffic. He noted a traffic signal will help tremendously; this is one that can be
      moved up the list. The consensus of the Council is to look at a signal in the near
      future.

      Mr. Cutler explained the developer was approved to build phases 6-12 prior to
      building the secondary access.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman added the Divario development does not go all the way
      to the south; there is some unincorporated land in between Dixie Drive and Divario’s
      southern corridor that Divario will not be building. Staff is negotiations with those
      landowners to dedicate the street during their annexation into the City.

      Resident Bob Routsong commented he is concerned with increased traffic in the
      area. He asked is there is a possibility of an additional access road.

       Community Development Director John Willis outlined the number of houses that can

       City Attorney Shawn Guzman noted the developer contributed to the completion of

       Resident Sam Schmutz inquired about the irrigation water. His family has a garden
       on his lot and used and counted on this ditch water for a long time. He believes his
       rights are being taken from him and hopes for a meeting at some point to discuss
       additional options as what the City offered is not enough.

       Resident John Miles stated he does not the City “poison” culinary water, he wants to
       keep his east city springs water or there will be a lawsuit.

       Doug Schmutz believes the City does not have the right to take the water from
       them. He believes they own the water rights and thinks it is worthwhile to continue
       with the water so they can distribute their crops to those who need it.

       Resident Connie Childs spoke about how City Councils can help preserve history.
       She asked that the Council to keep the irrigation water.

       Resident Sam McArthur stated they have had irrigation for quite some time. There
       are a lot of people who do not use it. He noted that he does not think this is the
       proper place for a full discussion and commented that he does not think taking
       irrigation away from citizens is right.

       Mayor Pike stated there may need to be further discussion.

       Councilmember Hughes noted that Water Services Director Scott Taylor is not wrong
       with what was presented to the Council a few months back. Some would say the
       City waste a lot of water on City facilities. This irrigation system is historical; he
       understands the passion. He asked for this to be discussed further.

       City Attorney Shawn Guzman stated the irrigation users will have the water until
       everything is worked out.

       Mayor Pike noted recently the Council state they encourage residents to have
       gardens.

ZONE CHANGE/ORDINANCE:
     Consider approval of an ordinance changing the zone from A-1 (Agriculture
     40,000 sq. ft. minimum lot size) to RE-12.5 (Residential Estate 12,500 sq. ft.
     minimum lot size) on approximately 21.04 acres for residential development
     generally located at approximately 2400 South Crimson Cliffs Way.

       Planner Dan Boles presented the request to change the zone from A-1 to RE-12.5 on
       approximately 21.04 acres generally located at 2400 South Crimson Cliffs Way,
       south of new high school near the Washington City limits residential subdivisions.
       The applicant requested RE-12.5, staff recommends RE-37.5, and the Planning
       Commission recommended RE-20 due to concerns of location by agricultural zones.
       He provided a PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics: zoning map
       and zone change exhibit.

       Applicant Dave Weller commented he understands the goal is to have farmland in
       this area. They are trying to build homes in this area. He noted the land east of the
       project is R-1-10 (in Washington City) so they thought the RE-12.5 was a good fit.
       Since the previous City Council meeting, they have amended their proposal to zone
       the horizontal piece as RE-37.5 and the vertical piece as RE-20. He explained
       feathering is what you use when deciding on zoning.

       Councilmember Hughes noted he is not against the line of reasoning and stated that
       this is the last area in Little Valley that created the “critical mass”. He commented
       this is difficult for him and he is not opposed to some feathering with half acre lots;
       however, RE-37.5 is what was agreed upon.
