5. PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on the Draft

Integrated Water and Land Use Element of the General Plan as Required by SB-110

(2022) and SB-76 (2023), City of Bluffdale, Applicant. Staff Presenters, Caitlyn Tubs

and Dan Tracer.
Planning Manager, Caitlyn Tubbs, reported that the draft document was revised in response to
feedback received at the November City Council and Planning Commission Work Sessions, as well
as comments from the Utah Division of Water Rights (“UDWR’”).
Ms. Tubbs reported that Bluffdale is experiencing robust growth. Although less than 9% of City land
remains developable, the population is expected to double by 2050. In creating the Integrated Water
and Land Use Element, Staff reviewed anticipated development patterns identified in the existing
Water Conservation and Land Use Plans, as well as their associated water demands over the coming
years. Most new development will occur in existing and planned urban and city cities along Redwood
Road, Porter Rockwell Boulevard, and east of the railroad tracks.
Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer, reviewed the Water System portion of the document and
indicated that few changes have been made since the last review. The UDWR requested clarification
regarding water projections. Staff met with the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District
("JVWCD"), which indicated that they have secured sources to supply Bluffdale will water to full
build-out capacity. Any substantial changes to development projections would require renegotiation
to ensure that the JYWCD has adequate capacity. Otherwise, the developer would be required to
secure water rights and sources. Preliminary plans for the Geneva parcel look similar to The Point
with a mixture of high-density residential, commercial, and industrial uses but that project is not
anticipated to begin for 20 to 40 years.
Ms. Tubbs reported that the contract between Bluffdale and the JVUWCD is reviewed every three
years. The City is allowed to use up to 120% of its contracted volume at the same price, and usage
above that percentage is billed at double the contracted rate. The JVWCD did not anticipate running

that Bluffdale exceeds the State goal of 176 gallons per capita per day, with an average use of 168
gallons. Based on that number, the City Council determined that the City’s current strategy is

ADMINISTRATIVE ITEMS
6. PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND VOTE on the Bridgesource Pre-Cast

Concrete Plant Site Plan, located at approximately 16115 South Pony Express Road in

the SG-1 Sand and Gravel Zone. Applicant, Colby Anderson — CIR Engineering. Staff

Presenter, Ellen Oakman.
Chair Cragun reported that the public notice had an error for this item as administrative items do not
require a public hearing. No members of the public were present to speak to the item.
Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman, presented the Staff Report and reported that the Site Plan was
for an 88,980 square foot pre-cast concrete plant on 7.4 acres of the 130-acre parcel. Site Plans greater
than 15,000 square feet in size require Planning Commission approval. The pre-cast concrete plant
will produce critical infrastructure for the Salt Lake Valley and beyond. The property is in the sand
and Gravel (“SG-1”) Zone east of the Jordan River, south of Porter Rockwell Boulevard and the
historical gravel pit area, and west of Pony Express Road.
The Site Plan, elevations, and renderings were reviewed. The project will include 48 parking stalls.
The site requires 24 total stalls, including 15 for the business office space and nine for the industrial
manufacturing use.

Chair Cragun stated that the City had spent a lot of time creating design standards and had tried to be
consistent in ensuring that applicants adhere to them. She asked if the standards should be updated
to exempt the SG-1 Zone. She agreed that it is unique, and many of the reasons the standards were
crafted are not applicable.
Commissioner Flynn remarked that he was comfortable with the deviations, especially the
landscaping deviation as that would help conserve water. He agreed with Chair Cragun that the
standards would need to be changed if there were more sand and gravel areas left in Bluffdale but
noted that this property was the only SG-1 Zone in the City.

Commissioner Swanson asked how the application compared to one received a few years previously
from Suncore Construction and Materials, Inc. Community and Economic Development Director,
Grant Crowell reported that modifications were required because that type of structure does not fit
well with the standards. Staff had discussed how development within the gravel pit could fit with the
design guidelines, which were catalyzed by projects in the General Commercial (“GC-1”) -Zone. The
UDC was the largest Site Plan approved by the Commission, and it was granted a deviation to use
metal panels. Chair Cragun noted that the UDC had many street-facing elements.
Mr. Crowell stated that a text amendment was a potential way to address the deviations if the Planning
Commission could not approve them based on current ordinance. There are still active gravel pits in
the City from Porter Rockwell Boulevard south to Lehi, and a small area between Holladay Oil and
Bluffdale Heights is also zoned SG-1.
Commissioner Woodruff expressed concern that a future developer could use the project as
justification and recommended that the decision to grant deviations be based on the subject property’s
location in the SG-1 Zone. The pre-cast concrete plant has a functional need to reduce the amount of
glazing, but another developer could wish to do so based on cost savings alone. Chair Cragun stated
that she suggested modifying the ordinance due to similar concerns. Mr. Crowell pointed out that
some properties in the SG-1 Zone are very close to residential neighborhoods.
Chair Cragun stated that Commissioner Woodruff raised a good point regarding setting a precedent.
If the deviations were approved based on the subject property being located in the SG-1 Zone, it could
set a precedent for the street-facing properties in the zone. Commissioner Woodruff stated that the
argument could also be made for a property in a different zone that is not street-facing or has other
similarities. Mr. Crowell stated that it is important to include findings in any decision to grant
deviations, and those findings can be very specific.
Jonathan Beisinger spoke on behalf of the Applicant and indicated that he also managed the design
and construction of the Suncore block plant. That project required a zone change to GC-1 because it
has a retail component, but the pre-cast plant will not be open to the public. The plant will create pre-
cast concrete structures such as vaults, manhole risers, and reinforced concrete pipes that are delivered

to seven years in the future.
Commissioner Flynn asked about operating hours. Mr. Beisinger reported that the offices will be
open during normal business hours, but manufacturing hours would likely be extended. Regarding
dust impacts, Clyde Companies has an environmental department that manages those aspects of all
its properties to ensure compliance with all state and local requirements. The dirt road into the
property is maintained and watered by the neighboring Olympus Precast plant.
In response to a question from Commissioner Luker, Mr. Beisinger stated that inasmuch as the facility
will be part of an operational sand and gravel pit, a reclamation plan is required. The areas of
hydroseeding will no longer be mined, but the slopes to the north and east of the Site Plan will
continue to be mined by Geneva Rock. One mining operations are completed, the area will be
reclaimed with a more gradual slope. Aggregates will be sourced from the Geneva Rock pit, which
has a 30-year plan for continued operation.
Mr. Tracer reported that the traffic impact study estimates a total of 216 daily trips to the facility.
Accelleration and deceleration lanes will be added to Pony Express Road to accommodate ingress
and egress to the facility. In response to a question raised by Commissioner Swanson, Mr. Tracer
stated that the Utah Department of Transportation ("UDOT") had indicated that they might convert
the road to one-way traffic on either side of the interstate, but the project was not planned or funded.
