**Ordinance: Camping or Obstructing Public Property and Park Rules Text Amendment**
The Council will accept public comment and consider adopting an ordinance that would amend various sections of the *Salt Lake City Code* relating to camping on public grounds, streets, parks, and playgrounds, park hours, disposing of unclaimed property, and obstructing sidewalks. The proposal would introduce an updated definition of what constitutes camping and consolidate and clarify the use and obstruction of public spaces and rights-of-way to reduce confusion and ensure proper enforcement.

For more information visit <https://link.slc.gov/Camping>.

**FYI – Project Timeline:** (subject to change per Chair direction or Council discussion)
Briefing - Tuesday, April 14, 2026
Set Public Hearing Date - Tuesday, April 21, 2026
Hold hearing to accept public comment - Tuesday, May 5, 2026 at 7 p.m.
TENTATIVE Council Action - Tuesday, May 19, 2026

  Staff Recommendation - Refer to motion sheet(s).

**Summary:**

Michael Sanders provided an introduction to the proposed camping ordinance update.

**Public Comments:**

**Greg Kelly** was called to speak but was not present.

**Theresa Ashman** spoke in support of the ordinance and described public health, safety and enforcement issues regarding camping in their Liberty Wells neighborhood.

**Wren Williamson** opposed the ordinance and stated the proposal criminalized individuals experiencing hardship and homelessness.

**Roy Davies** was called to speak but was not present.

**Quintin Noland** spoke in support of the ordinance, citing concerns about safety in Salt Lake City parks compared to parks in Chicago and urging approval to improve public safety and park accessibility.

**James Gallyer** opposed the ordinance and stated it would criminalize homelessness rather than address housing affordability and shelter access.

**Stuart Wheelwright** supported the ordinance and cited concerns regarding encampments near residential properties, including blocked access, drug activity, public safety impacts, and limited enforcement response from the City.

**Jaide Whitlock** opposed the ordinance and stated individuals experiencing homelessness needed additional resources and support rather than criminalization.

**John Gardiner** stated ordinance updates were needed to address illegal camping, RV occupancy, and public safety concerns in neighborhoods and parks.

**Emily A Sloan-Pace** opposed the ordinance and questioned whether enforcement would be applied consistently across public gatherings and unhoused encampments.

**Trevor Corey** statied the ordinance would criminalize homelessness and spoke of concerns regarding barriers to shelter access and of vehicle habitation as a means of survival for unhoused individuals.

**Shaylee Robinson** cited their family member’s struggle with homelessness and asked for understanding and grace for people struggling with addiction and homelessness.

**Bernie Hart** opposed the ordinance, stating homelessness had continued to worsen despite years of discussion and increased police funding, and argued the proposal would not address underlying issues or create long-term solutions.

**Amy Hawkins** supported portions of the ordinance restricting camping near schools, shelters, and senior centers, citing past encampments, drug activity, and public safety concerns near these community spaces.

**Natalie Clark**opposed the ordinance and shared lived experience with homelessness, stating many youth, particularly those aging out of foster care, rely on vehicles for shelter.

**Michael Croxford** opposed the ordinance, referenced legal and policy challenges in other cities with similar ordinances, and stated City resources would be better used on existing enforcement tools and supportive services.

The Council will accept public comment and consider adopting an ordinance that would amend the zoning of the properties at approximately 346, 350, and 354 South 800 East from RMF-35 (Moderate Density Residential Multi-Family) to MU-5 (Mixed-Use 5). The proposal would also amend the Central Community Plan Future Land Use Map of the properties at approximately 346, 350, 354, and 370 South 800 East and 775 East 400 South from Medium Density Residential (15-30 dwelling units/acre) and Medium Density Transit Oriented Development (10-50 dwelling units/acre) to High Density Transit Oriented Development (50 or more dwelling units/acre).
