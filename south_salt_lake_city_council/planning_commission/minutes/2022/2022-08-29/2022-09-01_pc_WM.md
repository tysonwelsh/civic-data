NEW BUSINESS

1.     Public Meeting – C-21-001 – A Conditional Use Permit Application for a Public Utility
       Facility, which will include the Installation of Piping and Construction and Operation
       of a Regulator System at 919 West 2100 South.

Action Item

Applicant:     Dominion Energy
Address:       919 West 2100 South




                                                T
City Planner, Hanna Tuia, reported that the above item is a request for a Conditional Use Permit
from Dominion Energy on property located at 919 West 2100 South in the Flex Land Use District.
The proposal includes the installation of new gas piping as well as building and operating a regulator
station on the site. The regulator station will largely be accessed for maintenance purposes
approximately one time per month. The building would be approximately 400 square feet in size
              AF
and 10 feet tall. Ms. Tuia shared the existing conditions with the Commission. There used to be a
restaurant on the site but the building had since been demolished. The lot was currently vacant.

The proposed Site Plan was shared. Ms. Tuia noted that it showed landscaping around the border
on 900 West and 2100 South. It was also possible to see the piping and where the proposed building
will be located. The applicant was in the process of designing the building to meet South Salt Lake
City standards. One of the Conditions of Approval would be for the applicant to submit compliant
plans. Staff recommended approval of the Conditional Use Permit based on the information
submitted by the applicant, the General Plan considerations, and the Ordinance analysis. The
Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of Approval were read aloud. The Planning
Commission had the option to approve, deny, or table the decision to a date certain.
R
Commissioner Pechmann noted that the second paragraph on the first page referenced a sewer
easement that needs to be vacated prior to construction. He wondered why that was not included in
the Conditions of Approval. Ms. Tuia explained that she had spoken to the City Engineer and he
indicated that the sewer easement had already been vacated. She shared information about the Knox
box and reported that it was for Fire Department access. The Fire Department will be given a key
so it was possible to open the fence, access the area, and address emergency concerns.
D

Chair Vernon asked the applicant to clarify what a regulator station does. JD Kesler identified
himself as an attorney for Dominion Energy from the law firm of Parsons Behle & Latimer at 201
South Main Street, #1800, in Salt Lake City. Mr. Kesler explained that a regulator station takes gas
from a high-pressure gas line and runs it through a valve that puts the gas out at a lower pressure.
Inside the regulator station, a pipe comes out of the ground, a valve, and another pipe goes into the
ground. One side will have higher pressure with lower pressure on the opposite side. The lower
pressure line goes out through the system in smaller lines.

Commissioner Pechmann wondered if it was the same in principle as an electric transformer, which
goes from high to low voltage. This was confirmed by Mr. Kesler. He explained that there are

regulator stations throughout the community already. Since there are more modern pipelines being
installed throughout Salt Lake County, regulator stations continue to be updated.

Brady Nowers from Dominion Energy introduced himself. He referenced an existing regulator
station that is located approximately 500 feet from the site. The intent was for the new regulator
station to eventually replace the existing regulator station.

Commissioner Dahlberg asked what will surround the regulator station building. Mr. Kesler
identified the building location on the Site Plan. There will be a concrete pad outside of that and the
rest will be a gravel area. There would be adequate room for a truck to pull in, turn around, and back
out. There will be no paved parking on the site. Chair Vernon was glad that it was a pervious surface
