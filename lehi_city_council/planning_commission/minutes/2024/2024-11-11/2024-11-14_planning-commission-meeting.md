3.3) Public Hearing and Recommendation of AWA’s Request for Review of a Development
Code Amendment to Chapter 37, Adding an Exception to the Connectivity Standards for
Vehicular Safety.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. The amendment would allow an exception to the connectivity
standards between residential and non-residential zones if it creates an incompatible traffic
pattern, such as a loading dock or a drive aisle. The amendment would still require bike and
pedestrian connections.

Commissioner Lockhart asked if the applicant had any other options. Kim Struthers said they
would need to find a different site, as there is currently no way to apply for an exception. Jacob
Curtis clarified that if this amendment is made, the applicant will still need to apply for the
exception. Kim said that there will likely be similar situations that would use the amendment in
the future. He also clarified for Commissioners Lockhart and Everett that DRC wants to keep the
amendment as restricted as possible to ensure as many connections as possible.

Rick Magnus was present on behalf of the applicant, AWA Engineering. He stated that the goal
of proposing the amendment was to allow a grocery store to be built on a specific lot. The
company was unable to find a way to situate the store differently on the lot so that it complied
with the current code.

Commissioner Eyre brought up an example of a similar situation in Pleasant Grove. He asked if
any exceptions could be made for grading purposes. Kim explained that the grading of the lot
isn’t steep enough to qualify.

Acting Chair Everett opened and closed public comment.

Acting Chair Everett stated that he would like to keep the exception narrow, to avoid mass
exceptions. He also stated that he would like to remove the word “however” from the
amendment. He said that he does not like changing code for one instance, and that a larger
overwrite of the clause in the future may need to occur. Commissioner Eyre stated that he would
be uncomfortable if the clause allowed the removal of all connectivity, but as it stands, he has no
issue with the amendment. Commissioner Lockhart mentioned that she would like to see it more
open ended in the future.

                Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to offer a positive recommendation to the
                City Council with the findings that the proposed development code amendment is
                in conformance with the purposes, intent, and provisions of the general plan and
                its various elements. He included all DRC comments. Commissioner Roberts
                seconded.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, no. Commissioner
                Eyre, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes. Commissioner Roberts, yes. Motion passed
                4-1.
3.4) Public Hearing and Consideration of Eight14 LLC’s Request for Approval of the Lehi
Vet Clinic Concept Plan Located on 1.25 Acres at Approximately Hardman Way and
Redwood Road, Requestion Exceptions to the Chapter 37 Design Standards Building
Orientation.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. The applicant is requesting a setback longer than the allotted 50
feet from a major road. They are having issues with the grading of the lot.

Acting Chair Everett clarifies that the only exception is the extended setback.

The applicants, Mark Tellemberg, Daniel Harris, and Jared Ford attended virtually. They stated
that they would be the first construction in the area.

Acting Chair Everett asked if the building would be easier to construct on a gentler grade. The
applicants stated that it would, and that they are taking steps to make the grade work with the
project. Commissioner Kunze asked about planned signage. The applicants said that they would
like free standing signage but hadn’t explored their options yet. Jacob was unsure if a pylon sign
would be allowed, or if they would be constrained to a monument sign. Commissioner Lockhart
asked staff if the Commission would be required to grant the same exception to the surrounding
properties, as they are all currently owned by the same developer. Acting Chair Everett clarified
that the exception would be only for the parcel being discussed today, and that it wouldn’t
necessarily set a precedent.

Acting Chair Everett opened and closed public hearing.

Commissioner Eyre clarified with the applicants that the exception was for aesthetic purposes,
rather than an actual construction issue. Commissioner Roberts said that being able to see over
the building wouldn’t bother him, but he sees the benefits of the extra parking that would be
allowed with the extended setback.

                Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to approve the request with the findings
                that the requested exception is consistent with the Lehi City development code,
                and that the requested exception conforms to the goals and policies of the general

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner

3.5) Public Hearing and Consideration of Dot Architecture’s Request for Approval of the
Valley Vet Clinic Concept Plan Located at 3055 North 1200 West in the Neighborhood
Commercial Zone with an Exception to Have the Door Face Away From 1200 West.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. He explained that the main concern was over animal safety. A
door facing the street would allow animals to run out into 1200 West, which is a major safety
hazard for pets, pet owners, and drivers.

The applicants, Alex Park and John Lee, were present. Alex currently owns Valley Vet in
Draper, and John is with Dot Architecture. Alex clarified that an animal escaping is not a matter
of if, but a matter of when. He wants to minimize the safety risks for all parties involved, by
funneling escaped animals into a fenced area with few or no moving vehicles. Acting Chair
Everett asked if a vestibule would work. John explained that with how the setbacks work
currently on the lot, the addition of a vestibule would result in the loss of multiple parking spaces
and/or exam rooms. There would also be a potential traffic jam problem if the door is placed
closer to the street. Commissioner Eyre asked about pedestrian access and the egress door facing
the street. The applicants explained that the egress door is primarily there for fire escape
purposes. A more pedestrian-friendly walk-up is not possible if they want to maintain the design
that funnels and confines escaped pets. Commissioner Eyre also asked if it was possible to make
this use specific. Kim Struthers let him know that it isn’t. Everett asked for a clarification of the
services that would be provided. The applicant explained that it will solely be used for veterinary
services. Occasionally a patient may need to stay overnight, but it will not be used for kenneling.

Acting Chair Everett opened public hearing.

Troy Sheffield lives directly east of the property. He is concerned about the height of the
building. He is also concerned about the height of the fence based on the different grading
between the two properties. He is also concerned about the potential of euthanasia and cremation
happening near his home, and the potential smells that would cause. His biggest concern is about
parking lot lighting and lighting on any potential signs facing his home.

Tyler Andersen lives down the street to the east from the site. He is also concerned about
fencing. He is worried about noise from dogs staying overnight. He wants to know if the
proposed dog run counts as part of the landscaping buffer required by code.

Shane and Jenna Rowley commented online. They are concerned about signage, minimizing
lighting, and ensuring that the landscape buffer is in place. They clarified that they see both
positives and negatives about the project.

Acting Chair Everett closed public hearing.

Acting Chair Everett asked staff what the proposed dog run would be classified as. Kim told him
that since the space is being utilized as part of the business, it would be counted as part of the
clinic. The applicants clarified that the dog run would primarily be used to allow pets to relieve
themselves before appointments or procedures and would be cleaned up after every usage. They
also stated that they would have more specific answers to all questions in later presentations, but
that they would do their best to answer them now. They intend to provide their own fencing.
They are very open to public suggestions about lighting. They only intend to have lighted signs
on the west side of the building facing the street. While euthanasia will happen on site, cremation
is taken care of by a third party. Euthanized animals will be picked up from a discreet, fenced
off, outdoor freezer two or three times a week. They also clarified that the building is entirely
soundproofed, and any areas where pets would be staying overnight are in the center of the
building where they will benefit from extra layers of soundproofing.

Staff were able to answer other questions. Jacob explained that the height of the building is 31
feet, four feet below the maximum allowed height. As it stands, the building would need an
additional 11 feet of landscaping, half of which can be parking. It currently meets all
requirements. Commissioner Lockhart clarified that this would be a small-animal only vet
practice. Commissioner Everett says the revised plan should address trash receptacles.

Jacob Curtis mentioned that the design districts will likely need to be reassessed now that 1200
has grown as much as it has. Kim Struthers mentions that the goal of the design districts is to
decenter the car somewhat and provide opportunities for pedestrians. Kunze mentioned that this
business is the best fit she’s seen for this neighborhood so far. She thinks that Lehi needs to start
thinking about 1200 as a mixed-use area.

                Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moves to approve the exception to the concept
                plan with the findings that approving the proposed exception will allow the
                concept plan to comply with the intent of the general nonresidential design
                district, that the proposed concept is consistent with the Lehi City development
                code, and that the proposed concept does conform with the goals and policies of
                the general plan. She included all DRC comments. Commissioner Roberts
                seconded.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner
                Eyre, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes. Commissioner Roberts, yes. Motion passed
                unanimously.
