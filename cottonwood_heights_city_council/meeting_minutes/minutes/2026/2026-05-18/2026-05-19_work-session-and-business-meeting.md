Item 7.2 was consideration of Ordinance 465 Amending Code Sections 19.75.050 and 19.76.030
Regarding Separate Utility Meters. Council Member Hyland asked if the ordinance was intended
to address separate metering for accessory dwelling units (“ADU”). Community and Economic
Development Director, Jim Spung, reported that it was a resident-initiated request to update code
to allow second utility meters for guesthouses and detached ADUs. He reviewed photographs of
the applicant’s property. The existing utility meter is on the opposite end of the property from a
proposed ADU, and both Rocky Mountain Power and Enbridge Gas recommended that he install
a second meter. It would greatly reduce impacts to existing infrastructure and landscaping and
save tens of thousands of dollars. Gas lines also lose efficiency over such a distance. Other
property owners with detached structures had made similar requests in the past.

Staff drafted the proposed Text Amendment to allow a second meter in cases where it would make
sense. The property owner would be required to provide evidence that it is not reasonable to
connect to the primary meter and be responsible for all utility billing on the site. For guesthouses,
they would also be required to record an affidavit with Salt Lake County clarifying that it is a

        4.2     Fireworks Map Discussion – Battalion Chief Riley Pilgrim.

UFA Battalion Chief, Riley Pilgrim, reported that there was a push to restrict fireworks east of
1300 East in 2020 or 2021, and all cities from Salt Lake City to Draper followed that
recommendation. In 2022 and 2023, cities like Cottonwood Heights began to feel like it was
overly restrictive and started walking the restrictions back. The fireworks map had since been
fine-tuned based on legislative requirements.

Per Utah Code § 15A-5-202.5-1(b), fireworks can be restricted in:

        1.1.1 Mountainous, brush-covered, forest-covered, or dry grass areas;
        1.1.2 Within 200 feet of waterways, trails, canyons, washes, ravines, or similar areas;
        1.1.3 Wildland Urban Interface areas (any urban interface areas where wild or
              undeveloped area commingles with structures);
        1.1.4 A limited area outside the hazardous areas described above to facilitate a readily
              identifiable closed area.

        4.3     Budget Discussion – Finance Director Scott Jurges.

Finance Director, Scott Jurges, reviewed a General Fund Balance Analysis from FY2018-2019 to
the FY2026-2027 Tentative Budget. He analyzed starting and ending fund balances, as well as
the required 6% minimum balance and compensated absence reserve. The compensated absence
reserve had been very stable, at approximately 9% each year. The analysis also included the
percentages of fund balance and compensated absences.

In FY2018-2019, the fund balance was 19% of revenues. For two years beginning in FY2020-
2021, the City experienced extreme sales tax growth in addition to an influx of one-time funds that
increased the ratio to 30%. It then began to decline. In FY2023-2024, there a significant decline
of approximately $1.8 million. FY2024-2025 saw an approximately $1.7 million decline. The
current year was projected to have a decline of approximately $1.9 million. The total ending fund
balance for the year was projected at $4 million, which was in line with FY2018-2029 but reflected
a percentage decrease from 19% to 14%. The FY2026-2027 Tentative Budget reflected a $220,000
increase in fund balance, which would maintain a percentage of 13% to 14% at the end of the year.
From a percentage standpoint, the City’s financial status was worse than FY2018-2019 when the

Mr. Jurges reported that there had been significant citing of Holladay’s budget policies, and
Cottonwood Heights also included budget policies in past-year budget documents. He combined
both policy sources and added descriptors and targets to create a seven-page draft policy document

The document began with General Fund balance policies, as the balance fluctuates, but other funds
rely on the General Fund. Fund balance categories include non-spendable, restricted, committed,

code section was cited. Those driven by the Cottonwood Heights Municipal Code did not yet have
citations.

If the goal is to maintain a General Fund balance of 15% to 25%, that becomes the target range,
with caution being needed to bring anything outside of it back within the target. A balance outside
of range would require City Council consent. Once reserves are used, they should be replenished
in a timely manner through a reduction in expenditures or an increase in revenues.
