The declining rates of coronavirus infection continue to be encouraging for Utah. We feel grateful for the
commitment of so many for so long to maintain proper health protocols of masks and social distancing
and for the marvelous, even miraculous, work that has led to the vaccines. We wish to take this
opportunity to encourage everyone eligible to get their vaccine as soon as possible and to thank the
many professionals and volunteers who are working hard every day to deliver the vaccines to the public.
This progress is what allows us now to be gathering again in person for our council meetings, a day we
have longed for as a council. While we have missed in person association, we have appreciated the
considerable work of our staff to make our online experience possible and the patience of the public
during this time. We are grateful that during this time we have continued to get valued feedback from
citizens online and by email. We will continue to rely on our staff and on your patience as we transition
to a hybrid model of our meetings so as to facilitate the needs of several of our council members and
many in the public who are still not vaccinated or who do not wish to risk coming to our meetings in
person.

As good as things seem at present, we do note that we are not quite out of the woods. We have not yet
crossed the threshold where a majority of the population is vaccinated. While we are encouraged that a
majority of the most vulnerable have received vaccinations, in Utah County, we are at only 22% fully
vaccinated overall. As we host this and future meetings and in the interest of our continued full recovery
as a thriving and bustling community, we encourage compliance with the recommendations of the CDC
and other health organizations to continue to practice social distancing and to wear masks when indoors
and unable to socially distance, especially where vaccinated and un-vaccinated people from mixed

 2.        An ordinance amending Provo City Code Chapter 14.20B. Citywide Application.
           (PLOTA20210026) (1:11:52)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt the ordinance as currently constituted, has been made by
                          council rule.

Melissa McNalley, Planner, presented. The applicant requested an ordinance text amendment to update
the current Freeway Commercial Two (FC2) zone specifically to apply to two parcels located on both
sides of Lakeview Parkway which was zoned as A120 and vacant. The proposed amendment would apply
to all FC2 zones. This amendment addressed building height in correlation to proximity of single-family
properties. During work session it was mentioned that this would also affect the area off the Center
Street interchange. The Southwest Neighborhood Land Use Plan encouraged this type of development
at this location.

Chair Sewell opened public comment.

Becky Bogdin, Lakewood Neighborhood Chair, thanked staff for considering the neighborhood in their
planning. However, she was concerned about the provision granting the department director the ability
to make minor exceptions regarding building height.

Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - May 04, 2021                                                      Page 4 of 8
There were no other comments from the public.

Robert Mills, Planning Supervisor, explained that in the past it had been helpful to permit the
department director to make minor exceptions under certain conditions, which were outlined in the
ordinance. But if the Council was uncomfortable with this provision, they were not opposed to removing
it.

Councilor Harding wanted to consider the impact to the other areas zoned FC2, this had not been
mentioned in the staff report. He also noted the purpose and intent had not been addressed. Mr. Mills
agreed the intent was important and it had been taken into consideration. This property shared the
same gateway characteristics of the other FC2 zones, and they shared the same intent. One reason they
wanted to implement a step back requirement was to consider existing homes near these zones.

Mr. Mills explained the existing FC2 zone on Center Street was bordered by RC, R1.8 and R1.6 zones. At
any point where these residential zones stop, for the first hundred feet from that line the buildings
would be restricted to four stories; and the closer you get to the freeway and further from the
residential areas, the taller the buildings could be up to six stories.

Councilor Harding asked for this item to be continued to the next meeting since this was the first
hearing. Councilor Hoban supported this request.

Councilor Ellsworth said this was an improvement and would help to advance economic goals for this
area.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                          Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.

 3.        Ordinance 2021-18 amending the General Plan Southwest Area Future Land Use Map
           designation for approximately 10 acres of real property, generally located approximately at
           1850 South 500 West, from LDR to Commercial. Lakeview Neighborhood. (PLGPA20210058)
           (1:31:23)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-18 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Melissa McNalley, Planner, presented. The applicant was requesting a general plan amendment to
update the current Southwest Future Land Use Map from LDR to Commercial. The proposed
amendment will redesignate one parcel approximately 10 acres in size.

There was a dike on the north side of the property which raised this land about seven feet above the
residential development on the north. All development in this area had to be brought to this height to
get the land out of the floodplain. The neighbors of this property were worried that having homes built
on the elevated land would pose a loss of privacy for the existing homes below, they preferred
commercial development.

Chair Sewell opened public comment, there was no response.




Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - May 04, 2021                                                       Page 5 of 8
Councilor Ellsworth supported this proposal. She agreed commercial made the most sense for this area
and this would provide a landscaped buffer between the businesses and neighborhood. There were
plenty of other opportunities for LDR west of the freeway. She noted this would be a great location for a
grocery store or major retailer.

Councilor Fillmore agreed with Councilor Ellsworth and thought this would be the highest and best use
for this property.

Chair Sewell was also in favor.

Councilor Harding agreed the best use for this was commercial, but his understanding was that this
would not actually increase the amount of usable commercial space in the area because this area would

Chair Sewell called for a vote on the implied motion.

 4.        An ordinance amending the Zone Map classification of approximately 75 acres of real
           property, generally located at 500 W. Lakeview Parkway, from Agricultural (A1.20) to
           Freeway Commercial Two (FC-2). Lakewood Neighborhood. (PLRZ20210025) (1:47:38)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt the ordinance as currently constituted, has been made by
                          council rule.

Melissa McNalley, Planner, presented. The applicant requested a zone change from Agricultural A1.20
zone to the Freeway Commercial Two (FC2) zone. The proposed rezone would provide commercial
development opportunities north and south of Lakeview Parkway adjacent to the 1-15 off-ramp. The
Southwest Neighborhood Land Use Plan encouraged this type of development at the location.

Customarily, rezone requests were associated with a specific development proposal and are
concurrently processed with some project application. In this case, however, no pending development
proposal was associated with the rezone request.

Councilor Ellsworth asked if there was a statement from the property owner. Mr. Mills said there had
been no formal statement, but they had been involved in conversations and remained neutral.

Becky Bogdin, Lakeview Neighborhood Chair, requested a continuance because the zone text
amendment had been continued and that amendment included a buffer the neighborhood wanted.

Councilor Handley requested a continuance and Councilor Harding agreed.

           property, generally located at 734 West 300 South, from Residential (R1.6A) to Low Density
           Residential (LDR). Franklin Neighborhood. (PLRZ20210059) (1:52:46)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2021-19, as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Dustin Wright, Planner, presented. The applicant was requesting to rezone his property from R1.6A to
accommodate a subdivision of the property located at 734 West 300 South into two lots. The proposed
lots would be smaller than the minimum lot size requirement of the existing zone so the rezone to LDR is
needed to accommodate the smaller lots. The existing single-family home would remain on one of the
lots and the other lot would accommodate a new single-family home to be built.

Councilor Harding asked if this followed the Franklin Neighborhood Plan. Mr. Wright felt this was
compatible, there was no proposed change to the land use which was residential.

Mr. Wright said this item had been presented to the neighborhood and posted on Open City Hall and
most were supportive.

The applicant, Jeff Rands, spoke to the Council. He wanted to build a home on the new parcel consistent
with the character of the neighborhood and thought it would be a good fit.

Ms. Ellsworth asked how old the existing home was and what the average age of other homes in the
neighborhood were. Mr. Rands said the existing home was built in 1896 and the neighborhood was
mixed, some were very old, and some were newer. The neighborhood was about half renters, half
owners.

Chair Sewell opened public comment, there was no response.

Councilor Hoban thought this was a good way to add housing stock without a lot of disruption and
changes to an existing neighborhood. He wanted to make infill like this a little easier.

Councilor Handley agreed with Mr. Hoban. Infill was highly desirable to consider and was more
affordable because the infrastructure was in place. Councilors Ellsworth and Fillmore agreed.

Councilor Harding agreed with this proposal, but his concern was whether this aligned with the
neighborhood plan.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                          Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.

 6.        An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding conditional uses in the Freeway
           Commercial (FC1) Zone. Citywide Application. (PLOTA20210000262) (2:17:38)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt the ordinance as currently constituted, has been
                          made by council rule.

Aaron Ardmore, Planner, presented. Brittany Johnson requested an ordinance text amendment to the
conditional use section of the FC1 zone to add a new conditional use of cannabis production with certain
restrictions (use code 2830). Cultivation was currently assigned to the A1.10 zone, production was
assigned to the Freeway Industrial Zone, and distribution was assigned to the Freeway Commercial
Zone.


Provo City Council Meeting Minutes - May 04, 2021                                                       Page 7 of 8
In this case, the applicant wanted to produce the product in an ancillary building behind an existing
distribution location that was already operating legally in the Freeway Commercial Zone. Staff reviewed
the application and believed that this met the economic development goals of the General Plan. Staff
and the Planning Commission recommended approval.

Chair Sewell asked if there was a limit on where the cannabis could be distributed, such as out of state.
Mr. Ardmore explained this particular facility worked like a traditional pharmacy. He was unsure if
shipping out of the state would be allowed.

Chair Sewell invited the applicant to speak.

The applicant’s representative, Jarmon Montgomery, explained they would package and produce the
product on site in a modular pre-made ancillary building.

Chair Sewell opened public comment, there was no response.

Councilor Fillmore understood the cities were required by the State to provide opportunities for medical
cannabis. He knew people who benefit from medical marijuana, but he also knew people who abused it.
He was unsure of the value this would bring the Provo.

Councilor Handley did not want to make the city more conducive to this activity. He asked if the
Administration if they had a recommendation. Mr. Parker described the location of the existing business
and said there were strict statutes around medical marijuana. If this were a fully enclosed facility, and
not used for cultivation, it would be similar to what NuSkin did in Mountain Vista where they prepare
their nutrients using raw materials. From his perspective, it seemed low impact and would support a
local business.

Councilor Hoban asked how they were currently getting their product. Mr. Parker assumed it was being
shipped in from outside the City. He thought this would shorten the supply chain and save the business
owner money in terms of production. Mr. Hoban noted that this use was already allowed in another
zone in Provo. He preferred to see where it was already allowed before making this decision.

Councilor Shipley did not understand the concern, this was a legal business, and they could do this
elsewhere, but he preferred to support them and allow this adjacent to the existing building. He thought
most of the concern was about the industry they were in. Councilors Ellsworth and Handley agreed.

Councilor Harding said he was concerned about the industry. The City had taken steps to be minimally
compliant with the State and he did not want to make the product more convenient or attractive. Chair
Sewell agreed.

Instead of voting against the proposal, Mr. Shipley preferred to continue it to allow more time for
research. Councilor Hoban agreed.
