                     Nephi City Council Work Session Minutes

1. Mr. Atkinson reminded the council that Nephi City contracts with Summit Energy to
   purchase natural gas for the City’s system. The city has enjoyed the benefits of a
   hedge rate for many years. Hedging protects the city from large fluctuations in the
   natural gas market but can also sometimes means that the City pays more than the
   market. Hedging is meant as a mechanism to smooth out swings in market prices and
   provide some consistency with our customers. The city’s large industrial customers
   use the market rate in their pricing. One customer, Utah Greenhouse (formerly
   Houweling’s Tomatoes) would like to obtain a hedge rate from Nephi City. The city can
   choose to allow this with a customer or not.
   By hedging, Utah Greenhouse is attempting to protect itself from swings in the market
   just like Nephi City. However, if a hedge were proposed, Utah Greenhouse would
   need to pay the hedged amount, regardless of the market price at the hedge price.
   They have provided usage figures for us for the next 3 years and would like to secure a
   hedge rate similar to the hedge that the city is obtaining from Summit Energy for the
   next three years. This hedge rate has been developed based on the city’s rate, plus an
   administrative premium. They would like to hedge 90% of their usage through March,
   then 75% through the end of the year. In 2024 they would like to hedge 50% of their
   estimated usage, and in 2025, 25%.
   The council directed the City Attorney to draft a contract that would require Utah
   Greenhouse to pay the hedged amounts through the 3 years of the contract.

    Work Session reconvened at 9:05 p.m.

    7.    City Attorney Kasey Wright updated the council about discussions he had participated
         in with the RV Park owners. Mr. Alex Johnson and his son Jared were in attendance
         and said that they would like to eliminate their RV park and convert the building located
         on the property to a daycare facility and to keep the laundromat with some major
         improvement to it. Mr. Johnson requested a year to accomplish the conversion. The
         council first said they would only like to give him until August 1st but after some
         discussion, advised Mr. Wright to draft a contract up with benchmark dates of different
         progress on the project being listed. The council agreed that all tenants should be out
         of the RV park by September 30, 2023.
           Mr. Wright then reviewed the updated draft of an ordinance that would change the
         current RV park code. Owners of the parks had given him their input and he requested
         that the council let him know of any revisions they would like to the amended code.

    8.   Councilor Memmott moved to adjourn the meeting. Councilor Worwood seconded the
         motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
