       A.     HOME OCCUPATION, “MERI SWEETS” A HOME-BASED BUSINESS TO
              BE LOCATED IN AN EXISTING HOME AT 11895 SOUTH 3200 WEST.
              APPLICANT – MARIAM RODRIGUEZ.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that Home Occupation
businesses are a Conditional Use if they cannot meet certain criteria. One of the criteria is
if there will be food preparation in the home. This automatically makes it a Conditional Use,
which requires Planning Commission approval. It is an administrative action for the Planning
Commission, which means that the Commission will ensure that the rules of home
occupation are met.

Meri Sweets Cookies is very low impact with no customers or visitors expected to come to
the home. There is an existing basement kitchen where they will bake the cookies and then
deliver them. If there was no food being prepared, this application would not have needed
to come before the Commission. Notices were sent to the neighbors within 300 feet of the
home. There is plenty of parking if there were customers, however, that would be the
exception. The basement kitchen will be inspected by the Building Inspector to ensure that
it meets all appliable Building Codes. The applicant will also be required to work with the
Health Department or the State Department of Agriculture. The City’s Business Licensing
process will ensure that the state licensing is in place before the Business License is issued.

Mr. Prestwich explained that all home occupations must meet fixed standards. If they can
meet the fixed standards and qualifications, Staff can review and approve the application
without the need to come before the Planning Commission. If, however, there is any
variation of the fixed standards such as more than eight visitors/customers per day or

operating hours outside of the standards, the request must be reviewed by the Planning
Commission. The ordinance also specifies that certain uses must come to the Planning
Commission as a Conditional Use, such as more than two home occupations, using the yard
or garage space, preparation of food or beverages, or if they have more than one full-time
employee. 16 activities are prohibited as home occupations.

After the Planning Commission Meeting, the applicant will register the business with Riverton
City and obtain outside agency approvals. In this case, it would be the local Health
Department or the State Department of Agriculture. If a Fire Inspection is required, the
respective entities will inspect the property. The applicant must maintain the Business
License with the Conditional Use or start the process over. In Riverton, the Mayor and
Council support small businesses and have eliminated the Business Licensing fees for
nearly almost all businesses. It was recommended that once obtained, the license remain
in effect rather than being allowed to lapse.

Staff reviewed the application and found that it meets the applicable requirements and
recommended approval with the conditions set forth in the staff report.

The applicant’s representative, Homar Dominguez, reported that the applicant, Mariam
Rodriguez makes treats from Venezuela. Initially, she will prepare three different types of
cookies.

Commissioner Breinholt asked for clarification about the potential for customers to come to
the home. Mr. Dominguez stated that the idea is to sell to markets that will then sell to the
public. As a result, customers will not be coming directly to the home.

hearing was closed.

       B.     FINAL SITE PLAN-RESIDENTIAL SUBDIVISION, “HARVEST GARDEN”,
              LOCATED NEAR 11800 SOUTH 3600 WEST. APPLICANT – IVORY
              DEVELOPMENT.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the request is for the Harvest Garden
neighborhood comprised of approximately 8.9 acres with 51 lots proposed. The application
came before the Planning Commission about eight months earlier for a rezone. It later went
on to the City Council and was ultimately rezoned to RM-6 with an Elderly Housing Overlay
and a Special Designation (“SD”). He displayed a diagram of the area and described the
location of the subject property.

Mr. Prestwich reported that the Planning Commission, on a legislative action item, is virtually
making a suggestion to the City Council. The Council makes the final decision and there is
an ordinance that is passed at this point. He identified the additional SD designations. When
there is stacked zoning and SDs, it is important to remember what the Council approved for
the property because it varies. The Special Designation for this property includes:

      All units adjacent to the west and south property lines be single-family detached units;

      All units be rambler style with only a bonus room allowed above the main level;

      Guest parking must be included in the site plan as determined in Site Plan approval;

      A stub road be included to the south property line as determined in Site Plan approval;
       and

      The adjacent zoning is considered compatible to determine rear yard setbacks.

Mr. Prestwich presented the layout of the property and stated that the development will
connect to the existing public street in Merced Estates, bringing it through the development
and lining it up with the road on the opposite side. The road is 60 feet in Merced, which is
an old City Standard, so the developer will decrease it to a 54-foot road, which is the new
Standard, in the center and continue it out. Two private roads are 32 feet wide, which allows
for parking on one side. One of the City Council requirements was to accommodate off-
street or guest parking. After meeting with the applicant, Staff suggested making the private
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                 4
January 27, 2022

roads wider to allow for parking on both sides of the street. The applicant felt that was a
good idea and widened the roads accordingly. It was noted that the private drives are signed
differently than public roads so that the public is aware that they are private.

The lots along the south and west are larger than others in the development. The Council
liked the RM-6 zoning with the single-family detached and added it as an SD on several
applications. The RM-6 zone allows for townhomes and condominiums. Because the
Council did not like the RM-6 zone without the SD, they amended it to only allow single-
family detached or twin homes. That ordinance was changed after the zoning was put in
place but will apply to this situation and the RM-6 zone going forward. It was mentioned that
lots that are less than 5,000 square feet in size are only buildable when paired as twin
homes. Detached units are not permitted on those lots. The minimum lot size for RM-6 is
5,000 square feet but it allows the developer to combine two lots that are less than 5,000
square feet in size. If they add up to 7,000 square feet, they can build a twin home and
share the property line. Lots along the west and south plat are only buildable as single-
family detached units. In the packet, Mr. Prestwich described the property and highlighted
the areas that can only be single-family detached and areas where lots are less than 5,000
square feet in size that can only be built as twin homes.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Site Plan with the private street, the public street with sidewalk
and curb and gutter on both sides, and the utilities that are stubbed to the south per City
requirements. Fencing along 3600 West and 11800 South will be six-foot solid masonry as
required by ordinance. Along the south and west boundaries, there will be six-foot vinyl
fencing bordering single-family homes. The developer provided a layout of the fencing to
Staff.

The stormwater for the development will be accommodated by an internal swale before it
connects to the City system. The HOA will maintain the swale, the private street, and
possibly the yards. Along 11800 South, there is a proposed park strip where the developer
will install small trees. 3600 West has existing stamped concrete that was put in place when
the City did a widening project about 10 years ago and that will remain.

Staff reviewed the plan and recommended approval with the conditions set forth in the staff
report. Specifically, item number 5, states that the City will vacate excess right-of-way on
3600 West. This was due to the situation when the City acquired the right-of-way in that it
was unclear how the property would develop. That had since been clarified and determined
to be just a couple of feet.

Mr. Prestwich stated that notice was sent to all properties within 300 feet of the property. No
comments were received.

Commissioner Cluff commented on a previous concern from a neighbor to the south.
Mr. Prestwich stated that the matter was resolved and stubs were required to the neighbor’s
property. He also identified where the neighbor accesses his home and stated that there
are resolutions in place.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the rezone applications for this
project began in 2016. The developer presented an application that went back and forth
between the Planning Commission and the City Council in 2019 until it was ultimately
adopted as RM-6, SD zone. The parcel is 11.77 acres in size. The application is for the
Master and Final Site Plan with 36 single-family homes, 10 townhomes, and open space in
the detention pond area.

This request was noticed to neighbors within 300 feet of the property. Staff received two
calls and one email. Mr. Prestwich reported that the surrounding zoning is commercial, RR-
22, CN (Commercial Neighborhood), and R-3 and R-4. The developer assembled several
parcels to create the current parcel. The Special Designations for the approval included the
following three areas.

      Area 1 would have a maximum of 14 lots with a minimum size of 10,000 square feet
       and meet the R-4 zone.
      Area 2 should have a minimum of 6,000 square-foot lots and otherwise comply with
       the setback requirements of the RM-6 zone.
      Area 3 may include 10 units consistent with the RM-6 zone with the exception that up
       to five units may be attached. Laurel Chase Drive shall connect to 11800 South at
       Redwood Road as a thru-street and Area 3 may include a direct connection to
       Redwood Road. The proposed plan was found to meet these requirements.

The overall Site Plan includes the road, a detention pond, and a way to access the
townhomes. Laurel Chase Drive is a 54-foot public street that widens out with the
intersection at Redwood Road. Because it is a Utah Department of Transportation (“UDOT”)
road, UDOT had comments on this intersection. At this point, they agree with the plans.
Any utility work on Redwood Road will need to be coordinated with UDOT. Maple Brook
Lane is a 54-foot wide public street that terminates into a private street. The drive approach
will be shared with the existing dance studio.

The pond shown on the plan is for future development. The City purchased the pond
property and was working with the developer to determine who will be responsible for the
various improvements. That will come forward as a separate approval.

In Phase 1, work will stop at a designated location and a fire turnaround will be required.
Currently, a T-road is shown, however, if a change is made to the design, staff will work with
the applicant to ensure that it meets the Fire Code.

Mr. Prestwich reported that there will be an HOA to maintain private spaces. He noted that
the plan shows which lots are greater than 6,000 square feet in size and those that are
greater than 10,000 square feet.

The fencing on Redwood Road will be six-foot masonry as it is adjacent to a major collector
road, and six-foot masonry along another property line. There is a six-foot vinyl fence with
a mow strip along the border of City-maintained property, six-foot masonry fencing along the
canal, and six-foot vinyl fencing along the border of the R-3 and R-4 residential
neighborhoods. The border of commercial zoning will be six-foot masonry fencing. There
is one area where there is a division of single-family and multi-family dwellings on the site
plan and vinyl fencing is proposed.

One of the challenges with the development is with the UDOT parcel and the UDOT pond.
The developer is addressing the drainage and the potential problems there.

Staff, including Fire, Planning, and Engineering, reviewed the plan and has worked with the
developer. Staff recommended approval with the conditions set forth in the staff report.
