Proposed Resolution 2022-6 adopting the Ogden Station Plan amendment
to the Ogden Onboard Master Plan

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
to approve an amendment to the Ogden Onboard Master Plan. The memo stated the current proposal is for the City Council to
formally adopt the Ogden Onboard Transit-Oriented Vision and Implementation Strategy, or the Ogden Onboard Plan, and a
subsequent amendment updating the station area plan for the Union Station campus and the intermodal hub area. The plan and
amendments have been adopted by the Utah Transit Authority Board of Trustees but have not been formally adopted by Ogden
City.

Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and stated the Ogden Onboard Plan is meant to provide direction
on land use changes that can be made along the Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) corridor as a way to catalyze transit-oriented
development in key areas and to maximize the usage of the BRT system. He discussed the goals of the original Plan and the
general concept for the BRT route, after which he noted that the amendments include revisions to the priority station urban center
concept and expansion of the scope of the Intermodal Hub to include the Union Station campus as an integrated development. He
presented an updated concept and density plan for the Ogden Station, after which he concluded the Planning Commission voted to
recommend approval of the Plan amendment.

Chair Nadolski then introduced in writing proposed resolution 2022-6, entitled:

“A resolution of the Ogden City Council adopting the Ogden Onboard Plan and the Ogden Station Plan
amendment to the Ogden Onboard Plan.”

Council member White referenced past discussions about secondary water restrictions for the 2022 watering season;
since those discussions, Weber Basin Water Conservancy District (WBWCD) has issued directives restricting watering to once
per week according to a schedule published by them. He stated that the different communications from different entities are
confusing the public and she asked that City Administration publish information to help people understand what regulations they
must follow. Mayor Caldwell deferred to Public Services Director Lowder but noted that the City is sending 24,000 postcards to
residents communicating the secondary water restrictions they must abide by. Mr. Lowder stated that there are different
regulations for Pineview customers than those who receive service from WBWCD, but all property owners should refrain from
watering between the hours of 10:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m.; if conditions worsen, further communication will be sent to all property
owners to give additional information. Vice Chair Lopez suggested town hall meetings be held in Ogden City to improve
communication with residents regarding drought conditions and water restrictions. There was brief discussion about current water
levels in reservoirs and rivers that serve Ogden City, with the entire Council agreeing that a town hall meeting would be helpful
for all residents and businesses in the community. It is important for all property owners to understand the restrictions and
subsequent code enforcement for violations of the restrictions.

Council member White asked for an update on efforts to make City residents eligible to participate in the WBWCD ‘flip
the strip’ program. Planning Director Montgomery reported the Planning Commission will be forwarding a recommendation to the
City Council soon; if the City Council approves that recommendation, City residents will be eligible for a rebate from WBWCD
for participating in the program.
