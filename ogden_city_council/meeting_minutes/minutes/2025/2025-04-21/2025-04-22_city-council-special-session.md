FlashVote Survey results
          Kevin Lyons discussed the participation in the City’s recent FlashVote survey, which asked questions about the City’s
strategic planning directives. The 2019 Strategic Plan was developed using an extensive public input process that yielded 2,000
public responses, while the FlashVote survey regarding the City’s strategic directives was quick by design and yielded 170
responses. The Strategic Plan process took broad input and information and condensed it into an actionable framework while the
FlashVote survey results provide specific, granular answers. He engaged in discussion with the Council regarding whether the
sample group for the FlashVote survey is large enough to be considered an appropriate representation of the City’s total
population; appropriate frequency to conduct a survey; and benchmarking local data with State of national data.
          Communications Director Garside then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to compare the relevance of the
Strategic Directives established in 2019 with the Community Needs FlashVote Survey of 2025. The Survey asked questions

Updated Strategic Plan discussion
          Council Assistant Executive Director Symes used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide an overview of the
purpose and intent of a strategic plan, in general, and why it is important for an entity to have a strategic plan. Ogden’s Strategic
Plan was developed in 2019 with the assistance of an outside consultant and an advisory committee. The plan included significant
outreach and public involvement, and it established four strategic directives and key initiatives for Economic Development,
Community Safety, City Image and Reputation, and Recreation. Ogden City added a fifth directive – Fiscal Sustainability and
Transparency. Efforts are now underway to transition to a new Plan given that the 2019 Plan had a five-year horizon, which ended
in 2024. City Administration reported Strategic Plan progress to the Council throughout February 2025, but the transition to a new
Plan is on hold because there are several significant plans are still in the works which can provide valuable information and public
input, including the following:
              • General Plan
              • Financial Sustainability Plan
              • Staffing Studies
              • Parks Utilization Study
              • Updated RDA Policies
          Mr. Symes also reviewed the plans and resources currently available in the City and noted that the City’s Strategic Plan
2.0 will be a unified vision for the future of Ogden and will integrate the Mayor and Council’s vision for the City. It will also
include measurable goals from existing and developing plans, a progress tracking tool, and reporting mechanisms. Crucial
questions for the Council to consider include:
              • How long do we wait?
                        o Plans are underway now, but we can’t wait forever.
                        o Uncertain when some plans and studies will be completed.
              • What do we need to be successful?
                        o Buy-in and unity from the Council, Mayor, City Staff, and the Community.
              • What are the next steps?
                        o Initially, wait until the current plans are completed – especially the general plan.
                        o Determine if other plans and studies should be completed prior to Strategic Planning efforts.
          For a copy of Mr. Symes’ presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          The Council engaged in discussion about the appropriate timing of finalizing an updated Strategic Plan, including how to
encourage the Mayor and City Administration to be involved in the process to update the Plan.

Public Comment Period
         Council Senior Policy Analyst Burton used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss public comment opportunities
based upon the City Council’s Rules and Norms. He noted Council staff has studied the public comment rules in 16 other cities
and found that 12 of the 16 allow for public comment at the beginning of a council meeting, before the council conducts its
business. Most allow three minutes per speaker; Salt Lake City limits public comment to one hour total, and two minutes per
person, Provo allows for 15 minutes of public comment and two minutes per person, and North Ogden allows five minutes per
person. Three of the studied cities regularly invite input multiple times throughout a Council meeting – outside of a public hearing
period: Farr West, Provo, and Orem. However, Provo limits public input to one opportunity per person. He concluded Council
staff have made the following findings:
             • Most cities have public comment at the beginning of meeting.
             • Most cities do not allow public input during other agenda items.

         Finally, the City Attorney’s Office has provided optional language that could be included in the Council’s Rules and
Norms to address concerns about public participation during City Council meetings: “Any Council member chairing a Council
meeting has discretion to manage the meeting in an efficient manner and may adjust the time limitations described in the
following subsections as the chair deems appropriate to efficiently manage city business. The chair may reduce time limits for
individual comments, set cumulative time limits for public comments on a topic, discourage repetitive comments, and issue other
limitations the chair deems appropriate.”

         The Council engaged in discussion regarding the benefits of public comments; the appropriate format for public
comments; and rules and restrictions that speakers must follow when addressing the Council during the public comment period.
Council staff indicated they can use the feedback provided by the Council to develop proposed amendments to the Council Rules
and Norms pertaining to public input.

Budget priorities
         Council Assistant Executive Director Symes provided the Council with the PowerPoint presentation that he gave to the
Mayor and City Administration staff as part of their recent budget retreat meeting. The presentation touched on informational
requests related to the budget; the format of Council budget presentations; significant budget decisions from last year; common
themes for Council budget priorities; projects for which there is Council interest in additional funding/focus; information
regarding specific high-profile projects; and other areas of interest. He noted that he stressed to Administration the following:
              • Council members would like more updates.
              • Council members know the Administration is working on a lot of these issues.
              • They don’t always get to see what is being done.
              • Council office is looking at ways to help facilitate additional communications.
         For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

Executive Director FY25 Goals/Accomplishments
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith distributed a handout that provided an overview of the performance evaluation
process for her position. Prior to meeting with the Chair and Vice Chair of the Council for her official evaluation, she wished to
identify some evaluation criteria for the Council to consider, as well as to discuss her goals and accomplishments with the entire
group.
          Chair White stated she felt it important to have a clear and formal process for the employee evaluation for the Executive
Director position; she also felt it appropriate for the entire Council to be aware of her goals and accomplishments and to visit with
her on a regular basis to understand the work she is performing on the Council’s behalf.

Council Comments
         Council member Graf referenced recent deportation activities occurring across the nation as a result of directives from
the federal government; he asked if Ogden City Administration has been discussing opportunities for the City to stand up for its
citizens who may be facing improper deportation. The Council discussed the issue and Council member Myers cited specific
examples of Ogden residents being targeted for deportation.


         The meeting adjourned at 7:15 p.m.
