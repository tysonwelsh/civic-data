     DISCUSSION – CARE Major Grant and Facility Presentations

Mr. Downs presented information regarding the three major grant applicants: Utah Metropolitan
Ballet (formerly Utah Regional Ballet) (UMB), Hale Center Foundation for the Arts (Hale), and
SCERA Center for the Arts (SCERA). He excused representatives from UMB but said there were
representatives from Hale and SCERA on the call to answer questions.

Mr. Downs first reviewed the request from UMB, including the amounts requested and the
amounts given going back to 2015-2016. He reviewed the achievements, including special
performances for Title 1 school children and the special show for Families Affected by Autism.
These special performances were often the first or only cultural events the children attended in the
year, and UMB was happy to provide those experiences for those children and families. Mr. Downs
shared the cost breakdown of UMB’s operating expenses.

Mr. Lambson asked if any Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) money was ever used
toward organizations like UMB that had special performances for Title 1 schools. Mr. Downs said
he was not sure UMB would qualify for CDBG funds because of the kind of organization they
were. In response to the question of how many performances actually took place in Orem, Mr.
Downs said UMB held five shows in Orem in the last year and planned to have additional

performances at the Noorda Theater at UVU. He explained that UVU was also the home of the
UMB.

Mr. Downs moved on to share information regarding the Hale organization. He clarified that major
grant applicants could request 35% of their operating expenses, but ultimately the City would
determine the amount awarded. Hale representatives Cody Swenson and Jeff Fisher shared details
from the past year, including special involvement in the City’s Centennial Celebration and
discounted ticket prices for performances for lower-income neighborhoods. Discussion continued
regarding the percentage of Orem residents that participated in Hale programs and attended
performances, as well as support from season ticket holders.

Mr. Fisher shared that the current situation with COVID-19 and social distancing requirements
meant their theater was not open, but they were beginning a program called “Hale at Home” where
patrons could pay a discounted ticket fee and stream a recorded performance at home. Mr. Fisher
said Hale was fortunate and had qualified for the Paycheck Protection Program, which gave them
eight to ten weeks of funding to cover payroll for their employees. He added that CARE money
was playing a role in covering those expenses as well, and was grateful for the support from the
City.

Mr. Downs then presented the information from the SCERA, and asked SCERA representative
Adam Robertson to share additional information. Mr. Robertson said this was the first time 86
years the SCERA had had to close their doors; it was unprecedented for the organization. He said
SCERA had also had special involvement with the City’s Centennial Celebration, including the
Summerfest outdoor concert with Josh Gracin and John Michael Montgomery that finished with
fireworks, and the actor that played Walter C. Orem at various events throughout 2019. Mr.
Robertson said they too were streaming performances for viewers at home, mostly of “theater for
young audiences” performances.

Mr. Downs said SCERA had seen steady growth in the past year; Mr. Macdonald complimented
Mr. Robertson on his ability to grow his programming while maintaining costs and utilizing the
CARE dollars well. Mr. Robertson thanked Mr. Macdonald, and said that was a conscious effort
on their part. He added that they had also qualified for the Paycheck Protection Program.

Mr. Downs said each major applicant was using their CARE money prudently and for the approved
uses. There was discussion on how the COVID-19 pandemic would affect the amount of CARE
dollars that would be collected, and the feasibility of giving the recommended award amounts. Mr.
Downs said there would not be any effect on this year’s granting round, but it was possible they
would see changes for next year.

Discussion continued on the likelihood of SCERA summer programs and camps being able to go
forward. Mr. Robertson said they were waiting to make those difficult decisions until they had
more information. Mr. Downs finished his presentations and said he would come before the
Council at a future meeting with final recommendations for major grant awards.

The City Council reviewed the evening’s agenda. Mr. Lambson said there was a minor correction
needed for the minutes to be approved. Mayor Brunst asked about the extension of the local
emergency to be considered as part of the Consent Agenda. Mr. Davidson said the extension would
be indefinite for now, as there was no predicting when the emergency would be resolved. Mr.
Stephens added that the city manager had the authority to extend the emergency 30 days at a time;
Mr. Davidson said he would follow the state’s lead.

At Mr. Davidson’s suggestion, Mr. Downs shared some thoughts from the Summerfest Committee.
He said the Summerfest Committee had discussed the possibility of what they were calling
“Summerfest-Lite” to take place August 14 and 15, 2020. It would coincide with the Senior
Center’s “Geezer Fest” event, and they would try to keep popular Summerfest activities like the
car show, pool party, kids’ crafts, and fireworks. There was the possibility of a children’s parade
as they would not be able to have the traditional large-scale parade. Other things they would likely
have to forego would be the carnival, Orem’s Got Talent, and the baby contest. Mr. Downs said
this was all contingent on the progress in flattening the curve with the COVID-19 pandemic.

Mr. Davidson advised the Council that the State Legislature would reconvene for a special session
of 10 days looking at issues that related directly to the emergency situation, particularly the state
budget and federal stimulus package, emergency powers and declarations, and powers of local
health departments to issue orders during times of emergency. Mr. Davidson said he expected they
would determine that the elections this year would be exclusively “Vote by Mail” statewide to
avoid public crowds and opportunities for the COVID-19 virus to spread. There was continued
discussion on business sales tax assessments and how that would impact the City.


     City Council Reports (Boards and Commissions, New Business, Etc.)

Mr. Peterson briefly updated the Council on his committee assignments. His assignments included
the UVU Community Outreach Committee, the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT)
Legislative Committee, and Orem Planning Commission. With the advent of COVID-19 Mr.
Peterson had not had much chance to meet with the UVU or ULCT committees. He said had the
opportunity to attend Planning Commission meetings and was impressed by the Planning staff and
commissioners. He said they did great work for the City, and he particularly wanted to recognize
Jason Bench for his impressive attention to detail and thoroughness.

Mayor Brunst commented that the ULCT was working with Congress to see if they could define
how the stimulus money would be distributed. In particular, smaller Utah cities would prefer to
receive the funding directly, rather than going through the counties. Mr. Davidson commented that
there was no framework in place for something on this scale, and it would be interesting to see
how it would unfold.

     PUBLIC HEARING – The City will receive public comment on the Community
     Development Block Grant (CDBG) Projected Use of Funds for Fiscal Year 2020-2021

Ms. Mathews said this was the first of two public hearings required when the CDBG Commission
made recommendations for grant awards. There was a 30 day comment period between the two
hearings, and then the CDBG Commission would bring the recommendations on May 26th, 2020
for approval. Ms. Mathews said Ken Ransom, CDBG Commission Chair, would later share the
commission recommendations. Ms. Mathews reviewed the qualifications of entitlement
communities, and said they received $700,335 from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban
Development (HUD) and had an additional $114,000 in rollover income. In response to a Council
question, Ms. Mathews said the Trump Administration had attempted to remove the CDBG
funding, but Congress had overturned that decision and the funding was made available. She added
that there was an additional $411,983 they expected to receive as part of the COVID-19 Stimulus
CDBG Funding.

Ms. Mathews spoke to the specific priorities of the City, Provo City, Utah County, and service
agencies. She said they had formed a Joint Commission with the other entitlements for Countywide
Public Services and Capital Projects. Mr. Ransom said the CDBG Commission was included in
the Joint Commission and had the chance to attend meetings and here presentations from the
applicant service agencies. Mr. Ransom reviewed the proposed joint public service projects,
including early head start program funding through Kids on the Move and a receiving center for
Wasatch Mental Health.

Mr. Ransom reviewed the proposals for Orem-only public services, specifically the Orem Police
Department’s Mental Health Response Team. He also addressed non-public services, including
the Orem Critical Home Repair and Home Rehabilitation Program, neighborhood revitalization
activities and park improvements, administration of the City’s CDBG program, and funding for
the City’s Neighborhood Preservation Unit (NPU).

Mayor Brunst thanked Ms. Mathews and Mr. Ransom for the information they shared, and opened
the public hearing for comment. No public comments were submitted electronically, so Mayor
Brunst closed the public hearing and turned time to the City Council for any further questions.

Mrs. Lauret asked if there was a deadline to spend the rollover funding from the previous year.
Ms. Mathews said there was a deadline, but also a set timeframe for correction if the deadline was
missed. Ms. Ransom said the Orem CDBG Commission proposals matched the proposal of the
Joint Commission. Ms. Mathews said the 30 day comment period was now open and they would
return when it was closed with final recommendations.

        PUBLIC HEARING
        ORDINANCE – Amending Section 22-6-9(G)(2) Private Schools and Commercial Day Care
        Services by clarifying language

Mr. Bench said the request before the Council was to clarify language in the City Code. The
language change would amend Section 22-6-9(G)(2) Private Schools and Commercial Day Care
Services to clarify that two commercial day cares could not be within two thousand six hundred
forty (2,640) feet of each other in a residential zone, but a commercial day care in a residential
zone could be within two thousand six hundred forty (2,640) of another commercial day care if
located in a commercial zone. Mr. Bench reviewed information regarding the setback requirements
in residential zones. He said the Planning Commission recommended approval.

Mr. Sumner asked if this was a simple “house cleaning” issue, or if there was pressing need based
on current applications. Mr. Bench said there was a current request for a commercial daycare in a
residential zone; he did not see it as a big issue but more as a clarification.

Mayor Brunst opened the public hearing for comment. No public comments were submitted
electronically, so Mayor Brunst closed the public hearing. There were no further questions from
the Council.

Mayor Brunst moved to approve amending Section 22-6-9(G)(2) Private Schools and
Commercial Day Care Services of the Orem City Code pertaining to proximity of commercial day
care services in a residential zone. Mrs. Lauret seconded the motion. Those voting aye: Richard
F. Brunst, Jeff Lambson, Debby Lauret, Tom Macdonald, Terry Peterson, David Spencer, and
Brent Sumner. The motion passed.

Mr. Bench reviewed the request regarding the PD-50 zone. The purpose of the proposed PD-50
zone was to allow the development of a temple site and a church building. The proposed zone was
designed to be applied only to a parcel of property located at approximately 1465 South Geneva
Road. No other use except churches, synagogues, and temples would be permitted in the proposed
zone.

Development on the parcel would be required to conform to the standards outlined in the text of
the PD-50 zone as well as the concept plan and exhibits included in Appendix XX.

The height of the proposed temple structure was approximately 70 feet to the top of the main
structure and 218 feet, 6 inches to the top of the central spire.

The number of parking stalls required for the proposed zone was based on the City’s existing
standards for churches, synagogues and temples as outlined in Article 22-15 of the City Code
which required one parking stall for every four fixed seats. The proposed temple will have 722
fixed seats and the church building will have 802 fixed seats. Application of the parking standard
for churches and temples therefore required a total of 381 parking stalls. The concept plan for the
site shows a total of 520 stalls with the possibility of adding another 136 stalls if needed in the
future.

Mr. Bench said Tom Heath, Project Manager with the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints,
was on the line to answer questions. Mr. Bench reviewed the proposal and showed the proximity
of the site to University Parkway and the I-15 exchange, FrontRunner, and Geneva Road. He said
1100 West would be extended to the south through the property, and any necessary widening of
the road would be in coordination with UDOT. Mr. Bench showed the rendering provided by the
applicant, and commented that it was not a complete rendering as many details and complete
elevations were still awaiting approval by The Church. He said the applicant’s architects were
comfortable with the code language proposed for the PD-50 zone. Mr. Bench said the Planning
Commission recommended approval of this request.

Mr. Peterson asked about the center spire and whether the Angel Moroni would be added and if
that was included in the proposed height. Mr. Heath said the inclusion of the Angel Moroni on the
spire was in discussion with the Presiding Bishopric and First Presidency of the Church. He added
that not all temples included the Angel Moroni statue at present. Mr. Peterson said he would like
that addition, but understood that the applicant would ultimately make that decision. In response
to Mr. Lambson’s inquiry, Mr. Heath said they would be able to work within the proposed height
with or without Angel Moroni.

Mrs. Lauret asked if there would be delays for the ground breaking. Mr. Heath said they were
aiming for a late summer or early fall timeframe. They were still working through the internal
approvals of the architecture, and if all went according to plan he did not see a reason for any
delay.

Mayor Brunst opened the public hearing for comment. No public comments were submitted
electronically, so Mayor Brunst closed the public hearing. There were no further questions from
the Council.
