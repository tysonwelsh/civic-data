OVERVIEW
The proposal for a Walmart Supercenter and fuel center along Lakeview Parkway, west
of I-15, is being brough forward by Spencer Hymas and South Point Development. This
proposal includes a 176,000 square foot retail center, with a drive-thru pharmacy and
grocery; and a fuel station fronting Lakeview Parkway.

The applicant has been working with the Provo City administration for some time to
develop these plans in helping to meet the city goal of providing a west-side grocery
store. Official application for this project was submitted on February 11 th, 2025.
Department staff have been communicating with the applicant team on specific studies,
standards, and requirements for this to move forward since that time; and an official
CRC review was sent in the first week of March. There are some additional traffic
studies that need to be seen to completion to understand impacts on nearby roadways
and any improvements that would be required due to this project.

The portion of this property that will be used for the Walmart project is approximately
twenty (20) acres of the total thirty-six (36) acre parcel. Subsequent to the Planning
Commission decision and CRC approval, the applicant will need to submit a final
subdivision to record with the County. The remaining sixteen (16) acres are anticipated
to be developed under future applications, which will come before the Planning
Commission and City Council.

The Walmart will face Lakeview Parkway, setback from the road with landscaping,
parking, and the fuel center placed between the supercenter and the parkway. The
parking lot provides for 50% tree shade coverage (at tree maturity) and currently shows
791 parking spaces. The applicant has provided a parking justification study to support
the reduction request from the code standard of 880 spaces to the proposed 791 (a
reduction of 89 stalls). Staff are comfortable with the proposal for reduced parking,
acknowledging the justification study and the experience of the Walmart Corporation in
always providing sufficient parking for their stores.

FINDINGS OF FACT
   1.   The proposal is in the FC3 Zone (Chapter 14.20C, Provo City Code).
   2.   The proposed uses are permitted in the FC3 Zone (Section 14.20C.020).
   3.   The required parking for the combined uses is 880 parking spaces.
   4.   The proposed parking for the site is 791 parking spaces (justification study provided).

CONCLUSIONS
It is a major goal of both the General Plan and of the current Administration, to get
commercial uses, and specifically a grocery store, to the west side of Provo. Getting this
project at this location is why the FC3 Zone was created and adopted for this site.
Careful attention has been paid to the design, colors, and layout of this proposal as the

OVERVIEW
Eric Yergensen is requesting a zone map amendment for approximately thirteen acres
of land near 3410 West Center Street, to go from the A1.10 zone to the AI zone. This
request is being made to match the zone to the west to be able to create a flex/industrial
campus over a total of thirty-eight acres. A nearly identical rezone request was
approved in 2023, but for that applicant not recording the development agreement, this
would already be zoned AI. Staff have attached that report of action for reference.

The concept for this area shows three large warehouses, one to two-stories in height,
totaling approximately 581,000 square feet of new flex space. These buildings and
associated parking lots will be buffered on all sides with a public trail and increased
public amenities against the river.

The land is bordered by the river to the north (with unincorporated Utah County on the
other side), Lakeview Parkway to the east (with single-family residential zoning on the
other side), and Center Street to the south (with agricultural zoned land on the other
side). The General Plan calls for airport related activities on the subject properties and
for those across Center Street, to the south.

STAFF ANALYSIS
The General Plan map for this property was changed from residential to the airport
related designation in 2022, with a zone change and project similar to this request
expected to follow. Justifications from that amendment would hold true for this proposed
zone change, with its proximity to the airport, major roadways, and access to I-15.
Section 14.02.020, Provo City Code, provides specific criteria for staff to show how this
request aligns with the General Plan, as follows: (staff responses in bold)

       Before recommending an amendment to this Title, the Planning Commission
       shall determine whether such amendment is in the interest of the public, and is
       consistent with the goals and policies of the Provo City General Plan. The
       following guidelines shall be used to determine consistency with the General
       Plan:

       (a) Public purpose for the amendment in question.

       Staff Response: The applicant states “This rezone request is intended to
       support the continued economic development and job creation in the area,
       specifically surrounding the Provo Airport. The transformation of these
       parcels into industrial and warehouse spaces will bring significant benefits
       to Utah County while also supporting the growth of Provo and the Provo
       Airport.” Staff agree with the above purposes and in aligning the zone with
       the General Plan due to surrounding uses and the airport. Staff also believe

OVERVIEW
Eric Yergensen is requesting concept plan approval related to a zone map amendment
for approximately thirteen acres of land near 3410 West Center Street. This request is
being made to match the zone to the west to be able to create a flex/industrial campus
over a total of thirty-eight acres. A similar concept plan and rezone request was
approved in 2023, but that applicant never recorded the development agreement, so the
approvals expired. Staff have attached that report of action for reference.

The concept plan for this area shows three large warehouses, one to two-stories in
height, totaling approximately 581,000 square feet of new flex space. These buildings
and associated parking lots will be buffered on all sides with a public trail and increased
public amenities against the river. Parking will need to be determined at a later date
based on uses that come in with the project plans and may be accompanied by parking
reduction requests to the Planning Commission at that time. Though this is only a
concept plan, a parking study on expected uses has been provided and is attached to
this report. In addition to future project plan applications, the owners will need to go
through a final subdivision process as well; if the zone change is approved by the City
Council.

FINDINGS OF FACT
   1. The properties are currently zoned A1.10 (Chapter 14.08, Provo City Code).
   2. The proposed zone is AI (Chapter 14.17D).
   3. Warehouses and offices are permitted uses in the AI Zone (Section 14.17D.020).
   4. Required parking for the proposed buildings could be between 967 to 1,216 spaces
      based on eventual uses within the buildings (Section 14.37.060), but actual numbers will
      be verified at project plan approval and may require a parking reduction request.
   5. The concept plan shows 695 parking spaces, approximately one stall per 834 gross
      square feet of building.

CONCLUSIONS
Conceptually, this plan demonstrates what could come forward if the zone change is
approved. Staff are comfortable with the uses, site, and elevations that have been
provided to this point. Future subdivision and project plan applications will allow staff to
verify that all AI Zone standards are met.

PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
   • There was a large amount of discussion surrounding the Provo River, The Provo River Trail (on the north side of
       the river), and the access along the river through the subject property.
   • The Planning Commission reiterated reasons that the city has moved away from residential uses towards airport
       related activities for the subject property.
   • The Commission discussed the importance of trees along the river through the development of the site, and
       possible connections to other trails in the area.
   • Planning Commissioners expressed a desire to move the buildings and parking further from the river.
   • The Commission and staff acknowledged that the river through this area is more of a “trickle” now that the River
       Delta project has taken the majority of the flow.
   • Planning Commissioners felt that there is a good balance between the open nature of the area to the north that
       will remain that way, and the economic opportunities provided by this request.
   • The Commission was united in wanting to have more trees along the river and more space between the river and
       the parking and building.
   • The river flow and future plans for this section of the river was discussed with David Day (Provo City Engineer).
