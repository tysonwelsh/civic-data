Reports from Other Municipal Service Providers

Unified Police Department

                 Council Member Prokopis stated the Unified Police Department Board of
Directors met last week in an 8-hour retreat and workshop. It discussed making its budget more
transparent. The previous budget was a total of five pages for a $75 million police department.
The new budget will be more in-depth, showing exactly what Magna is paying for and if it is getting
what it is paying for. The UPD is also updating the interlocal agreement. With Herriman City
leaving, there will be gaps in that agreement. The UPD is considering adding weighted voting,
either by a percentage of the budget or by population. The UPD has hired a new Chief Financial
Officer (CFO), Lisa Dudley. Additionally, the Kearns and Magna joint precinct is separating into
two precincts, and there will be two precinct chiefs. The interview and selection process for those
positions will take place the first part of November.

               Council Member Peel asked when the precinct separation was going to take
place.

                Council Member Prokopis stated the precincts will separate immediately, and
funding for the chiefs will be available January 1, 2019. Right now, there is an acting-in chief.

               Sergeant Mike Hill, UPD, separating the precincts will help with communication.
He reported there was a shooting in Kearns this past week, and it involved several officers and
detectives. The situation quickly changed from deadly force to saving a life. None of the officers
were injured, but one person is in critical condition. The investigation is ongoing.

Resolution

               Paul Ashton reviewed the following resolution repealing the stormwater impact
fee from the Magna consolidated fee schedule. The fee does not accomplish the purpose it was
intended for. Collecting and accounting for it is more of a hassle than it is worth, especially since
the fee has to be kept in separate funds:

RESOLUTION NO. 18-10-01                                                     Date: October 23, 2018

        A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL REPEALING
        THE STORMWATER IMPACT FEE FROM THE MAGNA CONSOLIDATED FEE
        SCHEDULE

                                            RECITALS

     WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township (“MAGNA”), as a part of its adoption of the 2018
Magna Budget also adopted the 2018 Magna Consolidated Fee Schedule (the “FEE
SCHEDULE”); and

       WHEREAS, during a cost-benefit review of all fees performed during the summer, it was

to discontinue its imposition on development; therefore, be it RESOLVED, the Magna Metro
Township Council hereby amends the Fee Schedule to delete the Stormwater Impact Fee as a
fee imposed by Magna on property development.

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following requests:

Application #30815 – Adam R. Loser requesting approval of an initial Master Development
Agreement for the development of certain real property, pursuant to the requirements of the
Planned Community (P-C) Zone, for property located at approximately 4000 South and 8400
West.

               Paul Ashton stated the public hearing is still open and Council Members can
continue taking public comment.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Ferguson, moved to close
the public hearing for Application #30815. The motion passed unanimously.

                 Mr. Ashton reviewed Application #30815 and a resolution of the Magna Metro
Township approving and adopting a Master Development Agreement by and between Magna,
D.R. Horton, Kennecott, and the Granite School District for the development of a planned
community located at approximately 4000 South and 8400 West, hereafter known as Little Valley
Gateway. Magna annexed this property, and it is now part of a planned community (P-C) zone.
The Magna Metro Township Planning Commission heard the application and held a public hearing
on October 20, 2018, at which time, a new map of the overpressure zone was presented showing
the standard for the entire proposed subdivision had dropped from 0.3 to 0.2. Under the Master
Development Agreement, this subdivision is subject to the laws that exist as of the adoption of
the Master Development Agreement, and the law still shows the overpressure zone standard at
0.3. So, the developer has to build to meet the 0.3 standard, unless the Council amends the
ordinance. If the Council chose to accept the standard as a condition, he suggested involving all
the entities’ attorneys. The resolution did address other concerns raised by staff.

He walked through each section of the resolution stating that the Magna Metro Township Planning
Commission recommended the attached Master Development Agreement, subject to the
conditions, as set forth in the record, as follows:

   1) The document must be approved “as to form” by legal counsel, with all exhibits attached
      thereto.
   2) Necessary actions need to be taken to show the property is subject to the Planned
      Community Zone and Overpressure Zone Ordinance.

                                               5
DATE             TUESDAY                             OCTOBER                             23, 2018

   3) More details need to be obtained showing the designated minimum square footage with
      specific residential use types.
   4) The Magna Metro Township staff and legal counsel need to review redlined comments in
      the draft submitted by the planning commission and county planner before the Council
      considers and incorporates it.

Some things the planning commission wanted are already in law so are not in the agreement;
however, it did ask about the possibility of reducing the term of the agreement to 20 years.

By approving the resolution, the Council would be authorizing the Mayor to sign the agreement in
substantially the same form as the attached, upon his satisfaction that the conditions have been
or will be fulfilled in those agreements. Then, once he (Mr. Ashton) signs off on the agreement,
the Mayor can sign it; it would not have to come back to the Council.

                Council Member Prokopis stated he assumed that if in 20 years the project had
not been built, all agreements would be off.

              Mr. Adam Loser, applicant, stated the development agreement would not be
enforceable then.

                 Council Member Prokopis asked if there was a big difference between 0.2 and
0.3 standards.

                Mr. Loser stated the difference would be the size of windows, whereby the 0.3
standard would require a smaller window to absorb a blast, as set forth in the recommendation in
the 1987 report, which was a single-pane aluminum window product type. The developer would
be using double-pane argon-filled pressurized vinyl windows and is asking that the 0.2 standard
be reflected in the record. If there were concerns, and the Council wanted bigger windows, the
developer could put in thicker double pane or tempered glass.

                 Council Member Prokopis asked how many total units there would be.

                 Mr. Loser stated 13 and 60.

                 Council Member Peel asked if the Granite School District attorneys were
involved.

                 Mr. Ashton stated he would have to reach out to them.

                   Mr. Loser stated the developer has a tri-party agreement between Kennecott, D.R.
Horton, and the school district, which obligates the developer to solve the road and infrastructure
issues by a prescribed date. The developer submitted the development agreement to the school
district for it to review to make sure that tri-party agreement was being met.

               Council Member Peay asked if the bypass road would be the road that ran under
the school, and what the intent was for 4100 South.

                Mr. Loser stated the road under the school would not be the bypass road. He
anticipated the bypass road would eventually follow the rail line on the south side of the driveway.
Then, 4100 South is contemplated as an access point. The hope is to have that road be the
main lighted intersection, but it will not happen until the property across the street gets developed.
For now, while the first phase is under development, the main entrance between the apartments
and the condos will be the road in front of the high school.

               Council Member Peel asked if the northwest corner would be a dead end.

               Mr. Loser stated there will be two dead ends – one heading north into railroad
property, the other one heading west into Little Valley Gateway, which is owned by Kennecott.
Those will be access points for the future. D.R. Horton is also applying for an underground utility
crossing to tie its storm drain, gas, and sewer into the Elk Run development. Then, the
Department of Transportation and the railroad company are willing to sell it a triangle parcel north
for the at-grade crossing coming down from the cemetery, but that is three to three-and-a-half
years out.

               Council Member Hull asked about the open space plan.

                Mr. Loser stated there will be green in front, a detention pond on the northwest
side, soccer fields, a pool and clubhouse next to the school, a habitat enhancement that will
include a secondary irrigation pond, a park feature for the townhome residents, and some
walking/jogging paths, benches, and maybe a barbeque to the west on the foothills. The center
will be the hub, which is where homes will be sold out of.

               Council Member Prokopis asked what the biggest lot size was.

               Mr. Loser stated detached products, such as townhomes and condominiums will
be a minimum of 800 square feet finished above grade, cottage homes will be 5,000 to 6,000
square feet, and bigger single-family homes will be 8,000 and 12,000 square feet.

                                         RECITALS

      Magna pursuant to Utah Code§ 10-3b-501.

   3. Utah Code § 46-4-201 allows for the use of electronic signatures on legally binding
      documents and contracts, unless otherwise disallowed.

   4. The Utah State Legislature and Government Agencies uses electronic signatures for the
      effective and efficient processing of legislation and other legal and administrative work of
      the State.

   5. Municipal Code Online is a Corporation that hosts the Magna Code of Ordinances, and
      among the features of its software can allow for the secure use of electronic signatures in
      the approval of Ordinances pertaining to changes, additions, and/or repeals of Magna's

   6. Certain legal and business documents processed by the administrative agents of Magna
      require the efficiency and effectiveness provided by using electronic signatures.
   7. It would be a benefit to Magna and its residents to use this regulate and use feature.

                                         RESOLUTION

      BE IT THEREFORE RESOLVED, the Magna Metro Township Council does hereby
adopt the following ordinance, to be added to the Magna Metro Township Code of
Ordinances:

                      CHAPTER 2.85 - ELECTRONIC SIGNATURES

2.85.010-Purpose

       The ordinance codified in this chapter regulates the use of electronic signatures by
elected officials and other parties in approving legislation, contracts, and/or other legally-
binding documents of Magna.

2.85.020- Uses of Electronic Signatures Allowed

       In any document accepted by Magna in which a signature is required or used, Magna
may authorize the use of an electronic signature so long as it complies with the requirements
of the most currently adopted version of Utah Code § 46-4: Uniform Electronic Transactions
Act.

2.85.030 - Rules Regarding Force and Effect of Electronic Signatures

       The use of an electronic signature shall have the same force and effect as the use of
a "wet" or manual signature if:

       A.     The signature is capable of verification;

       B.     The signature is under the sole control of the person using it; and


                                               12
DATE               TUESDAY                            OCTOBER                               23, 2018


         C.     The signature is linked to the data in such a manner that it is readily
         ascertainable if the data is changed after the signature is applied.

2.85.040-Determination of Approved Technologies, Vendors, and Uses of Electronic
Signatures

        Upon the recommendations of Staff, the Council shall determine and approve
acceptable technologies and vendors under this section consistent with industry best
practices to ensure the security and integrity of the data and the signature. The Administrator
shall further determine and recommend to the Council the documents for which Magna will accept
electronic signatures.

2.85.050 - Enactment Date

This Ordinance shall be effective on the 31st day after the date of its adoption by the Council.
