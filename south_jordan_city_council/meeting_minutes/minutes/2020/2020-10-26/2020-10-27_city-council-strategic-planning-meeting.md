       A. Welcome and Introduction to Electronic Meeting – By Mayor Dawn R. Ramsey

Mayor Ramsey welcomed everyone present. At this meeting, they are looking at high level
strategic priorities for the next year.

Mayor Ramsey introduced the electronic meeting. She noted that all members of the public are
invited.

       B.      Invocation – By City Manager, Gary Whatcott

CM Whatcott offered the invocation.

       C.      Discussion: Strategic Priorities, Revised Strategic Goals and Objectives, and
               future action items for FY 2021-22

CM Whatcott said this is the foundation for the budget. He said they want to make sure that their
goals are correct. So far, the city is doing well financially. They want to make sure things keep
going well. There will be challenging times in the future to keep service levels high and
determine the appropriate levels of taxation.

Strategic Services Director Tingey reviewed a prepared presentation (Attachment A).

Council Member Shelton asked what is meant by inflation effect? CM Whatcott said the last
time that the city added any new revenue from property taxes was 2007. It costs more to run the
city now versus then, and they have not picked up any new tax dollars to pay for that. They are
not capturing the inflationary factors of growth. It was noted that there was a tax increase, but it
paid for debt service. It did not go to the general fund.

Council Member Shelton asked if the level of service is lower today than in 2007? CM Whatcott
said yes. They have added many acres of new parks. They have added only a small number of
staff members. They are being as efficient as they can, but they cannot meet the demand. He said
anything funded out of the general fund, including police, fire, streets, and public works is short
staffed.

Council Member Shelton asked if there has been an effect on response time? CM Whatcott said
the case load continues to grow more than what they are adding on the service side. Police Chief
Carr said the response times have gone up 1 minute 40 seconds since 2015. Part of that is a
function of the city because they have to respond further away.

Council Member Shelton asked if they are adding more expenses or more revenue? CFO Naidu
said a new home being built in the city costs the city $750-$800. The property tax it generates is
$380 on an average $400,000 home. He said the revenue versus expenditures do not add up in
that way.

CM Whatcott said they are not growing the pie like they were in earlier years. They are not
adding new shopping centers and a lot of retail stores. They receive revenue from online sales
now, but it is at a different rate than if they were shopping at the stores. They have a lot of
leakage that is occurring in places like Herriman.

Council Member Marlor asked what is the cost versus revenue for a commercial property? CFO
Naidu said commercial pays 100 percent of their value. The tax revenue they generate is higher
compared to a residential property.

CM Whatcott said they need to keep that in mind when making land use changes. There are
fiscal consequences, both positive and negative. He said retail has changed over what it was in
2007. He reiterated that they are in good standing financially, but they are not growing revenue
fast enough to meet service needs.

Mr. Tingey reviewed the list of items under the situation section. That list was generated by staff.
Mayor Ramsey said that was a comprehensive list.

Mr. Tingey reviewed the list of core competencies by staff. They discussed the high household
income in South Jordan, which is good, but also comes with high expectations for service.

Mr. Tingey reviewed the list of obstacles. Council Member Marlor said one large obstacle is
legislation and resulting state regulations. He said it is an obstacle from a day to day taxation
standpoint and potentially in future density determinations. Council Member Harris concurred,
but said they have made significant improvements on that obstacle by having a seat at several
tables.

Mayor Ramsey said having a seat at the table with other agencies is a core competency. They
have more of a seat at the table than any other city she is aware of. She agreed that there is not a
lot that they can do with mandated legislation, but their lobbying has resulted in some better
outcomes. She said South Jordan has the opportunity to be more influential. Council Member

Harris concurred. Council Member Zander said that can also be a prospect. There may be times
that they need to use that opportunity with the legislature.

CM Whatcott said South Jordan has a lot of credibility and is looked to as a place that does it
right.

Council Member McGuire said he feels daybreak fits under core competency and obstacles. He
said they have made some progress but there will still be obstacles moving forward. One other
thing they need to work toward is being a unified city.

Mr. Tingey reviewed the list under prospects. CM Whatcott said the Daybreak Town Center
could be considered an obstacle, not a prospect. They were originally told that there would be a
lot of commercial buildings. He is fearful that they will see Daybreak getting to their 20,000
units, and it will be filled with high density housing and they will only get service oriented
businesses. That will not help them pay the freight. Mayor Ramsey concurred.

Council Member Marlor said they approved the bonds for infrastructure so Daybreak could be
ready to provide commercial areas that are looking for space. He said he has not heard them talk
about it once since the bonds were approved. CM Whatcott said the CDA is not producing any
money. It is not providing the vision the city had for it. The default mode for Daybreak is
housing. He is concerned that they are not bringing in commercial that will help the city with
sustainability. Mr. Tingey concurred.

Council Member Shelton said the city has some bonds that are maturing in the next couple of
years. That could provide a prospect of financing a project. CM Whatcott said it may help with
revenue for the general fund. CFO Naidu said the only bond that is coming to mature in the next
few years is the water bond, and that money will go to the enterprise fund.

Mr. Tingey reviewed the list of expectations.

Council Member Zander said one expectation is the new Bingham Creek park and how that will
affect the city, and how that will be structured and managed.

CM Whatcott said he feels the residents will have a higher expectation for recreational
opportunities, such as trails and team obstacle course races.

Mayor Ramsey said a lot of people continue to have the expectation that Mulligans will be
preserved.

Council Member Harris said there is a huge expectation regarding growth and good traffic flow.
Mayor Ramsey concurred. She recommended they add air quality under sustainability initiatives.

Council Member Harris said they need to balance the conflicting land use densities, as the
residents have an expectation for less density, and the legislature has an expectation for more
density.

CM Whatcott said there will also be future expectations to be more green including electronic
cars, solar panels, etc.

Mr. Tingey reviewed the strategic priorities.

Council Member Shelton recommended they rename capital improvement to reliable public
infrastructure. Mr. Tingey, CM Whatcott, and Mayor Ramsey said they like that title better.

Mayor Ramsey noted that Riverton City is now using South Jordan’s strategic priorities as a
template.

Mr. Tingey reviewed the objectives under safe community.

The City Council added listening under the responds line objective. They also determined to add
that they enforce the law respectfully. Council Member Harris said having a healthy community
(mental health and physical health) provides a safe community. The City Council added some
language about health and well-being.

Mayor Ramsey encouraged them to keep the DARE program. CM Whatcott said that falls under
the engaged part of the safe community. The City Council determined to add some language
regarding providing education throughout the community. They added language about listening
to concerns.

City Attorney Loose asked about including emergency preparedness in the city. ACM Lewis said
that is a task under protects the public.

The City Council reviewed objectives under reliable public infrastructure. Council Member
Shelton said a lot of the infrastructure that goes in the city is not built by the city. They added
language about overseeing and regulating the work to build infrastructure.

They added language about ensuring funding by working with multiple stakeholders.

Mr. Tingey noted the list of projects. He said that will be prioritized as part of the budget
process.

Council Member Zander said there is a need for cell phone towers on the west side of the city.
That is not their responsibility, but how do they move that needle? Mr. Tingey said word is
getting out. He understands Daybreak and AT&T are in discussions.

Council Member McGuire asked about the $200,000 for Christmas lights. CM Whatcott said
many of their lights are old and broken. He said this is to replace the lights and to try to get a
reasonable amount of lights. The plan is to concentrate the lights around city hall. He said they
want to celebrate the holiday, but they are also criticized because it is a religious holiday. They
still feel it is appropriate to celebrate for their community. The Hanukkah display in city hall was
noted. There may be need for more diversity in the future.

Council Member Shelton said on upcoming projects, they need to add paving of the Bingham
creek trail from 4800 West to the trail. Also, the trail that crosses the creek and goes to Welby
Elementary needs to be paved. He said he also did not see a lap pool facility and the city’s
contribution to that project. He also noted that he did not see the parks/ball diamonds in
Daybreak on the list.

CM Whatcott said the parks in Daybreak are not on the list because it is over the 3-5 year period.
Mr. Tingey said there is a design number in park impact fees for Daybreak parks. The lap pool is
on the 5 year plan. He said the $1 million city contribution is reserved. That project is 3-4 years
out. Mayor Ramsey said the money that the County intended to contribute is not available right
now, due to Covid. She said that project will still get done.

CM Whatcott noted a collector street wall issue brought up by a resident in Council Member
Marlor’s district. They can add that to the list and see how that is prioritized. Council Member
Marlor concurred to add that to the list, and they can address that at the appropriate time.

The City Council reviewed the objectives under balanced regulatory environment.

The City Council reviewed the objectives under desirable amenities and open space. They added
art to the line about partnering with community stakeholders. They discussed who the various
community stakeholders are. Council Member Zander said they need to considering coupling
arts with businesses, and getting some corporate sponsors for the arts. CM Whatcott said one
holdback for the city is that they are lacking a venue. Mayor Ramsey concurred that there are
things that will require a public/private partnership. They could consider that in other arenas like
a lap pool.

The City Council reviewed objectives under the economic development section. Council
Member Harris noted CM Whatcott’s earlier comment that changing the land use on property
from commercial to residential will affect their economic development.

The City Council determined to combine the language in items 1 and 2. They added coordination
with stakeholders in item 4.

Council Member Harris asked how to make the commitments of the stakeholders stronger
regarding economic development? CM Whatcott said there is a delicate balance of the property
owners’ rights and the city’s needs for overall zoning. Council Member Harris said he does not
want to see the prime commercial land going away when the housing market gets hot.

City Attorney Loose said the method to control that is through the city’s zoning power. Planning
Director Schaefermeyer said another question is how to structure conversations with developers
so the City Council and staff feel empowered to say no, and indicate that they have to stick with
the current zoning. He said there is a balance between benchmarks and giving enough flexibility
for their developments.

CM Whatcott said if the city was given other mechanisms other than property tax, they could
leave the land use element out of it. He noted that in other states, cities share income tax. He

said the city needs to have a diverse portfolio; that is done out of necessity and good planning
efforts, not just because of tax revenue. Council Member Marlor said they need to hard coat
some commercial on the next MBA. He said the residential component will always be there.

The City Council reviewed the objectives under sustainable growth. They determined to take
item 3 and include that language, split between item 2 and 6. They also determined to add air
quality to item 2.

The City Council reviewed the objectives under engaged community. They determined to add
language to item 4, encouraging people to run for office and to vote. They also added language
regarding service.

The City Council reviewed the objectives under fiscally responsible governance. There were no
changes.

CM Whatcott said staff will build the budget on these priorities.

         D.        Summarize Next Steps: FY2021-22 Strategic Planning and Budget

CFO Naidu reviewed plans for the upcoming budget meetings. They will be held January 27th,
February 10th, and February 24th (if needed). The tentative budget will be approved in March.
The final budget will be approved in May. They determined to start the upcoming budget
meetings at 5:30 p.m.

Council Member Shelton announced his retirement from his profession at the end of this year,
and indicated that he may be traveling in early January.

CFO Naidu indicated that he is available to meet with the City Council to discuss any budgetary
questions.

The City Council determined to cancel the December 15, 2020 City Council meeting, unless
there is something critical that needs to be discussed. They also discussed having a social dinner,
when conditions are appropriate to do so.

ADJOURNMENT

Council Member Shelton made a motion to adjourn. Council Member McGuire seconded
the motion. The vote was unanimous in favor.

The October 27, 2020 City Council strategic planning meeting adjourned at 7:49 p.m.

South Jordan City provides fiscally efficient and effective governance through best practices,
innovation, program evaluation, competitive pay, professionalism, and continuous improvement


• FRG-1. Workforce: ATTRACTS, motivates, develops and retains a high-quality, engaged and
  productive workforce.
• FRG-2. Transparency: FOSTERS fiscal responsibility, operational excellence, trust and
  transparency by ensuring accountability, efficiency and innovation in all operations.
• FRG-3. Resource Alignment: PROTECTS, manages, optimizes and invests in its human,
  financial, physical and technology resources to ensures alignment with planning and
  budget
• FRG-4. Regulatory Compliance: ASSURES regulatory and policy compliance to minimize and
  mitigate risk.
• FRG-5. Communication: PROVIDES responsive and accessible leadership, facilitates timely
  and effective two-way communication and utilizes input from all stakeholders.
• FRG-6. Vision & Planning: SUPPORTS decision-making with timely and accurate short-term
  and long-range analysis that enhances vision and planning.
