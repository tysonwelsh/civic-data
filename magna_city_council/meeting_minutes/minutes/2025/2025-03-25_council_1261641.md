5. STAKEHOLDER REPORTS
A. Unified Police Department
Lt. Shane Manwaring introduced Lt. Chris Benedict, a newly promoted watch commander
responsible for overseeing law enforcement operations when administrative staff are off duty.
Lt. Chris Benedict provided his contact information and emphasized his commitment to
ensuring the community remains safe and well-maintained. Lt. Manwaring then presented an
overview of the "Broken Windows" theory (Attachments A and B), explaining its origins from a
1982 article by James Wilson and George Kelly. He shared a personal anecdote to illustrate
the concept and explained how minor neglect, such as an unfixed broken window or
abandoned tires, can escalate into larger issues. He emphasized the importance of proactive
policing and crime prevention efforts in the community.
Council Member Trish Hull expressed concerns about the implementation of the Broken
Windows theory, referencing past instances where it led to controversial policing practices,
such as stop-and-frisk policies. She emphasized the importance of community members
feeling safe, regardless of statistical crime data, and supported efforts to clean up neglected
areas while avoiding problematic law enforcement tactics.
Lt. Manwaring acknowledged these concerns, citing studies showing that while crime rates did
not always decrease with police presence, residents still reported feeling safer. He highlighted
recent efforts, including a meeting with various departments to address community issues
collaboratively while maintaining dignity and respect.
Council Member Hull reiterated her trust in the local police department but cautioned against
excessive enforcement practices that had previously caused issues in other cities. Lt.
Manwaring agreed, noting that reforms had been made to avoid such problems in the past.

Council Member Trish Hull noted that the previously discussed spending threshold for P-cards,
requiring mayoral approval for any expense over $2,500, might be too low given the scale of
grants the city manages. She suggested increasing the limit but were open to postponing
approval to the next meeting to allow time for review.

situations like travel. She referenced an incident where staff were stranded in Chicago and
required emergency funding for hotel accommodations. In that case, they were able to contact
Brian Hartsell, who manages P-card approvals, to temporarily raise the spending limit.

Mayor Eric Barney asked if the council had any questions.
Council Member Audrey Pierce stated that $2,500 is a common P-card limit in government,
though exceptions can be made when necessary. She suggested the MOU could specify
conditions where the limit could be temporarily raised rather than increasing the base amount.

A. Discussion and Potential Action Regarding Ordinance 2025-0-01, Amending
Sections14.12.060, Sidewalks; 18.12.050, Lots; 19.50.060, Landscaping of Park
Strips; 18.14.080,Street Lighting; Clarifying Language on Pavement, Turf, Plants
and Ground Cover, Street Lighting Requirements, and Trees in Park Strips — Brian
Tucker, Planning Manager
Brian Tucker presented updates based on prior council input, particularly addressing concerns
over gravel size and inorganic mulch. He proposed limiting gravel to half an inch or smaller to
prevent potential hazards, while allowing boulders over 20 pounds.
Mayor Eric Barney expressed concern that restricting certain rock sizes might be too limiting
and noted that cobble is commonly used in park strips.
Council Member Audrey Pierce shared concerns about children throwing rocks, citing past
incidents of broken windows.
Brian Tucker confirmed that weed barriers would be required for inorganic mulch but not for
organic mulch, as it naturally decomposes. He also incorporated street tree guidelines,
considering root depth, canopy height, and branch structure to avoid interference with
sidewalks and infrastructure. Mayor Barney requested the removal of Blackhaw Viburnum from
the approved tree list, as it is not readily available, and suggested adding Zelcova varieties,
which are currently considered one of the best street trees.
Council members then raised concerns about inconsistent enforcement of street lighting and
tree requirements, particularly in new developments.
Council Member Trish Hull pointed out that a recently completed development lacked street
lighting despite bond requirements.
Council Member Sudbury noted a similar issue behind a 7-11.
Mayor Barney explained that developers are required to take out a bond to ensure
infrastructure completion, but enforcement has been challenging. In some cases, developers
delay installing required lighting, hoping to get the bond released without fulfilling obligations.
Dan Torres committed to tracking these issues more closely now that engineering is under the
MSD, and he requested details on specific developments for follow-up.

Mayor Eric Barney noted that this topic had been discussed at the previous meeting and asked
if there were further comments.
Council Member Prokopis inquired whether additional cities had opted for Ranked Choice
Voting to help reduce shared costs.
David Brickey responded that only one more city had joined, bringing the total to five. While
Ranked Choice Voting could be more cost-effective in terms of ballot printing due to
eliminating the need for a primary, the education costs associated with voter training could be
significant—up to $10,000 for mailers. He cited issues from Sandy City’s past experience,
where misinformation led to ballots being voided when voters failed to rank multiple choices.
Council Member Prokopis questioned why additional education was necessary if instructions
accompanied the ballot.
David Brickey elaborated that Sandy had not anticipated the extent of voter confusion, which
resulted in many ballots being invalidated. He suggested that if the city chose Ranked Choice
Voting, it should collaborate with other participating cities to ensure a unified education effort.

violations. The goal was to present the parking component of Title 11 to the council on April 8
and potentially implement new regulations by the end of April. The subcommittee aimed to
simplify winter parking rules, which currently vary by date and location.
Council Member Hull observed that fines ranged from $25 to $100, with the opinion that $25

improper corner parking, should incur higher penalties. There was also discussion about the
need to educate residents on public vs. HOA-controlled streets, as different parking regulations
apply.

Mayor Eric Barney — provided a detailed update on the MSD budget season, discussing a
recent budget presentation from Public Works. The proposed budget for Public Works is $12
million for the MSD cities, with $3.7 million allocated for variable costs like materials and $8.3
million for fixed costs, including employee wages and street maintenance. He emphasized that
Magna’s roads are in the worst condition within the MSD, with many classified as yellow
(needing maintenance) and a significant portion in orange or red (likely requiring complete -
reconstruction). A discussion about the road maintenance budget revealed that while other
cities have not experienced significant increases in road surface, Magna has added
considerable new paved roads without corresponding increases in funding over the past eight
years. A follow-up plan is being proposed to address these concerns.
Council Member Hull inquired about snow removal, and Mayor Barney clarified that there is a
plan for snow removal, with priority roads identified as high need. However, there was some
uncertainty about when exactly snow removal should be triggered, as Public Works stated it
depends on safety concerns but did not specify thresholds for when plowing would begin.
Mayor Barney also discussed the MSD’s general fund and capital fund budgets, projecting a
slight increase for fiscal year 2026. However, after accounting for one-time expenses in 2025,
the operational budget increase is closer to $2 million. He highlighted concerns about cities like
Brighton and Copperton requesting substantial increases to their administrative budgets,
despite having their own revenue generation capabilities, raising the issue of whether the MSD
should fund these requests or whether cities should use their own funds. Regarding the $5
million reserve, Mayor Barney explained that statutory funds like sales tax and B & C road
funds are designated for specific services, such as road maintenance. He also noted that road’
funds are distributed based on the classification of roads and population size, which means
that Magna, with significant development, generates substantial revenue. However, he
expressed the importance of the MSD focusing on its core services and cities like Brighton and
