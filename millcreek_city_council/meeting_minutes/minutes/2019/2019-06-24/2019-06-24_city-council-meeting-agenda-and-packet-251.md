a. ZM-19-009, Rezone from R-1-8 to R-1-5 for an 8-unit PUD Development Located at
4236/4248 South 900 East; Francis Lilly, Community Development Director
Francis Lilly said the rezone was a marginal increase in density but was similar to
surrounding uses. The staff recommended time to work out a development agreement to
guide the terms of the rezone and the subsequent development plat. He said the existing
parcels each contain a single-family dwelling that have separate access on 900 East.
Neighborhood feedback suggested that in addition to the subject properties being under
maintained, there have been numerous complaints about previous tenant activity and
nuisances. The density difference between the zones is 3 units. He showed a rendering of
the project as a potential gated community. The final project review will be technical in
nature, but the rezone needs to occur first. The applicant has supplied concepts of what the
houses might look like. The maximum lot coverage for the proposal is 49%, which is less
than the required 60%. The total open space for the proposal is 51%, which is more than
the required minimum of 40%. There will be 14 guest parking stalls; 8 driveway stalls and
6 separate parking stalls. The off-street parking requirements will not change. The
neighborhood feedback expressed concern about height of homes and traffic, but there was
excitement that the area would be redeveloped. Staff recommended that height not exceed
30 feet per fire code. He said that the proposed plan would have little to no impact on the
existing level of service for 900 East. Lilly said 900 East is slated for improvements and
widening; the project will have one access for entrance and exit, eliminating an existing
driveway; the project is subject to a small road dedication and public improvements that
consist of new curb, gutter and sidewalk, and possible storm drain improvements; and
Public Works is looking into painting driveway approaches in the immediate area red
indicating a no parking zone as to protect the line of sight when entering onto 900 East.

The Millcreek Community Council and Planning Commission unanimously recommended
approval of the project. The Planning Commission recommended that the rezone be subject
to approval of a development agreement with the following conditions found in their staff
report:
e Limited to 8 two-bedroom units as indicated in site plan;
Limited to a maximum of 28 feet in height;
e Project must meet the minimum off street parking requirement under Chapter 19.80 of the
Millcreek Code for an 8-unit Planned Unit Development;
e Project must meet the minimum landscaping requirements under Chapter 19.77 of
Millcreek Code;
e Dark sky compliant street lighting only;
e Upgraded fencing with landscaping and shrubs on the perimeter of the project for privacy
of the neighbors;
e Any tree that is removed due to development must be replaced with two trees;
e Development must have CC&Rs that at a minimum include:
o Residents under HOA restraints may not have loud or obnoxious events
o Pets must be leashed at all times;
o No guest parking outside of PUD boundaries
e Any existing ditches and waterways will be protected and/or upgraded (including the use of
underground culverts as appropriate per the City Engineer);
e Receive conditional use approval for an 8-unit Planned Unit Development and meet all
applicable zoning, building, health, fire, and safety requirements;
e Obtain subdivision plat approval and comply with all technical review requirements
required by staff and Millcreek Subdivision Code; and

e Satisfy any property and road dedication that is in public right-of-way

b. ZM-19-006, Rezone from R-2-8 to RM Subject to a Development Agreement for a 22
Townhome PUD Located at 950 Grape Ivy Way; Erin O’Kelley, Planner
Mayor Pro Tem Uipi said the Council did not typically allow public comment in the work
session, but they would allow it because this was an important project to the area. She said
the public could provide comment during this meeting or the regular meeting at 7:00 p.m.
Erin O’Kelley said the proposed 22 units would be 3 stories tall and there would be three
amenities. The future land use map of the General Plan designates the area as
Neighborhood 2, which includes single-family to multi-family from 1-3 stories with a
density of 6-18 units. There was a neighborhood meeting on March 29, 2019. The applicant
then put the application on hold to mitigate some of the concerns raised during that
meeting. The Millcreek Community Council saw the application on May 7, 2019 and
recommended denial of the rezone because of the high density. The Planning Commission
reviewed the application on May 15, 2019 and they also recommended denial because the
density was too high for the property.

Michael Brodsky, Hamlet Development, said when he met with Millcreek staff, the
application was well-received. He said the surrounding developments are zoned RM. He
was surprised that the project became controversial. He felt the proposal was appropriate at
22 units per the surrounding developments. He said the 30-foot right-of-way was adequate
per the Fire Department’s opinion and the impact to the street would be the same as if
Hamlet built 14 duplexes. Hamlet has offered to take on the full maintenance of the access
road. The developer has received permission from the neighboring apartments to install a
crash gate. He said the current property owner owned the surrounding parcels and as they
were sold off, they were rezoned with the zone designated by the General Plan at the time.
He pointed out that the opposing HOA development is zoned RM. He read a letter from
Rebecca Russell dated June 6, 2019 (see attached). He asked the Council to approve the
rezone request subject to a development agreement.

presentation.
