 Finally, with respect to Affordable Housing incentives, Ms. Murillo reported that the current City




                                                                  20
 structure was found in the Affordable Master Planned Development (“AMPD”) process. The
 AMPD process provides incentives for projects that provide over half of the units as affordable to
 households earning no more than 80% of the Area Median Income (“AMI”).




                                                         7.
 Ms. Murillo explained that they proposed to replace the existing AMPD with a tiered structure that
 would provide more flexibility. Rather than a “one size fits all” approach, they looked at flexibility
 across different tiers that would allow more opportunity for projects to qualify for incentives, with

                                                 .2
 the overall goal of achieving more affordable and attainable housing. She presented the tiers to
 the Commission and highlighted that they included adjustments in terms of the level of affordability
 as well as the set aside of those affordable units. She explained that the lowest tier, Tier 1 would
                                      03
 provide an opportunity for projects that set aside half of the units to an average of 100% AMI. Tier
 1 would qualify for the lowest level of incentives.

 Ms. Murillo explained that the most significant tier, Tier 4, would provide opportunities to qualify
 whether they had average AMIs of 60% to the traditional 80% AMI. She noted that the set aside
                        ed


 would be adjusted across the tiers so there would be more opportunities to access the incentives.
 She explained that the rationale behind this proposal was that more incentives would be granted
 to developments that provide more housing to lower-income households. Having lower income
 and deeper affordability would provide opportunity for more substantial incentives. She added
             v



 that they did not propose any changes to the incentives for Building Height, Parking, and Open
 Space. However, these incentives would be adjusted based on the tier.
          ro




 Ms. Murillo highlighted that they were looking at treating the General Commercial (“GC”) zone

   p




 that Bonanza Park is an area that is more appropriate for increased height. She emphasized that
 a very important incentive for affordable housing is density. The AMPD does not contain a density
Ap




 limit; rather, they addressed the building envelope as a way to get additional units to help

 explained that being able to provide a larger building envelope would allow the production of more
 units and would also allow for additional revenue support through the market-rate units to
 subsidize the additional provision of affordable units.
