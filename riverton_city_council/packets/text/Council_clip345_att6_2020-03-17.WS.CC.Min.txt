33                 Systems.
34
35   Mayor Staggs introduced William Richardson from Pure Stream and Total Water Management
36   (“TWM”).
37
38   Mr. Richardson thanked the City for the opportunity to present at the meeting and stated that
39   TWM has a myriad of technologies. They analyze water problems in various environments and
40   assess what problems can be solved. He identified the following four challenges TWM
41   encountered as they assessed Riverton City’s current water situation:
42
43           1.    The City’s desire to utilize their own water.
44
45           2.    The water system needs to be redundant, scalable and robust to meet the
46                 expectation of the life usage of the system.
47
48           3.    The cost of the water system.
49
50           4.    The water system needs to be certifiable.

 8   President and CEO of TWM, Neil Richardson, reviewed the technology behind the company’s
 9   water systems and the history of the company. He explained that the company has two separate
10   portions, one being Pure Streams, which is the industrial type of water management and the other
11   being TWM where water logistics is more significant. He explained that water logistics are suited
12   for Riverton City because of the City’s distributed source. The Council viewed a video about the
13   company.

15   Neil Richardson recommended that Riverton City use the Reverse Osmosis (“RO”) system, in
16   conjunction with the company’s Version F treatment. The Analytics Specialist for TWM reported
17   that the RO system meets the requirements and needs of the City. He reviewed other possible
18   options and explained why they were not appropriate for Riverton City. A picture of an RO system
19   was displayed. A flow chart of Riverton City’s potential wells was displayed and explained. He
20   reviewed the City’s water characteristics and the process of the RO system. 98.8% of total water
21   pulled from the well is sent for culinary use. The remaining 1.2% is disposed of.
22
23   Mayor Staggs reported that in the year 2013, Corolla Water Engineers prepared a report
24   analyzing water treatment methods and RO was one of them. The estimate to implement a
25   concentrated plant to service the City’s wells was at least $50,000,000. The most significant issue
26   at that time was handling the concentrate. This concentrator from TWM is a solution that is key.
27   The size and mobility of the system placed on a well is unique to the system seven years ago.
28
29   The Specialist stated that the package TWM is offering is being evaluated on a four-well site
30   basis.

33   that with proper maintenance the system can last up to 20 years. Council Member Stewart
34   recognized the importance of preparing for the replacement of the RO system in 20 years. Neil
35   Richardson stated that the TWM team implements improvements during the lifespan on the
36   system to help prevent the need to replace it after 20 years.
37
38   William Richardson stated that TWM’s business model does not require any capital expenditures
39   from the City. They provide the design, build, maintenance, and operation of the equipment over
40   the course of the contract. TWM does not require City funding to implement the system. As TWM
41   went through the evaluation process, they were mindful of the maintenance that would be required
42   for this system. William Richardson stated that TWM was careful about the water process they
43   selected for Riverton City.
44
45   William Richardson stated that TMW has the ability to assess any water issue and has a unique
46   advantage as they assess. TMW has the capability to be forward-thinking and prepare for the
47   future of their water systems. TWM provides four acre-foot pricing through a service contract they
