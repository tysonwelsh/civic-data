      A. RECEIVE PUBLIC COMMENT REGARDING AUTHORIZING A ZONE MAP
          AMENDMENT LOCATED AT 52, 30, & 24 W CENTER ST AND 7710 S
          STATE ST FROM TRANSIT-ORIENTED DEVELOPMENT ZONE TO
          TRANSIT-ORIENTED DEVELOPMENT OVERLAY ZONE.
Elizabeth Arnold said the project proposal involves a rezone of 4 parcels totaling
approximately 3.46 acres. The applicant’s justification for the request and supporting
documents are included in the attachments.

Midvale City Code 17-3-1(E.) outlines the criteria necessary for granting a rezone as
follows:

17-3-1 Criteria/Required Findings. …rezoning of land is to be discouraged and allowed
only under the limited circumstances herein described. Therefore, the planning
commission may recommend, and the city council may grant a rezoning application only
if it determines, in written findings, that the proposed rezoning is consistent with the
policies and goals of the general plan and that the applicant has demonstrated that the:

      1. Proposed rezoning is necessary either to comply with the general plan proposed
         land use map or to provide land for a community need that was not anticipated at

      the time of adoption of the general plan;
   2. Existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or that
      it failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the natural
      characteristics of the land, including but not limited to, steep slopes, flood plain,
      unstable soils, and inadequate drainage; or
   3. Land or its surrounding environs has changed or is changing to such a degree that
      it is in the public interest to encourage redevelopment of the area or to recognize
      the changed character of the area.

Staff has reviewed the proposal against the criteria listed above and finds that the rezone
request satisfies the first and third criteria. The request satisfies the first criterion because
the proposed rezone is consistent with the goals and policies of the Midvale City General
Plan, specifically the vision for the Middle State Street Opportunity Area.

The Midvale City General Plan identifies nine Opportunity Areas. Opportunity Areas are
where changes in the types and/or intensities of current land uses are anticipated. In
these areas, denser residential uses, mixed residential and commercial uses, and larger,
taller buildings are anticipated to take advantage of the transportation infrastructure and
to generate more tax revenue for the City.

The 2016 Midvale City General Plan identifies the subject property as being in the Middle
State Street Opportunity Area and lists eight goals for the area:
   1. Support development of higher-value commercial and business uses through the
       development of mixed-use and higher density residential uses
   2. Develop a small area plan for the development near the TRAX station
   3. Support and encourage development to take advantage of transit and
       transportation opportunities
   4. Focus commercial and mixed-use development at the nodes of 7500 South and
       7720/7800 South
   5. Support redevelopment of the underutilized commercial properties between the
       commercial nodes into mixed-use residential development
   6. Explore the potential for unifying urban-design elements and beautification of the
       area
   7. Employ design elements to buffer the effects of higher intensity uses in the core
       areas on adjacent existing residential uses
   8. Strengthen pedestrian connections to the Center Street TRAX Station

The proposed rezone is consistent with several goals for the Middle State Street
Opportunity Area. Specifically, the proposed rezone would facilitate higher-density and
mixed-use development (Goal 1), leverage the site’s proximity to transit infrastructure
(Goal 3), support concentration of development near identified nodes (Goal 4), and
encourage redevelopment of underutilized properties into mixed-use residential
development (Goal 5).

The proposal meets the third criterion in that the surrounding area has experienced
incremental redevelopment and increased intensity of land uses. These changes indicate

       B. RECEIVE PUBLIC COMMENT REGARDING AN AMENDMENT TO TITLE 17
           OF THE MIDVALE CITY MUNICIPAL CODE RELATING TO HOME-BASED
           MICROSCHOOLS AND MICRO-EDUCATION ENTITIES.
Elizabeth Arnold said this text amendment implements S.B. 13 from the 2024 Utah
Legislative General Session. The amendment adds definitions for “Home-based
microschool” and “Micro-education entity,” establishes new chapters within
Supplementary Regulations to govern these uses, and updates the use tables to permit
both uses in all zones.

Public notice has been sent to affected entities as required in 17-3-9.B of the Municipal
Code. No comments have been received as of the writing of this report.

-ZONING CODE AMENDMENT CRITERIA-

Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) outlines the criteria necessary for amendments to the zoning
code. A proposal may only be approved if it demonstrates one or more of the following:
   1. The proposed amendment promotes the objectives of the general plan and
      purposes of this title;
   2. The proposed amendment promotes the purposes outlined in Utah State Code
      10-20-101;
   3. The proposed amendment more clearly explains the intent of the original
      language or has been amended to make interpretation more straightforward; or
   4. Existing zoning code was the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact.

Staff finds that this proposal meets the first and second criteria listed above. The
amendment promotes the objectives of the General Plan by supporting a broader range
of educational opportunities and allowing flexible educational models within residential
neighborhoods. The amendment also advances the purposes outlined in Utah State Code
10-20-101 by promoting the health, safety, and general welfare of residents through clear
operational standards for home-based microschools and micro-education entities.

       C. RECEIVE PUBLIC COMMENT REGARDING A ZONING CODE TEXT
           AMENDMENT MODIFYING SETBACK EXCEPTIONS IN VARIOUS
           SECTIONS OF TITLE 17 BASED ON CHANGES IN THE UTAH STATE
           CODE FROM H.B. 476 IN THE 2024 GENERAL SESSION
Jonathan Anderson said the proposed amendment modifies every applicable Section of
Title 17 that relates to residential rear setback exceptions. House Bill 476 of the 2024
Utah Legislature enacted UCA 10-9a-540 (renumbered to 10-20-623 on 11/6/2025) which
permits “allowable features” as defined within the rear setback area of a residential
dwelling, see Attachment 1.

Amendments are also proposed to further clarify how the International Residential and
Building Codes are applicable for window wells for setbacks on single family homes, and
amends language to be more clearly understood for International Building Code
applicability for building setbacks.

Public notice has been sent to affected entities as required in 17-3-9.B of the Municipal
Code. No comments have been received as of the writing of this report.

-ZONING CODE AMENDMENT CRITERIA-

Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) outlines the criteria necessary for amendments to the zoning
code. A proposal may only be approved if it demonstrates one or more of the following:
   1. The proposed amendment promotes the objectives of the general plan and
      purposes of this title;
   2. The proposed amendment promotes the purposes outlined in Utah State Code
      10-9a-102;
   3. The proposed amendment more clearly explains the intent of the original
      language or has been amended to make interpretation more straightforward; or
   4. Existing zoning code was the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact.

Staff finds that this proposal meets criteria 1, 2 and 3 listed above. The proposed
amendment promotes the general plan, purposes of this title, and purposes of Utah State
Code 10-9a-102 (renumbered to 10-20-101) by conforming Title 17 to the governing Utah
State Code as amended by the State Legislature. The proposed amendment also amends
language to make interpretation more straightforward for setbacks in relation to the
International Residential and Building Codes as applicable.

        A. CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2026-O-17 ESTABLISHING THE
           COMMUNITY CLEAN ENERGY PROGRAM IN MIDVALE.
Dustin Gettel said Midvale City is a member of the Utah Renewable Communities (URC),
a coalition of Utah municipalities and counties working in partnership with Rocky Mountain
Power (RMP) to develop and implement the Community Clean Energy Program
(Program). The goal of the program is to provide a net 100% renewable electricity option
for participating communities within the next several years. Midvale joined the URC
through an interlocal cooperation agreement in 2024, allowing the City to participate in
program design and negotiations with RMP. The Program is authorized under Utah’s
Community Clean Energy Act, and Rocky Mountain Power filed the Program application
on behalf of the URC. Following several years of development and regulatory review, the
Utah Public Service Commission (PSC) approved the Program on March 4, 2026.

The Program establishes a new clean energy option (from renewable energy resources)
for residents and businesses served by Rocky Mountain Power. Participation is structured
as an opt-out program, meaning eligible electric customers within participating
communities would be automatically enrolled, with the ability to opt out. The Program
includes an initial cancellation period, during which customers may exit the Program
without incurring a termination fee. After the cancellation period ends, customers may still
opt out at any time; however, a termination fee would apply, as approved by the PSC and
outlined in Program rules.

The PSC approved an initial residential flat rate of $4 per month, which is made up of
$3.88 per month plus a $0.12 per month low-income program surcharge. For all other
(non-residential) customer classes, the PSC approved a volumetric rate of $0.00609 per
kilowatt-hour (kWh) and a $0.12 per month low-income program surcharge. The low-
income surcharge collected from participating residential and non-residential customers
helps fund bill credits for income-qualified customers enrolled in Rocky Mountain Power’s
Home Electric Lifeline Program (HELP). These customers will receive a bill credit equal
to the $3.88 clean energy charge, allowing them to participate in the Program at no cost.
Program rates may be adjusted periodically, but no more than annually, consistent with
standard utility ratemaking practices. The PSC indicated that rates are not expected to
increase significantly over time and may decline as administrative and reserve funds are
established.

State law requires that each participating community adopt a local ordinance within ninety
(90) days of PSC approval in order to make the Program available within its boundaries.
The deadline for ordinance adoption is June 2, 2026. If Midvale City adopts the
participation ordinance, the Program would be implemented locally, with customer notices
anticipated in late 2026 and Program charges beginning in early 2027. If the ordinance is

        B. DISCUSSION OF FY2027 PROPERTY TAX IMPACT SCHEDULE.
Mariah Hill said during the 2026 Utah Legislative Session, HB 236 – Truth-in-Taxation
Amendments was enacted, making several changes to Utah Code § 59-2-919 and 59-2
924, which govern notice, disclosure, and public hearing requirements when a tentative
budget includes a proposed property tax rate increase, including municipal property tax
increases. These provisions are intended to promote transparency and ensure the public
is provided clear information regarding the potential impacts of a proposed tax rate prior
to final budget and tax rate adoption. Accordingly, the City is required to prepare and
present a Property Tax Impact Schedule that illustrates the estimated effect of the
proposed tax rate on typical residential and non-residential properties and present and
make it available to the public separate from other budget documents.

The Property Tax Impact Schedule is included on this agenda as a discussion item to
satisfy the statutory requirement that it be presented at any meeting where the budget is
discussed. Its inclusion ensures compliance with state law and provides an opportunity
for the City Council and the public to review and understand the projected impacts of the
proposed tax rate.

The Property Tax Impact Schedule was adopted by the Midvale City Council on May 5,
2026. This action does not finalize the property tax rate. Final consideration and adoption
of the property tax rate will occur following the required public hearing and completion of
all applicable notice and review periods.

FISCAL IMPACT:
Adoption of the Property Tax Impact Schedule itself does not have a direct fiscal impact.
The schedule is a disclosure document that illustrates the estimated effect of a proposed
property tax rate increase. Any actual fiscal impact will be determined by the final property
tax rate adopted by the City Council as part of the final budget process.
