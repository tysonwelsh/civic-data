Agenda Review

Chair Nadolski and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m. There was a brief focus on an item on the City Council meeting agenda regarding the requested
zone change for property at 682-712 7" Street responsive to public feedback the Council has received in the form of electronic
communication from other property owners in close proximity to the subject property. Chair Nadolski concluded that it would be

act on the proposed ordinance or table it to request additional information from the applicant regarding their development plans if
they are able to secure R-3 zoning for the property.

Recycling Program Update
Public Services Director Lowder provided the Council with an update on the City’s recycling program and service
options; he first provided background information, noting on August 12, 2022, Public Services were notified of an illicit discharge
located in a storm water ditch near Recycled Earth. The Utah Department of Environmental Quality (DEQ) collected both water
and soil samples along the reported route and pollutants were detected. On August 17th, it was verified that the pollutants did
come from Recycled Earth. Citing the importance of recycling to the community, the discharge issue from Recycled Earth creates
aneed for Ogden City to pursue other recycling options. From August to September 2022, Public Services researched all viable
options in order to continue providing a recycling program, specifically reaching out to the following four facilities:
e Wasatch Integrated Waste (Layton)
e Rocky Mountain Recycling (SLC)
e Mountain Fiber (Hyrum)
e Mountain View Landfill (SLC)
Based upon those efforts, Administration is now recommending that the City contract with Wasatch Integrated Waste
(WIW) for recycling services. Administration believes that WIW’s goals are inline with the City’s goals in terms of recycling;
however, contracting with them for their services will come at an increased cost associated with delivering recycled materials to
Davis County. The current level of service is weekly recycling collection, but Administration is evaluating whether it would be

Proposed Rezone — 305 Harrison Boulevard from R-1-8 (Residential Single-
family) to R-2 (Residential Two-family)

Deputy Planning Manager Simpson explained that Mr. William Sneddon has submitted a petition to rezone property at
305 Harrison Boulevard from R-1-8 to R-2. The former market site fronted on both 2nd Street and Harrison Boulevard with a
small convenience store located on the corner of 2nd Street and Harrison Boulevard. Since the time the market closed, several
significant changes have taken place on the property. The market building was removed from the site in the early 2000s and the
property sat undeveloped until 2017 when the Leadership Learning Academy was constructed on the western half of the property.
In addition, around the same time, the intersection of 2nd Street and Harrison Boulevard was realigned to the north to line up with
Sheridan Drive. The eastern portion of the former Sherwood Market site remains undeveloped and is the subject of the petition.
The Planning Commission has recommended approval of the petition finding that the proposed R-2 zoning is consistent with the
Hillcrest/Bonneville Community Plan.

Brief discussion among the Council and Planning staff centered on the existing uses in the general vicinity of the subject
property, after which the Council offered their support for the application moving forward to a meeting for action.
