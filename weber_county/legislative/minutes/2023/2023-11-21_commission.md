     1. APPROVE RECOMMENDATION TO FORWARD THE CRIMINAL JUSTICE COORDINATING
        COUNCIL STRATEGIC PLAN TO THE STATE.
            Dr. Daniel Lancaster (Public Defender’s Office): State statute passed last year requires creation of CJCCs in
            all counties in Utah. The CJCC Strategic Plan is required every year to qualify for grant funding from the
            state. The report needs to do three things, which we have done. Number one, we need to identify and map
            out all resources in the county at the disposal of the criminal justice system. This ranges from enforcement
            specific police departments, the Sheriff's Office, the courts to treatment providers that we can lean on to help
            in rehabilitative efforts and treatment efforts; it also includes some diversion resource sources. We have
            gone a step further and utilized a nationally recognized model called the sequential intercept model to map
            out which resources can be helpful in moving individuals either with substance use disorder or psychiatric
            conditions out of the criminal justice system and into treatment where they will be at reduced risk for
            committing future crime. The plan also identifies some recidivism goals and what it is that we want to do.
            The mission statement that the CJCC adopted officially is: “The Weber Morgan CJCC seeks to enhance
            public health and Community safety through collaboration and data-driven action.” The members who sit on
            the CJCC are outlined by that state statute and I will note that there has been a high level of collaboration and
            engagement especially over the last several months from everybody listed the county attorney's office,
         defense attorneys, District judges, Municipal judges, the Sheriff's offices of both Weber and Morgan
         counties, local police Chiefs; we've got a lived experience individual who also represents the re-entry Weber
         County re-entry Coalition. We all get to sit the table talk about how to strategize making our community
         safer beyond enforcement. The last thing that this report does is it outlines our plan for being data driven in
         our approach utilizing existing Community Resources and agencies. The CJCC will begin to make monthly
         requests of specific data from police departments, from the sheriff's office, from the defender group, from the
         County Attorney prosecutor's office. All of this so that we can understand how to best utilize our resources
         and to eliminate waste. We will forward this not just onto the state but onto every relevant individual and
         agency in the county so that we can be unified as we move forward in addressing crime.

         Commissioner Bolos: Thank you to Daniel for the work he has done. This is a state statute I think a lot of
         counties are still trying to figure out. As a group, we think we are leading the way in adopting this strategic
         plan. All the participants on the council work well together, they all participate and give input.
         Commissioner Bolos moved to approve recommendation to forward the Criminal Justice Coordinating Council
         Strategic Plan to the state; Commissioner Harvey seconded.
         Commissioner Harvey – aye; Commissioner Bolos – aye; Chair Froerer – aye
