Recognizing retired Ogden Nature Center Executive Director Mary
McKinley for nearly 20 years of service to the Ogden community

A memo from Council staffregarding recognition of Mary McKinley for her 20 years of service to the Ogden community
as Executive Director of the Ogden Nature Center came before the Council for consideration. Council member Choberka read the
certificate of recognition issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Caldwell and presented Ms. McKinley with the certificate.

Ms. McKinley thanked the Mayor and Council for recognizing her; it has been a pleasure working with the City, but she
cannot accept this recognition without also recognizing the Board of Directors of the Ogden Nature Center as well as her staff.
She loves Ogden and it has been a pleasure to be involved with the improvement of the Center.

Council member Choberka stated that she has served as member of Board of Directors of the Ogden Nature Center and
has enjoyed her relationship with Ms. McKinley. She has also spent a great deal of time at the Nature Center and can speak to the
value it brings to the community.

Council member Richey stated that he is also a member of the Board of Directors and spoke to the contributions Ms.
McKinley has made to the Center and the entire community. He stated Ms. McKinley is entirely responsible for earning this
recognition, but stated it is in her character to recognize everyone around her as well. He stated that when people speak to Ms.
McKinley, she has the ability to make them feel like they are the most important person.

Council member Blair stated it has been a pleasure to work alongside Ms. McKinley; she is a tireless worker and problem
solver and has been one of the great leaders in the community.

Council member White stated that she agrees with all that has been said about Ms. McKinley and thanked her for her
efforts to protect the Ogden Nature Center, which is a gem in this community.

Mayor Caldwell stated that he had the pleasure of knowing Ms. McKinley before she began serving at the Ogden Nature
Center; she has led with her heart and soul and has impacted so many lives through her service. He wished her the best in her
future endeavors and thanked her for her friendship.

Council member Hyer spoke to the number of lives that Ms. McKinley has touched through her service at the Ogden
Nature Center.

Chair Nadolski stated that his private employment is in the wildlife management field and there is a big community
throughout the State of Utah and the western United States that are grateful to Ms. McKinley for her work.

Recognizing the many volunteers behind Ogden Untamed Softball for
making competitive play accessible to the Ogden community

A memo from Council staffregarding recognition of the Ogden Untamed Softball program for making competitive play
accessible to the Ogden community came before the Council for consideration. Chair Nadolski read the certificate of recognition
issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Caldwell and presented Edd Bridge with the certificate, on behalf of the coaches and
players who were present.

Mr. Bridge thanked the Mayor and Council for this recognition; the true heroes that deserve this recognition are the
coaches and other volunteers who have dedicated many hours and much effort to making this program successful. Coaches change
the lives of players, but the players are also very dedicated and committed to being successful in this program.

Chair Nadolski agreed with Mr. Bridge’s sentiments about the dedication of coaches and players; he added that many
people have worked on their behalf, and it is important to recognize Mr. Bridge and his staff for supporting the concept of a
competitive softball program. He also recognized the coaches of the Untamed Softball program and thanked them for their
dedication to their players and the program.

Coaches addressed the Council and Mayor and thanked them for their support of a competitive softball program in the
community; the kids in this community deserve these opportunities.

Council member Richey acknowledged Chair Nadolski for the amount of time that he has also dedicated to this
competitive softball program.

Council member Hyer stated it is gratifying to see so many young women who are passionate about something that has
the potential to teach them lifelong lessons; it gives him hope for the future. The players are building character, patience,
sportsmanship, and camaraderie and these traits are priceless and will serve them their entire life.

Council member Blair spoke to his experience as a young athlete and the lessons that he learned that have stayed with
him his entire life; he made lifelong friends that he still keeps in touch with and still considers them to be his best friends. The
people who coached him made a profound impact on him and they truly changed his life. He thanked the coaches and players
from the competitive softball program and stated that he hopes they have the same experience he had.

Council member White stated that she grew up playing softball and it is delightful to see so many young softball players
tonight; this experience will teach them to do hard things their entire life and it will make them better people. She stated she is
proud of them for their efforts and dedication to their sport. She thanked the coaches for their dedication as well.

Council member Choberka congratulated the players and coaches present tonight and stated she expects the time and
effort they are dedicating to their sport at this time will help them develop strong leadership qualities that will stay with them their
entire life.

Mayor Caldwell echoed the comments made by the Council and also thanked the City’s Recreation Department for their
support of this program. He stated he has a daughter who is an athlete, and he can attest to the lessons that young people learn
from coaches and others who support them in their craft. He thanked the coaches for giving up their time to improve the lives of
young people and congratulated the youth for their dedication to their sport. He stated it is an honor to celebrate this group tonight.

Public Comments

Bob Giboney presented the Council with photographs of deteriorating asphalt on 2™ Street near the Bingham Fort
markers. He stated that he feels the asphalt was not poured correctly or did not cure; it is more pea gravel than it is asphalt, and it
is cracking and the conditions will only worsen over the coming winter months. The person who is responsible for the issue is
ignoring pleas to improve/repair it, but other residents will soon be taking him to court regarding other problems he has created.

Mayor Caldwell referred Mr. Giboney to City Engineer Anderson to discuss the asphalt conditions. Chair Nadolski
thanked Mr. Giboney for his input.

Angel Castillo thanked Council staff for their efforts to provide electronic means of participating in open meetings of the
Council; she is typically unable to attend the 4:00 p.m. work session meetings. Tonight, she listened to the meeting and paid
careful attention to the discussion regarding the City’s receipt of $1.3 million in American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds and the
various entities in the community that should be eligible to receive a portion of that funding. She discussed the current operations
of Your Community Connection (YCC) and stated they desperately need funding; they have been directly impacted by the
COVID-19 pandemic and they are working to secure additional housing units for the clients they serve. She stated many people
are purchasing housing units and using them as short-term rentals, so it has made it difficult for non-profits to secure available
housing units. She recommended the Council consider crafting a grant program that will make the funding accessible for non-
profits that truly need it. She then addressed the ongoing discussion of the proposed annexation of property that sits on the border
between Ogden and North Ogden cities; she agreed that the City should not be simply giving land away to another community,
but if an agreement is reached to allow that property to be annexed into North Ogden, the City needs to craft some type of
agreement that provides for aesthetically pleasing design of the development project so that Ogden residents are not forced to live
near the back of a building that is not pleasing to look at.

Manager Garrett regarding the reasons that airlines are leaving the Airport. One reason he offered was the pilot shortage, which he
acknowledged is real, but the actions of the Airport Manager and Ogden City regarding the revocation of hangar leases at the
Airport have contributed greatly to the pilot shortage. The Ogden Airport has gone from a beautiful and thriving Airport where

Mayor Comments

Mayor Caldwell stated that it was great to be able to recognize the competitive softball program; it created a great energy

Council member Comments

Council member White referenced Ms. Castillo’s comments and stated that she believes that Your Community
Connection (YCC) is a non-profit entity that would qualify for a non-profit grant according to the current draft of the program
guidelines.

Council member Choberka asked for an update on the current wildfire near the mouth of Ogden Canyon. Chief
Administrative Officer Johnson stated the fire is 10 percent contained and fire fighters are drawing water from two sources. The
City is receiving mutual aid from other fire agencies in the area, and they are working hard to stop the fire.

Council member Blair stated he was contacted by a constituent recently who asked if the City has a regulation regarding
the use of e-bikes on the trail system; the bikes can travel at higher rates of speed and could create hazards for pedestrians. Mr.
Johnson stated that it may be appropriate for the Council to solicit counsel from the City Attorney’s Office regarding reasonable
regulations. City Attorney Williams noted the City can regulate speed on the trail system and he believes the current speed limit
on the parkway is 10 miles per hour. He stated that e-bikes should be treated like common bicycles unless the Council wishes to
regulate them differently. Mayor Caldwell stated he believe the popularity of e-bikes will increase and it may be appropriate for
Administration and the Council to work collectively to develop appropriate regulations in anticipation of the increased use of such
bikes on the City’s trail systems.

There being no further business to come before the Council, COUNCIL MEMBER HYER MOVED THE MEETING
ADJOURN AT 6:56 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA, ALL VOTING AYE.
Vice Chair Lopez was not present when this vote was taken.
