Zone Text Amendment _— Low/Moderate Income Housing Parking
Requirements

Planning Manager Montgomery reported the Ogden City Planning Division is proposing a change to the zoning
ordinance to amend the minimum and maximum required parking stalls for dedicated low- and moderate-income housing in
residential and commercial zones. The proposed amendment would reduce required parking for dedicated low- and moderate-
income housing projects in residential zones from 2 stalls per unit to between | and 1.4 stalls per unit. For dedicated low- and
moderate-income housing projects in commercial zones, the proposal would reduce the parking maximums from 1.75 stalls per
unit to 1.4 stalls per unit but would maintain the minimum 0.8 stalls per unit. The proposed change was based on a parking study
commissioned by the City. The change to residential parking standards is being proposed as a follow-up to the amendments to
design standards for multi-family residential development in commercial zones that were approved in October 2020.

Zone Text Amendment — Waterwise Landscaping Requirements

Mr. Montgomery reported that in an effort to reduce the amount of water used to sustain landscaping in Ogden, the
Administration is proposing amendments to several sections of landscape-related code. These amendments place a limit on how
much landscaped space may have turf grass in new commercial, industrial, and multi-family developments. The proposed changes
also allow more rock and mulch to be used than was previously allowed. This report also outlines other various changes to the
City’s landscaping requirements. If approved, these amendments would qualify Ogden City residents to apply for the Flip-Your-
Strip program with Weber Basin Water Conservancy District offered in conjunction with the City’s own park strip conversion
program.

Mr. Montgomery engaged in discussion with the Council regarding plantings and landscape materials that meet the

Park Strip Conversion Program Information Item
Public Utilities Manager Herd provided information regarding the park strip lawn conversion program, the purpose of
which is to:

Promote water efficient landscaping and assure sensible use of the City’s water.
Enhance the visual environment
Allow for increase in population, while ensuring adequate water supply within Ogden City.
Assist in meeting state and local water conservation goals.
e Provide education and awareness.

The City is collaborating with Weber Basin’s Park Strip Lawn Conversion Program (Flip Your Strip) and proposes a City
incentive of $1.25 per square foot of conversion, which will be matched by Weber Basin Water Conservancy, making residents
eligible for an incentive of $2.50 per square foot. The average park strip size is 600 square feet and with the amount of funding
available for the program, Administration estimates that 133 park strips could be converted. Mr. Herd then summarized program
qualifications:

Residential customer within the Ogden City’s service area (Single Family Dwellings)
Must have a Park Strip

Attend Lawn Conversion class through WBWCD

Park strip must be well maintained lawn

Remove all the lawn in the park strip and replace it with water efficient landscaping
Converted park strips will be watered with a drip irrigation system or hand watered
Converted park strips must be covered by at least 3 inches of mulch or accepted ground cover permeable to
water and air.

Trees must be planted or left in the landscape as required by the City.

Must complete within 10 months

Park strip must not be returned to lawn

All work must comply with City regulations

In conclusion, Mr. Herd summarized the planning and approval process for incentive applications.

Discussion among the Council and Mr. Herd centered on the term of the program and partnership with Weber Basin;
plans for allocating funding in future years for the program; and documenting the conversion of a park strip in order to
communicate to future owners of a property that they are not allowed to convert the park strip back to grass; whether a landscape
contractor can apply for the incentive on behalf of a property owner; and whether elected officials are eligible to apply for the
program.

Proposed Fee Amendments — Planning Application Fees

Deputy Planning Manager Brierley explained the Planning Division has performed a thorough analysis of its application
review processes and analyzed current planning application fees and costs and have developed a proposal to amend the fee
schedule related to planning application fees. The study calculated the average cost of processing each type of application,
determined what the cost recovery would be, and developed a fee schedule proposal that would help the planning division recover
a larger portion of the actual cost of reviewing planning applications. Recovering a larger share of the cost of reviewing
applications would allow the Planning Division to have the resources needed to provide a wider range of planning services
including more robust long-range planning.

Discussion among the Council and staff centered on the impact the fee proposals will have on applicants.

Council/Board Business

City Council Meeting: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith discussed the formatting of the agenda for the City
Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m., mainly focusing on the combination of multiple agenda items under one public
hearing listing and limiting public input to two minutes for three issues listed under one public hearing heading. The Council
discussed whether the limitation is inappropriate, but ultimately concluded to maintain the format of the agenda at this time but
consider any request for continued time to speak during the meeting if such a request arises.

Elected Officials’ Salaries: There was continued discussion of past proposals relating to elected officials’ salaries; the
Council discussed the option of removing the funding for the salaries from the property tax increase proposal.

Council Calendar: The Council and Council staff reviewed the Council calendar for the remainder of the summer,
noting the inclusion of two public hearings on the budget in July and August.
