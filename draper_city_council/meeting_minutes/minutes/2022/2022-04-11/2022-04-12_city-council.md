1.0    Presentation: Fiscal Year 2022/2023 Budget

1.1    Finance Director Bob Wylie explained that he would speak about the revenue
       side of the budget that evening, and then next week he would go through the
       expenditure side of the budget. He stated that there was $37 million in the
       General Fund for that year, and they projected that there would be $42 million
       in the General Fund next year. He briefly explained why they estimated a
       higher amount for the following year. He said that they would not have the
       capital projects ready until the tentative budget, and he said any project that
       they had at that time would be fund balance appropriation. He listed the
       proprietary funds: water, stormwater, and solid waste, and said that water and
       stormwater were about the same as last year, and there was a slight increase
       in the solid waste fund. He noted that the Council had approved those
       numbers last May. He said that all the funds that he had listed totaled about
       $9 million.

       Mr. Wylie said that property tax was projected to be consistent, and added that
       they would not get more property tax information from the County until June.

He also discussed sales tax and said that they tended to project conservatively
and that so far that year their sales tax was very strong. He said that they had

increase as new buildings and infrastructure came into the City.

distribution had gone up substantially over the last couple of years. He said
that 63% of the sales tax came from the City, and the remainder came from
the State allocation. He stated that their point of sale was doing very well and
that their sales tax base was very strong. He also anticipated that it would
continue to increase. He also compared Draper to other similarly sized cities
in the nearby area and said that based on the data, it appeared that residents

revenue.     He noted that the online sales tax figures had increased

had passed legislation that would impact the way that cities collected sales tax
from online sales.

2.0   Discussion: Dominion Energy Infrastructure

2.1   Assistant City Manager Bret Millburn discussed growth in the residential area
      and said that there was an increased need for infrastructure, and specifically,
      natural gas as the population grew. He said that there was a need for natural
      gas services in the SunCrest area specifically. He said that given the rate of
      growth in that area, they needed to cater to the existing residents, and also
      anticipate the needs of future residents. He indicated on the map the area
      that he was referring to and noted that the site requirements for a structure
      in that area would have a larger footprint. He said that the actual structure
      would be about 12 by 12 feet, although the space surrounding the structure
      would be larger. He discussed that they had received some comments and
      concerns about noise, but said that the structure should not make excessive
      noise, so that would not be an issue. He outlined that they would need
      cameras and a fence for security purposes, and indicated that there were
      more details in the packets that had been distributed. He referenced that
      there were other similar size structures in other areas throughout Draper.

      Mr. Millburn briefly noted that they had considered several areas for the site,
      and had faced some difficulties as there were natural deviations in the ground

                                                                        Page 3 of 13
      that would make the site sit disproportionately high, and there was also a
      conservation easement that ran through the area, and the site could not be
      located in the easement. He indicated three options for the proposed site on
      the map and said that their goal was to be as unobtrusive as possible. He
      noted that the site could sit lower than the rest of the area, but not higher so
      that it did not obstruct the view. A Councilmember pointed out that they had
      to make sure to avoid both open space and the surrounding residents. Mr.
      Millburn referenced that the yellow line on the map was where the gas line
      was proposed to go. Councilmember Roberts commented that it was not a
      perfect area, and reported that most of the concerns he had heard from the
      public were that it would significantly impact the view. Councilmember T.
      Lowery thought it would be best to put the structure near the top, and said
      that the first option that Mr. Millburn had proposed was her top choice,
      followed by the third option. The other Councilmembers agreed that the first
      or third option would be best, and said that they did not like the second option.
      Mr. Millburn thanked the Council for their input and said that he would bring
      it back before Dominion for their review. He said that there would be a public
      process after that, in which Dominion would prepare an informative
      presentation for the public.

3.1   City Manager David Dobbins said that there had been a neighborhood
      meeting regarding South Mountain, and reported that he had gotten a lot of
      feedback. He said that there had been a very mixed response, and some
      people wanted parking to stay the same as it had been, and some wanted it to
      be changed to accommodate short-term rentals. He said that some people
      had voiced concerns about ADUs, and wanted ADUs and short-term rentals to
      be banned.

      Public Works Director Scott Cooley summarized that everyone had very strong
      opinions about the issue, and said that not everyone was happy with their
      solution to post “no parking” signs on one side of the street. He explained that
      since there were no spaces that cars could pull into, only street parking was
      available, and this could cause issues concerning emergency access. He said
      that ambulances and fire trucks would not have enough room, so they had
      determined that the alleyways could be utilized, which would provide better
      access to fire hydrants. He noted that currently, the alleyways were mostly
      open, and people did not use them for parking, so it could be a good solution.
      He added that they would have to make a change in the Code to mandate that
      parking was not allowed in alleys. The Council discussed the feasibility of

                                                                          Page 4 of 13
enforcing a no parking rule in the alleyway, and they felt that it would be easy
to enforce that rule.

Councilmember T. Lowery was interested to see how this played out over the
next several months, and she wondered if the increased parking was solely
due to the short-term rentals, or if there were other factors contributing to the
parking issue. She asked about the cost if they were to move the fire hydrants
to a more accessible location, and Mr. Cooley pointed out that if they did move
the hydrants, it would be even more important to enforce parking in the alley.
Mr. Cooley added that the roads there were the standard width of roads
throughout the City, and said that the most pressing issue was when there
were cars parked on both sides of the street. He explained that this posed a
threat to emergency access, and blocked driveways. Mr. Cooley elaborated
that part of the issue as well was that when the neighborhood had been
developed, the concept had been for the area to be less car-centric, and so
there were no driveways or enough space for people to store their cars.

Mr. Dobbins summarized that they would have to resolve the current ADU
situation to bring it into compliance, and said that they also had the option to
install fire hydrants, which would be costly. Mr. Cooley commented that the
ADU rentals had been an issue for a long time, although the volume of rentals
had increased lately, which was what led to the complaints from residents. He
noted that currently, the Code stated that the rentals had to be owner-
occupied, meaning that as long as the owner of the property lived in an
adjoining building on the property, or a room in the main house, then it was
legal and the City could not prohibit that.

The Council briefly discussed Airbnb and wondered if Airbnb would block
Draper residents from making listings if the City banned short-term rentals
overall. The Council also mentioned that there had been several stories about
shootings and gun violence at short-term rentals, and commented that some
residents had safety concerns. Mr. Dobbins said that if the Council wanted to
consider banning short-term rentals overall, they could pursue that option,
and he could do more research to determine the feasibility of doing so. He
discussed that St. George had an ordinance about short-term rentals, and he
could look into more details of the ordinance.

6.0   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1535, an Ordinance of Draper City amending the
      text of the Land Use and Development Code (Title 9) and the Land
      Development Code (Title 17) of Draper City Municipal Code relating to
      definitions, exactions, special regulations in commercial zones, deviations
      from design standards, building design, site design, parking location, parking
      area design and construction, height limitations and exceptions, cross access,
      and connectivity, and other typographical and de minimis corrections in code
      sections 9-3- 040, 9-11-070, 9-22-030, 9-22-040, 9-22-050, 9-25-050, 9-25-080,
      9-27-120, and 17-1-040; and enacting sections 9-5-220, 17- 1-160, and 17-5-
      035.

6.1   City Planner Todd Draper clarified that they had included regulations for
      helipads in the first notices that had been sent out, and those regulations had
      since been pulled from the legislative text and were no longer included as part
      of the plans. He reported that there had been a 3-2 vote in favor from the
      Planning Commission, and he noted that some of the changes in the document
      had been made since the time of the Planning Commission’s approval. He

                                                                        Page 7 of 13
      explained that they hoped to hold a public hearing that evening and solicit
      feedback from the Council and the public, and then would bring the item back
      before them the following week.

      Mr. Draper stated that the bulk text amendments were intended to address
      the standards related to site design, standards for mixed-use and non-
      residential building design, and also address allowable deviations related to
      the site and building design. He indicated on the map a commercial area in
      Riverton and analyzed their site design. He highlighted some problems that
      the area had in terms of cross access and circulation and explained that they
      aimed to circumvent those issues with their development. He shared a visual
      representation, to explain in greater detail how access would work in the
      development. He added that they would change some of the definitions
      included in the ordinance as well, and shared some slides of the proposed text
      amendments. He also overviewed the parking section of the ordinance briefly
      and said that they would have the same standards for access in the parking
      area, although there were allowances for deviation from the required cross
      access in parking lots.

      Mr. Draper explained that the ordinance also addresses height requirements
      of the buildings, and said that they would not allow for unlimited deviations
      for commercial buildings, for which the ordinance currently allowed. He said
      that they would tighten up the deviation standards, and only allow for
      deviations for architectural features that were less than 14 feet tall. He added
      that they had also proposed to remove height allowances for things like
      screened-in mechanical equipment. He asked the Council for feedback if they
      wanted to include exceptions for only churches, or for other buildings that
      might have special features such as towers or belfries.

      Councilmember F. Lowry asked if there was a compelling reason to allow for
      height exceptions in buildings other than churches, and the Council discussed
      if there were buildings other than churches that required architectural designs
      such as steeples. Councilmember T. Lowery suggested that a building could
      choose to apply for a permit if they needed to add a special feature, and
      thought it might standardize the Code better if they limited the exceptions to
      churches only.
