   1. EMPLOYEE PRESENTATION
          a. After 46 years of service, Ed Park, Nephi City Streets Department Superintendent, is
             retiring. Mayor Seely and Kyle Marchant, Public Works Director, presented Ed with a
             plaque featuring the Streets Department logo, which Ed created. Mayor Seely honored
             Ed as being knowledgeable and meticulous in caring for his equipment and city streets.
          b. Each council member and others expressed appreciation to Ed for his expertise, service,
             and friendship. Several members shared memories. It was noted that Ed took
             considerable pride in his work and enjoyed his job.
   2. COMCAST PRESENTATION
          a. Kyle Biederwolf, representing Comcast, introduced himself and Marcus Jessop. He then
             discussed the company’s desire to partner with Nephi City and expand cable and
             broadband services to the residents of Nephi.
          b. The presentation explained the cable franchise agreement and federal law surrounding
             the agreement.
          c. The council asked questions about Comcast’s past presence in Nephi, why they left, and
             now why they want to return. Also mentioned was the feasibility of cable services.
             Further discussion touched on the difference between cable and broadband services.
             Mr. Biederwolf clarified that the franchise fees are specific to cable services, not
             broadband. The council expressed concern about losing revenue from existing providers
             and the need for a fair agreement.
   3. AIRPORT PROJECT AWARD
          a. An airport runway rehabilitation and parking lot project has been planned for this year.
             Six bids were received for the project. Seth Atkinson announced that Black Forest Paving
             had the low bid. The bid was 25% lower than the engineer’s estimate and the contractor
             had good references.
          b. The council was asked to authorize the contract with Black Forest paving and then it
             could be ratified at the June 16 council meeting. The Federal Aviation Administration
             (FAA) can then award the grant.
   4. HAZARDOUS INSPECTION REPORT – OLD GYM
          a. The city qualified for a grant to have a hazardous inspection report of the old gym to
             identify hazardous materials inside the building.

   5.   DONATIONS POLICY DISCUSSION
            a. The annual car show requested a donation from the city. Mr. Atkinson stated that state
                law prevents the city from making specific donations unless there is a direct benefit to
                the city.
            b. The city would like to devise a donation policy that allows enterprise funds to be used
                and thus promote community-owned utilities by advertising with the logo of the specific
                enterprise.
            c. Mr. Atkinson requested approval to take $50 from the gas fund for the car show
                donation and to draft a policy regarding donations which requires some kind of
                promotion material or advertisement for the utility that donates. The council agrees.
   6.   COMMERCIAL ELECTRIC DEMAND CHARGE
            a. Mr. Atkinson discussed a discrepancy in commercial demand charges and suggested they
                be aligned with the Utah Municipal Power Agency’s (UMPA) charges. We are currently
                charging less than them.
            b. UMPA charges us a certain amount on demand for power. The demand charge occurs
                when we have maxed out our level of use. UMPA is required to find addition power to
                meet the peak demand.
            c. Each commercial and industrial business is only charged for their individual demand
                once peak usage is reached.
            d. The problem has occurred because the city lost some big businesses that contributed
                heavily to our load factor. We are looking for smaller data centers to help boost the load
                but not have a major impact on the community.
   7.   TIER TWO PUBLIC SAFETY RETIREMENT COSTS
            a. The state has increased the cost for tier two public safety retirement. Mr. Atkinson asked
                the council if they would like to continue covering the cost or put the cost on the
                employee.
            b. The council agreed to continue covering the costs, recognizing the importance of
                incentives for public safety employees.
            c. A policy will be created and sent out for signatures approving the agreement to pay the
                additional costs.
   8.   CITY PAY PHILOSOPHY REVIEW
            a. The city currently uses a step and grade system for the employees rather than a merit-
                based pay system. Pros and cons of both systems are discussed.
            b. The council discussed the need for fair and equitable compensation across departments.
                The idea of allowing departments to have discretionary funds for merit-based bonuses
                was mentioned. The council agreed to explore additional options for encouraging
                employee growth and additional skills.
