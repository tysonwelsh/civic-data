Copperton Park Arborist Plan

                Council Member Bailey stated last month the Council discussed putting out an
RFP for an arborist for the park. The trees are aging and in order to keep the current tree canopy,
a plan needs to be implemented. She wants someone to inspect the trees and determine which
ones are in good condition and which ones will not last. Copperton Park has always been a tree
park and the central focus of the community. A 5, 10, 25-year plan with goals needs to be included
in Copperton Metro Township’s master plan, a capital improvement schedule needs to be created,
an irrigation plan and drip irrigation system needs to be investigated. The park name, park type
and acreage need to be identified. A map that will set out a comprehensive plan and preservation
plan need to be created to keep what is already there. The Council has the opportunity to utilize
Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation’s arborist. Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation will
need to agree to let Copperton use the arborist in the way they see fit.

               William Pruitt, Arborist, Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation Division, stated
there are approximately 218 trees in the park and a lot of trees are dying. He knows how to solve
most of the issues in the park and how to maintain the park. He wants to sustain the trees that
are already there and prepare for some of the trees that will gone in the next 10-15 years. The
trees around the playground were not planted correctly.

              Council Member Pazell stated he was of the understanding that the Council was
going to pursue an RFP for an arborist.

               Council Member Bailey stated Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation hired an
arborist with all the qualifications that the Council is looking for. This was another option she
wanted to explore.

               Mayor Clayton stated if the Council uses Salt Lake County Parks and
Recreation’s arborist the Council can stop the process at any time because the arborist would not
be hired through an RFP.

               Council Member Pazell stated it is worth while to go out for an RFP.

                Corey Bullock, Facilities Manager, Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation
Division, stated if the Council would like to move forward with Mr. Pruitt, Salt Lake County Parks
and Recreation will do the best they can.

               Mr. Pruitt stated when you put together the RFP, it is important to use the right
terminology for what the Council wants. He suggests asking for a consulting arborist not a tree
service. There are three consulting arborists in Utah, he is one of them and he will provide the
Council with the other two individuals contact information.

               Nathan Bracken suggested amending the RFP to specify what type of certification
and licensing the Council wants.

Code Enforcement Ordinance

             The Council reviewed the following Ordinance enacting a code enforcement and
community enhancement program:

ORDINANCE NO. 19-09-03                                                   Date: September 18, 2019

       AN ORDINANCE ADOPTING TITLE 12 OF THE COPPERTON METRO
       TOWNSHIP MUNICIPAL CODE TO ENACT A “CODE ENFORCEMENT AND
       COMMUNITY ENHANCEMENT PROGRAM”

      WHEREAS, the current remedies available for ordinance enforcement rely heavily on fines
and court action to resolve violations of the Copperton Metro Township Code; and

       WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code Ann. §10-2a-414(3), the Copperton Metro Township’s
code enforcement provisions are based on Salt Lake County’s ordinances and were enacted
pursuant to Title 17, which pertains to counties, as opposed to Title 10, which governs
municipalities; and

        WHEREAS, resolving code enforcement actions through the current enforcement process
is often costly, can take years to resolve, and can allow violations to exist unabated for significant
periods to time, forcing citizens to endure an unsightly and unhealthy living environment; and

        WHEREAS, under the County’s process, fines and penalties would accrue on a daily basis
while violations of the Copperton Metro Township Code awaited resolution in court, potentially
resulting in substantial, cumulative fines for relatively minor violations; and

        WHEREAS, other municipalities in Utah utilize a combination of administrative, civil, and
criminal remedies to bring about compliance with municipal ordinances, and such an approach
can bring about compliance in a reasonable amount of time and in a more cost-effective manner;
and

         WHEREAS, the Copperton Metro Township Planning and Zoning Commission
(“Commission”) held a public hearing on July 9, 2019, to review and take public comment on the
proposed adoption of a new, more comprehensive code enforcement program (the “Program”)
that is based on Title 10 of the Utah Code and would be set forth in Title 12 of the Metro Township
Code; and

      WHEREAS, after holding the public hearing on July 9, 2019, the Commission
recommended the adoption of the Program and the amendments of Chapter 19.94; and

       WHEREAS, pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-9a-205(3), the Council held a public
meeting on August 19, 2019, to review the Program as recommended by the Commission and to
take public comment; and

        WHEREAS, after reviewing Program as recommended by the Commission, the Council
instructed the Copperton Metro Township attorney to amend the Program as set forth in the
version of the Program attached hereto; and

        WHEREAS, the Council is authorized to enact the Program and the amendments to
Chapter 19.94 pursuant to Utah Code Ann. §§ 10-3-702 – 703.7, 10-3-716, 10-8-60, and 10-11-
1, et seq., among other applicable laws and authorities;

        WHEREAS, the Council hereby determines that it is in the best interests of the health,
safety, and welfare of the citizens of the Copperton Metro Township to adopt immediately the
Program to facilitate compliance with municipal ordinances and to safeguard the appearance of
the community.

       NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED by the Copperton Metro Township Council that:

        Section 1.     Enactment. Title 12 of the Copperton Metro Township Code, as set forth in
Exhibit A, is hereby enacted.

       Section 2.     Amendment. Chapter 19.94 of the Copperton Metro Township Code is
revised pursuant to the changes set forth in Exhibit B.

        Section 2.        Repealer. Any provision of the Copperton Metro Township Code found to
be in conflict with this ordinance is hereby repealed.

       Section 3.     Effective Date. This ordinance will go into effect immediately after its
posting pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-3-711(1)(b)(ii) and Utah Code Ann. § 10-3-712.
