 4.           An ordinance granting Rocky Mountain Power a nonexclusive franchise to install, maintain,
              and operate power poles and other related facilities in Provo, Utah. (23-039) 0:30:05

        Motion:       An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-36, as currently constituted, has been
                      made by council rule.

Brian Jones, City Attorney, presented. He explained that companies who operated in Provo’s right of
way needed express permission through franchise agreements. These agreements were routine,
covering services like gas and telecom companies. He mentioned that the situation was somewhat
unique because Provo had its own energy utility. He discussed Rocky Mountain Power also had few
facilities they served in Provo, their main customer being the State Hospital.

Councilor Ellsworth asked if provisions were added into the agreement that required removal of
abandoned lines.

Mr. Jones stated that in those situations, other agencies attach their lines to the city’s poles. In this case,
Rocky Mountain Power would be using their own poles, so an abandoned line provision would not be
necessary or applicable. He mentioned the ability to ask Rocky Mountain Power to move their poles,
assuming we provided them with an alternate route for their facilities, in the event of an annexation or
development that required it.

Councilor Handley asked if the agreement includes provisions related to Rocky Mountain Power
expanding their clientele in Provo.

Mr. Jones answered that the agreement stipulates that they cannot do so without the explicit approval
of the council. He explained this requirement aligns with the current state law, preventing outside third-
party electrical providers from serving anyone within the city or its boundaries without the explicit
consent of the city's governing body.

Chair MacKay asked about the price or cost of a franchise agreement.

 5.           A resolution appropriating $57,635 in the General Fund for water conservation and a
              transfer of that same amount to the Parks & Recreation Capital Improvement Fund. (23-
              056) 0:37:20

        Motion:       An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-35, as currently constituted, has been
                      made by council rule.

Doug Robins, Assistant Director of Parks & Recreation, presented. He provided history of the irrigation
technology that the city has used throughout the years. He discussed modern irrigation management
and explained the current solution being used is Weather Trak Solutions.

James Cornaby, Parks Superintendent, provided some background information. He mentioned that they
were responsible for managing approximately 800 acres of irrigated turf spread across 112 controllers at
83 park sites. This included a breakdown of 1,726 individual zones, which is a significant number
compared to the few zones one might have at their home. He emphasized that this number was
continually increasing with new park and site developments. He also noted that this equated to over
15,000 individual irrigation heads. Mr. Cornaby highlighted the effort required to maintain these
irrigation systems, especially when factors like lawnmowers or sporting activities could easily disrupt
them. He added that they regularly conduct system audits to ensure everything is functioning as
efficiently as possible and even sites irrigated from secondary sources were managed through their
software. He explained that the most effective way to be responsible and have a meaningful impact was
to enhance the capabilities of the irrigation software. This involved updating some of our master valves
and incorporating flow sensors into the system. The crucial information provided by these sensors
allowed the system to learn the flow rate of each individual zone, enabling precise control over water
usage. By setting specific parameters, if the flow exceeded or fell below those limits, the system would
automatically shut down. While most of our sites already had this equipment in place and is functioning
correctly, some still require updates to the master valves and flow sensors. He said that in the past,
without this technology, systems would run unchecked until concerned citizens reported the issue,
leading to hours of unnecessary water waste. With these updates, the system would shut down within
seconds of detecting excessive flow, conserving this valuable resource efficiently. He said they would
begin installation this fall after the irrigation systems were winterized and would finish in the spring.

Chair MacKay opened public comment. With none, she brought the discussion back to council.

Councilor Ellsworth shared this was one of the ideas the council put forward to improve water
conservation in the city. She thanked the Parks and Recreation Department for their support in that
objective.

 9.           A resolution approving the first amendment to an interlocal agreement for the Mix
              Community Reinvestment Project Area. (23-045) 1:07:55

        Motion:       An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-37, as currently constituted, has been
                      made by council rule.

Mr. Morey presented. He discussed the amendments to the interlocal plan.

Councilor Shipley asked for clarification on the resolution. He confirmed this resolution is to expand the
scope of the project and increase the timeframe.

Chair MacKay opened public comment. With none, she brought the discussion back to council.

Councilor Shipley discussed his reservations about expanding the project's scope, not its timeframe. He
said that during the work meeting, it was mentioned that the primary reason for revisiting the project
was the escalation in costs, which had significantly increased for both construction and project
development. He added that it is worth noting that investor groups had also reaped the benefits of
these inflationary trends. Over the past few years, they have undertaken substantial housing projects,
leading to considerable returns on investment for the same development group, albeit with different
investors involved. Councilor Shipley expressed his reluctance to subsidize a different set of investors for
the new phase of the project. He recalled that, if his memory served him correctly, he was one of only
two individuals who voted against it. Therefore, while revisiting the matter, his stance remained
unchanged—he was supportive of extending the project's timeframe but could not endorse the
expansion of its scope.

Sean Poor from Provo, Utah, representing Brian Communities, responded to Councilor Shipley’s
statement. He affirmed that rents had indeed risen substantially during the COVID period. However, he
pointed out that there was a crucial window during which the developer could have greatly benefited
from these rent increases, but they missed that opportunity due to financing constraints. He explained
that the availability of this window was tied to financing. Since they were still in the construction phase
and did not have permanent financing secured, they were susceptible to new interest rates. He said
consequently, their returns had decreased significantly, even for the residential projects that had
experienced rent increases.

Councilor Shipley sought clarification regarding the reduced returns and mentioned that this decline was
a result of their decision not to sell any of the units, opting instead to rent them exclusively.
With no other council discussion, Chair Mackay called for a vote on the implied motion.

        Vote:         The motion was approved 5:2 with Councilors Fillmore, Handley, Hoban, MacKay, and
                      Whipple in favor. Councilors Ellsworth and Shipley opposed.
