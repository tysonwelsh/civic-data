3.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONS ID ERATION, AND VOTE on Preliminary Subdivision
       Application for Reagan Cove for Three Lots located at approximately 13987 South
       2700 West, Howland Partners, Applicants. {Application 2021-29) - Staff Presenter,
       Jennifer Robison.

Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison, presented the staff report and noted that that agenda items
3, 4, and 5 would be considered together but voted on separately. She reported that the zone was
adopted by the City Council in early 2021. She reviewed a map of the subject property and the
surrounding area and stated that two parcels have existed for a long time. Parcel 1 is owned by
Howland Partners and is zoned GC-1 General Commercial. It will undergo future commercial
development. Parcel 2 is owned by the Midvalley Bible Church. The portion on the rear of
Parcel 2 was rezoned to the Senior Independent Living Zone (R-SL). As a result, a third parcel
was created. All three parcels were included in the subdivision. Parcel 2 has been divided and
has a new legal description, which resulted in Parcel 3. The parcel lines were changed to allow
for shared access to the subject property where the senior living development will be located.
The parcel on which the church is located will remain R-1-43.

Mrs. Robison next reviewed the three-lot subdivision that comprises Reagan Cove. She
identified the locations of the accesses. With the proposed changes, the church will change to
one street access for the parking lot. Mrs. Robison clarified that construction cannot take place
until the Final Plat is approved by staff. She described how the senior living units will be placed
on the subject property. Anything beyond the actual living units will be called "open space" or
"common area" and will be owned and maintained by the Homeowners Association ("HOA").
She identified the easements that will be needed for sewer and water.

Mrs. Robison next addressed the site plan. She identified the shared access and the layout of the
28 units that have been proposed for the project. Some units will be part of a four-plex and two
will have two residences each. She identified the location of the main access and the emergency
access. The streets within the project will be private and will be owned and maintained by the
HOA. The drive courts will be a minimum of 25 feet in width. Each unit must also have a
driveway that is at least 20 feet long and a two-car garage. She stated that a trail will go around
the property and be accessible from the open courts. There will be a clubhouse and a pickleball
court as well. Guest parking will include one additional parking space for every three units for a
total of 56 garage stalls (for 28 units) and 16 additional parking spaces. Mrs. Robison clarified
that she did not include the driveway as an option in the parking calculation.

Mrs. Robison next reviewed the proposed elevations, including the architectural elements,
rooflines, and types of windows. A robust mix of design elements was proposed. An
architectural priority was to ensure that the units look like large homes with architectural features
around the building to add quality and longevity to the project. Mrs. Robison next reviewed the
proposed floor plans. She noted that a six-foot concrete fence will go around the entire perimeter

reside within the project. The Code does not allow for basements or families with children. All
of the units will have two bedrooms consisting of a master bedroom and a smaller bedroom that
can serve as an office. The garages can have open lofts. Snow removal and site maintenance
will be managed by the HOA.

       Amendment Application to Create the Centrum at Bluffdale Special District (SD),
       which is located at the SW Corner of 14600 South and Porter Rockwell Boulevard,
       which Proposes Amendments to the Bluffdale City Land Use Ordinance, Title 11,
       Chapter 11.110, Special Purpose and Overlay Zones, to Create a New Zoning
       Ordinance Chapter and Map Designation from Mixed Use (MU) to SD-X The
       Centrum at Bluffdale Special District for 236 Residential Apartment Units Along
       with Various Commercial Uses, Including the Associated Development and Design
       Standards for Approximately 6.7 Acres, LH Perry Investments, LLC, Applicants
       (Application 2021-31). Staff Presenter, Grant Crowell.

City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, presented the staff report and
stated that the matter was continued from the September 15, 2021 , meeting at which time the
Planning Commission requested a copy of the Traffic Impact Study. In addition, the Planning
Commission asked that the applicant meet with the residents at The Marketplace. That meeting
took place on September 22, 2021. Mr. Crowell suggested that the public hearing be reopened .
The applicant also updated the plans and exhibits, which included the removal of the C-store.

Mr. Crowell described the changes made since the last meeting. He first displayed the
conceptual site plan, which was tentative. The site plan review would be the time to solidify the
actual site plan to be approved and implemented. Rather than a C-store, there were two retail
pads added to the conceptual plan. Mr. Crowell discussed the possibility of widening the street
by the main intersection at Rising Star Way to facilitate on-street parking.

Commissioner Walston noted that the street modifications would be the responsibility of
Bluffdale City and asked what the costs would be and the availability of the funding, along with
the potential timing of those changes. Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer, explained that
Rising Star Way currently includes eastbound and one westbound lane. The Traffic Impact
Study identified the need for an additional eastbound lane. The cost of that improvement would
be borne by the developer and not the City. As for the improvements on the east side of Porter
Rockwell Boulevard in the Rockwell Ridge Business Park, Mr. Tracer stated that there are still
options being considered for the alignment of the road. The funding source for that side of the
road was still unknown.

Mr. Crowell added that typically analyses are conducted to determine what project
improvements need to occur for a project to go forward. The funding would have to be
determined by the City Council. If a traffic light was warranted, the Utah Department of
Transportation ("UDOT") would provide full funding for the light.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Flynn, it was confinned that the current width
of the road is 30 feet. Residential streets are typically 30 feet wide. Collector roads, bike lanes,
and tum lanes result in greater width. In response to Vice-Chair Luker's question regarding the
potential enhanced width of the road, Mr. Tracer stated that the tum lane will be added to the

Commissioner Flynn asked about the availability of funds, which are controlled by the City
Council. He asked if it would be possible to purchase more width for Rising Star Way.
Mr. Crowell explained that the City purchases right-of-way depending on the public need. In
some cases, the funds come from the Impact Fee Plan, which Rising Star Way is not part of. In
some cases, some projects fall within an Economic Development Area. This property falls
within the Eastern Bluffdale Economic Development Area. Other funding goes toward projects
on the Capital Projects List, which is in the annual budget and comes from the General Fund.

Mr. Crowell explained that the widening of Rising Star Way is not listed on any of the budget or
funding sources. However, the City Council could review options and address the need if they
felt it is warranted. There would have to be findings to justify funding of any project.
Commissioner Flynn felt an urgency to address Rising Star Way because it is a current
deficiency that the City needs to address.
