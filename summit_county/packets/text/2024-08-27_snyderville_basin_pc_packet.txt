the Outlets proposed “the enhancement of existing structures together with the construction of the 
new commercial structures and related improvements . . . .”

2. Why re-zone the Outlets from the Town Center Zone to the Neighborhood Mixed Use zone?  
 
The Applicant is committed to the Outlets and the Kimball Junction community. The Snyderville 
Basin General Plan indicates that “Town Centers . . . are permitted in designated locations that are

redevelopment areas that are: [m]ixed use, [p]edestrian focused, and [t]ransit-oriented.”

3. The NMU rezone of the Outlets is consistent with the general plan and the requirements of 
the Snyderville Basin Development Code.  
 
The mission of the Snyderville Basin general plan is “to preserve natural open space and vistas, 
prevent suburban sprawl, and promote our mountain resort community.” One way this mission is 
accomplished is by clustering “density into designated mixed use areas.” These areas are referred 
to as Town Center and Resort Areas. The Outlets are located in a Town Center. Town Centers “are 
permitted in designated locations that are intended as areas where density should be concentrated.” 
And the “character of these centers, particularly the Town Centers, should be designed to reflect 
both traditional and new patterns in urban communities and phased to ensure proper growth and 
concurrency management.”

4. The Applicant has finalized site plan and projected mix of uses and seeks feedback on the same

In the last Planning Commission hearing, the Applicant presented (at a preapplication stage) the 
concept of an NMU rezone and complete redevelopment and upgrade of the Center. Since that 
hearing, the Applicant has put pencil to paper and finalized the site plan, mix of uses, unit mix and 
counts, and compliance with the requirements of the NMU zone generally.  
 
The current site plan at full redevelopment results in a reduction of the total commercial density 
down from 322,000 square feet of retail space to a total of 311,341 square feet of general

commercial square footages, it is not obligated to provide affordable housing for the commercial 
component of the project, which is grandfathered under prior approvals.

unit equivalents, resulting in 269,757 square feet of added residential density to the project. The 
proposed residential units will include a mix of market rate and affordable housing types including 
25 Studios, 146 One Bedroom, 88 Two Bedroom and 12 Three Bedroom units, along with 37

Junction Commons Phasing Plan 
Singerman Real Estate (SRE) is proud to present a Rezone and Master Plan Development Application to 
redevelop the existing Outlets Park City site into a new mixed-use neighborhood for the west side of 
Kimball Junction.   
The plan converts the asphalt sea and fortress-style outlet mall to a vibrant mixed-use development. A 
space where residents can live, work and play in the midst of retail, services, food, beverage, and 
entertainment. All connected to broader Kimball Junction through enhanced multi-use trails, improved 
transit option, and newly constructed entryways to the center.  
The evolution of the site would be accomplished in ﬁve principal steps: 
1. Lower loop retail environment upgrades that are currently underway (planned for completion 
before the holiday season) including: 
a. Added gathering areas for a better pedestrian experience.   
b. New service, food, and beverage tenants to attract locals and visitors increasing 
employee and customer dwell times on site.   
c. Improvements to the existing linear park including new lighting, gathering areas, and 
pedestrian prioritized crossings.   
d. Added local-focused tenants.  
e. A complete facelift with façade improvements.   
2. Relocate retail tenants from the upper loop to the lower loop to facilitate redevelopment. 
Construct a new entry park to connect the east and west sides of the site by foot and provide a 
gathering space with seating for adjacent food and beverage operations.  
3. Redevelopment of the large retail boxes in upper loop into a residential neighborhood featuring: 
a. A range of housing from deed-restricted aﬀordable rental units to for sale condominiums 
and townhomes (pods 1-4.)  
b. New community spaces and a neighborhood club house.  
c. Retail building pods A and B will be retained and renovated to accommodate 
neighborhood-focused uses like a café, deli/convenience store, childcare, healthcare or 
education.   
d. Improved trail connections toward Walmart and Wholefoods with (pending cooperation 
of neighboring property owners) a new trail connection toward the existing Kimball 
Transit Center, all with the goal of shortening non-vehicle travel time for the entire area. 
e. Upgraded plaza between upper and lower loops with landscaping and park-type 
amenities to provide a transition from the commercial core to the residential core of the 
property. 
f. The plaza will also feature an improved transit stop and bike-share station to serve 
residents and guests including those in the adjacent existing residential communities.  
4. Open portals through the east and west lines of shops in buildings C and D to improve existing 
building breaks and allow new sight lines and trail connections into the heart of the site from the 
residential neighborhood to the west and the commercial areas to the east.  The eastern portal 
will connect to the heavily used Millenium Trail fronting the property and shorten travel 
distances for pedestrians from the western neighborhoods to popular destinations in Kimball 
Junction.

At this moment in time, the opportunity
currently exists to re-think and redevelop the
outlet mall property. As the properties
around the outlet mall have developed, the
site is now in a unique situation that abuts
several multi-family uses, and also abuts
hotel, traditional retail, and grocery uses.
The Kimball Junction Neighborhood Plan
(The Plan) points to a direction that this
property can redevelop. Application to
change the existing zoning of T own Center
to Neighborhood Mixed Use provides the
catalyst to make this redevelopment happen
in a way consistent with the Plan.
The existing condition of the property is deﬁned by two oversized parking lots, surrounded by
regional commercial spaces that are historically focused toward outlet retail businesses. As
developed, the outlet mall is an auto-centric retail use and is entirely inward focused, turning its
back on the surrounding uses. This inward focus currently creates a wall of buildings that deters
pedestrian access to the retail from the multi-family uses located to the west of the property. The
buildings also inhibit walkability from the multi-family to the grocery, retail, and transit center that
exists to the east of the property. The Plan points out these current condition of isolated properties
as being consistent detriments to creating a neighborhood. The Plan describes neighborhood
connectivity and walkability as potential opportunities. Developing a new Master Plan and
rezoning the property will allow this site to become a connecting point for the neighborhood.
Other opportunities described in The Plan include strengthening the mix of uses, reestablishing a
traditional neighborhood building-street pattern, enhancing community spaces, and improving the
visual quality of the built environment. This proposal works to achieve these goals by removing the
second phase (the upper lot) buildings of the outlet mall and redeveloping market rate and
affordable housing that is integrated into the redevelopment of the ﬁrst phase (lower lot) of the
outlet mall.
Within the ﬁrst phase of the outlet mall, this proposal removes portions of the existing wall of
buildings and opens up connections on the east and west of the property to allow connections
to surrounding uses.
This concept is intended to create better walkability to and through the site as described in The
Plan. Designed in the existing large parking lot, is a new mixed use commercial core with more
traditional building-street patterns and enhanced community spaces, providing a place to shop and
gather in the neighborhood.
The overarching goal of this proposal is to create a place that can develop over time to become a
central part of the surrounding properties and the entire area can truly become a neighborhood.
The initial phase of the development is proposed to include a signiﬁcant supply of a ffordable
housing along with enhanced trails and transit connectivity through the new neighborhood to
adjoining amenities and neighbors.

Proposal 
 
The applicant is requesting to amend Lots 6 and 7 internal boundaries of the White Pine 
Ranches Subdivision (Phases 1 and 2). The amendment would also result in widening of the 
White Pine Lane right-of-way (a private road). The purpose of the amendment is to 
accommodate the right-of-way for the previously approved “Vehicle control gate” at the 
entrance to the White Pine Ranches Subdivision. Since Lot 6 is located in the Phase 1 
Subdivision Plat and Lot 7 is located in the Phase 2 Subdivision Plat, both plats are required to 
be amended.  
54
2 
 
Vicinity Map 
 
 
 
Background 
White Pine Ranches Subdivision consists of 12 lots and is located at the White Pine Canyon 
Road and White Pine Lane intersection in the Canyons neighborhood. The subdivision was 
approved as part of two phases. Phase 1 was recorded in 1983 and Phase 2 was recorded in 
1992. All 12 lots are accessible via White Pine Lane, a private cul-de-sac owned by the White 
Pine Ranches HOA. The subdivision is primarily bordered by single-family residential uses and is 
also bordered by the Park City Ski Resort’s Tombstone Lift.  
On April 9, 2024, the Planning Commission approved a Conditional Use Permit to allow for a 
“Vehicle control gate” at the entrance to the White Pine Ranches Subdivision. One of the 
conditions of approval was that the applicant was required to amend the subdivision to 
accommodate the right-of-way for the proposed improvements.  
Analysis and Findings 
 
Before an amendment to a recorded subdivision plat can be approved, it shall comply with the 
standards set forth in Section 10-3-18 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code: 
 
Grounds for Changing a Plat:

3 
 
1. If the County Manager or Land Use Authority is satisfied that neither the public nor any 
person will be materially injured by the proposed vacation, alteration or amendment, 
and that there is good cause for the vacation, alteration or amendment, the County 
Manager or Land Use Authority, may vacate, alter or amend the plat, any portion of the 
plat, or any road or lot. 
Analysis: On April 9, 2024, the Planning Commission approved a Conditional Use Permit 
to allow for a “Vehicle control gate” at the entrance to the White Pine Ranches 
Subdivision. The Planning Commission found that the applicant demonstrated a need for 
a vehicle control gate to effectively control an ongoing health, safety, and welfare 
situation caused by patrons to the Park City Mountain Resort. In order to accommodate 
for the approved vehicle control gate, the applicant must accommodate additional right-
of-way for White Pine Lane. The proposed plat amendment would uphold the Planning 
Commission’s decision for a vehicle control gate to effectively control an ongoing health, 
safety, and welfare situation and there is a good cause. COMPLIES AS CONDITIONED 
2. No plat amendment shall be approved which results in an increase in density.

Ranches Subdivision. COMPLIES
