         The purpose of the work session was to receive an update on COVID-19 from the Weber/Morgan Health Department;
review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; discuss the proposed rezone of 1400 and 1475
West Midland Drive; review youth proposal for creation of Ogden Youth Council; discuss arts grants; discuss Council business;

Agenda Review
         Chair Choberka and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6 :00 p.m. The Council heard from the Chair of the Airport Advisory Committee, Mike Nichols, regarding
the Committee's recommendation that the Council not approve the proposed increase to lease rates at the Airport. The
Committee's discussion of the fee proposal centered on long-term airport needs as well as challenges lessors are current facing
due to the COVID-19 pandemic. He referenced a letter included in the Council's meeting packet that details the Committee's
concerns and objections to the fee increase. Chair Choberka invited Administration to respond to the information provided by Mr.
Nichols. Mayor Caldwell thanked Mr. Nichols for the information provided; he noted this is the first he is hearing of some of the
concerns of the Committee and he committed to research the issues with appropriate staff in order to provide an informed
response.

Proposed Rezone - 1400 and 1475 West Midland Drive
          Council Deputy Director Symes explained a petition has been filed by David Taotua to rezone property at 3048 South
14 75 West from R-1-10 to M-2. In addition to the petitioner's property, the Planning Commission is recommending the rezone of
eight additional properties in an area south of Midland Drive between 1400 and 1475 West. The properties were part of the 2018
West Ogden annexation and were zoned R-1-10 when incorporated due to the residential use. Prior to annexation, the properties
were zoned M-1 under Weber County's zoning. Mr. Taotua has indicated that he wishes to construct a contractor's shop building
on the property, which the R-1-10 zoning does not allow. In reviewing the petition, Planning staff and the Planning Commission
determined that the additional properties that had been zoned R-1-10 upon annexation should be rezoned to M-2 along with the
petitioner's property. Inquiries were made of the other property owners and all who responded indicated they were in favor of the
rezoning.
          Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the intent of the petition. The
subject property is currently zoned R-1-10 and the petition's original proposal was to rezone the property to the M-2 zoning
designation to allow for development of a contractor shop building. This original proposal was considered during the December 4,
2019 Planning Commission meeting and community members who own property within the R-1-10 zone along 1400 West and
1475 West voiced their desires to rezone their property to M-2. Following the meeting, property owners were notified and given
the opportunity to provide a response to the proposal with the explanation that if the property were rezoned, residential units
would become nonconfonning. Five of the eight property owners responded and indicated they would like their property to be
rezoned to the M-2 designation; combined, the residential properties within this R-1-10 zone occupy a total of 3.9 acres ofland.
Prior to 2018, the property was unincorporated and zoned M-2 by Weber county; it was annexed into the City in 2018 and
consisted of25 parcels with 10 single-family homes along 1400 West and 1475 West. The homes were zoned R-1-10 and the
remainder of the property was zoned M-2. During the 2018 annexation process, the decision regarding zoning took into
consideration the avoidance ofnonconfonnity and the need to offer some protection from surrounding manufacturing uses for the
existing homes in the areas. New owners have purchased these homes and have entered the area knowing that it is largely made up
of manufacturing uses. Nonconfonning homes lose their right of use once they have been vacant for a year. The areas to be
rezoned serve as a transition between residential and manufacturing uses; there are currently two buildings under construction
along 1475 West: a manufacturing building that fronts Midland Drive; and a pole barn located directly north of the applicant's
property in a R-1-10 zone. Mr. Montgomery presented photographs of the applicant's property as well as properties surrounding it
and indicated the Planning Commission's consideration of the application was focused on compliance with the City's General
Plan and zoning policies; they considered whether the rezone creates an edge or appropriate buffering between types ofuses and
if the rezone request reflects the character of the area. The voted unanimously to rezone the entire R-1-10 area to M-2.
          Mr. Symes stated that if the Council is comfortable proceeding with fonnal consideration of the petition, the matter can
be scheduled for the August 4 business meeting.

Youth Proposal for Creation of Ogden Youth Council
         Amir Jackson and students involved in Nurture the Creative Mind provided a PowerPoint presentation to discuss their
proposal to create an Ogden Youth Council. Nurture the Creative Mind Ambassador, Mercedes Rantan, stated the mission of the
Ogden City Youth Council would be to empower youth, advocate for a more impactful future, and prepare the leaders of
tomorrow. This group could bring the community together and allow the youth to have a voice in the community, government,
and future. The youth council will also provide fresh insight, encourage other youth to participate in local and State govermnent,
and help to increase voting turnout rates. The benefits and objectives of the proposal to create a youth council include:
              • Brings the community together
              • Increased youth community service
              • Develops mature citizenship
              • Promotes leadership
              • Deeper understanding of the political system
              • Provides a sense of personal achievement
              • Encourages innovative thinking and critical problem solving
         Youth selected to be part of the council would make an 18-month commitment; they would need to be between the ages
of 14 and 18. The group would be made up ofno more than 12 members and terms would be capped at two. The group would be
governed by a chair, vice chair, secretary, and treasurer and these individuals would make up the executive committee. Other

Arts Grants
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey explained each year, the Ogden City Council appropriates grant funding to various local
arts entities and programs. The Ogden Arts Advisory Committee and Administration review each application and provide
recommendations to the City Council. The Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 budget includes $100,000 for aits grants.
          Ogden City Arts Coordinator Buckley explained the Administration, along with the Ogden City Arts Advisory
Committee, has submitted its recommendations for arts grants. The recommendations include summaries and analysis from the
committee that satisfy state law regarding the study required. There were 3 8 total applications this year, and the Administration is
recommending that most of these organizations receive some level of funding, with the exception of nine (9) applications that
were deemed ineligible based on criteria or Arts Grants subcommittee scoring. The grants are a combination of general support
and project support. She referenced the summary of each arts grant proposal, which was included in the meeting packet. She noted
the grant program is becoming more competitive as proven by the total number of applications and the quality of the projects for
which applicants are seeking funding.
          Chair Choberka noted that she noticed several projects that will receive funding are through the Weber State University
(WSU) Arts Department. She disclosed that her husband does a lot of work with WSU and her action on this item could be viewed
as a potential conflict of interest; however, she declared that her husband is not in a position ofreceiving any financial gain from
any of the grant awards.
          Ms. Mabey asked Ms. Buckley to briefly discuss modifications to the reporting requirements for the grant program
responsive to COVID-19. Ms. Buckley stated that some grant recipients that received money for live events have been given an
extension of time or the opportunity to amend their programming to ensure that they can still proceed with their project and utilize
the grant funding they received from the City.
          Brief concluding discussion centered on the scoring and rating criteria used by the City to evaluate each grant
application, after which Ms. Mabey reviewed the timeline for considering the proposed arts grants awards, noting that final action
can take place during the August 4 business meeting.
