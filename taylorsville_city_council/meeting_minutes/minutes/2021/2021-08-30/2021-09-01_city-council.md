14           1.2      Mayor’s Report
15
16         Despite being a non-golfer, Mayor Kristie Overson attended a recent ChamberWest golf
17   tournament to talk with business owners and find out what the city could do to assist them during
18   these difficult times. She later joined the Chamber at a ribbon-cutting for a remodel at Granite
19   Credit Union on 4700 South.
20
21   She and Wayne Harper had recently had lunch with Congressman Burgess Owens at Greek House.
22   Congressman Owens had a keen interest in small business and was able to meet with Winston Asif
23   and hear his story. She enjoyed the opportunity to show him how much Taylorsville valued its
24   businesses, both large and small.
25
26        She and Councilmembers Burgess and Harker had attended Granite School Districts budget
27   lunch, where good questions were asked about how the district came to the numbers that they had.
28   She also enjoyed the opportunity to meet new superintendent Dr. Rich Nye and discuss issues that
29   involved Taylorsville, particularly Taylorsville High school.
30
31   Regarding the project on 5400 South Bangerter that was once the home to Kmart, the sale of the
32   property had been finalized. The developer’s plans were well underway, and the project had been
33   named Volta which was a Greek word that referred to a promenade or leisurely walk. She was
34   excited about the name because it encompassed the exact feel and lifestyle that she anticipated
35   would be created by the development.
36
37         Mayor Overson had met with the Arts Council Board of Trustees which included Chair
38   Cochran. They enjoyed hearing what the Arts Council had in store for the coming season. Peter
39   Pan, Jr. had recently sold out and both she and Councilmember Harker had attended one of the
40   shows at the Mid-Valley Performing Arts Center.

 1
 2   Boy Scout Troop #117 regularly met at the Taylorsville Museum and in exchange for the use of
 3   the meeting space, they offered their services to the Historic Preservation committee to assist with
 4   work needing to be done on site. The city had found some old and tattered American flags which
 5   were then turned over to the troop whose members would retire them in a respectful and dignified
 6   manner.
 7
 8   The city was preparing to potentially work remotely once again, testing things out to ensure that
 9   to the extent possible, the city was prepared to serve the citizens from home if need be. She gave
10   credit to Human Resources Director Stephanie Snow, Scott Harrington and I.T. for ensuring that
11   the city could function well remotely if needed.
12
13         During the month of August, 162 business license applications had been submitted with 187
14   being issued. Code Enforcement had opened 113 new cases and resolved the same number. There
15   were still 527 active cases. In Development, eleven plan applications had been submitted with
16   five completed. The Building Department issued 169 building permits and there were currently
17   35 active permits.
18
19   Mayor Overson explained how a former Taylorsville High School student felt that the U.S. military
20   should have a lounge for their use at the Salt Lake International Airport. He started writing letters
21   to those he felt could make the lounge a reality a couple of years ago, then came a petition and it
22   now appeared that his idea would become a reality.
23
24   Some city residents had gotten together to clean up a small portion of I-215. The freeways were
25   the responsibility of the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) and she hated when she did
26   not have the authority to act on a problem that affected city residents. She was appreciative of the
27   folks that had taken it upon themselves to take care of a problem.
28
29   Another resident had arranged for a walk-through of Gary C. Swensen Valley Regional Park with
30   not only the mayor but Salt Lake County Councilmember Aimee Winder Newton who brought
31   along some individuals from the County’s parks management team. They spent an hour discussing
32   parks operations and the needs of various parks.

21           3.1      Taylorsville Dayzz Report – Jim Dunnigan
22
23         Mr. Dunnigan expressed his gratitude to the council and was glad that Taylorsville Dayzz
24   had taken place in late June before COVID-19 began surging again. He introduced the committee
25   members who were present and explained a bit about what each individual brought to Taylorsville
26   Dayzz.
27
28   He mentioned what a great job the police and fire departments had done, and the police told him
29   there were no “reportable” events that took place during the event. He presented some interesting
30   statistics regarding the effort it took to plan and work the event. Not counting the work put in by
31   the actual committee members, he explained that:
32       • There were 413 individuals who volunteered their time
33       • Those individuals contributed a total of 1267 hours of time
34       • The volunteers came from 17 different organizations
35       • 1,000 mini-Bundt cakes had been distributed to people wishing to celebrate Taylorsville’s
36            25th birthday as a city

38       • 17 live cannons were set off when the Utah Symphony performed Tchaikovsky’s 1812
39            Overture

 7            3.2     Parks and Recreation Report – Jared Smith
 8
 9        Mr. Smith explained that there needed to be a discussion about park hours and ensuring they
10   were consistent throughout the city. He said that he had met with Chief Cottam, and they felt that
11   6am to 11pm would be ideal. Mr. Smith believed that 11pm was more objective than the word
12   “dusk.” He also mentioned that not all lights in the pavilions were operational and in some cases
13   were non-existent. He felt this was a public safety issue.
14
15   He also wished to know why portable restrooms were brought in by leagues using the baseball and
16   softball fields rather than using the permanent facilities already on site. He felt the leagues allowed
17   the portable restrooms to remain in place too long without being picked up. Mr. Taylor explained
18   that was a matter for Salt Lake County to address since they were apparently issuing permits to
19   include portable restrooms.
20
21   Mr. Smith expressed his appreciation to the city for adding sidewalks to the area around Vista
22   Park.
23
24   He wrapped up his report by providing an update of the Labrum Park project, including photos of
25   many of the volunteers.
