Item 1. Discussion with Kevyn Smeltzer, Salt Lake County, Regarding Pavement Management Plan

addition, they would like projection for next year.

Mr. Smeltzer informed the Council the asphalt paving and slurring is complete, but he has to check on

brought in another contractor to help with the project without notifying the County.

Mr. Smeltzer addressed the location where individuals drove through the slurry seal. He noted it cost

contractor the City’s appreciation. The Mayor also addressed a parked car on the street that interfered

Discussion followed regarding the striping of the roads. It was said everyone needed to be on the same
page. Mr. Smeltzer indicated he was happy to be involved. Mayor Silvestrini said he appreciated Mr.

something.

Mayor Silvestrini inquired if Mr. Smeltzer had a plan for next year. Mr. Smeltzer said a lot of things
changed for them. They are going to revamp how they do things. He explained they used to have the
County budget; however, now they need to get the City’s plan and set those budgets up front.

Mr. Smeltzer said that part of the Pavement Plan was looking at all the arterial roads and doing a five to

Item 2. Discussion with Nick Schou, Utah Rivers Council, Rainwater Harvesting Program

Mr. Schou, Utah Rivers . He explained the purpose of their program was to have a partnership with local
entities to enable residents to get rain barrels for emergency supply and to harvest water for their use.

Mr. Schou indicated that he had shown the presentation to Mayor Silvestrini and Council Member
Jackson, and he would like to discuss rainwater harvesting. He discussed how rainwater harvesting came
about. He explained that Utah’s water usage is the highest water usage per person in the United States,
which is twice the national average. He explained that water in Utah is utilized 70 percent in the
municipal sector outside the home. Mr. Schou noted that rain barrels were an opportunity to educate
the consumer and there are a lot of cities that offer rain barrels to use outside the home and improve
the water quality.

Mr. Schou informed the Council that rain water harvesting is legal state-wide up to 2,500 gallons per
parcel. He said water harvesting is easy for the residents, and it’s affordable. In addition, they partner
with the municipalities, and it incentivizes residents to do it. Furthermore, rain harvesting can help
municipalities get compliance with their EPA requirements and water quality.

Mr. Schou discussed rain harvesting in other communities. He stated it was almost an immediate
success in Murray City and the County; the program went viral, and they had 500 hits an hour and 6,000
hits a day on their website. He noted since 2015, the program has sold 3,300 barrels and interacted with
over 2,000 households.

Mr. Schou said Utah Rivers Council created a website, and they drove everyone to the site to purchase
the barrels online. The barrels they sold were $50 or there is an option for a barrel, which wholesales at
$75 and retails at $150. When the cities partner with Utah Rivers Council, the city subsidizes the
program by paying $25 of the $75 the residents pay. He informed the Council there is always a limited
quantity; they, the company, tell us the quantities per household allowed.

Mr. Schou indicated they receive a little help marketing the rain barrels, and it is just social media. The
rain barrels are purchased up front, and then they order the amount ordered. Council Member Jackson
inquired what a minimum number would be. Mr. Schou stated they could order a minimum of 100
barrels of the $75 barrel.

Mr. Schou explained the company delivers the barrels on a Saturday, and they have a “barrel party”; the
barrels are distrusted over a three to four hour period. They provide some information, put the barrel(s)
in the car, and let the residents go. He stated their goal was to make a real impact and provide water
conservation education.

Mr. Schou explained they had met with every city in the valley over the past three years; in addition,
they are working on meeting with two dozen more cities. He went on to say, they understand the
budget process, and they give cities the time they need to make a decision.

Council Member Marchant stated there were other way to conserve water, and he pointed them out.
He said there should be more than a barrel; he questioned if this should be an education system.

Mr. Schou said he agreed; however, it was a foot in the door for them, and it gives them the ability to
talk about water conservation. He added their site has dozens of tips too.

Mayor Silvestrini pointed out this is a pilot program, and the City would receive credits on its storm
water management program. He thought the Council should consider it and discuss it. It made a lot of
sense to him and it was educational. Mr. Schou said that is what he liked best about the program; itis a
lot of education.

Mayor Silvestrini explained the barrels have screens on them too so they do not get mosquitos in them,
and they have a locking mechanism to keep children out of them.

Mr. Schou indicated these barrels were the most user-friendly barrels they could find. In addition, they
were 100 percent recyclable. They are trying to make the program easy, fun and affordable.

Mrs. Lund inquired if the barrels would fit in a normal size car. Mr. Schou explained the barrels stack
inside of each other; he said he fit three barrels inside a Prius.

Item 3b. Discussion with Kent Merrell, Millcreek Business Academ

Mr. Kent Merrell addressed the Council regarding his business, which helps companies train their
employees. He noted he went to the Salt Lake County records when Millcreek was becoming a city, and
found that Millcreek has roughly 3,000 businesses. He said he looked at the businesses, and he thought
to himself, what could they use, what could enhance them? That was when they created the Millcreek
Business Academy. He explained it is 12 on-line training courses, and it would be offered to every
business in Millcreek at a cost of .50 cents per month, per business. He suggested the cost of the courses
come from the business license fee the business Owners are already paying.

It was Mr. Merrell’s opinion the City would receive more sales tax and a better perception if business
owners went through these on-line courses. He added another part of the program would include a
business directory, and each business would be able to access their own business and add a biography.
In addition, there would be a section on rules and regulations of the City.

Council Member Jackson inquired what would be accessible to the general public. Mr. Merrell indicated
everything would be with the exception of the courses.

Council Member Uipi question how many employees Mr. Merrell had; did he plan to recruit; and would
he be the vendor for the City app? Mr. Merrell indicated as of right now, he had three employees, and
he needed to add a customer service representative. The rest of his business is IT driven. He went on to
say, they are in the process of building the app in-house.

Council Member Uipi inquired if Mr. Merrill’s business was a Chamber replacement; she noted the City
is being courted by a lot of other organizations right now. Mr. Merrell indicated the previous things he
discussed is what he could offer, and the he would receive all of the information from the City.

Council Member Uipi questioned what the per course payment would be if an individual wanted to take
a course. Mr. Merrell indicated a course would probably be in the $79 range.

Council Member Uipi stated what was being proposed is very similar to a Chamber but out of the box.
Mr. Merrell’s opinion was that a business has to be very dedicated to join a Chamber; there are
thousands of businesses but only 170 join the Chamber. He thought the Chamber only appealed to
certain businesses.

Mayor Silvestrini clarified it would cost .50 cents per month per business. Mr. Merrell explained it would
not cost the businesses within Millcreek anything; Millcreek would pay for the courses and the cost
would be coming out of their business license fee.

Ms. Uipi queried what would be the main draw for the City’s businesses to use this on-line program;
there are a lot of on-line courses to choose from. Mr. Merrell indicated this program is designed for the
businesses in this location, and it is vetted. He noted the business owners have to want to improve their
business.

Council Member Jackson inquired if there is an interactive component to his program. Mr. Merrell they
could develop that. They are hoping to make the program priceless for people.

Council Member Uipi said the City is trying to assist the businesses; she questioned how Mr. Merrell had
been able to gain a needs base service or go about creating it? Mr. Merrell explained they would
present it as if they are a business owner; he had a business with 33 employees for years. He went on to
say, as he went to different individuals, he asked what are they called on to do; what do people need;
and they had gone off the fact of what people need and what are they buying.

Mayor Silvestrini indicated he thought the idea was interesting; as the Council got together he thought
they will be able to determine what is needed. Council Member Marchant added he thought the idea
was like a super Chamber of Commerce. It provided training and information.

Mr. Geilmann explained to the Council that he thought what Mr. Merrill is presenting falls under
procurement of services, which would require the bidding process.
