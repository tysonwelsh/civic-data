Grid Study Update (00:02:37) Jon Nepstad presented the MPO statewide grid study, a legislative initiative aimed at developing an optimal transportation network across Utah's four MPOs. The study focuses on analyzing current transportation systems, brainstorming improvements, and creating a live map of potential grid enhancements. By emphasizing travel demand, accessibility, and eﬃciency, the team seeks to create a more interconnected transportation network. Jon Nepstad stressed the preliminary nature of their proposals and actively encouraged feedback from local experts, with the goal of developing a refined list of recommendations by September and completing the study in the fall. Wetland Mapping Presentation (00:19:02) Peter Goodwin from the Utah Geological Survey presented a comprehensive wetland mapping project for Utah Valley, updating 7,000 features across 120,000 acres and replacing 30-40 year old mapping. The project provides a reliable estimate of probable wetlands, useful for permitting, real estate, zoning, and recreational planning. By analyzing wetland functions and loss patterns, the team created updated mapping through the National Wetland Inventory mapper and a UGS wetlands web app. Jessica Stern, a new state wetland coordinator, invited feedback from the committee about their use of wetland data and potential future research needs. TIP Modification - Orem 1600 N/400 E Roundabout - Scope Change (00:33:15) Bob Allen presented a TIP Modification request from Orem City for the 1600 North 400 East roundabout project. Originally planned as a roundabout to improve pedestrian safety at the Murdoch Canal Trail crossing, further analysis determined a grade-separated crossing would be more effective. Due to the high cost of a grade separation, Orem City is requesting to modify the project scope to implement short-term pedestrian safety improvements. The estimated cost is $360,000 to $400,000, with any remaining funds to be returned to the funding pool. Jason Bond moved to recommend that the MPO Board approve modifications to the Orem 1600 N 400 E Roundabout project to include pedestrian intersection improvements as presented. Corridor Preservation Provo 800 North (00:38:40) Calvin Clark presented a Corridor Preservation fund request from Provo City for a property at 825 West 800 North. The single-family home has an appraised value of $700,000, with total requested funds of $700,575. The property is located along the Provo 800 North corridor (RTP corridor H 42) and is needed for the ultimate project. Vern Keeslar from Provo City confirmed the property's relevance to the bridge work, noting they will temporarily close nearby 850 West and allow the current residents to continue living there. Richard Nielson moved to recommend that the MPO Board approve the city of Provo's request for $700,575 from the Corridor Preservation Fund, finding that it adheres to the fund's requirements. John Dorny seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. RTP: Population and Employment 2023 Estimates Review (00:42:25) Tim Hereth presented the RTP population and employment 2023 estimates and review, emphasizing the importance of accurate base year data for transportation modeling. The process involves updating building information, applying American Community Survey data, and using real estate market models to locate households and population. Tim Hereth explained that they will be sending out an app for TAC members to review population, household, and employment data at the TAC level, with the option to review individual parcels. The goal is to ensure accurate calibration of travel demand models, with data expected to be ready in the next two weeks and a review period of two to three weeks. It was stressed the importance of this process, particularly in reviewing employment data, and encouraged members to review the data thoroughly with their planning departments. MAG MPO FY2026 Work Plan (UPWP) Draft (00:52:24) LaNiece Davenport presented the MAG MPO FY 2026 Unified Planning Work Program (UPWP), a federal requirement for receiving transportation planning funds. She highlighted the document's key elements, including 10 federal planning factors and four regional goals: improving transportation choices, promoting housing options, ensuring parks and open spaces, and enhancing city and town centers. The UPWP outlines the MPO's accomplishments, with 87% of 154 major tasks completed in the previous year. The FY 2026 planning priorities include developing a regional policy framework, advancing the grid transportation network (aligned with Senate Bill 195), improving data in transportation planning, and implementing a comprehensive safety action plan. The document details five core programs and provides extensive information on specific work tasks, planning factors, and anticipated products. LaNiece noted that the plan will go through a 30-day public comment period and will be brought back for adoption at the next meeting Other Business and Adjournment (01:06:31) Chair Jered Johnson stated the next MPO TAC meeting is scheduled for June 23, 2025.
