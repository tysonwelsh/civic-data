   1. APPROVE THE FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF
      WEBER COUNTY AMENDING THE FEE ORDINANCE SECTION 16-2-11- SHERIFF’S OFFICE.
         Julie Stoddard (Sheriff’s Office): In doing a thorough review of our fee schedule we have a couple of items
         that we would like to modify. First, we discovered that credit card processing fees were not listed on the fee
         schedule and should have been. That is an 11% fee that is just passed on from the vendor we use. We would
         like to make a couple of changes to the GRAMA request fee schedule. We're finding that the audio and video
         recordings are taking significant time and we would like to change the fee from $35 per case to $35 per
         deputy or vehicle to allow for the additional time they have to do if there are multiple deputies or multiple
         vehicles involved. With the police, incident, and accident reports, we would like to up that to $50, from $15,
         because of the amount of time we're spending on that. Under the offender fees, the haircuts need to be
         updated to $10 and we'd like to add beard trims for $4. We would like to change the prescription to inmates
         to it varies depending on the nature of the prescription similar to the over-the-counter meds. And last, due to
         our new pay structure with the deputies, we would like to increase the fee we charge for overtime for the
         deputies from $63.60 to $98 an hour. We would like to remove all the additional ranks because typically we
         thought that they would ask for a sergeant to oversee that but that has not been the case. So, we'd like to just
         do a flat $98 overtime fee for both internal and external customers.

         Commissioner Bolos: I do have a couple of questions on the credit card processing fees. 11%?

         Ms. Stoddard: That's correct. That is what the company who handles our inmate trust as well as our civil
         processing charges us. But they handle all of the chargebacks. And all of the negotiations. We don't have to
         do anything with it. So, there's zero risk to us. And that's why it's a high rate.

         Commissioner Bolos: One other question on the audio and video recordings. $35. Well, yeah. $35 going
         from per case to deputy or vehicle. Is that $35 an hour?

         Ms. Stoddard: No that would that would be to do all of our redactions for the video. The $35 would be per
         deputy and the vehicle they're driving for that video. So if there's seven deputies that show up and there's two
         hours of video on each deputy, that would go towards recouping that cost of staff time to redact those videos
         and get those put out.

         Commissioner Bolos: This is my hang up. I don't want it to go toward the cost. I feel like it should cover the
         cost. If it's $35 per deputy, but you're spending six hours doing that, that's not compensating us for our time.

         Chief Colby Ryan: To go with the state law, we have to average our costs on the time actually spent by the
         lowest paid employee that can do that process. With the new program we have, it probably cut in half of
         what it would take a year ago to do that.

    2. APPROVAL OF AN AGREEMENT WITH MHTN ARCHITECTS FOR ARCHITECTURAL,
       ENGINEERING, INTERIOR, LANDSCAPE, SPECIALTY DESIGN, AND BUILDING
       PERFORMANCE SERVICES ASSOCIATED WITH THE WEBER CENTER RENOVATION
       PROJECT.
         Scott Mendoza (Community Development): Weber Center Feasibility Study has been conducted so far with
         results showing improvements available for both the employees and the public at the Weber Center, including
         making the County offices easier to access and find, improve office layouts for increased efficiency and safety,
         building performance, and parking. The design process is ready to continue by preparing schematics designs
         and then construction documents, eventually leading to the construction phase which hopefully can begin in
         December or January. The cost of MHTN’s services is $1,844,465.

         Commissioner Bolos: The number one reason for me why we are doing this is the safety of our employees.
         Improving wayfinding and the public’s experience in our office is secondary.

         Commissioner Harvey: I know of other counties that have spent four and five times this amount of money for
         the same effort. We will have the same results but with far less billed to the taxpayers. Scott, thank you to
         you and your team.
         Chair Froerer: The County’s prior parking structure had to be torn down and our commitment to Ogden City
         was to replace it due to the number of stalls required for this building. We acquired additional property
         adjoining the Weber Center allowing us to use on-site parking versus a new parking structure, which structure
         was estimated to cost $25M-$30M. The money saved allows us to focus on the internal structure of the Weber
         Center and accomplish the vision we have for the public and our employees.
