The following are the minutes of the Planning Commission Meeting held on Thursday, August 20, 2020 at
6:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 W. Herriman Main Street (12090 South), Herriman,
Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in City Hall, on the City’s website, and
delivered to members of the Commission and media.

Presiding: Chair Chris Berbert

Commission Members Present Electronically: Brody Rypien, Adam Jacobson, Andy Powell, Lorin Palmer,
Joy Kaseke, Heather Garcia

Commission Members Absent: Jackson Ferguson, Andrea Bradford

Staff Present in Person: Communication Specialist Destiny Skinner, Deputy Recorder Wendy Thorpe

Staff Present Electronically: Assistant City Planner MacDonald, City Engineer Jonathan Bowers, City
Attorney Chase Andrizzi, Engineer Josh Peterson, Planner Sheldon Howa and Assistant City Manager Wendy
Thomas.



Chair Chris Berbert called the work meeting to order at 6:05 pm.




Assistant City Planner MacDonald informed the Commission there were no City Council updates.

Assistant City Planner MacDonald said the trail item had been reviewed before and there were no new
changes. The last three items were regarding the Herriman Corners precast wall. That applicant requested a
modified fence requirement, due to the preexisting fence. Commissioner Jacobson expressed frustration with
developers requesting amendments for items that have already been decided by the Commission. Chair Berbert
added that pre-existing rail fences would not change the requirements. Perhaps staff could inform the
applicants that fence amendments would be very likely declined by the Commission. The fence/wall
requirement should be standardized. Assistant City Planner MacDonald floated the idea of requesting
developers to appeal the decision, instead of requesting amendments. City Attorney Chase Andrizzi agreed. He
added any change to existing conditional use required review of the entire use, for future inquiries.
Commissioner Jacobson voiced future homeowners could complain about the area not meeting City Code.
Commissioner Rypien expressed he did not see a need for the precast wall in that location, near the open
space. He thought the open space buffer was enough, and the precast wall was not necessary. Assistant City
Planner MacDonald stated in the future staff would request developers to appeal the decision within ten days,
instead of allowing them to request amendments from the Commission.


Assistant City Planner MacDonald requested feedback for creation of a new manufacturing zone, to fill a hole
in the zoning ordinance. This zone would be for light manufacturing among other uses. Chair Berbert stated
the importance of compatible uses and asked the intention for the zone. Assistant City Planner MacDonald
responded either C-3 zone or separate manufacturing or flex space. It would be a different type of commercial
and not retail or manufacturing. Chair Berbert said he is more interested in architectural style and not having
manufacturing next to commercial. Manufacturing zones have very few architectural requirements.
Commissioners discussed the usefulness of flex zones.

Current City ordinance simply states 40% brick or stone was required on the front of houses, with a current
debate of how best to measure. Modification of the standards may also be up for consideration. Assistant City
Planner MacDonald requested Commissioners think about how to measure the brick or stone requirement.
They were also asked to consider possible ordinance changes. Some Commissioners proposed measuring
surface area, not including the garage. Commissioner Jacobson stressed the importance of a contiguous look
around the visible sides. Other material types were discussed. Chair Berbert stressed that other materials were
required to be an equal or superior product. Commissioners discussed incorporation of porches and other
design elements as options. Commissioner Rypien talked about neighborhoods with lack of design and color
variation. The current standard created many similar looking houses.

format be sent to them, so they may add comments.

to the Springville Reservoir. Each resident receives a pass, non-residents pay $10 to park. Commissioner Adam
Jacobson agreed the trails were meant to draw people to Herriman but the City needed to recoup costs. Many
canyons charge use fees. Chair Berbert said parking problems would get worse as the population continued to

Assistant City Planner Macdonald explained that items 3.2, 3.3 and 3.4 would be discussed simultaneously.
Generally subdivision amendments were not public hearings, however due to the nature of the request, a
public hearing had been noticed. She reviewed the location where the precast wall was required. The applicant
requested to not build the wall around the detention pond. A three tier vinyl fence was already installed on a
portion of the area. Pictures of the current fence were displayed. This was a condition required for lot four, lot
seven, and the subdivision. Three separate motions were required for the amendment. Commission needed to
determine if the existing 3-rail vinyl fence provided an adequate buffer, or if the condition of approval should
remain and the pre-cast wall be built.

Applicant Dave Murdock with Northpoint Construction said they requested to not continue the fence. He said
when the subdivision application was originally approved, he worked with the City and was told where the
fence should end. He was not aware an additional wall was required. He was willing to install a gate along the
south fence.

Chair Berbert opened the public hearing.

Deputy Recorder Thorpe read the following comment:

Thank you for your willingness to serve our community. I do not agree with the proposal to remove
the condition to build a precast wall along the property line. The wall will not destroy the open space as
the applicant claims any more than all the traffic, noise and light from the development has already
destroyed the park-like setting. The cars shine their headlights into the park. The Ace building shines a
very bright light into our neighborhood. The only option we will agree to is to have mature evergreens
planted on the edge of the park to hide the traffic, noise and lights, with a cash bond held by the city for
one year to ensure the trees survive, with the landscaping plan submitted to the city for review and
approval. And the lighting on the Ace building needs to be turned off every night when the store closes.
The applicant knew what the requirements were and can either renegotiate all the conditions, or live with
the existing conditions. Letting the applicant make the change as he is requesting is to place a higher
priority on someone that does not live in this community than the residents who suffer the impacts of his
development. Thanks again for making Herriman a great place for its residents and ensuring that our high
standards continue to be met.
Sincerely,
David and Lisa Dobbins

Kip Webster requested that the fence be continued past his property line with trees as a barrier. He was
concerned about kids going through the fence.

Derek Rasmussen supported trees along the fence. The light from nearby business distracted from the nice
neighborhood. He does not think a six foot wall is necessary along the section near American Burger.

Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Rypien supported the fence transition from the detention pond to the businesses with trees to
reduce light and noise. He thought the precast wall would create a fortress and limit pedestrian access.

Commissioner Jacobson stated he remembered making the original motion and thought they had been
very clear it should be extended past the old Subway location, but staff stated it could not extend that far.
The Holiday Oil by RSL proposed a detention pond behind the gas station, as a buffer. The Commission
diligently considered and decided the location needed a precast wall. The Ace Hardware lighting was
supposed to be landscape only. He stressed the City needed to get involved drastically and enforce
installation of the precast wall as outlined.

Commissioner Powell proposed installing the wall along the Webster property and leaving the rest open. Chair
Berbert expressed concern as that section was not owned by this developer. He added the precast wall would
provide a safer space for children and prevent crimes of opportunity from the backyards of residents. He
added the City Ordinance needed to be enforced. Commissioner Rypien expressed he was strongly against the
August 20, 2020 – Planning Commission Minutes                                                  Page 7 of 9


precast wall, as it would prevent access and walkability for nearby residents. Commissioner Jacobson
responded that lots of kids play in the detention pond, and he identified a trail next to the subway dumpster,
which would provide access. If anything the precast wall should be installed and trees should be planted as a
buffer.

Commissioner Rypien would like to discuss walkability more at a later date. Commissioners debated the use of
natural barriers versus precast walls. Chair Berbert added open fields were nice but safety was more important,
in this situation. Commissioner Jacobson requested for staff to look into landscape and lighting requirements
for the burger place and Ace Hardware. He thought a wall and landscaping were required on the commercial
side to blend the wall, into the surrounding area. He thought the dumpster was in the wrong location and
motions were not being enforced. Assistant City Planner MacDonald stated she listened to the meetings to
verify the motions, and stated the approval letters were in the packet. Commissioner Jacobson requested for
the Ace Hardware lights to be turned off at night.

to be considered as buffers between uses. Commissioners requested which uses required precast walls.
Assistant City Planner MacDonald responded between residential and commercial zones and between
residential and commercial uses. Precast walls were not required between similar uses. Commissioner Brody
Rypien wants a continuous feel. Chair Berbert mentioned the area at 5600 West and Tuscany area north of
Silver Crest as an example. It has a precast wall, but inside the neighborhood there is a big, open park and the
August 20, 2020 – Planning Commission Minutes                                           Page 9 of 9


area flows. He offered that in most areas a precast wall or fence makes sense between residential and
commercial. Commissioner Rypien talked about areas along Rosecrest with large boulders and landscaping
that feel more open. Commissioner Rypien offered to collect his thoughts and send an email at a later date.
Commissioners discussed other areas that have more options for alternate buffers, such as boulders, or large
