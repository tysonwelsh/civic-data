       a. Resolution No. 24-030 regarding donation of an America 250 Patriots Plaque from
          the Utah State Society, Daughters of the American Revolution to be placed in West
          Jordan Veteran’s Memorial Park
          Public Services Director Cory Fralick oriented the Council with a request from the Utah
          State Society, Daughters of the American Revolution to install an America 250 Patriots
          plaque on a boulder at Veteran’s Memorial Park.

           Vice Chair Lamb opened a public hearing at 6:53 pm.
           Comments:
           Rob Bennett commented that several options were listed in the packet for placement of
           the plaque and asked if exact placement had been decided. Mr. Bennett was in favor of
           having the plaque in the community.

           Chris McConnehey, West Jordan resident, really liked the proposed placement of the
           plaque right next to memorials for other branches of military service and felt having the
           plaque at Veteran’s Memorial Park would prompt discussions with his family about
           history and the roles played by ancestors.
           Leslie Gates, West Jordan resident, said she was a member of the Salt Lake Chapter of the
           Daughters of the American Revolution. Ms. Gates thought she would bring her
           grandchildren to the park to see the plaque and felt it was a point of patriotic pride to
           show and bring up the topic of the founding of the country.

       b. Ordinance No. 24-23 a petition from Nate Shipp/DAI requesting approval of a Master
          Development Agreement/Plan for Oneleven approximately 53.2 acres located at
          7141 South Highway U-111 subject to the approval of Ordinance No. 24-24 amending
          the General Plan Land Use Map for Oneleven from Research Park to Medium Density
          Residential and a rezone from Business Research Park Zone (BR-P) to Integrated
          Housing Development Zone (IH-D)
          Chad Andreze, representing the applicant, outlined the request for the Council to change
          the Future Land Use Map for Oneleven, located at 7141 South Highway U-111, to Medium
          Density; amend the Zoning Map from BR-P to IH-D; and approve the proposed Master
          Development Agreement (MDA). Mr. Andreze showed a concept plan for 324 residential
          units (6.1 units per acre overall), with planned amenities and 10% of the development
          space reserved for commercial space. He showed planned phasing of the development,
          planned connectivity and parking, and said the applicant agreed with changes to the MDA
          recommended by City staff. Mr. Andreze felt confident in the ability to build and sell units
          that people would live in and be proud of.

           Council Member Whitelock noted that Council was looking for homes at all income levels
           that future residents could buy, and asked how the Oneleven development would help
           achieve that goal. Mr. Andreze said different types of units were integrated in the
           development, and housing options would range from affordable to less affordable.
           Council Member Whitelock asked when the applicant would begin marketing for the
           commercial element. Mr. Andreze reported that discussions had already occurred with
           commercial users, and a marketing sign could be put up as soon as infrastructure was in
           place. Council Member Whitelock said Chair Jacob had previously suggested integration
           of townhomes and single-family homes together through the development. She said she

           moderate-income units were grouped together. Mr. Andreze said the MDA included a

       c. Ordinance      No.     24-31, a   petition    by     Jeff    Vitek/Bangerter      Station
          LLC requesting approval of a Third Amendment to a Master Development
          Agreement for Jordan Valley Station TOD located at 3400 West 8600 South for the
          purpose of extending the expiration date
          Senior Planner Tayler Jensen oriented the Council with the request for a third amendment
          to the Jordan Valley Station TOD Master Development Agreement to extend the expiration
          date. He said the Planning Commission reviewed the request and forwarded a positive
          recommendation.

           Council Member Whitelock asked that the item be referred to the Committee of the Whole
           because she felt more information was needed.

           Vice Chair Lamb opened a public hearing at 7:57 pm.

           Comments:
           None

           Vice Chair Lamb closed the public hearing at 7:58 pm.



City Council Meeting Minutes               Wednesday, July 31, 2024                          Page 5
           Alec Tayler, representing Jordan Valley Station, said the requested expiration date
           extension was intended to allow the developer to continue providing Class A construction
           in phased development, with each phase taking 4-5 years. He gave a presentation about
           Jordan Valley Station, a transit-oriented development originally approved in 2010.

           Council Member Green said he was in favor of postponing the item and referring it to the
           Committee of the Whole. He said he was surprised the development had not yet been
           completed. Council Member Green referred to paragraph 1.5, and said he wanted to know
           more about the Bangerter Station operating agreement with UTA. He was concerned
           about the Jordan Parkway access road and the intersection at Jordan Line Parkway and
           wanted to see the engineering study. He commented that a turn lane called out in the
           agreement for Phase 3 had not yet been added at Haun Drive. Council Member Green
           suggested the traffic study done in 2010 needed to be updated, and said he had an issue
           with the impact fees. He referred to the agreement requirement that the developer
           provide the City with funds to maintain the roundabout and median on Jordan Line
           Parkway, and said he was not aware that any such reimbursement had occurred. Council
           Member Green spoke of the tax increment agreement, and expressed concern that the
           developer was in breach of contract. He asked what the City would gain from the
           requested extension.

           Vice Chair Lamb asked that Council Member Green provide a list of his questions and
           concerns to the Council and Council Office staff. Mr. Tayler asked to receive the list as well.

           MOTION: Council Member Shelton moved to REFER Ordinance No. 24-31 to a
                   future Committee of the Whole meeting on a date to be determined.
                   Council Member Bloom seconded the motion.
           The vote was recorded as follows:
           YES:       Bob Bedore, Pamela Bloom, Kelvin Green, Chad Lamb, Kent Shelton,
                      Kayleen Whitelock
           NO:
           ABSENT: Zach Jacob
           The motion passed 6-0.

           Council Office Director Alan Anderson mentioned that the Committee of the Whole

       d. Ordinance No. 24-30 a petition from Tanner Hansen/Victory Builders requesting a
          rezone of approximately 2.54 acres from Neighborhood Shopping Center (SC-
          1) and Community Shopping Center (SC-2) to General Commercial (C-G) located at
          1390 West 9000 South
          Tanner Hansen with Victory Builders explained they were requesting a rezone for
          property located at 1390 West 9000 South because the proposed project would cover
          40% of the land, and current zoning would only allow up to 30%. He said the planned
          buildings would be small business incubators, with 1500 square foot units designed to
          help small local businesses grow and stay in West Jordan.
