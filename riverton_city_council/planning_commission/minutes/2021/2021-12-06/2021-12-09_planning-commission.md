         A.    BLOCK SCALE PLAN, “MOUNTAIN RIDGE WEST” LOCATED NEAR 4701
               WEST 13400 SOUTH, APPLICANT – EDGE HOMES.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the Planning
Commission will be acting as the final decision-making body tonight. No public comment
was to be taken. He explained the request involves SLR zoning on the west side of the City
follows the following three-step approval process:

        Community Scale Plan;
        Block Scale Plan; and
        Detail or Final Plans.

This proposal is the Community Scale Plan that was approved by the Planning Commission
earlier this year. The current proposal pertains to the west side of the plan for a Block Scale
Plan approval north of 13800 South and south of Rose Creek Lane between Mountain View
and Sentinel Ridge Boulevard. This approval process is slightly different than the typical
process and procedures, but it ultimately arrives at the same point with engineered drawings
that are reviewed by Staff and approved by the Planning Commission.

Mr. Prestwich displayed a rendition of the proposed phases and stated that there are
approximately 515 units proposed. There is a mix of single-family, small-lot residential,
condominiums, and townhomes in the development. Mr. Prestwich noted that there is a mix
of public and private roads throughout the project. Sentinel Ridge Boulevard and 13800
South are considered arterial or collector streets. He described the layout of the plan
regarding the proposed fencing and noted that there is very little fencing required because
of the way it is laid out, which provides an open feel for the neighborhood. There is a six-
foot masonry wall proposed on Mountain View Drive on the west side of the property. It
could be as high as 10 feet depending on the ordinance. Six-foot vinyl fencing separates
various areas of community space and multi-family from single-family.

Traffic studies were performed to determine the correct spacing between intersections and
the use of slip lanes. The overall plan includes a lot of community open space, trails, extra
parking stalls in numerous places, and a diverse network of streets to spread out the traffic
routes. Staff reviewed the plan and recommended approval. The plan was found to meet
the Community Scale Plan (“CSP”) and the provisions of the SDD and the MDA.

It was noted that the creek on the property is not part of the development as the property
owner split the Rose Creek Channel off and the ownership runs along the top of the bank.
The applicant, Edge Homes, will follow the top edge of the creek with an asphalt path that
allows for more community open space in the development and allows Salt Lake County
access to the channel for maintenance.

Commissioner Breinholt asked if the property owners on the far side of the Rose Creek
Channel have a fence or if it is open as well. Mr. Prestwich stated that there is vacant
property between the property owners to the north (a car wash and a dental office) and the
channel. As further development occurs, there are likely to be more paths or maintenance
roads along the north side of the creek. Currently, there are no plans for development in the
area zoned SLR. Mr. Prestwich noted that the proposed path is10 feet wide.

Commissioner Brown asked for clarification of a slip lane. Mr. Prestwich explained that a
slip lane is a buffer between the front yards of the homes and the main arterial road,
separated by curbing and landscaping. It is a narrow one-way street to access the homes
along the roadway. It was also noted that the sidewalk will be inside the slip lane, which
adds a level of safety for pedestrians. Ms. Brown asked what the Commissioners should be
looking at for Block Scale approval. Mr. Prestwich reported that throughout the process, the
level of detail increases. At the Block Scale level, the applicant is showing feasibility with
road cross-sections, plan and profile sheets of the roads, and complete details of the
engineering of utilities. At the Block Scale level, certain aspects are necessary such as
stormwater planning but exact size and details will come in the Final Plan stage. At this
level, it is most important to ensure that the plan, as it is laid out, is feasible.

Ms. Brown asked about the size of the roads and if there would be enough parking in the
development. Mr. Prestwich explained that each home will have a two-car garage and two
parking stalls in front of the garage. The multi-family homes also have two-car garages and
possibly enough depth to park a car in front of the garage. There will be visitor parking
throughout. The public streets have enough room to park on both sides and the applicant

       A.     ORDINANCE AMENDMENT, RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING AN
              AMENDMENT TO THE “STANDARDS AND SPECIFICATIONS PLAN
              MANUAL” AMENDING AND ADOPTING A STANDARD FOR NARROW
              TRENCH EXCAVATION.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                        5
December 9, 2021


Mr. Prestwich reported that the City has standards to help guide development. Occasionally,
it is necessary to update the Standards to fit with the changing times. The Standard and
Specifications Plan Manual was last updated on September 29, 2021. The Planning
Commission makes the recommendation, the City Council adopts it, and it has the weight of
a City Ordinance. The Standard in question is for narrow trenches and burying conduit in a
City street. The proposed Standard was reviewed by the City Engineer, the Public Works
Director, and others. Staff recommended approval.

Mr. Prestwich stated that this is a legislative action item, meaning that the Planning
Commission will be making a recommendation and the City Council will take action to
approve.

Commissioner Cluff asked if there was a specific incident that caused the need for the
amendment. Mr. Prestwich stated that it could have been in response to someone who was
attempting to do something similar. He noted that occasionally modifications are necessary
as technology and construction practices change. Notices are not sent out for ordinance
changes, but this item was noticed on the City’s webpage and on the State’s Public Notice
website.

Greg Hill, a Riverton resident, and a business owner, expressed concern that the proposed
change as it will impact his business, which is an internet service provider, since the cost of
cutting the trenches may increase since cutting concrete is more expensive than cutting
asphalt. The ordinance does not address transverse connections across the road. The
Standard limits the narrow trenches to two on a given road. It does not address a location
for a possible second trench. Requirement number six specifies that it should not be located
within five feet horizontally of any public utility asset. All of the assets listed seem to be
municipal utilities and it was unclear whether the intention is to include public utilities. On
the drawing, it calls out a particular mastic and Mr. Hill would ask that the City consider
including a local vendor who manufactures a mastic for this use because it is a local Utah
company.

There were no further public comments. Chair Cannon closed the public hearing.

City Attorney, Ryan Carter, verified that the Standard does call out the preferred location for
the narrow trench being at the joint between the asphalt and the concrete to maintain the
integrity of the asphalt for as long as possible. He recognized that there are circumstances
where it is not possible to be right at the joint but as it is stated as a ‘preferred’ location, there
is a range of tolerance with the Standard. The Standard in question is concerned with how
to build the trench and addresses the more complicated issues of how it would relate to other
utilities in the area. Franchise Agreements and other overriding property rights must be
taken into consideration before building ensues.                    Every company involved in
telecommunications will have a Franchise Agreement with the City that will cover the issues
of concern mentioned. The engineering standard is separate from the steps leading up to
construction.

Mr. Prestwich described the location of the subject property and the details of the application.
Notice was sent out to the neighbors within 1,000 feet of the proposed rezone. The parcel
is at 2356 West 11800 South. It is 726 feet on the back parcel and 190 feet along Midas
Creek. When the parcel was developed in 2012 through 2015, a canal crossing was
discussed, and it was decided by the Planning Commission and City Council that a
connection should not be made. The Ivory development includes one-quarter acre lots and
north of Midas Creek are one-third acre lots and zoning. The current zoning is one-half and
one-third acre. There are two parcels in the area that allow for animal rights. The zoning is
harmonious but the lot sizes and use varies.

Some neighbors were concerned about the irrigation rights and Staff assured them that it
would be handled with a site plan. The current zoning is RR-22 with animal rights. The
General Plan anticipates change in the area. The current General Plan designation calls for
low-density or R-3 zoning. The applicant is requesting a change to medium-density which
would allow for R-4 zoning. The differences between R-3 and R-4 are lot size. R-3 includes
is 14,000 square-foot lots while R-4 is 10,000 square-foot lots. Lot widths for R-3 are 90
feet and R-4 is 80 feet. Staff had seen multiple layouts for the property without anything
coming to fruition. It is a difficult shape and creates issues for development. Occasionally,
developers will ask for R-4 zoning because they need a narrower frontage, which may or
may not be the case in this situation. This application has received numerous comments
from neighbors.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Commissioner Brown asked why the applicant is requesting R-4 instead of R-3 zoning.
Mr. Howell stated that the 90-foot width requirement for R-3 makes for an awkward layout
with extra-large side yards that potentially use excess water. The development lays out
better as R-4 zoning and will ultimately only add an additional two units.

Commissioner Cluff asked what the square footage of the lots would be. Mr. Howell
explained that the minimum would be the required 10,000 square feet, but several of the lots
will be closer to 13,000 to 14,000 square feet. There will be a maximum of 15 homes in the

Commissioner Cluff clarified that the applicant understood the limit of 150 nights per year,

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Darren Mullberg has lived on Kell Lane about three houses from the subject property since
2007. He stated that there are CCR’s for the neighborhood. He was concerned with
potential lawsuits that could result from an approval of this application because of the current
CC&Rs.

Emily Bright stated that she lives next door on the north. She stated that 20 children live in
the cul-de-sac, including her own five children. Short-term renters are not aware of the

will come and go with no background checks. The renters could potentially be criminals

property, she would want to have recourse. She stated that safety, liability, and parking
issues were reasons to deny the application.

coming into the neighborhood. He has spoken with several neighbors regarding this
proposal, and no one supports it. He asked that the applicant reconsider the application.
