	•	Fraud Risk Assessment Presentation, Staff Presenter, Bruce Kartchner.

Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner reported that the State Auditor has been mandating steps be taken as a result of fraud issues in municipalities.  The presentation was part of the State Auditor’s effort to ensure that City Council and Board Members are aware of their responsibilities with respect to fraud risk.  

Mr. Kartchner reported that over the years the accounting world has studied fraud and how to reduce it.  As a result, a fraud risk triangle has been developed.  When fraud occurs it is because of pressure, opportunity, and rationalization.  Examples of why people commit fraud were given.  Organizations cannot prevent pressure or rationalize situations; however, they can discourage opportunities.  Each year during the City’s financial audit, external CPAs conduct an analysis for the City.  Two reports at the end of the audit were displayed and reviewed.  The State Auditor asked the City to perform an internal review of fraud risk, which was completed.  Mr. Kartchner reviewed the Fraud Risk Assessment.  The major issue identified as a risk was the separation of duties.  The Basic Separation of Duties Questionnaire was reviewed.  

Mr. Kartchner explained that the Fraud Risk Assessment has a total score of 200 points.  Bluffdale City currently receives a full score on the Basic Separation of Duties questionnaire because the City has an outside contract accountant who prepares the reconciliations and other journal entry tasks.  The City does not have enough personnel internally to separate the duties adequately.  This contractor allows the City to completely separate the responsibilities and duties with respect to handling cash, which is key.  

Mr. Kartchner reported that the State focuses on written policy and requires professionals to sign that they understand what constitutes committing fraud.  Auditors and accountants question why this is required because people will still sign untruthfully.  Mr. Kartchner reviewed areas where the City needs to improve or items to include in their annual practices.

Mr. Kartchner stated that the City does not currently have a Credit Card Purchasing Policy in writing, however, the Finance Department controls the cards and potential activity.  He had not received his annual review but assumed that goals will be created for areas the City has not yet implemented.  Elected officials will be required to sign an Ethical Behavior Statement.  The Finance Department will prepare this during the coming year so that next year it can be completed during the Fraud Risk Assessment.  

The State Auditor provided a 30-minute online training for Board and Council Members.  An email with a link to the training site was sent to each Council Member earlier in the day.  Each member was asked to complete the training, sign the Completion Certificate, and forward it to Mr. Kartchner or Stephanie Thayer.  

Another area that can be added to the City for the assessment is a Fraud Hotline.  This can be a fill in the blank web form or an email specifically designed to pick up fraud questions internally and externally.  Other cities currently include waste or other personnel complaints with their fraud hotline.  The Finance Department will bring ideas to the Council to determine what the City’s hotline will include.  

Mr. Kartchner reported that having a formal audit function for the size of Bluffdale City is challenging due to the lack of personnel.  This frequently contains a subset of the Council or Board and can be analyzed and discussed.  

In response to a question raised, Mr. Kartchner explained that the formal Audit Committee integrates with the external auditors and hires the auditors.  The City does not have an official Auditing Committee, but the Council performs many of the same functions.  Mr. Kartchner reported that a problem with the State’s approach is that they have a check the box option.  

City Manager, Mark Reid gave an example of an audit committee he was involved with previously.  He explained that the City does not have many cashiers, so there is no reason for an Audit Committee.  Mr. Kartchner reported that that City department heads might assume that the City has an Audit Committee because of questions he is asking them about certain expenditures.  

Dave Kallas stated that this is what he would assume the Audit Committee would be for.  The City is not a cash business, however, a significant amount of money passes through with bills and expenses.  An example was given where Lehi City is billed for road salt that was not being delivered.  The committee would prevent this from happening.  Mr. Kartchner reported that if someone desires to be dishonest, all of the fraud protections put in place will not guarantee that it will be stopped.  Usually, if good controls are in place, there will come a point in time that it will be detected.  

The City’s external auditors have commented that the City has good internal controls.  The score from the assessment put the City in the moderate range.  Mr. Kartchner felt that the City is in the low or very low range because of the controls that are in place.  

	•	Discussion Relating to Transportation Impact Fees for Proposed Medical Building, located at 13825 South Redwood Road, Presenter, Jenny Taumoepeau/Fort Street Partners.

Fort Street Partners Representative, Jenny Taumoepeau, presented the report and stated that her company purchased the parcel on the corner of 13800 South and Bangerter Highway from the Woodbury Corporation.  The building will be a 25,000 square-foot medical office building.  She was presenting on behalf of South Valley Women’s Health, an OB/GYN clinic, which will occupy the entire top floor of the building.  Family Source Pediatrics will occupy the entire second floor.  The land was purchased late last year and has been going through the design and planning phases.  

Ms. Taumoepeau stated that her company was alarmed when they received the Transportation Impact Fee.  Riverton City calculates its impact fee using square footage while Bluffdale City bases it on the number of trips.  The City calculated 870 trips for the building, which is multiplied by 400.  The original impact fee was $340,000, which is nearly 10 times more than buildings in surrounding cities.  Staff has been responsive and helpful in discussing the fee.  Ms. Taumoepeau reported that her company hired engineers to conduct a traffic study and they determined that the building will have 576 trips, which decreases the fee to $230,000.  This was submitted to the City, but the City denied it and recommended the company speak with Ryan Hales from Hales Engineering.  

Ms. Taumoepeau emailed Mayor Timothy who invited her to present at this meeting.  She reported that she met with Ryan Hales who recommended that they use a method of comparing other buildings and have their engineers revise the Traffic Study.  Currently, the study is down to 511 and has been submitted to the City.  Mr. Hales and the City were reviewing it.  

In response to a question raised, Ms. Taumoepeau stated that South Valley Women’s Health and Family Source will own the building.  She explained that the fee at 511 would be $204,000.  She understands how the City determines impact fees but felt that the revised number is the fair cost since they know who will be coming to their business.  There has been frustration because Mr. Hales did not initially review the report before it was denied.  Ms. Taumoepeau reviewed the fees and building details for the buildings in Riverton and South Jordan. 

Mayor Timothy reported that Bluffdale City’s zoning and infrastructure differs from other cities, which makes the calculation of impact fees different.  

Taylor Woodbury from the Woodbury Corporation apologized that he at first was not aware of the public process involving impact fees and for not engaging sooner.  He explained that a retail project is inherently difficult to look at in terms of trip generation for a single building.  If a customer visits two different buildings on a site, both businesses will be assessed for trip counts.  The methodology the City should be considering is determining the actual trips to the center, not necessarily to the building.  

Mr. Woodbury understood and was aware of the infrastructure and road concerns the City has had over the years.  He understands why the impact fee is made to address some of those concerns.  If an applicant is determining a regional area to build, they may choose another city that has lower impact fees.  

Mr. Woodbury gave an example of a grocery store trip count.  Bluffdale City residents will not be adding additional trips to Bluffdale City roads to go to the grocery store in the City.  However, people coming from outside of Bluffdale will result in additional trips into the City.  Mr. Woodbury was concerned that those factors are not included in the trip generation analysis.  He was aware that the Council will not rewrite the law but wanted to bring those points to their attention.  Mr. Woodbury recommended that the City use a more aggressive methodology to evaluate the true trips to the medical building to bring the impact fee closer to those in the surrounding communities.  

Mr. Woodbury reported that he was not promising leads right now but they received commitments from certain groups who will build if he can get authorization for a small grocery store.  This is actively being worked on, but COVID-19 has caused most groups to postpone new development.  He was getting positive traction and will continue to pursue it.  

Mr. Kartchner asked Mr. Woodbury if he was asking the City to reduce the impact fees.  Mr. Woodbury confirmed that he was.  He realized that it may impact him, but the challenge is realizing that the cost outweighs the near-term damage to Woodbury in terms of Transportation Fees and the reimbursement of 14600 South.  

Jeff Gaston commented that if Mr. Woodbury has a concern with impact fees he could come before the Council and they can determine what they would be.  Certain factors used for impact fees will involve contributing to the City and the roads, public safety, etc. are funded through it.  Because of this, the Council determines impacts on a case-by-case basis.  Council Member Gaston stated that Mr. Woodbury should not be concerned about restaurants, grocery stores, and certain economic impacts coming into the City with the current fees.  

Mr. Woodbury stated that he would not let a deal pass by because of impact fees in Bluffdale City without a conversation.  He stated that it is important to have rules in place to create an equitable environment.  

Ms. Taumoepeau introduced their General Contractor, Dave Frampton.

Mr. Hales explained that there are different ways to calculate impact fees.  The fees were reduced from $620 per trip to $400.  That was what the Council opted to do to promote business development in the City.  The calculation can be based on square footage, or employees and looking at the number of trips for each unit.  Mr. Hales reviewed the impact studies and stated that the Council asked that it remain as fair as possible.  He received and was in the process of reviewing the study.  

Mayor Timothy stated that there are extenuating circumstances when dealing with calculations.  He stated that the above item is necessary because residents have medical needs.  Because the City does not have that particular service, the residents are using City roads to go to another city for the service.  The above item may not be taking away an impact, but it is providing a service that does not currently exist in Bluffdale City.  This should be taken into consideration as the City determines the impact fee.  

Dave Kallas asked when the impact fee was reduced to $400.  Mr. Hales reported that this was changed during a work session and voted on by the City Council.  Mr. Hales reported that this was the last fee schedule approved.  Dave Kallas did not understand why the Transportation Fee is so high and had heard similar comments from several builders.  

Mayor Timothy stated that it is determined by lane miles and population.  Bluffdale City has more lane miles than other cities with triple or quadruple the population.  Per capita, Bluffdale has a significant amount of transportation.  

Dave Kallas asked if the fee is based on maintenance costs.  Mr. Kartchner explained that the impact fee is not a maintenance fee.  All of the City’s maintenance is paid out of the City’s General Fund.  Impact fees go into the Impact Fee Fund, which is strictly regulated.   

Mr. Tracer reported that impact fees can only be used for a project related to growth.  He explained how the base impact fee of $400 was determined.  Several projects are transportation-related and caused by new growth without a large number of people to pay for them.  In addition, staff decided to add a buy-in component, which means that if a road is only 60% full, the remaining 40% capacity of the road can be charged to the impact fee.  This has been done for several roads to recoup costs the City has incurred constructing roads that are not yet fully utilized.  He stated that it is a valid methodology that has not been used before.  Mr. Tracer commented that the impact fee does not take grants into account since they are not guaranteed.  

Mr. Kartchner reported that grants affect the impact fees because they come from outside sources using EDA or RDA money for infrastructure.  This will change the model moving forward.  Mayor Timothy explained that the model will be due to the infrastructure no longer being needed.  

Mayor Timothy reported that other cities reduce their impact fees to attract businesses and pass the cost on to existing residents.  It is difficult for the City to do this because there is a significant amount of transportation per capita.

	•	PUBLIC HEARING:  Consideration and Vote on Ordinance 2020-13, a Text Amendment to “Robert’s Rules of Order,” Staff Presenter, Todd Sheeran.

City Attorney, Todd Sheeran presented the staff report and stated that this is the third time Robert’s Rules of Order has come before the Council.  Revisions were completed to allow the Council to add items to the agenda with the approval of two Council Members.  The Council did not want to create a conflict by allowing two Council Members to add an item and two to remove an item.  As a result, language was added requiring three or more Council Members to remove an item from the agenda.  

Mr. Sheeran conducted research on subpoenas and reported that this portion of City Code became was inactive in 1974 and has not been amended since.  This allowed for Hearing Officers to subpoena witnesses.  Mr. Sheeran reported that other sections that have been removed from the Code included were iterations of the State Code.  If a requirement is listed in the State Code, it is duplicative to include it in the City’s ordinances.

	•	PUBLIC HEARING – Consideration and Vote on Ordinance Number 2020-17, a Text Amendment to Business License Appeal, Staff Presenter, Todd Sheeran.

Mr. Sheeran presented the staff report and stated that the proposed ordinance comes as a pending ordinance that was passed about one month ago.  The Council desired to address the issue of solicitation in the City quickly due to a significant number of solicitors in the area.  One provision being revised in the pending ordinance was the Appeal section that was discussed at the last meeting.  Appeals will go to a Hearing Officer, who is typically an attorney the City is contracted with.  The amendment specifies that a Business Licensing Official will conduct an investigation of the findings and requirements in order to deny, suspend, or revoke a business license.  The amendment also includes a procedure for the Hearing Officer to follow.  It was noted that the proposed amendment will result in a better process and clearer direction for appeals.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Sheeran stated that if an application is given to the City that does not meet the requirements, the denial would be effective immediately.  If the application is a suspension revocation it will be effective immediately if there are health and safety concerns.  Mr. Sheeran recommended that Subsection F of the ordinance include language allowing the Hearing Officer to Stay an effective date of the business license.    

Mr. Sheeran reviewed the appeal process.  Dave Kallas indicated that the business will be immediately shut down due to safety issues, which need to be carefully considered.  Someone from the City will need to review the situation before it reaches that point.  Mr. Sheeran stated that if there is a safety concern, the Business License Division will rely on a professional to assist with the decision.  

Council Member Hales asked what liability the City has if a business continues to operate without a license and there is a safety issue.  Mr. Sheeran explained that if the business continues to operate despite the issue, the City will be required to demonstrate that it took active measures to address the safety concern. 

Traci Crockett asked if it is enough to mitigate the City’s liability if the appeal goes to a Hearing Officer who suggests that the safety issue be waived until the appeal process occurs.  Mr. Sheeran responded that there is no case law for this circumstance.  If there is a request for a Stay, the City will submit their opinion to the Hearing Officer on why the Stay should be denied.  If the Hearing Officer is an attorney, they will understand the risk to the City.  

Mark Hales asked if the City has an Alternate Hearing Officer if the current contract attorney is not available.  Mr. Sheeran confirmed that he would reach out to prior Hearing Officers or attorneys who would be available on an expedited basis.  

Council Member Gaston asked if most cases can be turned around within three days.  Mr. Sheeran stated that appeals concerning business licensing can be finalized sooner than a Land Use Appeal.  Jeff Gaston stated that if the business owner desires to appeal, they should have that right to continue to work within three days.  Three days is an industry standard and a fair amount of time.  

Mr. Sheeran reported that in the pending ordinance, there is a 20-day requirement for the Hearing Officer.  If there is a need to expedite a hearing, staff will work with the business owner.  Council Member Kallas stated that there is not a lot of case law on the risk to the City if a business remains open after a safety issue has been identified and the license revoked.  Mayor Timothy responded that the Code would be a liability if the business owner remains open during the 21-day period.  Dave Kallas stated that the Hearing Officer would make a Stay of Decision until the hearing.  

Council Member Hales asked if it is necessary to broaden the definition of safety and if this should be done by ordinance.  Mr. Sheeran responded that this will be researched and if there is a safety issue cited, there will be evidence and a basis for it.  If the Hearing Officer receives a request to have a business operate during the pendency of an appeal, the safety issues can be analyzed and a determination made as to whether the City was justified.  

Mayor Timothy opened the public hearing.

He reported that the City is currently trying to use the new ordinance on the Fuller Swim School.  Last week his client was served with a memorandum that included Rights of Appeal that cited the proposed resolution as if it was already adopted.  Doing so bypasses the Council’s vote, which he considered to be very condescending.  He stated that a Hearing Officer was also hired prior to the Council’s discussion and vote.  

Mr. Osborne reported that Fuller Swim School has served thousands of children and youth in Bluffdale City.  There were at least two present at the meeting who have children enrolled at the school.  Fuller Swim School is located across from the City Manager’s home.  There are police officers and firemen who have children going to the school and one was there the previous week in uniform when the citation was issued.   Not once, until about two weeks ago, was an issue raised about the way Fuller Swim School has done business.    

Mr. Osborne reviewed the timeline of when the school began to have issues with the City.  On July 13, for the first time, Compliance Officer, Mr. Henry came by and raised concerns about parking.  It was noted that there can be 20 to 25 vehicles parked on the road.  This road is  75 feet wide with clearly marked lines.  His client has posted signs and flags to create a safe environment.  Customers are educated on the website about how to drop off and pick up children.  Details of the success and reputation of the business were discussed.    

Mr. Osborne stated that when Mr. Henry first visited, he wanted to discuss parking.  Mr. Osborne’s client expressed a willingness to comply with the City’s requirements and various ideas were discussed.  Mr. Henry gave every indication that there was not a problem.  A resident complained for the first time and there were more discussions with Mr. Henry.  

Last Monday, Fuller Swim School hired Mr. Osborne to examine an easement that would provide the potential parking for dozens of vehicles.  That same day, Mr. Henry came out and reported that the City wanted to meet with Ms. Fuller to discuss the parking issues for next summer.  Mr. Henry was aware that the Fuller Swim School’s season was nearly over.  The meeting was not held but the purpose was to present his client with a memorandum from the City’s Building Code Officer stating that everything needs to be changed or the school will be shut down.  

The memorandum included the right to appeal.  After several communications between the City Attorney and Mr. Osborne, 24 hours and a few minutes later his client’s business license was revoked.  There was no effort made on the part of the City to work with his clients to keep the business open.  Less than 48 hours later, his client was charged with a Class B misdemeanor.  Ms. Fuller has decided to keep her business open.

This issue was brought up because the Council has a right to know what is going on. Mr. Osborne stated that Bluffdale City is growing rapidly which creates difficult challenges.  He questioned the impression this situation will leave on the community.

Mr. Osborne reported that there is nothing in the new ordinance that would guide a Hearing Officer on how to issue a Stay.  It would give the Hearing Officer complete discretion.  Mr. Osborne reported that if he were going to turn a case over to a Hearing Officer rather than a governing board, he would give very clear and delineated powers, responsibilities, and duties so that they do not abuse it.  The City is discussing potentially turning over the entire department to a Hearing Officer.  He noted that a Hearing Officer can be efficient, but problematic.  The appearance of a Hearing Officer who is hired and paid by the City is biased.  In addition, the City Attorney may not hire the Hearing Officer again if a decision goes against the City.  

Mr. Osborne stated that cities have great discretion, but appearance is important in matters such as this one.  The appearance that this hearing was not scheduled until 48 hours ago appeared to target his client.  Due process was also of concern since a business is shut down after seven successful years.  The Building Official was concerned about safety when no one else in Bluffdale City has been for seven years except for perhaps one resident who filed a complaint one month ago.  Mr. Osborne requested that if the ordinance is passed that it not include his client’s case.  He reported that his client is not the only business that has traffic coming and going and gave examples of other businesses with a similar circumstance.

Mr. Sheeran pointed out that Fuller’s Swim School was cited on a safety issue.  

Jeff Gaston asked if ensuring that businesses are properly insured is included in the ordinance.  Mr. Sheeran stated that it is not and is in another portion of the pending ordinance.  Council Member Gaston reported that cities require businesses show an insurance certificate as well.  

Mr. Sheeran reported that he would recommend that the citation given to Fuller’s Swim School be removed and provide the business an opportunity to request a Stay.  If the Hearing Officer denies the request and the business continues to operate, a citation will be issued based on the business operating without a valid business license.  During the three-day period, the business will be closed until the Hearing Officer determines there is no longer a safety issue.

Mark Hales stated that the Council should not discuss a pending case due to potential litigation issues.  He stated that the Hearing Officer learns that the City was incorrect in reporting a safety issue, the citation will be revoked.  Council Member Kallas stated that the business did not have an opportunity to appeal the safety decision.  Currently, the owner has three days to file an appeal.  The citation that was issued is now in conflict with the discussion.  

Jeff Gaston stated that the above item will assist businesses and give them an opportunity to remedy any issues so they can stay in business.  He considered this to be extremely fair.  He noted that the above item makes the City transparent.  

Mayor Timothy stated that the issue is complicated with a great deal of detail.  He requested that the City Council table the matter to allow for it to be put in writing and for further review.  Jeff Gaston agreed with taking that action since he would like businesses to have an opportunity to come back into compliance legally.

Dave Kallas stated that the pending ordinance is currently in place.  He wanted to see the language in the ordinance and ensure that the above item is exactly how the Council desires it.  Discussion ensued on whether the matter should be tabled and the impacts of the pending ordinance on Fuller’s Swim School.  Mr. Sheeran reported that the Business License Official is required to cite a specific reason for the revocation.  

Mark Hales stated that if a business is cited for a safety issue it needs to be addressed quickly.  Mr. Sheeran explained that the safety issue can be removed from the effective date and give the person who has had their license revoked an opportunity to file an appeal within 10 days.  It does not become effective unless an appeal is filed to avoid the stay issue.  

Jeff Gaston and Mark Hales provided language to be included in the ordinance.  Mark Hales recommended that the Council take a break to allow Mr. Sheeran time to write the new language into the ordinance.  

Jeff Gaston moved that the Council take a 10-minute break.  Mark Hales seconded the motion.  The motion passed with the unanimous consent of the Council.  

Mr. Sheeran read the following new language and reported that it is to be included in Section 3.101.080(C)(3) at the end of the sentence:  “However, if a license is suspended or revoked based on health or safety reasons, the party may request from a Hearing Officer a Stay of Suspension or Revocation upon filing an appeal.  The Hearing Officer shall issue a decision within three business days of the Request of Stay.  Any operation during the Request for Stay is an automatic denial.”

Jeff Gaston moved to approve Ordinance Number 2020-17, a text amendment to Business License Appeal with the changes as presented.  Traci Crockett seconded the motion.  Vote on motion:  Mark Hales-Aye; Jeff Gaston-Aye; Traci Crockett-Aye; Dave Kallas-Aye.  The motion passed unanimously.
