1. Discussion Items
1.1 Discussion of Public Engagement App; Scott Knudson

Mayor Silvestrini said he invited Scott Knudson, Bryce Miller, and Alan Feuerlein to
share the details of their public engagement app with the Council. Scott Knudson said
the app is to power people in government with technology. He brought up the recent
issue of the Cottonwood Mall site with Holladay and Ivory Homes. He said Holladay
had over twenty public meetings, went seven months past their deadline, and over $1
million was expended. He said the attendance at those public meetings was about 300
people and they applauded the plan’s compromises. Holladay’s City Council made a
unanimous vote to rezone that property though the public ultimately disapproved of
the project by 57%. Knudson said his company was trying to help cities avoid
complicated issues. Knudson and his team knocked on 200 Millcreek residents’ doors
and 79% said they would be more involved if Millcreek had an app. He said with an
app, the people get education, engagement, and reporting. Knudson walked the
Council through the app. Council Member Uipi asked if Knudson would support the
content management or if it was something the City had to staff. Knudson said they
would support the app, but Millcreek would manage the content. He said the City

would get dashboard tracking, downloads, analytics data, and continuous polling with
relevant data. The users provide demographic and geographic information about
themselves when creating an account and that account can only vote once in polls.
Council Member Catten asked about integrating other systems that does polling to get
more analytics. Knudson said their app only does the analytics for the polls it creates.
Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek has done amateur polling through the electronic
newsletter but there is not a way to control multiple voting by a single person.
Council Member Jackson asked how they would know if the people are registered
voters. Knudson said they app could ask for drivers license information to verify the
voter registration. Mayor Silvestrini asked about pricing and competition. Knudson
said it would cost Millcreek $20-30,000 for the initial setup and $2-3,000 a month for
maintenance.

Council Member Uipi asked what other cities the developers were contracted with
and how the app messaging gets out to the public. Knudson said they are trying to
create a form of communication to show that residents are heard. He said Millcreek is
the first city they have reached out to and that they have been in business for about
two months. Council Member Catten asked how this app was different than
Facebook. She expressed concern about starting a resident following base in another
platform instead of using the existing social media bases. Knudson said Facebook is
unregulated and it is hard to verify how many people are looking at it. He said
feedback through the app is more relevant. He said the problem with the Cottonwood
Mall is that the minority was driving the feedback, there were relatively low numbers,
and there was an uneducated audience. Council Member Catten said Nextdoor is used
a lot in Millcreek and wondered what would make Knudson’s app worth it as a City
to invest in. The developers brought up that the app sends out push notifications.
Mayor Silvestrini said an advantage of the app is getting one persons’ feedback at a
time and not the one angry person on Facebook who dominates conversations.
Council Member Uipi brought up that she was not sure if Millcreek had an employee
who had time to keep content fresh. The developers said they could help with content
management. John Geilmann recommended the developers set up an appointment
with city staff to go over specifics of the app.

1.2 Strategic Planning; Laurie Johnson, HR-Finance Director
Laurie Johnson reviewed the strategic plan document she distributed to the Council
and asked for feedback. She asked the Council if they had thought about any other
strategic priorities other than those in the General Plan. She said the budget should tie
to the strategic plan and at the end of the year the Council could see what was
accomplished. She provided a list of strategic plan projects, which included: business
license fee study, Central Wasatch Commission membership, City Center Master
Plan, community council support, community reinvestment area creation, emergency
management CERT training, financial management software implementation, Jordan
River Commission membership, lobbyist efforts, Millcreek Arts Discovery project,
Millcreek Business Council, Millcreek Youth Council activities, Promise Program
support, public works operations feasibility study, Sustainability Coordinator support,
Transportation Master Plan, Utah League of Cities and Towns membership, Utah
Venture Out activities, and website update. She asked the Council to put the listed
items under the seven General Plan themes of Great Connections, Outdoor Lifestyle,

Enriched Culture, Vibrant Gathering Places, Thriving Economy, Health &
Environment, and Unique Neighborhoods. The Council took time to write projects
under the themes on poster board. Francis Lilly said that the Wasatch Front Regional
Council had only seen one city tie the General Plan to the budget and said it spoke
well of Laurie Johnson.

Council Member Marchant brought up community communication/outreach under
Great Connections. Council Member Marchant suggested putting projects that touch
all the seven priorities as a separate category, as the highest priority, like the City
Center Master Plan. He said engagement and facilitators are unique and are separate
from the categories and engagement should be anyway Millcreek outreaches. Johnson
said there could be an engagement category with goals and strategies. Mayor
Silvestrini said Millcreek is highly engaged and involved. Council Member Jackson
brought up that outreach requires money. Johnson said engagement should be
identified because it is spelled out in the City’s Vision Statement, which says;
“Millcreek is a diverse community where residents and businesses are empowered to
respectfully engage and interact with each other in governance and volunteerism to
maintain a fiscally responsible, environmentally sustainable City that provides a
‘welcome home’ feeling to everyone — past, present and future.” Mayor Silvestrini
said the category should be called, “Engaged Community” to be in line with the
existing themes. Items the Council added to the list were storm water, Parks Master
Plan, and RCOZ & FCOZ permitting. Council Member Uipi said she would like to
see updates or progress reports on the various plans.

1.3 Mayor and Council’s Discretion on Discussion Topic
Council Member Marchant said he received a notice in the mail from the Salt Lake
City Public Utilities department about upcoming public hearings. He showed the
Council a picture of the corner of his street where the utility company tore up the
street and did not repair it. Mayor Silvestrini recommended he go speak about it at
the public hearing and said Millcreek needs better representation with Public
Utilities. John Miller said the leaks are not planned and asphalt can only be laid at
certain times during the year. He said small repairs are not cost effective for
contractors to do. Miller said he does not have teeth with the complaints about roads.
Mayor Silvestrini recommended bumping up efforts in communicating
dissatisfaction with them. Council Member Jackson said Millcreek pays 30% more
than Salt Lake City residents for water. Mayor Silvestrini said Salt Lake City Public
Utilities needs to file for an excavation permit so that staff can make sure the permits
get closed properly.

Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek has been having boundary adjustment discussions
with Salt Lake City and one of the areas discussed is the area that runs through
Millcreek’s proposed city center (Miller Avenue). Council Member Marchant asked
if Salt Lake City would consider the roundabout on 2300 E. as part of the
negotiations. Miller said the current maintenance of the roundabout is between the
County and UDOT. Miller said if the Council wanted a great connection with a
pleasing entry into the City, then the City should acquire it. Mayor Silvestrini said
Millcreek and Salt Lake City talked about a process for identifying parcels that are
split between cities and rectifying those situations. Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek

John Miller said Neff’s Creek flooded on Friday through the old channel and a house
was flooded. He said crews did a good job taking care of it. He said logs acting like a
bridge were blocking the channel which created the diversion. Rita Lund said water
lines broke, which caused the flooding, she was not sure the creek caused the
flooding at the house. Lund said the new FEMA floodplain map will come out this
year. Mayor Silvestrini said the driveways in the Olympus Cove were sandbagged
by the emergency management staff. Mayor Silvestrini said it would be a good
communication tool to educate residents that flooding is possible in Mt. Olympus.
Lund said she could do a mailing to the neighborhood or do an open house to
encourage people to buy flood insurance. She said when the map comes out, the
insurance will be more expensive. Council Member Jackson said Millcreek should
highlight that because Millcreek is a city, there was an emergency management team
able to work on the problem. Council Member Uipi asked for better notification to
the Council about emergency issues. Geilmann said flood management is under the
County’s auspices. Lund clarified that the new FEMA map is out, but it has not been
published yet though it would be this summer. Mayor Silvestrini said the map would
also affect what residents can build.

Mayor Silvestrini said he had been to more meetings on the County public works
contract. The County came out with a feathered plan to defer the additional increase
over three years. He met with Taylorsville and Holladay and they uniformly decided
to not do a three-year contract with the County for services. He said they will sign a
one- year contract with the additional $1 million increase. The Millcreek Council
had authorized a study to look at other public works service options. The other two
cities will be part of that study and they want it to be done by an operations person.
The cities are not ready to buy into the additional County contract amount until it is
known what it accounts for. Miller said the proposal did not include cuts from the
original, just the implementation of the proposed cost. Mayor Silvestrini said there is
a break down of the operation component of the budget but not the administration.
Miller said the Municipal Services District (MSD) is increasing what they are
spending, and the cities are paying for it. Laurie Johnson recommended looking at
hiring a combination of proposers from the RFP. Mayor Silvestrini said there needs
to be a balance of who is involved in the study.

Mayor Silvestrini said the Unified Police Department (UPD) is trying to figure out
the $1.1 million shortfall in revenue in the budget. He said there are two more DEA
officers county-wide. He said Millcreek is in a position to put another $1 million
towards the UPD contract to give the officers an increase to remain competitive and
cover Millcreek’s portion of the revenue shortfall. Council Member Jackson asked if
other cities are able to do the same. Mayor Silvestrini said Holladay will look at a
