       A.      CONSIDER ORDINANCE NO. 2O23-O.12 PROHIBITING THE
                NEGLIGENT DISCHARGE OF FIREWORKS
Aaron McNight said Midvale city council members have expressed concern about the
use of fireworks in the City. Some interest was expressed in prohibiting the use of
fireworks on City property. However, in 2018, the Utah State Legislature passed a law
significantly limiting what prohibitions a municipality can impose on the use of fireworks.
That law prevents the City from outright prohibiting the use of fireworks on City property.
                                              4
Proceedings of City Council Meeting
September 19, 2023

However, state law allows a municipality to impose a prohibition against the negligent
discharge of fireworks, which allows the City to enact policy regarding firework safety.
Staff has drafted the attached proposed ordinance. The proposed ordinance would make
it a class B misdemeanor to negligently or improperly discharge fireworks in the City.

While any negligent use or discharge of fireworks would violate the ordinance, the
proposed amendment identifies several specific scenarios that would violate the
ordinance. Those specific scenarios are (1) using or discharging a firework in such a
manner that the firework projects over or onto someone else's property without their
consent, (2) using or discharging a firework from or into any motor vehicle, or at or near
any person, and (3) using or discharging a firework in such a manner as to cause any
building, structure, or other property to catch fire.

This proposed ordinance is intended to incentivize responsible and safe use of fireworks.
It also will help the City and law enforcement to hold people accountable who are
negligent or reckless in their use of fireworks and who start fires as a result of their use
of fireworks. The penalty of a class B misdemeanor gives prosecutors and judges
sufficient leverage to order and enforce restitution orders, which often becomes an issue
in fireworks cases.

STAFF'S RECOMMENDATION: Staff recommended adopting Ordinance 2O23-O-12 to
prohibit negligent use or discharge of fireworks and to provide law enforcement with a
greater ability to prosecute cases where fires are started due to fireworks.

        B.     DISCUSSION REGARDING A CURFEW TO BE SET BY ORDINANCE
               FOR ALL CITY PARKS
Glen Kennedy said there has been a significant increase in vandalism, graffiti, and
camping, along with other crimes in Midvale City parks over the last year. The negative
impacts of these activities include increased costs and time spent repairing, replacing,
and cleaning, and in some cases having to take park assets and facilities out of servlce
for periods of time. As a result, the parks and amenities are less inviting to patrons
pertaining to safety. To assist in reducing these negative activities, and to help the Unified
Police Department with enforcement and policing activities in the parks, staff are
proposing a seasonal curfew to be established by ordinance.

The proposed curfew is as follows: PARKS; SEASONAL CURFEWS: Except for an
individual in attendance or participating in a City-permitted event, it is unlawful for any
person:

1. From April 1st through September 30th each year, to be at any City Park between the
hours of 10:00 P.M. and 6.00 A.M. the following morning.

2. From October 1st through March 31st each year, to be at any City Park between official
sunset and sunrise the following day per the official sunrise/sunset table placed on the
City's website.

Any person who is in violation of the provisions of this Section is guilty of a class C
mlsdemeanor.

Council Member Dustin Gettel said he would prefer closing the parks at 10 pm or a set
time.
