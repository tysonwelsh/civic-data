3.5) Consideration of CIR Engineering’s request for approval of the Moto United Site Plan
located at 450 South Millpond Drive.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. Moto United is an indoor powersports and marine vehicle
retailer. The company is asking for approval of a concrete exterior, which is not typically
allowed in the design district. They also want an exception to the number of trees typically
required. The plan that was submitted to the city has about 30 trees per acre, rather than the usual
40 trees per acre.

Andrew was present on behalf of the applicant. He explained that a portion of the property is
protected wetland, which limits the space on the lot available to plant the required number of
trees. He also mentioned that the concrete exterior is to maintain congruity with other Moto
United locations, which all have similar looks.

Commissioner Everett asked if the required trees per acre would be reached if the wetland wasn’t
taken into consideration. The planning department explained that the undeveloped area is about
2.6 acres of the 8.4 acre lot. Commissioners Eyre and Jones both expressed comfort with
approving, based on this information.


Commissioner Roberts asked staff for clarification on the number of trees required. Mike West
pointed out that Moto United put in additional parking lot islands that were otherwise not strictly
necessary to increase the number of trees on the lot.

                Motion: Commissioner Jones moved to approve the plan with the inclusion of
                both exceptions, finding that the proposed use at the proposed location will not be
                detrimental or injurious to property or improvements in the vicinity, it will not be
                detrimental to the public health, safety, general welfare, or convenience, it will be
                located and conducted in a manner compliant with the goals and policies of Lehi
                City General Plan and the purposes of this code, that the property on which the
                use building is proposed to be established is of adequate size and dimensions to
                permit construction of the facilities and the conduct of the use in such manner that
                it will not be detrimental to adjoining properties in the area. He included all DRC
                comments. Commissioner Kunze seconded.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Eyre, yes. Commissioner
                Kunze, yes. Commissioner Jones, yes. Commissioner Roberts, yes.

                Motion passed unanimously.

3.6) Public hearing and recommendation of Ivory Development’s request for preliminary
subdivision review of Holbrook Place Phase 12, a 14-lot residential subdivision located at
approximately 3600 Turpin Lane.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. All lots meet the frontage and size requirements. Three lots fall
within the Jordan River overlay. The planning division has asked for a 50 foot overlay so that no
permanent structures are placed too close to the river.

Commissioner Roberts asked about the grading of the river bank, and where the 50 foot zone
would start. Mike West clarified that the zone begins where the land drops off. Vegetation and
small structures (such as sheds) will be allowed in the zone.

The applicant, Brad Mackey, was present. The lots have been configured so that they have as
little slope as possible. Access will still be available for future developers.

Commissioner Eyre asked where the subdivision is in relation to the proposed Clubhouse Drive.
The planning division showed that they are adjacent to each other.

Commissioner Everett opened public comment.

Laura Hardman represented the Hardman family and Jordan properties. She expressed
appreciation for the Joran River Overlay Zone, and suggested that the Commission visit the
property on foot. She had concerns about building too close to the river, especially considering
points where the ground is sandy and erodes easily in the rain. She expressed concern over
destruction of wildlife habitats along the river, and with storm runoff potentially going into the
river. Additionally, there is a piece of land owned by the federal government that she advised
obtaining permission to build on before starting the project. She mentioned that other than these
concerns, she thinks it is a great plan for the area.
Commissioner Everett closed public comment.

Mackey highlighted a historic discharge path and explained that the plan is to maintain it for a
small portion of the storm drainage. The rest will be going into the system on 3600 West. He also
mentioned that he had been in talks with representatives about acquiring the government
property she mentioned, but that they had led nowhere, and that the acquisition likely won’t
happen.

Commissioner Eyre asked about the grading plans for parcel A. Mackey explained that the only
plan there currently is for parcel A is the placement of a park between two lots. Commissioner
Roberts asked if there was any other use for parcel A. Mackey stated that it would be open space
for the HOA.

                Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to offer a positive recommendation to the
                city council, with the findings that the proposed subdivision meets the
                requirements of the Lehi City Development Code regarding subdivision
                approvals, and that the proposed subdivision meets the requirements of the
                Holbrook Farms area plan, including the distance of structure from the overlay
                area. He included all DRC comments. Commissioner Jones seconded.

Mackey asked a clarifying question about State Law requirements for public meetings. Mike
West assured him that he should be fine, but said he would double-check to make sure
everything was above ground.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Eyre, yes. Commissioner
                Kunze, yes. Commissioner Jones, yes. Commissioner Roberts, yes.

                Motion passed unanimously.

Commissioner Kunze asked a clarifying question about the two church lots. The planning staff

3.10) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for review of a
Development Code amendment to Chapter 12, to update standards regarding barbed wire
fencing.

Jacob Curtis presented the item. The planning division wants to adjust the zones in the code that
are allowed to use barbed wire, removing industrial zones and adding public facility zones.

Additionally, they clarify that barbed wire is allowed for agricultural purposes, and that it may be
used temporarily in construction sites. Razor wire will be prohibited in all zones.

Commissioner Eyre asked if razor wire is considered a type of barbed wire. Mike West,
representing the city, clarified that it is not.

Commissioner Everett opened and closed public comment.

Commissioner Everett worried about the possibility of the city wanting to use razor wire in the
future and being unable to, such as in the use of water security. He mentioned his preference for
remaining silent in the code if possible. West stated that there are no plans to use razor wire for
water facilities. He also clarified that the code states that anything not directly outlined as
allowed is automatically not allowed, so in this case remaining silent would have the same effect
as the clause forbidding razor wire.

                Motion: Commissioner Kunze moved to give a positive recommendation to the
                city council, finding that the proposed development code amendment is in
                conformance with the purposes, intent, and provisions of the general plan and its
                various elements. Commissioner Eyre seconded.

                Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Eyre, yes. Commissioner
                Kunze, yes. Commissioner Jones, yes. Commissioner Roberts, yes.

                Motion passed unanimously.
