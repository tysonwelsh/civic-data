The purpose of the joint work session was to receive annual training regarding the Principles of Modern Zoning
Ordinances and discuss items of joint interest.

Annual training — Principles of Modern Zoning Ordinances

Planning Manager Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide information regarding modern code
organization, zoning standards, and best practices; modern approaches to zoning have led to changes in how zoning codes are
developed compared to the traditional Euclidean zoning format used in most current zoning codes. The City has received a grant
that will aid in rewriting the City’s zoning ordinance and he would like to complete a rewrite of the subdivision and sign
ordinances simultaneously with the zoning ordinance. He indicated that zoning codes should inform community plans, which
should inform the City’s General Plan and the organization of the zoning code is very important; they should define administration
of the ordinance and procedures, highlight zoning standards, and highlight development standards. He presented a chart
illustrating multiple procedure types for various zoning matters as well as the approval authority and appeal authority for each.
Planning staff would like to streamline the review process for various types of applications; when streamlining, it is important to
use subjective criteria sparingly and clearly identify decision makers. Objective standards have been commonly used in zoning
ordinances, but they may be rigid and incomplete. Mr. Brierley presented examples of very detailed lists of approved uses in a
certain zone and suggested that these lists may be too cumbersome and do not include newer uses; the old style should be replaced
with a new style that includes use categories. For example, a list including the uses arcade, athletic club, billiard parlor, bowling
alley, boxing arena, dance hall, gymnasium, health club, indoor batting cages, miniature golf, pool hall, racquet club, roller-
skating rink, and social hall could be replaced with ‘indoor commercial recreation’. He then touched on permitted uses and
conditional uses; State Code requires the City to approve a conditional use if reasonable conditions are proposed or can be
imposed to mitigate the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of the proposed use in accordance with applicable standards.

The City can also include discretional uses in a modern zoning ordinance. He then discussed the manner in which the City’s
current code measures density; in an updated ordinance, a density range can be identified for each zone. Additionally, an updated
zoning ordinance can discuss dwelling units, mobility, parking standards, public transit, pedestrian paths, bike parking, and sign
types.

Throughout his presentation, Mr. Brierley engaged in discussion with the Council and Planning Commission regarding
issues they have noticed with the City’s zoning ordinances; the group expressed support for moving in the direction if
implementing modern zoning codes for the community. They also discussed the need to be proactive in planning for continued
growth in the City, particularly high-density housing development responsive to the demand for housing coupled with ever-
increasing housing prices.
