RESOLUTION 24-96: SUPPORT THE WEST VALLEY PERFORMING ARTS
CENTER
Ifo Pili, City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 24-96 that would support the West
Valley Performing Arts Center.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       For several years, the City has owned and operated the West Valley Performing
       Arts Center. The WVPAC has made a substantial contribution to the arts
       community in the City. However, the WVPAC requires significant repairs and is
       incurring large operational losses that make its continued operation infeasible for
       the City. This resolution expresses support for the valuable contributions made by
       the WVPAC and provides that the City will explore the possibility of transitioning
       West Valley Arts to a community-based organization. The resolution also provides
       for an initial subsidy for WVPAC in a yet to be determined amount.

The City Council will consider Resolution 24-96 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
June 4, 2024 at 6:30 P.M

WITH THE DEPARTMENT OF WORKFORCE SERVICES
Steve Pastorik, CD Director, presented proposed Resolution 24-97 that would approve
Amendment No. 1 to an Agreement With the Department of Workforce Services

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       A temporary overflow homeless shelter managed by Switchpoint opened in
       November of 2023 and was anticipated to close on April 30, 2024. However, the
       State has decided to keep the shelter open until at least the end of September of this
       year with the possibility of the shelter being open until April 30, 2025.

       Since the shelter opened, the City has seen a significant increase in the demand for
       public safety services. As a result, both the Police and Fire Departments are staffing
       overtime shifts to meet the increased demand for services.

       The Utah Department of Workforce Services offers cities with homeless shelters
       within their borders mitigation funding to help offset the increased costs resulting
       from the additional public safety service demands from the shelter and surrounding
       areas. The original contract amount for the mitigation funding was $408,776.26.
       This amendment would add $345,622.59 of additional mitigation funds to the
       contract to be used by September 30, 2024.

Councilmember Whetstone clarified that the intent is for the shelter to be open until April
and asked when staff would anticipate receiving additional funding from the State. Steve
replied possibly in October but he is unsure for certain. Councilmember Nordfelt clarified
that the shelter is expected to close in April 2025. Steve replied yes.

The City Council will consider Resolution 24-97 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
June 11, 2024 at 6:30 P.M

RESOLUTION 24-100: AUTHORIZE THE MAYOR TO SIGN A LOCAL
GOVERNMENT UNDERSTANDING AND AGREEMENT FOR CONSULTING
SERVICES FOR THREE STATION AREA PLANS
Steve Pastorik, CD, presented proposed Resolution 24-100 that would authorize the Mayor
to Sign a Local Government Understanding and Agreement for Consulting Services for Three
Station Area Plans.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The State of Utah requires West Valley City to have a Station Area Plan for the
       one-half mile radius area surrounding each light rail station. For reference, the
       State’s Station Area Plan requirements are found in Section 10-9a-403.1 of Utah
       Code. These plans are intended to promote the availability and affordability of
       housing, promote sustainable environmental conditions, enhance access to
       opportunities and increase transportation choices and connections.

       Funding for these plans has been made available by the Governor’s Office of
       Economic Opportunity. These funds have been allocated to Wasatch Front
       Regional Council (WFRC) to assist communities who are in need of preparing these
       plans. To comply with State Law, West Valley City applied for and received
       funding from WFRC for the three Station Area Plans noted above.
       MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JUNE 4, 2024
                            -6-



       To assist West Valley City and WFRC with these Station Area Plans, WFRC
       engaged a consultant selected by West Valley City. WSP USA Inc. (WSP) has
       been selected to provide assistance to complete the work required in the time frame
       allotted.

       West Valley City staff has reviewed the consultant’s qualifications, scope of work,
       schedule, budget and deliverables and is committed to working with both WFRC
       and WSP to make this effort a success. The Local Government Understanding and
       Agreement form indicates that West Valley City will in earnest consider the final
       products for formal adoption.

Mayor Lang asked when this legislation passed. Steve replied either 2022 or 2023. Mayor
Lang clarified that this isn’t a proposal to make any changes but is meant to create a plan.
Steve replied yes. Councilmember Whetstone noted that the River Trail radius extends into
South Salt Lake and asked if the two cities would collaborate. Steve replied that each City
would plan for only what is within their jurisdiction.

The City Council will consider Resolution 24-100 at the Regular Council Meeting
scheduled June 11, 2024 at 6:30 P.M

    Nichole Camac, City Recorder, provided a PowerPoint presentation summarized as
    follows:

               -   Municipal Alternate Voting Methods Pilot Project (Ranked Choice
                   Voting)
                      o Instant Runoff Voting in Utah Municipalities
                               In 2018, the Utah state legislature passed HB 35 that
                                 established a pilot program in which cities can use
                                 instant runoff voting. It passed 22-0 in the senate, 67-3
                                 in House; 11-1 in interim committee; and 7-0 in Senate
                                 Gov’t Operations. Gov. Herbert signed it into law in
                                 March 2018.
                      o Pilot Project ends in 2026
                      o To participate in the 2025 Ranked Choice Voting Election, the
                          City must pass a resolution and provide written notice to the
                          Lieutenant Governors Office prior to May 1, 2025.
               -   Ranked Choice Voting

           o Voters can rank as many or as few candidates as they want in
               order of choice.
           o All 1st choice votes are counted, and if no candidate has received

           o The process continues until there is a candidate with a majority
               of the vote cast (50% + 1), who is declared the winner.
