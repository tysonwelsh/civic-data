1.   Discussion of C-2/CP-2 allowed uses
     Mr. Rypien indicated the Commission had tabled this item at its previous meeting, asking for further
     discussion relating to the location of an automatic car wash next to residential uses. He reviewed the
     history of the Ordinance development, stating the Commission had established a pending ordinance
     in an effort to address high impact uses which are currently listed as either conditional uses or permitted
     uses in the ordinance. Once high-impact uses were identified, Staff had first considered restricting
     them to being a minimum of 100’ from a residential zone boundary. Then upon reviewing the zoning
     map, it was found nearly all C-2 and CP-2 zones are adjacent to single-family residential zones and a
     better approach may be to identify means whereby effects of these uses could be mitigated. He
     reviewed existing screening regulations as well as additional language proposed specifically to reduce
     impacts of these high-impact uses. These include limiting hours of operation, addressing noise and
     lights as well as additional screening requirements. Additional standards also were prepared to provide
     additional mitigation for car wash uses adjacent to residences, including noises studies, distance of
     building and equipment from residential zone and entry and exits be oriented away from residential
     property. He has identified existing uses in this zone and prepared maps showing restrictions if
     distance requirements were implemented.
     Because the previous discussion centered on the noise created by vacuums at car wash uses, Staff
     has prepared options relating specifically to the location of automatic car wash uses.
         Option A      No restriction on location
         Option B      Allow when at least 100’ of residential with added mitigation.
         Option C      Allow when at least 300’ of residential without mitigation.
         Option D      Allow when at least 300’ of residential with mitigation.
         Option E      Remove use from C-2/CP-2 zone.

Commissioner Graf asked if automatic car washes are allowed in other zones, noting they should not
be zoned out of the City. Mr. Rypien indicated they are a permitted use in the C-3 zone, independent
of existing residential zone boundary lines. Discussion continued among the Commission relating to
the allowed locations if restricted to either 100’ or 300’ as was mapped. Commissioner Southwick
asked if a map also could be provided showing restrictions if the distance is 200’ and whether there is
data to determine an appropriate distance or whether these numbers are arbitrary. Commissioner
Aaberg favored Option E. Commissioner Graf asked if there is a benefit to the City for these uses,
noting they pay no sales tax and have few employees. He asked if there are any positive effects they
bring to neighborhoods. Chair Sandau expressed concern these are typically tall buildings which
cannot be repurposed if the use were to abandon a particular site. Commission questioned whether
there is a need for these at all, noting the benefit is clean cars. Commissioner Blaisdell felt the role of
the Planning Commission is to determine what is in the best interest of the City for the future by
developing code requirements. Commissioner Williams stated these uses become an eyesore, they
do not benefit the City by bringing jobs or tax revenue. Discussion continued relating to the distance
requirements, noting if restricted to beyond 300’ from a zone boundary, there would be few locations
they would be allowed. Commission felt if they were allowed, mitigation of each of the effects should
be implemented by code. They continued discussion relating to other identified impacts from the use.
Commissioner Williams felt these uses eliminate the charm of Ogden’s neighborhoods and create an
individual loss of property value for homes nearby. Commissioner Shinoda felt the use could be an
appropriate use if managed correctly to reduce the effects of noise. He felt they can co-exist with
neighborhoods.
Mr. Rypien presented options relating to noise from vacuums, noise from the dryers, noise from
customer stereos as well as effects of traffic, lights and signage. He asked the Commission to consider
which of these might be most appropriate as well as to determine whether other options might be
appropriate to protect surrounding neighborhoods.
Options to be considered relating to noise from vacuums:
    Option A     Require tunnel and vacuums to be at least 50’ from residential boundary.
    Option B     Require a noise study if within 300’ of residential boundary.
    Option C     Require vacuums to be screened.
    Option D     Require manager to monitor vacuums daily.
Commission felt an on-site manager would be a beneficial requirement, noting there is typically an
employee on site, but expressed concern the monitoring of vacuum noise may not be enforceable.
Commissioner Southwick asked if the number of vacuums were limited if overall noise might be
reduced. It was suggested there might be a requirement for vacuum to be turned off when not in use.
Commission favored the implementation of both Options A and D.

Options to address noise from dryers and equipment in tunnel
    Option A     Require vacuums and tunnel to be 50’ from residential boundary.
    Option B     Require a noise study if within 300’ of residential boundary .
    Option C     Require dryer system to be insulated.
    Option D     Require entrance and exit from tunnel to close during each wash or face away from
                 residential uses.
    Option E     Require a noise study, require dryers to be insulated, require building materials to
                 reduce noise.
    It was suggested a noiseless, dripless system should be in place upon vehicles exiting the car
    wash. It was suggested a completely enclosed system may have little impact on surrounding
    neighbors.
Options to reduce noise from car stereos:
    Option A     Require car wash to post signage to prohibit customers from using car stereos when
                 doors or windows are open.
    Option B      Post signage and require on-site manager to enforce rule.
    Option C     Post signage to ask customers to be considerate and not use stereo while
                 vacuuming.

2.   Discussion of Short-Term Rentals in Downtown
     Mr. Brierley stated the Commission has held several work sessions as well as a public hearing to
     determine whether short-term rentals should be allowed in the downtown area. He noted Visit Ogden
     had approached the City in an effort to increase visitor stays in the downtown rather than in outlying
     areas. He reviewed the existing standards, stating current regulations identify a hotel as more than 15
     units with an on-site manager, and indicated individual short-term rentals are not allowed. He stated
     concern had been expressed regarding mixing short-term and long-term rentals due to potential noise
     created by short term rentals. At the public hearing, owners identified the need for limited time short-
     term rentals such as temporary housing for visitors who may be temporarily employed in the area or
     contract employees. Staff is proposing apartment buildings may lease as either all short-term or long-
     term rental, but not both or may convert to a hotel use with an on-site manager. The proposal would
     allow existing apartments to be used as a short-term rentals for a longer period of time so long as these
     are not the primary residence of tenants. These would be required to be furnished apartments with
     utilities in the owner’s name, and leased on a month-to-month basis. Other units in the same building
     can also be listed as short-term rentals, but may be for only a few overnight stays at a time. This allows
     the long-term resident to be aware the adjacent unit may be for a more limited period of time.
     Commissioner Southwick felt the terminology should assure fair housing is available, such as corporate
     rentals, stating the buildings with long-term tenants typically do not attract vacation travelers.
     Commissioner Safsten asked if measures also include reducing the potential of warehousing people.
     It was indicated standards for short-term rentals are based on the number of bedrooms, with a limit of
     two beds per room. Commissioner Williams asked if these could be operated by nonprofit
     organizations and become a service to the homeless population. Mr. Brierley acknowledged this is a
     concern, and the regulations governing short-term rentals indicate the tenant has a permanent address
     elsewhere and it is not a primary dwelling. However, if the entire building is leased as long-term

3.   Discussion of Subdivision Ordinance Changes

     Mr. Brierley stated due to recent changes in State laws regulating subdivisions, which need to be
     codified prior to the overall unified code is developed. The regulation limits review of single-family,
     duplex and townhome subdivisions to one land use authority, while Ogden’s process is to be
     reviewed by both the Planning Commission and Mayor. He stated some communities require both
     preliminary and final reviews by both the Planning Commission and City Council, causing cost
     increases in the development process. He stated the Commission should recommend whether this
     authority should be the Planning Commission or the Mayor, indicating administration favors the
     authority to be the Mayor.

     Chair Sandau expressed as ordinances are in place regulating subdivisions, the Commission’s role is
     to assure these standards are implemented. She stated the Commission may sometimes identify
     items which need further review. Mr. Brierley indicated there are often important decisions or
     recommendations the Planning Commission should be made such as the location of road
     extensions or other details, and typically the Mayor agrees with the recommendations of the
     Planning Commission. Chair Sandau expressed the Commission often feels frustrated when
     residents attend in the hopes of stopping the development of a subdivision when the Commission
     has no power to deny the use so long as it meets Ordinance requirements. Commissioner
     Southwick asked if it would continue to be a public meeting, and whether neighbors would continue
     to be notified. Mr. Brierley indicated whether the Commission or the Mayor is the approval body,
     residents would be notified, and a public meeting held. While these would be public meetings,
     public comment may or may not be allowed. He stated while administration is recommending this
     be the Mayor, the Planning Commission may recommend it continue in their setting, and as this is
     an ordinance amendment, the final determination would be made by the City Council. Commission
     expressed it may be more helpful if approved by the Planning Commission as more eyes reviewing
     the same item often makes for a better project. Discussion continued relating to the intent of this
     bill as developers often are frustrated by the timing process.

     Mr. Brierley stated the Commission would continue to review site plans for group dwellings or
     planned unit developments, then if the units were later subdivided, the review would be the single
     authority. He stated standards of the Ordinance would be applicable in either situation, as well as
     building and fire codes and engineering standards. He recommended reasons for their
     determination should be clearly defined and articulated in the motion. Commission further
     discussed both options, stating a public body such as the Planning Commission would provide
     better transparency, while the Mayor, as an elected authority may be more appropriate. Chair
     Sandau commented as the Commission is more responsible to Ordinance development, the
     standards are mostly in place and there is little need for review by either body. Commissioner Graf
     felt as volunteers, the Planning Commission creates a buffer between residents and administration.
     Chair Sandau felt the dual approval currently in place creates a quality check for projects.

     Mr. Brierley indicated the subject bill also will require some other technical changes to the
     Ordinance, particularly relating to the appeal process which also will be presented for their review in
     October.
