GENERAL PLAN\ZONE CHANGE APPLICATIONS
GPZ-13-2021 Petition by BRIAN PITCHER requesting a General Plan change from Low
            Density Residential (3 to 4 units/acre) to Light Manufacturing and a zone change
            from R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 sq. ft.) to
            M (Manufacturing). The property is located at 4312 South 4000 West and 4270
            South 4060 West on 1.81 acres. (Staff – Steve Pastorik at 801-963-3545 or
            steve.pastorik@wvc-ut.gov)

Discussion:   Steve Pastorik explained that the subject property is made up of two unusual
              parcels developed between 1950 and 1960. At that time, Steve stated, parcels
              were not given a zone designation. Steve noted that because the subject property
              was developed prior to it being designated a zone, all of the current uses on the
              property are non-conforming uses. He explained that the parcel to the east is a U-
             shaped property wrapping around an auto repair shop. This parcel includes a
             triplex and mobile homes along the perimeter extending into the property to the
             west, where there is also a storage unit building. Steve then presented the
             applicant's concept plan. The applicant would like to put smaller storage units on
             the parcel to the east and one large storage unit building on the property to the
             west. In speaking with the fire department, Steve explained that the applicant
             would need fire access. He said they could either put the access around the large
             storage unit or break up the building for better circulation.
             Steve said staff would not usually see value in changing R-1-8 zoneing to M to
             add storage units. He stated this situation is different in that all uses currently on
             the subject property are non-conforming. Storage units are already built on the
             property, and the proposed plan could improve the property's condition.
             Commissioner Fuller asked Steve if he had come up with a plan that includes fire
             access. Steve explained that he had received feedback from the fire department
             earlier this week. He has provided the applicant with that information, and they
             are working on a revised concept plan for the Planning Commission to view at
             next week's public hearing.
             Commissioner Fuller asked if there would be an onsite manager. Steve said that
             there is an office. However, he believes this office will be used for leasing and not
             a live-in manager.
             Commissioner Lovato asked if there will be a fence or wall between the auto
             repair shop and the subject property. Steve said that there would be a wall along
             the back of the auto repair shop. He said he would confirm what type of wall or
             fence they are planning to put there.
             Steve asked the Commission how they were feeling about the subject property
             being a storage use. Commissioner Lovato said that the subject property had had
             non-conforming uses on it for so long that it would be hard to say what should or
             should not be developed there. He feels the proposed plan would be an
             improvement over what is existing currently. Commissioner Wood also feels this
             would be an improvement and help clean up the subject location.

DISCUSSION ITEM
Small Planned Unit Developments (PUDs)

Discussion:   Steve Pastorik recapped what had been discussed in the prior study meeting. He
              explained that there was discussion about things that could be done within the
              ordinance to help smaller HOA’s be successful. Steve gave the example of
              having standards for private streets, so they are well built and will last longer.
              Steve presented several smaller PUDs within the City to look at and compare the
              overall maintenance of these developments.

              Commissioner Fuller asked if most of the units are owned by someone other than
              the HOA, wouldn’t the maintenance of the buildings be the responsibility of the
              owner not the HOA? Steve said that is true for the inside of the unit, but for
              things like roofing or siding, the HOA would be responsible for that. Steve said
              he has seen some exceptions and asked the Commission if they were aware of any
              exceptions.

              Commissioner Woodruff thought that they had placed a limit on the number of
              rentals a development was allowed to have. Steve explained that the City has not
              put a limit on the number of rentals. He said someone could purchase a home to
              keep as an investment and rent it out, so there isn’t really a way for the City to
              limit how many units become rentals in the future.

              Commissioner Lovato asked if there are some maintenance issues that code
              enforcement could help with. Steve explained that if the issue violated the City
              ordinance, then code enforcement can be involved. Steve stated that if the issue is
              addressed in the CC&Rs, the City could not enforce.

              Steve said the main question here would be if there is anything the City can do to
              increase the likelihood of a smaller HOA being successful. He asked the
              Commission if they feel increasing the minimum size of the property, in terms of
              acreage or having a minimum number of units would be helpful.

              The Commission discussed how there does not seem to be a strong correlation
              between the smaller PUDs and how the property is maintained when looking at
              the age of the development or if the units are rented or owned. Commissioner
              Woodruff feels that the City might be doing as well as they can with supporting
              the smaller HOA’s. Commissioner Lovato agrees with Commissioner Woodruff.

              Steve stated that he could share this presentation with the Commission so they can
              review it again, where there is an abundance of information. Steve said staff
              would like to move forward with instituting private street standards if the
              Commission is comfortable with that.

              Steve gave a recent example of a proposal for a 10 unit development on just under
              an acre that was denied. He said the recorded reason for the denial was the size of
              the development. Steve stated the developer had mentioned if the smaller
              developments are a concern, passing that information on to the developers would
              be helpful. Commissioner Woodruff remembers that proposal and stated he voted
              against it because the zone was just changed a year ago and he does not want to
              keep trying a different project in that same location every year.

              Commissioner Fuller pointed out that if we view the City as an HOA, there are
              projects that still need to be finished, so maybe the size isn’t the issue.
