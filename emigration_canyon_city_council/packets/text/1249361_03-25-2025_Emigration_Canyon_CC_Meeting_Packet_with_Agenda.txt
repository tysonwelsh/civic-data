both cancellations. He mentioned efforts to get Station 119 crews involved in conducting home
assessments for the Firewise community initiative, with training planned for early March.
Chief Case reminded the council about the Comprehensive Emergency Management Plan

input if discussed further. He provided updates on two relevant House Bills:
1) House Bill 48 - Wildland Urban Interface (WUI) modifications bill. This addresses risks in

   evaluate properties annually and enter cooperative agreements with the Division of
   Forestry, Fire and State Lands. The bill also provides guidelines for insurers regarding
   high-risk property assessments.

Chief Case indicated that UFA representatives will likely participate in the rule-making process
for both bills.

   F. 2025 Municipal Election Voting Options (Rank Choice Voting vs. Primary/General
      Election) – Diana Baun, City Recorder

Diana Baun presented information on voting options for the 2025 municipal election. She
explained the differences between ranked choice voting and standard primary/general
elections, including potential costs and implementation challenges. For ranked choice voting, it
was highlighted that the city would share the cost burden of the necessary software, costing
$26,000, which would be split among all participating cities. If only a few cities choose ranked
choice voting, the cost could be higher; conversely, if many cities participate, the cost would be
lower. For smaller cities like Emigration Canyon, ranked choice voting might be more
expensive due to the up-front costs. To implement ranked choice voting, they would need to
file their intent with the Lieutenant Governor’s Office by April 1st. Estimated costs were
provided for different scenarios, explaining that a primary election could cost between $1,800
and $6,009, depending on the number of cities holding primaries. The general election was
estimated to cost about $3,600, plus a $500 administration fee bundled in as a deposit. In

Council members discussed the options and their implications. Cameron Platt, the city
attorney, provided additional information on the recent change to city status and how it affects

   G. Salt Lake County Justice Courts – Cameron Platt, Legal Counsel

Cameron Platt provided an update on Senate Joint Resolution 3 (SGR003), discussing the
amendments made, which move the effective date to June 30, 2027. This resolution is crucial
for the county as it sets the groundwork for withdrawing from providing justice court services.
The expectation is that upon its passage, the county will notify the judicial council, which will
then have to approve the cessation of the county justice court. If this occurs, the city of
Emigration Canyon will need to explore alternatives for handling its justice court needs. He laid
out potential options, including contracting with existing justice courts or pursuing the
certification of their own justice court. However, he noted that setting up a city-specific justice
court would involve significant financial investment and a lengthy certification process. He also
elaborated on the distinctions between criminal and civil code enforcement. He suggested the
city consider transitioning to a civil code enforcement approach, which could offer enhanced
flexibility and a more considerate or "gentler hand" for addressing violations, such as parking
infractions and nuisance issues. Civil code enforcement operates on a lower burden of proof,
needing only a preponderance of evidence (51%) rather than the higher standard required in
criminal cases, which is beyond a reasonable doubt (90-95%). Unlike criminal violations, which
can carry the risk of criminal records and jail sentences, civil code enforcement would involve
fines set out in the city’s fee schedule and would focus on corrective orders rather than
punitive measures.

Mayor Smolka emphasized the importance of thoroughly exploring this option, noting the
potential benefits it could provide in streamlining enforcement practices. He suggested the city
revisit the topic of civil code enforcement in a future council meeting to consider its feasibility
and implementation further, particularly in the event that the legislation passes and
necessitates an organizational shift within the city's judicial processes.

9. City Attorney Updates

Cameron Platt provided clarification on election rules, emphasizing that council members do
not need to resign from their current seats to run for another office. However, they can only
declare candidacy for one elected position at a time. He explained various scenarios for how
seats would be filled or vacated depending on election outcomes. He noted that if a current
council member decides to run for mayor and wins, their council seat will become vacant at the
end of the current year, and the council would need to appoint someone to fill the position. If
the council member runs but does not win, they can continue to serve their current council
term. For the upcoming election, he highlighted the change from metro township governance
to city status, which affects how roles are structured. The mayor, now elected independently,
will no longer be selected from among council members. This change is aimed at maintaining
a staggered election cycle for council seats, aligning with the requirement for approximately
half of the council seats to be up for election following a presidential election year. Platt also
briefly discussed options for changing the form of government, including potentially moving to
a six-member council if desired by the community. However, as explained previously, that
change would require public voting and wouldn't take effect until 2029 or later.

       This Interlocal Cooperation Agreement (this “Agreement”) is entered into by and
between SALT LAKE COUNTY, a body corporate and politic of the State of Utah, on
behalf of its County Clerk’s Office, Election’s Division (the “County”); and EMIGRATION
CANYON, a municipal corporation of the State of Utah (the “City”). The County and the
City may each be referred to herein as a “Party” and collectively as the
“Parties.”

                                     R E C I T A L S:

       A.     The County and the City are “public agencies” as defined by the Utah
Interlocal Cooperation Act, UTAH CODE §§ 11-13-101 to -608 (the “Interlocal Act”), and
as such, are authorized to enter into agreements to act jointly and cooperatively in a
manner that will enable them to make the most efficient use of their resources and
powers.

      B.    Utah Code § 20A-5-400.1 permits the County to enter into interlocal
agreements with local municipalities to conduct their elections.

       C.      The County desires to provide the services of its Clerk’s Office, Elections
Division, to the City for the purpose of assisting the City in conducting the City’s 2025
primary and general municipal elections.

       D.     The City desires to engage the County for such services.

                                   A G R E E M E N T:

      NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual representations, warranties,
covenants and agreements contained herein, the sufficiency of which is hereby
acknowledged, the Parties represent and agree as follows:

                         ARTICLE 1 — ELECTION SERVICES

        1.1. Scope of Work. The services to be provided by the County shall be as set
forth in the Scope of Work, attached hereto and incorporated by reference as Exhibit
“A.” Generally, the County shall perform the listed election functions as set forth in
Exhibit “A” and as needed to ensure implementation of the City’s 2025 primary and

                                             1
general municipal elections, and shall furnish all materials, labor and equipment to
complete the requirements and conditions of this Agreement.

        1.2. Cost. The City shall pay the County the actual cost of conducting its
election. A good faith range of costs of such services (as well as any amount of pre-
payment required by the County) shall be provided in Exhibit “B,” and will be attached
hereto and incorporated by reference. At the conclusion of the elections, the County
shall provide the City with a final invoice in writing based on its actual expenses, and the
City shall pay the County within thirty days of receiving the invoice. The invoice shall
contain a summary of the overall costs of the election and shall provide the amount
billed to the City for participating in the elections. In the case of a vote recount, election
system audit, election contest, or similar event arising out of the City’s election, the City
shall pay the County’s actual costs of responding to such events, as set forth in the final
invoice. The City acknowledges that the final invoice amount for these additional
services may cause the total cost to the City to exceed the good faith range given to the
City by the County.

        1.3. Legal Requirements. The County and the City understand and agree that
the City’s 2025 primary and general municipal elections are the City’s elections. The
City shall be responsible for compliance with all legal requirements for these elections.
The City agrees to translate ballot issues, if any, into Spanish. The County will provide
the remaining Spanish translations for the ballot and other election materials as required
by law. The County agrees to work with the City in complying with all legal requirements
for the conduct of these elections and conduct these elections pursuant to the direction
of the City, except as provided in this Agreement and Exhibit “A.” The County agrees to
disclose and maintain election results through its website merely as a courtesy and
convenience to the City. The City, and not the County, is responsible to resolve any and
all election questions, problems, and legal issues that are within the City’s statutory
authority.

       1.4.   Rank Choice Voting.

               (a)    The County and the City understand and agree that if the County
       provides services or resources to conduct an instant runoff voting election as
       described in sections 20A-4-603 and -604, UTAH CODE (2024) (“Rank Choice
       Voting”), the actual cost of administering such an election will be added to the
       City’s invoice.

             (b)    If the City decides to hold a Rank Choice Voting election, then, in
       accordance with Utah Code § 20A-4-602(3)(a), it shall provide the Lt. Governor
       and the County with notice of its intent to use Rank Choice Voting as its selected
       method of voting, no later than April 30, 2025.

              (c)   The County reserves the right to refuse to conduct a Rank Choice
       Voting contest with more than 10 candidates. The City acknowledges that, if the
       County agrees to conduct a Rank Choice Voting contest where more than 10
       candidates appear on the ballot, then voters may only rank up to 10 candidates.

Agreement, and said exercise shall be without any liability of, or penalty to, the
County.

1.5.   Independent Contractor.

       (a)     Because the County is consolidating election functions in order to
conduct multiple, simultaneous elections on August 12, 2025, and on November
4, 2025, certain decisions by the County referenced in Exhibit “A” may not be
subject to review by the City. It is therefore understood by the parties that the
County will act as an independent contractor with regard to its decisions
regarding resources, procedures and policies based upon providing a consistent
type, scope and level of service to all participating jurisdictions made for the
benefit of the whole as set forth in Exhibit “A.”

        (b)    The County, as part of the consideration herein, shall comply with
all applicable federal, state and county laws governing elections. The City
agrees that the direction it gives the County under Utah Code § 20A-5-
400.1(2)(a) and this Agreement shall likewise be in strict compliance with all such
applicable laws. The County shall be under no obligation to comply with any
direction from the City that is not demonstrably consistent with all applicable
federal, state and county laws governing elections.
