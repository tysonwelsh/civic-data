GENERAL PLAN/ZONE CHANGE APPLICATION
GPZ-1-2021 PETITION BY ALEX WINDER REQUESTING A GENERAL PLAN
           CHANGE FROM GENERAL COMMERCIAL TO LIGHT
           MANUFACTURING AND A ZONE CHANGE FROM C-2 (GENERAL
           COMMERCIAL) TO LI (LIGHT INCUSTRIAL). THE PROPERTY IS
           LOCATED AT 5718 W 3500 S ON 5.35 ACRES. (STAFF – STEVE
           PASTORIK 801-963-3545)

             Discussion: Steve Pastorik explained that several years ago, the City adopted an
             update to the Hunter Town Center Small Area Plan, which is part of the General
             Plan. The plan anticipated that the Kmart site would be redeveloped, and that did
             not happen. Steve stated there is now an Alpine Storage and a Vasa Fitness
             located on the property, so the likelihood of redevelopment here is low. Steve said
             the question now is what will fit on the subject property. Steve stated there is no

               direct access off 5600 West or 3500 South to the subject property, but there is
               access through two easements. From staff's perspective, Steve explained that
               putting in a retail business or residential homes would be a challenge not having
               direct access to the subject location. He stated there was a conversation about
               putting in multi-family residential housing; however, the subject property is not
               eligible for multi-family. He noted the applicant wants to put in flex space, which
               is office/warehouse space. The current zone is C-2 (General Commercial) and
               does allow for office/warehouse uses, but warehouse space is limited to 50
               percent of the building. Steve explained that this is why the applicant is asking
               for the zone change to LI (Light Industrial).

               Commissioner Fuller asked Steve if the 50 percent warehouse space pertains to
               the entire building or each tenant. Steve stated it pertains to the whole building.
               So, one tenant could have more than 50 percent, but the total would need to equal
               50 percent for the entire building.

               Steve presented the conceptual plan that shows storefront and overhead doors on
               both sides of the building.

               Commissioner Woodruff asked if they could have signage.
               Steve said they could coordinate with the other tenants to add signage to the
               existing multi-tenant sign.

               Commissioner Winters asked if the light industrial zone allows for self-storage.
               Steve said self-storage is permitted in a manufacturing zone but not the light
               industrial zone. Commissioner Lovato asked if the adjacent tenants would be
               impacted by the noise level from the light industrial zone. Steve stated the light
               industrial zone does not allow outside storage or any outdoor uses such as
               manufacturing or working on vehicles. So, any noise, except for vehicles, would
               occur inside, minimizing any noise impact.

               Commissioner Lovato stated there is a development similar to this proposal on
               2700 West and Parkway Blvd. that has a line of cars down the street. He asked if
               this is permitted. Steve stated there is a mobile home park on the east side of
               2700 West, south of Parkway, and the parking challenges there are mainly from
               the mobile home park residents.

               Commissioner McEwen asked what kind of tenants would occupy the
               office/warehouse space. Steve indicated that the applicant does not have specific
               tenants in mind. He explained this flex space would be 10 to 20% office, and then
               the balance being warehousing space or light industrial space. Steve gave an
               example of a contractor renting a space to keep materials stored inside the
               building and park his company truck onsite.

               Jody Knapp agreed with Steve's explanation and stated that the light industrial
               zone does not allow for a retail business or a restaurant.

SUBDIVISION APPLICATION
SA-1-2021 PETITION BY DAVID DRAPER, REPRESENTING THE PROPERTY
          OWNER, IS REQUESTING PRELIMINARY APPROVAL FOR THE
          DECKER STATION SUBDIVISION. THE SUBDIVISION WILL
          CONSIST OF 1 LOTS, IS ZONED RM (MULTIPLE UNIT DWELLING
          RESIDENTIAL) AND IS LOCATED AT 3100 SOUTH DECKER LAKE
          DRIVE. (STAFF - STEVE LEHMAN AT 801-963-3311)

               Discussion: Steve Lehman explained this application to resolve some property
               issues related to the new Decker Station Apartments. Steve gave a background of
               the original plat in 2008 that created the E-Center Retail Subdivision and the 2015
               amendment to create the Amended E-Center Retail Subdivision. Steve stated this
               application would amend that plat to consolidate the subject property from two
               lots to one lot. He pointed out a public utility easement running east and west
               through the subject property that can only be vacated through the public hearing
               process would also be part of the amended plat. Steve explained this application
               would vacate the public utility easement, consolidate the 2 into 1 lot and dedicate
               portions of Decker Lake Drive and 3100 South.

CONDITIONAL USE APPLICATION
C-1-2021 PETITION BY SHUPE BUILDERS REQUESTING CONDITIONAL USE
         APPROVAL FOR AND OFFICE/WAREHOUSE FACILITY. THE
         PROPERTY IS LOCATED AT 1875 W. 3600 S ON .45 ACRES AND IS
         ZONED C-2 (GENERAL COMMERCIAL). (STAFF – JODY KNAPP 801-
         963-3497)

               Discussion: Jody Knapp explained this application is for an office/warehouse use
               in a C-2 zone, and the applicant is proposing a 50/50 split. The subject property is
               one lot of a subdivision on a private road. The building is just over 4800 square
               feet with four spaces. She explained the applicant is looking to occupy two of the
               spaces and lease out the other two spaces. Jody explained this property is adjacent
               to a residential zone, so along with the landscape setback will be an addition of a
               wall. Jody noted a few trees would need to be added to the landscaping. Jody
               recommended that the applicant take a closer look at adding more detail to the
               east elevation to meet the commercial design standards.


DISCUSSION ITEM
Potential City initiated zone changes

               Discussion: Steve Pastorik noted this item was discussed at the January 6, 2021
               study meeting. He reminded the Commission that this discussion item pertains to
               the properties within the city that are zoned for duplexes or multi-family;
               however, they are not developed as duplexes or multi-family.
               Steve stated that the Commission requested a way to rank the properties to see
               which locations would have more reasons to be rezoned. Steve explained a 0-5
Planning Commission Study Meeting

               ranking where 0 would have no reason to retain the current zoning to 5, five
               reasons to maintain the current zoning. He then explained the categories for
               which the properties would be scored.

               1. Would the property be eligible for RM zoning? (1 point)
               2. Is the property adjacent or joining existing multi-family and, if so, on how
                  many sides? (1 point per side)
               3. Is it next to commercial use? (1 point)
               4. Is it developed as either commercial or mobile homes? (1 point)
               5. What is the General Plan designation? (1 point)
               Commissioner Lovato asked how the applications would be brought to the
               Commission. Steve stated this could be done in several ways. We could look at
               each property one at a time or as a group. He explained they would need to be
               noticed separately so, we would probably have separate applications for each one
               and put several on one agenda. Commissioner Lovato asked how long this process
               would take. Steve stated it would depend on how aggressively it is pursued.

               Commissioner Woodruff asked Steve what the advantages would be to rezone
               these properties. Steve gave an example of a single-family home that is developed
               in a multi-family zone. He explained rezoning this property would retain the
               already developed single-family home.

               Commissioner Woodruff stated he could see properties that rank 0-2 being looked
               at to rezone. Commissioner Winters agreed with Commissioner Woodruff.

               Steve thanked the Commission for their feedback and explained this would be
               presented to the City Manager and then to the City Council. Depending upon the
               result, the Commission might see one of these properties on a future agenda.

There being no further business, the meeting adjourned at 4:49 p.m.
