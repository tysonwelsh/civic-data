Public Comment No comments 2018 MPO Project Selection Process – Shawn Eliot, MAG Staff Every two years MPO staff review with the MPO committees the process for selecting projects to be awarded transportation funds. The MPO selects projects for Exchange, MPO Federal and UC Trans Tax. Total draft funding is $43.8m. Eligible projects include but are not limited to new roads, widening projects, intersection improvement, active transportation. Road projects must be on a state functional class map, and listed or supported in the Regional Transportation Plan. Project Idea report timeline: December 1: Submit draft Project Idea Report December 4-7: Review draft with MPO staff December 15: Submit final Project Idea Report January 8: Sponsors present to MPO TAC for their review and scoring February 1: MPO RPC reviews TAC scores February 9: Submit draft Concept Report February 12-15: Review draft with MPO staff March 8: Submit final Concept Report March 29: Once scored, MPO staff disseminate results to MPO TAC for review April 9: MPO TAC holds a Field Review of the projects April 23: MPO TAC meets to consider each project on its technical merits May 3: MPO RPC is presented the draft Project Priority List June 7: MPO RPC discusses priority order and approves final Project Priority List July 23: MPO TAC reviews draft TIP and makes recommendations to MPO RPC August 2: MPO RPC approves final TIP Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee November 9, 2017 Page 3 Project Priority Report can be found at: www.mountainland.org/transportationfunding Utah County Corridor Preservation Fund Annual Report – Jim Price, MAG Staff An annual report was provided on the current disposition of properties purchased through the Local Option Corridor Preservation Fund program and not yet utilized by completed roadway projects. Also included is the estimated fund balance available for Rights of Way acquisition. The program is overseen by the Regional Planning Committee for and in behalf of the Utah County Council of Governments. Cities and counties can apply at www.mountainland.org A correction was noted on the annual report handout. Both listings are in Utah County. Low Income Veteran’s Transit Program – Andrew Jackson, MAG Staff United Way Continuum of Care announced a program that provides transportation for Utah County veterans so they can get to and from their medical appointments and for employment. Transit passes are funded by MAG Aging Department in partnership with UTA, Provo City and the Utah County Veterans Association. Please send referrals to the MAG Aging Department. Passes available for bus, frontrunner and tracks. Street Connectivity Study Findings – Tim Hereth, MAG Staff Development through recent history has changed from a square block and grid street network to a disconnected hierarchical street network. This study discovers why street connectivity matters, how to measure it, and how to improve it. It looked at three difference geographic levels and multiple land-use development types. The end product is the “Utah Street Connectivity Guide” which is a guide to help communities improve quality of life through better street connectivity. MPO staff recommends that cities review the “Utah Street Connectivity Guide” and see how its recommendations can fit within their plans. The guide can be found at https://mountainland.org/img/transportation/Studies/Utah%20Street%20Connectivity%20Guide.pdf Utah Valley Rides Program – Chad Eccles, MAG Staff Utah Valley Rides is a door-to-door, pilot program providing reliable transportation to seniors, low income families, and persons with disabilities in Provo and Orem. The program provides rides from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays. Utah Valley Rides is a cooperative partnership with MAG, UTA and United Way of Utah County. The program is a volunteer driver program with two ADA equipped vehicles funded by the partners along with formula FTA 5310 money. Initially the program will operate in Provo and Orem with the limited times of 9:00 am to 2:00 pm. As the program grows with more vehicles and volunteers they hope to offer the service for greater lengths of time and to a much larger coverage area. More information can be found at www.utahvalleyrides.org Third Quarter Cent Sales Tax Discussion - Andrew Jackson, MAG Staff The MPO Finance Committee met earlier today to discuss the third quarter sales tax and it was decided to keep the bill file open, look at amending the MPO bylaws, and that the county should not repeal the sales tax. The Regional Planning Committee had a general discussion on the MPO Finance Committees decision. Commissioner Ivie suggested that if a project gets a certain percentage of the votes it has a certainty of execution and guaranteed to be funded. If it does not reach a certain threshold it would go to the county for a second review. Mayor Brunst moved to have the bill file stay open; have the MPO Regional Planning Committee work with the legislature for clarification on Utah Code 59-12-2218 and make consistent the language in the code; make adjustments to the MPO bylaws; and to bring back the findings to the body for further discussion. Mark Thompson seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.
