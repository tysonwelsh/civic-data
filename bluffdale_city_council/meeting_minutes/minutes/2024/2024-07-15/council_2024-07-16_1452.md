       3.1        Resolution 2024-ResoIution 2024-57- Consideration and Vote on Reduced

Economic and Community Development Director, Grant Crowell, presented the Resolution and
reported that it was to approve the Transportation Fee reduction and authorize Staff to execute an
agreement between the City, Bluffdale Marketplace, LLC ("Woodbury"), and Kroger Corporation.
He stated that the project has encompassed many years and the calculation ofImpact Fees has changed
over that time.


Mr. Crowell stated that construction of 14000 South was part of the original agreement between
Bluffdale and Woodbury, which also included a Reimbursement Agreement. All Transportation
Impact Fees collected from The Ranch go toward reimbursing the $1.4 million paid by Woodbury to
construct the road, and there was some initial concern that any Impact Fee reduction would result in
insufficient funds for repayment. Staff discussed the issue with all parties and concluded that the
reduced fee would be sufficient. Mr.Crowell noted that there are still available pad sites in the project,
as well as some sites that have been sold but not yet developed. That future growth,in addition to the
proposed $849,000 Transportation Fee from Kroger Corporation, will cover the frill amount due to
Woodbury.


It was Mayor Hall's understanding that Bluffdale would ask Kroger to begin construction in July and
asked ifthat was discussed with the parties. Mr.Crowell reported that he received verbal confirmation
from Woodbury that they would agree to the terms of the Transportation Fee agreement if Kroger
agreed to begin construction by July 20, 2024. That is not an agreement with Bluffdale. From the
City's perspective, contractors are now working on the Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan and
Land Disturbance Permit, which are the final steps before pre-construction, but there is no agreed-
upon start date. Mr. Donaldson added that Staff believes they are ready to begin construction, and
that is why the Special City Council Meeting was called. Mayor Hall thanked Staff for their time in
effort in moving the project forward in a timely manner.

Council Member Wilding stated that he would be presenting both his own questions and those of
Council Member Austin. He asked if any other site plan would be affected by this agreement.
Mr. Crowell stated that the agreement is specific to the Smith's Grocery Store and Kroger
Corporation. Other site plans are in process, including an office building, but the agreement would
not apply to that or any other site plan. Mr. Donaldson clarified that anyone may apply for an Impact
Fee reduction, but it is ultimately a City Council decision. Mr. Crowell added that companies may
apply for a reduction before the fees are paid. After they are paid,it would require an appeals process.

Council Member Wilding asked ifthe agreement would change the Impact Fee calculations for other
properties or encourage others to also request a reduction. Mr. Crowell stated that most commercial
applicants have questions about Impact Fees, but the Council would need to consider their unique
circumstances before granting a reduction. He noted that any fees collected in addition to the $1.4
million due to Woodbury would be placed in the City's Impact Fee Fund, but Staff did not believe
additional fees would be collected. Council Member Wilding stated that development of the
remaining parcels should generate enough fees to repay Woodbury. He was in favor ofthe reduction.
Mr. Donaldson stated that the reason for the detailed recitals in the Resolution and agreement is to
demonstrate that it is a unique circumstance as it is the first grocery store in Bluffdale.

Council Member Lord asked if the Impact Fees were increased due to the City's expectation that
construction costs would be higher. Mr. Crowell stated that that was one aspect of it, but the main
reason is the expiration ofa 40% reduction made by a previous City Coimcil. Administrative Services
Director, Bruce Kartchner, indicated that state law requires the City to reevaluate its Impact Fee
schedules regularly. That usually involves adjustments and normally increases because of the
inflationary cost ofthe infrastructure. The actual projects that will be part ofthe Impact Fee schedule
must also be considered because additional projects may have been added since the last calculation.
He indicated that the biggest change was the 40% reduction made by the Coimcil previously in order
to spur development that has since expired.

Mr. Donaldson added that the methodology for calculating the Transportation Impact Fee changed
significantly. It was previously understated and now more accurately reflects the actual costs.
Mr. Kartchner added that it was previously so complicated that developers had to do traffic studies
