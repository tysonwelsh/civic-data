11            City Noise Ordinance and Common Code Enforcement Issues
12
13   Justice Goble, Resident of Riverton City, addressed the Council regarding the City's noise
14   ordinance, specifically a section that restricts the operation of off-road machinery, snowmobiles,
15   and snow blowers within 800 feet of a dwelling. The resident explained that this ordinance,
16   originally adopted from Salt Lake County, may not be practical for the City. He became aware of
17   the ordinance after a neighbor reported his children riding dirt bikes in his backyard. He
18   proposed that the City consider modifying the ordinance to align with neighboring cities like
19   Herriman and Bluffdale, which do not have blanket distance restrictions but instead prohibit off-
20   road vehicles in specific locations, such as public parks or protected lands.
21
22            Business Licensing Code
23
24   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, provided an overview of the City’s business license code, a 67-page
25   document that requires updates to remove outdated provisions. He cited alcohol licensing as an
26   example, noting that the City's proximity requirements for alcohol sales are already dictated by
27   state law and do not need to be duplicated in local code. The revision process is not intended to
28   change existing policy but rather to align the code with state regulations and remove
29   unnecessary provisions. Given the size of the document, he suggested two possible
30   approaches for review: forming a small committee of Councilmembers to examine sections in
31   detail before presenting updates to the full Council, or breaking the document into chapters and
32   reviewing them one at a time.
33
34   Councilmembers suggested bringing the business licensing code back in sections to be
35   reviewed over a few Council meetings.

 3            Resolution No. 25-08 – Resolution of Support for HB 81, Eliminating Salt Lake
 4            County Regulations to Fluoridate Drinking Water
 5
 6   Mayor Staggs shared that he had previously reached out regarding House Bill 81, which seeks
 7   to eliminate the mandate for fluoridation of culinary water systems. The bill has already passed
 8   out of committee with a favorable recommendation. He explained that Salt Lake County, along
 9   with Davis County, is one of the only counties in the state with this mandate. He emphasized
10   concerns about the questionable benefits of adding fluorosilic acid to the water supply. The
11   resolution supports the bill and expresses general approval of its current form. If the bill passes,
12   the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District, which supplies water to half of the City, would
13   stop adding fluoride to the water, reducing both the cost and obligation of doing so.
14
15   Mr. Carter highlighted a potential risk with fluoridation, referencing an incident in Sandy where a
16   power outage caused a pressurized system to release an unintended, concentrated dose of
17   fluorosilicic acid into the water supply. This imbalance resulted in fluoride reacting with metal
18   pipes, potentially leading to lead and copper contamination in the water. He pointed out that
19   such events can go unnoticed for an extended period, making it difficult for residents to
20   understand the cause of any issues until much later.
21
22   Councilmember Haymond MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 25-08 –
23   Resolution of Support for HB 81, Eliminating Salt Lake County Regulations to Fluoridate
24   Drinking Water. Councilmember Pierucci SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for
25   discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows:
26   Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed
27   unanimously.
