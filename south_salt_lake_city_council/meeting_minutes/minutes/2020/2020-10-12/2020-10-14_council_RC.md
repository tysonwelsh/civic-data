NEW BUSINESS

          1. Consideration of a Resolution of the City Council (The “City
             Council”) of the City of South Salt Lake, Utah (The “City”)
             Authorizing and Approving the Execution and Delivery of a Bond
             Purchase Agreement by The City of South Salt Lake Redevelopment
             Agency (the “Agency”) and a Second Amendment Interlocal
             Agreement between the City and the Agency, Both Pursuant to the
             Agency’s Issuance and Sale of its Excise Tax and Tax Increment
             Revenue Refunding Bonds, Series 2020 in an Aggregate Principal
             Amount not to Exceed $11,000,000, in one or more Series and with
             such additional or Alternate Designations as the Agency may
             Determine (the “Series 2020 Bonds”); and related matters. Economic
             Development Consultant, Randy Sant, explained that this is a pledge
             agreement where the City is pledging to the RDA certain excise tax and
             other fees for the payment of the bond that is being refunded. The pledge
             agreement says the City will pledge those funds to the RDA who will use
             it to make the payment. The City cannot pledge these funds for any other
             use until a bond test has been reached of two hundred percent coverage.
             This means for the next ten years these funds will most likely be pledge to
             this bond. The agreement says the RDA has to make the first pledge. To
             do this the RDA will use the tax increment generated from the Market
             Station Project Area. The balance will then come from this excise tax.

              City Attorney, Hannah Vickery, clarified that the tax increment financing
              (TIF) from the Market Station Area is pledged as the primary payment. So
              this agreement does allow and commits those certain excise taxes of the
              City to pay the debt in the event the TIF can’t cover the debt. There is no
              current TIF being collected in the area. It is anticipated at some point that
              will come on line. Once it does it is expected the RDA’s own TIF will be
              covering those debts and the City’s excise tax and other pledged
              obligations will only be used in the event of a deficit.

              Mr. Sant advised that they plan on triggering the Market Station tax
              increment next year.

          2. Fire Department Annual Report. Fire Chief, Terry Addison, reviewed
             the annual Fire Department report with the Council. A copy is attached
             and incorporated by this reference.

              Council Member Siwik asked how many of the increased calls for service
              can be traced to the Men’s Homeless Resource Center.

              Chief Addison said he believes it’s eight to eleven percent.

              Council Member deWolfe asked if the calls were trending to what was
              anticipated.

              Chief Addison said it is trending upward. They do spend a lot of time
              there. Staff is working diligently with members of the Road Home staff to
              decrease a lot of these calls.

              Council Member deWolfe asked if the Chief could summarize the
              activities along the Jordan River.

              Chief Addison said the Jordan River is an area where they constantly
              respond to grass fires. There has been an increase of human caused fires
              along there.

              Council Member deWolfe asked what the percentage of voluntary
              turnover is for the department.

              Chief Addison said turnover has basically gone down to zero.

              Council Member Siwik asked if there was any progress on making some
              memorial for Assistant Fire Chief Plant, who passed away in the line of
              duty in the 1950’s.

              Chief Addison said they have not made any progress on that because of
              the COVID outbreak and trying to keep firefighters safe. It has been
              postponed for the moment. However, Chief Plant has been recognized by
              the Utah State Firefighter Museum and at the National Fallen Firefighters
              Memorial in Emmitsburg, Maryland.

              Council Member Siwik would like to see something done for Chief Plant
              in the City.

              Council Member Pinkney asked if response time is four minutes anywhere
              in the City since most of the stations are on the east side.

              Chief Addison explained that they have a four minute response time in
              their districts from the time they go in route to the time they arrive.

              Council Member Mila asked if the new system they just put in could speed
              up response time.

              Chief Addison said it absolutely could. Each fire apparatus has a GPS
              locater on board so the system can locate the closest apparatus and send it.
              They have only had this system since September 29, 2020 and are still
              collecting data to evaluate how well it works.
