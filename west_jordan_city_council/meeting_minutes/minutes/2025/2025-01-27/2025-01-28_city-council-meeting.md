 6. PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Wasatch Electric MDA/P and Rezone
           • Ordinance No. 25-07 a Master Development Agreement/Plan for Wasatch
             Electric (approximately 54.199 acres at approximately 9000 South 6800 West)
           • Ordinance No. 25-08 a Rezone (approximately 54.199 acres) to Southwest
             Quadrant – Mixed Use Zone (SWQ-MU)
           Ben Seastrand with Gardner Group gave an overview of a Wasatch Electric project located
           at approximately 9000 South 6800 West. Responding to a question from Vice Chair
           Whitelock, Mr. Seastrand said ground would be broken on Wasatch Electric (Phase 1) in
           a few weeks, and Phase 2 (with residential) would likely break ground in 2026.
           Responding to a question from Council Member Green, Mr. Seastrand said 400 employees
           or more would work at the Wasatch Electric building when built. Mr. Seastrand said
           Wasatch Electric took a leap of faith in selecting the location since it would be the first
           building in the area. He said a rezone application for an additional approximately 900
           acres would soon be submitted to the City.

           Senior Planner Tayler Jensen said the subject property was located just off of the newly
           constructed 9000 South extension. He said the proposal complied with the Future Land
           Use Map (FLUM) for the Southwest Quadrant. Mr. Jensen said the area currently
           contained zones no longer used by the City, and rezoning would be necessary regardless
           of what happened on the property. He said the Southwest Quadrant–Mixed Use Zone
           (SWQ-MU) had subzones that would allow for tremendous flexibility to ensure all uses
           supported and worked together. The proposed rezone of approximately 54 acres would:
              • Primarily Industrial and Flex Commercial Office (HTML Subzone)
              • Facilitate the building of the headquarters for Wasatch Electric
              • Up to 164 dwelling units (COM U Subzone).

           Mr. Jensen said Exhibit B in the MDA would need to be replaced with Exhibit B included
           in the agenda packet.

           Council Member Bloom said it was her impression that staff had wanted to get away from
           piecemeal zoning. Mr. Jensen said staff would have liked to see the whole approximately
           1,000 acres come in for approval at the same time, but when Wasatch Electric committed
           to come to West Jordan, a time crunch was created that would not allow time to do a good

       b. Ordinance No. 25-01 Amending West Jordan City Code Section 8-14-7 Regarding
          Grave Decorations
          Public Services Director Cory Fralick presented a proposed Code revision for Section 8-
          14-7 regarding grave decorations to provide more time for holiday decorations, a broader
          list of recognized holidays, and allow for administrative handling of grave decorations, as
          previously requested by the Council.

           Council Member Shelton returned at 7:51 pm.



City Council Meeting Minutes                Tuesday, January 28, 2025                          Page 4
           Council Member Bloom said she wanted to make sure the recognized holidays were
           comprehensive enough to accommodate the cultural and religious practices of West
           Jordan’s diverse population, and asked how the City would handle requests for additional
           holiday recognition or accommodations. Mr. Fralick confirmed inclusion of State holidays,
           high-volume cemetery holidays, and flexibility to work with residents regarding special
           requests. Senior Assistant City Attorney Patrick Boice said flexibility for staff was the
           basis for the proposed Code amendments.

           Vice Chair Whitelock asked about the cleanup policy for holiday decorations. Mr. Fralick
           said the policy would allow holiday decorations up to three days before a holiday and for
           a week after, for a total of 10 days, with a few holiday exceptions. Vice Chair Whitelock
           suggested the length should be clearer in the policy. Mr. Boice said the draft he was
           looking at had different language from the draft included in the agenda packet, and said
           he would make sure the updated version with clearly stated length of time would be
           attached.

 7. BUSINESS ITEMS
       a. Resolution No. 25-006 authorizing the use of eminent domain to acquire property
          located at 8393 South Temple Drive, West Jordan, Utah
          Real Property Administrator David Clemence said the City had a project along 1300 West
          to widen the road, include bike lanes, curb and gutter, and ADA ramps. He said the City
          had worked closely with UDOT and the Federal Government to receive aid to make the
          project happen. Mr. Clemence said it was necessary to purchase strips of property from
          approximately 35 property owners between 7800 South and 9000 South. He said in the
          process of negotiating with property owners, staff had reached an impasse with the
          property owner of 8939 South Temple Drive. Mr. Clemence asked the Council to consider
          approving the use of eminent domain as an option. He said staff would continue talking

       b. Resolution No. 25-004 City Council to accept or deny the Copper Meadows Annexation
          Petition
          City Recorder Tangee Sloan presented a Copper Meadows Annexation Petition for review
          and consideration. She said the City Recorder’s Office received a Notice of Intent to file an
          annexation petition for 1.33 acres on December 16, 2024. She said required notices had
          been mailed, and the Planning Commission had recommended zoning and Land Use Map
          designations. Ms. Sloan said the Salt Lake County Clerk sent notices to land owners, and
          the Recorder’s Office provided the petition sponsor with a blank annexation petition with
          which to collect signatures. She said the petition sponsor officially filed the petition on
          January 16, 2025.

           MOTION: Council Member Shelton moved to APPROVE Resolution No. 25-004 City
                   Council to accept the Copper Meadows Annexation Petition.
                   Council Member Jacob seconded the motion.

           Vice Chair Whitelock explained that she voted against the development when it was first
           approved by the Council, but would vote in favor of the annexation since a majority of the
           Council had already approved the development.

           The vote was recorded as follows:
           YES:       Zach Jacob, Chad Lamb, Bob Bedore, Pamela Bloom, Kelvin Green, Kent
                      Shelton, Kayleen Whitelock
           NO:
           ABSENT:
