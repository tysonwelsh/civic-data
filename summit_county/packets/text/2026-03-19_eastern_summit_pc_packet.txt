Background 
In order to apply for development, a parcel must be a conforming parcel. Parcel NS-358 was 
verified as two lots of record on July 12, 2011 by the Summit County Community Development 
Department. The total acreage of both lots was identified as 48.83 acres, with 
acknowledgement that Chalk Creek Road divided the parcel and created two parcels. The parcel 
south of Chalk Creek Road is now identified as NS-358 and consists of 36.06 acres. 
 
Based on the AG-5 zone district, the applicants are eligible to divide the property into seven 
lots. This property contains a wellhead for Cluff Ward Water District and an associated 750-foot 
radius wellhead protection zone. The property also contains a wellhead for underground gas 
storage, as well as associated easements. 
 
Review Process 
This application is being processed according to 11-4-5 of the Code which regulates 
subdivisions. The recently adopted subdivision language states the following: 
 
• “The CDD or designated planning staff member shall conduct a review that includes all 
affected agencies and Service Providers no later than 15 business days after the day on 
which an Applicant submits a complete Subdivision Application. Upon completion of the 
Subdivision Review, the CDD or designated planning staff member shall prepare a report 
and make findings and recommendations and shall schedule a public hearing before the 
Planning Commission as soon thereafter as may be practicable.” 
 
• “Because of the limited time frame for preliminary review, compliance with standards 
may be written in the form of conditions of approval to be met during the Subdivision 
Plan Review part of the Final Subdivision plat.”  
 
o Staff has placed conditions of approval on items that are not yet completely 
vetted due to the limited timeframe, as identified in the Analysis and Findings 
section of this report.  
 
•  “...if a preliminary subdivision complies with the applicable standards and specifications, 
the Planning Commission shall approve the preliminary subdivision. After preliminary 
subdivision approval, the applicant may submit a final subdivision plat, to be reviewed 
and approved by the County Manager.” 
 
• “The Planning Commission may hold no more than one public hearing for the preliminary 
subdivision plat.”

Subdivision Review Standards 
The proposed subdivision shall comply with the following.  
 
Standard 1: The Subdivision is compliant with the Eastern Summit County General Plan.  
  
Analysis: The Eastern Summit County General Plan Section 2.1 Policy C states the 
following: “Work to ensure that single-family residential development minimizes 
disturbance and is clustered where appropriate”. The proposed subdivision is located 
near Coalville City and development will need to comply with the existing easements 
and setbacks. COMPLIES 
 
Standard 2: The Subdivision is designed to fit into the topography of the site. Lots shall be sited 
to best fit into the natural terrain, to minimize excessive site grading and to mitigate impacts on 
the natural environment and resources of the surrounding area. The subdivision layout shall 
demonstrate the preservation of watercourses, drainage areas, wooded areas, rough terrain 
and similar natural feature areas.  
 
Analysis: The proposed lots fit the topography and conform to the Cluff Ward pipeline 
easement. The southern portion of both lots contain the 750-foot radius Cluff Ward 
Water District wellhead protection zone as well as a natural gas pipeline and a portion 
of a Flood Zone surrounding Chalk Creek which runs through the southernmost portion 
of both lots. There may be wetlands on Lot 1 and Lot 2. Lot 2 contains a wellhead for 
underground gas storage. Planning Staff recommend plat notes be added regarding a 
wetland delineation study, regarding written permission from the easement holder of 
the underground gas storage, and regarding the 750-foot radius wellhead protection 
zone.  COMPLIES AS CONDITIONED 
 
Standard 3: The Subdivision considers adjacent land uses. Site design shall mitigate any 
potential adverse impacts including but not limited to flooding, erosion, subsidence, sloping of 
the soil or other dangers or nuisances.  
 
Analysis: The adjacent uses are agricultural, residential, and utility-related infrastructure 
of natural gas underground storage and pipelines and spring/well protection areas for

Findings of Fact: 
1. On January 22, 2026, the Summit County Community Development Department 
received a complete Preliminary Subdivision application for a two lot subdivision on 
Parcel NS-358, called Chappell Estates Subdivision.  
2. Parcel NS-358 is owned by Annette Moore and Frankie Donaldson.  
3. NS-358 is in the Agriculture-5 (AG-5) Zone.  
4. Parcel NS-358 is estimated to be 36.06 acres.

Community Development Department with a note that Chalk Creek Road divided the 
two. The parcel south of Chalk Creek Road is now identified as NS-358 and consists of

Parcel.

17.02 acres and Lot 2 is proposed as 19.04 acres.

with Chalk Creek Road meeting the infrastructure standards in Section 11-6-8.

Chapter 5: Economic Development and Agriculture 
 
The Economic Development Element outlines policies and strategies to support a resilient 
and sustainable economy in Summit County while maintaining the small-town  rural 
character, natural landscapes, and community values that deﬁne the area. Rather than 
pursuing large-scale growth, this element focuses on strengthening existing communities, 
supporting local businesses and agriculture, and identifying economic opportunities that 
are compatible with the region’s environment and lifestyle.  
 
Context 
 
For more than a century, Eastern Summit County's economy was shaped by ranching, 
mining, and timber production. Ranching, particularly sheep and cattle operations, remains 
an important part of the area’s cultural and economic heritage, while coal mining historically 
played a signiﬁcant role in the region's  development. Over time, however, these industries 
have declined, and the local economy has evolved.  
 
Today, Eastern Summit County  increasingly serves as a recreational gateway to the Unita 
Mountains and surrounding public lands. Outdoor recreation, tourism, and agriculture -
related activities contribute to the local economy and reﬂect the regions’ natural assets and 
rural traditions. These trends present opportunities to support new economic activity while 
maintaining the landscapes and community character that residents value.  
 
Economic activity in Eastern Summit County remains limited compared to other parts of 
Summit County. Many jobs, services, and commercial centers are in nearby municipalities 
or in the Snyderville Basin. Within the unincorporated county, small -scale commercial 
activity is generally concentrated around historic communities such as Wanship and Echo.  
 
Looking forward, economic development efforts in Eastern Summit County will focus on 
supporting existing communities, strengthening agricultural viability, and exploring  
compatible opportunities such as recreation- based businesses and agr itourism. Careful 
consideration of infrastructure, transportation, access, and environmental stewardship will 
be essential to ensure that future economic activity contributes positively to the long -term 
sustainability of the region.  
 
Existing Conditions 
 
Summit County currently supports approximately 35,801 jobs, including employees 
counted in the Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW), non- QCEW 
employees, and self -employed workers. When extended proprietors —individuals holding 
secondary jobs or s ide businesses —are included, the total number of jobs increases to 
21
approximately 53,547. This indicates that Summit County supports more jobs than resident 
workers, reﬂecting the county’s role as a regional employment center. 
 
According to projections from the Kem C. Gardner Policy Institute, Summit County is 
expected to add approximately 6,600 jobs over the next decade, or about 660 jobs per year. 
This growth will likely occur unevenly across the county, with most of the  employment 
continuing to concentrate in established commercial and employment centers. 
 
Eastern Summit County currently accounts for approximately X,XXX jobs. The largest 
employment sectors in the area include Accommodation and Food Services, Arts, 
Entertainment and Recreation, Construction, Retail Trade, and Government. These sectors 
reﬂect the area’s connection to recreation and tourism, as well as the presence of small -
scale service businesses that support local communities and visitors. 
 
Compared to other areas of Summit County, Eastern Summit County has a relatively limited 
commercial base and fewer concentrated employment centers. Many residents commute 
to jobs in nearby municipalities, the Snyderville Basin, or adjacent counties. Existi ng 
commercial activity in the unincorporated county tends to occur near historic communities 
and established transportation corridors, including areas such as Wanship and Echo. 
 
Community Engagement Feedback 
 
Community engagement conducted through the Our Summit Community Vision and 
Strategic Plan (202 4) highlighted several priorities related to economic development and 
long-term economic resilience. Residents, business owners, and community stakeholders 
expressed a strong interest in maintaining Summit County’s diverse economic base while 
identifying opportunities to support emerging industries and local employment. 
 
Protecting economic diversity, including sectors such as agriculture, education, healthcare, 
and recreation, was identiﬁed as an important goal. Community members also emphasized 
the importance of expanding employment opportunities while ensuring that economic 
development remains compatible with the county’s rural character and natural 
environment. 
 
Survey responses also highlighted a desire to focus on  new growth in strategic locations. 
Approximately 65% of respondents indicated that incentivizing redevelopment in areas near 
public facilities, transit, trails, and existing services would be an appropriate or very 
appropriate economic development strateg y. Similarly, 65% of respondents indicated that 
new housing should be located near existing commercial areas, while 40% supported the 
redevelopment of commercial areas to incorporate additional housing.

diversiﬁed, suggesting interest in exploring new economic opportunities that can strengthen 
the local economy while maintaining the community’s quality of life.

Chapter 6: Natural and Cultural Resources  
 
The Natural and Cultural Resources Element provides guidance for protecting and 
enhancing the natural landscapes, cultural heritage, and community identity that deﬁne 
Eastern Summit County. The policies and strategies in this chapter support the conservation 
of open space, protection of environmental resources, and recognition of the cultural history 
that has shaped the region. 
 
Eastern Summit County’s natural environment, including its agricultural lands, wildlife 
habitats, waterways, and scenic landscapes plays a central role in the area’s rural character 
and quality of life. This element seeks to ensure that future land use and development 
decisions are made with careful consideration of these resources. By encouraging 
thoughtful land use patterns and the conservation of important environmental features, the 
County can help maintain the landscapes and ecological systems that residents value. 
 
This element also recognizes the importance of preserving and celebrating the cultural 
history of Eastern Summit County. From ranching , mining, logging,  and railroad heritage to 
the longstanding presence of Native peoples, the region’s cultural resources contribute to a 
shared sense of place and identity. 
 
This chapter is separate from the Resource Management Plan, which is required under Utah 
State Code. Summit County’s Resource Management Plan addresses the management of 
natural resources on federally managed lands within the county, including lands 
administered by the Bureau of Land Management (BLM) and the U.S. Forest Service. 
The strategies in this chapter focus primarily on the protection of natural and cultural 
resources as they relate to land use and development within unincorporated Eastern 
Summit County, outside of federally managed public lands. 
 
Context 
 
Eastern Summit County serves as a gateway to several signiﬁcant natural and recreational 
areas, including Rockport State Park, Echo State Park, the Historic Union Paciﬁc Rail Trail 
State Park, and the Uinta- Wasatch-Cache National Forest. These protected lands, along 
with extensive agricultural open space, contribute to the region’s scenic landscapes and 
help maintain the rural character that residents value. 
 
Agricultural lands have historically played an important role in preserving open viewsheds, 
wetlands, and wildlife migration corridors throughout the region. As development pressures 
increase, careful planning will be necessary to ensure that these important environmental 
resources continue to be protected while accommodating appropriate growth. 
 
Community engagement efforts consistently highlight the strong connection residents feel 
to the natural environment. Open space, scenic landscapes, and access to outdoor

recreation are frequently cited as key contributors to quality of life in Eastern Summit 
County. 
 
The cultural history of the region is also closely tied to its natural landscape. Ranching, 
mining, and railroad development shaped many of the communities that exist today. 
Although relatively few historic structures remain, these industries continue to inﬂuence the 
identity and heritage of the area. 
 
Eastern Summit County also sits within lands historically used by Native peoples. While this 
history is an important part of the region’s cultural landscape, there are currently limited 
public opportunities for education, interpretation, or cultural recogn ition related to Native 
communities. Future efforts may provide opportunities to better acknowledge and share 
these histories. 
 
Finally, Eastern Summit County’s population has gradually become more diverse in recent 
years. Although the area remains predominantly white, the Latino population has grown and 
is an important part of the community. Ensuring that community events, planning processes, 
and cultural initiatives reﬂect and include the diversity of residents can help strengthen 
community identity and participation. 
 
Existing Conditions 
 
Eastern Summit County contains signiﬁcant natural and cultural resources that shape land 
use patterns and contribute to the area’s rural character and quality of life. Large portions of 
land within the planning area are protected through federal, state, and local conservation 
efforts. 
 
Federally managed lands account for approximately 44% of the land area within the planning 
district, while lands protected by the State of Utah account for approximately 2 %. These 
lands are generally managed for conservation, recreation, and resource protection 
purposes, which limits the potential for private development. 
 
Beyond federal and state ownership, approximately 9 % of land within Eastern Summit 
County is protected through conservation easements , many of which support ongoing 
agricultural operations or preserve open space and recreational landscapes. These

Community Engagement Feedback 
 
Community engagement conducted as part of the General Plan update highlighted strong 
public support for the protection of natural resources, open space, and agricultural lands as 
essential components of Eastern Summit County’s identity and quality of life. 
 
Water resources were identiﬁed as a primary concern, with 45% of survey respondents 
indicating that water availability and quality require the greatest attention to maintain long -
term community sustainability. This reﬂects the region’s reliance on healthy watersheds for 
agriculture, ecosystems, and residential needs. 
 
Residents also expressed strong support for the preservation of open space. Seventy -nine 
percent of respondents indicated that open spaces are important for maintaining ecosystem 
health, including the protection of native plants, wildlife habitat, and water resources. Many 
respondents noted that open space contributes not only to environmental quality but also 
to community character and recreation opportunities. 
 
29
Agricultural lands were widely recognized as a deﬁning feature of Eastern Summit County . 
Sixty-ﬁve percent of respondents indicated that agricultural lands should be considered an 
important form of open space and that agriculture remains a viable and valued industry. This 
feedback underscores the community’s desire to maintain working landscapes as part of 
the region’s rural identity. 
 
Community members also expressed strong place -based connections to speciﬁc open 
spaces. Fifty-nine percent of respondents indicated that their favorite open space is 
important for supporting plant and animal life, while 53% noted that proximity to their homes 
or neighborhoods contributes to its value.  These responses suggest that both ecological 
function and everyday accessibility shape public perceptions of open space. 
 
Finally, 63% of respondents indicated that the community has experienced noticeable 
growth and change in recent years.  This feedback reﬂects an awareness of evolving 
development pressures and reinforces the importance of balancing growth with resource 
protection and preservation of community character. 
 
Goals, Objectives, Policies & Strategies  
 
Eastern Summit County’s natural landscapes, agricultural lands, and cultural heritage are 
central to the community’s identity, quality of life, and long -term sustainability. As growth 
continues to occur within the region, careful stewardship of these resources will be essential 
to maintaining the rural character and ecological systems that residents value. By 
coordinating land use decisions with resource protection efforts, supporting working lands, 
and recognizing the cultural history that shapes the commu nity, Summit County can guide 
future development in a manner that preserves the deﬁning qualities of Eastern Summit 
County for current and future generations. 
 
GOAL: Preserve and enhance Eastern Summit County’s natural landscapes, agricultural 
heritage, open spaces, and cultural resources while supporting ecological resilience, rural 
character, and thoughtfully managed growth.

community identity.

Commission to identify, document, and preserve culturally signiﬁcant sites, 
structures, and landscapes. 
 
Strategy 6.1.1: Develop partnerships with local historic organizations, 
municipalities, and community groups to support preservation and 
interpretation of cultural resources.
