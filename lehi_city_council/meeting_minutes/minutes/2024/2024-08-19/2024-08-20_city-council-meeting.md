2. Regular Agenda
   2.1 Public Hearing to receive input with respect to the proposed Lehi City tax increase.

Dean Lundell, Finance Director, explained the concept of certified tax rates in Utah. The goal is
to ensure that cities receive the same revenue as the previous year, adjusting only for new
growth, in order to remove the impact of real estate appreciation/depreciation. Lundell noted that
the city's proposed tax rate would be considered a tax increase under state law although the rate
is lower than the previous year.

Mr. Lundell discussed how the relative value of properties, rather than just the absolute value,
impacted individual taxpayers. He provided an example of a property whose taxes had fluctuated
over the years, sometimes increasing faster than inflation and other times decreasing. Mr.
Lundell justified the proposed tax increase by stating it would primarily fund raises for police
and fire employees, as well as add a new parks maintenance worker.

Mr. Lundell compared Lehi's property tax rate to other cities in the area, showing Lehi was in the
middle range. He also presented data on the city's sales tax revenue growth, which had outpaced
inflation. Mr. Lundell acknowledged the challenges of balancing the needs of the community
with the burden on taxpayers, especially those on fixed incomes.

Mayor Johnson opened the public hearing.

The council members expressed a range of perspectives on the proposed tax increase. Councilor
Newall suggested that the city should do more to raise awareness about the residential tax
exemption available for those on fixed incomes, recognizing the strain that rising costs were
placing on some residents. Mayor Johnson acknowledged the difficult balancing act of meeting
the city's growing needs while minimizing the burden on taxpayers, noting the impact of state-
level decisions that had increased costs on cities. Councilor Stallings questioned whether the tax
increase was truly necessary, suggesting the city explore alternative funding sources and exercise
more fiscal restraint, particularly around large capital projects. Councilor Albrecht emphasized
the importance of retaining quality public safety personnel and maintaining sustainable city
services.

The proposed tax rate will be voted on at the August 27, 2024 City Council meeting.

3. Adjournment

    The motion passed unanimously. The meeting was adjourned at approximately 6:30 p.m.
