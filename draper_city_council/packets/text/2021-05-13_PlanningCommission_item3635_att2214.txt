BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Plat Amendment for two existing subdivisions
that total approximately 23.311 acres located on the south side of Pioneer Road, at
approximately 2108 E. Pioneer Road and 12352 S. Deer Shadow Cove (Exhibit B). The
larger subdivision Ironhorse is zoned RA1 and the smaller subdivision, Deer Run, is zoned
RA2. The applicant is requesting that a Plat Amendment be approved to reconfigure lot



Ironhorse Run Plat Amendment                                      App. # SUBD-1078-2020
Plat Amendment Request                        1
lines in each of the subdivisions and to also relocate the shared plat boundary between the
two plats.

The Ironhorse subdivision was approved by Draper City and recorded on February 3, 2006
(Exhibit E). The plat consisted of five lots. Four of the lots have access from Pioneer Road
and one lot has access from Highland Drive. The Deer Run subdivision was approved and
recorded on January 29, 2008 (Exhibit F). This plat also consists of five lots. All of these lots
are accessed from a private road called Deer Shadow Cove that connects to Pioneer Road
along the north side of the plat boundary.


ANALYSIS
General Plan and Zoning. The Land Use Map of the General Plan calls for the Residential
Low Medium Density for the subject properties (Exhibit C). That category is characterized
as follows:




Ironhorse Run Plat Amendment                                          App. # SUBD-1078-2020

The properties have been assigned the RA1 and RA2 zoning classifications, supporting
approximately one to two dwelling unit per acre. The purpose of both the RA1 and RA2
zone is to “foster low density development with little impact on its surroundings and municipal
services; to generally preserve the character of the city's semirural areas; and to promote and
preserve conditions favorable to large lot family life, including the keeping of limited numbers of
animals and fowl. The predominant use in these zones is intended to be detached single-family
dwellings, protected from encroachment by commercial and industrial uses.”

The existing subdivisions are surrounded by RA1, and RA2 zoning designations on the
north, south, and west sides and the A5 (Agricultural, 5 acre minimum) zoning designation
on the north and east side.

Subdivision Layout. Although this is a single Plat Amendment application request, there are
two plats that will be amended as part of this request. Therefore, each of the subdivisions
will be discussed separately in order to provide a clear view of that request.

Ironhorse
The existing subdivision consists of five lots (Exhibit E). This request will reconfigure the lot
lines between Lots 3 and 4 in Ironhorse and adjust the plat boundary with Lot 4 in
Ironhorse and Lot 1 in Deer Run. When Lot 4 was developed, a portion of a basketball
court and its surrounding landscaping was built on Lot 3. The two property owners would
like to adjust the shared lot line so that the basketball court and associated landscaping is
all located on Lot 4. Both Lots 3 and 4 will continue to meet all the minimum size and
dimension requirements for the RA1 zoning district. There is also a joint access on the
original Ironhorse Plat along the driveway area that is no longer needed. That joint access
will be vacated as part of the plat as Deer Run was developed with its own private road and
will not have access through Ironhorse.

The boundary between Ironhorse and Deer Run is requested to be adjusted. A small
portion of the eastern portion of Lot 4 is what is proposed to be made part of the Deer Run
plat. The overall square footage transferred is minimal but will help the owner of Lot 1 in
Deer Run to complete improvements to his property and meet all zoning standards.

Deer Run
The existing Deer Run plat is similar to the Ironhorse in that contains five lots. A private
road that connects to Pioneer Road provides access for all the lots and the road is
commonly owned by all the properties in Deer Run. The original request for this
application was to complete the items described above for Ironhorse, the lot line
adjustment and subdivision boundary adjustments. However, in reviewing the application,
staff found that some of the lot lines within Deer Run had been adjusted with no City
approvals. Staff asked that both plats be made part of the application so that boundary
between the two subdivisions could be adjusted and the changes on Deer Run approved by
the City. Submitting two separate plats was determined to be the easiest way to
accomplish this due to the private road in Deer Run. The applicant agreed that this was the
best course of action.

In January of 2017, a real estate purchase agreement transferred and reconfigured some of
the lots in Deer Run. As noted, this was not done with City approvals and was discovered
during the review of this application. The proposed adjustments to the lots in Deer Run do
comply with all minimum standards for the zoning district and the recordation of this
updated plat will create parcels adjusted with City approvals.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
Staff finds that the application complies with the DCMC and recommends that the Planning
Commission review the request, receive public comment, and approve the application
based on the findings listed below and the criteria for approval, as listed within the staff
report.

If the Planning Commission decides to approve the request, staff recommends they include
the following conditions of approval:

1.      That all requirements of the Draper City Engineering, Public Works, Building,
        Planning, and Fire Divisions are satisfied throughout the development of the site
        and the construction of all buildings on the site, including permitting.
2.      Clarify the 20’ Access Vacation labeled on the plat is a private access vacation.
3.      If the plat amendment is not recorded within one (1) year from the date of approval
        or construction of the required landscaping and infrastructure has not commenced,
        such approval shall be null and void. This time period may be extended by the
        Planning Commission for up to one additional six (6) month period for good cause
        shown.
