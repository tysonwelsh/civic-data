PUBLIC COMMENT PERIOD
Arianna, a student from Whittier Elementary, addressed the Mayor and City Council on
behalf of the Whittier Falcons. She expressed appreciation for the opportunity to speak
again and reiterated a previous request for the honorary naming of the street in front of
their school as "Whittier Way." She shared the school’s rich 140-year history of community
involvement, highlighting past contributions such as community members providing water,
food, and transportation during snowstorms, and sharing a church building when a
schoolhouse was unavailable. Arianna emphasized that while community involvement
looks different today, it remains strong. Current examples included collaborative efforts to
build a life-size Rube Goldberg machine, food drives, Pinewood Derby races, STEAM
events, and literacy and math nights. Arianna noted that while many high schools have
honorary street names, Whittier would be the first elementary school in the Granite District
to receive such a designation. She emphasized the importance of recognizing the collective
achievements of the school and its community, regardless of student age. The Council was
invited to attend Whittier’s 140th birthday celebration, scheduled for May 15, 2025, from
6:00 to 8:00 PM. The students hoped to unveil the commemorative sign during the event
but extended the invitation regardless of the Council’s decision on the naming request.
Arianna concluded by thanking the Council for the opportunity to participate in civic
engagement and shared how much the students had learned from the experience.

Mike Markham expressed ongoing concerns regarding code enforcement in his
neighborhood. He referenced a nearby property, one house away from his own, where a
severely damaged Hyundai vehicle had been left in the front yard for an extended period.
Despite his initial report to Code Enforcement lacking a specific address, the officer
searched the area but did not recognize the extent of the damage due to limited visibility
from the street. Eventually, Code Enforcement contacted the residents and the car was
covered with a car cover, though it remained in the front yard on gravel. Mr. Markham

RESOLUTION 25-58: ADD THE COMMEMORATIVE STREET NAME
“WHITTIER WAY” TO 6000 WEST IN WEST VALLEY CITY
Mayor Lang presented proposed resolution 25-58 that would add the Commemorative Street
Name “Whittier Way” to 6000 West in West Valley City.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Whittier Elementary School was established in 1882 with eight students. For over
       one hundred years, the school has served the youth of the City and the community
       at large. Whittier Elementary School is a center for community activities, youth
       programming, and service projects. The City desires to recognize Whittier
       Elementary School’s essential role in the City and offer a tangible reminder of the
       legacy of its students and staff.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

       Councilmember Huynh moved to approve Resolution 25-58.

       Councilmember Whetstone seconded the motion.

RESOLUTION 25-59: APPROVE THE DEDICATION AND RENAMING OF THE
JILL ROBINSON MEMORIAL BUILDING
Mayor Lang presented proposed resolution 25-59 that would approve the Dedication and
Renaming of the Jill Robinson Memorial Building.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       The Code Enforcement Department proposes naming the Code Enforcement
       building at 4522 W 3500 S, West Valley City, UT 84120, in honor of the late
       Officer Jill Robinson.

       A dedication ceremony will be held to recognize her contributions, with her family
       in attendance. The event will include the unveiling of new building signage,
       renaming it the “Jill Robinson Memorial” building, along with a commemorative
       plaque featuring her photo and a brief biography.

       On August 9, 2018, Jill Robinson, a dedicated code enforcement officer with over
       a decade of service in West Valley City, Utah, was tragically killed in the line of
       duty. While performing a routine follow-up at a residence, she was fatally shot by
       the homeowner, Kevin Wayne Billings, who was reportedly upset over code
       violations cited on his property. Following the shooting, Billings set fire to
       Robinson's city vehicle and his neighbor's house.

       In February 2019, Billings pleaded guilty to aggravated murder, aggravated arson,
       and other charges. He was subsequently sentenced to life in prison without the
       possibility of parole.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.
