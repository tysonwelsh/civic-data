PC 26-008 Old Ephraim Apartments Resubmittal 4 [Design Review] Lance Anderson/GBN
Enterprises LLC is requesting a design review permit to construct ten, four-level townhome units with
six single occupancy bedrooms for a total of 60 occupants and 103 shared parking stalls (93 on site
and 10 off-site), located at approximately 655 East 400 North in the Campus Residential (CR) Zone;
TIN 06-058-0005; -0006; -0007, in the Adams Neighborhood.

Staff: Aimee Egbert, Planner, reviewed the request for a Design Review Permit to construct ten new
four-level townhome units at 655 East 400 North in the Campus Residential (CR) Zone. The project
includes demolition of one existing residential structure at 643 East 400 North and a previously
demolished structure at 655 East. The existing Campus Academy Youth Treatment Center at 675
East and the two existing student housing buildings at 645 % East will remain. The ten proposed
townhomes will be constructed along the 400 North frontage. The proposal also includes 93 on-site
parking stalls, and an alternative parking plan providing 10 off-site stalls.

The Land Development Code (LDC) requires a minimum 25-foot front building setback. The proposed
setback of 23 feet does not meet the requirement.


The frontage design includes an 8-foot sidewalk easement adjacent to 400 North, which would result
in a portion of the sidewalk being located on the applicant’s property. A public access easement will
be required to accommodate this configuration.

The LDC requires landscaping and useable open space totaling 30% of this site area. The proposal
provides approximately 27%.

Staff noted that the Planning Commission previously approved this project with a reduced front
setback of 23 feet and a landscaping /open space reduction to 27%.

Proponent: Lance Anderson stated that the project can accomodate either a seven or eight foot
sidewalk. If an eight foot sidewealk is provided, approximately one foot would be located on private
property. The applicant requested a reduced front setback of 22 % feet.

Mr. Anderson also noted uncertainty regarding direct access from the proposed alternate parking
area to the site but stated that an existing easement remains in place for previously approved off-site
parking located within 600 feet of the property.

Public: Jeannie Simmonds requested that the sidewalk along 400 North be at least eight feet wide.

David Bradford expressed concerns regarding the City’s prior approval of the home demolition,
stating the lot has remained vacant and overgrown for several years. Mr. Bradford also referenced a
previous four-story campus housing proposal he submitted that was not approved.

Commission: Russ Holley, Community Development Director, addressed public comments and
clarified that the LDC requires a 25-foot front setback, with the Planning Commission authorized to
grant up to a 10% reduction, as well as a 35-foot height limitation. R. Holley further explained that
property owners have the right to demolish structures, though vacant lots do remain subject to
property maintenance standards.

The Commission discussed the setback, landscaping, and off-site parking and determined to allow a
reduced front setback of 22 % feet and allow the landscaping and useable outdoor space to be at
27%. The Commission also added a condition requiring any off-site parking to be located within a
600-foot walking radius of the property.

PC 26-010 Island Market Expansion [Conditional Use & Design Review] Christopher Sands/island
Investment Properties LLC, authorized agent/owner is requesting a conditional use and design
review permit to renovate and expand the existing 3,193 square foot building to the east by
approximately 491 square feet, expand the existing parking lot to include 5 additional parking stalls,
add a dumpster enclosure, and landscaping located at 400 East Center Street in the Neighborhood
Center (NC) Zone; TIN 06-086-0001; -0002, in the Wilson Neighborhood.

Staff: Russ Holley, Community Development Director, reviewed the request for a Design Review
and Conditional Use Permit to renovate and expand the existing Island Market. The primary purpose
of the expansion is to accommodate a new ADA-compliant restroom and food preparation area along
the east side of the building. The proposal includes front fagade and roofline improvements, site
upgrades, landscaping, and parking enhancements.

A Conditional Use Permit is required in this zone when a structure exceeds 3,000 square feet and
when a legally existing non-conforming structure is proposed for expansion or enlargement. The
Land Development Code (LDC) states that a non-conforming structure may be expanded at the
discretion of the Planning Commission if findings demonstrate that the building remains compatible
with surrounding properties, the degree of nonconformity is not increased, and the expansion will not
create a detrimental impact on public infrastructure.

The proposed addition represents approximately a 15% increase in the total square footage and is
anticipated to have minimal impact on surrounding properties or neighborhood character. Site
improvements include elimination of the driveway on the east side of the property and installation of
additional landscaping. These changes are expected to reduce traffic noise and provide improved
buffering for the adjacent property to the east.

The building currently sits at a zero setback. The proposed expansion is not expected to further
impact or create a detrimental effect on public infrastructure. Additionally, the reduction of the roofline
overhang will remove encroachment into the public right-of -way and improve vertical clearance.

The proposal does not increase the degree of nonconformity, as it maintains the same north setback
as the existing structure.

The LDC requires current setback conformance, however, achieving full compliance would require a
10-foot jog in the building wall for only a small portion of the addition, which would not meaningfully
address the larger issue of the existing structure's zero setback. The Planning Commission will need
to determine the appropriate north wall setback and define the boundaries of the Conditional Use
Permit.

R. Holley noted minor typographical errors in the water and engineering conditions and referenced
Exhibit A to correct and replace those conditions.

Proponent: Christopher Sands stated that the intent is to improve overall compliance and reduce
the building's degree of nonconformity.

Christian Wilson, project architect, explained that the existing overhang will be removed to clean up
the fagade and restore the building to a more original and streamlined appearance.

PC 26-011 Public Works Campus Phase 1 [Design Review] Kevin Blalock/Logan City, authorized

agent/owner is requesting a design review permit to construct phase one of the new Public Works
Campus which consists of an approximate 20,000 square foot Fleet Maintenance Building, a 13,700


square foot Parks and Recreation Vehicle Storage Building, a 4,300 square foot vehicle wash
building, a fueling island, and covered parking located at approximately 950 West 600 North in the
Public (PUB) Zone; TIN 05-062-0056; -0017; -0004, in the Ellis Neighborhood.

Staff: Aaron Smith, Senior Planner, reviewed the request for a Design Review Permit. This proposal
represents Phase One of a long-term expansion and redevelopment of the existing Public Works
Campus, which currently includes the Service Center (fleet maintenance and Parks and Recreation
operations), the Public Works Street Division, and the Light and Power building.

The overall campus encompasses approximately 43 acres, with Phase One impacting approximately
4.9 acres. The site layout and design are intended to integrate with the anticipated Phase Two
development to the west, which will redevelop the existing Service Center site. Phase One primarily
provides vehicle and equipment servicing and storage facilities.

Pedestrian Connections must be provided throughout the site to allow safe circulation between all
streets and internal pathways and to ensure connectivity between future phases.

The Land Development Code (LDC) specifies maximum driveway width of 52 feet, minimum driveway
spacing of 200 feet from another driveway or street intersection, and one driveway per 300 feet of
street frontage. There are Three driveways are proposed along 800 W. Two exceed the maximum
permitted width, and all three are located closer than the required 200-foot separation.

The LDC allows these standards to be modified with approval from the Director of Public Works.
However, because the Director is also the project applicant, this presents a potential conflict of
interest. The Commission should determine whether an independent third-party review is appropriate
for the requested modifications.

While the Public zone does not specify minimum requirements for usable outdoor space or
landscaping, public developments that include shops and storage areas are not exempt from
standards. Staff applied the Industrial zone standard of 10% useable outdoor space and 10%
landscaping. The proposed plan includes approximately 26,000 square feet (12%) of potential
landscape area, primarily around the northern parking lots and within islands along 800 West. For
the 4.9 acres Phase One site, this equates to approximately 98 trees and 245 shrubs, flowers and
ornamental grasses.

Usable outdoor space should be proportional across phases. Greater opportunities for such spaces
are anticipated in future phases near planned office buildings. In lieu of providing the full useable
outdoor space during this phase, the applicant may submit a comprehensive development plan
demonstration overall site compliance with LDC standards.

The north wall of the vehicle wash building requires additional wall articulation or breaks. The west
wall of the Parks and Recreation Storage Building requires a second wall-break treatment.

The three-sided covered parking and storage area located south of the Parks & Recreation building
is proposed at an 8-foot setback and does not meet the minimum required 20-foot side setback.

Proponent: Paul Lindhart, Public Works Director, explained that phase two will begin as soon as
possible. And requested a 10% reduction in the side setback for the 3-sided covered storage to 18
feet. He also said he will be reviewing the driveway to reduce and eliminate some to get closer to
compliance and similar to other existing driveways along 800 West. P. Lindhardt spoke about a plaza
area that is planned for Phase Two that will be located near the administration building.

be acceptable.
