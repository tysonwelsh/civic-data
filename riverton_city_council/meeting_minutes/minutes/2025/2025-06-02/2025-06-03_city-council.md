Citizen Comment

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;

Chris Harding, Salt Lake County Auditor, explained an upcoming change to the County’s
property tax Notice of Valuation process. He shared that each property owner will receive a
notice in July, which will now feature a QR code linking to an enhanced electronic version of the
notice. This digital version will allow municipalities to add direct links explaining proposed tax
increases, giving residents clearer information about why increases are being proposed and
what they will fund. Additional improvements include an add-to-calendar feature for Truth in
Taxation hearings and a “pizza tracker”-style system for those appealing property valuations,
allowing them to receive real-time updates on the status of their appeal.

Mayor/Council Reports

Councilmember Haymond reported on attending the recent Wasatch Front Regional Council
(WFRC) meeting and explained that a key point of discussion was the push to expand light rail
into the southwest part of the Salt Lake Valley. He expressed some skepticism about the
emphasis on rail, citing concerns over high capital costs and what he referred to as the "heavily
subsidized" nature of such transit projects.

Councilmember Buroker thanked veterans in recognition of Memorial Day, and said the
Memorial Day program was one of the best she’s attended. She reminded both
Councilmembers and residents about the recent fire in Daybreak caused by overcharged lithium
batteries, such as those found in scooters. She encouraged the public to watch Unified Fire’s
YouTube safety videos to learn more about preventing such hazards.

Councilmember McCay shared appreciation for the Memorial Day program and reported on the
Wasatch Front Regional Council meeting, where discussions centered on extending transit
service from Daybreak to The Point. She also attended the Tee Box Golf ribbon cutting, a Utah
Faith Leadership meeting, and a UTA event where she drove an electric bus and praised its
maneuverability. She expressed support for UTA’s ongoing efforts to expand Riverton's transit
options and accommodate future growth.

Councilmember Pierucci reported attending the Wasatch Front Regional Council’s transit
meeting and emphasized the importance of ensuring Riverton receives adequate transit
services. He highlighted the need to prioritize Mountain View Village in future transit planning
due to its critical role in Riverton’s economic health. He praised the Memorial Day program and
also raised concerns about the ongoing impact of the Bangerter project on local businesses.

Councilmember McDougal reported that there will soon be a proposed agreement for Herriman
City to join the Trans-Jordan board, which manages sanitation services. With a new transfer
station being built and associated costs expected to rise, Herriman’s buy-in would provide
much-needed funds during a time of low cash reserves and help reduce future financial impacts
on residents.

Mayor Staggs noted that with Herriman potentially joining the Trans-Jordan board, the City’s
equity percentage—currently around 17–18%—will shift. He expressed appreciation for
veterans and American Legion Post 140 for their annual Memorial Day event, where he was
honored to speak. He reminded the public that the Council is in the final stages of approving the
FY 2025–26 budget, totaling about $71 million. He highlighted Riverton’s economic growth,
especially from increased sales tax revenue, and emphasized the City's ability to maintain low
taxes and utility fees while delivering essential services. He also announced he will not seek re-
election after 12 years of service.

Public Hearing(s)/Action Items

         Ordinance No. 25-18 – “Priority Builders Rezone,” General Plan Amendment and
         Rezone of approximately 4.28-acres located near the northwest corner of 11800
         South and Redwood Road. Changing the General Plan Designation to High
         Density Residential and the zoning to RM-8, a multi-family residential zone up to
         8 dwelling units per acre

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the applicant is requesting a
rezone and general plan amendment for approximately 4.25 acres located at the northwest
corner of 11800 South and Redwood Road. The site currently includes a mix of zoning: a
commercially zoned parcel that has not been developed, a half-acre residential (RR-22) zone,
and a recently rezoned R-4 parcel. Surrounding areas include RM-8 multifamily to the north and
west, and various residential zones to the south and east. The proposal is to rezone the entire
area to RM-8, with an SD (Special District) overlay to limit the development to townhomes only.
This meeting addressed zoning only—specific details like access, fencing, and layout would be
considered during a future site plan process. The Planning Commission recommended approval
with the townhome limitation, although the City code does not define the difference between
condos and townhomes. Staff supports the recommendation, but suggested additional
clarification from the applicant on how the townhome-only requirement would be interpreted.

Scott Yermish, Priority Builders, explained that the proposed project would be similar in style

and quality to Riverton Peaks, with slightly larger and upgraded units in three- and four-plex
configurations. Regarding the townhome-only condition, he stated he was comfortable with it if it
aligns with the legal designation used in Riverton Peaks, and clarified he is not proposing multi-
story condo buildings. He also acknowledged prior concerns about flooding related to Midas
Creek and committed to working with city engineers to address drainage issues.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;

Robert Baird expressed support for the rezone.

Peter Fanaro expressed support for the rezone.

Virginia Baird expressed support for the rezone.

Shelly Sieverts urged the Council to consider limiting the density of the project, noting that traffic
on 11800 South is already dangerous with frequent accidents and near-misses, even before
recent construction. She shared concerns about increased access through her side of the
community, which includes farmland and livestock, and recounted past issues with trespassing
and safety, particularly involving Midas Creek.

Luke Sieverts also urged the City to maintain reasonable density levels in line with the Planning
Commission’s recommendations. He noted that past developments have already altered FEMA
floodplain maps, bringing the flood zone dangerously close to his property, which could have
triggered flood insurance requirements.

Noah Dixon expressed concern that increasing density would negatively impact residents’ daily
lives through traffic, trespassing, theft, floodplain changes, and general overcrowding. He asked
the Council to carefully weigh how such changes would affect neighboring property owners and
urged them to maintain the current R-22 zoning.

Carol Sieverts said her primary concern was that the new project could restrict or block access
to her street, making it difficult for residents and emergency services like fire trucks or
ambulances to enter. She urged the Council to ensure that any new exits or road changes do
not interfere with access to her neighborhood.

Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
Hearing.

Mr. Yermish explained that, as part of the development process, an independent traffic impact
study would be conducted to assess how the proposed project would affect local traffic. This
study would include estimates of additional vehicles and recommend improvements, with results
reviewed by entities such as UDOT if necessary. He emphasized that his team is aware of the
community’s concerns and aims to be collaborative and said he is committed to addressing
traffic and other impacts responsibly and will take all feedback into account.

Councilmember Haymond expressed appreciation for the community’s understanding that
change is inevitable, even when it’s difficult. The main concern raised was the impact of any
development near Midas Creek on FEMA flood maps. He stressed the importance of
coordinating closely with the appropriate agencies to ensure that changes to the area do not
negatively affect downstream property owners—specifically, that no development should result
in others being required to purchase flood insurance due to altered floodplain boundaries.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, clarified that Salt Lake County—not FEMA—is responsible for
regulating discharge rates into Midas Creek and Rose Creek. He explained that, due to the
creeks being near capacity during a 100-year storm event, the County reduced the allowable
discharge rate from 0.2 to 0.02 cubic feet per second within the last five or six years. This
change means modern developments, including the proposed project, are now required to
retain stormwater on-site rather than simply detain it, significantly reducing the potential impact
on downstream properties.

Councilmember Buroker expressed appreciation for the Seivert family's long history in Riverton
and acknowledged past efforts to address Midas Creek flood concerns. She stated her support
for the rezone, citing traffic safety on 11800 South, where multiple driveways already contribute
to congestion, and noted that consolidating access points could improve safety.

Councilmember Pierucci emphasized the importance of understanding stormwater impacts. Mr.
Lethbridge explained that discharge rates into the creek have been significantly reduced, and
stormwater management will be a major component of the site plan review, ensuring any runoff
is appropriately routed and does not adversely affect downstream properties.

Councilmember McDougal emphasized that the vote was solely on zoning and that much work
remains before development begins. While initially concerned about density, he appreciated the
developer’s willingness to reduce it and work with the City and Planning Commission. He
supported the development because it would create a more cohesive, managed area instead of
a patchwork of inconsistent growth. He concluded that the proposal will enhance Riverton’s
entrance, address current issues like trespassing, and ultimately create a well-integrated, safe,
and attractive community.

Councilmember McCay asked the developer to work with the adjacent HOA to gain access from
the west. She complimented the appearance of the existing townhomes in the area and voiced
support for the proposed development.
