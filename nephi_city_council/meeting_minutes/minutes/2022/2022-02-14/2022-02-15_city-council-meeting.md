PUBLIC HEARING: SALES TAX BOND 2022-FUNDING FOR RECREATION FACILITY

  Mayor Justin Seely opened the public hearing and explained that the purpose of the hearing was to
receive public comment on the sales tax bond being issued to pay for construction costs related to “The
Hive” recreation and school facility. The bond is anticipated to be approximately $3.2 million. This will
consist of the original agreed contribution from the City, gap funding to complete the project, and an
amount designated for the purchase of the neighboring Bishop’s Storehouse building. This project is a
joint venture between private donors, the Juab School District, and Nephi City. The Juab School District
has agreed to pay the portion of annual debt service associated with the purchase of the Bishop’s
Storehouse. The remaining debt service will be the responsibility of Nephi City. It is anticipated that the
city will use revenue generated from The Hive, sales taxes, transient room taxes, PAR taxes, and parks
and recreation impact fees to help pay the annual debt service.

No Public Comment was given.
Hearing Closed at 7:05 p.m.

AIRPORT RESCYE GRANT AGREEMENT APPROVED:

  City Recorder Lisa Brough explained to the council that the federal government has enacted the
American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) to help provide funding for municipalities and other entities to recover
from the recent pandemic. Some of those funds were specific to airports. The city recently was sent an
opportunity to apply for an ARPA grant to assist our local airport with projects.
   The grant was signed by the Mayor and needs ratification by the City Council. It provides funding of
$32,000 for operational, personnel, cleaning, sanitization, janitorial services, and anything else that can
combat the spread of pathogens at the airport. The city’s fuel pumps are needing some upgrades and
these funds can be used for that purpose. In addition, the city is trying to bring broadband access to the
airport and would be eligible for these funds.
   Councilor Memmott moved to ratify the Airport Rescue Grant Offer Agreement. Councilor Parady
seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

MALCOLM BUCKLEY DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT APPROVED:

   City Planner Shauna Talbot explained to the council that the city had been approached by Malcolm
Buckley, who would like to construct a home on a portion of a large lot at the edge of the city’s block
system. The lot is large enough to subdivide into 3 lots and therefore does not fall into the city’s
exemptions for the subdivision process. Mr. Buckley would like to develop on about a third of the
property. If the property is not subdivided, the policy of the city has been to require frontage
improvements along the entire parcel. This parcel is a corner lot and if the city’s policy is carried out,
there would be approximately 360 feet of frontage improvements to complete, including street, curb,
gutter, and sidewalk. The development proposed would only require 76’ of frontage improvements.
The frontage that would be developed would be along 700 South. The city does not necessarily need
the frontage improved on 200 West at this time since there is a home that is an obstacle for 200 West
continuing southward.
   To assist this individual, while still adhering to city policy, staff recommends entering into an
agreement with Mr. Buckley that would allow for 76’ of frontage improvements but not allow any other
primary or exterior dwelling units to be allowed on the rest of the parcel without the subdivision process
or another development agreement for the remaining frontage improvements.
   Ms. Talbot explained that this situation is unique and dwindling as the block system continues to be
built out. From a staff perspective, this development agreement seems like the best way to ensure the
city’s policy is carried out while recognizing that an individual home builder may not have the resources
to subdivide a larger lot.
   The council asked several clarifying questions about the subdivision rules. Councilor Memmott
moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the development agreement with Mr. Malcolm Buckley and to
have it recorded and attached to the property deed. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion
passed on a unanimous vote.
