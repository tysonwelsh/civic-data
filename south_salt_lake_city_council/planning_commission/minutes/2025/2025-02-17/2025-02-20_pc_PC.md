       An Application for Preliminary Subdivision Plat for a One-Lot Plat, Located at 3303
       South 460 East.
       ACTION ITEM
       Applicant: Avenue Consultants
       Address:     3303 South 460 East

City Planner, Jed Shum, reported that the purpose of the Preliminary Subdivision Plat was to
consolidate two lots into one. The parcels are zoned R-1 Single-Family Residential. The minimum
square footage for new lots in this zone is 6,000 square feet, and consolidating the lots will create
a developable parcel. There is currently no proposed development. The plat shows a road
dedication to meet engineering standards, as well as suitable easements, and the lot will front 460
East. Photographs of the existing conditions were reviewed, indicating that the lots are currently
vacant. The American Land Title Association (“ALTA” survey showed no outstanding matters.

Staff deemed that the application met Land Use and Neighborhoods Goal 5: Enhance the livability
of existing and planned residential neighborhoods as it would reduce the number of nonconforming
lots in the zone and create a developable lot. Based upon the information submitted in the
application, supporting documentation, and analysis of the relevant Code sections and General
Plan goals, Staff recommended the Planning Commission move to approve the Preliminary
Subdivision Plat to plat the two parcels into one lot located at 3303 South 460 East, based on the
Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of Approval for the subdivision as listed in
the Staff Report.

Commissioner Southey inquired as to the possibility of a future request to rezone the property and
noted that it is strange to have R-1 zoning on a busy road. Mr. Shum reported that his understanding
is that there are currently no plans to change the zoning.

Jeremiah Larson spoke on behalf of the Applicant. They are requesting to consolidate the parcels
to meet zoning standards for a usable lot. They previously desired to build a home on the site, but
it did not conform to the required standards, so they were moving to consolidate the lots to meet
standards.

Chair Carter opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
closed.

Commissioner Southey stated that the parcels are in her neighborhood, and she is happy to see
something happening to the vacant lot.

2.       PUBLIC HEARING
         A Petition to the Planning Commission to Forward a Positive Recommendation to the
         South Salt Lake City Council for an Ordinance Amendment to Update Regulations
         Related to STRs, including updating the Land Use Matrix. It is proposed that the
         Ordinance will Require all STRs to be Owner Occupied or to be Owned by a South
         Salt Lake City Resident. This will Apply in all Zones Except for the Downtown
         District. It is anticipated that STRs will be Limited to Only Residential Districts.
         ACTION ITEM
         Applicant: South Salt Lake City



Community and Economic Development Director, Jonathan Weidenhamer, presented the Staff
Report and provided background on the potential Ordinance to further regulate STRs (“STR”).
The amendment would also include cleaning up clarifying language in other sections of Code. He
noted that the City appreciates the value and benefits that STRs bring to the community and the
investment people have made in the community. However, this topic has been front-of-mind for
the Mayor and City Council for the past two years due to the impacts on neighborhoods and
residents’ quality of life-based on poorly regulated or managed STRs. Mr. Weidenhamer clarified
that many hosts are doing a good job and only a handful are poorly behaved, but they leave a
terrible impact, and his primary role is to protect the residents of South Salt Lake City and their
quality of life and build community.

Three City Council Work Meetings were held to discuss this topic over a period of approximately
18 months, and in each session, the amendment got more granular and concise. Staff received very
specific and consistent feedback from existing licensed hosts asking for more balance, and their
requests were incorporated into the following alternatives:

     •   Posting requirements could be changed to allay safety concerns. Signage could be placed
         in a less conspicuous location, such as the front door or side of the home.

     •   Anyone with an existing license would be grandfathered. As previously written, hosts
         living outside the City would be allowed to continue operating their STRs in 2025, but
         beginning in 2026 the home would have to be owner-occupied to continue that use. The
         alternative language would allow existing licensed hosts to continue the use.

Mr. Weidenhamer reported that discussions on restrictions began after a shooting on Penney
Avenue during a large party at a STR. There were numerous noise complaints regarding STRs, but
Staff struggled to quantify them because they did not have the required software or communication
between the Police and Community Development departments. Investments were made to update
software and systems, and there is now mandatory reporting for noise complaints. He also works
closely with Code Enforcement, which receives regular complaints regarding the impacts of STRs.

The Ordinance was drafted at the City Council’s direction, and Mr. Weidenhamer indicated that
all written communication regarding the amendment was included in the Staff Report. Notice of
the public meeting was sent on January 3, 2025, and the Staff Report was posted on February 14,

anyone who is currently operating a nightly rental to enjoy that right under their business license

Mr. Weidenhamer reported that the Council discussed whether the requirement should be to host
live in Salt Lake County or South Salt Lake City, as well as cohosting where a property manager
or agent would need to live within 10 to 30 minutes of the property. He then presented the Zoning
Map and noted that the R-1 District is primarily in the eastern part of the City, and approximately
one-third of residents live in that zone. There is also a small R-1 District on 3300 at the Old Granite
High School. The residency and owner-occupancy requirements will apply primarily to the R-1
districts.

The recommendations presented to the Planning Commission were based on the Council’s
feedback on the following topics:

       1.      Allow STRs in the Downtown and Residential Multiple Districts, as
               commercial and hospitality uses align with that zoning.

       2.      Allow STRs in R-1 Districts if owner-occupied or the owner lives in South Salt
               Lake.
               Millcreek and Sandy are much more restrictive and have decided that only owner-
               occupied STRs are allowed, but the Council felt that limiting STRs to residents was
               much more lenient.

       3.      Allow existing licensed STRs to continue as non-complying uses.
               As proposed, the use would be grandfathered until 2026, at which time properties
               would be required to meet all requirements. An alternative proposal would allow
               them to be grandfathered beyond that time.

       4.      STRs would not be allowed in accessory dwelling units (“ADU”).
               The Council felt that ADUs are intended to add to housing stock and should be
               limited to long-term rentals.

       5.      Unlicensed operators were given the opportunity to come into compliance.
               Public notice of the meeting and pending Ordinance was mailed in early January to
               over 5,000 entities to provide them with the opportunity to become vested. Less
               than five applications were received as a result of that notice.

       6.      A new Business License Fee has been established to cover administrative costs
               that will take effect in the new budget year.

       7.      Establish the Good Landlord Program offering reduced fees to hosts who
               agree to be good neighbors and follow through on that commitment.
               The Council rejected this idea because they believe the R-1 District should look
               like a single-family neighborhood and if someone is conducting business in it, they
               should pay the appropriate fees.

       8.      Allowing only one STR per property.
               Currently, there are hosts who have more than one licensed STR on their property.
               Moving forward, that will not be allowed. Anyone with a vested license would not
               be affected by this regulation.

       9.      Establish standards for health, safety, and predictability.
               This is standard for most STR ordinances.

       10.     Require onsite parking.
               The Council agreed that one parking stall should be required for every five
               occupants in addition to the stalls required for a single-family home.

       11.     Occupancy cap.
               Mr. Weidenhamer originally proposed a cap of 15, but the Council determined that
               10 was more appropriate.

       12.     Posting requirements as discussed earlier in the meeting.

In summary, Mr. Weidenhamer reported that the Ordinance was drafted at the City Council’s
request to address citizens’ concerns. It was not intended to disregard people’s investments in the
City. They recognize that people have intentionally invested in South Salt Lake City, and the
Council has talked about their ability to retain their property as a long-term rental if they are no
longer eligible for a short-term rental. There is a continued drive from the Council to have more
long-term residents and build a real community.

licensees to be grandfathered in was not included in the notice. He believes Mayor Wood and the
City Council would approve of a measure encouraging non-compliant owners to apply for a

In response to a question, Ms. Ungricht provided an explanation of how grandfathering would
work. Any business license that has already been issued and is current could legally operate as-is
without meeting any standards listed in the Ordinance, but they would be required to pay updated
fees and meet current safety regulations. In response to a question, Ms. Ungricht clarified that the
use would run with the land, so the grandfathering would remain in place as long as the property
is lawfully operating. The business would be transferable as long as the license has not lapsed for
the period specified by Code. If it were to be converted to a long-term rental or private residence
and they then wanted to operate a STR again, they would have to comply with the standards in
