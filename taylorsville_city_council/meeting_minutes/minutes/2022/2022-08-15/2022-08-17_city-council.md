1.      WELCOME, INTRODUCTION AND PRELIMINARY MATTERS

Chair Barbieri called the meeting to order at 6:29 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance. A roll
call was conducted wherein all councilmembers were present.

        1.1  Opening Ceremonies – Pledge/Reverence – Councilmember Harker (Opening
        Ceremonies for September 7, 2022 to be arranged by Mayor Overson

Councilmember Harker directed the Pledge of Allegiance before sharing some thoughts about
education. She explained that she was a schoolteacher of 3rd graders who would be starting school
the next day and she felt so grateful that the U.S. educated everyone. She then quoted Nelson
Mandela when he said, “Education is the most powerful weapon which you can use to change the
world.” Councilmember Harker believed that education was important because it taught our
children how to be good people, good stewards, and shape a better society to live in. She then
offered a Reverence.

        1.2      Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Kristie Overson reported that she had recently attended a “highly energized” ribbon
cutting at the newest Dutch Bros. Coffee in town. She appreciated everyone that attended the
opening.

The mayor had been keeping the council updated on the status of the search for winter overflow
homeless shelters. It appeared that the old Calvin Smith library in Millcreek was the most likely
location to be chosen by the sub-committee tasked with reviewing the options. She was grateful to
Mayor Silvestrini and Millcreek for being willing to step up and approve that decision.

She had recently attended Granite School District’s giant pep rally at the Maverik Center. It had
been her pleasure to be there in support of the teachers, bus drivers, crossing guards, etc. who
worked in Taylorsville schools. She asked drivers to be patient and cautious now that school had
resumed, and children would be walking to and from school.

Mayor Overson had recently attended the Legislative Policy Committee meeting through the Utah
League of Cities and Towns with John Hiskey. They were provided with a snapshot of what to
expect in the coming legislative session. She pointed out that the ULCT would be holding its
annual convention on October 5-7, 2022 at the Salt Palace. If any councilmembers wished to
attend, they should contact Council Coordinator Kris Heineman.

The yearly Night out Against Crime had recently been held and fortunately the weather cooperated.
The public safety committee put a great deal of work into the event, and she appreciated the many
people and various agencies for their efforts.

The Mayor had appointed Dean Paynter as the Chair of the Citizen Advisory Board which had
been trained and had met the previous evening with a long and aggressive agenda. The city had
known when it decided to form a new police department that this board would be an important
element.

Det. Jordan Lenz of TVPD had recently been the recipient of the August WOW award from the
Salt Lake County Children’s Justice Center. He received this recognition for continually going
“above and beyond to ensure victims are safe” and he advocated on their behalf. Mayor Overson
was grateful not only for Det. Lenz but for all that the police officers did on a daily basis.

Community and Economic Development Director Wayne Harper, in his capacity as a State Senator
was elected by his peers to serve as Vice President on the National Conference of State
Legislatures. The mayor pointed out that he was very well-respected, and she appreciated
everything he did both for the city as a staff member and for the larger community as a legislator.

Mayor Overson expressed her appreciation to those staff members who put the nets up at the “new”
pickleball courts at Millrace Park. The courts were in use every time she drove by.

The Mayor concluded her remarks by explaining that city staff and their families took part in an
end-of-summer event at the Centennial Plaza. She thanked the councilmembers who attended and
for their support.

        1.3      Citizen Comments

      Chair Barbieri invited any members of the audience to step forward and address the council
on the topic of their choosing.

Tina Waite said she lived on Atherton Drive and had lived in the city for thirty years. She
explained that for the last ten years she had problems with flooding from storms. The last two
years had been particularly problematic. She did her best to personally keep the drains clear of
debris, but debris was no longer the primary problem. Instead, it was the sheer volume of water in
the system. Her insurance company said that if she purchased flood insurance, they would not pay
a claim unless at least six homes were flooded. The situation was untenable, and she appealed to
the city to provide a better drainage system. The current system was appropriate when it was
designed in the 1960s, but it was no longer sufficient.

There were no additional citizen comments, and Chair Barbieri closed the citizen comment period.

        3.1      Police Department Report – Chief Brady Cottam

      Chief Cottam asked that Deputy Chiefs Miller and Gray join him at the podium.

The Employee of the Month for March 2022 was Sgt. Ryan Carver who had arrived on a fire call
before the fire department, entered a burning home and removed a resident who had been unable
to escape. He then was also able to rescue the woman’s dog as well. Chief Cottam thanked Sgt.
Carver for all he did for the department and for the community.

The Chief then invited Det. Jeremy Dean to step forward and be recognized as the April 2022
Employee of the Month. Det. Dean had done an amazing job of building the department’s sex
offender registry program from the ground up. He was also involved with peer support through
the Fraternal Order of Police and was typically the first person to step up when needed. Chief
Cottam said that Det. Dean truly epitomized the term “Team Taylorsville.”

The Chief then explained that Det. Kyle Andrew was unable to attend the meeting that evening
but his supervisor, Sgt Scott Lloyd stepped forward to accept the award for May 2022 Employee
of the Month on his behalf. The Chief explained that Det. Andrew was “one of a kind” and did an
outstanding job with the training program, and firearms.

Chief Cottam was pleased to present the city council with information pertaining to the fourth
quarter of the police department. He stated there were no remarkable changes from the previous
quarter other than traffic issues. He attributed this to the current ability to be proactive on traffic
offenses which was a particular problem that had been identified (particularly on Bangerter
Highway.)

Councilmember Harker mentioned a complaint she had seen on social media about speeders and
drivers who ran red lights and she asked how such situations were typically handled. Chief Cottam
explained that TVPD officers had discretion to determine if a warning or a citation was most
appropriate for any given situation.

The Chief then presented heat maps which depicted traffic accidents in the city as well as the
citations issued-- both traffic and criminal. He pointed out that there were no citation quotas that
TVPD officers were expected to meet.

The Chief was particularly pleased about the low number of burglaries that had taken place in the
city during the 4th quarter. He also presented data regarding the large amount of work that the

The Chief then presented a statistical analysis of officers’ response times during the previous
quarter. Although they had improved, he wanted them to continue to trend downward.

Many people had asked Chief Cottam how he thought the first year for the police department had

5.      PLANNING MATTERS

        5.1      Ordinance No. 22-08 – An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville Approving
                 Text Amendments to Taylorsville Municipal Code Section 13.25.010 related to
                 General Landscaping Standards for Park Strips – Terryne Bergeson

      Planning Intern Terryne Bergeson explained that the proposed ordinance was designed to
address landscaping standards for park strips. She indicated that the planning department had gone
through this particular code section and determined that it needed some updates. She presented
Taylorsville City Council Minutes
August 17, 2022
Page 8



information comparing what the current code said and the ways the proposed update would
improve it. The amendment would provide clarity and also support water-wise principles.

There was much more water efficiency language in the proposed ordinance, and it established clear
minimum vegetation requirements. It made clear the allowable maximum for hard surfaces and
defined what materials were allowed and which ones were prohibited. Ordinance No. 22-08 would
be applicable in all zones within Taylorsville.

Ms. Bergeson displayed photographs to illustrate some of the things that were encouraged in park
strips as well as prohibited items in park strips. Lawns were not prohibited with the update and
there was a minimum 25% vegetation requirement so that park strips could not be mere rock
gardens. Drought tolerant vegetation was encouraged. Ms. Bergeson explained that a public
hearing on this matter had been held before the Planning Commission forwarded a unanimous
positive recommendation to the city council. Ms. Bergeson recommended that the council approve
Ordinance No. 22-28 as presented.

Councilmember Harker asked what would happen if a resident chose to completely cover their
park strip with cement. Ms. Bergeson responded that would be a violation that would be referred
to code enforcement. Councilmember Harker appreciated the pictures that were included in the
staff report as they were very helpful. She also stated that Ms. Bergeson’s report was very well-
written and clear.

Councilmember Burgess asked what would happen if residents had existing park strips that would
no longer be considered compliant if Ordinance No. 22-28 were to be adopted. Would they be
grandfathered in? Mr. Harper responded that that issue would be addressed when the new land
development code was brought to the city council for review and potential approval at a future
date. Staff wished to set the standard of what was to be done moving forward. He felt Ms. Bergeson
had crafted an ordinance that was much more responsive to the city residents.

MOTION:          Councilmember Knudsen moved to adopt Ordinance No. 22-28, approving
                 text amendments to Taylorsville Municipal Code Section 13.25.010 related to
                 the General Landscaping Standards for Park Strips. The motion was seconded
                 by Councilmember Harker.

7.      OTHER MATTERS

        7.1      Resolution No. 22-25 – A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville Approving an
                 Interlocal Cooperation Agreement with the Salt Lake County Officer Involved
                 Critical Incident Task Force – Chief Brady Cottam

       Chief Cottam explained that this agreement involved many law enforcement agencies
throughout Salt Lake County. It stated that if a Taylorsville officer was involved in a critical
incident, the incident would be investigated by an outside agency—not by Taylorsville Police. It
also meant that when critical incidents occurred in other jurisdictions, Taylorsville might be called
on to investigate those matters, just as they were the previous weekend. He felt it was particularly
important to have both transparency and accountability in these situations.

The Chief indicated that he had been asked in what way having an incident investigated by an
outside agency would differ from what the Community Advisory Board did. He explained that
they shared the same core principle—getting policing right. However, the Citizen Advisory Board
was more internal.

MOTION:          Councilmember Cochran moved to approve Resolution No. 22-25, authorizing
