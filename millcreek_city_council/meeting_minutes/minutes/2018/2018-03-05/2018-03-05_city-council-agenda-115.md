Welcome and Introductions

Mayor Silvestrini started the meeting and invited scout Spencer Carter to lead the pledge of
allegiance. Introductions were made by those in attendance.

Mayor Silvestrini prefaced the meeting by stating that the community councils are volunteers
and they advise the Planning Commission. He thanked those who serve in that capacity. The
Mayor talked about how the City is growing and what services are currently being provided. He
mentioned that City Hall will be expanding into the other side of the building.

Budget Discussion

Mayor Silvestrini said the councils need to fill out volunteer and disclosure forms with Rita
Lund. The City Council needs year end accountings for what the City provided to the council last
year and a budget request for the upcoming fiscal year. He requested the councils provide that
information prior to March 20" when the City Council will discuss the budget. Lund brought up
that maybe the community councils should switch to a fiscal year budget cycle instead of a
calendar year cycle to be in sync with the City; the Mayor also recommended it.

Discussion of Capital Improvement Requests/Potential Community Council Area Tour
Mayor Silvestrini said that the community councils previously established priorities that did not
get taken care of when the County was governing the township. The Mayor requested that the
councils resubmit their updated priorities. He explained that it is easier to fix the priorities when
road work is already taking place. He explained that the City does preventative maintenance on
roads before doing a complete reconstruction. The Mayor said he wants to work with the
councils to explain that process and identifying the priorities.

John Miller said the information that engineering has is from the County over the last 10 years.
He showed the audience a chart of sidewalk requests that are ranked by safety. The Mayor said
when a request would come in at the County, that the engineers would go out and give it a
ranking in a spreadsheet. A community council member asked how the City can get requests
done for state-owned roads. The Mayor said by obtaining funding through grants. Miller said the
safe sidewalk grant application is due this month. Miller said the City gets class b and c road
funds ever year and tries to spend 60% of it on pavement preservation. The sidewalks get
overlooked due to lack of funding. When preservation projects are going on, sidewalks will get
addressed at the same time. The City has about $1.2 million for roads, sidewalks, and storm
drains. The Upland Dr. sidewalk installation this year will cost $500,000. The Mayor said the
City is constantly looking for grant money to build sidewalks.

Miller showed a traffic calming spreadsheet from the County logging over 100 requests. Council
Member Uipi asked if the County put requests on the list if a petition was submitted with at least
10 impacted neighbors. Miller said yes. Mayor Silvestrini wants the councils to see if the listed
requests are still the wanted projects and to determine what are the most important priorities.
Miller said the list will be distributed to the councils tomorrow. Lund said there are some errors
in the lists. A question was asked if Miller could supply the criteria for the rankings. Miller said
the 6 elements for traffic calming are: speed of the road, volume of traffic, bus or bike routes,
existing sidewalks, elementary schools, and how many pedestrian generators there are. Miller
was asked if there was a cost benefit analysis. Miller said it is difficult to estimate costs on every
project, so it is easiest for engineering if the councils give top priorities so that estimates can then
be made. A community council member asked about police patrol in high speed areas as a means
of traffic calming. Mayor Silvestrini said he is working with the Unified Police Department on
targeting areas. An officer in attendance said police officers have three “e’s” they like to use
when problems come up: enforcement, education, and engineering. He said engineering is the
most effective because it provides a permanent solution. Miller requested 2-5 priorities per
community council for projects. The Mayor said the chairs of the community councils could go
on a tour with the engineers to highlight the priority projects. It was requested that a column be

Planning/Zoning Discussion

Mayor Silvestrini said staff has been observing the community council meetings and they want
to educate the councils so that the City can provide the best planning service to the community.
He said most of the feedback to the Planning Commission thus far has been valuable, but the
community councils need to stay away from situations where the City is being exposed to
liability. He stated that he wants the councils to be mindful of the criteria allowed to use to
evaluate the applications. He suggested that the council chairs remind the audience at meetings
that with a conditional use permit, it is not a question of whether it can be denied or not, but to
determine the conditions to put on it because of its impact in the area. He said design standards
are not open to discussion with a conditional use permit. The councils need to give reasons for
conditions because they need to be targeted toward addressing the impacts of the project. Mayor
Silvestrini said zone changes have more latitude as legislative actions. Francis Lilly thanked the
councils for working with staff. He said staff is trying to be responsive by posting notice for
public hearings on the subject property and sending out mailed notice to property owners within
300 ft. of the subject property for the applications. This lets the public know there are multiple
options to participate in the process. He said the planning staff is available to the councils for
questions and to help fashion recommendations. Lilly gave recent examples on good community
council insight and recommendations coming from each district. The Mayor thanked Lilly on
getting applicants to meet with concerned residents. Lilly appreciates a written report from the
councils for recommendations for the Planning Commission.

There was a request for posting building permits on site. John Geilmann said that it is a good
suggestion. A comment was made that there is appreciation when the City participates in coming
up with solutions with the applicants. It was mentioned that the County will continue with
existing building permits through March, but will hand everything off to Millcreek April 1st.

John Brems gave a presentation on conditional use permits. He went through the legal definition
per Utah Code Ann. 10-9a-507. The presumption is that the conditional use permit shall be
granted if reasonable conditions can be imposed to mitigate the detrimental effects, but it may be
denied. He said Millcreek ordinance states that conditional uses shall be approved on a case by
case basis. He went through the process for reviewing conditional use permits: identify potential
detrimental effects on the record, determine if those effects can be mitigated, identify the
conditions that can mitigate the anticipated detrimental effects on the record, if the detrimental
effects can be mitigated then it must be approved, and if detrimental effects cannot be mitigated
then it may be denied. Lilly said detrimental effects come up in transitional areas between
commercial and residential zoning. Brems said the Planning Commission has final jurisdiction
on determinations; community councils are recommending bodies. The Mayor brought up the
appeal process on land use applications. He said the process is different for rezones because there

Community Council Election Discussion

Mayor Silvestrini said elections for community councils are in even numbered years and the City
Council is in odd numbered years. Previously, the election was done through ballots in the paper
newsletter. He said one newsletter would give notice of the election and the next issue would
provide bios on candidates with a ballot. People can mail in the paper ballots or vote online.
They had appointed an elections committee to count the ballots. Some people would put ballots
in a box after confirming voter registration with the County, and then other people counted the
ballots to preserve anonymity. He said the newsletter gets mailed to every residence in Millcreek.
He asked for recommendations from the community councils to do it another way. One
community council member wants the community council election to be on the same ballot as
the City Council. The Mayor said the City could explore the idea, but it would probably be too
expensive. Council Member Catten recommended offering polling stations to vote in person at
city events. Lund would like information from each community district about advertising for
elections. The question was asked if the voting process is still necessary if the candidate is
running unopposed. Mayor Silvestrini said yes. He also said that the councils could have
everyone up for election for 4-year terms and 2-year terms or have half of the council up for
election every other election. The consensus was to use the newsletter for elections.

Community Council By-Laws Review

The Mayor suggested that the councils should be looking at and updating their bylaws, especially
since Millcreek is now a city. The Mayor wants to make sure the community councils are elected
and that there is accountability for city funds.

Other Items

Council Member Jackson requested that the councils email the City Council their meeting
agendas. Mayor Silvestrini asked for content suggestions from the community councils and for
pictures of their districts for the City’s electronic newsletter. A comment was made to have a
new businesses section in the newsletter. The Mayor also invited the councils to attend the City
Council meetings.

John Brems gave a presentation on holding an effective public meeting. His recommendations
are: to be prepared for the meeting, explain the process and rules of the meeting to the public,
show respect and maintain order, the council chair should control the meeting, listen and keep an
open mind, and keep to the agenda and stay on topic.
