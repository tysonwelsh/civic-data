Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs)

Accessory Dwelling Units were only allowed in West Jordan Residential Estate Zones. Mr.
Langford asked the Council to explain their main concerns associated with ADUs.

Councilmember Lamb stated his two main concerns were parking and public safety,
particularly fire safety. He commented that enforcement of ADU parking requirements
could potentially be a problem. Councilmember Jacob commented that parking issues can
exist in any residential zone, with or without the presence of ADUs.

Councilmember Whitelock stated her concerns were infrastructure and being just. She said
she did not believe it was fair to the rest of the City to only allow ADUs in new
development. She commented that ADUs in existing development may look different than
ADUs in new development and suggested both should be a possibility. Several people
present   expressed      agreement.




Councilmember Jacob said he believed the group needed to define what constituted an
ADU.



It was pointed out that high housing prices were forcing people to rent, and a majority of
ADU requests had come from older residents. Councilmember Jacob said he would like to
first address the problem issues associated with ADUs, because some of those problem
issues may exist independent of the ADU issue and should be resolved independently.
Staff stated the last major update of the entire General Plan was done in 2012, although
portions of the Plan were updated more recently. Councilmember Whitelock said she
believed that if the future land-use plan were a good plan, there would not be a lot of
requests to change it. She suggested that since the City received a lot of requests to change
the plan, it was not a good plan. Commissioner Hatch responded that the world was
constantly changing, and the City needed to be able to respond to market changes. It was
suggested that the future land- use map should be examined every two years. Staff said the
last significant update was done 2- 3 years ago.


Councilmember Green agreed that the future land- use map/ plan needed to be updated and

Planned Community                 Form Base ( PCFB) Zone


Larry Gardner commented that dialogue with both the City Council and Planning
Commission was invaluable for Staff. Mr. Gardner stated the purpose of a city ordinance
was to protect the health, safety, and welfare of the city. He explained that a majority of
West Jordan            was    developed       using    conventional,   "   Euclidean"      zoning,      and    explained


positive and negative aspects of conventional zoning.

Korban Lee stated that, in his opinion, tax base per linear foot of street frontage is the
biggest indicator of the future financial sustainability of the City. Mayor Burton agreed
with Mr. Lee.
