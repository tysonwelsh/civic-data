              Kyle, Administrative Services Associate Directors, Janell Payne and Colby Hill)

Administrative Services Director Kyle introduced Associate Director of Parks Colby Hill and
Associate Director of Recreation Janell Payne. Mr. Kyle said they have had questions come up from
residents and City Council members about how they program the use of community parks. He said

into the parks master plan.

Mr. Hill and Ms. Payne reviewed the prepared presentation (Attachment A).

Mr. Kyle said they want the parks used at an appropriate level. He said he would prefer that parks be
heavily used rather than underutilized. He said there is a resident who has had concerns with how the
Jordan Ridge Park is being programmed. He said it was approved as a neighborhood park, but is now
classified as a community park. He said when the park was approved, there was a general
understanding that it was subject to change as the city grew and the needs changed. Currently, the
Jordan Ridge Park is being programmed. Utah Glory is a soccer league that rents the park 2 evenings
a week and Saturdays. He said the concerns are that the park is overused, and restricts access to the
neighborhood. There are also complaints about noise and spill over parking. Mr. Kyle acknowledged
that parking in this location is not ideal as there is 3 separate parking lots. The park is also
completely surrounded by homes. He said even though the park is rented 2 days a week and
Saturdays, it does not prevent other teams from using the field at other times, or kids just playing, or
practicing in the park on an informal basis. He said they also have had concerns expressed about
Highland Park and the north City park. He said the purpose of this discussion is to see if the City
Council feels they are programming the parks appropriately or if changes are needed.

Council Member Marlor said he is very supportive of the parks program. He agreed that the parks are
to be used, and not just seen. He said Jordan Ridge Park was built as a neighborhood park. Most of
the participants think that there is only 1 parking lot, off of 2200 West. That is where everyone goes
and pretty soon, there is parking everywhere. On a Saturday, for example, the park is used all day
until dark. He said most days of the week, you can find league play going on at the park. He is not
sure what the contract is, but it is being used well beyond what was described. There is also noise
that goes along with it. He said it was designed as a neighborhood park, but it is now more of a
regional competition venue and it was not designed to hold that competitive level and the people that
go with it. He concurred that the park should be used. He walks in this park regularly.

Chris Barker, resident near Jordan Ridge Park, expressed concerns about traffic in the area. He said
the park is overrun. They can’t use the park and feel it has been taken from them. It is a big park
because of the detention pond, but it is not in a great location. He said they don’t want to get rid of
Glory, but just have some sensibility how the park is used.

Mayor Ramsey asked what is the solution? They want the residents to be able to use the park.

Council Member Marlor said the current use is way overboard for what the city agreed to.

Ms. Payne said this past year, Utah Glory had it rented Tuesday and Thursday from 4-8, and
Saturday 8-6. Council Member Marlor said that is not what they are doing. Utah Glory is there every
day until dark, except Sunday. Mr. Barker said even in the dark, they will put their car lights on so
they can see. In bad weather, they sit in the car and honk their horn instead of cheering. He said it is
way past dark when they leave.

Mr. Kyle said on nights when Utah Glory is not scheduled, the field use is first come, first serve.
They should be going through the city when there are games scheduled. He said staff can have a

tell them that noncompliance can get their license revoked.

Mr. Barker said Utah Glory has had 8 games at the park, at least, since the time it was reported that

CM Whatcott said if the City Council is not opposed to using the park for some league play, as long

can do it administratively through the contract. If they are okay with Tuesday and Thursday and part
of the day Saturday, they can draw up a more restrictive contract.

Council Member Zander concurred with CM Whatcott. She said they might need an officer on
occasion at the beginning. She feels it is better to penalize them through fees and allow them to
police themselves. She is concerned about spectators honking their horns in lieu of cheering. That
needs to be specifically un-allowed. They will stop their people if it hurts them in the pocketbook.

Council Member Shelton concurred. He said they want the parks used, but not abusing the
surrounding neighbors. On the leagues, they could possibly set a 3 strikes you’re out clause with a
penalty each time. Mayor Ramsey concurred.

Council Member Marlor said one issue that they are having with parking is that they are holding
multiple games at a time. They need to restrict it to one game at a time. They have multiple teams
there all day. They need to let others use the park.

Mr. Kyle recommended that staff do an analysis of Jordan Ridge Park and how many games and how

Council Member McGuire concurred to address the issue through the contract process.

Council Member Harris said the problem is this group has taken over and are ruling the park. The
residents are not feeling welcome in their own park. They need to address the capacity limitations
with the soccer group. They need to establish parking, time limits, and noise rules. They don’t want
to kick them out, but they need to reign them in.

Mayor Ramsey said Jordan Ridge is in a unique location. She asked if they can have nuances in each
location without being discriminatory between the different athletic groups. She said all league play
contracts need to have the same rules. CM Whatcott said they can have nuances for each park. He
feels they should be consistent in the punitive clauses if rules are violated. He said they may get push
back from the leagues. He noted that other parks might have issues with parking in adjacent
commercial areas that is problematic.

Council Member Zander asked if there are any negatives if Utah Glory walks away? CM Whatcott
said there are kids that reside in the city that play there, and they may feel it is unfair. They get a
small revenue from it. Revenue is not the main goal; they want to make sure they have activities for
the youth.

Mr. Hill said Utah Glory is a good feeder for the city’s recreation program. The residents send the
younger brothers and sisters there. The city’s recreation league is established, it might not have an
impact.

Council Member Zander asked how many parks are being programmed by Utah Glory? Mr. Hill said
just Jordan Ridge.

Council Member Shelton concurred that this is a capacity issue and what can the park and
neighborhood bear. They want recreation opportunities available. If a league has grown beyond the
capacity, they may need to move on and go to a school. They need to focus on the capacity issue.

Mayor Ramsey said the traffic and parking situations created at Jordan Ridge park are unsafe.

City Attorney Loose said it’s hard to say that the park is open to anyone, but then not allow the
league to use it. They can do a lot with the contract, but they can’t fix everything that people will do
to get around it and make the situation work for them. They can address when games are played.
There should be an expectation during the time that it is not rented (free time), teams may come
down and still use the field and that is difficult to manage and enforce.

Mr. Kyle said it will be hard from an enforcement standpoint. They can tell the league that if they
don’t self police, the city can choose to not do a contract in the future.

Council Member Harris asked if there are other areas that this group can be directed to? CM
Whatcott said no. Mr. Kyle said the community parks are fully booked. They may be able to find a
smaller park for informal practices. It sounds like this league has outgrown the location. They will
need to either move or operate out of more than one location. That will be up to the league to
determine.

CM Whatcott said as they get more restrictive, the City Council will hear complaints. He said there
are areas in some parks where the grass does not have any rest time and they are not able to get back
to full strength. He said they may have to turn people away, and the leagues will go to the City
Council with complaints. He said they need to take care of the parks properly so they will last.

Council Member Harris said the parks are important and there is limited amount of park space. They
need to make sure the city provides enough green space so certain areas don’t get overwhelmed like
this.

         E.2.     Property on 10200 South – Ponds & Parks (By Public Works Director, Jason
                  Rasmussen and City Engineer, Brad Klavano)

Brad Klavano reviewed a map showing a city owned property on 10200 South. He said Jordan
Valley ran a water line and the city upgraded the storm water system and ran a secondary water line
from the Welby canal to Bingham Creek. He showed a concept plan for the property (Attachment B).
There are plans for a pump station on the property. They are looking at moving the detention basin so
it can accommodate what is needed in the area. They could consider a senior center on the property
in the future. He noted an existing tree nursery that would stay. They are also looking at doing a
trailhead for the Welby canal trail.

CM Whatcott said the city received this property in the late 90’s, early 2000’s. He said it was given
to the city for civic use purposes. There is a reverter clause in the contract. The neighbors do not
want a formal park on this property; they just want the area left open. The city has always planned to
use this property. If they don’t use it, it has to be given back to the developer, and the developer
could put homes on it.

Public Works Director Rasmussen said they tried to make this a park as much as possible, versus just
a storm water retention pond and a pump house. This iteration is a result of much discussion from
staff.

Mr. Hill noted the 2 shades of green. The darker green is a more traditional park. The lighter green
area is more of a passive park with less lawn and more natural grasses and trails.

Mayor Ramsey said if the Council likes the concept plan, would they do this right away? There is no
funding in place.

Mr. Klavano said they would like to build the pump station over the next year or two to get the water
up to the park, and they want to build the detention basin. The idea is that they place everything so
that the plan can progress overtime.

Mayor Ramsey asked if they have an indication how the neighbors would feel if they didn’t turn it
into this and it became homes instead? Mr. Klavano said he does not feel they would like homes. CM
Whatcott said the neighbors like it just how it currently is. There was a plan to develop the property
in the past, but due to opposition, it was tabled. He said they need a plan to follow. He feels this plan
utilizes the property in the best way possible. They did not want to start developing it, if the City
Council was not okay with the plan.

Council Member Harris said there was a clear expectation that the land was given to the city to use it.
They have an obligation to beautify the city. It will not be an active park. The plan is low impact and
staff took the concerns of the neighbors into consideration.

Strategic Services Director Tingey said they have held numerous hearings and neighborhood
meetings on this property. The 2-3 main desires are that the property remain open space, not be an
active park, and that the property eventually be beautified. This master plan accomplishes the desires
of the neighborhood.
