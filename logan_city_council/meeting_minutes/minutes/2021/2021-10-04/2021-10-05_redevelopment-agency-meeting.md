ACTION ITEM:

PUBLIC HEARING - Consideration of a proposed resolution approving Agency
Assistance to Triiio Group, LLC for its redevelopment project in the Downtown
Redevelopment Project Area (RPA) – Resolution 21-49 RDA – Kirk Jensen,
Economic Development Director

At the September 21, 2021 Council meeting, Economic Development Director Kirk
Jensen addressed the Redevelopment Agency regarding the proposed resolution. He
explained that the Triiio Group, LLC, referred to as Renewal - Mixed-Use Project
("Project"), located at the northeast corner of 300 South and 100 West in the Downtown
Redevelopment Project Area ("RPA") is proposed to consist of 69 residential units and
will be approximately 13,400 square feet of commercial space and will have various
other amenities. It will also have a 1,200 sq. ft. corner bistro, commercial office space,
and retail space. This project will contribute significantly to the achievement of the City’s
economic goals, including: the fiscal growth of the City through property and sales taxes;
and additional economic return for the City in terms of the Project serving as a catalyst,
encouraging additional development in the downtown area; the creation of new jobs; the
improvement of the appearance of a key City commercial area. The Economic
Development Committee has reviewed the proposed investment and recommends its
approval. They also approved the following incentive package:

Triiio Group, LLC will receive $250,000 from the Agency’s Affordable Housing Fund
(“Fund”) upon completion of its project as conditionally approved by the City of Logan
Planning Commission in Design Review Permit PC#21-039. Monies from the Fund can
be used for market-rate housing projects located in redevelopment project areas where a
blight finding exists. All construction plans are to be approved by the City of Logan
Planning Commission. Additionally, the Redevelopment Agency encourages Triiio
Group, LLC to utilize local contractors and vendors whenever possible.

Councilmember A. Anderson inquired if the funding is for the entire RDA or the specific
project?

Kirk Jensen, Economic Development Director answered that it was specific to the entire
Downtown RDA Area.

Chair M. Anderson opened the meeting to a public hearing.

Bronwyn O'Hara, a resident of Logan asked for the definition of what affordable housing
meant to the project.

Mr. Jensen responded that the Affordable Housing Fund consists of dollars specifically
earmarked when revenue is flowing into the RDA. There are statutory requirements that
apply to specific project areas when created one of which is the Downtown
Redevelopment Area. For every tax dollar that is generated and received ten percent is set
aside for affordable housing. The revenue can be used for affordable housing projects via
construction, improvements to any area within the vicinity of Logan City, or any type of
housing including market-rate housing within the project area. That is because there was
a finding of blight in this area. The legislature when they allowed and created that
provision saw value in introducing housing in a project area be it affordable or market
housing in terms of revitalizing that area. In our case the Downtown to bring necessary
ingredients into the downtown for revitalization.

John Shivik, a resident of Logan commented about the impact or rather lack of affordable
housing in the City. He would prefer that funds go to affordable housing and expressed
concern as for the need for funds to complete the project.

Vice Chair Simmonds explained that the City rarely receives a project for affordable
housing from a developer. Thereby putting the Council in a bind to use the funds
otherwise the funding reverts back to the State. The projects brought to Council are
typically a little bit affordable and mostly market-rate housing. She completely
understood Mr. Shivik's concerns, but it is preferable to use the funds rather than return
them to the State.

Michael Jewell, Developer of the proposed project explained that applying and being
approved by Litech (Low Income Housing Tax Credits) is the only manner for a
developer to create a low housing project. It is extraordinarily competitive in the State
and the system ironically grants based on the number of Litech projects created. It is a
rather difficult market to break into.

Vice Chair Simmonds asked if the system was a State or Federal system.

Mr. Jewell responded that the funding is Federal, but the system is through the State.
Developers must compete with other developers and thereby making it difficult. It is
ironic, but Litech projects are extraordinarily profitable. From a developer's perspective,
the only way to drive down housing costs is to create sufficient inventory to meet the
demand for supply.

There were no further comments and Chair M. Anderson closed the public hearing.
