PUBLIC HEARINGS

1.   City-Initiated Request to Amend Sections 17-3-5, 17-7-9.2. B, 17-7-9.12.3.3, 17-7-
     10.11.3. B, 17-7-10.12.2. E, 17-7-12.1.8, and 17-7-17.9 of the Midvale City Municipal
     Code to Reflect Changes in the State Law regarding Development Agreements.

      This city-initiated request to amend the Midvale City Municipal Code provided in Exhibit
      A harmonizes the local code language with changes that took place in the 2023 General
      Session of the Utah Legislature.

      House Bill 406 modified Utah Code Annotated (UCA) 10-9a-532, which is entitled
      “Development Agreements”, to read as follows:

      (d) A municipality may not require a development agreement as a condition for
      developing land if the municipality's land use regulations establish all applicable
      standards for development on the land.

      This change preempts the municipalities in Utah and requires that the following areas in the
      Midvale Municipal Code omit the requirement for a development agreement because the land
      use regulations establish all applicable standards for development on the land:

             - 17-3-5 Large Scale Master Planned Development (see Attachment A)
             - 17-7-9.12.3.3 Large Scale Master Plan Requirement (see Attachment C)

              - 17-7-16.5 Development Agreement (see Attachment G)
              - 17-7-17.9 Development Agreement (see Attachment H)

       Two sections retain a modified version of the development agreement feature based on
       preexisting master development agreements; these are:

              - 17-7-9.2. B Planning and Zoning Process (see Attachment B)
              - 17-7-10.11.3 Development Review Process (see Attachment D)

       Public notice has been sent to affected entities as required in Section 17-3-9 of the Midvale

DISCUSSION:

Chair Liedtke wanted to know the original intended purpose of the Development Agreements and
the reason why Midvale City has used many of these in the past.



PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION SEPTEMBER 13, 2023.
Mr. Knobloch shares that Midvale had the opportunity to use a Development Agreement in the
past to make the details of a project very specific. In which these details were translated into a
signed document to hold the developer to these elements. The zoning ordinance does not specify
one particular use for a singular parcel or anything that detailed.

 Adam Olsen, Community Development Director, states that there are both advantages and
disadvantages to Development Agreements. One of the reasons these have been required in the
past was a request of the City to tie a specific use to a specific parcel. Certain projects brought
before the Planning Commission and City Council could be very much preferred and the
Development Agreement would solidify that use for that area.

Mr. Olsen continues stating that in the future, the Planning Commission may need to review and
alter the allowed uses in each zone.

Mr. Knobloch also sees the positives and negatives of a Development Agreement. The specificity
in the agreements can be limiting to the use of that land. It has created several requests for zone
changes to City Staff. However, it also confirms that in a zone change request, prior to approval
the developer is required to propose and complete the type of project they have presented to
Midvale City. However, in order to be compliant with Utah State Law, we must immediately stop
the use of Development Agreements for ongoing and future projects.
