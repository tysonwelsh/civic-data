RESOLUTION NO. 2023-01-03                                              Date: February 13, 2023

      A RESOLUTION OF THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL EXPRESSING
                  APPRECIATION FOR SENATOR KAREN MAYNE

      WHEREAS, since 2008, Senator Mayne has represented the 5th District, which includes
 Kearns, in the Utah Senate; and

        WHEREAS, Senator Mayne sponsored S.B. 199, Local Government Revisions, in the
 2015 General Session of the Utah Legislature, which allowed Kearns and other previously
 unincorporated communities within Salt Lake County to incorporate as metro townships; and

DATE          MONDAY                         FEBRUARY                                  13, 2023

        WHEREAS, following the incorporation of the metro townships, Senator Mayne
 vigorously supported and defended the interests of Kearns and its residents; and

        WHEREAS, during the 2020 General Session of the Utah Legislature, Senator Mayne
 championed an effort to develop and pass S.B. 214, Gambling Machine and Sweepstakes
 Amendments, which addressed the criminal and public nuisance challenges that Kearns and
 other communities throughout Utah experienced because of fringe gambling machines; and

       WHEREAS, during the 2021 General Session of the Utah Legislature, Senator Mayne was
 instrumental in developing and passing S.B. 58, Metro Township Amendments, which
 authorized metro townships like Kearns to enact municipal energy and telecommunications
 taxes, placing Kearns and the other metro townships along with their residents on equal footing
 with other municipalities; and

      WHEREAS, Senator Mayne’s tireless efforts and leadership in passing S.B. 199, S.B. 214,
 and S.B. 58 are only three examples of the many actions she has taken to support and assist
 Kearns.

       NOW, THEREFORE, be it RESOLVED, effective immediately that:

        1.    The Kearns Metro Township Council expresses its sincere appreciation, gratitude,
 and praise to Senator Mayne for her dedicated and exemplary leadership; recognizes her time,
 energy, devotion, and commitment in successfully supporting Kearns and its citizens.

        2.   The Kearns Metro Township Council wishes Senator Mayne a very happy
 retirement.

       PASSED and ADOPTED this 13th day of February, 2023.

Council Request for Appearance: David Juma Code Enforcement-Business License

               Mayor Kelly Bush stated David Juma had been asked to appear before the
Council to give an update regarding the progress of his recently purchased business property
located at 5344 South 4015 West in Kearns. There had been some code enforcement concerns



                                              3
DATE           MONDAY                          FEBRUARY                                   13, 2023

because someone had been living at the property, which was zoned for business use. She also
asked about the status of his business license.

                David Juma explained that the person living on the property was living out of his
car and keeping watch over the lot. That person is no longer residing on the property. Delays in
repairing the grading and water retention system and structure itself, along with pricing increases,
have been preventing Mr. Juma from being able to obtain a business license. He expected to be
done with the necessary upgrades in the next couple months and asked if he could be issued a
temporary business license in the interim, as he was losing a lot of money due to not being able
to operate. He stated it would be mutually beneficial for Kearns to give him a temporary license
because if he had to move his inventory (roughly 50 cars) to operate somewhere else, he would
lose money and the township would lose out on a couple hundred thousand dollars in sales tax
revenue.

                Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated land use
approvals had to be done before Kearns could issue a business license. He also reminded the
Council of its previously adopted zero-tolerance policy toward those operating without a business
license. He advised the Council that issuing a temporary license could set an unwanted
precedent.

              Mayor Bush advised Mr. Juma to continue to keep the property up to code,
continue to work with Curtis Woodward at the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(MSD), and do what he needs to do to keep his business operating, even if that involves moving
the cars elsewhere so he can sell them legally. She asked him to be vigilant in keeping the lines
of communication open if he runs into issues, and reminded him she has eyes and ears
everywhere in Kearns.

Legislative Update

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, reviewed the following
bills:

    -    SB 174 Local Land Use and Development Revisions

              This bill would do two things. First, it would make apartments above a garage a
permanent use like an internal accessory dwelling unit (ADU). It will also change the subdivision
review process, prohibiting city councils from approving subdivisions. This bill is likely to pass.

    -    HB 291 Short Term Rental Amendments

         This is a short-term rental bill that would create a pilot program that would incentivize
people running short-term rentals without a business license to get a business license and a tax
ID. It is a voluntary program that would allow cities to receive tourism tax revenue. The bill would
require cities that want to participate to rezone to allow short term rentals in 80 percent of the
residential areas. This bill would also allow cities to use online advertisements as evidence of

DATE            MONDAY                          FEBRUARY                                    13, 2023

zoning violations, but it would have to be in conjunction with other evidence such as a neighbor
complaint.

   -       HB 232 Railroad Crossing Amendments

                Mr. Bracken and the Mayor have been working with Union Pacific to do a crossing
as part of the 4700 South project. Union Pacific wants Kearns to take over permanent
responsibility for maintaining their infrastructure in perpetuity in exchange for being able to do the
project. Mr. Bracken and the Mayor have been working with the bill sponsor and with the Utah
Department of Transportation (UDOT) to come up with a solution.

       -   HB 374 County Sheriff Amendments

              This bill initially would have removed Sheriff Rivera as CEO was changed to
invalidate UPD effective 2025. It is opposed by the Utah Association of Special Districts (UASD)
and the Utah Association of Counties (UAC).

       -   HB 301 Gas Tax and SB 175

               These bills would make tweaks to gas tax. The State is considering removing
sales tax on food. This legislation could affect Kearns.

Stakeholder Updates

Unified Police Department (UPD)

                 Chief Levi Hughes provided official letters from UPD accepting funds to the Metro
Township Clerk. The precinct started construction and is preparing for the least to go into effect.
The detectives will be moving into a larger space and patrol officers will get a patrol room. This
is important because they currently must use whatever space is available and sometimes
information is lost from one shift to another. Staffing was a struggle last year and still is. There
are three employees on light duty and five vacancies. This has negatively affected proactive
enforcement. UPD is receiving help from other precincts but lacking the necessary follow-up.
Stolen vehicles are down from January thanks in part to Kearns putting information out to the
public. There was a drive-by shooting in January and there is currently no suspect. There was
another drive-by shooting in February and UPD is on it. Reporting crimes is important because it

Other Business

Social Media/Website Work

               Jake Carpenter stated he recently contracted with Magna to create a social media
presence and have a channel of communication with the public. He feels he can do the same for
Kearns. The vision would be to have official Facebook and Twitter pages where residents can
learn about current events. He could also take requests from Council members. Anything on the
social media would also be put on the website kearnsutah.org. Since he started working with
Magna, there has been an increase in community engagement and more citizens have appeared
for public comment.

               Council Member Snow asked what the fee would be.

               Mr. Carpenter stated it would be a flat fee of $1000 per month, or $500 per service.

               Mayor Bush stated doing a Twitter and Instagram account might be preferable.

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated the Council
would need to officially adopt the social media policy it recently drafted if it is going to have a
social media presence. Otherwise, there could be first amendment issues with moderating public
comments.

               Mr. Carpenter stated Magna does not allow for comments on its pages; it just puts
out information. He would be offering both moderation and administrative services.

             Mr. Bracken stated disabling the comment function would make a lot of his
concerns go away.

               Council Member Schaeffer, seconded by Council Member Butterfield, moved to
authorize the mayor to obtain these services pursuant to standard procurement policy. The motion
passed unanimously.
