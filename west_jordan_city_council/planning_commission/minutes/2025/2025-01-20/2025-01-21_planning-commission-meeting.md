Preliminary Development Plan and master development agreement.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the preliminary subdivision plats for
Copper Rim Phases 5 through 9. Located at approximately 6150 West 7800 South in a Planned
Community (P-C) zone subject to the conditions and requirements listed in the staff report.

4.      IHC Copper Hills Clinic; 5557 Highlands Loop Road; Preliminary Site Plan; SC-2 Zone;
        FFKR Architects/Keith Jensen (applicant) (#34250; parcel 20-25-351-002)

Keith Jensen, applicant, provided an overview of the area, which is primarily retail shopping. The
proposed health clinic, which is allowed in the zone, will provide a needed community service to an
underserved area of West Jordan. Challenges with grading, access points, and building orientation
were worked through and this plan is designed to maximize the services offered and allow for future
expansion. The building front faces 5600 West and is set back from the street. Public access is from
5600 West and Highlands Loop Road with a service entrance from a private road on the east.
Pedestrian access points and bicycle pathways are provided. The primary exterior finish is brick with a
stone accent at the entrance and some glazing and metal panel accents. The building is one-story with a
screened mechanical penthouse. This building will be mid-sized and consistent with massing and
sizing of the buildings in the area. The exterior design is a new prototype for IHC, but the function of

6.     Review of Balanced Housing Report for 2024

Mark Forsythe provided information on the Balanced Housing Report for 2024. The ordinance was
initially adopted in 2014 in response to concerns from residents and elected officials with increased
numbers of high-density, high-intensity developments. The purpose is to guide these types of
developments to areas where infrastructure and roads are sufficient. With some exceptions, the
ordinance does not allow new multi-family development until the city can balance the ratio of 77%
single-family to 23% multi-family. A report on the status is required at the beginning of the new year,
which is currently 73% single-family. He discussed some of the trends over the last few years.

It was clarified that the units within the exceptions are still counted in the calculations for multi-family,
but they are approved outside of the ordinance. With the completion of water tanks, the number of
single-family homes should increase this year. The 77% to 23% ratio was established as a goal in the
2012 general plan. While the ratio is no longer in the general plan, it is in the city code.
