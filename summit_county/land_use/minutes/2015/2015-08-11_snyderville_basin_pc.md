2. Public hearing and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit for an 
expansion to the Silver Creek Water Reclamation facility; Mike Boyle representing 
Snyderville Basin Water Reclamation, applicant – Ray Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Principal Planner Ray Milliner presented the staff report and indicated the location of the 
sewer treatment plant.  He explained that this proposal would double the size and 
capacity of the treatment plant, which is needed because of the growth in the Snyderville 
Basin and Park City area.  Currently, the plant emits a significant amount of nitrogen and 
phosphorous, which chokes off the fish in the reservoirs and streams.  This expansion 
will allow the Water Reclamation facility to reduce the amount of phosphorous and 
nitrogen that goes into the Weber River Basin.  A special exception application was 
reviewed by the County Council to allow an increase in height for three of the buildings 
and relief from the requirement for façade shifts on the buildings.  He presented a site 
plan of the proposal showing both the existing and new facilities.  Staff recommended 
that the Planning Commission hold a public hearing and approve the conditional use.  He 
made a correction to the final phrase of Condition 2 to read “in compliance with the 
Snyderville Basin Development Code.” 
 
Mike Boyle, representing the Water Reclamation District, explained that this facility is 
not in the Silver Creek contamination area. 
  
Commissioner Klingenstein asked which of the facilities shown on the plan are old and 
which are new.  Mr. Boyle explained that they will demolish all of the old facility in a

positive recommendation to the County Council.

study of the roundabouts, ingress and egress, Landmark Drive, and additional mitigation 
measures they propose to make the intersection function adequately and safely.

and explained that the grade is quite steep at the intersection.  There is a 5% grade 
coming into the roundabout, and they would have to flatten the roundabout area, which 
would make the grade approaching the roundabout steeper.  It would be difficult for 
vehicles coming down the road to stop as they go into the roundabout, especially in 
snowy conditions.  Mr. Paddock noted the impact of the amount of square footage that 
would have to be removed from the proposed project and the property across Landmark 
Drive to accommodate a roundabout.  The proposed project would lose 43 parking stalls, 
and the roundabout would make truck ingress and egress very difficult.
