3.2 Public hearing and recommendation of PHI Properties request for review of R-1-22
(residential/agriculture) zoning on the Jacketta M. Clark Annexation, approximately 12.29-
acres of property located at 1300 South 2000 West.
    Sierra Pierson presented information on agenda item.

    The applicant was not present to represent the agenda item.

    Commissioner Hemmert opened public hearing on agenda item 3.2.

    Annette Gomm had questions on zoning, public comment, noticing requirements, and
    Planning Commission proceedings. She was concerned about proper noticing of the agenda
    item and asked if there would be another opportunity to give public input. She stated she has
    asked some of her neighbors if they had been noticed and felt that there had been inadequate
    notice. She asked if it was possible to request a more comprehensive effort of noticing. She
    was concerned about traffic if item is approved. She expressed concern about the City
    looking after themselves and not talking the public into consideration. She stated that the lots
    were not half acre lots and that if it is planned for a half acre lots then it should be expected
    to be half acre lots. Mrs. Gomm also stated the City needs to find another way to get parks.


    Commissioner Hemmert explained the process of noticing and the normal Planning
    Commission proceedings and stated that Staff has adhered to the codes and laws for noticing
    this item. He stated a comprehensive traffic study will be conducted to help with the plan of
    the annexation. He stated this is preliminary phase.

    Justin Cropp stated he was at the concept plan meeting. He was concerned the backyards
    don't line up and that there were not any half acre lots at the meeting he attended. He is
    concerned about the zoning change will affect the school near Dry Creek and Pioneer
    Crossing being heavily affected with traffic. He was concerned about exceptions being made
    for pre-planned lots. He would like the lots to line up so there aren’t double fences.

    Commissioner Hemmert closed public hearing on agenda item 3.2

    Commissioner Hemmert asked Staff if they see a concept before zoning is improved
    wouldn’t developers have to comply with the zoning.

    Mike West stated a concept plan is to receive feedback and to see if what the applicant is
    proposing is feasible. The concept was done in anticipation of the R-1-22 zone. Some of the
    lots are smaller because of the creek channel and potential flooding risk.

    Commissioner Hemmert stated if a concept plan was approved with smaller acres that the
    Commissioners can't do much about it, but City Council can. He recommended that Mrs.
    Gomm take Ross Dinsdale’s business card to keep in contact with Staff to express her
    concerns and stay updated on the item. Commissioner Everett stated there was a lot of public
    the night the original plan came to Planning Commission. He confirmed that all of the lots
    were a little under half acre because of the trail. He stated the item could be tabled when the
    applicant was present or move forward. Commissioner Peterson stated there were two things
    to consider, that concept has already been approved, and that zoning in a VLDR area is
    appropriate for the general plan. Commissioner Everett stated the public was not against R-1-
    22 zoning it was traffic and schools.

    Commissioner Hemmert stated he would entertain a motion.

Motion: Commissioner Nielsen moved to recommend approval of PHI Properties request for
review of R-1-22 (residential/agriculture) zoning on the Jacketta M. Clark Annexation,
approximately 12.29-acres of property located at 1300 South 2000 West; including all DRC
comments from January 29, 200 and based on the recommendations in the Staff report.

3.4 Public hearing and recommendation of Jennifer McMurrough’s request for review of a
Development Code amendment to Chapters 5 and 39, adding a Small Breed Dog Care
Facility use in certain non-residential zones and a definition for the use.
    Sierra presented information on agenda item. DRC had a couple of suggestions, including
    specific hours for daytime operations and nighttime hours, as well as removing the use from
    Neighborhood Commercial.

    Commissioner Ellis asked why it would be removed from Neighborhood Commercial. Sierra
    stated the concerns from any potential noise. Commissioner Nielsen asked if the were
    required to be indoors in the evening hours. Sierra stated they would like into that.

    The applicant, Jennifer McMurrough, was present. Commissioner Everett asked how this
    definition is different from Kennel. She stated the dogs would be able to go outside during
    daytime hours and the City’s kennel ordinance did not specify that. Commissioner Hemmert
    clarified that it included a size limit for dogs. Jennifer stated she has been in business in Salt
    Lake City for over twenty years and that there are rental houses North and South of the
    property and have great relationships with those owners. She is not opposed for the
    Neighborhood Commercial being removed from the zoning requirements.

    Commissioner Everett asked as part of the definition if the applicant would consider a cap on
    the number of animals at any given time. The applicant did not have an issue with a cap on
    the number of animals and stated 150 animals would be appropriate. Her staff to dog ratio is
    12 to 1 and the dogs are well taken care of.

    Commissioner Hemmert opened public hearing on agenda item 3.4.

    Commissioner Hemmert closed public hearing on agenda item 3.4.

    Commissioner Everett stated he has a hard time rewriting code for a specific niche and
    suggested modifying code that was already in place. He didn't feel that the outside space is a
    problem. He expressed concerns with problems it could create, such as medium size animals
    or the number of animals. Commissioner Jackson stated he didn't think number of dogs
    would be the issue, but that staff to dog ratio may be appropriate. Commissioner Hemmert
    stated a staff to dog ratio seems appropriate but questioned if it was up to the Commission to
    decide that. Commissioner Ellis stated that it is hard to come up with the number.
    Commissioner Bunker stated there is a facility in Salt Lake City and wondered if there was a
    code that covered those issues. Commissioner Jackson stated he would like to see what Salt
    Lake City has done. Commissioner Nielsen stated the kennel definition should be modified
    since includes all animals. Commissioner Everett stated that kennels were only allowed in A-
    1 and A-1 zoning and what to do with noise and other nuisance problems. Commissioner
    Nielsen stated the same issues with this zoning would face similar issues as the kennels.
    Commissioner Eyre stated the zone they choose would dictate the size of the footprint of the
    facility so they are limited.

    Commissioner Hemmert stated he appreciated all comments and would like to find a happy
    medium that works for the City as well as the applicant.

    Mike West stated it may a good idea to contact our Animal Enforcement Officer to be
    notified and involved and see what input he would have.

    Commissioner Hemmert stated that the applicant would need to be involved in whatever is
    decided. Commissioner Peterson liked what was being proposed and to keep kennel as it is
    and felt it was sufficient for the specific use. Commissioner Peterson stated if definitions are
    changed on kennel then all of the sudden the pandoras box is opened. The proposal is small
    dogs, zone, outside. Commissioner Everett stated a kennel permit is required and stated if we
    want to add a need for a certain regulatory check and balance. Commissioner Jackson stated
    kennel would need to be tweaked because small dog breed falls under the kennel law.

    Mike West stated if recommendations are being made, that the applicant has a right to take
    forward their application.

    The applicant, Jennifer McMurrough, readdressed the Commission to answer some issues
    and questions. She stated the pet care industry is poorly recommended and that if the kennel
    zone is recommended, people will be running dogs all over, because that is the part that is
    missing with her proposal. She stated it would be in the best interest not to modify kennel.
    She addressed Neighborhood Commercial and that they just proposed whichever seemed to
    fit and in Salt Lake City they are in M1 zone. She stated one of the properties they are
    interested in is in a heavy manufacturing zone and their intentions are not to be right near
    residential areas. She stated the facility is operated with universal precautions and is cleaned
    like a hospital and is highly regulated. She would like the recommendation to have more of a
    niche definition to avoid misuse.

    Commissioner Nielsen stated that some dog owners may not be as careful as the applicant
    and suggested being more definitive. Commissioner Hemmert asked if it would make sense
    to motion for conditional use. Commissioner Everett stated he agreed with Commissioner
    Nielsen about most not being as careful or responsible. Commissioner Eyre asked why it was
    limited to small dogs. The applicant stated it is because she only takes care of little dogs and
    can’t say from her experience that large dogs would be intolerable. She stated it is hoped that
    it protects the industry from unlawful animal care. She stated she has a business license and a
    kennel license. Commissioner Ellis stated a lot of those issues are addressed in the permit.
    Commissioner Nielsen asked if the regulation fell in the kennel permit. He suggested an
    animal control enforcement officer be involved.

    Mike West suggested that some zones could be conditional use and best practices must be
    abided by. Mike West stated a recommendation of approval with certain conditions to put in
    the code, or the item could be tabled.

    Commissioner Everett stated he would like to see code in best practice and kennel permit.
    Commissioner Hemmert stated it would need to be tabled at the request of the applicant. The
    applicant just wanted to clarify that she wants to do right by code and the City. She stated she
    wants to keep agriculture in a safe space. Commissioner Hemmert asked Commissioner
    Peterson on what direction he would suggest. Commissioner Peterson stated he felt ok giving
    a recommendation to the City Council and adding things, such as review by Animal Control
    Officer, best practice language be included, and be a conditional use with specific zones.
    Commissioner Ellis stated it would most likely meet all animal requirements in the code.
    Commissioner Nielsen stated he is a fan of not adding language and that he wouldn't narrow
    the size of dog and thought the simpler the better and recommended just modifying kennel.
    Commissioner Everett stated that may the advice that an Animal Control Officer would give
    and leave the motion open to leave suggestions and modifications.

    Commissioner Hemmert stated he would entertain a motion.

Motion: Commissioner Peterson moved to recommend approval of Jennifer McMurrough’s
request for review of a Development Code amendment to Chapters 5 and 39, adding a Small
Breed Dog Care Facility use in certain non-residential zones and a definition for the use; with the
recommendation that the Animal Control Officer be given the opportunity to make any
recommendations to the code for review by City Council, that the use in any non-industrial areas
are conditional use, that best practice language is added to the code and reviewed by Staff, noting
a license or permit be required; including all DRC comments and findings stated in the report.
Commissioner Ellis seconded.

Vote: Commissioner Ellis, yes; Commissioner Everett, yes; Commissioner Peterson, yes;
Commissioner Hemmert, yes; Commissioner Nielsen, yes; Commissioner Bunker, yes;
Commissioner Hereth, yes. The motion passed unanimously.
