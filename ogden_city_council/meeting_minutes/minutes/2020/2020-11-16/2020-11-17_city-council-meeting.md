system improvements in Water, Sanitaiy Sewer and Stonn Sewer, as outlined in the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) ai1d utility
master plans. This proposal recognizes revenues from refunding and issuing Water, Sanitaiy and Stonn Sewer utility bonds, and

          A memo from the City Comptroller regarding Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (CARES) Act grant
funding came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the City Council is considering a proposed budget
amendment to recognize and appropriate $6,612,850 in additional CARES Act funding. Additionally, the Council will consider an
addendum to the Interlocal Agreement with Weber County by further defining and allowing up to $4.3 million of distributed grant
funding for nonprofits and small businesses. Finally, the Council will consider creation of the Ogden CARES Nonprofits Grant
Program. The U.S. Government established the CARES Act with $150 Billion in funding to address needs related to COVID-19.
According to the U.S. Department of Treasury, this funding is designed to provide assistance to various governmental entities, and
requires that funds be used to cover expenses that are necessary expenditures incurred due to the public health emergency with
respect to COVID-19; not accounted for in the budget most recently approved as of March 27, 2020 for the govermnent entity;
and incmTed between March 1, 2020 and December 30, 2020. Tue Council has approved two budget amendments and
appropriated CARES Act funding for business and nonprofit grants. Currently, Administration is requesting consideration of a
resolution to establish the Ogden CARES Nonprofits Grant Program, similar to the current program for small business grants. To
date, nonprofits have been awarded 35% of the total amount of approved Ogden CARES grants. As impacts of the COVID-19
pandemic continue to increase, many nonprofits have identified emerging critical funding needs. Current budget proposals include
an additional CARES Act allocation of $4 .3 million dedicated to small business and nonprofit grant programs. This includes a
commitment ofup to $500,000 specifically for nonprofits, as outlined in a proposed addendum to the City's interlocal agreement
with Weber County.
          Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and made a presentation to review the proposed budget amendment. Revenue
sources include a CARES Act grant from the State of Utah in the amount of $2,312,850 and a CARES Act grant from Weber
County in the amount of$4.3 million. Acceptance and pass through of the County funds requires an addendum to the Interlocal
Agreement that was initially approved in September 2020. She briefly reported on the total amounts of CARES Act funds the City
has received thus far from the State (~$7.5 million) and County (~$7.3 million) and indicated that the total of$14.8 million has
been divided to cover City purposes and business/nonprofit grants. Tue City has awarded $3 .3 million to nonprofit entities and
$6.3 million to small businesses, with remaining funds of$1.6 million to award. Tue Community and Economic Development
Department has dedicated a great deal of time and effort to work with applicants for the funds and, to date, has awarded money to
44 nonprofits and 190 small businesses. Tue average award is $76,000 for nonprofits and $33,000 for small businesses. Ms. Stout
provided a breakdown of how the money set aside for City purposes will be allocated; $1.4 million will be spent on public safety
hazard pay and $2.8 million will cover projects and equipment purchases that have been deemed necessary to respond to the
COVID-19 pandemic.
          Business Development Division Manager Meess showed a presentation to provide background information regarding the
guidelines for the grants that Ogden City has awarded to nonprofits under the existing Ogden CARES Grant Program; $3 .3 7
