PAINTER SMITH ANNEXATION PETITION APPROVED:

   City Planner Shauna Talbot reported to the council that the city had received an annexation petition
from Russell Smith with Patrick Painter helping with the annexation process. The petition was
requesting the annexation of a parcel just north of Sunset Rail and contiguous with the city boundaries.
He is proposing an industrial zone that would extend the current industrial area but seems to fit with the
businesses in the area. The area being requested for annexation had recently been changed from Level 2
to Level 1 on the Annexation Policy Plan map. Councilor Travis Worwood asked Mr. Smith if his business
creates noise or if it uses chemicals. Mr. Smith answered that there is some noise associated with his
business but there are no hazardous chemicals used. He also indicated that he would have outside
storage and large trucks involved with his business. Mr. Smith desires to move his business from
Spanish Fork because too many residential homes have been built around his business.
   Councilor Travis Worwood moved to accept the Painter Smith Annexation Petition and allow it to
move forward in the annexation process. Councilor Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed
unanimously.

TEMPORARY LIVING ORDINANCE REVIEWED:

   City Attorney Kasey Wright took a few minutes to explain the constitutionality of private property
rights. He stated that the city can require some regulations because of the health, safety, and welfare of
the residents. Councilor Travis Worwood stated that it is already illegal to live in an RV outside of an RV
park. The proposed ordinance gives residents more liberties than they had. The proposed ordinance
would allow residents to get a permit to stay in an RV for 14 days. Two permits would be allowed per
calendar year. The enforcement of the ordinance would be complaint driven.
   The council members indicated they did not want the situation to become permanent. They do not
want people living in an RV or tent permanently. Mayor Seely said he had phone calls and text
messages from residents about this issue, but once he explained what the council was trying to achieve
the residents seemed satisfied with what the council was trying to accomplish by the Temporary Living
Ordinance.
   Attorney Wright said that more revisions were needed to the ordinance and that staff would bring
the final draft back for the council’s official action at the May 7th meeting.

ORDINANCE 04-16-2024 REZONE OF 1025 S MAIN STREET ADOPTED:

   City Planner Shauna Talbot updated the council that a request had been made to rezone the parcel
the Best Western hotel sits on in conjunction with a subdivision and upcoming site plan application. The
intent is to have two separate lots, one with the original hotel on the North, and one with a new hotel
on the South with 60 new rooms. The current zoning is CU1(Combined Use 1), and they are requesting
to rezone to HC1 (Highway Commercial 1). The parcel is located on Highway 28 just North of the Red
Cliffs Elementary School, and just South of Val Jones’ RV Park, on the East side of the road. This parcel is
on the highway and is adjacent to or in the neighborhood of similarly zoned parcels, such as the Holiday
Inn Express at 1507 S Main. Some of the reasons this would be a recommended rezone are:

    1. The project is located along Highway 28, a main arterial road with excellent traffic accessibility.
    2. The property is located due North of Exit 222 (south freeway exit) in Nephi
    3. There is excellent visibility to the I-15 corridor to facilitate clientele.
    4. The proposed project would be similar in size, use, and location to the current Holiday Inn
       Express hotel, as well as other hotels in the general south interchange area that are all zoned
       HC1.
    5. The proximity of the hotel to the City’s property, directly east, that the City is beginning to
       master plan for. This location would likely be for recreational uses that would be
       complementary to attracting overnight stays.
    6. The City is planning to install 1000 South Street, and this project would directly participate in the
       installation cost of this leg of the street.
    7. Both zones allow for a hotel, however, the CU1 zoning prohibits buildings greater than 5,000
       square feet in footprint size, greater than 3 levels, and 45 feet. This proposed project would

       allows buildings up to 6 levels or 75 feet.
    8. The lot size minimum and setbacks for the HC1 zone versus the CU1 zone are more protective of

    9. This rezone, subdivision, and site plan concurrent proposal will solve some traffic concerns
       UDOT has for this parcel and the general area for better traffic flow, and safety.

PARAMETERS RESOLUTION 04-16-2024-ELECTRIC FUND PROJECTS BOND ADOPTED:

   Finance Director/City Recorder Lisa Brough reviewed with the council a parameters resolution that
would dictate the limits allowed for any bond issuance for the 138 KV electric line project. The bond
would be paid back with existing and future electric utility fees. Although a bond would be allowed by
the resolution, staff will explore other cost-saving options, such as a direct bank placement. The
resolution has been prepared by the city’s bond counsel, Brandon Johnson from Farnsworth Johnson.
   The parameters resolution specifies the upper limit on the amount for which bonds will be issued
($8,500,000) and the maximum interest rate (6.0%) that will mature in not more than 26 years from the
date of issuance. The competitive market should allow the city to receive a rate below the maximum
interest rate. As staff reviews the needed bonding amount, considering the grants received, it will most
likely be less than the maximum bond limit set forth in the parameter’s resolution.
   Although the Electric Fund has a healthy fund balance it cannot fund this project out of fund
balance. The city has received two grants totaling over $2.4 million that will be a huge help in lowering
the amount of debt needed.
   Councilor JD Parady moved to adopt Electric Bond Parameters Resolution 04-16-2024. Councilor
Callaway seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
