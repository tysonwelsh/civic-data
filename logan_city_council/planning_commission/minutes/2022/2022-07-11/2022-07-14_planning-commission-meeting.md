 PC 22-033 Madden Short Term Rental -Continued from the June 23 meeting. [Conditional
 Use Permit] Gregory & Carrie Madden are requesting a Conditional Use Permit to rent out a
 portion of their home as a short-term rental located at 120 North 200 West in the Traditional
 Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) zone; TIN 06-021-0011 (Ellis Neighborhood).


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a conditional use permit to rent out a portion of a home
as a short-term rental. The owner will be living on the main floor of the home and renting out the
two-bedroom living space on the upper floor as a short-term rental. The LCD requires two designated
parking stalls for the long-term residence plus an additional two parking stalls for the short-term
rental. No parking stalls can be located within the front 25-foot setback and be a minimum of 9’x18’
in size. The parking plan currently proposed does not meet the required standards and will need to
be modified before a business license can be obtained.

Commissioner Guth asked about changing it to a one-bedroom rental to reduce the required parking
stalls.

Commissioner Croshaw asked about a firewall to separate the short-term rental space from the main
living portion of the home like what is required in multi-unit housing. R. Holley said that this is still
considered a single-family home with additional guests.

PROPONENT: Gregory Madden, the homeowner said that they are planning on only renting to one
party, with one vehicle.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.


COMMISSION: Commissioner Heare asked how the proposed parking plan does not meet the code.
R. Holley stated that the driveway is not long enough to fit four parking stalls without encroaching
into the front setback.


MOTION: Commissioner Guth moved to conditionally approve a Conditional Use Permit for PC
22-033 with the conditions of approval and findings for approval as listed below. Commissioner
Doutre seconded the motion.

PC 22-037 West Pointe Subdivision Amendment [Subdivision Permit] Jeremy Raymond/Julie
Stoddard are requesting a Subdivision Permit for a five-lot subdivision to amend the current plat
of the West Pointe Condominiums. This project is located at 310 West 1400 North on 1.36 acres
in the Mixed Residential Medium (MR-20) zone; TIN 05-089-0001; -0002; -0003; -0004; -0005; -
COMM (Bridger Neighborhood).


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a 5-lot subdivision of the West Pointe Condominiums
located on 1.36-acres at 310 West 1400 North in the Ellis Neighborhood. West Pointe is a
condominium complex that consists of five (5) fourplexes that are each individually owned with co-
ownership of the land that is maintained and governed by the West Pointe Homeowners Association
(HOA). The intent of the subdivision is to change from a condominium plat to a traditional subdivision
plant so the owners can own both the building and the land underneath. An HOA would still be
required to maintain the common areas such as driveways, and open space.

This proposal is a follow-up to the February 2022 (PC #22-001) rezone request from MR-12 to MR-
20 on the 1.36-acre parcel. The rezone was required to legally subdivide the overall site because
the existing 20 units exceed the allowable density under the MR-12 zone. On May 17, 2022,
Ordinance #22-06 was approved authorizing the zone change to MR-20 was approved by the City
Council and as a result, allows the density for the current subdivision request. A deed restriction was
recorded requiring the owner to apply for a downzone of the property from MR-20 back to MR-12
following the recordation of the approved subdivision plat.

R. Holley suggested that condition number 9 be amended to add that if the subdivision permit expires
or the final plat is not recorded the city will initiate the downzone of the property back to MR-12.

PROPONENT: Jeremy Raymond said that they are fine with the updated condition, they do not plan
on waiting that long to record the final plat.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Doutre asked what latitude the commission has with this subdivision.
R. Holley said that this case is very black and white, it is an established property, and the commission
is just looking at compliance with the density and lot size, either they meet it, or they don’t.


Commissioner Lewis said that this was a great compromise.

MOTION: Commissioner Heare moved to conditionally approve a subdivision permit for PC 22-
038 with the amended conditions of approval as listed below. Commissioner Croshaw seconded the
motion.

CONDITIONS OF APPROVAL
1. All standard conditions of approval will be recorded with the Subdivision Permit and are
   available in the Community Development Department.
2. Five (5) lots are approved with this subdivision permit.
3. All building setbacks shall comply with the MR-20 zone and shall be reviewed and approved
   prior to final plat approval.
4. Two parking stalls per unit will be included within each lot.
5. The overall open space shall comply with the MR-20 zone requirements.
6. The final plat shall be recorded within one (1) year of this action.
7. An HOA, or similar management entity, shall be established to maintain all common areas and
   open space.
8. Cross access easements shall be granted for access, parking, open space, and utilities.
9. The owners shall apply for a downzone of the properties from MR-20 back to MR-12 following
   the recordation of the approved subdivision plat. If the permit expires or the approved subdivision
   plat does not get recorded, the city shall apply for the downzone.

 PC 22-038 Land Development Code Amendment-Mixed Use, Chapters 17.10 & 17.34 [Code
 Amendment] Mike DeSimone/Logan City are requesting a Code Amendment to the Land
 Development Code to Chapter 17.10.100 Mixed-Use Development Standards and Chapter 17.34
 Residential Density and Height Bonuses. The changes modify specific density, design &
 development standards for projects in the mixed-use zones as well as establish a density bonus
 program for mixed-use projects.


STAFF/ PROPONENT: M. DeSimone reviewed the proposed amendment to the Land
Development Code (LDC) chapters 17.10 and 17.34, to modify the City’s approach to Mixed Use
Development by clarifying the intent and expected outcomes in the Mixed Use zone, modifying the
base density, adding performance-based density bonuses, increasing the proportionate amount of
commercial building area, requiring commercial uses along the street frontages, requiring a variety
of residential structure types, modifying the allowable building height, and allowing for a reduction
in minimum parking requirements.

The Vision Statement for Mixed Use in the 2008 General Plan states that Mixed Use Centers
(MUC) will have concentrations of commercial and office uses with residential uses integrated,
allows a residential density of 7 to 20 units per acre with multi-story structures with office and
residential uses above commercial uses. The Mixed Use areas are compact development
designed for people to live, work, and play within a walkable center, is integrated with mass transit,
and is intended to be somewhat self-sufficient by providing its own customers to support the
commercial businesses. MUC development will be pedestrian-oriented with an emphasis on


quality urban design and landscaping. The benefits of Mixed-Use zones are the reduction of traffic
and air pollution, lower infrastructure costs, a more diverse tax base, increased health benefits
from walkability/livability, and development flexibility. Successful mixed-use developments will
allow for greater housing variety and density, will reduce the distance between housing,
workplaces, and retail, encourages more compact development, strengthens neighborhood
character, and promotes pedestrian and bicycle-friendly environments.

There are some challenges to consider when reviewing a proposed mixed-use center. There isn’t
one right way to design and build a mixed-use development. Uses, forms, and structures in a
mixed-use project should be based on the unique conditions of the community. It is important to
understand that the economy’s focus is changing to big box retailers from small locally owned
businesses. The American dream is changing, not everyone wants to own a home on a ¼-acre lot.
Reducing density doesn’t necessarily make a better project. A mixed-use project needs to occur in
a location where it can become an integral part of the community where it will be successful, and
not every location is right for mixed-use.

Local examples of Mixed-Use Projects include:

Horizontal Mixed Use – A mixture of uses spread out over a project site, e.g., Riverwoods
(residential, commercial, office, hotels), Homestead (residential emphasis).

Vertical Mixed Use – A mixture of uses arranged vertically within a building, e.g., Morty’s, Blue
Square, Renewal, Westfield Lofts, Solo Vista.

Hybrid – Mixture of uses both arranged within a building and spread out over a project site, e.g.,
Park Avenue (residential, commercial, live/work type of units, commercial pad sites), Brickyard
(residential, commercial), Bordon Lofts (residential, commercial, live/work).

The proposed changes to the Mixed-Use Development Standards are as follows:

Refine the Intent Statement to clearly state our expectations:
The intent of the Mixed-Use Zoning District (MU) is to facilitate the integration of a variety of
compatible uses with the goal of creating a community that offers “live, work, and play”
opportunities within convenient walking distance of each other as well as with the existing
neighborhood. Mixed-use developments shall have both residential and commercial elements
scaled to ensure compatibility with surrounding neighborhoods while promoting a pedestrian-
friendly environment and a more compact design

Articulate Desired Design Characteristics of a Mixed-Use Project:
All mixed-use developments shall exhibit the following characteristics: wide sidewalks, street trees,
street furniture, community gathering spaces, shared parking, integrated public transit (where
available and/or anticipated), innovative and distinctive building architecture, a variety of
commercial uses & activities, and a thoughtful and integrated mixture of different housing types.

Reduce the Base Density while enabling Higher Densities for Better Design through
Performance Based Density Bonuses:
Base Density: Units/acre                20
Minimum Density: Units/acre              5
Density Bonus: Units/acre             1-30
Increase Minimum Commercial Building Area Requirements:

              Overall Project Size                       Minimum Commercial Building Area


                  Up to 3 Acres                                    3,000 square feet
                    3 – 5 Acres                                    6,000 square feet
                   5 – 7.5 Acres                                   9,000 square feet
                  7.5 – 10 Acres                                  12,000 square feet
                  10 – 15 Acres                                   20,000 square feet
                  15 – 20 Acres                                   30,000 square feet
                                                      40,000 square feet plus 1,000 sf/acre
                    20 + Acres
                                                           for projects larger than 20 acres

Require Ground Floor Commercial Uses on all buildings adjacent to high-volume streets:
All buildings, or portions thereof, fronting onto an arterial or collector street as designated on the
Logan City Transportation Master Plan, shall have at least 50% of the ground floor designed and
built for commercial uses.

Encourage More Commercial:
Free-standing commercial buildings are permitted along all street-facing locations regardless of
street classification.

Encourage More Open Space in Lieu of Commercial:
The Planning Commission may permit the substitution of open space at a ratio of 1/1 for up to 50%
of the required commercial area provided the proposed open space is integrated into the overall
design of the project and provides functional public gathering areas.

Better Define Ground-floor Residential Space:
Residential uses are not permitted within the required ground floor commercial area of a vertical
mixed-use building. Accessory residential uses in a vertical mixed-use building including, but not
limited to, lobby, mail room, mechanical, storage, bike areas, lounge, fitness area, pool, sauna, etc.,
shall comprise no more than 20% of the building footprint on the ground floor. Enclosed parking on
the ground floor of a mixed-use structure is not considered commercial space.

Establish where Residential is not to be Located:
Freestanding residential buildings are not permitted on an arterial or collector street.

Require Proportionality of Construction Timing:
If the project is to be constructed in phases, each phase shall include an amount of commercial area
proportionately scaled to the amount of residential development. For example, if phase 1 is
approximately 3 acres in size, at least 3,000 square feet of commercial building area shall be
constructed during the same phase. No phase shall consist solely of residential unless at least 50%
of the required commercial area has been built in previous phases.

Mixture of Residential Structure Types Required:

                                                      Number of Required Residential Structure
              Overall Project Size
                                                                      Typed
                  Up to 3 Acres                             1 Residential Structure Type
                  3 – 10 Acres                             2 Residential Structure Types
                   10 + Acres                              3 Residential Structure Types

Performance Based Residential Density Bonus Program:
A performance-based residential density bonus of up to an additional 30 units per acre may be
granted by the Planning Commission for project enhancements


   •   Structured Parking
   •   Enhanced Open Space & Public Gathering Areas
   •   Vertical Mixed Use
   •   Enhanced Commercial
   •   Affordable Housing

           Project Enhancement                                      Density Bonus
             Structured Parking                                           20
   Enhanced Open Space & Public Gathering
                                                                           20
                    Areas
             Vertical Mixed Use                                            15
        Enhanced Commercial Areas                                          15
          Affordable Housing Units                                         5

The potential 30 du/ac density bonus is cumulative for a project site meaning a proponent could
utilize a variety of enhancements to achieve the total potential density bonus available in the Mixed-
Use zone.

Added Specific Residential Front Building Setback:

               Commercial Front                                         0’ – 10’
                Residential Front                                       0’ – 20’
                   Side (min)                                              8’
               Side Common Wall                                            0’
                   Read (min)                                              10’

Increased Maximum Building Height to 70’:
Added the following Language:
The maximum building height along a street frontage is 58’. The maximum building height may be
increased up to 70’ by applying a height transition ratio of 2’ horizontal/1’ vertical.
Projects adjacent to an NR zone shall comply with the height and setback transition standards in
Section 17.12.040.

Added Alternative Parking Language:
Parking Reductions: Parking requirements may be reduced by up to 25% for vertical mixed-use
projects with the approval of an alternative parking plan demonstrating how the overall project design
is able to accommodate its intended uses and how the proposal will not shift the private parking
burden onto the public or adjoining property owners. The alternative parking plan shall identify the
range of compatible uses intended for the project, the anticipated parking demands of all residential
and commercial uses, the specific parking management strategies to be implemented, and
adequately demonstrate how pedestrian and alternative methods of transportation will be integrated
into overall project design. The approval of a parking reduction is discretionary by the Planning
Commission.

Parking requirements reductions up to 25% for vertical mixed-use through an Alternative
Parking Plan:
   • Demonstrate how the design accommodates its intended uses
   • Don’t shift private parking onto the public or others
   • Identifies range of compatible uses in the project
   • Anticipated parking demands of all uses


   •   Specific parking management strategies
   •   Integrate pedestrian and alternative modes of transportation
   •   Shared parking arrangements

Residential Density Bonus Program (17.34):
   • Up to 30 Additional Units/Acre
   • Performance Based (Structured Parking, Open Space, Vertical Mixed Use, Enhanced
      Commercial Areas, Affordable Housing)
   • Cumulative – can use multiple enhancements
   • Can be Granted Proportionally Based on Activity
   • Discretionary

Structured Parking:
A residential density bonus of up to 20 additional units per acre may be granted if the project design
includes a parking structure for at least 50% of the required parking stalls. Structured parking
excludes individual garages, carports, or other similar structures.

Enhanced Open Space & Public Gathering Areas:
A residential density bonus of up to 20 additional units per acre may be granted if the total amount
of open space is increased by at least 100% and includes public gathering areas integrated
throughout the overall project design.

Vertical Mixed Use:
A residential density bonus of up to 15 additional units per acre may be granted if all buildings fronting
a public street regardless of street classification have street-facing commercial space on at least
50% of the ground floor and residential units above. No residential uses, including parking, are
permitted within this commercial area.

Enhanced Commercial Areas:
A residential density bonus of up to 15 units/acre may be granted if the minimum amount of
commercial space is increased by 50%.

Affordable Housing Units:
On project sites larger than three acres, a residential density bonus of up to 5 additional units per
acre may be granted if at least 5% of the total residential units are designated as affordable units
(tied to AMI income thresholds).

Commissioner Croshaw asked about existing buildings taller than 70’ in Logan. Commissioner Guth
asked for clarification on the changes in maximum density, as the existing code permits 30 dwelling
units per acre with a potential bonus of 10 additional units compared to the proposal to reduce the
density to 20 dwelling units per acre with a potential density bonus of up to 30 units per acre.
Commissioner Guth asked how the density bonuses would be applied to a project. M. DeSimone
said that the potential density bonuses would be based on the project submittal and how well the
applicant is meeting the requirements for each of the density bonus categories. Commissioner Guth
asked if open space could be better defined and if there could be more clarity on how the density
bonuses are earned. Commissioner Doutre expressed that we should better define the Design
Characteristics so that is clear what the numerical standard or requirement are for such items as
wide sidewalks, street trees, shared parking, etc. Commissioner Doutre said that it would be
beneficial if the density bonuses could be better defined and if there could be minimums established
with each type of bonus to help with the PC’s decision making on projects. Commissioner Doutre
also suggested to remove or eliminate some of the ambiguity with some of the language to ensure
that applicants and the City are on the same page when submitting and/or reviewing a project in the


Mixed Use zone. Commissioner Heare brought up the issue with establishing minimums, and once
we establish minimum standards, we will only get a minimum design and project. M. DeSimone said
that you have to look at the context of each project in applying the density bonuses, and that some
discretion on the part of the PC is going to be required. There was additional discussion about
articulating standards for good or preferred design. Commissioner Doutre also asked about different
residential structure types. M. DeSimone explained that the current Land Development Code defines
residential structure types. Commissioner Lewis asked about the commercial space on approved
mixed-use projects, and whether those approvals would be consistent with the proposed commercial
requirements. Commissioner Guth asked about whether we considered limiting the types of uses
allowed in the Mixed Use Zone. M. DeSimone explained that we would default to the current use
table.

PUBLIC: Michael Jewell, part owner of the Renewal project and the 10th West Mixed-Use project,
said they are excited about the proposed changes to Mixed-Use Code. Their 10th West project is
about 30 acres, under the old code, they would have been allowed 900 residential units and 7,500
square feet of commercial, which is not a mixed-use development. Under the proposed code their
10th West project will be closer to 500 residential units and 50,000 square feet of commercial. There
needs to be flexibility in mixed-use projects to create what a mixed-use zone should be.

Joy Reyes, a resident in the Ellis Neighborhood, said they want the proposed changes to enhance
their neighborhood. She also mentioned concerns with the proposed density bonuses, the proposed
height from 58’ to 70’, and reducing the amount of parking.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Heare likes the proposed commercial to residential ratio and the
requirement to develop the commercial along with the residential. He would also like to see more
clarity between open space and landscaping.

Commissioner Guth said this amendment is a great improvement and it addresses a lot of the
concerns that come up during the review process. She asked if a wider sidewalk should be defined
as a multi-use path and if open space can be better defined as a public space. Commissioner Guth
also stated that the commission should have more discretion on the commercial uses allowed in the
mixed-use zone to make sure they are compatible with residential. M. DeSimone said the use table
for mixed-use can be reviewed to see if there are allowed uses that may not be compatible with
residential.

Commissioner Lucero asked about adding language to the intent of enhanced commercial. M.
Desimone said we can add additional numeric standards to the commercial language but is also
going to require some discretion by the Planning Commission. Also discussed integration as it relates
to the project internally and integrating the overall project to the external neighborhood.
Commissioner Doutre asked if the bonus standards could be changed from numeric and from being
based on size to also being based on quality to avoid just having to judge a project based on how
much additional space versus how much better it will be for the project.

Commissioner Guth brought up the height concern and parking concern brought up in the public
comment. M. DeSimone said that there are height transitions required depending on the adjacent
zone to soften the increase in height. The parking standards remain the same, the different use
types need to be looked at to create a shared parking plan that will accommodate all of the different
needs.

Commissioner Lucero summarized the items in the amendment the commission would like more
clarification on, including the enhancement of open space, commercial, and public/community
gathering areas, connectivity, and mixed-use design characteristics.


MOTION: Commissioner Heare moved to continue PC 22-038 to the July 28, 2022 meeting to have
more clarity added to the proposed code amendment. Commissioner Guth seconded the motion.

Moved: K. Heare                    Seconded: J. Guth                   Approved: 6-0
