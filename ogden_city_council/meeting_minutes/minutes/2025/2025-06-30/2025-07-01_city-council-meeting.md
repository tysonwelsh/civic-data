Proposed Ordinance 2025-20 vacating a portion of Stewart Drive, quit
claiming the property within the vacated portion of street to abutting
property owners as their interest may appear; reserving a right-of-way for all
utilities that may now exist; and directing the City Recorder to record this
ordinance in the office of the County Recorder
           A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed partial street vacation at
331 Stewart Drive in Business Depot Ogden (BDO) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated The Boyer
Company is requesting the vacation of a portion of public right-of-way along Stewart Drive within Business Depot Ogden. The
area proposed for vacation is a narrow strip, approximately 440 feet in length, with a maximum width of 8 feet, totaling 2,475
square feet.
           Assistant Planning Director Simpson summarized the memo and stated petitioner is in the process of developing property
in BDO, anticipating a new industrial building at 398 Stewart Drive. The purpose of the request is to adjust the right-of-way line
adjacent to the property so that it follows the existing street improvements more closely and to allow more space on the front of
the building to meet the twenty-foot front setback requirement. The Planning Commission reviewed the request at its February 5,
2025, meeting and recommended approval of the proposed vacation, subject to applicant providing a clear legal description of the
area right-of-way being vacated, which has been satisfied.
           Vice Chair Hyer asked for confirmation that the vacation does not include any area of the street that has existing
underground infrastructure. Mr. Simpson stated that is accurate.
           Chair White invited input from the petitioner.
           Aaron Austad, The Boyer Company, stated the proposed action is an attempt to appropriately align the right-of-way line
adjacent to the property so that it follows the existing street improvements more closely and to allow more space on the front of
the building to meet the twenty-foot front setback requirement.
           Chair White introduced in writing proposed Ordinance 2025-20, entitled:
                    “An ordinance of Ogden City, Utah vacating a portion of Stewart Drive; quit claiming the property within the
                    vacated portion of said street to the abutting property owners as their interests may appear; reserving a right-of-
                    way for all utilities that may now exist in said portion of such street; directing the City Recorder to record this
                    ordinance in the office of the County Recorder; and by providing that this ordinance shall become effective
                    immediately upon posting after final passage.”
           Chair White then opened the public hearing.
           There being no persons appearing to be heard, VICE CHAIR HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC HEARING BE
CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF, ALL VOTING AYE.
           ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA,
ORDINANCE 2025-20 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL

Proposed Resolution 2025-13 adopting the Certified Tax Rate
         A memo from Council staff regarding the certified tax rate came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated
Mayor Nadolski presented his proposed 2025-2026 budget to the Council on May 6, 2025. On June 17, 2025, after holding a
public hearing, the City Council adopted the Fiscal Year 2025- 2026 Final Budget. Normally as part of the Final Budget, the
Council would adopt a certified tax rate. This sets the tax rate for the city’s portion of a property owner’s annual property tax bill.
This year, the final certified tax rate was not received by the City until the day of the final budget adoption. As such, the rate could
not be adopted by the Council. This action is to adopt the County’s certified tax rate for 2025. No adjustments to the City’s
budgeted property tax revenue or the city’s certified tax rate are being considered. The certified tax rate provided by the county for
2025 is 0.002193.
         Council Assistant Executive Director Symes summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
expound on the certified tax rate; according to Utah Code 59-2-924(1)(g), certified tax rate means a tax rate that will provide the
same ad valorum property tax revenue for a taxing entity as was budgeted by that taxing entity for the prior year. The certified tax
rate allows taxing entities to receive the same property tax revenue from the previous year plus new growth; as the city’s total
taxable value increases, the certified tax rate decreases. If the City’s total taxable value decreases, the certified tax rate increases.
No inflationary adjustment is built into the calculation. Mr. Symes presented a graphic to illustrate the manner in which the rate is
calculated; the City’s total budgeted revenue is divided by the total property valuation and new growth is then added in. He also
presented a chart illustrating the manner in which the City’s certified tax rate has adjusted from year to year based upon the
formula used by the County. Property owners wishing to appeal their property valuation can do so through the Weber County
Board of Equalization; applications are due by September 15, 2025. Mr. Symes also provided information about tax relief
programs available to residents. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2025-13, entitled:
                   “A resolution of the Ogden City Council determining the general City certified tax rate for Fiscal Year 2025-
                   2026.”
         ON A MOTION BY VICE CHAIR HYER AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF, RESOLUTION
2025-13 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL MEMBERS
BLAIR, CHOBERKA, GRAF, MYERS, RICHEY, VICE CHAIR HYER, AND CHAIR WHITE. VOTING NO – NONE.

Public Comments
          Nick Humphries reported he is an artist from Gallery 25, LLC, and once a month the LLC members meet to discuss
relevant matters; each month, there are more and more concerns about parking conditions on 25th Street and surrounding areas and
the potential for the City to impose parking regulations and a paid parking program. He is concerned that a parking program may
put the gallery out of business and the artists and other LLC members have not been given the opportunity to provide input to the
City regarding the matter. He has heard a survey has been conducted, but no member of the LLC has been provided with a survey
to complete. Adjoining business owners have the same concerns.
          Travis Pate stated he attended the kickoff event for the America’s 250 celebration; this is a significant milestone for the
nation, and he is excited about the emphasis on the history of the Country, State and each City in the State. He encouraged the
City Council to participate in the celebration as there are some tremendous opportunities for shining a spotlight on Ogden’s
history.
          Debra Darrington stated she is passionate about the historic preservation of Ogden City; she asked each Council member
to ask themselves if they truly are about the history of the community. She has encountered some problems related to her efforts to
preserve a home she owns in Ogden. She has no business plan whatsoever related to the Leroy Eccles Home. She previously
restored the Chantilly Mansion in Ogden City, and she learned a great deal about the history of the original owner of that home.
She recently discovered that Leroy Eccles was the president of First National Bank of Ogden, and she has chosen to preserve his
home. She discussed the issues she has encountered as she has begun the preservation project and has been required to submit to
the Landmarks Commission for their approval of her work. She started the application process and has found that she will be
required to spend a considerable amount of money and time before she is even allowed to start actually working on the home.
          Chair White asked Mayor Nadolski to respond to Mr. Humphries’ question. Mayor Nadolski stated that David Sawyer,
Assistant Executive Director of Community and Economic Development is overseeing the community engagement element of the
downtown parking program, and he advised Mr. Humphries to meet with Mr. Sawyer after the meeting. He stated Administration
is working hard to empower businesses and affected entities to provide input and he wants to hear Mr. Humphries’ input.
          Mayor Nadolski then referenced Mr. Pate’s comments and noted there are a few individuals that will represent Ogden as
part of the America 250 celebration; he will be sending a transmittal to the City Council regarding the efforts currently underway.
He concluded that he would look into Ms. Darrington’s concerns and respond to her.
