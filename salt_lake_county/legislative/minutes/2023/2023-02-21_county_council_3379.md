Ms. Lorianne Szendre , Victim Counseling Program Manager, Victim Support 
Services, District Attorney ’s Office, stated the District Attorney ’s Office has 
received the Victim of Crimes Act (VOCA) grant for the last six years.  It is federal 
funding, awarded on a two -year grant cycle.  The District Attorney ’s Office was 
awarded $ 885,671 for the two -year award period; this year, it received $ 394,828.  
This grant funds six full -time victim case managers who serve victims of crime .  
The primary service categories are information and referral, i .e., giving victims 
information on the court process and what is happening on their case, and referrals 
to needed community resources; personal advocacy, such as helping attorneys or 
other community agencies understand their needs; providing emotional support; 
and safety services, such as safety planning and emergency funds.  The grant 
allowed Victim Support Services to serve victims it would not have been able to 
serve otherwise.  The grant also enabled Victim Support Services to meet or 
surpass all of the performance outcomes and goals it set forth in the grant 
application.  Statistics from the last quarter report show Victim Support Services 
served 643 victims of crime with this grant, 530 of whom were victims of domestic 
violence, both children and adults.  Although the individuals Victim Support 
Services serves are victims of a variety of crimes, under this grant, there is a heavy 
focus and a heavy need to help victims of domestic violence.  In the next two to 
three months, the District Attorney ’s Office will be submitting a renewal application 
for the next grant cycle.  In June when the grant year ends, Victim Support Services 
will come back to the Council and give a full report on all the accomplishments and 
grant expectations it met for the last two years.
