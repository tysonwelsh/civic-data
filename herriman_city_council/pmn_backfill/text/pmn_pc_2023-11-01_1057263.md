West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by
law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the

6:45 PM WORK MEETING (Fort Herriman Conference Room)
Chair Andy Powell called the work meeting to order at 7:00 p.m.

1. Commission Business
   1.1. Review of City Council Decisions – Michael Maloy, Planning Director

Planning Director Michael Maloy announced City Council has not met since the latest PC meeting. He
further reported the next Commission work meeting and regular meeting would both take place in the
Council Chambers, due to Council scheduling a special meeting the same evening in the work room.

   1.2. Review of Camden Commons Final Development Plan for 11998 S Anthem Park Boulevard –
        Michael Maloy, Planning Director

Planning Director Maloy reported the contractor was working on infrastructure for the Camden
Commons Final development plan with the plat to be recorded tomorrow. Project Architect Jason
Dunham from Bradford F. Houston Design Studio reported the five acre project would contain 33
homes. He presented updates on the development, showcasing images and drawings. The presentation
covered current footprints, landscape plans, and the placement of trash bins. Emphasis was placed on
negotiations with utility companies for proper placement of utilities. He explained the inspiration was
from London's new towns and included the incorporation of hedges for delineation between public and
private spaces. Detailed views of different types of townhouses, including twin and triple units, were
presented. The architect discussed the orientation of doors, outdoor spaces, and the utilization of
hedges and gates for landscaping. The presentation underscored efforts to maintain architectural
authenticity, such as steep roof pitches and high-grade windows. The presentation concluded with a
discussion on potential upgrades, such as fireplaces and chimneys, with consideration for market
demand. The overall theme centered on creating a community with well-thought-out public and
private spaces, emphasizing the importance of design details and authenticity in the development.

   1.3. Pinterest Visual Preference Survey of City Planning and Development Concepts – Laurin
        Hoadley, Planner I

Planner Hoadley reviewed the Pinterest Visual Preference Survey of city planning and development
concepts. She explained the process of adding pins to the PC landscape board, providing step-by-step
instructions for Commission members. Commissioners were encouraged to explore and add pins
related to urban landscape ideas, with a focus on making the process user-friendly. Questions arose
about categorizing pins and the ability to move them into subcategories. Planner I Hoadley assured
members that such adjustments could be made after the initial pinning process. Once the group has
created a few different boards and we have enough maybe images to then talk about as a group. With
the goal of building this image library as a resource. The Commission engaged in conversations about
creating lush and green environments in Herriman, with a focus on more variety in landscaping and less
grass. The idea of using concrete with permeable surfaces was explored as a means of filtering water
before it returns to the ground. Members expressed the desire to categorize pins based on themes
such as multifamily housing and cities. Planning Director Maloy encouraged members to send out
emails notifying others of new pins and suggested using the Pinterest platform for collaborative
discussions on urban development ideas.
   1.4. Review of Land Review Use Development, Standards, and Updates – Michael Maloy, Planning
Director

Planning Manager Spencer provided a map of currently underway projects with construction updates.
The meeting proceeded to discuss various projects, starting with Teton Commercial, included a Wiggy
Wash and Kum & Go/Maverik. The DAI property featured another Wiggy Wash, office building and
Extra space storage. Other projects in Anthem Commercial such as Kiddie Academy, Pet Suites, Pickle
N’ Poach, Slackwater, Panda Express and Corner Plaza were reviewed. Development projects were
planned in Academy Village, including apartments, and in Rockwell and Camden Commons. The Cove
expansion was no longer being developed by Toll Brothers and the property owner was seeking a new
development team. Planning Manager Spencer also provided current Olympia development projects.
Collaboration efforts with service providers for code reviews were currently underway. Future
considerations included the Rapid Transit Overlay and discussions with the Wasatch Front Regional
Council on corridor preservation funds. The commission shared insights into modifications allowed by
state code for transit-oriented developments in conjunction with a bus rapid transit system.
