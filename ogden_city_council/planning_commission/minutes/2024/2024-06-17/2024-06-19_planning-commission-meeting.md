1. Discussion of General Plan update and work session

Mr. Rypien broke down the process for the General Plan workshop explaining that the people will get to
choose different options from multiple scenarios to create the vision they want before the workshop begins,
so that during the workshop they can place homes and jobs on the map according to their vision and have
open discussion on the pros and cons.

Mr. Southwick asked if there would be a software package to assist with that.

Mr. Rypien said they do have one in place with existing buildings already in the system and it will be a tool
used in the workshop. Current conditions to help clarify where they are at right now with race and ethnicity
as a city, our Hispanic/Latino population is 30%, with 55% of Ogden School District being Hispanic as well.
So that gives an idea of how our population will change in the future.

In highlighting our housing stock, 40% of Ogden’s housing stock was built before 1950. In the past we have
discussed the option of having design guidelines for established neighborhoods, and that because of the
age of our housing stock, our housing is more affordable. Though, as of 2022, 27% of those households that
pay a mortgage, and 41.6% of those households that pay rent are cost burdened. Which means that any
household that is spending more than 30% of their gross income on housing is considered cost burdened.
Our median household income is lower than the counties, so as a state, 8.2% of Utah is considered in poverty,
and ours is higher, so that is something that needs to be considered. Although we are fairly affordable within
state, an affordable housing unit would be about $257,000 which would be $1,545 a month. The median
house sales price is $422,000 which would require a household to bring in $113,000 a year, and that’s for a
30 year mortgage with a 6.3 percentage rate. It makes sense our poverty percentage includes the homeless
shelter. Our poverty level may be higher as well because we have more affordable housing here and they can
afford to live here more so than other places.

So, a good question that will be asked to help people understand the current situation in trying to buy ahome
is, if you had to buy your home today, could you afford to do so?

In talking about our major job industry, manufacturing is at 19%, public administration at 50%, Healthcare at
12%, retail trade at 8% and education services at 7%. We've had a 15.8% job growth in the last 5 years with
some of our major employers including IRS, McKay Dee Hospital, Autoliv, Weber State University and
America First Credit Union. 75% of the residents that work in the county live in the county, so only 25% leave
the county for work.

Historically, Ogden does really well, having 54 of our local properties in the historic registry. We have 40 parks,
with 27 of them on the National Parks and Recreation. We have 200 acres of park space as well as 16
reservable buildings. Moving on to areas of potential development or redevelopment, so areas that are
vacant, or land value is higher than the building value, we have highlighted major corridors and gateways
and roads into the city such as 12th St, Wall Ave, Harrison Blvd, Washington Blvd etc. (color specific map
was displayed). We will look at land uses, existing access, like UDOT as far as safety, etc. For future
population growth we are estimating between 16,00-20,000 residents in the next 25 years. The number of
households will be less because people live together, but the estimated number of households will be
between 10,000-15,000. During the mapping exercise we will ask people to place a specific number of
households in areas on the map where they want growth to occur and have them make that decision. Job
growth is estimated at 11,000-21,000 new jobs. We are creating station area plans for five OGX stations at
32"4 and Harrison, 36" and Harrison, the main campus stop, the stop near the student housing, and the Event
Center. Back to job growth, we talk about family sustaining jobs, so we want to recruit businesses that
provide the ability for families to support themselves.

Mr. Safsten commented that in reading employment statistics on a national level, if you took out the
government job growth of the statistic, it was radically changed. And how does that kind of projection affect
Ogden?

Ms. Williams added when you're talking about that and more people, what are the plans for more schools?
Mr. Brierley responded it is very interesting, as they are looking more at plans to close schools rather than
plans to add them due to people not having children.

Mr. Southwick agreed and said also contributing to that is the Charter school enrollment. Then when you
look at the student scores for Ogden schools and how they are declining, it encourages parents to move
their children to different schools/school districts other than Ogden. We also have the highest taxes in the
county and virtually half of that goes to the schools, who are not achieving a quality product.

Ms. Williams said she gets a lot of calls for people moving to Ogden for employment, but who don’t want to
live in Ogden because of the schools.

Ms. Sandau referred to some people, including most of her kids’ friends, who don’t go to Ogden schools due
to their parents preferring to bring them to schools out of the Ogden area. North Ogden is having a boom
right now and have pretty much said they are not accepting students from Ogden anymore, which may in
turn push a lot of the Ogden resident students back in the Ogden schools.

Mr. Barton moved on to saying that a lot of our growth is/will be coming from imports.

Mr. Rypien added that growth has flipped where it always used to be from immigration but reports show it's
now mostly people coming from imports from other states such as California, Washington, Nevada, Oregon,
etc. So, we will have that be part of the workshop as well. We'll have a couple of groups that will focus on
the stationery and plan portion, so we'll have specific forecasts for the area, along with the objects. And once
we go through this exercise, we will have a better idea of what we may need to change/allow and how to
look at what we can do differently within the zones. We do want to increase the number of housing units
around the stations and Weber State University has expressed their desire to increase their student housing
and update their master plan. They feel that this is all good timing, and we definitely want to encourage a
variety of land uses around those stations. Considering the stationary plan, we will have large, detailed maps,
or menus as we will call them, with different development types on the tables, each with it’s own title,

2. Discussion of Zoning Ordinance amendment limited multifamily in C-2 and C-3 zones.

Mr. Brierley stated terms in preparation for an ending ordinance on limiting multifamily in C-2 and C-3
zones. A hearing for that will be on July 3"¢. It’s not so much about stopping apartment development, but
it's making sure that when it happens it's done in the right place about getting the right thing in the right
place. So, our overall vision and housing goals, are housing that is well maintained, vary in cost and mixed
density, and is located in safe, stable, and revitalize neighborhoods throughout the city. We have a quality
Neighborhoods Initiative. Quality neighborhoods are healthy neighborhoods that include a variety of
commercial and residential properties, mixed use space, usable, open space, multimodal transportation
options are visually interesting and create memorable experiences provide meaningful system interaction
and social activities are secure and safe and promote economic sustainability. Part of what we did is look
at the housing supply and the potential for housing. Getting into household incomes now and what they
can afford based on rent and what's available, it comes down to about 2/3 of single families, are going to
need a duplex or townhome.

Mr. Safsten commented that if your talking 2/3, it really destabilizes the educational situation we've got in
our city.

Ms. Sandau added that every time a child is brought in that doesn’t speak English, that drives the scores
down, yet it doesn't show how successful they are as well.

Mr. Brierley continued what we've issued recently is 62% of the dwelling units have been multifamily and
34% have been townhomes. Here’s what's in review approved and under construction; there’s 2,650 mid-
rise apartments, 797 low rise townhome duplex, and 83 single family homes. If we take the household
forecast, on what's existing and the needs for new based on income levels, and what's the capacity for
where they can be built and where various housing types are appropriate (maps shown), then looking at the
forecast for commercial, there is the need for more shopping centers to keep up with growth. Looking back
on the history of Ogden in the 50’s, we were the retail center of northern Utah. So, you do have these long
strips of commercial that now it's a home or block converted, and there are plenty of little spots that are
not really prime commercial. We did carve out a few exceptions in the proposal for biking places and senior
housing.

Ms. Williams liked the concept of mixed-use especially in the area of senior housing to be able to
incorporate Insta Cares and grocery stores, so seniors don't have to get on a bus or call for a ride.

Mr. Brierley noted along that line the plan could also include some housing in the areas with OGX stations.
Mr. Southwick said if implementing this change forces, the surrounding communities to take their fair
share of higher density, affordable housing, he thought it was a positive.

Mr. Brierley concluded with the fifth grader version of the plan being that, currently you can build an
apartment in those commercial zones, so the planning commission would be discussing whether to
