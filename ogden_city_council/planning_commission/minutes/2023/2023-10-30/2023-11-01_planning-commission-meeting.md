3. Proposed Subdivision Amendment, for Hampton Cove, 5341 Skyline Parkway.

  Mr. Robert Wallace indicated that he has purchased this property and desires to construct a single-
  family home. He stated the plat requires a 20’ setback from the fault easement and he is requesting
  it be revised to be 15’ based on a recent geotechnical report indicating a 15’ setback is adequate as
  well as current USGS guidelines which concur.

  Ms. Hale stated this lot is in the R-1-10/SA zone, consisting of over 18,000 square feet, and has
  previously been associated with the adjacent lot, containing only a sports court. She referenced both
  a geotechnical study conducted at the time the subdivision was approved in 1996-1997 as well as a
  fault trace identified in 2000. When the subdivision was recorded, it identified a requirement for a 20’
  setback from the fault trace. The owner is asking the plat be amended to allow a 15’ setback. She a
  new geotechnical study has been completed at the expense of the current owner, which indicates a
  15’ setback from a fault trace is adequate. She also indicated in 2003 an amendment was made to
  the plat for Lots 23 and 24 which reduced the required setback from 50’ to 15’. This lot (Lot 22) was
  not included in that request. She indicated while no detailed geotechnical report from 1996 is
  available, she has contacted the Utah Geological Survey who confirmed the minimum setback for a
  residential structure of 15’ is consistent with the latest guidelines for geologic hazards. The
  identified fault at the rear of the lot was not confirmed with the recent trenching. While the trench did
  not extend into the steep portion of the lot, it continued beyond the requested 15’ setback. She
  stated without the reduced setback, the lot would become unbuildable and the current USGS
  guidelines provide sufficient protection to both the property owner and the surrounding community.
  She indicated the owner has expressed interest in constructing an accessory building on the
  northwest corner of the property, and suggested notes be placed on the plat which would limit any
  structure to be built outside the building envelope must be uninhabitable and have a slope stability
  analysis done showing it would meet minimum safety standards. She also identified other measures
  which could be completed to protect the public safety such as requesting a third party review of the

5. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, Floodplain Ordinance Update.

  Mr. Taylor Nielsen, Assistant City Engineer, stated in order to comply with Federal Emergency
  Management regulations, existing flood plain maps and codes need to be updated. He stated FEMA
  has provided flood maps identifying potential flood risk to allow insurance providers to determine
  need and cost of flood insurance for individual property owners. He stated while flooding may have
  various causes, many mitigation measures have taken place over the past 20 years to reduce the
  potential of flooding. Flood zones are identified and as Ogden participates in the National Flood
  Insurance Program, reduced rates for flood insurance become available for its citizens. He reviewed
  the process the City and State officials have been working with FEMA over the past several years
  relating to water and watersheds of the County. He stated the Public Works Department has been
  proactive in protecting existing assets and many areas were not impacted this last spring when there
  were runoff problems around the state. He stated standards identified flood potential flood risk as
  high, moderate and low. He identified areas on the map where flooding potential is changed from
  previous maps, stating many areas have now been mitigated due to new infrastructure. The request
  is to adopt all new maps and ordinance language relating to base flood elevations, records retention,
  application processes and other details.

  Commissioner Safsten commended the City on its accomplishments relating to flood mitigation and
  asked what is planned in the future. Mr. Nielsen responded the main focus is river projects, stating
  these rivers both beautify Ogden but can provide additional recreation opportunities. Next in line
  would be working on the storm drain system. Chair Sandau asked if potential buyers are ever made
                                                                                            8|Page

  insured loans typically require the flood zone be identified, but many owners are not informed. He

  A resident on 2nd Street between Washington and Wall Avenue indicated they had purchased this

6. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, Short-term rentals in the downtown.

  Mr. Brierley stated this is a request to allow short term rentals in the C-ENT and H25 zones in the
  downtown area. He defined a short-term rental as a dwelling unit with a kitchen, bathroom and
  sleeping area which is self-contained. These would be allowed to be rented for a short time period so
  long as the tenant has a different permanent residence. The intent is to provide options for visitors
  coming into Ogden and is typically rented for less than 30 days. While hotels are allowed in
  commercial zones, short-term rentals are not listed. The definition of a hotel in the downtown area
  requires at least 15 rooms while short-term rentals can be a single loft apartment.

  He stated while this had been previously reviewed by the Commission, several questions arose and
  the Commission has since held a few work sessions to discuss options for the downtown area. He
  indicated the proposed amendment was initiated by conversations with Visit Ogden who felt the
  downtown is the preferred location for short-term rentals rather than existing neighborhoods. The
  proposal would allow short term rentals in the C-ENT and H25 zones so long as certain standards are
  satisfied. One of the initial concerns of this use was the lack of on-site management, as hotels have
  this. Another was the length of stay as short-term rentals are typically limited to 30 days and hotel
  stay is limited to 90 days, but there is often a need for contract workers or other visitors desiring to
  stay more than 30 days. The proposed regulation would allow long-term stays so long as there is no

7. Discussion, Landscape standards and limits on turf grass.

  Mr. Rypien stated Weber Basin Water has recently expanded an incentive program for waterwise
  landscaping for front and side yards. While Ogden currently participates in the park strip incentive
  program, the ability to participate in the program for on-site waterwise landscaping would require
  some ordinances to be amended to meet their standards. Staff desires input from the Planning
  Commission to determine whether such standards should be adopted City-wide before drafting
  ordinance language. He reviewed terminology, defining waterwise plants, drought-tolerant plants,
  xeriscape and zero-scape, stating waterwise landscaping is desirable in that it allows drought-
  resistant plants to be utilized and groups plantings into similar water need areas.

  There has been some success in the City’s participation in the “Flip Your Strip” program, and current
  ordinances define the City’s regulation relating to the use of the public right-of-way. He stated cities
  desiring to participate in the lawn exchange program are required to adopt the stricter guidelines of
  Weber Basin Water District which would apply city-wide. He reviewed existing regulations compared
  to the standards which would allow participation in the program for front and side yards. One area of
  concern is that areas less than 8’ in width are not allowed to be in lawn and due to the small lot size
  and limited side yard setbacks, this would limit the ability to have lawn on some lots. As there is
  currently no requirement, this may be difficult to enforce throughout the City. Weber Basin also would
  restrict front and side landscaped areas to be in lawn for single-family residential lots to 35%, and if
  lawn is replaced with waterwise landscaping, it may not return to lawn in the future. Because Ogden
  is mostly built out, there are few single-family homes being developed and the benefit is limited. The
  current ordinance limits new development for commercial to 15% and multi-family to 35% while
  Weber Basin’s standards would limit both to 15%, with an exception for active recreation areas.

  He indicated Staff has identified some pros and cons for participation in the program, and indicated
  once adopted by Ordinance, it becomes a question of whether participation is voluntary or not. While
                                                                                            11 | P a g e
  the use of waterwise plantings is desirable, it is often more difficult to maintain than mowing lawn
  and can become unsightly over time and contribute to blight. There is concern about enforceability
  of these and concern that new homeowners may not be made aware of the deed restriction requiring
  the waterwise planting to remain. The current standard for parkstrips in Ogden City is that plants
  reach 50% coverage at full growth while the Weber Basin standard would allow 35% coverage in one
  year. Waterwise landscaping may reduce the cost of water, but removal of lawn increases the cost of
  heating and cooling. The cost of waterwise plantings also may exceed the cost of lawn. There also
  is some concern about spending City funds on work being done on private property rather than the
  public right-of-way as well as having to enforce a regulation of a different entity other than Ogden
  City. Staff feels the existing landscaping standards for front and side yards could be updated to
  include better waterwise options and landscape design as part of the ongoing unified development
  code update without the mandatory restrictions of Weber Basin Water District. He expressed
  concern that the ordinance would be effective city-wide and while those participating in the program
  may receive a financial incentive, it would be difficult to enforce the same standards for others who
  elect to not participate. He also expressed concern that the agreement is perpetual for future
  homeowners at a point when it has diminished in its benefit and becomes difficult to maintain. He
  stated while the Staff supports the “Flip your Strip” program, it is a benefit to the public right-of-way
  and owned by Ogden City, while the front yard incentive creates a concern that public funds are
  available for the improvement of private property.

  Commissioner Safsten most existing “waterwise” landscaping is actually zero-scape, as residents
  remove grass and throw down rock onto dirt which quickly becomes unsightly as weeds are more
  difficult to pull as their roots are under the heavy rocks. He felt this contributes to neighborhood
  blight and reduced home value. He also expressed concern about a different entity telling the City
  what its regulations should be and felt the City itself should determine what is best for the
  community. Chair Sandau asked if the requirements would be only for those desiring to participate in
  the incentive program or whether it would apply to all residents. Mr. Rypien responded the ordinance
  amendment would be applicable City-wide, but existing residents could opt whether or not they chose
  to or not. Chair Sandau expressed concern about maintenance, stating many residents do not care
  for lawn, and waterwise landscaping could create more work. Mr. Rypien indicated an existing lawn
  must be in good condition in order to participate in the incentive program, so those that are unkempt
  may not qualify. Commissioner Graf felt education is more beneficial for homeowners than financial
  incentives, indicating the incentive would not compensate for the cost of either a landscape architect
  or installation costs, and may not be enough to cover the cost of materials. Mr. Rypien indicated a
  landscape plan would be required to be approved, which includes both plant material and watering
  systems. Commissioner Southwick expressed concern with the limited amount of grass which would
  be allowed, particularly for existing small lots as well as the deed restriction, stating a new
  homeowner should not be required to be bound by a previous owner’s decision, particularly when that
  previous owner had received a financial benefit which is not available to the new homeowner. He felt
  existing standards are adequate and felt State regulations would become more targeted at reducing
  the cost of water for agricultural uses than for homeowners. Commission continued to discuss other
  water saving options such as improved infrastructure and continued education as well as
  establishing regulations for institutional uses. It was expressed turf is not always a bad thing.
  Commissioner Blaisdell indicated front yard play area is often important for families or for those with
  special needs or wheelchairs. Commissioner Graf felt the City should establish its own standards for
  front-yard landscaping rather than having Weber Basin dictate what can and cannot occur. It was felt
  there is a consensus to not pursue the amendments to allow participation in the Weber Basin front
  yard incentive program.
