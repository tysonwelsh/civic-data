        1.2      Mayor’s Report

       Mayor Kristie Overson explained that she had attended a ribbon-cutting at Feast Box. She
said it was a great place for meat lovers to dine.

The mayor had the opportunity to participate at Splish Splash Story Time at the Taylorsville
Recreation Center where she was able to read to children in the shallow end of the swimming pool.

Attendance was increasing each week at Starry Nights on the Plaza. Mayor Overson expressed her
appreciation to Councilmember Harker and the Parks and Recreation committee as well as Mr.
Harrington who handled the audio/video equipment for the event.

The City-wide cleanup event was very successful, as always. The mayor thanked Councilmember
Burgess, code enforcement, the police department and the Taylorsville High football team and
coaching staff for their help.

Earlier in the day Mayor Overson had joined ChamberWest for a golf event at Stonebridge Golf
Club. There were 192 golfers, and it was a golden opportunity to meet with community and
business leaders.

She recently attended a UFA board meeting wherein fireworks were discussed. She reminded
everyone on which dates they could be sold and discharged. UFAs website had an interactive map
where residents could see where certain personal fireworks were allowed.

The Plaza+Art committee had chosen new pieces which would soon replace those currently on
display at Centennial Plaza. She encouraged anyone who hadn’t yet seen the 14 current sculptures
to take a walk along the plaza and view them before they were replaced by the new pieces.

5.      PLANNING MATTERS

        5.1      Ordinance No. 23-08 – An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville approving a
                 Zoning Text Amendment to Taylorsville Municipal Code Chapter 13.25 related to
                 updates to water efficient landscaping standards – Terryne Bergeson

        Planner Terryne Bergeson explained the subject matter of the proposed ordinance and
indicated it was a result of the city’s desire to support water conservation efforts and also to help
residents take advantage of reimbursement programs administered by the state. Some changes
proposed in the ordinance were that lawn would be prohibited in new park strips and that if
irrigation was installed in park strips, it needed to be a drip system rather than a traditional sprinkler
system.

There were also some proposed changes for commercial properties. Specifically, landscaped area
of commercial buildings was not to exceed 20% lawn cover and there was to be no lawn in parking
lots.

Councilmember Harker wished to confirm that park strips of new homes could no longer have
grass in them. Ms. Bergeson confirmed that was correct but pointed out that existing park strips
with grass were grandfathered in.

Councilmember Cochran expressed concern about an inconsistency in language where some
portions of the ordinance used the word “encouraged” as opposed to “required.” Mr. Harper
Taylorsville City Council Minutes
June 21, 2023
Page 8



explained that for residents to be eligible for state reimbursement, the ordinance language had to
match that of the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District.

Councilmember Barbieri expressed concern that the ordinance seemed to encourage removal of
trees and other foliage from park strips which reduced shade and increased heat which resulted in
a need for more water rather than less. Therefore, she questioned whether the proposed ordinance
would truly result in water conservation.

Councilmember Cochran asked Mr. Cowdell to weigh in on the proposed ordinance and its effect
on property owners’ rights.

Mr. Cowdell responded that as policy makers, the Council had to balance individual civil liberties
with the collective good of the community. He described the proposed ordinance as “aspirational”
and said it was more of a carrot than a stick. It did not require that anyone change their park strip,
but if someone did decide to do so, it provided standards.

Councilmember Barbieri stated that she was not interested in codifying something that was
unenforceable.

MOTION:          Councilmember Barbieri moved to deny Ordinance No. 23-08.

The motion died for lack of a second.
