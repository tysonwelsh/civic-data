4. Petition to Vacate a portion of 3rd Street between Porter and Jefferson
  Ms. Hale indicated the request is to vacate 31’ on the north side of the street and dedicating 25’ back to
  Ogden City on the south side of the street. The Commission is to determine there is good cause for a
  street vacation, that the public or no person would be materially injured by the request and the request
  is consistent with the General Plan. She reviewed the history of the property, stating the vacant church
  property was recently rezoned to R-1-5 while the remnant parcels across the street remain in the R-1-6
  zone. The owner desires the vacation in order to develop additional lots on the vacant property. The
  area proposed to be vacated has been used for parking in front of the previous church at this location.
  The south side consists of an irrigation canal and trees. This section of 3rd Street is narrow, with an off-
  set intersection at Jefferson. If the street were to be approved, the intersection at Jefferson would still
  be off-set, and the intersection with Porter Avenue would become off-set where it now is straight. Staff
  is concerned with street connectivity and traffic circulation for both vehicles and pedestrians. The
  proposal would reduce the available right-of-way and would also cause a residential driveway to be
  located on the southeast corner of 3rd and Porter, while the current standard is a minimum of 50’ from
  an intersection. The Lynn Canal is on the property being vacated, at the southwest corner of Jefferson
  and 3rd Street and would need to be piped or relocated.
  She noted while her initial recommendation had been for denial, the applicant and his engineers have
  since met with City engineers and are working towards a solution. Staff recommendation is to continue
  the item to allow the applicant to work with the City Engineer and find an alternative which would satisfy
  City standards. It was noted the residential driveway could be relocated.
  Mr. Steve Diamond stated he has revised his request to acquire the same amount of property being
  vacated. He felt the proposed vacation would retain the existing pavement width and would not affect
  the existing canal, stating the canal is not in the public right-of-way but is on private property. He felt
  the issues could be worked out within a few days and expressed concern about the timing of the next
  meeting. He intends on adding curb and asphalt on the south side to complete the construction of the
  street as part of his project.
  Mr. Richins stated the existing right-of-way width should not be reduced, there may be a need for
  additional right-of-way in the future. He stated while there are some design constraints, it would be
  helpful if both intersections could line up. Although he is unaware of the existing pavement width, he
  felt the pavement width should not be reduced in favor of development.
  Commissioner Safsten expressed concern asking if the discussion is beyond the authority of the
  Planning Commission and should be worked out with professional engineers and the applicant. He
  suggested a recommendation for approval could be given at this time and allow the applicant to work
  with Engineering as the project progresses. Commissioner Blaisdell asked if the canal should be
  covered. It was indicated anything done to the canal would require approval from Lynn Irrigation.
  Commission expressed this section of third street appears to get little traffic and it was felt there is little
  potential of its needing to be widened in the future. Concern was expressed about creating obstacles
  for developers.,
  Ms. Hale indicated this review is only for the street vacation and the applicant also would be required to
  subdivide the property.
  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Southwick to continue this item until the
                September 6th meeting. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed
                unanimously, with Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten, Schade, Shale,

5. Request for Encroachment Permit, for Wonder Block, 2557 Grant Avenue.
  Mr. Mitch Vance stated in developing a mixed use project for the Wonder Block area, the Make Ogden
  plan was carefully studied. The building design proposed pulls the buildings to the street, but because
  of the massing of the building, it had been suggested awnings be added. He stated there are no insets
  or popouts in the design due to the limited size of residential units. Due to the mass of the building,
  they are now proposing to add balconies on the corner building only, with other buildings having Juliet
  style balconies which would not encroach over the right-of-way. He stated there is no required
  setback from the property line, but if buildings are built to the property line, the ability for balconies is
  lost. He felt as these are above the right-of-way, they would not interfere with pedestrian traffic or with
  trees to be in the right-of-way area. The floors of the balconies are proposed to be grated, and are on
  the west side of the building, so there should be little likelihood of ice build-up.
  Mr. Simpson stated both the subdivision and site plan for these buildings were previously approved.
  The applicant now desires to provide balconies on the west side of the corner building which is
  proposed to be located on the property line. The Commission is to consider whether there is good
  cause when reviewing an application for an encroachment permit, as well as whether it could interfere
  with the use of public land. The applicant is requesting an encroachment of 4’8” over the public right-
  of-way, and it would be above the minimum height of 15’. While it would not interfere with pedestrians
  walking on the street, there is concern about public safety as items may fall from the balconies. He
  reviewed the recently adopted downtown ordinances indicating while buildings are to be built close to
  property lines, it favors inset balconies and expects new buildings to be designed into the building and
  not require an encroachment. The provision for balconies on existing buildings limits their
  encroachment to 2’ over the public right-of-way. This request would exceed that provision as well.
  While Staff agrees the appearance of the building is improved with balconies, the determination that a
  loss of floor space inside the building does not warrant good cause for an encroachment. Staff feels
  the balconies could be designed to be incorporated into the building without an encroachment and
  recommends denial. He stated the request also has been reviewed by Engineering and Legal
  departments and both are recommending denial citing potential damage to the City infrastructure, trees
  and pedestrians using the walkway.
  Commissioner Graf noted the City should not violate the recently adopted downtown ordinance and
  asked if the building could be constructed in a way that both improves the appearance and satisfies the
  code. Mr. Simpson responded, stating while the site plan shows the location of the building on the
  property line, the interior space could be designed to allow recesses with balcony space, which would
  improve the appearance of the building. He felt the Juliet balconies appear to be non-functional, but
  expressed concern the ordinance should be respected and was concerned about the potential of
  unlimited balcony space above the right-of-way. Chair Sandau expressed she dislikes walking under
  balconies which encroach over the sidewalk and asked about the width of the sidewalk. Mr. Vance
  answered the sidewalk width is 10’ and felt there would be adequate walking space without walking
  under the balcony area. Commissioner Southwick expressed concern that the code is a challenge in
  that it allows zero setback from the front property line, yet it does not allow for balconies over the right-
  of-way. He also expressed concern that if they are allowed to be added to existing buildings, the same
  standard should exist for new construction. Commissioner Shinoda stated balconies are allowed so
  long as they are designed into the building. He felt the distance of the proposed overhang makes
  almost 5’ of the sidewalk useless during winter months.
  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to recommend denial of the request for
                encroachment based on the findings there is no need other than economic benefit of the
                developer and the encroachment could be detrimental to the City, suggesting the
                balconies be designed so as to not overhang the right-of-way. Motion was seconded by
                Commissioner Safsten and passed 7-2 with Commissioners Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten,
                Schade, Shale, Shinoda and Sandau voting aye and Commissioners Aaberg and
                Southwick voting no. Commissioner Aaberg stated he felt the balconies improve the
                appearance of the building and felt inset balconies may have the same safety concern of
                items falling. Commissioner Southwick felt the applicant made their case that there is just
                cause, and the aesthetics of the building is improved with the balconies.

6. Downtown Site Plan with Exceptions, Lotus City Walk, 2261 Grant Avenue
  Ms. Allie Platt, representing Lotus, indicated they are proposing a mixed use project on vacant
  commercial space west of the Junction, consisting of multi-family residential and commercial uses.
  The project would contain a 13,000 square foot clubhouse, commercial and office space on the ground
  floor as well as live/work units. They are requesting a reduction of the commercial space requirement
  on the ground floor in favor of residential units as well as the requirement of 50% minimum lot
  coverage.
  Mr. Simpson stated the property is in the C-ENT zone which is an important part of the urban core. He
  reviewed the existing use as well as the proposed use, indicating much of the surface parking will be
  eliminated and a parking structure would accommodate needed parking for the residential units,
  providing 1.19 spaces per unit. He reviewed each of the proposed buildings stating the building
  material of brick and glass fits with the character of the district. The first request for exception to the
  Ordinance is for residential live/work units on the ground floor at a 32’ depth rather than 60’ as required
  by code. The minimum commercial space on the main level is 1500 square feet where 1000 is
  proposed. He stated balconies are shown at 10-11’ above ground level where 15’ is required and
  suggested the height of the ground level should be raised to meet that standard. The use of a
  restaurant as an anchor and pickleball courts are appropriate uses on the ground floor. The applicant
  also is proposing three residential units on the ground floor facing 22nd Street. He noted surrounding
  uses on this side are apartments and schools and felt three units on this side would be appropriate.
  He reviewed the intent of the C-ENT zone, stating it is intended to be a destination zone where people
  come and interact with others. The use of commercial space is necessary to meet that intent, and
  retail, restaurant or other public uses are vital to its success. He felt live/work could be allowed so long
  as there is space available for the sale of their goods or services. He stated the base of buildings in
  the downtown need to make a statement of presence with a minimum of 15’ in height.
  Staff recommends the Planning Commission approve granting exceptions for the following as allowed
  by the C-ENT code:
  1. To allow three residential units fronting 22nd Street only at ground level to blend with existing
       residential and school uses;
  2. To have live/work units along Lincoln Avenue with a commercial ground level space with a depth
       of no less than 32’ and an area square footage of no less than 1,000 square feet; and
  3. To have approximately 29% building coverage of the lot.

        5. Screening utilities and dumpsters from public and surrounding property’s view, screening
            structures being compatible with the design, materials, and colors of the buildings on the site.
        6. Meeting reflective glass requirements.
        7. Establishing a financial guarantee with the city for site improvements.
        8. Balconies meeting the minimum 15’ clearance from ground level.
        9. Having no residential uses on the ground levels of the proposed buildings along 23rd Street

7. Public Hearing, Proposed Rezone, from R-3EC to MU property at 543-549 25th Street
  Mr. Simpson explained this is a Staff-initiated follow-up from the previous meeting where surrounding
  property was proposed to be rezoned as MU. Upon review of that petition, Staff noted these two
  properties would become isolated as R-3EC. Because the existing office uses are now nonconforming
  but would be legal in the MU zone, Staff felt it would be appropriate for them to also be rezoned as MU,
  although they would not become a part of the development agreement for the other properties.
  Because legal notification had not been given at the previous meeting to include these two addresses,
  Staff recommended they be noticed and rezoned to MU at that time. As the notification now has
  occurred, Staff is recommending they be rezoned to MU in order to not become isolated and to not
  create spot zoning as well as making them more compliant with the zoning regulations.
  Mr. Travis Pate, 2546 Jefferson indicated he had not been notified of either this rezone request or the
  previous petition considered in the July meeting. He stated the Adams Avenue CRA had been
  approved by the City Council but this project does not comply with its expectations. He stated the CRA
  directs infill use as single-family detached housing as a high priority. It also limits multi-family to be
  along the main street frontage and existing buildings should be occupied with their original use so far
  as possible. He stated the proposed MU zone is adjacent to the Jefferson Historic District and the
  proposed three-story townhomes are not compatible with the surrounding community and would be out
  of context with current uses. He was concerned the use would remove existing homes which have
  been there since the 1800’s. He suggested the item be continued until the next meeting and allow him
  to visit with Staff. Chair Sandau reminded him the action for today’s meeting is consideration only of
  the two homes, and not the overall project. She also informed him the decision of the Commission is
  only a recommendation to the City Council and he would have an opportunity to address them at that
  time.
  Ms. Debra Darrington, 2555 Jefferson noted these buildings have architectural detail that is beautiful
  and creates pride in the community. She was concerned the historic features become lost when
  surrounded by new modern structures and asked the historic structures be respected as development
  occurs.
  Chair Sandau asked Mr. Simpson to address the future use of the two buildings proposed to be
  rezoned. Mr. Simpson stated the Maguire Duplex is recognized as a local historic resource and has
  been on the National Register of Historic Places since 1982. The adjacent building appears to have
  been built as a single-family home but there is no research regarding its historic status. Both buildings
  now are being used as offices and have nonconforming certificates for that use. The proposed MU
  zone would eliminate the need for a nonconforming use certificate as an office use is permitted in that
  zone. If the use as office space were discontinued, there is potential they could be redeveloped as
  single-family homes as a residential component of the overall MU zone.
As there were no additional comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Schade to close the public
hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Graf and passed unanimously.
  It was again noted the item would be referred to the City Council for final action. The rezoning of these
  individual parcels would conform with the Community Policies of not isolating properties and be
  forwarded with the previous action but would not become part of the development agreement. It
  appears most of those in attendance are opposed to the townhome use rather than the rezoning of
  these properties, and they would have an opportunity to address that use when considered by the City
  Council. Commission felt the proposed action would allow both the present uses to continue, while
  allowing future use as residential or continued as office space.
  MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to recommend approval of the rezoning of
               these two properties based on the findings the proposed rezone will conform with the
               General Plan and the Adams Avenue Redevelopment Plan. Motion was seconded by
               Commissioner Schade and passed 8-1 with Commissioners Aaberg, Blaisdell, Grad,
               Safsten, Schade, Shinoda, Southwick and Sandau voting aye and Commissioner Shale
               voting no. Commissioner Shale stated as she had voted opposing the MU zoning at the
               previous meeting, this vote was consistent with that action.

8. Public Hearing, Proposed Zoning Ordinance Amendment, M-1 and M-2 zone permitted and conditional
   uses.
  Mr. Brierley indicated he had been asked to review uses allowed in the manufacturing zones to
  determine whether they were adding value to Ogden City. He reviewed the locations of M-1 and M-2
  zones, typically between the railroad tracks and Wall Avenue and reviewed the purpose of each zone.
These are typically beneficial to the City in that they provide employment, generate taxes and support
existing businesses and citizens. However, some use provide little employment and generate little tax
value and can cause damage to soils which are difficult to mitigate when another use is desired. After
reviewing all permitted and conditional uses in the manufacturing zones, Staff has prepared the
following recommendations:
  Junk and salvage yards       These have low tax value, low employment and can create
                                environmental impacts. Staff feels there are enough existing facilities
                                that no new uses should be allowed.
  RV Storage                   Currently allowed so long as there is no building. While these create
                                low tax revenue, they provide a service and can serve as a place-
                                keeper for future industrial uses. Staff suggests these continue to be
                                allowed but to also permit the construction of an on-site office building.
  Manufactured Home Display             These are currently not allowed in either M-1 or M-2 zones. Staff
                                         suggests they be a permitted use in the M-1 zone.
  Outdoor manufacturing         Staff suggests buffering be increased to protect adjacent uses.
  Personal self-storage         These provide low taxes, generate little revenue to the economy and
                                 have low employment. While they provide a service to the community,
                                 the industry standard is 10-13 square feet per capita, and as existing
                                 uses provide 11 square feet per capita, Staff feels future use should be
                                 restricted. Staff is recommending units may be added to existing
                                 facilities as an infill, or allowed on upper levels in other buildings, but
                                 no new locations should be allowed, unless removing an existing
                                 nonconforming location and locating in a manufacturing zone.
                                 However, with each census, the amount will be identified according to
                                 the population which may allow some future use.
  Gymnasiums/dance studio Should be prohibited in industrial zones due to potential conflict with
                                 industrial equipment.
  Adult live entertainment      Should be allowed only in the M-2 zone.
  Go-cart tracks                Due to the amount of noise generated, these are appropriate in the
                                 manufacturing zones.
He stated Staff also has reviewed development standards for buildings in these zones and is making
additional recommendations relating to site and buildings.
     Eliminate the limited impervious surface as current storm water standards can address water
        runoff and storage.
     Eliminate required 20’ rear yard setback
     Create maximum height restriction at 90’
     Identify options for better screening and use of setbacks adjacent to residential uses, including
        solid wall, trees and shrubs, as well as restricting to no building openings adjacent to a
        residential use.
     Building material of a brick or concrete base to extend building durability and improve
        appearance.
     Establish some minimum architectural standards, but allow the owner to identify three of the
        eleven items listed.
     Any interior improvement would trigger site improvements in proportion to any upgrade.
  He presented ordinance language which would address the above concerns and recommended it be
  forwarded to the City Council with a recommendation for approval.
  Commissioner Schade asked if outdoor gun range might also be eliminated from these zones as
  they create no value to the community, there is no area for the use and the Commission is limited in
  its ability to impose conditions of approval when listed as a conditional use. Commission and Staff
  agreed the use could be eliminated.
  Mr. Travis Pate, 2546 Jefferson, expressed concern with allowing more impervious surface as it

9. Public Hearing, Proposed Zoning Ordinance Amendment, Group Dwellings
  Mr. Rypien stated the Commission has been reviewing the group dwelling ordinance over the past
  several months in response to a petition. At the previous hearing, the Commission had asked for some
  minor revisions. Staff is suggesting adding building material requirements for the first floor to be brick,
  stone, precast concrete or cement fiberboard. Architectural details have been discussed and Staff is
  suggesting adding to the list of options, including 30% brick or stone as ana architectural feature. The
  R-2 zone currently requires two building types when more than four acres, but as that zone is restricted
  to single-family and duplex uses, Staff is suggesting a building would fit that requirement if it has a
  height variation of eight feet or more, different roof style, different colors, siding materials or projections
  as well as a portico or entry feature. He stated some additional standards are proposed for
  development in all zones, including requiring one covered parking stall per unit at least 50% of the units
  to have functional balconies or a front or rear patio and that a boundary fence be required. Other
  architectural features are identified as options, allowing the developer to determine which features to
  implement. The list of outdoor amenities is proposed to be revised with the option of a swimming pool.
  Staff recommends approval of the ordinance amendments as proposed, along with the items
  previously discussed, and indicated the proposed ordinance language is included with his
  recommendation.
