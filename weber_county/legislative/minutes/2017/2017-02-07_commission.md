E. PRESENTATION ON THE WEBER COUNTY ATTORNEY'S OFFICE
   Chair Ebert stated that the County Commission was starting something new this month—the highlighting of a
   county department once a month as a way to inform our community about the offices, what services they
   provide, etc. Chris Allred, County Attorney, expressed thanks to his very hardworking staff and outlined the
   various services that they provide including prosecuting s every felony that occurs in the county, in addition
   to thousands of criminal cases annually. Historically, they have maintained one of the highest caseloads in
   the State. The office has great victim coordinators who help victims in the county to navigate the legal
   system, office investigators who are on call 24/7, robust civil staff and incredible secretarial staff. The
   commissioners expressed appreciation to this great group of professionals.

    3. RESOLUTION APPOINTING MEMBERS TO THE POWDER MOUNTAIN WATER AND SEWER DISTRICT –
       RESOLUTION 7-2017

        Commissioner Gibson stated that this is a small district with many second homes and the statute is
        somewhat difficult and requires serious discussion regarding who is eligible to serve. Several years ago
        the district was in major trouble and the commissioners were appointed on a temporary basis only. At the
        1/17/2017 Commission meeting, after having received applications, the commissioners made two
        appointments but subsequently the County Attorney’s Office believed they were ineligible under statute.
        The County Assessor is doing a comprehensive review of first and second homes. As of yesterday the
        Commissioners thought that three applicants were eligible but the Assessor had stated that at least one
        was listed as a second home owner with a Bountiful full-time residence. Because their terms have
        expired and need to be filled, Commissioner Gibson recommended reappointing the commissioners until
        further clarification, and the commissioners hope to move forward as quickly as possible to make this a
        more locally-based board.
        Commissioner Gibson moved to adopt Resolution 7-2017 reappointing Commissioner Gibson and
        Commissioner Ebert and appointing Commissioner Harvey to fill the three open seats on the Powder
        Mountain Water and Sewer District until the research is complete and they can move forward;
        Commissioner Harvey seconded.
        Commissioner Gibson – aye; Commissioner Harvey – aye; Chair Ebert – aye
        Commissioner Gibson was excused at this time.

H. PUBLIC COMMENTS (were regarding item G.3):
   Bob Behrendt, Powder Mountain Water & Sewer Improvement District Vice Chair, said that he had a clear
   understanding of the nomination process and was reiterating an email he had sent to the commissioners. He
   had recommended two of the applicants that were appointed two weeks ago. He said that there have been
   many qualified applicants and all but two either live in the district, own property in the district or are
   agents/owners of property in the district. The only two applicants that do not meet the criteria are
   commissioners Gibson and Harvey. There was a vigorous review on eligibility and additional review needs
   to continue because of the fluidity of those residents. He said that today there are absolutely two eligible
   applicants based on living full time in the District—Gary Urdang and Michael Moyal—who are highly
   qualified professionals. The assertion that there is lack of eligible applicants today is not true. He believes
   that now is the time to move forward to a more community-based board. The commissioners had saved this
   district and tremendous progress has been made under its guidance/support, but it is time for change to occur
   and allow the full time residents to serve—the commissioners have huge additional responsibilities and
   simply cannot fully participate to the needed level. He recommended that the Commission reconsider their
   motion on item G.3 and appoint the two eligible applicants. He also recommended a phased-exit strategy and
   to reappoint Commissioner Gibson, and later either of the other two commissioners, but not continue with
   three county commissioners because of interim confusion on eligibility. It is important to move in this
   direction so that there is no perception of wearing two hats, etc., which is one current concern for the
   residents.

    Brent Clyde, the referenced homeowner and board applicant who lives in Bountiful, concurred with Mr.
    Behrendt’s comments. He noted that water issues are very difficult in the Ogden Valley and referred to
    competing and common interests stating that it puts them at a disadvantage to weigh the board members
    towards the developers as well as to exclude full time homeowners in the District. The information he
    received is that full time homeowners were not considered for the seats but that two were considered that
