Planner Strader said the first part of the document discusses the uses.  Additionally,

language was used to establish the wording for an employee dwelling unit, but with 
modifications.  This includes living quarters within a portion of a main building, or an

Neighborhood Commercial zone.  It will not be found in any of the residential zones.

property and along with the non-residential use, but that use must be permitted in the 
zone district.  A resident would both work and live in the unit.   
 
Planner Strader said a use that has been identified is a bar/tavern.  Staff added a 
maximum square footage, including the patio space.  In total, it should not exceed 2,700 
square feet.  Staff came up with this number after comparing similar uses in SLC and 
other locations.  The intent is to limit the size so the use will fit with the Neighborhood 
Commercial zone.  They don’t want to draw people from outside the neighborhood.  
 
The existing zone states that no single structure can exceed 5,000 square feet in floor 
area.  They are proposing to remove that restriction, but add a maximum district size of 
seven acres.  Planner Strader stated there is an existing Neighborhood Commercial zone 
in the Silver Creek neighborhood which is seven acres.  Other areas were looked at, 
such as the Blue Roof.  These examples are where the seven acres came from.

A
CCESSORY APARTMENTS

neighborhood.  Staff is suggesting prohibiting the rental of Accessory Dwelling Units for 
less than 30 days.

has been removed.  A new requirement is that an accessory dwelling unit cannot be sold 
separately.   A single-family home cannot be condominiumized.

Planner Milliner said this is a combination of work space and living quarters.  In the 
past, an application was submitted to have living units above a work unit.  Because it 
didn’t fit well with the Code, there were some problems.  The idea at the time was to

Use zones.  Staff has done research and come up with the following criteria:  
1. The residential and the commercial space must be occupied by the same 
tenant.  No portion of the live/work unit may be rented or sold separately 
2. Residential areas must be located above or behind the commercial

Planner Milliner explained this is to ensure the commercial unit faces the street

3. The commercial component shown on the floor plan approved through the 
Low Impact Permit shall remain commercial and cannot be converted to 
residential use.

commercial use. 
5. The commercial component shall be restricted to the unit and shall not be 
conducted in the yard, garage, or any accessory structure. 
6. External access for the commercial component shall be oriented to the street 
and should have at least on external entrance/exit separate from the living 
space.   
7. The entrance to the business component shall be located on the ground level. 
8. The live/work unit shall be required to provide parking in accordance with 
Section 10-4-of this title. 
9. No more than one employee (excluding residents of the dwelling unit) shall 
work or report to work on the premises.
