North Lakeshore Area Study Conclusion - Shawn Eliot, MAG The North Lakeshore Area Study reviewed traffic and commute patterns within the south Lehi and east lakeshore of Utah Lake. Freeway volumes are projected in the area and current infrastructure including Pioneer Crossing and I-15 cannot handle future demand. The study used a new UDOT process called Solutions Development looking at a holistic picture of the needs and wants of the region rather than just transportation need. Public and stakeholder input was used to gauge the importance of land use, transportation, the natural environment, safety, and community. This and further input were used to formulate different scenarios to review. The final story map report and projections was reviewed at the meeting and can be found at https://mountainland.org/northlakeshore/. Further work will determine which corridors and policies to implement. The study recommended that Pioneer Crossing and Vineyard Connector evolve into a grade separated freeway system, Pony Express PKWY be completed as a minor arterial, and transit projects already identified in the regional transportation plan be implemented. Mayor Johnson stated that there are elements of the study he does not support. He expressed concern of increasing lanes as there are already three freeways intersecting Lehi. He is willing to have a conversation with MAG to discuss the matter further. TIP Modification: Spanish Fork Center St. Funding Transfer – Bob Allen, MAG The Center Street Widening Project was selected in 2016 and was awarded $5,171,000 in County Funds. The project widened Center St to 5 lanes from 900 E to US6 and modified the intersection at 1150 E. Due to scope adjustments approved by TAC and RPC and unforeseen ROW requirements, the project cost $2,082,274 more than the original estimate. The US6 Pedestrian Crossing Project was selected in 2018 and was awarded $1,790,435. The project intended to build a pedestrian undercrossing at US6 to allow students at the nearby school and other pedestrians to cross US6. UDOT requested jurisdiction of the project and cost estimates have now risen to $6.5m. Since the project was selected, Nebo School District has modified school boundaries, no longer requiring students to cross the busy intersection. Due to the increased costs and reduced demand for the undercrossing, Spanish Fork is requesting that the US6 Undercrossing Project be eliminated and the existing MPO Regional Planning Committee August 5, 2021 Page 4 $1,790,435 be transferred to the Center Street Widening Project to cover some of the cost overruns. Modifications will be made at 1430E to improve pedestrian safety and connectivity. Not part of this project. MAG Staff recommend US6 Undercrossing be eliminated from the TIP and $1,790,435 be transferred to the Center Street Widening Project. MPO Regional Planning Committee August 5, 2021 Page 5 Utah Valley Express Bus Rapid Transit No Fare Solution Update – Andrew Jackson, MAG In 2018, Utah County enacted the 4th Quarter Cent Sales Tax (4thQ) that allocated a portion of the revenues to UTA with the condition that there be an interlocal agreement identifying how UTA would spend it. An overview of the requirements from the interlocal agreement were discussed. 1. Start making bond payments 2. Pay for operations and maintenance 3. Pay back past bond payments 4. Pay back past operations and maintenance 5. Pay back interest charges 6. Reserve account to pay bond early 7. New service level agreement Andrew Jackson presented a section of the interlocal agreement that states ”UTA shall apply additional Fourth Quarter Transit Revenues (to the extent available) to pay actual, documented O&M Costs incurred by UTA with respect to the BRT System.” To date UTA is doing items one through four and working on item five. The past three years of payments should be paid back within a year. In addition, UTA has taken over the associated costs and will have some reserve accounts to pay the bond off early and start new service. An analysis was done by UTA to determine the actual cost of operating the UVX BRT route. To determine the comparative cost of the prior baseline service, UTA used comparable service platform hours paired with 2019 Timpanogos business unit actual costs. The total adjusted cost is $3.6m annually. Due to the increase in the 4thQ revenue, Andrew Jackson stated that there is enough money to pay for operations and maintenance completely, in addition to the other requirements in the interlocal agreement. Additionally, 80% of the fares on UVX are paid by BYU and UVU. The process of collecting the remaining 20% will cost approximately $600k a year if a fee is put in place and it will cost approximately $1.2m to install the system to collect fees. It will take two years to complete the system which will be obsolete by the time it is finished. The cost to collect the fares and the reduction in ridership must be balanced. Concerns were expressed that having a no fare route might hinder the progress on the South County FrontRunner. Shawn Seager explained that MAG is working with UTA to get the South County FrontRunner in a position to be shovel ready when funding from the Federal Infrastructure Bill becomes available. MPO Regional Planning Committee August 5, 2021 Page 6 Utah County Refinance of 2012 Transportation Sales Tax Bonds – Shawn Eliot, MAG Information was presented on the proposed refunding of the 2012 Transportation Sales Tax Revenue Bonds. The current bond of $52m was issued in 2012 for 27 years. About $2.5m has been paid each year at a 4% interest rate. The bond was issued to fund several large transportation projects in the region. Utah County wants to refinance the bond at a 2.5% interest rates opposed to 4%. They presented different scenarios at the MPO Finance Committee meeting the day before that included: 1. Keep the remaining 19 years on the current bond and refinance at 2.5% interest. 2. Reduce repayment by 2.5 years off loan and try to pay it off sooner. 3. Use 3rd quarter cent sales tax allocated to build selected projects to pay down the bond. Mayor Johnson recommended that Utah County refinance the bond at a lower interest rate and not put additional funds or contributions toward the bond. Mayor Child seconded the recommendation.
