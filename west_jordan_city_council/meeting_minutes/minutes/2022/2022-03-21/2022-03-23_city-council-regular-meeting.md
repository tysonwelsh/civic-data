A.     Current and Future Utility Infrastructure Discussion
Public Works Utility Manager Greg Davenport showed the existing West Jordan City water system
on a map, and explained that 90% of West Jordan’s culinary water was purchased from Jordan
Valley Water Conservancy District, with water delivered to high pressure areas (west hillside)
costing more than lower pressure areas. Mr. Davenport stated that all recent water line breaks had
been in water lines that were over 50 years old. Responding to a question from Vice Chair Green,
Mr. Davenport said the City currently had four active wells. He explained the wells that produced
the most water in West Jordan were connected to an aquifer contaminated by Kennecott mining.
Mr. Davenport said the City had more water rights than were currently used, but needed to avoid
drawing the contamination with increased use.

Staff explained that the new master plan specified which water lines were 50 years or older, and
included a maintenance plan. Mr. Davenport said the new master plan included an average daily
water use of 650 gallons for one West Jordan single-family and apartment residence. Vice Chair
Green suggested the master plan numbers should be adjusted to account for anticipated ADUs that
would result from House Bill 82. Mr. Davenport responded that 650 gallons was a number given to
the City by the State. Council Member Jacob suggested the average number of people per household
would be the number that would matter. City Attorney Rob Wall added that the number assigned
by the State was also required to be used in an impact fee analysis. The City would need to financially
provide for any additional anticipated impact. Mr. Davenport said the 650 number would change
over time, and explained the calculations used.

Mr. Davenport explained Equivalent Residential Unit (ERU) calculations used for different types of
uses. He said the master plan anticipated how and where the City would need ERU capacity in the
future. Council Office Director Alan Anderson said staff were working to refine processes to be able
to provide the Council with a public facilities report along with rezone requests. Mr. Davenport

   Mr. Davenport showed the existing West Jordan sewer system on a map, and spoke of the need for
   ongoing maintenance and replacement of older pipes. He answered questions about existing sewer
   trunk lines. Vice Chair Green expressed concern about the 7800 South sewer line and future

   planned development. Mr. Davenport emphasized that sewer system capacity should be considered
   with any request to increase density. Mr. Anderson commented that both water and sewer systems
   were built to handle peak demand.

   Mr. Davenport outlined the current Sanitary Sewer Projects 5-Year Plan: 1300 West Sewer Project;
   New Bingham Highway Sewer Project; and 9000 South Sewer Project. He showed the existing West

   Project; Constitution Park; and New Bingham Highway Detention Park. Mr. Davenport explained
   the State requirement that every new development keep the first half inch of water on site.
   Responding to a question from Council Member McConnehey, Mr. Davenport stated the Salt Lake
   Mosquito Abatement District would help with mosquito control in areas of standing water if they

 5.    EXECUTIVE REPORTS TO COUNCIL
       a. Mayor’s Report
Mayor Dirk Burton reported the housing shortage affecting the nation was also affecting West
Jordan City. He expressed the opinion the City was doing a good job increasing the number of units
available, and spoke of amenities in West Jordan that contributed to a friendly environment and
sense of community. Mayor Burton commented that additional residential units and amenities
resulted in increased needs, and spoke of employment opportunities currently open with West
Jordan City. Mayor Burton provided the Council with magnets that included emergency and non-
emergency contact numbers for City departments.

       b. City Administrator’s Report
Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck spoke of compensation letters sent to West Jordan
employees. Public Works Director Brian Clegg updated the Council regarding a U-111 construction
project. Public Services Director Isaac Astill spoke of an upcoming Easter event on April 16th, and
welcomed Council participation. He provided an update on the City Hall remodel project. Mr. Astill
reported on improvements to a local dog park.

Council Member Pack commented that the weekly staff report reported zero positive COVID-19
cases and asked to what that could be attributed. City Administrator Korban Lee said he attributed
the decline to the general community-wide decline and increased vaccination rates.

 6.    PUBLIC HEARINGS
       a. Copper Hills Baseball Fee Waiver Request
Public Services Director Isaac Astill oriented the Council with a proposed Service in Lieu of Fees
Agreement for the use of Ron Wood Baseball Complex and Veterans Memorial Park Baseball
Complex by Copper Hills Baseball League for 2022 and 2023 Spring Seasons and End of Season
Tournaments. He noted the League was dedicated to providing value with service.

Robert Dawson, President of the Copper Hills Baseball League, said the League had supported the
West Jordan community for over 20 years, and spoke of several service projects participated in by
the League. Mr. Dawson explained the desire to invest in two batting cages for players over the next
two years. Council Member Worthen thanked Mr. Dawson and his group for giving back to the
community.

Chair Whitelock opened a public hearing at 7:40 pm

Citizen Comments:
None

Chair Whitelock closed the public hearing at 7:40 pm

Responding to a question from Vice Chair Green, City Attorney Rob Wall said he believed the
application and information included in the packet met statutory requirements and the intent of the
law. Vice Chair Green said he felt a 10-82 study verifying that requirements were met needed to be
clear and not buried in the agreement in the future.

Council Member McConnehey thanked the League for all they did for the community. He expressed
the opinion that the value of the benefit anticipated to be provided was somewhat low. Vice Chair
Green asked how the anticipated benefit value was calculated, and asked who assessed the market
value of volunteer time. Mr. Astill responded the valuation was agreed on as fair value between City
staff and the League for installation of the batting cages. Documentation of League volunteer time
and valuation was explained. Vice Chair Green suggested such documentation should be included
with a 10-82 study in the packet. Mr. Wall said he agreed with the suggestion to include a clear 10-
82 study in the future.
