Community Development Director Patrick Putt said that a packet was sent out that 
inadvertently omitted the suggestions provided by Commissioner Sonntag; therefore, 
he distributed a hard copy in case they had not printed the emailed copy.     
 
Director Putt reminded the Commission the current Code prohibits someone with a lot 
of record to build if the lot consists of slopes greater than 30% or if the structure would 
project into the County skyline.  The only remedy is to seek a special exception from the 
County Council at a cost of $1,000.   
 
Director Putt said a few weeks ago, Staff brought language from the Snyderville Basin 
Planning District.  Some adjustments to the language were suggested.  He referred to 
the last page of the Staff Report.  He said that under the current proposal, the ridgeline 
criteria would not apply within 1/3 mile of the residence.  This will address the issue of a 
structure skylining when viewed close up.     
 
Commissioner Ure asked where the 1/3 mile comes from.  Was the determining factor 
the views from SR 32?  Director Putt said it had less to do with the state of the County 
roads, but took into account the dimension of close proximity to the house.  He added 
that there could be a different measurement such as ½ mile or ¼ mile.  Commissioner 
Ure said he would rather see it at ½ mile because that it is an even number.    
 
Commissioner Clyde said while he appreciates the concept, he sees a problems with 
the numbers.  The “glass house” is probably ¼ mile from I-80 and is very visible.  It is 
still visible from 1½ miles away.  With this language the glass house would be 
permitted.  Director Putt said a home could be built on the lot, but it would not 
necessarily be the same one.

Commissioner Clyde asked if this language would also apply to future subdivisions as 
well.  Would this allow a developer to put houses on a ridgeline?  He doesn’t want to 
permit ridgelining in future subdivisions.   
 
A discussion ensued about the houses on the ridgeline as one approaches Rockport 
Reservoir.  Commissioner Sonntag noted these would be subject to the exception.  The 
landowner would be subject to the standards found in Section 11-4-16.  Commissioner 
Clyde said this would be true for an existing subdivision, but not for a new subdivision.  
Commissioner Ure said there should be no reason to allow ridgelining in a new 
subdivision.   
 
Commissioner Ure noted the “bull’s eye” is 1.5 miles wide.  He guessed the North 
Summit corridor is only 1.5 miles wide from ridgeline to ridgeline.  He asked if they 
should add language that states no new subdivision can break the skyline.  
Commissioner Clyde agreed that new subdivisions should not be allowed on 
ridgelines.   
 
Director Putt responded that one way to approach this may be to have the exception 
only apply to subdivisions and legal lots of record in existence at the date of the action 
of this ordinance.  Commissioner Clyde said his complaint is that the ridgeline 
definition becomes null after 1½ mile for a new subdivision.   
 
Commissioner Sonntag suggested they remove Letter C from the language.  He said if 
someone has a lot of record, they should be able to build a house even if there is not a 
location that wouldn’t skyline.  Commissioner Clyde agreed, but he doesn’t believe 
that removing Letter C would solve the problem.

Commissioner Henrie said they are talking about current and future subdivisions.  
The language they are considering seems to apply to existing lots.  Commissioner 
Clyde said Letters A, B, and C apply to both.     
 
Commissioner Sonntag asked Director Putt to further explain Staff’s recommendation.  
Director Putt said the basic premise is that if someone is close to a house, the house will 
skyline.  Staff chose to not hold skylining views accountable within 1/3 of a mile, but it 
could be more or less.  Outside of the inner circle, if a person would be able to clearly 
see the roofline of the building, it would be deemed as a structure that skylines.  Staff 
chose the outer circle to be from 1/3 to 1½ miles.     
 
Director Putt said that Staff debated at what point the structure becomes so small on 
the horizon, that there really is no visual intrusion.  Commissioner Ure said there is a 
large house in Promontory that is visible from SR 32.  If it wasn’t so large, it wouldn’t be 
seen.  Director Putt said the dimension could be greater if the Commission desires.  Staff 
felt that sphere, in conjunction with the existing criteria, would be a reasonable remedy 
to offset any visual problem.   
 
Commissioner Clyde said that for existing lots, he has no problem with what has been 
recommended.  Letter H needs to be modified to prevent new subdivisions from 
skylining.  Commissioner Sonntag responded they could designate that the exceptions 
only apply to existing lots of record, not new subdivisions.                       
 
Commissioner Henrie said that some subdivisions will skyline at different viewpoints, 
but not at others.  Administrator Barnes said Staff will look to see if a house skylines 
from any road within the targeted allowance.  If it does skyline, they would turn to the 
criteria.  If there are no options, the LIP process kicks into place.  He said from 3-4 miles 
a house will probably not be visible.

Commissioner Clyde recommended Letter H reads as follows:  
Ridgelines: Development shall not be placed on any hillside or Ridgeline in 
a manner that causes any portion of a structure to extend into the skyline 
as viewed from public roadways.   
He recommended the remainder of the sentence be deleted.  The exceptions would be 
for existing lots.  He said another question is if Criteria A-C is needed.  Commissioner 
Sonntag said he likes A-C because it gives a safe harbor for planning purposes.   
 
Commissioner Clyde said the “glass house” could still be built if this language is there.  
Commissioner Ure recommended this be moved to the exceptions that follow Letter H.  
It would help to spell it out.  Commissioner Clyde said he is okay with it being listed in 
the exceptions.  If a structure falls under the Criteria A-C, they would have to go through 
the low impact permit (LIP).  Commissioner Sonntag said these shouldn’t be 
conditions to the exception. 
 
Commissioner Henrie verified that if two houses are built ½ a mile apart, the bulls-eye 
moves with the houses.  One house may ridgeline and the other one may not.  
Commissioner Sonntag said if the house is in the green zone, an exception would be 
needed.  This would be determined by Criteria A-C.   
 
Different possible wording of the language was discussed.  Commissioner Clyde 
recommended the following: “If an existing lot meets Criteria A-C, it is not considered to

should be changed to “structure.”   
 
Commissioner Henrie said the language needs to say that the green zone would not

a house could still be built but it could only be 26 feet high and the owner may have to

Commissioner Sonntag said he can think of places where a subdivision could be built

new building pads from ridgelining from current roads.  Commissioner Ure said he

Director Putt said sometimes a person, or a group, can over think a problem.  He said 
the language Staff first brought to them from the Snyderville Basin says they can’t build 
on 30% or steeper slopes.  They can’t build a structure that projects into the skyline as 
viewed from a public road unless they go through a low impact permit and a reasonable 
location is established and the house is built to the designated standards.   
Director Putt said since that has been put into place, he can only think of one home that 
has been constructed that created any significant neighborhood controversy.  This was 
not the glass house.  This language has worked very well for the Snyderville Basin.  
Sometimes the simple solution is the best.   The language does not prohibit a landowner 
from building a home, only that a reasonable location would be found.  The height 
would be dropped to 26 feet.  They would make sure it is not made of shiny material.  
The LIP creates a case-by-case review of where the reasonable location to build would 
be.  There is always the ability to appeal the LIP decision to a higher authority.
