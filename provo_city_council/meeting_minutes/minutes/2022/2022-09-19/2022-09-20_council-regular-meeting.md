Public Comment (12:22)

Chair Handley read the public comment preamble and opened the public comment period.

Eric Speckhard, of Provo, asked council members to support the townhome construction project on 300
South. He said in the area there are several apartment buildings being constructed but this project
would allow for more permanent residents.

Wayne Leavitt, of Provo, requested council to create a Citizen’s Diversity and Inclusion board. He
discussed ways it would strengthen trust by having adequate representation for all citizens.

Sam Oman, of Provo, suggested council have the agenda printed for members of the public who come
to the meetings. He also asked council to look at owner occupied housing.

Robin Roberts, of Provo, encouraged more business ownership in Provo to give other business owners a
tax break. He suggested the council find ways to help small businesses get started.

With no other public comments, Chair Handley closed the public comment period.

Judge Vernon Romney presented. He shared the impacts that the pandemic brought on the functions of
the Justice Court. He explained the benefits of the Justice Court to hold live, in person hearings. He also
shared the statistics from fiscal year ending June 30, 2022.

Councilor MacKay asked if the Justice Court is behind due to the pandemic.

Judge Romney explained that due to the decision to hold live hearings, they have been able to catch up
and are now current with cases.

Councilor Hoban thanked Judge Romney for his presentation.

Councilor Ellsworth asked if there is anything that the council could do to show more support to the
Justice Court.

Judge Romney said there is nothing else that can be done and that they feel supported. He invited
members of the council to stop by to visit.

Brandon Larsen, Planner, presented the report. He shared that the staff feels confident in this strategy.

Chair Handley opened public comment.

Jeff Hammond, of Provo, said the city needs to make ADU’s more legal to assist with moderate income
housing.

Sharon Memmott, of Provo, asked various questions.

Chair Handley closed public comment and brought discussion back to council.

Councilor Ellsworth said she sees this as a report instead of a strategy to implement. She stated there is
room for growth.

Councilor Shipley stated he believes this is a way for the state to allocate money to cities who are
actively trying to improve housing. He also discussed how the interest rates can affect affordable
housing.

Councilor Fillmore asked Mr. Larsen if he felt like there has been adequate public input.

Mr. Larsen shared this went through the General Plan process, which is the most rigorous when it comes
to public feedback. He said there had been some updates, but the main points were the same.

Hannah Salzl, Planner, shared the items that were revised or added.

Councilor Fillmore asked if Mr. Larsen agreed with Councilor Ellsworth’s statements of this being more
of a report.

Mr. Larsen said he does believe the beginning of the document is reporting statistics but that the end
document is a high-level plan.

Councilor MacKay suggested that we call for a vote.

Chair Handley called for a vote.

      Vote:        The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Hoban,
                   MacKay, Shipley, and Whipple in favor.

Chair Handley addressed Sharon Memmott’s questions.

Councilor Whipple shared that owner occupied housing is addressed in the report.

     Motion:      An implied motion to approve an Ordinance, as currently constituted, has been made

Bill Peperone, Director of Development Services, presented Agenda Items 4, 5, and 6 together as they
are all related. He addressed concerns of traffic, soil contamination, trail access, parking, and space.

Jason White, Project Representative, shared proposed site plan and discussed vision for project.

Councilor MacKay asked about if the trails will have public access.

Mr. White shared their intention is to improve the trails and still allow for public access.

Councilor MacKay also asked what the percentage of open space would be and how many bedrooms
would be built.

Mr. White responded that there would be two-, three-, and four-bedroom options. He explained the
intention of the horizontal mixed-use design. He also discussed the reason to rent the townhomes vs.
sell them.

Councilor Ellsworth shared concerns of the trail and asked if the site plan could include a trail that
follows the river more.

Mr. White answered.

Councilor MacKay stated she would like to see the trail access included in a development agreement.

Councilor Whipple asked if there is a public easement or right of use on the trail.

Mr. White responded.

Councilor Whipple also asked about the road design close to the bridge on the west side.

Councilor Ellsworth clarified her question about the trail access.

Chair Handley asked how long the trail is. He also shared his support for the project if there was a
development agreement which includes public access to the trail.

Mr. White discussed another option that had been proposed. He said they are open to whatever
solution that makes the most sense.

Jim Ivory, Development Lead of Foulger-Pratt, reviewed the staff requirements around the dedication of
an easement. He also explained the parkway that will be designed, and the trail being upgraded to
current city standards.

Chair Handley opened public comment.

Dave Horn, of Provo, shared some concerns that he did not feel like were addressed. He said he does
not believe there is enough green space and requested a clubhouse.

Ronald Wooley, part owner of adjacent building, said he recently learned of this proposal and does not
feel like the interests of the business owners are being considered. He shared his desire to leave zone as
commercial.

Sharon Memmott, of Provo, suggested adding a light where the future road would be. She also
suggested adding a small fence to prevent small children from entering the river.

Matt Heaton, owner of adjacent building, requested a continuation of this item so surrounding business
owners could be prepared to weigh in.

Jeff Hammond, of Provo, discussed his likes for the plan.

Dan Heaton, General Counsel of Ace Real Estate Holdings, shared concerns of other business owners in
the area. He also requested a continuation of this item.

Tim Heaton, of Ace Real Estate Holdings, discussed coal ash and suggested the city request a phase 1
and phase 2 site assessment. He added that the travel study conducted by UDOT did not consider the
surrounding building’s levels of occupancy when they are at max capacity.

Hans Magleby, business owner and tenant of Scenic View Academy, shared his concern about open
space. He also requested a continuation.

Kevin Curtis, Chief Operating Officer of Heritage Schools, shared they are in support if some minor
modifications could be made. He explained the concerns that they have regarding the shared boundary
line.

Barry Webster, of Qualtrics, shared reasons why Qualtrics is in support. He also said he is the Vice
President of the River Woods Office Business Park Association.

Sam Oman, of Provo, shared his opinion that this project is like Vineyard and said he does not believe
that is the model of what we want Provo to be.

Chair Handley closed public comment and brought discussion back to council.

Councilor Shipley voiced his opinion to continue the item until the next meeting. He emphasized that
the land is going to be developed and that this project would be a good fit to add more housing and
commercial space.

Councilor Fillmore shared his support for continuing and provided explanation.

Councilor Ellsworth asked Mr. Peperone if there are efforts made to notify surrounding businesses. After
confirmation from Mr. Peperone that businesses within 500 feet would have to be notified, she
explained how business owners are notified. She shared her support for the project but stated she
would like to see a development agreement.
