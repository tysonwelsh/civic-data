Final Land Use Authority: Summit County Council 
 
Proposal 
The subject Parcel, NS-852, is a Conforming Parcel that is not part of a recorded Subdivision Plat. 
It is located in the Agriculture-20 (AG-20) Zoning District. The property is currently vacant and is 
relatively flat. No development will occur on slopes greater than thirty percent (30%). Access to 
the property is from South Echo Frontage Road. 
 
The proposed Use of Guest Lodge is listed as a Conditional Use in Development Code 11-3-16. 
The applicant, Kelvin Judd, is requesting approval of a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) for the Use 
of Guest Lodge to be located on Parcel NS -852. Concurrent with the CUP application, the 
Commission will review a Master Planned Development (MPD) due to the Commercial Use having 
more than twenty thousand (20,000) square feet of floor area (see Development Code 11-4-12).  
 
The written description of the proposal is attached as Exhibit A. The description states that the 
proposed Guest Lodge can accommodate up to 34 guests and will include a private indoor riding 
8
2 
 
arena with horse stalls, tiny cabins along the river, a small chapel , and a greenhouse . The 
description also states that there will be recreation amenities available for guests. Additionally, 
it states that the lodge will be staffed 24/7 with more seasonal intensive use during spring -fall. 
They anticipate 15-25 total employees with 7-10 employees on-site daily. 
 
More specifically, the proposal includes: 
 
• A lodge (approximately 28,076 square feet) including: 
o 17 guest rooms 
o Two (2) Spas 
o Three (3) Laundry facilities 
o Living space 
o Flex space 
o Therapy rooms 
o Library 
o Two (2) Lounges 
o Dining Area 
o Kitchen 
o Reception Area 
o Offices 
o Staff Break room 
o Garage 
o Indoor Sports Court 
• A chapel (approximately 603 square feet) 
• Ten (10) cabins (approximately 1,088 square feet each – total of 10,880 sf) 
• Private indoor riding arena (approximately 20,000 square feet) 
• Green house (approximately 400 square feet) 
 
The total proposed floor area including all buildings is approximately 60,000 square feet. 
 
Background 
In February 2024, the County received a Master Planned Development (MPD)/Conditional Use 
Permit (CUP) for a Recovery Center. On April 4, 2024, the Planning Commission held a work 
session to discuss the Applicant’s original proposal for a Recovery Center. Based on the 
discussion, the Applicant chose to modify the application. Staff met with the Applicant again 
and discussed the proposed uses on the site. The Community Development Director (CDD) 
made the determination (per Eastern Summit County Development Code 11-7-11) that the 
proposal would be considered a Guest Lodge (see definition below). 
 
GUEST RANCH OR LODGE: A commercial use consisting of recreational activities that 
may include, but are not limited to, horseback riding, fishing, hunting, skiing and 
snowmobiling. The guest ranch may include overnight lodging, food service, meeting and 
conference facilities as well as other uses.  
9
3 
 
 
Based on the CDD Determination, the Applicant submitted a revised application on April 29, 
2024 requesting a Conditional Use Permit for a Guest Lodge. The Applicant and Staff had 
several meetings to discuss what information would be required prior to the first Public Hearing 
with the Planning Commission. The project was stalled mainly due to the requirements for 
water and sewer outlined in Section 11-2-3.  
 
Issues for Consideration  
The Eastern Summit County Development Code Section 11-3-16 identifies a Guest Ranch or 
Lodge as a Conditional Use in the Agriculture-20 (AG-20) Zone. Due to the size of commercial 
use exceeding 20,000 square feet, Development Code 11-4-12 requires a Master Planned 
Development (MPD). 
 
Staff has included a list of applicable Code Sections below: 
• Development Evaluation Standards (11-2) 
• Agriculture-20 Standards (11-3-5) 
• Lot/Parcel Requirements for Development, Subdivision, and/or Uses (11-4-2) 
• Final Site Plan Standards (11-4-6) 
• Conditional Use Permit Standards (11-4-7) 
• Master Planned Development Standards (11-4-12) 
o The MPD review also requires compliance with the Infrastructure Standards 
outlined in Development Code 11-6-8 
 
The Applicant will provide a presentation on the proposed Use and will be available for any 
questions.

Re: Water Source Availability for Hope River Ranch 
 
Dear Mr. Judd: 
 
HAL has investigated the hydrogeology in the vicinity of the Hope River Ranch parcel. We have 
evaluated well driller’s reports for three wells that were drilled on the property and reviewed this 
information with the contractor who drilled the wells.  We have also reviewed the “Geologic map 
of the Salt Lake City 30’ x 60’ quadrangle, north-central Utah, and Uinta County, Wyoming” 
prepared for the U.S. Geological Survey (Map I-1944) by Bruce Bryant and D.J. Nichols. 
 
Based on our evaluation, the most viable source of water for the facility is a relatively shallow 
alluvial aquifer with a thickness of about 50 to 55 feet below the Hope River Ranch parcel.  The 
bedrock below the alluvium is the Henefer Formation which consists primarily of clay and siltstone 
which does not produce much, if any, water.  However, the shallow alluvium consists of 
unconsolidated gravels and sands which have been demonstrated to produce up to about 85 
gpm.  Static water level in the alluvial aquifer is less than 10 feet below ground surface. 
 
Due to the shallow nature of the aquifer, it is not possible to install a 100-foot grout seal as required 
by the Division of Drinking Water (DDW) to classify the well as a drinking water well. Therefore, 
a well completed into the alluvial aquifer would be considered by DDW to be “under the direct 
influence of surface water." This water can still be used in a public drinking water system if it is 
treated to LT2 requirements for ground water under the direct influence of surface water. In other 
similar cases, we have helped small public drinking water systems to implement a Harmsco 
Municipal Pleated Microglass Cartridge filtration system that meets the LT2 requirements. These 
filters are relatively inexpensive and are easy to maintain. 
 
HAL recommends drilling a small diameter (6” or 8”) well to a depth of 50 to 60 feet with screens 
set in the well to capture water from the sands and gravels in the bottom 10 to 15 feet of the 
alluvial aquifer.  We also recommend installation of a LT2 compliant cartridge filtration system to 
meet DDW requirements. We are confident that the recommended well and treatment system will 
yield sufficient water quantity and quality to meet the drinking water requirements of the Hope 
River Ranch drinking water system. 
 
Thank you for the opportunity to work with you.  Please let me know if you have any questions.
