5:00 p.m. - WORK SESSION

Mayor Silvestrini welcomed everyone to the work session. He said John Miller, Salt Lake
County Associate Director of Public Works Engineering, would address the Council first due to
time constraints (Item 9.1).

John Miller discussed the proposed Wasatch Front Regional Council (WRFC) grant projects. He
explained the Regional Transportation Plan Map, that can be found on WRFC’s website,
showing the routes that need to be improved in the coming years. There are 4 grant programs;
Surface Transportation (STP), TAP, CMAC, and TLC. Engineering has met with the Mayor and
the City Manager and they have identified 3 priorities for projects in the City. 1) STP funding -
the 3900 S. corridor from I-215 on the west side to the east side of the valley is in poor shape. 5
cities share 3900 S.; Millcreek, Holladay, South Salt Lake, Taylorsville, and West Valley. The 5
different mayors who border this road would be included on the proposal. Mayor Silvestrini
explained how this project has evolved and that he approached Senator Wayne Harper about the
project and he is in support of it as a joint effort. Mayor Silvestrini asked John Miller to put
together a letter of intent for the other city mayors to sign. The Mayor expressed concern that
there might be damage to the 3900 S. bridge from the previous fire, the City is waiting on a
report of the structural damage. The bridge deck may need to be replaced in addition to
resurfacing. 2) STP funding — when using the Operation Condition Index (OCT) for the roads on
the plan, 1300 E. between 3300 S. - 3900 S. also needs to be addressed. It’s scheduled for a mill
and overlay next year but it may need a full reconstruction. 3) TAP funding — last year the 3300

$300,000. The funding from WRFC is phased.

Council Member Marchant asked if the reconstruction of a road included sidewalk and gutters.

1. Continued Discussion Regarding the Proposed Amendments to the M-1 and M-2 zones
and the Proposed M zone

The Mayor said there will be breakout groups for discussion based on topics. He hopes to have
the conversation today, accept written comments and suggestions for a week and the Council
will take them into consideration, and then they will give the planning staff direction to move
forward.

John Janson, Planner, said the 4 topics for discussion are:

Table 1 — “Other” miscellaneous topics with John Janson
Table 2 — Uses with Kurt Hansen

Table 3 — Non-conforming Uses with John Brem

Table 4 — Google views of property with Francis Lilly

3 twenty-minute sessions took place.
Reconvened at 6:18 p.m.

The Mayor attended two table sessions, and he observed that the definition of light
manufacturing is too vague and there is a concern about the storage concept for non-conforming
uses. An option to help with storage is not to limit the amount of storage but require a visual
barrier. Another issue is that some structures would be non-conforming uses because of the
height or the use. He suggested adding language that said replacement of existing improvements
is okay.

Table Reports:
1 — Janson said they talked about the difference between cement and concrete. There was an

idea that as residential construction comes in, that notes get added to the plat that there are
neighboring industrial uses. He said that with Code 19.66.050 outside storage rotation, citizens
want to better distinguish active equipment versus junk.

2 — Hansen said questions came up about railroad spurs. Some of the businesses use railroad cars
and they need clarification. One concern was brought up about a fertilizer business and that the
ordinance should add mixing and blending to the definition of light manufacturing. The
Chairman of the Planning Commission, Tom Stephens, said instead of updating the M Zone
ordinance, that if a new business comes in whose use is currently not allowed, that the City has
the option to rezone the specific property in a spot zone.

3 — Brem heard from the citizens that they don’t want a rezone or any more subsidized
residential housing. If citizens have a legal use and the zones changes, the property becomes a
non-conforming use, so conceptually citizens can increase size of the non-conforming use by
25% if certain improvements are made. He brought up adding a definition of non-conforming
use and what uses make it non-conforming. Also, a concept like requiring rotation of inventory
may help solve the storage problem.

Council Member Uipi asked about the rotation on inventory; whose responsibility is it and who
will enforce it. Brem said that these are just ideas. He also suggested changing the definition of
warehousing to allow inside and outside as long as there is a visual barrier.

4 — Lilly said businesses wanted to know how the ordinance would impact their property. The
issue that become evident with two businesses was the height of structures. The ordinance does
allow for the replacement of the non-complying structure. Also, when property expansion
occurs, at wondered at what point would the mitigation measures that the ordinance identified be
triggered.

Council Member Catten said that the city should send letters to business owners in these districts
stating that they are in compliance with the zone for assurance purposes.

The Mayor invited the public to send in written comments on the ordinance, so the Council can
evaluate them. The topic will then come back for discussion and a public hearing. If any citizens
want to get notice of when that will be, please let the City know. The Mayor thanked the public
for coming to participate in the discussion.

4.5 Report by the Unified Fire Authority

Battalion Chief Dwayne Woolsey discussed the call volume trends with the three stations
in Millcreek; 101, 112, 106. There were 222 calls in August at Station 101 and it is
trending up. Station 112 had 80 calls. Station 106’s call volume is trending down. Falls
are the most common medical call. Fire alarm calls are the most common fire calls that
they get, but few are actual fires. Tours have taken place at Station 101. He reported the
outreach activities for each station. Some engines were sent out to California to assist in
fire suppression through mutual aid agreements. Unified Fire Authority Wildland Bureau
has billed $2.2 million at this point and it will be closer to $2.5 million when the season is
over. They will retain $3-400k fund balance for next year. They did fill the Director of
Communications position. Fire safety week just ended, and it is one of the longest
running public health observances. The Chief said the downward trend in calls could be
due to great public outreach, but it is more likely just a trend that will change. They have
recently purchased a database program.

Council Member Marchant asked how many UFA firefighters went to California; 10 on
engines and 1 in a supervisory role.

9.2 Discussion and Consideration of Resolution No. 17-77, a Resolution of the
Millcreek Council Approving a Tax Increase for Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement
Service Area (SLVLESA)

Andrew Keddington, Assistant Finance Administrator, presented the certified tax rate to
the Council. In August, the SLVLESA Board unanimously approved of the notice of
intent to increase the tax rate by 5%. He explained that there will be no impact on
taxpayers in Millcreek City, but the legislative body of each city must approve it as part
of the Truth in Taxation process. The increase will provide for 3 goals; financial stability,
structurally balance the budget, and dedicate new revenues to new resources. The tax
increase will increase revenue by $702,000.

The Truth in Taxation hearing will be held in June. The notice now is for the intent to
increase the tax. The Mayor recommended approval of the resolution to enable the Board
to be able to increase taxes. Keddington has already visited townships and they have
voted in favor of the resolution.

Council Member Marchant moved to approve item 9.2. Council Member Jackson
seconded the motion. Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. The motion passed
unanimously.
