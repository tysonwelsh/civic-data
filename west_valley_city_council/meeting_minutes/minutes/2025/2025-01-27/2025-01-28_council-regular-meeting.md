PUBLIC COMMENT PERIOD
Barry Gittleman, President of Hamlet Homes, addressed the council regarding the
company's ongoing residential developments in West Valley City. He noted that Hamlet
Homes has been building in and around Salt Lake County for 30 years, completing over
80 subdivisions. Currently, the company is constructing 58 homes in Weston Field on 4100
South in District 4. Despite significant interest, with over 3,000 visitors touring a model
home during the Salt Lake Parade of Homes, sales in 2024 have been slow, with only three
homes sold. Mr. Gittleman attributed this challenge to the shifting housing market and
rising mortgage rates since the project's approval in 2021. He requested the opportunity to
meet with city staff to discuss minor modifications to the existing development agreement,
specifically revisiting three sentences in the 13-page document. He emphasized that these
changes would not be substantial but would allow the company to adapt to current market
conditions while maintaining high-quality home construction. He referenced the city’s
mission of "Unity, Pride, and Progress" and reiterated Hamlet Homes’ commitment to
building high-quality homes while addressing affordability concerns.

Mike Markham thanked everyone for being here today.

Kirk Lowe addressed the council to reiterate concerns regarding access to tee times at the
city golf course. He noted that despite a reduction in the number of allocated passes this
year, the issue persists, and many believe the root cause is not the number of passes but
rather the scheduling system. Mr. Lowe pointed out that league players, including the
Men’s League, Ladies League, and Veterans League, are allowed to book tee times two
weeks in advance, whereas the general public can only book one week ahead. Additionally,
league play during the day further limits availability for non-league golfers. With the golf
   MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING JANUARY 28, 2025
                          -3-

course set to reopen next month, he urged the council to review the current booking policies
to ensure fairer access for all golfers. He concluded by thanking the council for their time
and consideration of the issue.

Gayle Clark addressed the council to discuss concerns regarding the recently enacted city
regulations requiring all owners of non-owner-occupied properties to obtain a West Valley
City business license and have the properties designated as rentals. She explained that she
has owned a property at 3379 Hillsdale for nearly 30 years, which has been a family home
for her disabled older brother, his wife, and children. For the past two years, she has
complied with the city's business license requirement, despite the fact that the property had
never before been considered a rental or business. Ms. Clark detailed how she and her
younger brother purchased the property in 1995 to provide a home for their family
members, and no rent has ever been charged. She has maintained the property, paid taxes
and insurance, and even established an LLC to address issues with a difficult neighbor.
However, with the recent changes to the city’s regulations, her family home is now
categorized as a rental property, resulting in unwanted solicitations and pressure to sell the
property to real estate ventures.While she acknowledged that there may have been issues
with property management abuses in the city, Clark expressed frustration that the city's
regulations, originally intended to address such problems, have been applied to her
legitimate family support situation. She urged the council to reconsider how the regulations
affect genuine family-owned properties like hers.
