body is to pesticides and that there were only a few foods that she can eat. She said she has asked for
help many times but the spraying still goes on. She asked the council to make Nephi a better place to
live.

abatement ordinance draft. She thanked Councilor Memmott for standing up for the people. She felt

JOHNSON PROPERTY ANNEXATION AREA LEVEL CHANGE:

  Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that Alex Johnson owns some property south of the city and
that he would like to be part of the annexation request that has been discussed previously with some
other property owners adjacent to the Johnson property. The Johnson property is currently a Level 3
area in the annexation growth area map. Mr. Johnson has requested that the property be changed to a
Level 2 area. Councilor Memmott asked the property owner, Mr. Johnson, who was at the meeting if his
intentions was to use the property for industrial purposes. Mr. Johnson answered yes. Councilor Seely
asked Mr. Johnson if in the future he would share with the council a more detailed plan for the property.
Mr. Johnson agreed to do that. Councilor Jones moved to change the Johnson property from a Level 3
to a Level 2 in annexation growth area map. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed
on a unanimous vote.

ANNEXATION AREA BOUNDARY CHANGED:

   Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that several businesses near the city’s current annexation area
boundary have expressed interest in being annexed to receive city services. The first step for these
businesses has been undertaken. They have approached the Planning Commission and requested that
Nephi City’s annexation area boundary be expanded to include these properties and to be designated as
Level 3 found in the city’s annexation policy plan. The City Council still has authority to allow any
properties to change from a Level 3 to a Level 2 or 1 and also has authority to allow annexation and
designate any zone upon annexation.
   He further explained that the requests from businesses are separated geographic. The southern
request has originated from two sources, the city and a group of private businesses. The city owns a
property south and west of the parcel owned by Owens Corning. It was purchased as an opportunity to
build a second industrial park. One business has already expressed interest in moving their facility into
this future industrial park and state economic development funds may be available to expand utilities
into this parcel. A group of private businesses have also expressed interest in annexing into the city near
the Old Pinery Road. These businesses are already in existence and use some city utilities. They would
like to expand and also use the city’s gas, electric, and water utilities. With the expansion of the city’s
water system to the water tank on the southern end, it has become very feasible to supply utilities to
these companies. Not only would they supply ongoing utility revenue for the city, but they would be
required to pay impact fees for the infrastructure upgrades completed by the city. The increase to the
city’s infrastructure to maintain would be marginal. The main reason for expanding the city’s
infrastructure was to allow for commercial/industrial growth of this kind. When the Planning
Commission examined the request, they recommended that the whole area between the properties
requesting inclusion be part of the area expansion.
  Mr. Atkinson stated that the geographical request on the north end of the city is different from the
requests on the south in the sense that it will be a much longer extension of some of the city’s utilities.
However, the reasoning is the same as the area on the south end. With the construction of the north
side substation, the National Guard water line constructed, and a gas line near the new water line, the
city is in a position to look into the possibility of providing utilities to this area. A hard look at the utility
extensions will be necessary to determine if the city has the resources to maintain those extensions into
the future. Similar to the south properties requesting inclusion into the annexation area boundary, the
properties to the north are in the process of being constructed or are already in existence. This means
that the businesses will be paying utility fees to some entity. If that entity is the city, it would be helpful
for future utility cash flow. They would also be paying into the city’s General Fund with property taxes
and potentially sales taxes to assist with providing general city services. It is a given that at some point,
these businesses will make use of some of the city’s general services (i.e. roads, police services, etc.).
   Councilor Worwood said he would like to consider the north and south requests separately. He said
he is in favor of the south area expansion request, but the north request needed much more analysis.

NUISANCE AND ABATEMENT ORDINANCE 07-21-2020 ADOPTED:

   Councilor Memmott explained that the council and staff had taken a considerable amount of time
working on the proposed Nuisance and Abatement Ordinance. The council and staff had considered
property rights of all people. He stated that they had worked on finding the middle of the road solution
to nuisances that infringe on others’ rights while a the same time protecting the land-owners’ rights.
Councilor Memmott moved to adopt Ordinance 07-21-2020 AN ORDINANCE AMENDING CHAPTER 2
TITLE 4 OF THE NEPHI CITY CODE, PROVIDING FOR THE REGULATION AND ABATEMENT OF PUBLIC
NUISANCES WITHIN NEPHI CITY. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed on a
unanimous vote.

IRRIGATON LINE AGREEMENT APPROVED:

    Mr. Atkinson updated the council about the property near the Nephi City freeway exit and across
from the Flying J easement issue with a private land corporation called South Nephi, LLC and Nephi
South Water Conservancy Company. There was a dispute over the existence of a prescriptive easement
for the irrigation line has been resolved by installing a new line and abandoning the old line. Nephi
South Water Conservancy Company has performed the labor to install the new pipeline, the city has
drawn up the easements necessary to place the new pipeline and all parties have agreed to abandoning
the old line. Mr. Atkinson presented an agreement that accomplished the abandonment of the line as
well as indemnifying all parties from future lawsuits regarding the line and set forth the compensation
                                                                                             Page 3 of 4
amounts of each party. Councilor Jones moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the irrigation line
agreement. Councilor Memmott seconded the motion. The motion carried on a unanimous vote.
