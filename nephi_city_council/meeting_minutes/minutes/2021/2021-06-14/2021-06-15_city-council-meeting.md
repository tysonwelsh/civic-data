PUBLIC COMMENT:

    Bobby Luckau who is a resident of the Clothes Spin RV Park asked if it was the city or the landlord that
was making the residents of the RV park move.
   JR Johnson stated that ever since Bob Steele was mayor, the city has never enforced the 30-day limit
code at the RV park.
   Janet Johnson asked the council if they would consider adding a grandfather clause to the code to
allow the residents, which includes her sister, to remain at the RV park.
   Donald Ball stated that the city was pushing the landlord Alex Johnson to come into compliance. All
the tenants of the park had been there more than 30 days and would need to move or Mr. Johnson
would be found in violation of the code and would be fined.
  City Attorney Kasey Wright commented that all RV parks within the city are being treated the same
and those that are not in compliance would need to have the tenants move if they were living there
beyond the 30-days.
  City Recorder Lisa Brough said that the Frosty Freeze and High-Country RV parks have 10 permanent
spots in each facility that had been grandfathered in long ago. She stated that Mr. Alex Johnson’ family
had been made aware of the code when they were issued a business license.
 Janet Johnson asked, “Are you going to consider grandfathering or enforcing?

BRYCE’S LANDING COMMERCIAL SUBDIVISION FINAL PLAT TABLED:

   City Administrator Seth Atkinson described to the council Bryce’s Landing commercial subdivision that
is just south of the hospital and north of Custom Signworks. The development will have six commercial
pads and had been conditionally approved by the Planning Commission based on their plans showing
items the Development Review Committee had recommended as well as access approval from UDOT.
Mr. Atkinson reported that the developers had not completed the items that the Planning Commission
had required.
   Councilor Memmott moved to table the Bryce’s Landing Subdivision approval until the developers
completed the required items. Councilor Seely seconded the motion. The motion passed on a
unanimous vote.

WINN RIDGE QUIT CLAIM DEED APPROVED:

  Councilor Worwood moved to approve a quit claim deed needed from the city for the Winn Ridge
Subdivision development to move forward. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed
on a unanimous vote.

CERTIFIED PROPERTY TAX RATE ADOPTED:

  The council reviewed the certified property tax rate that has been proposed by Juab County.
Councilor Memmott moved to adopt the proposed certified tax rate of .000863. Councilor Worwood
seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

FIRE RESTRICTION RESOLUTION 6-15-2021 ADOPTED:

   Mr. Atkinson presented Resolution 6-15-2021 that strongly recommends that no fireworks are used n
the area between the 1-15 freeway and the foothills and prohibits the use of fireworks on any
municipally owned property to the east of I-15. Councilor Worwood moved to adopt Resolution 6-15-
2021. Councilor Memmott seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

WORK RELEASE FROM SUNRISE ENGINEERING ON WATER RIGHTS SERVICES AUTHORIZED:

   Mr. Atkinson updated the council that Sunrise Engineering water rights specialist engineer has been
helping the City proof up and secure existing water rights at the state level with the Division of Water
Rights. He has successfully negotiated a change application with a portion of the city’s water rights. This
change moved some of the water rights from one source point to the Worwood well.
   Mr. Atkinson stated that the time has come to engage in a more involved process to secure the city’s
water rights and to ask for a change application on the seasonal nature of the city’s water rights. This
would allow for increased access of the water the city has rights to, regardless of the flow rate. The
change application process is lengthy and involves hearings at the state, and passage through the state’s
Division of Water Rights. Successful completion of this process will allow the city to require water rights
of new annexations to keep the city’s 40-year plan as a migrating plan.
   He presented a contract to assist the city with this process. The cost is estimated to be $42,828 over
a two-year time frame. This amount does not include the cost of a water right’s attorney. It is
recommended that an attorney specializing in water rights be retained for this process as well.
   Councilor Ostler moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the work release for Sunrise Engineering to
begin work on the water rights process. Councilor Seely seconded the motion. The motion passed on a
unanimous vote.
