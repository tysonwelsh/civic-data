 B.    Art Center Discussion
Council Office Director Alan R. Anderson stated Council Members Whitelock and Worthen
requested a discussion regarding the Art Center. Mr. Anderson shared history of the planned art
center. Council Member Worthen said she did not believe the currently planned art center was a
smart idea economically and proposed reviving discussions regarding a recreation center on the
west side of the City, with an art center incorporated in the same facility. Council Member Whitelock
said the School District was converting a school in West Jordan into an art school, and said she was
asked if the City would want to be involved. Council Member Whitelock suggested the art school
could serve as a temporary art center location until a formal art center was completed.

Council Member Pack said he liked the idea of a recreation center/art center facility but pointed out
the location of the art center discussed was to be more centrally located, while residents on the west
side of the city had responded more favorably regarding a potential recreation center. Council
Member Whitelock said she agreed with Council Member Worthen regarding the need to be fiscally
responsible but commented that historically residents of the area valued the arts and had chosen to
build art facilities even when they had less economically.

Council Member Whitelock commented it would be the residents of West Jordan paying for the
proposed facility, whether through a City budget line item or through County property taxes. She
added that West Jordan would have more control over the facility if the funding were through the
City budget. Council Member Worthen asked if the Council were interested in Staff exploring the
possibility of a combined recreation center/art center facility.

City Administrator Korban Lee said the current art center project was construction-ready, with all
architectural drawings and necessary permits obtained. He said the County had indicated
willingness to help fast-track the project with renewed grant funding. Council Member Lamb said
City Council Work Session Minutes – July 28, 2021                                               Page 2

he had thought the project was moving forward and said he would be opposed to starting all over
again. Council Member Worthen said she was worried about having enough money for construction
and concerned the facility may run at a deficit.

Vice Chair Green said he believed exploring the joint facility option would send mixed messages to
the County. He estimated that a delay of the art center would put the facility out another 5-10 years.
He spoke in favor of the current planned location of the art center. Vice Chair Green said he
preferred the terms “community art center” or “community center” over “cultural art center”. He
emphasized the community needed a gathering place.

Council Member McConnehey said he was in favor of moving forward with the project as planned.
He expressed concern with proposed alignment of the facility when compared with the master plan
for the area and spoke of the benefit the facility could provide with appropriate planning.

Council Member Worthen said she wanted to make sure whatever moved forward did not put
additional strain on staff, did not put additional financial strain on the City, would be a benefit to
many, and would be able to support sponsorships. She questioned whether the planned facility
would be large enough and suggested the current planned location would not be ideal for
sponsorships. Vice Chair Green responded the questions she asked had been discussed by multiple
Councils over many years, and said he believed the project needed to move forward.

Council Member Worthen said she wanted to put a recreation center back on the table for discussion
whether or not combined with an art center. A majority of the Council indicated a desire to move
forward with the art center project as currently planned. Chair Jacob, Vice Chair Green, and Council
Member Whitelock expressed interest in renewing discussions regarding a recreation center on the
west side.

A.      Proposed Alcohol Ordinance Discussion
Vice Chair Green stated the City Alcohol Ordinance was out of date and spoke of proposed drafted
amendments. He said the proposed changes would improve economic development, consolidate the
licensing process, allow better communication between Code Enforcement and Zoning, and give the
City a better mechanism for dealing with violations.

Community Development Director Scott Langford commented that the streamlined Code would
dovetail nicely with the new software being implemented, and said he agreed the amended Code
would be beneficial for economic development. He said the proposed changes had been fully vetted.
Council Member Whitelock thanked Staff for their work. Mr. Anderson said the item was scheduled
for a public hearing at the August 25, 2021, meeting.

Senior Planner Ray McCandless oriented the Council with two proposed ordinances relating to The
Town Center at Copper Rim development. He explained changes made since last reviewed by the
Council in February of 2021. Proposed commercial in the development was increased from
approximately 10 acres to 23.1 acres. Proposed mixed-use was eliminated from the plan, and the
number of townhome units and single-family dwellings reduced, for a total reduction in the overall
dwelling units of 152. Mr. McCandless said the proposed plan met the City’s open space
requirement. Council Member McConnehey requested that staff reports be available to the public
with meeting agendas prior to Council meetings.

portion of commercial in the second phase, and remaining commercial in the final phase. Mr.
McCandless showed a table of proposed lot sizes, and shared potential residential design styles. He
stated utilities to adequately service the property were available.

proposed 23.1 acres of commercial would be a reduction.

maintained to mitigate safety/liability concerns. Mr. Gardner commented that permissions would
need to be received from property owners. Vice Chair Green suggested the portion of the street
owned by the City should be rehabilitated and clearly striped, with signs explaining the situation.

Member McConnehey pointed out the packet indicated the matter was not urgent.
