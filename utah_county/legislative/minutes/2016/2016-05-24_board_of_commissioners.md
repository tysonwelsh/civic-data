1.       DESIGNATE PAST DUE ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE AS UNCOLLECTIBLE, AND APPROVE
         AND AUTHORIZE THE AMOUNTS TO BE WRITTEN OFF
(Track A, 11:13)
Commissioner Lee asked Utah County Clerk/Auditor Bryan E. Thompson to address why these accounts are
uncollectible. Mr. Thompson explained that when the decision was made to use Everbridge for a county-wide
system, all cities were to participate. An automatic billing was sent out to the cities, but a couple of them already
had existing individual contracts with Everbridge that would overlap some of the time covered through the county
contract. Alpine City and Cedar Hills will be joining the county system once their current agreements have
terminated, since those two cities have already paid Everbridge directly for services received. Commissioner Lee
asked, “So we’ll be able to collect these in the future, is what you’re saying?,” and Mr. Thompson replied “yes.”
Commissioner Lee said, “So in this case, we paid this?,” and Commissioner Ellertson replied “yes.” Commissioner
Graves summarized Mr. Thompson’s comments, and Mr. Thompson confirmed the information. Mr. Thompson
also noted that even without payment from the two cities with concurrent contracts through Everbridge, the county
is still saving money over the previous system used. Commissioner Lee added, “If, though, we realize that the prior
time we were paying out of grant funding…. There’s a little bit of difference there.” Mr. Thompson confirmed.

2.     APPROVE (OR DENY) VOORHEES SUBDIVISION, PLAT “A,” SUBJECT TO THE CONDITIONS
       IN THE STAFF REPORT
(Track A, 14:04)
At Commissioner Ellertson’s request, Brandon Larsen of the Utah County Community Development Office spoke
about the reduction of the required fire break for this subdivision. Mr. Larsen noted that requirements call for 1.5
acres of irrigation water per one acre of land for the lot, but the applicants have requested an exception. Exceptions
can be granted if each building has a 30-foot wide irrigated band of landscaping around it, and if a 100-foot wide
fuel break then surrounds that irrigated band. County ordinances also allow for a reduction of the width for the fuel
break.

Brandon Larsen went on to note the applicant has been working towards a resolution with Utah County Fire Marshal
Jack Snow, and Fire Marshal Snow has submitted a letter indicating he approves of the reduced fuel break.
Commissioner Ellertson asked about the growth and fuel load around the 100-foot area, and Mr. Larsen confirmed
that it is quite sparse. Commissioner Ellertson asked, “How do we guarantee that would be maintained going
forward?” Mr. Larsen acknowledged the applicant does not control the adjacent land, and Commissioner Graves
pointed out any other property owner would hopefully realize the importance of protecting their home.
Commissioner Lee noted there were massive fires in Sanpete County years ago that consumed many houses, saying
“a lot of it came because they didn’t plan properly for the fire breaks – so there is concern.” Mr. Larsen pointed out
this particular lot would still be required to have the 30-foot wide irrigation band, and that cannot be varied.

Commissioner Ellertson asked what would occur if the Board denies this action today. Brandon Larsen replied the
applicant would not build there because there is not enough space. Mr. Larsen noted the applicant is a private
property owner, not a developer. Utah County Fire Marshal Jack Snow came forward to explain that while he would
not usually grant permission for the reduction of the 100-foot wide fuel break, in this circumstance the terrain allows
for easy access in case of an emergency; the property is adjacent to a major maintained highway, and there is a
wildland fire department within five miles. Fire Marshal Snow approached the bench to show pictures of the area to
the commissioners, and Mr. Larsen noted the property owner will be required to install a fire suppression system
since the home is part of the Urban Wildland Interface.

9.      ADOPT A RESOLUTION ENCOURAGING THE UTAH TRANSIT AUTHORITY TO OPEN ITS
        SUBCOMMITTEE MEETINGS TO THE PUBLIC
(Track A, 23:50)
Commissioner Ellertson mentioned a newspaper report regarding the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) that has come
under scrutiny by the public. Commissioner Lee explained this is a simple resolution encouraging UTA to keep
their meetings open. Commissioner Ellertson noted it may be premature to adopt a resolution before UTA has
clarified their position, because they may not in fact be closing their meetings to the public. Commissioner Lee
replied that the Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG) Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO) has
begun drafting paperwork similar to this resolution he is proposing. Commissioner Lee stated it would be good
practice for the commissioners to encourage transparency by UTA, a group which will be spending almost forty
million dollars of funds from Utah County. As stakeholders, Commissioner Lee expressed, “It is important that the
public have a voice.” Commissioner Ellertson said it is his understanding that the meetings are open, and
Commissioner Lee argued that UTA has said they will be closing the subcommittee meetings.

Commissioner Graves stated his concern: “If they’re still working on it, are we going to need to come back and
amend this resolution?” Commissioner Lee replied, “No – a resolution is not binding. I saw a direct quote from
[UTA Board] Chairman [H. David] Burton in which he said, you know, we’re going to close these meetings because
when the press comes in there, they screw it up.” Commissioner Lee replied to Commissioner Graves’ question that
if the UTA Board changes their direction, an amended resolution would not be needed; “we just clap our hands and
say well done.” Commissioner Graves asked Utah County Civil Division Chief Deputy Attorney David Shawcroft
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS
MINUTES – May 24, 2016

whether he had reviewed the proposed resolution, and Mr. Shawcroft responded in the affirmative. Commissioner
Lee mentioned Mr. Shawcroft helped to write the resolution.

Barbara Petty, Vice Chair of Legislative District 60, added public comment. She stated UTA’s lack of general lack
of accountability is outrageous, calling their mismanagement of tax dollars dishonest. Ms. Petty mentioned a 2014
audit that showed UTA is poorly managing public funds, and her concern that UTA is now trying to keep things
from that same public. She read statements from Utah House Representatives Jake Anderegg, Brad Daw, and Kay
Christofferson, all in support of Commissioner Lee’s proposed resolution:

         “Jake Anderegg said – we spoke this morning, he and I – he’s very much concerned with UTA’s
         actions and policy which in his opinion violates the Open Meetings Act. Brad Daw said that he is
         a big fan of openness and transparency, and always likes to have that on the record. Kay
         Christofferson texted me this message; he said: ‘Regarding UTA, I appreciate the new position to
         make binding decisions at the Board level. I’m not sure why that would make it desirable to close
         meetings to the public. Therefore I would recommend keeping committee meetings open to the
         public for monitoring and input.’ We feel like these are appropriate and speak in support of
         Commissioner Lee’s resolution. Thank you.”

When Commissioner Graves asked whether the UTA subcommittee meetings had been open to the public prior to
this article in the newspaper, Commissioner Lee replied, “That is an interesting question.” Commissioner Lee
explained that he has read and heard accounts of a Salt Lake Tribune reporter attempting to attend what they thought
would be a subcommittee meeting and being told one was not in session, but when that same reporter tried to contact
members of the UTA Board, they were told by the secretaries of those board members, “They’re at a meeting with
UTA.” Commissioner Lee said this causes speculation.

Commissioner Graves addressed the last two sentences of the proposed resolution, reading “…the Utah Transit
Authority Board of Trustees has determined to close certain Utah Transit Authority subcommittee meetings to the
public…. The Board of County Commissioners… hereby encourages the Utah Transit Authority Board of Trustees
to open its subcommittee meetings to the public.” He asked if the resolution is directed to all UTA meetings, or only
the ones that are currently under consideration to be closed, since the overall goal is transparency in every aspect.
Commissioner Ellertson clarified that it may be semantics, but the subcommittee meetings do not generally include a
quorum of the Board. This means they are not required to be open to the public, though past practice has been to
allow public attendance.

Commissioner Ellertson reiterated the subcommittee sessions are not Board meetings where official action takes
place, but instead where they work on various issues and then make recommendations to the UTA Board.
Commissioner Lee said that while he appreciates and respects that, he has been around government long enough to
know that subcommittee meetings are usually “where the heavy lifting gets done, and where the ideas are generated,
and where the thought processes go.” Commissioner Lee noted he would hate for the subcommittee meetings to be
closed and allow potential for “wink wink or nod nod” discussions wherein decisions are not officially made, but
issues end up being “rubber-stamped” because there were no opportunities for public input. Commissioner Lee
repeated this is only a simple resolution to encourage openness and transparency. Commissioner Lee noted he is not
suggesting the Board go as far as Salt Lake County, which has suggested withholding funding. Commissioner
Graves agreed again that transparency is the overall goal, and Commissioner Ellertson duly noted that the
subcommittee makes recommendations to the UTA Board after coming to a consensus of opinions without hiding
anything.

Saratoga Springs resident Heather Williamson came forward to add public comment. Ms. Williamson stated it is in
UTA’s best interest to restore public trust, and it is concerning to her that subcommittee meetings have been open in
the past and will now be closed. As elected officials of the people of Utah County, the commissioners have a vested
interest in UTA spending millions of local tax dollars. Ms. Williamson asked the Board to set a good example in
adopting this resolution, which would help to show people that UTA can be trusted.

said, “I know I shook my head when I heard it.” Commissioner Ellertson went on to note that UTA might be using a

adopting this resolution. Commissioner Lee brought up a suggestion by Commissioner Graves to add a sentence
