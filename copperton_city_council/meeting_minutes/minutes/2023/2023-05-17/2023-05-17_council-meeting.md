Title 18 and Title Adoption Resolution

              Curtis Woodward, Senior Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District,
reviewed the non-substantive and substantive changes to Titles 18 and 19.

              Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated the resolution
could be approved today if the Council saw fit. He suggested amending the fee schedule at the
next meeting.

              Council Member Stitzer stated she would feel more comfortable having a public
hearing before adopting a resolution since there were so many changes.

             Nathan Bracken stated a public hearing was held at the Planning Commission.
The changes do not require a public hearing, but it is at the Council’s discretion to hold one.

              Mayor Clayton stated there are three people in town waiting for this to pass so
they can obtain permits. If this was going to be pushed for two weeks, he would want a verbal
promise from all the Council members that they would read it.

              Council Member Stitzer stated there were things regarding animals that needed

one section. She stated, for the record, that she appreciated all the hard work of the Planning

topic by email to avoid another prolonged conversation.

             Council Member Bailey asked if having conversations by email outside of public
meetings was allowed.

              Nathan Bracken stated the Open and Public Meetings Act allows it because
decisions and policy changes are not being made. However, Council Members should not text
or email each other during public meetings.

               Mayor Clayton surmised that language to the section involving animals should be
complete by end of business that coming Friday. Edits regarding the rest of Title 19 should be
submitted by Memorial Day. After that, Curtis Woodward will make the appropriate changes and
email them to the Council and Nathan Bracken for final review. Adoption is anticipated to take
place on Tuesday, June 6th at the beginning of the meeting, as the remainder of the meeting is
reserved for discussion regarding the Unified Police Department (UPD). He will provide seven
days public notice so that the fee schedule can also be adopted.

               It was decided that Title 18 would also be adopted at the June 6th meeting.

Legislation to Convert Metro Townships to Cities or Towns

                Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, asked the Council for
permission to work with the other metro township attorneys to compose a letter of intent to be
sent to the State Legislature. This letter would notify the Legislature of the metro townships’ intent
to pursue legislation that would omit metro townships from the State Code and convert them into
cities/towns based on population, effective a certain date, to be determined.

               Mayor Clayton, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved to instruct Nathan
Bracken to participate in drafting a letter of intent to the State Legislature for the Council members
to review and sign. The motion passed unanimously.

Future Copperton Law Enforcement

                Council Member Olsen delivered a summary of the last Unified Police
Department (UPD) Board meeting. The Board has agreed that Chief Jason Mazuran will assume
the role of overseeing the restructuring of UPD. This means his position now needs to be filled.
Undersheriff Jake Peterson will move to only doing County business, which means his salary will
be paid solely using County funds. Wayne Dial will be acting as Chief Mazuran’s interim
replacement. These changes have resulted in UPD being overstaffed by one lieutenant. UPD
has offered a $25,000 incentive to any lieutenant who wants to voluntarily retire early. There are
multiple lieutenants who have acquired enough years of service to do this, but so far, nobody has
accepted the offer. Sheriff Rosie Rivera intends to increase the amount until someone accepts,
and it could go as high as $65,000. UPD continues to hold work sessions every two weeks.

             Mayor Clayton stated he would reach out to Herriman, Riverton, South Jordan,
and West Jordan cities to see if any of them want to contract with Copperton for law enforcement
services.

Pet Scanners

                 Council Member Severson stated a lot of the local cities are getting scanners so
they can identify lost animals before they reach the shelters. He would like Unified Fire Authority
(UFA) officers to participate in the program and be issued a few scanners. Scannable microchips
are typically planted in the neck area of an animal, but sometimes they shift downward over time,
so training on how to properly use the scanners would be needed.

              Mayor Clayton asked what the Township’s liability would be as some animals can
be dangerous and UFA.officers are not always properly trained for these situations. He can check
the metro township’s insurance policy to see if volunteers would be covered, but he feels UFA
has enough to do without being involved in this project.

               Council Member Stitzer stated scanners can be great, but not knowing the
animal’s vaccination history can create a safety risk, and sometimes the information about an
animal or its owner can be protected. Also, sometimes the microchips do not work or stop
working.

               Mayor Clayton stated he would like to get some public response on the issue.

               Council Member Severson stated he would put the issue out to the public.

       Council of Governments (COG)

met last Wednesday. The Audit Committee, which he chairs, approved the check registers. This
