The purpose of the joint work session was to conduct fact finding on the proposed WonderBlock Development, the
proposed Paid Parking Management System, and the proposed Project Financing Plan; review the agendas for the City Council,
Special Redevelopment Agency, and Special Municipal Building Authority meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.; discuss
Council/Board/Trustee business; and hear Council/Board/Trustee comments.

WonderBlock Development Project

Mayor Caldwell provided an introduction; he noted that there has been much discussion over the past several years
regarding the MakeOgden Plan, which identifies strategies and goals for building upon downtown Ogden’s existing assets, driving
infill development, and ensuring long-term economic sustainability for the City. He thanked everyone who has been involved in
the development of the plans that will be discussed tonight, including consultants, City staff, City leaders, and citizens who have
become engaged in the dialogue regarding the proposed project. The WonderBlock is one of the key deliverables for making
downtown Ogden an active, vibrant, unique place where people can visit. He considers the project to be visionary and he loves
what has been collectively accomplished in the City over the past several years that has led up to this point for this specific
project. He briefly identified key project partners and City staffers who have been intimately involved in the project. Tonight, he
hopes it will be possible for the group to listen and work together; there are many large initiatives that will be discussed tonight
and it is Administration’s goals to provide as much information as possible about those initiatives and also to collect feedback
from the group to inform the next steps of the process. He is encouraged by the turnout for tonight’s meeting and stated he is
grateful to all who are engaged in this matter.

Public Private Partnership with JF Capital WonderBlock Partners QOZB, LLC.: Community and Economic
Development Director Cooper used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the WonderBlock project; he first reiterated
some of the initiatives of the MakeOgden Plan, which were mentioned by the Mayor during his introduction, after which he
presented images of the WonderBlock property and the proposed site plan for the redesign/redevelopment of the property. The
WonderBlock project will be a highly visible new development in the heart of Ogden’s downtown including the following:

e 354 residential units featuring high quality amenities and walkable access to Ogden’s Arts, Culture and Events
o New downtown housing increases safety

e 100,000 square feet of Class “A” commercial office space

e 50,000 square feet of street level retail store space

e 20,000 square foot grocery store

e Boutique hotel

e Pedestrian pathways and public outdoor amenity spaces
e Convenient covered parking structures

The WonderBlock residential, retail, grocery, office, and hospitality elements represent a $188,000,000 total investment.
The proposed project would be a joint venture between J. Fisher Companies and the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) whereby both
parties would be investing in the project. The RDA’s investment contribution will not require an increase in property or sales taxes
for the community. The developer will make a $124 million investment and the RDA would issue a $64 million tax increment
bond to serve as its investment sources. The debt repayment sources include federal/state grants of $6.5 million, retained tax
increment (TIF) of $21 million (generated by WonderBlock new tax revenue), and Business Depot Ogden (BDO) Lease Revenue
of $36 million. He concluded more information about the WonderBlock project can be found at
www.ogdencity.com/WonderBlock.

Implementation of Paid Parking Plan: Mr. Cooper then noted that one question that has arisen as a result of this project
relates to parking; some have wondered where people will park upon the increase in residential and commercial development in
Ogden’s downtown. City Administration is aware of current parking demands and deficiencies; while current parking capacity is
generally adequate across entire downtown, some blocks/lots are now full most of time — and nearby businesses have complained
that congested parking is hurting their business. The MakeOgden redevelopment, including the WonderBlock, will do the
following:

Increase business/residential activities.
Reduce existing surface parking.
Increase existing problem of limited prime retail parking on 25th Street.
e Overwhelm existing parking capacity.
e Increase overall parking demand and need for new parking structures to support increased
density/walkability/vibrancy downtown (as envisioned by MAKE Ogden)

Administration initiated a parking study to assess needs and develop strategies to support planned development; the study

was completed by Kimley-Horn and Associates and their study results include the following:
e During peak activity, occupancy for all Downtown parking is about 50%
e There are some blocks/lots where parking reaches or exceeds the effective capacity threshold (85%)
o Which negatively impacts accessibility for businesses in particular — discourages visitors.
e The study confirmed that upcoming MAKE Ogden developments will overwhelm existing parking capacity.

The study recommends that Ogden City implement a Paid Parking Management System to address current and future
parking challenges. The study indicated advantages of the system includes:

e Support for health and vibrancy of a developing downtown

e Allows for equitable/efficient management of limited resource.

e Encourage turn-over within the most convenient/desirable parking spaces (on-street), while adding/managing
off-street resources

e Reduction in traffic congestion

e Increase in overall attractiveness to visitors.

Contingent on City Council approval, the proposal is to start pro-actively managing existing parking and add new
parking facilities. Implementing a paid parking system will address existing parking challenges in high-demand areas and support
the catalytic redevelopment of Ogden’s downtown core — as the MAKE Ogden Plan proposes by providing more parking capacity.
It is proposed that the city start pro-actively managing existing parking supplies within next two years beginning with the addition
of new parking facilities and paid parking in the most high-demand areas (phase 1) and then expanding over the next several years
(phases 2 and 3). Mr. Cooper presented a map and legend to identify the areas included in phases one through three. Two new
public parking structures would be located at the WonderBlock site and paid parking system updates throughout the downtown
represent an approximate $53,500,000 investment. All public parking elements will be built/owned/operated by Ogden City
Municipal Building Authority (MBA); the MBA investment will not require an increase in property or sales taxes. Kimley-Horn
has suggested that the revenue generated by the paid parking system will be sufficient to satisfy debt repayment and Mr. Cooper
reviewed the proposed fee structure for paid parking, after which he noted that more information about the paid parking system
can be found at www.ogdencity.com/GoParkOgden. Visitors to that site can also provide feedback and ask questions about the
new parking system and its implementation.

Bond Parameters for Project Funding: Bond Counsel, Laura Lewis, then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
discuss the project funding plan for the WonderBlock and Paid Parking Plan projects; two bonds will be issued: a tax increment
and sales tax revenue bond for the WonderBlock project and an MBA lease revenue bond for the parking structures. She provided
the following information about the tax increment bond:

e Will fund debt necessary to incentivize developer to take on the risk associated with the planned high-end,

e Secured by Tax Increment (TI) Revenues to come from the development and a Pledge of the City’s Sales Tax

Ms. Lewis summarized the estimated sources and uses of the bond proceeds and noted the debt will be amortized over a
period of 35 years with an estimated annual debt service payment of $4.41 million. Per the development agreement for the
WonderBlock project, the RDA will be able to use proceeds from the sale of the project to pay down all or a portion of the bonds
after the call date. If all bonds are not paid off, the balance will likely need to be refunded on a taxable basis. The parameters
included in the proposed resolution are higher than the estimated sources and uses noted due to the fact that the bonds will not be
finalized for several months. The maximum par is $75 million, with a maximum term of 37 years. She then discussed the MBA
lease revenue bonds; proceeds will be used to construct two new parking structures and purchase associated equipment, software,
and electronics necessary to administer on-street paid parking. The debt will be secured by a first trust deed in the physical
facilities; additional structures can be added to the master lease for financing purposes in the future if desired. Using the MBA
provides flexibility to use sales tax revenues to pledge for future projects and/or secure debt of other facilities under the master
lease. The finance plan will be to pay the debt using parking revenues projected in the parking study. Interest will be capitalized
for three years until parking revenues start to flow. She noted she anticipates a rating of ‘A’ or better due to the general credit
strength of the City and interest rates will be higher than a Sales Tax Revenue pledge due to risk of annual appropriation and
portion that will be taxable. The parameters in the proposed bond resolution are higher than estimated sources and uses due to the
fact that the bonds will not be finalized for several months. The maximum par is $85 million, and the maximum term is 40 years.

Council member Choberka asked if the Municipal Building is being used as collateral for the MBA bond. Ms. Lewis
answered no; the only assets that can be used for collateral are those that have debt against them or that are being improved. She
stated that if there were still debt against the Municipal Building, it may help the City to secure better bond terms, but there is no
debt against the Municipal Building at this time.

There was brief discussion regarding the repayment of the bond debt; Ms. Lewis stated that after year 10 and beyond,
there would be no pre-payment penalty for either of the bonds.

Chair Nadolski called for a recess to allow those present to convene in an open house meeting to allow the public to
engage with City leaders and project partners to ask any questions they have about the project and the details that have been
presented tonight. Following that, the Council, RDA Board, and MBA Board of Trustees will consider action on proposed
parameters resolutions early in 2023. If an additional open house meeting is necessary, such will be scheduled.
