Councilmember Earnest asked how they could continue to grow the number of people using the

overnight.

the city council and mayor so they could identify where the content was coming from. Mayor
Fullmer felt that the staff had come to council. She mentioned that the $500 project came from
staff at their planning retreat and the council wanted to put it to a vote from the public.

      9.2 DISCUSSION AND ACTION – Hiring Procedures for Directors and Department
          Heads
          The mayor and City Council will discuss the hiring procedures for directors and
          department heads. They will take appropriate action.

Mayor Fullmer turned the time over to City Manager Jacob McHargue.

Mr. McHargue explained that when they hired the last department head it was done in a public
meeting. He felt that if it was a public record and then someone did not get the job, it could make
things uncomfortable at their current place of employment. He suggested that staff could vet the
applications and then interview those applicants who qualified with a panel that would include
one or two city councilmembers. He also suggested that they could have more than one panel if
there were more councilmembers who were interested in interviewing the applicants. He asked
if the councilmembers were comfortable with these new procedures, which would be added to
the Policy and Procedures Manual. Mr. McHargue explained that the city code had the
department heads reporting to the city manager. This also included the hiring and firing of the
department heads with the exception of the city treasurer, city recorder, and city attorney.

Mayor Fullmer explained that before they hired Mr. McHargue, people were being hired in
random ways and then they brought it to the council. She said that they hired a city manager to
simplify the hiring process. She felt that a department head led the vision out of their department,
changing the way the city functioned, and having the city council be a part of the panels would
allow for transparency.

Mr. McHargue mentioned that the policy manual amendments would be brought to the city
council for approval this spring.

Mayor Fullmer asked if the change was put was in the policy manual it would become a part of
the ordinance. Mr. Church replied yes, and that Mr. McHargue would be required to follow the
policy manual.

Mayor Fullmer called for a motion.

Motion: COUNCILMEMBER FLAKE MOVED TO APPROVE THE ACTION DISCUSSED
FOR HIRING PROCEDURES AND DIRECTIONS FOR DEPARTMENT HEADS.
COUNCILMEMBER WELSH SECONDED THE MOTION. MAYOR FULLMER,

10.         TRAINING – Open and Public Meetings and Municipal Officers’ and Employees’
      Ethics Act Training
      City Attorney David Church will present annual training on Open and Public Meeting
      procedures and requirements. This training is provided annually pursuant to Section 52-4-104
      of the Utah State Code. This will also include Municipal Officers' and Employees' Ethics Act
      training. The City Council as well as members of the Planning Commission will attend this
      training.

Mayor Fullmer turned the time over to City Attorney David Church.

Page 5 of 6; February 26, 2020 City Council Meeting Minutes
Mr. Church explained that the Utah State Code required the chair of each public body hold
annual Open and Public Meetings Act training. He said that this was a requirement in the State
Law and by the State Auditor for compliance. This year they were adding Municipal Officers’
and Employees’ Ethics Act Training.

Mr. Church read the Ethics Act from State Code. He said that they needed to fill out a
“Disclosure Business Interest” form and disclose it orally in a public meeting. The disclosure
form must be filed with the city recorder and mayor.

Mr. Church gave a summary of the Open and Public Meetings Act (OPMA), which requires all
public bodies to conduct their business transparently. He explained what the difference between
a public meeting, a chance meeting, and a social gathering. This included anything electronic
such as social media, texting, or emails. He also explained that public meetings had to be noticed
and posted in a timely manner and had to be recorded and minutes taken. He mentioned that
minutes did not have to be a transcription but must contain the substance, the motions, and the
votes by name on issues discussed. It also must contain the substance of public comments and
presentations. Minutes must be available in draft form, within 30 days of the meeting, for public
viewing at the same time it is given to council for review. He noted that Planning Commission
did not have to have their minutes done in the same time frame. The minutes must be posted
within 3 business days of approval. Councilmember Judd asked if YouTube would act as the
digital copy. Mr. Church replied that it was any recording.

Mr. Church explained that the council may close a portion of the public meetings but the motion
must be made in a public meeting. He said that there was an exclusive list of topics for which
they could hold a closed meeting. Closed (sessions) meetings were to be recorded except for
meetings about employee issues and security devices.

He explained that the city could be sued if someone felt they had been harmed by a violation to
the OPMA. If the public body acted on something not on the agenda then people had 90 days to
challenge the action, which would then void it. If there was no challenge, the action would stand.
