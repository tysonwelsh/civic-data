Unified Police Department (UPD)

               Chief Levi Hughes, Unified Police Department, presented the Officer of the
Month award to Officer Grant Martinez, who, in serving as a School Resources Officer at Kearns
High School, verbally de-escalated an incident involving a suicide threat.

been sent.

in a row despite spending $135,000 on overtime costs.

on 6200 South.

firefighters from Kearns Station #109 were able to assist a stabbing victim during a police
emergency. Using “warm zone” techniques, the firefighters, who were unarmed and not wearing
bullet-proof vests, entered a scene that was mostly, but not fully, secured. Everything was done
perfectly, and the extreme bravery exercised by these workers helped to save a life during a high-
risk situation.

WHEREAS, the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District provides services to the five Metro
Townships in the Salt Lake Valley, unincorporated areas, and the Town of Brighton; and

WHEREAS, Kearns Metro Township adopted each county ordinance in effect at the time of its
incorporation pursuant to Utah Code Ann. Subsection 10-2a-414(3) and has authority to amend
or repeal the county ordinance when it determines it is necessary; and

WHEREAS, Kearns Metro Township is a municipality and has authority to regulate zoning and
subdivisions in general pursuant to Utah Code Ann. Subsection 10-3c-103(2); and

WHEREAS, Kearns Metro Township has authority to adopt zoning and subdivision regulations
pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 10-9a-501 in accordance with the Municipal Land Use,
Development, and Management Act, (“MLUDMA”), Title 10, Section 9a, Utah Code, to protect
public health, safety, and welfare; and

WHEREAS, the Council deems it necessary to amend its ordinances in order to ensure
compliance with State Statute, encourage conformity with the Kearns General Plan, promote ease
of use for residents and staff, and preserve the unique character of the Kearns community; and
for the protection and preservation of the public health, safety and general welfare.

BE IT ORDAINED BY KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL as follows:

1. Chapter 9.60, Section 14.12.050, and Section 14.12.060 (Subsection E) of Kearns Municipal

Attachment 1.

3. Title 19, excluding Chapter 19.70, is repealed and replaced in its entirety with the revised Title
19 attached hereto as Attachment 2.

is unconstitutional or invalid, then such portion(s) of these Ordinances, or specific application of
these Ordinances, shall be severed from the remainder, which shall continue in full force and
effect.

4. Implementation. Kearns staff are instructed to take any administrative steps needed to prepare
and finalize Attachments 1 and 2 for publication to Municode, including but not limited to making
any formatting, grammatical, or other non-substantive changes to the Ordinances that may be
needed.

5. Posting and Effective Date. After Kearns staff have prepared Attachments 1 and 2 for

Moderate Income Housing Resolution

               Kayla Mauldin, Senior Long-Range Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal
Services District, reviewed the resolution.

              Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated impact fees
were not imposed by the Salt Lake County Council, meaning a lot of the expense is being born
by the taxpayers of Kearns. There is a specific process governing impact fees, so they cannot
be set the same way a fee schedule is set. This resolution precludes impact fees from being
applied to moderate income housing.

               Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Schaefer, moved to adopt
Resolution No. 2023-06-01 of the Kearns Metro Township Council committing to future impact
fee waivers for moderate income housing. The motion passed unanimously.

RESOLUTION NO. 2023-06-01                                                DATE: June 12, 2023

       A RESOLUTION OF THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL
       COMMITTING TO FUTURE IMPACT FEE WAIVERS FOR MODERATE INCOME
       HOUSING.

                                          RECITALS

WHEREAS, Kearns Metro Township is a municipality and has the authority to pass all ordinances
and rules, and make all regulations, not repugnant to law, necessary for carrying into effect or
discharging all powers and duties pursuant to Utah Code § 10-8-84; and




                                               5
DATE           MONDAY                         JUNE                                      12, 2023

WHEREAS, Kearns Metro Township, as a municipality, has the authority to leverage fees on
development, including impact fees, pursuant to the Impact Fees Act, Utah Code § 11-36a-101,
et seq. (the “Act”); and

WHEREAS, Kearns Metro Township is currently undergoing a fee audit, which may lead to
changes in permitting fee structures and the eventual adoption of impact fees on new
development; and

WHEREAS, the Kearns Metro Township Council timely approved amendments to its Moderate
Income Housing Plan (“MIH Plan”), in compliance with House Bill 462 (2022); and

WHEREAS, the adopted MIH Plan selects Strategy L from Utah Code § 10-9a-403, which reads,
“Reduce, waive, or eliminate impact fees related to moderate income housing”; and

WHEREAS, the preservation and construction of Moderate Income Housing is a priority to Kearns
Metro Township, and the municipality is committed to implementing the recommendations of its
adopted MIH Plan, which requires that Kearns Metro Township proactively commits to waiving
future impact fees on moderate income housing; and

WHEREAS, the Kearns Metro Township Council finds that the benefits of subsidizing future
moderate income housing outweigh any potential costs to the municipality’s general fund and is
in the best interest of the community.

                                         RESOLUTION

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL,
effective immediately:

1. The Kearns Metro Township commits to waiving all future impact fees on development projects
where at least 15% of residential dwelling units are made affordable to moderate-income
households (those earning 50% to 80% of AMI) and are held affordable for a period not less than
twenty years from the date of occupancy (the “Commitment”).

2. The Kearns Metro Township directs its staff and agents to honor the Commitment when
preparing impact fee facilities plans, analyses and certifications, calculations, enactments, and
taking other steps needed to impose an impact fee under the Act.

3. This ordinance shall go into effect upon its posting pursuant to Utah Code § 10-3-711.

PASSED and ADOPTED this 12th day of June, 2023.

Resolution Amending Fee Schedule for Consistency with Amendments to Titles 18 & 19

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated when Kearns
Metro Township inherited Salt Lake County’s code, the civil penalties were included in the actual
text of the code. This resolution would remove civil penalties from the code and place them in
the fee schedule, so the code does not have to be amended on a yearly basis. It also takes
language from Title 5 and places it in the fee schedule.

              Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Schaefer, moved to adopt
Resolution No. 2023-06-02 of the Kearns Metro Township Council adopting the amended 2023
fee schedule. The motion passed unanimously.

RESOLUTION NO. 2023-06-02                                                     DATE: June 12, 2023

       A RESOLUTION OF KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL ADOPTING THE
       AMENDED 2023 FEE SCHEDULE

                                             RECITALS

WHEREAS, the Kearns Metro Township (“Kearns”) inherited the version of Salt Lake County’s
Code that was in existence on the date of Kearns’s incorporation in 2017 pursuant to Utah Code
§ 10-2a-414(3); and

WHEREAS, as a result, Section 19.94.070 of the Kearns Metro Township Code (the “Code”) set
forth the civil penalties for zoning violations (the “Civil Penalties”) within the Code rather than
within the Kearns Fee Schedule; and

WHEREAS, as part of Kearns’s recodification of its Code, the Kearns Council (the “Council”)
desires to move the Civil Penalties from Title 19 of the Code to the Kearns Fee Schedule; and

WHEREAS, when the Council adopted the 2023 Kearns Fee Schedule on December 21, 2022,
the revisions to Title 19 were still in process and, as a result, the Council adopted the fee schedule
subject to the civil penalties remaining in Title 19 until such time as Title 19 could be restated to
remove the Civil Penalties; and

WHEREAS, Kearns has adopted a final version of Title 19 that does not include the Civil
Penalties, and




                                                  7
DATE           MONDAY                           JUNE                                         12, 2023

WHEREAS, because Title 19 no longer includes the Civil Penalties, the Council desires to amend
the 2023 Fee Schedule to include the Civil Penalties that were previously set forth in Section
19.9*4.070; and

WHEREAS, the Council also desires to revise language in Section 5.02.060 of the Code regarding
the fees Kearns applies to business license withdrawal fees to coincide with the 2023 Fee
Schedule.

NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED BY THE KEARNS METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL that
effective immediately:

1. The attached amended 2023 fee schedule is adopted: and

2. The previous 2023 fee schedule shall be replaced in its entirety by the attached amended
2023 fee schedule; and

3. Section 5.02.060.B of the Code is amended to read as follows:

       If prior to a license being granted or issued, the application for a license is withdrawn by
       the applicant for any reason, the license official shall retain a portion of the application fee
       if authorized to do so in the metro township fee schedule. However, the mayor may, in
       their discretion, authorize the license official to refund the entire application fee if they
       determine a refund is appropriate under the circumstances.

4. Kearns staff are instructed to file this resolution and the attached amended fee schedule with
the Kearns Metro Township Clerk; and

5. Staff are instructed to post this resolution and the attached amended 2023 fee schedule to the
Kearns Metro Township website and to the “Municipal Resolutions Book” within Kearns’s
Municode page; and

6. Pursuant to Utah Code § 10-3-719, the amended 2023 fee schedule will go into effect on the
date this resolution is approved and adopted.

Legal Updates/Information

SLVLESA/Legislative Interim Update

                Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated he had recently
met with Salt Lake County Mayor Jennifer Wilson and the other metro township mayors. All the
other metro township mayors are on the same page with wanting to become cities/towns through
legislation. He has had multiple telephone conversations with Representative Jordan Teuscher,
who, along with Representative Anthony Loubet, has introduced the interim study request.
Representative Teuscher believes this bill will resolve his concerns and does not see a need to
study the Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA). A hearing will be held this
Wednesday, wherein Representative Teuscher will introduce the study. He will be working on
legislation over the summer and hopes to work out any kinks before the next legislative session
starts.

There is also the issue of the two options Kearns Metro Township has for law enforcement. One
option would be to restate the interlocal agreement with Unified Police Department (UPD) to
remove County and Sheriff Rosie Rivera as CEO. Harry Souvall, Legal Counsel, UPD, is working
on drafting a new interlocal agreement to that effect. The other option would be to contract with
the Sheriff’s Office for police services. Mr. Bracken has requested bids for both options, and he
expects to receive rough estimates this week. In his discussions, he has gotten the sense that
the County very much wants the metro townships to contract with the Sheriff’s Office.

Entering into a Rental Agreement with Granite School District for the Use of Oquirrh Hills Middle
School

               Kayla Mauldin, Senior Long-Range Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal
Services District, stated Mayor Kelly Bush recently had a conversation with Ben Horsley,
Associate Director, Granite School District, who is willing to rent Oquirrh Hills Elementary School,
which is currently vacant, to Kearns Metro Township for $1 a month. In exchange, the Township
would cover utilities and custodial costs for the building, which are estimated to be $8,500 a
month. The building is 43,000 square feet, which is more than the Township would need. Other
community organizations such as The Off Broadway Theater, My Kearns Community Coalition,
Pacific Islander Chamber of Commerce, Salt Lake County Economic Development, and the
Unified Police Department have expressed interest in sharing the space and would contribute to
the costs. The School District is not willing to sell the property, and the term of the lease would
be 20 years.

                Chief Levi Hughes, Unified Police Department, stated the southwest wing would
suffice for his precinct’s needs. His current lease contract is ending, and the walk-through is
scheduled for June 26th. He will use all his influence to move into the building owned by Granite
School District because it is, by far, the best deal possible for his precinct cost wise.

               Mayor Kelly Bush stated it would be more cost effective for Kearns to put its
Justice Court within the community and house it at the building as well. Even though it is a large
space, with so many partners, it needs to be planned carefully. The realtor for Kearns Metro
Township’s was not able to provide any comparisons because of the uniqueness of the situation.

                Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated with a 20-year
lease, there is a lot of potential to bring in business that could create tax revenue and rent revenue
for the Township.

             The Council agreed the lease was a “golden opportunity” with no foreseeable
disadvantages.

               Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Peterson, moved to move
forward with talks with the Granite School District regarding leasing the property. The motion
carried by a unanimous vote.
