                                  PLANNING COMMISSION MINUTES
                                         Meeting of November 17, 2022
                            Logan City Council Chambers (290 N 100 W Logan, UT 84321) www.loganutah.org

Minutes of the meeting for the Logan City Planning Commission convened in regular session
Thursday, November 17, 2022, Commissioner Lucero called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.

Commissioners Present: Jessica Lucero, Roylan Croshaw, Jordy Guth, David Lewis, Ken Heare
Eldon Peterson, Sara Doutre

Commissioners Excused:

Staff Present: Russ Holley, Tanya Rice, Mike DeSimone, Amanda Pearce, Mohammed Abdullahi
Darren Farar, Jeannie Simmonds (City Council)

Commissioner Guth moved to approve the minutes from the October 27, 2022 meeting as submitted.
Motion seconded by Commissioner Doutre. The motion was approved unanimously.

PC 22-059 Land Development Code Amendment 17.24-Critical Lands Overlay Zone [Code
Amendment] Mike DeSimone/Logan City is requesting a Code Amendment to the Land Development
Code, Chapter 17.24 Critical Lands Overlay Zone to amend current standards regulating
development within or adjacent to Floodplains, Riparian Areas, Geologically Unstable Areas,
Wildland Urban Interface Areas, Lands above 4,850 feet, and Wetlands.

STAFF/ PROPONENT: Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director reviewed an
amendment to the Land Development Code, Chapter 17.24-Critical Lands Overlay Zone. M.
DeSimone covered the areas regulated under Critical Lands, Floodplains, Riparian Areas,
Geological Unstable Areas, Wildland Urban Interface, Lands Above 4,850 feet, and Wetlands. Prime
Agricultural Lands and Critical Wildlife Habitat areas were proposed for removal from the Critical
Lands section because we currently lack rules for them now, these areas would not be annexed into
the city, and there are other State or Federal entities managing them. M. DeSimone went over the
overall changes to the Critical Lands Overlay for each of the areas listed above as covered in the
Staff Report.

Commissioner Peterson asked about the essential views (including a map) that are mentioned in the
previous version of the Critical Lands Overlay but are not included in this amendment. M. DeSimone
said that the essential views are not defined, there are no standards, and there is no map identifying
essential views.

Commissioner Peterson Asked if endangered species should be added to this amendment, to show
what they are and where they are located. M. DeSimone said that endangered species are regulated
at the state and federal levels and would be reviewed and recorded with the delineation process.

Commissioner Peterson asked if the proposed amendment makes the development more restrictive.
M. DeSimone said this amendment requires the applicant to submit the information that is already
required by federal law upfront with the development process.

Commissioner Guth asked who will do the delineation of the riparian areas. M. DeSimone said that
any land with flowing water would be a riparian area. It is the responsibility of the applicant to hire a
professional to complete the delineation. R. Holley, Senior Planner said that the definition of a
Riparian area in the current code is an area associated with a natural watercourse that includes
wildlife and vegetation.

Commissioner Peterson asked if 4,850 feet is the maximum height the city can provide water to. M.
DeSimone said that the city provides water above 4,850 feet.

PUBLIC: Frank Howe, Chair of the Logan River Task Force said that they are in favor of the
proposed changes that provide greater protection to the Floodplains, Wetlands, and Riparian Areas
and commends the City Staff for their thorough and thoughtful work on the Critical Lands Overlay
and encourages the commission to approve the proposed changes. Stated that the 100 and 500-
year floodplain maps outside of the city limits are incomplete and underestimate the floodplain areas,
in the event of an annexation these incomplete maps should be updated before the annexation is
considered. Suggested that under 17.24.60 (limiting the removal of trees larger than 12” DBH unless
they are considered a hazard, diseased, or dead and identified in a river or stream restoration
program) the ‘and’ be changed to ‘or’ because the Task Force works with the City to develop


restoration projects to remove large invasive Crack Willow trees whether they are healthy, a hazard,
diseased or not. Mr. Howe also stated that under 17.24.120 there is no specification that public
notice will be given, or that any public input process is available on a request for a Reasonable Use
Exception, or who will be approving or denying the request.

COMMISSION: M. DeSimone addressed Mr. Howes’ comments. The request for a Reasonable Use
Exception can be updated to follow the track two process where the Planning Commission is the
decision maker and public notice is required. The ‘and’ will be changed to ‘or’ on the removal of
trees. It is required with the annexation process that all critical lands are identified, and the
delineation is required with the proposed development.

Darren Farar, City Engineer stated a county-wide floodplain study has been in the works for the last
5 years and as of May 2023 a Floodplain delineation will be approved. Logan City Engineering has
also been working on Floodplain Delineation for the Logan River which should be approved soon.

Commissioner Doutre asked if any Riparian areas are already mapped and how an applicant would
determine if there is a riparian area on their property. M. DeSimone said that when an application is
submitted, planning staff will look at a set of Base maps that indicate if there is water located on the
property. Staff can also do an onsite inspection if needed.

The Commission requested to see changes to the proposed document before making a
recommendation to the Council, including a definition of a Riparian area, the inclusion of the review
process for the reasonable use exceptions, and the edits discussed during the meeting.

MOTION: Commissioner Heare moved to Continue PC 22-059 to the January 12, 2023 meeting to
view the changes discussed. Commissioner Croshaw seconded the motion.
