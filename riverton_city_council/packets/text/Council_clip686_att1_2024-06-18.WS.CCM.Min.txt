37          Riverton Community Arts Presentation
38
39   Scott Reid, Chair of Riverton Community Arts, presented a detailed breakdown of the funds
40   allocated from the 2023-2024 City budget. The budget included expenditures for sound
41   systems, lighting systems, and miscellaneous items. The operational budget for the year was
42   $7,500, intended for maintaining and repairing the newly acquired equipment.
43
44   Mr. Reid highlighted the efficient use of the seed money, emphasizing the advanced equipment
45   that attracted talented volunteers, particularly from high schools. He mentioned the importance
46   of maintaining the equipment within the annual budget and outlined plans to install video
47   monitoring systems backstage.
48
49   Mr. Reid said that the current board consisted of the himself, Pam Henderson, Dennis Paige,
50   Michael Whitely, Kim Ostler from the Riverton Arts Council, and Kerrie Neu from the Riverton
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   Jazz Band. He mentioned the volunteer efforts, totaling 425 hours, mainly from high school
 2   technical crew members.
 3
 4   Councilmember Buroker expressed gratitude and asked for clarification regarding the structure
 5   and operations of the organization. In response, Mr. Reid clarified that the Riverton Community
 6   Arts Board (“Board”) consists of the top four members, who are independent of any other
 7   groups that hold seats on the board. The president of the Riverton Arts Council sits on the
 8   Board to facilitate communication. The same structure applies to the Riverton Jazz Band, with
 9   their representative sitting on the Board to address scheduling, building usage, and other
10   relevant matters. This setup ensures that each group's needs are conveyed and managed
11   effectively through their respective channels.
12
13   Mr. Reid explained that the framework they have established is open to any organization that
14   meets the criteria, not just the current groups. If a group does not have its own 501(c)(3), they
15   can operate under the Riverton Community Arts' umbrella, which has one. New groups can join
16   as individuals or entities without a 501(c)(3) and benefit from the existing nonprofit structure.
17
18   Mr. Reid said the Board meets on the first Tuesday of every month in the Community Center.
19
20   Councilmember Pierucci thanked Mr. Reid for his dedication and for engaging the youth in the
21   community, noting the positive impact. He asked about the metrics used to determine if a new
22   organization could participate in Riverton Community Arts. Specifically, he wanted to know if
23   one of the metrics was whether there was already an organization providing similar services.
24
25   Mr. Reid mentioned that the performing arts encompass many aspects, such as dance, and
26   there could be room for various groups under one umbrella. He expressed openness to
27   welcoming more groups, including dance groups or other nonprofits, into their organization,
28   highlighting the benefits of having more diverse participation.
29
30   Mayor Staggs agreed and acknowledged the City's role in providing funding and oversight but
31   recognized the organization's responsibility in maintaining a high-quality arts experience and
32   aligning with the mission statement.
33
34   Councilmember Pierucci highlighted the need for caution when deciding which organizations to
35   support and acknowledged the value and benefits of promoting the arts through community
36   organizations but stressed the importance of being mindful about how decisions are made to
37   avoid creating a situation where some organizations are unfairly favored or excluded.
38
39   Councilmember Haymond inquired about the current level of interest and demand for the new
40   facilities, asking if there are already people lined up or interested in using the space for plays,
41   performances, and other events. In response, Mr. Reid confirmed that there has been
42   substantial interest in using the new facilities, including the lighting and sound equipment. He
43   expressed a desire to see even more groups and events utilize the facilities, noting that the first
44   year has been a learning experience and they aim to increase usage moving forward. He
45   welcomed applications from new groups and explained that decisions are made by the entire
46   board through a voting process, ensuring a collaborative decision-making approach.
47
48   Councilmember McDougal inquired about the financial operations of the organization, asking
49   about the checks and balances on spending and who is responsible for managing the financials.
50   Mr. Reid explained that Pam Henderson would be responsible for overseeing the organization's
51   financials. The process involves the Board approving a budget, which then guides the purchase
     Page | 3 Minutes – City Council Meeting

 1   of equipment or other items. Currently, the organization does not have its own account and is
 2   awaiting the final balance from the City. Once established, any financial transactions will require
 3   two signatures for checks or expenditures, ensuring that multiple people are involved in
 4   managing the money.
 5
 6   Councilmember McCay wanted to know about the process for South Valley Performing Arts to
 7   become a strategic partner with the City. Mr. Reid explained that the process for an
 8   organization to become a strategic partner involves submitting a questionnaire and an
 9   application. The application would be reviewed by the Board and the Board would conduct
10   internal vetting to ensure that the organization is not misusing the nonprofit status for personal
11   profit. Once vetted, the application would be voted on, and if approved, the organization would
12   become part of Riverton Community Arts.
13
14   Mr. Reid mentioned that the Board has considered strategies for outreach, including building a
15   website and expanding their social media presence. They hope to utilize the City's
16   communication channels to promote their efforts once these platforms are established.
17
18   Kevin Hicks, City Manager, explained that for new organizations, the approach to the $7,500
19   annual budget would involve an initial front-loading of funds to support their startup. For
20   subsequent years, the organization would be required to submit a financial statement showing
21   how the funds were spent and a plan for future spending before additional funds are released.
22   This process aligns with standard practices used with other organizations, but differs in that it
23   involves providing initial funding to new organizations, whereas previously funds were
24   distributed at the end of the year after financials were reviewed.

 5   Mr. Lethbridge said this was a public hearing for a Rezone application brought by the applicant,
 6   Thomas Belchak, for a single parcel of 1.06-acres located at 3807 West 11800 South. The
 7   requested zone is R-3 with an Elderly Housing Overlay (EHOV). The subject property is
 8   currently zoned “R-3” which is a single-family residential zone with minimum 14,000-sf lots.
 9
10   The Elderly Housing Overlay is a zoning tool which limits housing within an area to 55 and
11   older, with the intent to provide for affordable and available housing options for the elderly. The
12   zoning includes allowance for additional density over that allowed in the underlying zone based
13   on the inclusion of what the ordinance terms “mobility/age friendly” features with the approved
14   units. Such features include access ramps, no step entries, main level living, etc, and are
15   required at a ration of one feature per unit of density.
16
17   The applicant is proposing construction of four (4) units on individual lots on the property, shown
18   as being accessed by a private lane. The R-3 zone, with a minimum lot size of 14,000 square
19   feet, would not accommodate the proposed development. However, as the applicant has
20   proposed the units for elderly housing, the Elderly Housing Overlay density bonus does permit
21   the reduction in lot size as proposed.
22
23   Thomas Belchak, Developer, expressed support for the elderly housing overlay primarily due to
24   its allowance for attached units. He indicated that he felt comfortable with the Planning
25   Commission's recommendation. He noted that the property is an infill project and emphasized
26   the suitability of the attached unit design due to the planned RV garages between each unit.
27   This design would provide necessary separation and comply with fire regulations.
28
29   Councilmember McDougal proposed not using the elderly housing overlay, suggesting instead
30   an R-4 zoning with an SD (Special Development) designation. He emphasized the need to
31   make the property compatible with its surroundings while ensuring it remains usable and
32   marketable. He preferred reducing the setbacks to allow standalone units, rather than attached
33   garages. He also recommended limiting the development to single-story homes to prevent the
34   construction of two-story buildings that could negatively impact existing neighboring residents.
35
36   Councilmember McCay agreed with Councilmember McDougal's suggestion of using an R-4
37   zoning with an SD (Special Development) designation for the property. She proposed giving the
38   landowner the option to decide between zero lot lines or separate structures. Additionally, she
39   recommended allowing a second-story bonus room.
