DISCUSSION OF WASHINGTON COUNTY ASSISTANCE IN KEEPING ZION
NATIONAL PARK OPEN

Chairman Cox stated that a four (4) way partnership was implemented in order to keep
minimal services operating at Zion National Park during the current partial government
shutdown. The partnership is with St. George City, Washington County, Zion National
Park, and the Zion Forever Project, and each partner funding $7,327 dollars to keep
minimum park services running for a one (1) week period. If the shutdown continues,
additional funding may be allocated from the partners involved in order to keep Zion
National Park operational.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. _R-2019-2410, A RESOLUTION
APPROVING A LETTER OF AGREEMENT WITH COMMUNITY SOLUTIONS
CONSULTING FOR SERVICES DURING THE STATE LEGISLATIVE SESSION

Washington County Court Support Services Director Sharmane Gull explained that the
Court Support Services (CSS) Office has been in operation for fifteen (15) months and
receives a significant portion of its budget through state funding.

A dedicated representative is needed at the State Capital during the legislative session. It
is recommended for Community Solutions Consulting to perform lobbying and consulting
services with the Utah State Legislature and the Utah Governor’s Office to successfully
accomplish all objectives outlined by the Washington County Court Support Services,
which is a division of the County Attorney’s Office. The cost of this consulting agreement
is $10,000, which will be paid from the CSS budget.

CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. R-2019-2417, A RESOLUTION OF

THE BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF WASHINGTON COUNTY,
UTAH (THE "'COUNTY"') DECLARING ITS INTENTION TO DESIGNATE AN
ASSESSMENT AREA FOR THE PURPOSE OF LEVYING ASSESSMENTS
AGAINST PROPERTIES WITHIN THE ASSESSMENT AREA TO FINANCE

THE CONSTRUCTION AND INSTALLATION OF ROAD IMPROVEMENTS;
AND TO FIX A TIME AND PLACE FOR PROTESTS AGAINST THE

ASSESSMENT AREA AND ITS ASSESSMENTS, AND RELATED MATTERS

Washington County Engineer Todd Edwards explained that several residents in the
Harmony Heights subdivision requested that the roads in their subdivision be paved. Due
to the high costs of paving, the County could not fund that request. A majority of the
residents stated they would be willing to pay for the cost of the paving.
Washington County Commission

In order for that to happen, a special assessment would need to be imposed on all residents
of the Harmony Heights Subdivision. This resolution begins the process of implementing
a special assessment for the area.

Mr. Edwards and Deputy County Attorney Eric Clarke explained that the next step will be
a public hearing held on March 5, 2019, during the regular Commission meeting to hear
from the public on the proposed Assessment Area. Then a protest period will be held for
sixty (60) days for any residents opposed to the special assessment to submit written
protests. If received protests exceed the statutory limit, the special assessment area cannot

move forward.

Mr. Edwards further explained that if approved, the County would finance the road, after
which, the Assessment Area would repay the County over a ten (10) year period, with
interest.

MOTION: Motion by Commissioner Almquist to Adopt Resolution No. R-2019-
2417, A Resolution of the Board of County Commissioners of
Washington County, Utah (The “County”) Declaring its Intension to
Designate an Assessment Area for the Purpose of Levying Assessments
Against Properties within the Assessment Area to Finance the
Construction and Installation of Road Improvements; and to Fix a
Time and Place for Protests Against the Assessment Area and tis
Assessments, and Related Matters. Motion seconded by Commissioner
Iverson and carried by unanimous vote, with Commissioners Cox,
Iverson, and Almquist voting aye.
