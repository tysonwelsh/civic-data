Ahead of the City Council Meeting, the agenda items were discussed. Mayor Rob Dahle noted
that there will likely be members of the public present to share comments about the district split
initiative. He wants to allow people to make their comments but does not want to have a discussion
about the matter during the public comment period. He will make a statement before the public
comment period opens to let everyone know that there is an awareness of the issue.

Mayor Dahle was willing to meet with residents to discuss the district split initiative. He pointed
out that there is not enough time for this to be on the ballot this November. Mayor Dahle wanted
to be respectful to the residents and make sure everyone is heard. At the same time, it needs to be
made clear that there will not be a back-and-forth conversation during the public comment period.
Council Members Brewer & Quinn expressed a willingness to sit in on a future meeting with
residents to discuss the issue.

The City Council Meeting agenda items were reviewed. Mayor Dahle reviewed the calendar items.
He referenced the City Council Meetings scheduled for May 1, 8, and 15, 2025, and June 5 and
12, 2025. There is also the Arbor Day event, Tree Giveaway, and Community Pancake Breakfast.

III.     Public Comments.
Mayor Dahle opened the public comment period. He explained that when comments are shared,
the City Council is able to listen to those comments from members of the community. However,
there is not typically a back-and-forth discussion. Some emails have been received about the
district split initiative, so there is familiarity with the subject matter some of the public comments
will address. Mayor Dahle has received five or six emails about this and the issues are best
summarized in an email from Jed and Betsy VanDenBerghe. That email had a lot of information
and it has been reviewed by the City Council. Mayor Dahle expressed a willingness to meet with
residents to discuss this issue.

Trudy Jorgensen-Price shared updates about the library. She reported that tax season is over and
with the assistance of the library staff booking the appointments and the AARP tax volunteers,
there were almost 750 tax appointments. 42 people were moved off of the waitlist and there were
only 22 people who were unable to be assisted. She reported that the Seed Library is still open.
This month, an extra Storytime event has been added, so there is Storytime on Monday and
Tuesday mornings. From May through July, the Storytime event on Monday will take place at the
gazebo in the park. Ms. Jorgensen-Price reported that on April 26, 2025, there will be a
collaboration with Clark Planetarium.

Emily Lowe - 4295 Lynne Lane. She is a mother of four children and has a unique perspective,

Marissa Skinner - 1800 North Woodside Dr. She does not have an opinion about whether or not

IX.    City Manager Report - Gina.
Ms. Chamness shared updates with the Council. She reported that The Great Utah ShakeOut took
place earlier. There was an exercise held in City Hall to see what would happen if there was a
power outage with trees knocked down and damage done to the roof. Due to that exercise, there
is now a better understanding of the vulnerabilities, which includes contacting City Staff.

Ms. Chamness reported that there are a number of grant applications that will be made over the
next little while. The first is a reapplication for the earmark that the City is targeted to receive in
the current year's Federal Budget. She reminded Council Members that in lieu of adopting a
budget, the Federal government adopted a continuing resolution that will continue through
September 30. The earmark, which is part of the Community Project Fund, was put on hold. The
City will reapply for that and should have an idea of whether or not that will be funded by July
2025. Ms. Chamness reported that the application was for $2 million last year, but the committees
whittled that amount down to $1.65 million. The City will reapply for $2 million.

The City will be applying for a program that is funded through the Utah Department of
Transportation (“UDOT”) through the Transportation Alternatives Program (“TAP”) for a
sidewalk fill-in project on the south side of 5600 South from Highland Drive to Van Winke. There
are still some engineering cost estimates being worked through for that.

There will be an application submitted through the UDOT Safe Sidewalks Program for the second

out on May 23, 2025, and the City is looking at a project between $200,000 and $300,000. Ms.
Chamness noted that the City Council will be updated with a final number before the application

The City Council approved an agreement with Salt Lake County for TRCC funding for a study for

       a.      Discussion on Fireworks Ordinance.
The Council needs to vote on what will be submitted to the County for the restrictions. Council
Members reviewed the information including the previous restrictions. Discussions were had
about different locations shown on the map and the Spring Creek area. City Recorder, Stephanie
Carlson explained that descriptions are sent to the Unified Fire Authority (“UFA”) for the
mapping.

Mayor Dahle does not believe there is an issue with the map from last year in terms of the restricted
areas. The question is whether there is a desire to have restrictions east of 1300 East based on the
conditions. Essentially, the City Council needs to determine whether the current restrictions
should remain or if the map should become more restrictive. Ms. Chamness noted that the Fire
Marshal does not recommend making more of the City available for fireworks. Once an area is
open for fireworks, it is harder to move that back in the future. Some information was shared
about what the restrictions look like in cities like Cottonwood Heights and Millcreek.

Council Members discussed penalties for fireworks in restricted areas and past enforcement
measures. Chief Hoyal reported that there is a $1,000 fine for fireworks penalties. There are
normally officers out on holidays to patrol and enforce. Council Member Brewer asked about the
number of citations. Chief Hoyal clarified that there are not usually a lot issued. There are
typically a couple of fireworks citations issued per year. The difficulty is that if a call is received,
the use of fireworks is often over before an officer arrives on the scene. Council Member Brewer
acknowledged that there is a heightened fear due to the severity of the fires that happened in
California. He stressed the importance of enforcement, as the use of fireworks in a restricted area
is a safety issue that can impact everyone in the community. He feels citations should be issued.
Chief Hoyal shared information about enforcement efforts and the main areas of focus.

       b.     Code Update – Review of Title 2.
There was additional discussions about the proposed language. 2.11.280: Signing Authority is as
follows:

   •   The City Council may authorize the Manager to sign any agreement, purchase order,
       obligation, form, or other document. In addition, the Manager has authority to sign all
       documents necessary to allow the City to make minimal purchases and small purchases, as
       defined in this chapter, provided that funds have been previously appropriated by the City
       Council. When a specific appropriation has been made by the City Council, or for any
       contract or written obligations less than $100,000, the Manager may sign.

Mr. Blakesley explained that based on that language, the Manager could approve up to that amount
within the budget parameters. If it is a contract or purchase above that amount, then it would come
to the City Council. There is a requirement that a threshold must be established, but there is no
requirement for what that threshold must be. Cities will choose different thresholds based on the
size of the city. Ms. Chamness shared some clarifications about the proposed code language.

       c.       Budget Discussion.
                • Central Wasatch Commission (“CWC”).
Mayor Dahle asked if the Council Members took time to look at the CWC website. Council
Member Fotheringham reported that he reviewed the website and looked at the participating
member jurisdictions. Mayor Dahle believes the CWC is essentially a lobbying organization for
municipalities that are impacted by the canyons: Big Cottonwood Canyon, Little Cottonwood
Canyon, and Millcreek Canyon. The CWC started with the Mountain Accord process. Holladay
is not currently part of the CWC, but there was a recent discussion with the Executive Director,
Lindsey Nielsen, and there is a desire to see Holladay become a member jurisdiction of the CWC.
He added that the CWC is involved in conversations about long-term transportation challenges.

Mayor Dahle pointed out that there is a budget impact associated with becoming a member
jurisdiction. He asked if there is anyone on the City Council who is passionate about this work
and would be willing to serve on the CWC Board to represent Holladay. Council Member Gray
expressed an interest in the work of the organization. Mayor Dahle believes the budget impact
would be $25,000. Council Members expressed support for a presentation from the CWC about
the organization. Council Member Gray pointed out that Holladay does not border any of the
canyons directly, so she wants to better understand how the CWC envisions Holladay participation.
Mayor Dahle believes the role of the Holladay representative would be to contribute to the CWC
Board. There would be involvement in an organization that focuses on issues that impact the
canyons
