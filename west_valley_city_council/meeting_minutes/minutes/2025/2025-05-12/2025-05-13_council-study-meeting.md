REVIEW AGENDAS FOR REGULAR CITY COUNCIL MEETING OF MAY 13,
2025
Steve Pastorik presented an updated concept plan for application GPZ-1-2025. The new
layout would preserve the entire frontage of the property as commercial and include a cul-
de-sac, resulting in 24 residential units—fewer than the originally proposed 28. The
reduction in lots was necessary to accommodate required parking and open space. Steve
noted that the development agreement had been prepared and signed under the assumption
that 28 units would be possible. However, the finalized engineering concept, received after
the agreement was signed, showed that only 24 units were feasible. He clarified that the
property owner—not the developer—had signed the agreement, since the owner would
retain the commercial portion and had obligations tied to it. When the property is sold, the
developer will assume responsibility for the residential portion, including compliance with
all associated standards. Additional provisions would also now be required due to the
commercial-residential boundary, including a wall and new landscaping between the two
zones.

Councilmember Huynh asked if there was sufficient parking on the commercial lots. Steve
replied that there is enough parking for the Betos restaurant and added that parking would
be considered separately for any additional commercial use. Mayor Lang asked if a motion
can be made to change language in the Development Agreement to reflect a cap of 24 units
instead of 28. Steve replied that this can be done but if for some reason the property owner
did not agree to sign that document, that could complicate the application. Councilmember
Huynh asked if the item can be continued until a new development agreement with the
correct number of units can be signed. Steve replied yes.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang, members of the Council had no further questions or concerns
regarding items listed on the Agenda for the Regular City Council Meeting scheduled later
this night.

ACTION: ORDINANCE 25-08, AMEND SUBSECTION 7-10-103(2) OF THE
ZONING ORDINANCE TO PROVIDE LANDSCAPE ALTERNATIVES IN
ADDITION TO LAWN AND ARTIFICIAL TURF
Jody Knapp, CED, discussed proposed Ordinance 25-08 that would amend
Subsection 7-10-103(2) of the Zoning Ordinance to Provide Landscape Alternatives in
Addition to Lawn and Artificial Turf.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
information as follows:

       Section 7-10-103(2) currently requires the following:

       (2) Fifty percent of the area of the landscaped buffer shall be planted with
       grass. Artificial turf that meets the standards in Section 7-2-127 may be used
       in lieu of grass. The remaining 50 percent of the area shall include at least 50
       percent live plant material and may include specialty paving, Street furniture,
       and outdoor seating areas. There shall be a minimum of one tree planted for
       each 600 square feet of the gross area of the landscaped buffer.

       Staff is proposing the following language:

       (2) There shall be a minimum of one tree planted for each 600 square feet of
       the gross area of the landscaped buffer as well as one of the following two
       options below:

       a. At least fifty percent of the area of the landscaped buffer shall be planted
       with grass. Any remaining area that is not grass shall include at least 50
       percent live plant material coverage, not including tree canopies. Artificial
       turf that meets the standards in Section 7-2-127 may be used in lieu of grass
       and shall not exceed 50% of the area.

       b. Seventy-five percent live plant material coverage, not including tree
       canopies, with a combination of deciduous and evergreen plant material and
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – MAY 13, 2025

            at least two Mineral Ground Cover or Mulch varieties provided throughout
            the area. Twenty-five percent of the plants must be a minimum of 5 gallon.

     Councilmember Whetstone stated that a resident wanted to do a circular driveway
     on a major arterial, but current restrictions don’t allow for that. He asked if this
     ordinance change would affect that. Mayor Lang replied that this would only apply
     to commercial properties. Ifo indicated that residential landscaping requirements
     would be discussed on June 10th. Mayor Lang asked if Bangerter and USANA
     would be subject to this. Jody replied that the Bangerter and USANA area was done
     many years ago and would be separate. Mayor Lang asked if there would be
     changes to berms. Jody replied no. Mayor Lang indicated that the Jack in the Box
     that was recently opened has all gravel. Jody replied that they are correcting that.

     The City Council will consider Ordinance 25-08 at the Regular Council Meeting
     scheduled May 27, 2025 at 6:30 P.M.

Medium Density Residential for Property Located at 2996 South Redwood Road.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
information as follows:

   The applicant is proposing to demolish the two existing homes on the property and
   construct a 26-unit, two-story rental townhome development with front-loaded
   garages. The applicant’s original proposal included 49 units which were potentially
   possible through the use of the 2-acre Rocky Mountain Power owned parcel to the
   north for access, parking, and recreation space. However, the applicant was
   unsuccessful in obtaining ownership or a perpetual easement from Rocky Mountain
   Power, so the project was scaled back to the property the applicant has under
   contract. The proposed density of the project (excluding the Rocky Mountain
   Power property) is 10.4 units/acre. All units include 3-bedrooms and two-car
   garages. All garages have driveways that are deep enough to park in. By way of
   background, DAI developed the H2O Lofts project at 2864 S Decker Lake Dr. The
   townhomes proposed on the subject property are similar to those at the H2O Lofts
   project. The following points are provided for reference:

       •   There are eight developed or approved townhome developments that front
           or have access on Redwood Road.
       •   As part of the City’s General Plan update effort, staff discussed this
           property, along with other properties along Redwood Road, with both the
           Planning Commission and City Council. Both the Planning Commission
           and City Council indicated that they were supportive of townhomes at this
           location.
       •   Redwood Road is currently a 7-lane arterial street that is serviced by UTA
           bus route 217.
       •   Average annual daily traffic (AADT) on this segment of Redwood Road
           was 26,000 in 2023. A 7-lane arterial can handle a traffic volume of 40,000
           at a level of service (LOS) C and 46,000 at a LOS D.
