A.     Conceptual Plans for West Jordan Wheels Park at Ron Wood Park Area
City Administrator Korban Lee shared prior plans for unfinished areas of Ron Wood Park and
neighboring undeveloped land. He reported that moving the existing storm water detention basin
to the Rocky Mountain Power Corridor so the land could be used for ball fields would cost at least
$7 million. Rocky Mountain Power would lease, not sell, the land to the City. Mr. Lee said he would
hesitate to put major City infrastructure on leased land. He commented that storm water detentions
were critical infrastructure and expressed a preference for placement and design of infrastructure
that also allowed for recreational uses on the land. Mr. Lee explained the City hired a team from
Avid Trails, Evergreen Skateparks, and Kimley Horn to provide conceptual designs.

Jay Hoeschler with Avid Trails presented two conceptual plans for a wheels park, inspired by
terraces at the nearby copper mine, and utilizing a portion of the Rocky Mountain Power Corridor.
Mr. Lee said he would not want the project to create a lot of additional landscape maintenance for
City staff.

Vice Chair Green suggested the terracing in Concept B could possibly be designed to function as an
amphitheater. Council Member Whitelock said she had noticed that terraced lawns at Daybreak
tended to get pretty beat up. She asked if it would be possible to shut down use of the wheels park
if necessary. Mr. Lee said he agreed the City would need to be able to control access and hours of
use.

Responding to a question from Vice Chair Green, Mr. Lee said the conceptual plans were designed
with spectator visibility in mind but were not intended to compete with competition-level tracks.
Council Member McConnehey commented that competitions tended to contribute to scheduling
conflicts.

B.      Adopt a Storm Drain Program
GIS Administrator Clint Hutchings introduced the Adopt-a-Storm-Drain program to the Council. He
showed photographs of storm drains in West Jordan covered with debris and garbage, and
emphasized citizens had a duty to keep storm drains clean as much as possible. Mr. Hutchings
described the West Jordan Storm Drain System, with 200 total miles of pipe, 5,585 storm drain
manholes, and 7,964 storm drain inlets.

Mr. Hutchings suggested the first step in initiating an Adopt-a-Storm-Drain program would be
public education. The program would be an opportunity for residents in West Jordan to prevent
water pollution. Citizens not living near a creek, river, or lake still had an impact on the health of
City waterways.

Mr. Hutchings said an app for the program was ready. Residents would be able to identify on a map
the storm drain they wanted to adopt and would be emailed instructions and guidelines. Council
Member McConnehey asked if texting would be possible. Staff responded text messages would be
possible.

Council Member Whitelock asked about litigation issues, and Council Member McConnehey
responded the program would mirror the Adopt-a-Highway program used by the state. City
Attorney Rob Wall emphasized the importance of communicating clearly to the residents what was
expected – what should and should not occur. He spoke of possible ways to limit City liability, and

C. Process and Procedure for Approval of Meeting Minutes
Council Member McConnehey said he liked the process for approving meeting minutes outlined in
the report with the agenda.

Council Office Director Alan R. Anderson explained that the proposed process could be implemented
for boards such as Municipal Building Authority minutes that often wait for a year before approving.
He noted that Council Member Green communicated with Council leadership suggesting the
introduction of the proposed process for consideration.

Council Chair Zach Jacob referenced the RDA, MBA, FES board meetings as well noting that it seemed
as if some of those meetings were held simply to approve minutes of a prior meeting that took less
than five minutes to hold. He felt the proposed process could eliminate that need and make the
approval process more streamlined.

Council Office Clerk Cindy Quick reviewed both City and State Code regarding written minutes of
public meetings. She oriented the Council with the following suggested procedure:
       • The Council Office Clerk will prepare and distribute DRAFT minutes to the Mayor,
          Department Heads, and the Public Body via email.
       • The Mayor, Department Heads, and Public Body will have ten days to review the minutes
          and submit any corrections / comments to the Council Office Clerk.
       • Submitted corrections will be made and updated minutes will then be redistributed to
          members of the Public Body.
       • The process will continue until all changes have been made and no additional corrections
          are submitted.
       • The minutes will then be finalized by adding an affidavit outlining the process for
          approval with no further objections and the minutes will stand approved.

Chair Jacob said the proposed process would prevent the need to schedule a meeting solely to
approve minutes of a previous meeting. Vice Chair Green said the Open Public Meetings Act required
draft minutes to be posted for public access within 30 days of a meeting. He spoke of how agenda
management software could help the process.

Council Member Whitelock said she could see the proposed process would make sense for
committees and boards but said she would not be in favor of the proposed process for regular
Council meetings. She said she did not see any harm in continuing to include City Council meeting
minutes for approval on the agenda and questioned who would police the proposed process.

Vice Chair Green commented the Council had not amended minutes in the last two years and
questioned whether anyone read the minutes. Council Member Whitelock responded she read
Council meeting minutes and provided feedback to staff.

City Attorney Rob Wall stated the Open Meetings Act was not clear on whether the proposed process
could happen. He said he believed there were provisions he could use to defend the practice, but
there was no other Council/Mayor form of government in the State of Utah that used the proposed
procedure, and he had only heard of two cities in the State that followed the procedure for council
meetings. He said it was clearly not considered the best practice.

Mr. Wall explained that Utah State Code 52-4-203 specified “approved minutes” meant written
minutes of an open meeting approved by the public body that held the open meeting and pointed
out the provision did not specify approval needed to take place in open meeting. The provision also
stated a public body shall establish and implement procedures for the public body’s approval of the
written minutes of each meeting. Mr. Wall expressed the opinion that most cities did not follow the
proposed process because of the lack of clarity in the Open Meetings Act. He said the prevailing
interpretation of the provision regarding roll-call vote was that a motion and a yes or no vote were
implied. Mr. Wall suggested it could be argued that a lack of response to a set of minutes within a
set amount of time could be interpreted as a yes vote. He emphasized that including minutes for
approval on an agenda with a motion and a roll-call vote clearly met the Open Meetings Act. Mr.
Wall said he was familiar with the situation that resulted in the Legislature writing the subject
provision in 2014, and said the provision was clearly written with the intent for approval to take
place by motion in open meeting. He commented that an email only seen by the Council did not
contribute to transparency.

Council Member Pack, having served on the Board of Adjustment and the Planning Commission in
the past, said there were times when both bodies had met with the only agenda item being approval
of minutes. He expressed the opinion the proposed process made sense for the Board of Adjustment
or perhaps the Planning Commission but pointed out that approving a consent item did not take
long on a Council agenda. He asked if the proposed process was intended for all committees, or if
select committees or groups could be identified that did not meet often.

Council Member McConnehey said he liked the idea primarily for RDA, MDA, and agency board
meetings. He said he did not like the idea of a lack of response being considered an affirmation. He
expressed a desire to try the proposed process for agency boards. Council Member McConnehey
said he did not see the need to change the process for Council meetings at that time.

Vice Chair Green said he wanted to make sure the City was compliant with the Open Public Meetings
Act. He said he was comfortable with applying the proposed process to boards and not changing the
Council meeting minutes approval process. Council Member Worthen said she agreed with trying
the proposed process with board minutes.
