   1. RESOLUTION TO SUBMIT TO THE VOTERS THE QUESTION OF WHETHER A STUDY COMMITTEE SHOULD
      BE ESTABLISHED TO EVALUATE THE FORM OF GOVERNMENT.

      Sharon Holmstrom, of Eden, read that the undersigned were submitting an informal petition asking the
      Commission to draft a resolution to put the question on the ballot of whether a study committee should be
      appointed to consider and possibly recommend a change in the form of county government. She said that
      last November a group of interested citizens approached the Commission asking them to consider drafting
      a resolution to authorize a county-wide vote for said committee and the rationale was that many in the
      county feel underrepresented, that the current three-member commission form of government is
      problematic because it combines both legislative and executive branches and that by creating the resolution
      they could save the citizens the trouble of gathering signatures and perhaps save money for all concerned.
      The group, including the Ogden Valley GEM committee and the League of Women Voters, was composed
      of citizens who had done in-depth research of citizen opinion of the current system and desire to look at
      other forms of government. She said that nothing had come of those meetings and now there was a different
      reason for a legislative body resolution. They were concerned the petition effort was now driven by elected
      officials who by current statute have a weighty say in appointment of the first three members for the
      “appointment council.” State Senators and Representatives choose one member, County Commissioners
      one member, and petition sponsors one member, then those three members choose two more. She said it is
      difficult to see how the council would not be selected by a few elected officials with little input from
      ordinary residents who signed the petition, that at this time it seems moot to require a petition, especially if
      the outcome is a politically charged study committee rather than a broader swath of residents, and the ballot
      would give the public an opportunity to vote and for a clean and expeditious process without politicizing
      it.
      Christopher Crockett, Deputy County Attorney, explained the process to adopt an optional plan under Utah
      law: A) 3-5 sponsors file a citizen-driven signed petition of 10% of the voters that voted in the last General
      Presidential Election. The Commission is then required by statute to set the matter, whether a study
      committee would be formed to evaluate the form of government at the next special election, and depending
      on the petition submittal date and certification by the County Clerk, it would either go on the June special
      election or a November election; or B) The county legislative body adopts a resolution requiring the
      question to be submitted on the ballot and then follow the process for the appropriate election. If the study
      committee is approved by the voters, a 5-member appointment council is created to select between 7-11
      members to sit on a study committee who cannot be elected or employed public officials. The committee
      has up to a year to make a recommendation. A review process then begins and once that is completed, the
      process stops. If voters decide to adopt a new form of government, the county officers under that form of
      government will be selected at the following General Election.
      Chair Ebert asked about the timeline when a petition with accompanying signatures is filed and Mr.
      Crockett said that the statute is somewhat vague but when it is filed, the other statutory requirements will
      have to be looked at for holding an election. There is a certain point in March or April where it would be
      impossible logistically to get the issue printed on the ballot and to satisfy other State law components and
      the issue may have to be on the November election.
      Ms. Holmstrom addressed Commissioner Gibson’s questions. She said that she would not have been the
      presenter if several of the others had been available, that they came to this point collectively when
      Commissioner Gibson came out as a signatory on the petition, and that the climate was perhaps now
      amenable to having the Commission move on a resolution. Commissioner Gibson said that his answer was
      the same as when they had spoken a year or so ago about whether the Commission would adopt a
      resolution—that it should be a grassroots-driven event, and that it would seem inappropriate for the
      Commission to drive a process in which the Commission might appear to have an ulterior motive. He is
      not convinced that a change in the form of government is necessary in this case. He is convinced, however,
      that this should be driven by individuals that do not have skin in the game. Ms. Holmstrom said that the
      public’s perception when they consistently asked for this resolution was the opposite, that people want the
      resolution and not to have the citizens force them into it. Commissioner Gibson said that someone who
      claimed to have had a conversation with Ms. Holmstrom had told him that the reason she wanted to be the
      petitioner was to be the third member or to choose the third member. She does not want to be that member
      but feels the petition committee members would like to choose that person.

Mr. Crockett said that it is an important legal issue to raise as to the process for selecting who will sit on
the appointment council for an appropriate petition: one selected by the State Representatives and Senators
who have districts within the county, one by the county legislative body and one by the petition sponsors,
and those three select the other two. The resolution statutory process is unclear as to how that the third
member gets selected if there is no petition, and the county is communicating with the Lt. Governor’s
Office. This would not become an issue until after the voters determine whether to form a study committee.
Chair Ebert asked what would be the cost of a special election and Ricky Hatch, County Clerk/Auditor,
responded that it would be $130,000-$150,000. There is a very good chance that there will be a Primary
Election next year, however, if certain parts of the county do not have a Primary, then it would have a direct
cost.
Chair Ebert invited public comments and following is a summary:
North Ogden Mayor Brent Taylor, a petitioner, expressed concern with the Commission trying to silence a
petition that had broad support from almost every city as well as half of the legislators, many former county
and State officials and citizens. He said that citizens are happy to collect the signatures, that he was
concerned that the petition coming before the Commission would have the net effect of ending a sincere
petition and putting control of the appointment of the study committee into the hands of the Commission,
and that the cost is specious as there will likely be a Primary Election. He said that this petition process
would get thousands of residents involved. He asked the Commission to respect the will of the people and
right to petition and not undertake the resolution and put the Commission solely in charge of this process.
Terri McCulloch, President of the Weber County League of Women Voters, stated that this topic had been
covered for years, they believe that the citizens deserve the right to choose the type of county government,
and their study was available online. They had come to the Commission asking them to support this and
put the question on the ballot, and she said they had been turned away, and she questioned why.
Oscar Mata, of Ogden, said that he was part of the Weber County Forward group and petitioner who filed
with the County Clerk’s Office. He said there had been talk about this petition as a means to force the
Commission’s hand, but this was not true, however, people should be forcing the Commission and not the
other way around. He said that this was citizen-led and hoped the Commission would support it.
Pleasant View Mayor Toby Mileski requested that the Commission let the people’s voice be heard on this
petition that would create fairness in forming the committee. He expressed concern that there may have
been retribution against Commissioner Gibson for his support on this.
Marcia White, said that about 8,600 people signed the petition, which should signal the citizen engagement.
Roy City Mayor Willard Cragun feels that with wider County Commission representation people may feel
better represented, and he suggested moving forward and letting the people decide.
Lori Brinkerhoff, of the Hooper City council, said that she did not feel there was anything sinister in having
this placed on the agenda. She supports women/minorities having greater representation on the board.
Richard Hyer, of the Ogden City council, expressed frustration with the form of government, that mixing
legislative and administrative roles can be difficult to balance, that the county does not have distinct
represented areas, and that the commissioners have great benefits packages but should step back for an
organic movement.
The commissioners reiterated throughout the meeting that they do not oppose studying a new form of
government and have never tried to silence anyone. They want this to be a purely grassroots movement
and are committed to the process. Commissioner Harvey’s was concerned with the potential associated
cost of $130,000-$180,000 for a special election—as a steward for the taxpayer he has a fiduciary
responsibility. Chair Ebert stated that getting people involved is extremely important. He explained that a
resolution could go forward and when the signatures come in, the petition process takes precedence. He is
committed to move forward with putting the question on the ballot at a fiscally responsible time and does
not need to have someone he knows to be on the study group. There had been some concern about the
Commission adopting a resolution and he asked Christopher Crockett, Deputy County Attorney, to respond
to the claim made that if the resolution was adopted today it would stop any group’s ability to continue a
petition drive. Mr. Crockett stated that it would not stop the ability to file a petition. On the reverse, a
citizen-filed petition prevents a resolution from being adopted for two years or until the question is resolved
