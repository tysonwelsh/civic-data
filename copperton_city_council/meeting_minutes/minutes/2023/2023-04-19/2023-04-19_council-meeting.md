Copperton Law Enforcement Services

              Council Member Stitzer stated there was an emergency meeting last week
wherein the Council discussed a resolution pertaining to HB 374 and the dissolution of the Unified
Police Department (UPD). It also discussed Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area
(SLVLESA) and different ways to create a taxing authority. The Council decided to hold an
additional monthly work session and to form a steering committee comprised of two council
members, some citizens, and Rio Tinto. The Council majority did not feel the resolution being
considered by some of the other metro townships was a good option for Copperton.

               Mayor Clayton stated Copperton needs to get in front of the State Legislature with
its representatives and request an interim study on whether the metro townships should
incorporate or annex into surrounding municipalities. The study would also examine the feasibility
of giving metro townships taxing authority. It costs approximately $350,000 a year to operate
Copperton Metro Township, but the township only collects $140,000 in tax revenue. A lot of extra
revenue comes from the unincorporated Salt Lake County taxes.

              Mayor Clayton, seconded by Council Member Stitzer, moved to hold a Work
Session on the first Wednesday of every month at 6:30 with an anchor location and a virtual
option. The meeting would only be used to discuss law enforcement services. The motion passed
unanimously.

                Council Member Stitzer, seconded by Mayor Clayton, moved to form a steering
committee comprised of Copperton citizens and at least two council members for the purpose of
researching law enforcement needs, future taxing authority, and future legislation. The committee
shall meet at least once a month. The motion passed unanimously.

              Mayor Clayton instructed Council members to bring name suggestions to the first
work session. He also asked Council members to circulate their suggestions for the Resolution.

             Later in the meeting, the Council decided the first Work Session would be held on
Tuesday, May 2nd instead of Wednesday, May 3rd so the meeting could take place before Council
Member Olsen meets with the UPD Board.

Coppertonutah.org Update

               Council Member Stitzer stated the website, coppertonutah.org, had previously
been taken down because it was not compliant with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)
due to the pictures on the website not having labels with audio descriptions for the visually
impaired. Since taking the website down, she has spent an extensive amount of time working
with Maridene Alexander, Communications Manager, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services
District (MSD), to correct all the issues. There is still work to do and there are some additional
things she would like to add, but the website is back up and running under the same domain
name.

             Mayor Clayton noted Maridene Alexander was able to learn the platform used by
Copperton, GoDaddy, which is substantially less expensive than what the other metro townships
use.

       Unified Police Department (UPD)
       Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

              Council Member Olsen stated UPD met to discuss HB 374. He did not take notes
on any other topic. The Board decided to meet once a month to discuss that topic exclusively
and that meeting will occur on the first Wednesday of the month at 9:00 a.m. The Board also
wants to hire a general manager to oversee the restructuring. The members agreed Jason
Mazuran, Chief of Police, UPD, should be the one to oversee the transition. They feel he would
do well in supporting officer retention. It is assumed he will take the role of CEO. The board,
minus Council Member Olsen, agreed to band together and support the restructuring process
without any desire to examine other options. Mazuran has informally accepted the role and the
Board will make a formal vote on the morning of Wednesday, May 3rd. Council Member Olsen
emailed a memorandum to the Council regarding the details of HB 374, and Harry Souvall, Legal

    Council Member Bailey

       Unified Fire Authority (UFA)
       Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA)

               Council Member Bailey stated there was discussion on whether HB 374 would
affect UFA, which is different from the Unified Police Department (UPD) because the chief is
appointed by the Board rather than being elected. Subsequently, the director does not control
funds. There is no conflict of interest and no double taxation. The Board discussed fire school,
which the Council members are invited to attend if they wish. The Board also discussed seasonal
flood awareness. It is working on its budget for the upcoming fiscal year. UFA wants to maintain
a 4-man crew, which will require an increase in user fees. A fee increase would require a tax
increase, which would require that the Board go through the Truth in Taxation process. Council
Member Bailey advised the Board that Copperton will not support a fee increase, but ultimately,
Copperton’s vote will not make a difference. It has been a difficult discussion. UFA intends to
keep Dominick Spatafora as its lobbyist.
