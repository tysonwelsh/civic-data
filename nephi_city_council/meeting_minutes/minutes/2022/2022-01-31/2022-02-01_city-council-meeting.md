TRAVIS WORWOOD HONORED:

   Mayor Seely honored out going City Treasurer Travis Worwood for his fine work for Nephi City. The
Mayor presented Mr. Worwood with a T-shirt that listed many of the projects that he had worked on
for the city in addition to some other gifts purchased from local businesses. Mr. Worwood had worked
on several economic development and land purchase projects and was highly praised for his influence
on helping the city progress.

DRUG TASK FORCE OFFICERS REPORTED:

   Officer Mike Nielson and Officer Toby Kingston gave the semi-annual report on the West Central
Strike Force. They handed out a report showing the number of drug related cases in Nephi, Juab
County and Millard County from July 1 thru December 31, 2021. During this time period, there were 59
drug-related cases with 66% of the cases being generated in Nephi City.


OFFICER SARAH ROBISON REPORTED ON ATV/OHV SHIFTS:

   Police Officer Sarah Robison reported to the council about the ATV/OHV code enforcement shifts that
were worked last fall. Officer Robison stated that 36 citations were issued, and the information was
documented in Spillman. Both the parent and the rider were issued citations. She thought that the
shifts were successful and planned on doing the enforcement again during the time of year that there
are more youth riders out and about.

  Councilor Memmott asked about the police vehicle lease payment listed on the warrant register. He

W. WRIGHT INDUSTRIAL ANNEXATION AGREEMENT:

   Councilor Memmott moved to authorize the Mayor to sign the W. Wright Industrial Annexation
Agreement. Councilor Worwood seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
The agreement requires Mr. Wright to comply with requirements of the industrial zone and will be
responsible for half of the street pavement and the cost of curb and gutter. It was determined that it
would be a 60’ street right-of-way with 50’ of asphalt to match 200 West in the industrial park in front of
National Vinyl, Horizon Metals.

W. WRIGHT INDUSTRIAL ANNEXATION ORDINANCE 02-01-2022 ADOPTED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that Levi Wright the owner of Wright Buildings had petitioned
and gone through the proper steps to annex his property north of Sunset Rail. The property was
classified in the industrial zone, Councilor Worwood moved to adopt Ordinance 02-01-2022: AN
ORDINANCE ANNEXING CERTAIN PROPERTY TO NEPHI CITY AND ESTABLISHING ZONING CLASSIFICATION
FOR SAID PROPERTY. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

March 14-17, 2022, and a final request for himself to travel to the APPA national conference in June in

motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

JUAB SCHOOL DISTRICT LAND SWAP AGREEMENT APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council that last year the city negotiated a deal with the Juab School
District to help facilitate the movement of their bus barn. The negotiations took the form of a land swap
that enabled the school district to receive desirable property for their bus barn and provided the city
with property for a park on the south side.
The future bus barn property is located just south of the mulch pile and north of 1500 North along
Blackett Lane (approximately 250 West). The bus barn would begin on the south side of the property
and leave room for expansion to the north. The mulch pile could still be used in the near future but
would need to be moved in the long run.
    The property that will be acquired by the city is a triangle piece just north of the Red Cliffs Elementary
School. This property is adjacent to property already owned by the city and can be used to create a
large park for the south end residents. This property isn’t used frequently by the elementary school and
is tough to maintain. This agreement to exchange properties seems like the best use for both pieces of
land. This also assists the city with the goal found in the Parks and Recreation Master Plan of having
regional parks in each quadrant of the city.
  The agreement basically outlines the property exchange and obligates the city to assist with
annexation. It also requires, upon request, to fence off the triangle property from the elementary
property. The city also agrees to pay surveying, deed, and closing costs for the triangle property. In
exchange, the school district agrees to cooperate with the city on annexing the mulch pile property and
to cover the closing costs of the same. They also agree to allow the city to continue to use the mulch
pile until such time as it would be needed for bus barn expansion.
    Councilor Callaway moved to approve the Juab School District land swap agreement. Councilor Ostler
seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
