RESOLUTION 20-35: EXTEND THE LOCAL EMERGENCY DECLARATION
CONCERNING THE COVID-19 PANDEMIC
Mayor Bigelow presented proposed Resolution 20-35 that would extend the Local
Emergency Declaration concerning the Covid-19 Pandemic.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       On March 15, the City Manager declared a local emergency in response to the
       COVID-19 pandemic. The Utah Emergency Management Act provides that such
       a declaration cannot be effective for more than thirty days without the approval of
       the City Council. It is apparent that the COVID-19 pandemic will continue to be a
       major concern for federal, state, and local governments for some time and that
       emergency conditions will continue for more than thirty days. Accordingly, this
       resolution extends the declaration of a local emergency to May 15, 2020.

       If emergency conditions no longer exist, the local emergency declaration can be
       rescinded by resolution prior to May 15. If the emergency continues beyond that
       time, the emergency declaration can be extended in the same manner.

The City Council will consider Resolution 20-35 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
April 14, 2020 at 6:30 P.M.

RESOLUTION 20-36: APPROVE A COOPERATIVE AGREEMENT BETWEEN
THE CITY AND THE UTAH DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION FOR THE
INSTALLATION OF BLYNCSY SENSORS
Wayne Pyle, City Manager, and Mark Nord, CED, presented proposed Resolution 20-36
that would approve a Cooperative Agreement between the City and the Utah Department
of Transportation for the installation of Blyncsy Sensors.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:
       MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – APRIL 7, 2020

       UDOT is purchasing Blyncsy sensors to assist with traffic reporting and modualtion
       along 3500 South and along the Redwood Road corridor. UDOT has requested to
       piggy back on the West Valley City contract with Blyncsy and will be purchasing
       9 sensors to be installed along the State road (Redwood Road) for real time
       reporting of traffic counts. UDOT will provide WVC all of the data from the UDOT
       sensors to both reciprocate from the reporting.

       West Valley City has identified the corridor along 3500 south and Redwood road
       to study traffic patterns in order to plan for future growth. This information will
       also help economic development as we will be able to provide real time traffic
       reports to retail clients.

Mayor Bigelow asked how many sensors there are. Wayne replied 9 but indicated that the
City will likely do more in the future. Councilmember Buhler asked how far down
Redwood Road and 3500 South the sensors will go. Mark explained where the sensors will
be located. Councilmember Buhler asked if this is an accommodation for UDOT. Mark
replied yes but noted that the City can use the data as well to understand where traffic is
going when entering the City. Councilmember Buhler asked if this is a test for the City to
see whether this is useful information. Mark replied yes but noted that UDOT has already
used Blyncsy information in Park City. Councilmember Lang asked if the City will incur
any cost. Mark replied that UDOT will pay fees. Councilmember Huynh asked when the
City will obtain results. Mark replied that once the sensors are up and running, real time
data will be available. Mayor Bigelow asked if the sensors will capture Bluetooth or cell
phones that are turned off. Mark replied that there are a lot of different metrics used but the
most prominent is Bluetooth and cell phone. Councilmember Nordfelt asked if the
information is anonymous. Mark replied yes.

The City Council will consider Resolution 20-36 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
April 14, 2020 at 6:30 P.M.

COMMUNICATIONS
A.  COUNCIL CALENDAR
    Mayor Bigelow referenced a Memorandum previously received from the City
    Manager that outlined upcoming meetings and events.

      Mayor Bigelow stated that many events have been canceled due to COVID-19.
      Councilmember Christensen noted that many items will likely continue to be
      canceled into May as well. Wayne agreed and stated that larger events further out
      are being canceled. He stated that there is contemplation on whether WestFest will
      be able to proceed this year.

      Wayne indicated that the Council directed staff to utilize funds to construct a float
      for a 4th of July and Pioneer Day parade. He indicated that the company constructing
      the float is requiring that the City put a down payment of half the cost,
      approximately $8,500. Wayne stated that at this time, the City can’t predict whether
      there will be a parade for the float this year and asked how the Council would like
      to proceed. Councilmember Huynh asked if there is any indication on the status of
      the parades. Wayne replied no. Councilmember Huynh stated that he doesn’t feel
      money should be spent on a float at this time. Councilmember Fitisemanu asked if
      the company would be willing to delay payment given the current circumstances.
      Sam Johnson, Pubic Relations Director, stated that the company needs to proceed
      on building the float in order to meet the deadline and therefore require the
      payment. Councilmember Buhler stated that he doesn’t want to cancel too many
      events and he feels it’s important to keep as many people employed as possible so
      he would like to proceed as planned. He noted that if the float can’t be used this
      MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – APRIL 7, 2020
                           -8-


       year, it can be used next year. Sam agreed and noted that the City would just need
       to find a location to store it. Councilmember Christensen stated that he doesn’t want
       to cancel everything and would like to proceed. Councilmember Huynh stated that
       everyone wants everything to return to normal quickly but he feels it will be a
       gradual return and the parade likely won’t occur. Mayor Bigelow stated that it may
       be a risk but he supports moving forward with the float. Councilmember Lang
       stated that if the Council commits to the $8,500, they need to commit to the entire
       cost of the float. She noted that there needs to be a place to store the float. Wayne
       stated there is no confirmed location to store the float at this time. The Mayor and
       Council agreed to proceed with the float.
