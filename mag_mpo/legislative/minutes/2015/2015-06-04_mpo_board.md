Public Comment No public comments Adopt the Draft TransPlan40 - Shawn Eliot The draft TransPlan40 document and accompanying appendices shows where we are today, what we plan for the future, and how do we afford the investment. The Plan has 3 phases: Phase 1 2015-2024, Phase 2 2025-2024, and Phase 3 2035-2040. The proposed total funding for 25 years is $15.2 billion. Projected new revenue of $13.9 billion with a short fall of $1.3 billion for transit projects and maintenance projects. 80 highway projects for a total of $4.2 billion. Funding is projected to be available for the new and widened highway projects on arterial roads and freeways in the phase they are needed. Proposed transit projects total $2.7 billion that includes BRT Provo/Orem, State Street BRT American Fork to Orem, commuter rail to Payson and light rail Draper to Lehi. The official Public Comment went from April 4th to May 5th. We had three focus groups in the February and March with MAG Staff, a BYU graduate planning class, and the Utah County Realtors Association for a total of 48 participates. The Public Meeting was on April 29th, with 50 people attending. During the public comment period, in staff opinion, there were no significant comments that would require an additional 30 comment period. The Technical Advisory Committee on May 26, 2015 recommend Regional Planning adopt TransPlan40. Council Member Shellie Baertsch moved the MPO Regional Planning Committee, after adding the Utah Lake Shore Trail Saratoga Springs to Orem and based on the recommendation from MPO staff and the MPO Technical Advisory Committee, adopt TransPlan40 Regional Transportation Plan and its accompanying appendixes. Mayor Rick Moore seconded the motion. The motion carried unanimously. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning June 4, 2015 Page 3 Adopt the Draft Air Quality Conformity Determination Document - Susan Hardy The Air Quality Determination received four comments that were all editorial corrections. The Determination Document meets all federal requirements including being fiscally constrained and meeting air quality conformity rules. Based on the findings of this report, and pending EPAs publication of the Trading Rule (R307-111) for Utah County in the Federal Register - a positive conformity determination for PM10 is made for the 2040 Regional Transportation Plan and TIP. The Rule R307-311 – Utah County Trading of Motor Vehicle Emission Budgets for PM10 Transportation Conformity was published in the Federal Register on May 18, 2015, the rule becomes effective on July 17, 2015 if no significant comments are received. A concurrence of the Conformity Determination report of the Regional Transportation Plan by FHWA is necessary to Utah County to allow new capacity increasing transportation projects to be developed and constructed. Mayor Bert Wilson move that Regional Planning Committee adopt the Air Quality Conformity Determination Report pending EPA’s ruling. Mayor Mike Daniels seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Andrew was asked to summarize and give the history of the 4 quarters UCA Title 59 Chapter 12 1st quarter Section 2213–County, city, or town option sales and use tax to fund a system for public transit -- Base -- Rate. Adopted by public vote in Provo and Orem in 1984. Adopted by public vote in other communities 1989-2010. (In 2011, the smallest 6 towns and unincorporated Utah County were included by county action). May only be used for transit. Funds go directly to UTA 2nd quarter Section 2216–County option sales and use tax for a fixed guideway, to fund a system for public transit, or for highways -- Base -- Rate -- Allocation and expenditure of revenues. Adopted by public vote 2006. Designated 87% fixed guideway (Frontrunner), 5% other transit (like BRT), 8% roads. May only be used in the percentages voted on by the public. To change the percentages it must go back to a vote of the Mountainland MPO Regional Planning June 4, 2015 Page 4 public. Later changed to .30 due to removal of tax on food. 92% of the funds go directly to UTA, 8% go to Utah County 3rd quarter Section 2218 County, city, or town option sales and use tax for airports, highways, and systems for public transit -- Base -- Rate -- Administration of sales and use tax -- Voter approval exception. Adopted by Commission Action 2008 (law was later amended to require vote of the public, but exception still remains in title). May be used for airports, highways and systems for public transit. Silos for State, Regional, and Right-of-way projects taken out in 2014, while clarification on trails was added. Requires MPO approval of projects. All funds go to Utah County. 4th quarter Section 2219 County option sales and use tax for highways and public transit -- Base -- Rate -- Distribution and expenditure of revenue -- Revenue may not supplant existing budgeted transportation revenue. (Effective 7/1/2015) Not adopted. Requires a vote of the public. Transit 40%, Cities, Towns, and County 40% (50:50 pop/sales), and County 20%. Funds will go directly to UTA and the local jurisdictions based on the required allocation. Other funds for Transportation: Motor Fuel, Vehicle Registration, Special Permits, Auto related sales tax, CHNF sales tax. Committee members are invited to attend the MPO Finance Committee meeting on June 11th, 11:30 am, MAG Conference Room for more discussion on the Local Option Sales Tax.
