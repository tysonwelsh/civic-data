Citizen Public Input
Chelsie Kemper stated she is enthusiastic about food cultivation and would like
to share her personal experiences with the community. She offered to provide insights and
examples from her own gardening efforts, including input from others. She suggested planting
perennials and other various plants, noting that some plants have medicinal uses. She proposed
starting with small gardens and expanding based on community interest.
Janet Haskins stated she is interested in the Unified Police Department (UPD)
and Sheriff’s Office decision-making process. It is important to establish clear, objective criteria
for assessing each choice, and wanted to understand what the criteria would be.
Jeff Carlstrom, Local Historian, offered his assistance in various capacities,
including forming subcommittees and suggesting the creation of an informal history club. Utilizing
funds from the old Emigration Canyon Historical Society to support the subcommittee may be an
option.

Unified Police Department (UPD)
Detective Dawn Larsen stated Emigration Canyon had 32 calls for service. There
were five frauds in two months. She cautioned the community against providing money or
information to individuals. Most citations were for speeding. The race being held on April 6 has
been approved.
Council Member Pinon stated residents have concerns about bicycles not
stopping at bus stops.
Detective Larsen stated she had been following buses in the morning to enforce
this very problem.
Chief April Morse stated she previously provided information regarding the sound
camera. There is a proposed contract price for a trial and error period. She is available when the
Council is prepared to discuss the information further.

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)
Chief Bryan Case stated fire school is scheduled for April 12th and is an all-day
event. Various new vehicles are coming in. UFA has adopted the Strategic Plan which can be
viewed on UFA’s website. CPR classes are being offered to communities and can be scheduled
on the website. There were four responses in February, including a power line down, a
commercial smoke alarm, and a minor accident involving cars sliding into guardrails. One car
struck a firefighter while attempting to pass a fire truck, resulting in a bystander being pinned.
Emergency responders freed the individual, who was hospitalized, while the firefighter remained
unharmed.

Council Business
Municipal Code Update Project
Matt Starley, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD),
delivered a PowerPoint presentation on the municipal code update. He reviewed the general plan
guidance, including the character area, land use, and economic development work programs.
Project goals include measuring success by ensuring compliance with State Legislation,
conformity with the adopted General Plan, promoting ease of use for staff and residents, meeting
unique needs, and preserving the character of the community. He reviewed the project summary,
parties involved, anticipated work, and timeline.

Law Enforcement Determination
Mayor Smolka stated the criteria the Council is using to decide what law
enforcement agency to contract with includes, cost of law enforcement, level of service, backup
response time, and number of officers provided.
There is an $18,000 difference between the Unified Police Department (UPD) and the Sheriff’s
Office. With UPD, the Council can choose either 1.5 or 2 officers to service the canyon. The
backup response is better because UPD officers are in Millcreek as opposed to the other side of
the valley or in other canyons.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes stated part of the decision is being able to work with the
needs of the canyon.
Council Member Harris stated UPD has been more responsive to questions
asked by the Council and there has been a long-standing relationship with UPD.

Update on H.B. 35 – Converting Emigration Canyon Metro Township to a City
Cameron Platt stated after Emigration Canyon becomes a city, it will move to a
fiscal budget year. The Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) will propose a budget
based on budget history to project expenses. The Council will need to hold a public hearing to
receive public comment on the proposed budget. Emigration Canyon’s code continues to be
updated. A clerk/recorder will need to be hired.
Mayor Smolka stated Deputy Mayor Hawkes would be converting the website
from a .org domain to a .gov.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes stated emails will be changed to a .gov domain and emails
will need to be archived.
Mayor Smolka stated the Council needs to choose a name for the city and various
logo styles have been distributed.
Council Member Pinon stated he preferred Emigration Canyon.
Deputy Mayor Hawkes stated the logo does not need to be changed much. The
logo is simple and represents the community well. Council Member Pinon made some edits.
Mayor Smolka stated the Council needs to consider sending a postcard explaining
the conversion to a city.
