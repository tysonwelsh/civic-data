Public Comments

                Rebecca Colonna stated a police officer recently visited her home inquiring about
some trailers on her property. He asked how long the trailers would be there. Later, on June
21st, she received a letter from the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) giving her
until June 23rd (two days) to remove the trailers. She stated she has told the Magna Metro
Township Council multiple times that the trailers will not be there permanently, and that she is
simply trying to help Donnie Sweazey, who owns the trailers, until he can relocate them and get
them out of town as the Council has requested. Mr. Sweazey is one person and is working as
fast as he can. She has spoken with her attorney who says the trailers are not a hazard and are
not obstructing traffic. She told the Council if it wants to fight, she will fight.

               Todd Richards stated he received a letter from Salt Lake County regarding
fencing and right-of-way violation. He was told he needed to remove the fence to allow for public
right-of-way, but the letter did not state which fence needed to be removed.

            Sheenia Stuart spoke about the left-hand turn lane that was recently removed at
9180 West. She has seen a lot of accidents in that area. She heard through the grapevine the

Community Stakeholder Reports

4th of July

               Kari Duckworth stated things are coming together well for the Fourth of July. She
and Sarah work well as a team, and the committee has done a good job spreading the word.
They have 53 entries into the parade with a few multiple vehicles. She thinks the parade will last
somewhere between 60 and 90 minutes. The vendor count is at 20; three food trucks, six food
booths, and 11 non-food merchandise and information vendors. There are about 17 volunteers.
For safety reasons, they will have one entrance and one exit. Unified Police Department is helping
with that. She requested a budget adjustment somewhere between $100 to $225 to provide a
meal for the volunteers who would be working all day, as many items that were originally in the
budget are now being donated free of charge. She had extra shirts and breakfast tickets for
Council members. Information about the event is posted on the township website and the links
are on the Facebook page.

Pleasant Green Cemetery

                Sharon Nichols stated the cemetery sold three headstone sets, and had one full
adult residential burial. The cemetery has been fully mowed and cleaned up. She had a chance
to review the new software and is very impressed with it. It is web-based and has cloud storage.
There are GPS coordinates to assist the user in locating a specific grave. It works similar to
Google directions. The directions and pictures can be inserted into the existing software. The
cost for all the upgrades would be $6,290, the online subscription fee would be $550 annually,
and the web software subscription fee would be $800 annually. The total for the first year would
be $6,840. She gave the bill to Rori Andreason, but asked her not to pay it until the services have
been received.

Consider Resolution No. 23-06-03 Appointing David Brickey as the Full-Time City Administrator
for the Magna Metro Township Council

               Paul Ashton, Legal Counsel, Magna Metro Township, reviewed the resolution.

              David Brickey introduced himself to the Council and shared some personal
information about his background and work history.

               Council Member Pierce asked if Council Member Hull had provided any sort of
written statement.

                Council Member Barney stated, for the sake of transparency, the opening was
posted publicly, there were four applications, and he and Council Member Hull attempted to set
up interviews with each one. In the process of interviewing, two candidates withdrew and the
other two were interviewed by Council Members Hull and Barney. There was a closed session
at the end of the last Council meeting wherein the candidates were interviewed by the remaining
Council members. The Council then met two Mondays ago in another closed session to discuss
the merits of each candidate and ultimately, David Brickey was selected.

             Council Member Barney, seconded by Council Member Pierce, moved to approve
the appointment. The motion passed unanimously.

Council Reports

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD)

                Council Member Barney stated there was a lot of discussion about recycling at
the most recent meeting. Sometimes the District pays to recycle and other times it gets paid.
Contamination is a big issue currently; for example, greasy pizza boxes, plastic grocery bags, etc.
If contaminated batches come in, the entire batch is often thrown away because there is not time
to sort through everything. WFWRD wants to provide stickers to help educate people on what is
and is not recyclable. There is also talk on not recycling plastic grocery bags because recycling
them is costly and difficult. WFWRD is hoping they will be banned eventually. The reservation
program for the dumpsters is going well. WFWRD has implemented an apprenticeship program
to recruit and retain drivers.

Magna Mosquito Abatement District
