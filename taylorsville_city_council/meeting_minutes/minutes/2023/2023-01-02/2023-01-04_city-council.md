     We are at our best when we are civilized."

          1. 2    Mayor' s Report


Mayor Kristie Overson reported on the many city activities that had taken place throughout
December. Among other things, a holiday open house was held for city contractors, developers,
business owners and elected officials of other government entities. There had also been a
Taylorsville /Salt Lake Community College Orchestra holiday concert at the beginning of the
holiday season, and the Mayor expressed her appreciation to the Taylorsville Arts Council who
she felt was a great asset to the City.

As always, the annual Saturday with Santa event was a hit and brought a high amount of
community participation. The Mayor wished to thank the Historic Preservation and Parks &
Recreation committees as well as many volunteers including the Youth Council and various school
groups. She said it was particularly fun to see Santa arrive on a UFA fire truck.

Taylorsville participated in the Wreaths Across America program on December 17th and held a
brief ceremony at city hall. From there everyone moved to the Taylorsville Cemetery where
holiday wreaths were lain at the grave sites of each of the 255 veterans interred there. Mayor
Overson thanked the many people involved in honoring local veterans and making this a truly
memorable experience.




Mayor Overson explained that crossing guards were Taylorsville' s unsung heroes and helped local
children cross the streets safely. She had taken the opportunity before Christmas to visit each
school crossing guard while they were on duty as she had wished to express her appreciation to
them for the job they each did each day.

      Judge Chris Bown explained that there had been some turnover in the court during the
previous year but that it was fully staffed once again.

The Judge then explained the time-to- disposition goals for criminal, civil and traffic cases. ` Time-
to- disposition referred to the amount of time between when a case was filed in the court and when
it was ultimately adjudicated. The state' s goal for criminal matters to be adjudicated was within
180 days, for small claims cases it was 270 days and for traffic matters it was 90 days. Over the
past twelve months,      89% of the criminal cases had met the goal as had 100% of the small claims
matters. Judge Bown pointed out that the overall number of small claims cases filed in the court
had decreased due to the increased use of on- line dispute resolution which was a more efficient
way to resolve such cases. Finally, 95% of the traffic cases had met the time-to- disposition goal
of 90 days.


Councilmember Harker congratulated Judge Bown on the improved case disposition numbers.
Judge Bown pointed out that although he might be the face of the court, he credited the clerical
staff and said that they were the ones who did the work.

The Judge then spoke more in-depth about continuing to allow many cases to be handled
electronically.


City Attorney Tracy Cowdell pointed out to the Council that Judge Bown had been very
accommodating to one of the prosecutors who continued to recover from a very serious case of
Covid but who was still on oxygen and unable to come to the court in person. Mr. Cowdell
appreciated the judge for making it possible for the attorney to continue to remain in the workforce.

6.       FINANCIAL MATTERS


         6. 1     Discussion Regarding Mid- Year Budget Adjustments for Fiscal Year 2022-
                  2023— Scott Harrington



     Assistant City Administrator and Chief Financial Officer Scott Harrington explained the
approximately$ 2 million in proposed mid-year changes to the FY2022- 2023 budget.
         The amendments recognized the expense/ revenue for the purchase of$ 1, 000,000 in police
         vehicles which had been authorized the previous October
         There was an increase of some professional fees
         The proposed amendments included market adjustments for some police officers which it
         was hoped would be retroactive to January 1, 2023

Mr. Harrington explained those represented the bulk of the recommended changes. He also
reminded the councilmembers that he had emailed the proposed budget adjustments to them prior
to the meeting and was available to meet with them individually to address any questions they had.
He also pointed out that there would be a public hearing at the following council meeting so that
city residents could weigh in. To that end, the recommended budget amendments would soon be
posted on the city website for public review.

7.       OTHER MATTERS


         7. 1     City Council Chair and Vice Chair Elections— Jamie Brooks/Kris Heineman

El City Recorder Jamie Brooks explained that the city council rules specified that nominations
for Council Chair and Vice-Chair be submitted in writing to her. Councilmembers Knudsen and
Harker had both been nominated to serve as Chair for 2023 while the same councilmembers had
been nominated for Vice- Chair, as had Councilmember Cochran.


Council Coordinator Kris Heineman then distributed ballots for both positions. When all indicated
they had filled out their ballots, Ms. Heineman collected them. She then announced that there was
a 2- 2 tie for council chair so essentially, the vote failed. After discussing the matter with City

Attorney Tracy Cowdell, it was determined that Chair Barbieri would remain in place until the
following meeting when it was hoped that Councilmember Burgess would be present, and voting
could be repeated with the     full council present.
