4.   Public hearing and possible action for a two (2) lot minor subdivision 
Chokecherry Hill Subdivision, Parcel CD-119-C-1; 2601 West State Road 32, Peoa, 
Utah; Lorie Leavitt, applicant. – Tiffanie Northrup-Robinson, Senior Planner 
 
Planner Northrup-Robinson shared the background for this application. The applicant is 
proposing to subdivide the parcel into one additional lot. The application was submitted in 
February 2018, prior to the current Code and zoning that went into effect in June 2018. At 
the time of application, the Highway Corridor and Agricultural protection zones were still 
in effect. The Code allowed for parcels of at least two acres to be divided into two lots. The 
parcel has an existing residence, an accessory dwelling and an outbuilding. There is a 
stream that runs through the lot and the developable area will allow for the 100-foot 
stream setback to be met. Staff has found that the application would meet the Standards of 
the Code. Due to the high water table the Health Department is recommending an 
alternative septic system. Staff recommends the Planning Commission forward a positive 
recommendation to the Community Development Director for this application. 
Chair Clyde asked for clarification about the shared driveway. Planner Northrup-
Robinson showed the existing access and shared driveway. The applicant, Lorie Leavitt 
stated the existing driveway is a paved ag driveway that was cleared through UDOT. 
 
The public hearing was opened, no public comment was made. The public hearing was closed.

1. On February 28, 2018, Lorie Leavitt submitted a Sketch Plan and Final Subdivision 
application for a proposed minor Subdivision on Parcel CD-119-C-1.

4. The zoning for Parcel CD-119-C is Highway Corridor (HC) and Agricultural Protection.

Development Code that allows a parcel in the HC zone that contains two (2) or more acres to be 
split into two (2) lots.

7. Parcel CD-119-C-1 contains an existing residence and will become Lot 1 of the subdivision. 
8. There will be a total of two (2) lots within the boundaries of the subdivision. 
9. There is an existing driveway to serve Lot 1. Lot 2 is currently accessed from a joint access 
point from SR32. 
10. The subdivision plat contains a right-of-way easement that runs through Lots 1 and 2. 
11. Each lot has legal access, reviewed and approved by the Summit County Engineering 
Department and UDOT. 
12. The perimeter of the subject property is currently fenced. 
13. Lot 2 will be serviced by an individual well; proof of water has been submitted and will be 
reviewed and approved prior to building permit issuance. 
14. There is an existing septic system on Lot 1. A perc test has been conducted on Lot 2. The 
Summit County Health Department has reviewed and recommended the use of an alternative 
septic system, due to the high water table on the site. 
15. The proposed subdivision is not located on a hillside. 
16. The parcel is located within the FEMA Flood Plain Zone X. 
17. There are no wetlands within the proposed subdivision. 
18. There are no slopes exceeding thirty percent (30%) within the proposed subdivision. 
19. There are no ridgetops located within the proposed subdivision. 
20. The addition of one (1) lot will not cause the traffic volume on the adjacent public roads to 
fall below the design capacity of the roadways, based on a review by Summit County 
Engineering Department.

22. The proposed subdivision has year round access and is not located within a remote area. 
23. The applicant is not proposing gates to the subdivision that would be locked. 
24. Lot 1 contains 2.54 acres; Lot 2 contains 1.55 acres.

2. Access to the lots have been consolidated to the greatest extent practicable and reasonable.

5. The project complies with infrastructure standards provided in Chapter 6 of the Code. 
6. The minimum lot size for the parcels is greater than one acre. 
7. The lots have been placed within five hundred feet (500’) of the road.

5. Public Hearing and possible action regarding an amendment to Lot 5 and Parcel A 
of the Orgill Subdivision and Parcel NS-1135-X; 2465 E Chalk Creek Road, Coalville, 
Utah; Rodney Davis, Applicant. – Sean Lewis, AICP, County Planner  
 
Planner Lewis shared the background for this discussion. The Orgill Subdivision is located 
near the Upton Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints chapel. The owners of Lot 5 of 
the Orgill Subdivision have entered into an agreement with the Church to sell a portion of 
Lot 5 to the Church for the purposes of expanding the parking lot. The portion that will 
transfer between properties was shown. The portion of the Subdivision to be sold would 
technically be vacated, and the owners would like to take a portion of Agricultural sub 
parcel A into the subdivision to get a density bonus. Staff has requested that parcel A be 
added to the Orgill Subdivision for the parcel to meet the minimum one-acre size for the 
AG20 zoning district. There is a note on the plat that no structures other than agricultural 
structures be built on Parcel A and that restriction will continue for the Parcel A portion of 
Lot 5. 
Commissioner Wilde asked how close the new property line would be to the existing 
house on Lot 5. Planner Lewis answered that the 12-foot minimum setback will still be 
met.

6. Public Hearing and possible action regarding the proposed Camp Oakley 
Conditional Use Permit Amendment; Mile 4.7, Weber Canyon, Summit County, Utah; 
Paul Feser, Applicant. – Amir Caus, AICP, County Planner  
 
Planner Caus shared the background for this discussion. Camp Oakley was approved a few 
years ago in Weber Canyon as a camp that works primarily with children with disabilities. 
This amendment proposes two garages/storage buildings, a stable with temporary housing 
over the top, and a caretaker unit. The applicant would also like to incorporate Lot 6 of the 
ranch. Lot 6 would remain as is but be incorporated into the ranch operation. There would 
be no additional capacity, and Staff is recommending approval. 
 
The public hearing was opened, no public comment was made. The public hearing was closed.

Shawn Potter, Applicant. – Amir Caus, AICP, County Planner

Robin Milne, Promontory General Manager stated the developer is excited for the new 
segmented neighborhood and feels that all the Planning Commission concerns have been 
mitigated. There is demand for homes as proposed. 
Chair Clyde shared his concern about the public comment that was received from 
residents of the neighboring subdivision. The neighbors did not anticipate being next door 
to very large homes as proposed. There are no reciprocal covenants with the Pinnacle 
project and the neighboring lots, so there is no legally enforceable provision to prevent the 
Pinnacle homes being built as proposed. The legitimacy of the neighbors’ concerns about 
the larger home size not being anticipated should be addressed by Promontory with those 
neighbors. Ms. Milne stated there were no buying decisions made on assurances of the size 
of other homes other than what is in the Development Agreement. The request for the 
larger home size is because the market that is being addressed wants no limits on what 
they might imagine for a luxury property. So far, buyers do not want to build as large as 
22,000 square feet but from a marketing standpoint, the buyer should be able to build a 
luxury custom home. Chair Clyde stated that people will always stretch the limit, so if the 
22,000 square foot home size is approved, someone will build a home that size. 
Commissioner Rydalch stated the most recent public comment received indicated that 
the neighbor was assured by the architect at time of purchase that there would essentially
