Planner Milliner said this is the second public hearing in regard to lighting regulations.  
He began with a quick presentation of what is being proposed.  He showed photos 
demonstrating the light pollution from areas along the Wasatch Front.  The proposed 
amendments will update the County’s current regulations and bring them into 
compliance with best practices.   
 
Planner Milliner said guidance was given to Summit County on how to reduce light 
pollution by the Coalition for Dark Skies, the International Dark Skies Coalition, and the 
Utah Consortium for the Colorado Plateau.  The Colorado Plateau (defined as southern 
Utah and Colorado, along with parts of Arizona and New Mexico) is the darkest area 
within the contiguous 48 states.  These entities recommend that the ordinance 
addresses the following six items: 
1. Adding lights only when and where they are needed 
2. Light fixtures should be shielded to reduce glare 
3. Reduce blue light emissions 
4. The minimum amount of light necessary for the task 
5. The use of motion sensors and timers  
6. The use of energy efficient plans 
 
Planner Milliner said the proposed language includes an amortization of the regulation.  He 
explained that if a person comes to the County for a building permit, they will need to bring 
the entire building into compliance.  If a light fixture is damaged, it must be replaced with 
an updated complying fixture.   
 
Chair Peck asked if a light fixture on the outside of her house breaks, would she need to 
come to the Planning Department to get a building permit in order to replace it.  Planner 
Milliner said that is correct.  Director Putt added if someone replaces a light fixture that is 
not compliant with the Code, they could be subject to an enforcement action.  If a neighbor 
complains that a shielded fixture was replaced with one that is not shielded and now a very 
bright light is shining, the enforcement officer will be sent.  Defective light fixtures would 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
November 13, 2018

need to be replaced with one that complies with the Code.  Enforcement would be 
complaint driven. 
 
Planner Milliner said a goal of the ordinance is that within seven years all outdoor lighting 
will be brought into compliance.  Director Putt said they have dark sky standards now, but 
are trying to take it to the next level and enact ordinances.  He said the Basin is doing a 
good job at focusing lights downward.  The County is not doing a good job at preventing 
horizontal light trespass.  Planner Milliner showed examples of different types of light 
fixtures and how effective they are.  He pointed out the most effective fixtures.  Chair Peck 
asked if everyone has to come into conformance within seven years.  Planner Milliner 
responded that is correct.   
 
Planner Milliner said the document updates the language from foot-candles with lumens 
and Kelvins.  Kelvins measure the color temperature of the lights.   A warmer color 
temperature is preferred.  This is because cool lights have negative impacts on wildlife and 
people.  Examples were shown of different types of lighting.
