Election of Officers - Andrew Jackson, MAG Staff Each year in February a Chair and Vice Chair are elected. Article II, Section 4 (1), in the MPO Bylaws states: The Mountainland Regional Planning Committee, in consultation with MPO staff, shall nominate from its membership for the positions of Chair and Vice Chair. Both Chair and Vice Chair shall be elected officials from jurisdictions within the MPO. Ratification of nomination shall require a simple majority vote. The position of Chair and Vice Chair shall be for one-year terms. Successive terms are not limited. Individuals may request to be replaced at any time. Responsibilities of the Chair position include chairing both MPO Regional Planning Committee and MPO Finance Committee meeting, in addition to attending Joint Policy Advisory Committee, Mountainland Steering Committee, Active Transportation Committee, WFRC Regional Growth Committee, and ad hoc meetings in Salt Lake City. Mayor Steve Leifson and Mayor Jim Miller expressed interest in serving another term as Chair and Vice of the MPO Regional Planning Committee. Contingency Funding Requests - Bob Allen, MAG a. Canyon Road In 2014, Utah County was awarded $6,054,900 of county transportation tax funds, for the Canyon Road/Pleasant Grove 100 East project through the MAG TIP selection process. Due to additional right of way costs, unforeseen conditions and project delays, Utah County is requesting the 10% contingency funds for the project totaling $605,490. Contingency requests over 10% of the project cost and/or over $500,000, require MPO Regional Planning Committee approval. Utah County will make up the remaining balance. Recommendation and approval were given by TAC that $605,490 of contingency funds be released to the Canyon Road/Pleasant Grove 100 East project. Mayor Rees stated that approximately 14 years ago Pleasant Grove and Cedar Hills approached MAG regarding funding for improvements along Canyon Road. The County requested that Pleasant Grove and Cedar Hills take ownership of the road. Cedar Hills did not for a variety of reasons, therefore most of the improvements along the road are being done on the Pleasant Grove side. She reported that today’s request does not address any of the safety issues in Cedar Hills such as storm drainage and sidewalk gutters. Commissioner Ainge asked if there is a way seek additional funding to repair the Cedar Hills side. Andrew Jackson stated that the road is under county jurisdiction, therefore if the county approves the project, it can be done. It will need be treated a separate transaction. Mayor Fugal moved that $605,490 of contingency funds be released to the Canyon Road/Pleasant Grove 100 East project. Mayor Stout seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. b. Provo Lakeview Parkway Phase 2 In 2014, Provo City was awarded $6,602,581 of county transportation tax funds, for the Lakeview Parkway and Trail Phase II project through the MAG TIP selection process. Due to additional right of way costs, Provo City is requesting the 10% contingency funds for the project totaling $660,258. Contingency requests over 10% of the project cost and/or over $500,000 require Regional Planning Committee approval. Provo City will make up the remaining balance. Mayor Gustin moved that $660,258 of contingency funds be released to the Lakeview Parkway Phase II Project. Mayor Westmoreland seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee February 7, 2019 Page 4 Provo Canyon Parking Study Results and Implementation - Matt Parker, UDOT In June 2017, the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) contracted with a consulting firm to complete a parking study for US Route 189 in Provo Canyon. The project was conducted to identify, analyze, and recommend improvements to the parking areas in the canyon. The consultant recommended higher parking supply to accommodate future growth and create safer parking areas with less congestion. Key areas identified for improvement or expansion are: • 800 North park and Ride/Trailhead • Bridal Veil Falls • Frazier/Vivian Park • Old Highway Staging Area • Parking below the dam Matt Parker asked suggestions and collaboration to help resolve the issue. An update was given on the connector trail between Vivian Park and Deer Creek Dam. The project is being reevaluated due to the complexity of the project and cost. Corridor Preservation Request by Orem City - Jim Price, MAG In the absence of Jim Price, Paul Goodrich with the City of Orem presented on the Corridor Preservation request for Orem. Orem received TIP funding to begin environmental work and partial ROW acquisition for widening 1600 North between I-15 and US 89 from 3 lanes to 5. The project is a Phase 1 RTP project and is a Corridor Preservation approved corridor. The city has begun receiving multiple requests from homeowners to buy them out prior to any approved construction property. Purchasing said properties along this corridor would be within the purpose and need of the Corridor Preservation Fund policy. Requirements to receive Corridor Preservation funding include it being a Phase 1 project, already have design work, go through the environmental process and have a willing seller. Projects under construction are excluded. TAC recommends that this program be authorized for a period of up to 24 months, and the requested amount is $3.0 million. Concerns were raised in regards to obligating a large percentage of the funds to one project and how the funds will get replenished. Chad Eccles stated that Utah County approved a $10 fee per vehicle per registration goes into the Corridor Preservation fund. Revenues are approximately $2M annually. Chad explained that Corridor Preservation funds are not meant to be a saving account and should be used by all the cities. Commissioner Bill Lee moved that the Regional Planning Committee approve the described program of ROW acquisition for Orem 1600 North. Mayor Brunst seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee February 7, 2019 Page 5 Approval of Updated Public Participation Program - Chad Eccles MAG as the MPO is required through Federal Regulations to have a Public Participation Program that addresses how the MPO shares information and receives public comments as part of the planning process. The Public Participation Program document approved in 2015 details MAG’s policies and procedures when it comes to Public Participation. Upon approval, the updated policy document will be out for a 45-day public comment period. Key Changes include: • Allowing for professional translation services rather than rely on staff’s spotty linguistics skills. • Discarding references to exchanging ideas program. • Removing staff responsibility for direct interaction with neighborhood groups. • Changing agenda posting requirements from 7 days previous to 24 hours in accordance with state law. • Removing requirement to go out for additional 30 days if significant comment received. • Federal Regulations do not require this. • Remove requirement to provide MAG planning documents in any language free of charge. Mayor Miller moved that Regional Planning Committee approve the updated Public Participation Program as presented and that it be released for the required 45-day public comment period. Mayor Pray seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Update Reports UDOT Region Three: Rob Clayton reported that MAG funded a project to look at a central transit corridor through the county. UDOT has been working MAG, UTA and cities along the corridor. They are close to having the scope of work for the study that will be made public. The study will look at what types of services will make the most sense between Lehi and Provo, and how to tie it into existing services. He asked the Committee to help identify locations in their city that are in need of improvements and send him a list. A request has been made to obtain monthly ridership information over the last fifteen months that will be shared with the Mayors. FHWA: Steve Call reported that the Federal Transit Administration and the Federal Highway Administration conducts a planning review every four years for agencies receiving federal funds. The four-year cycle for MAG begins this summer. A team will begin the review process July 30- August 1, 2019 and hold a public meeting on July 30, 2019. Each year MAG self certifies ensuring they are meeting federal requirements. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee February 7, 2019 Page 6 UTA modifications were made and include repealing the name change of UTA, change name of UTA local advisory board to local advisory council, and allow COG to be part of the nomination process for UTA Trustees. Allowable uses of local options sales and use tax revenue were identified and will be made consistent within all counties and cities. Tax revenue generated can be used for the development, construction, maintenance, or operation of state roads, county roads, city/town roads, and rural non-paved roads. With the recent adoption of the fourth quarter sales tax, revenue can start paying for the BRT bonds, freeing up to $9M for projects. SB34 affordable housing modifications include updates on how cities and counties prepare their land use, transportation, and housing elements of their general plan, and enhanced coordination of housing development with transportation investment.
