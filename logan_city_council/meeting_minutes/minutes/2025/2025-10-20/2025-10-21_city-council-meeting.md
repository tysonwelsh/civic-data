OPENING CEREMONY:
Josh Thompson, CAPSA Chief Development & Communications Officer, addressed the
Council and stated that October is Domestic Violence Awareness Month and led the
audience in the pledge of allegiance.
Mr. Thompson introduced himself and thanked the Council for the opportunity, and for
the time and energy spent by the Council, who serve the City and residents. He thanked
Councilmember A. Anderson & Mayor Daines for their time in service. He feels that
public service requires courage and commitment, and a willingness to serve.
Mr. Thompson announced that October is Domestic Violence Awareness Month. This
month is about helping the community be aware of a grave issue that often remains
hidden. He said that 1 in 3 women and 1 in 7 men in Utah will experience domestic
violence in their lifetime. This means that statistically most of us will not experience
domestic violence, but it also means that every one of us knows someone who has. It
could be happening to neighbors, friends, or family members.
Nearly half of all homicides in the State of Utah are related to domestic violence. There
are not just numbers, but tragic events happening in our community. Over the last fiscal
year, CAPSA served over 2,500 individuals, with more than 1,900 from Logan City. This
is a significant share compared to the rest of the county. This does not mean there is more
domestic violence in Logan, but also a positive correlation that individuals know where
to go to get help. Residents are aware that there is hope, there are resources, and that they
do not need to face abuse alone.
Awareness is an indicator of a healthy community. A collaboration between CAPSA &
the Logan Police Department has occurred for over 30 years and a member of the police
department has served on the board of CAPSA. The professionalism and care that
officers show to survivors is exceptional. Logan City & CAPSA were the first in the State

services. It is not an exaggeration to say that this process has saved lives in our

happens. CAPSA provides safety and support when it does happen. They offer long-term
healing, therapy, and advocacy so the long-term violence does not continue. Hope begins

25 feet. There is also a proposal in there for roads that are within a wetland buffer to 20

Mr. DeSimone said there is also a requirement that if the property is adjacent to the
Logan River and Little Logan River, the property owner has to provide a 100 foot
conservation easement as well as a trail easement. There is protection as you expand
outward. There is really no land available for a new neighborhood in Logan City.
Everything new is in the West area and it would apply to those two standards.

Councilmember López stated that an email he received was misinterpreting the current
ordinance and the way a wetland can be filled in or not and he asked for clarification.
Mr. DeSimone responded that you can fill in a wetland. If there is a wetland on a
property, the property owner is required to get a wetland delineation. The Corp of
Engineers has to agree to the delineation and they regulate it as well. If the delineation is
                                                                                    10 | Page

disputed then it would go through the jurisdictional process. City code states that if you
go through the court permitting process, you can obtain a permit from the court to fill in
the wetland and go through the mitigation requirements and it is now out of the City’s
hands. If someone goes through the court process and the Corp of Engineers says it’s not
a jurisdictional wetland and is an isolated wetland then the City does not regulate that

Chair Simmonds added that “delineated” means that the Army Corp of Engineers has

America.
Mr. DeSimone responded that the land owner would have to hire an expert whether it be

Corp of Engineers for them to review and evaluate. They will either accept it or deny it. If
accepted then the City keeps jurisdiction over it and if the boundary is disputed then the
land owner can go through the jurisdiction process.
Chair Simmonds said when an individual comes to the City with a project on a piece of
property do, we already know where those wetlands are potentially located and do we tell
the property owner there are wetlands that need to be delineated.
Mr. DeSimone said there is not a process that triggers delineation. When someone applies
for a project staff evaluates the project and based on the national wetland inventory map,
which is a high level of mapping that says there might be wetlands here.

SUMMARY OF CHANGES
The proposed amendments will not necessarily be discussed in numerical LDC order but
will be reviewed by subject matter with specific code references.

Short Term Rentals - Remove CUP Requirement
The proposed changes in LDC 17.08.040 and 17.37.130 removes the Conditional use
Permit requirement for establishing an STR and shifts the review entirely to the
administrative process (business licensing) for permitting & licensing. Since the last code
updates for STR's, our practice with permitting short term rentals has demonstrated that
most STR's are being approved under the consent agenda and are relatively easy to
permit administratively. The most consistently heard issue is that people are generally
unaware of the original rules establishing STR's throughout Logan, and while the
Planning Commission's public hearing gives people a forum to voice their opinions, the
CUP process is not the process to legislate a change in rules. This procedural change does
not alter any of the other STR requirements, i.e., notification of surrounding residents,
parking, safety upgrades, etc., and would save time and money for both a STR proponent
and the City.

Beekeeping - Update to Match State Law Updates
The proposed changes in LDC 17.37.110 updates the City's beekeeping standards to
make them consistent with recent changes made to the Utah Bee Inspection Act in Title 4
of Utah Code (March 17, 2025). Generally, the rule change the minimum land area per
two hives (6,000 sf to 5,445 sf) and includes a 1O' setback from property lines.

Subdivisions - Increase Minor Subdivisions
The proposed changes in LDC 17.40 & 17.62 would increase the minimum threshold of

reviewed an average of ten subdivisions per year while staff reviews an average of six
minor subdivisions per year.

This change will probably have a minimal impact on the actual distribution of

Permit Expiration and Extensions
The proposed changes in LDC 17.51 would increase the length of time a permit (Design
Review, Conditional Use, or Subdivision) is valid from 12 months to 24 months, reduce
the number of permit extensions to one, and require a project or subdivision reapply after
36 months.

The language in LDC 17.51.01O.A should also include increasing the recording
requirements for the first phase of a subdivision from 12 to 24 months, while still
retaining the 12 month increment for subsequent phases.

architectural & historical character along Main Street and elsewhere varies. The inner

A primary design goal of the TC-1 zone is to have the bulk of a new building located

20% landscaping for residential developments. The LDC 17.32 generally describes
useable outdoor space as outdoor areas that are designed and used in conjunction with a
primary use for the benefit and enjoyment of the residents and their guests of the
development. Useable outdoor space may include public gathering areas, patios, decks,
walkways, plaza's, active recreational areas, and natural open space areas but do not
include parking lots, driveways, and other similar vehicular oriented areas. Landscaping
is defined as planted and natural areas containing trees, shrubs, grasses, mulches, and
other similar materials.

The LDC requires a minimum of 20 trees and a combination of 50 shrubs, flowers and
ornamental grass per acre of land for multi-family residential projects. The LDC also
requires minimum perimeter and interior parking lot landscaping to reduce the visual and
environmental impacts of asphalt parking lots. At 25.5 acres, 510 trees and 1,275
shrubs/flowers/grasses are required.

The 25.5-acre property (1,110,780 SF) is broken up into four areas, two commercial and
two multi-family. The commercial areas total 722,225 SF and would require 144,444 SF
(20%) of both outdoor space and landscaping. The multifamily areas total 388,555 SF
and would require 116,566 SF (30%) of both outdoor space and landscaping. Based on
the two standards and two development type areas, the project would require a total of
261,010 SF of landscaping and outdoor space. As conditioned with compliant
landscaping and outdoor space, the project meets the requirements of the LDC.

The LDC 17.37.090 requires adequate lighting that adds aesthetic quality and improves
safety while mitigating unnecessary glare, sky glow and light trespass. The LDC limits
freestanding pole height to 32 feet and luminaire fixtures on buildings and canopies to be
concealed source, down-cast and shielded from neighboring properties. Light
measurements are required to range between 0.5 - 4.0-foot candles, so areas are
sufficiently safe, but not excessively bright. As submitted, no exterior lighting has been
