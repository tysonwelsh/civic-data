Public Comment (10:10)

Chair MacKay read the public comment preamble and opened the public comment period.

David Faith-Farlow, of Provo, said he is opposed to and recommends against constructing a homeless
shelter in Provo. He discussed the potential problems shelters pose, even those that are well-
maintained. He believes a shelter will not help homeless people who are not willing to change their
behavior. He stated a shelter would be bad for the city, bad for residents, and bad for the homeless.

With no other comments, Chair MacKay closed public comment.

2.         A resolution authorizing the Mayor to approve an interlocal agreement with Utah County
           to conduct a vote-by-mail election for the Municipal Primary and General Elections to be
           held in Provo City in 2023. (23-038) (14:56)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-13, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Heidi Allman, City Recorder, presented. She said there are four open council seats that will be part of
this year’s election. The primary election will be held on August 15 and the general election will be on
November 7. She discussed the duties that each party would be responsible for.

3.         An ordinance amending Provo City Code regarding citywide fireworks regulation. (23-024)
           (17:19)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Ordinance 2023-6, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Lynn Schofield, Fire Battalion Chief, presented. He said the three proposed amendments include adding
Spring Creek to the list of designated parks, opening designated parks for New Year’s Eve, New Year’s
Day, and Chinese New Year holidays, and adding licensing requirements to obtain a permit for fireworks
displays.

Chair MacKay opened public comment. With none, she closed public comment. With no council
discussion, she called for a vote.

      Vote:     The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Hoban,
                Mackay, Shipley, and Whipple in favor.

After Chair MacKay realized there was not a public comment period for agenda item 2, she opened the
item for public comment. With none, she continued to item 4.

5.         A resolution approving a substantial amendment to the program year 2021 Annual Action
           Plan incorporating funds for HOME-ARP and approve the HOME-ARP allocation. (23-027)
           (23:39)

      Motion:      An implied motion to approve Resolution 2023-14, as currently constituted, has been
                   made by council rule.

Melissa McNalley, Community Grant Administrator, presented. She discussed where the funds came
from and the process in determining how the funds would be used. She said that funds for supportive
services and tenant based rental assistance would be the most beneficial.

Councilor Shipley asked if it would require the Council to hear this item again if funds were reallocated
down the road. After a verbal yes from Ms. McNalley, he asked if there is a planned timeframe to review
the allocations and adjust as needed.

Ms. McNalley said that there is no specified timeframe to evaluate. She determined that six to twelve
months would be a realistic time to review. She said she would be presenting to council again once a
third-party entity, who would be managing the use of funds, was decided.

Councilor Handley suggested aiming for one year to evaluate the success of the plan.

Chair MacKay opened the item for public comment. With none, she closed public comment.

Councilor Ellsworth thanked Melissa for all the work she has put into this plan.

With no other council discussion, Chair MacKay called for a vote.

      Vote:     The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Hoban,
                Mackay, Shipley, and Whipple in favor.
