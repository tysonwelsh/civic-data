NEIGHBORHOOD MEETING
A virtual neighborhood meeting was held on this project on June 28, 2022, via Zoom.
Eight Citizens joined the meeting via Zoom, all expressing support for the use and the
architectural revision to the existing storefront. Staff also received one email from a
citizen expressing support for the project. Staff did not receive any other correspondence
relative to the project, generated from the mailed neighborhood meeting notices.

REQUEST
Architects Joe Jacoby and Sanae Adams, with Jacoby Architects and Tom Gibbs, representing
the Utah State Department of Alcoholic Beverages Services (DABS) and Matthias Mueller,
representing the Utah State Division of Facilities Construction and Management (DFCM) are
requesting preliminary Site Plan Review to modify a portion of the existing, vacant
Albertson's Fresh Values food market building and parking lot, for use as a Utah State
Liquor and Wine retail store. The property is part of the Little Cottonwood Center
commercial shopping center project, which includes approximately 45 acres of
commercially zoned land with many restaurants and retail shops and two large "anchor"
stores, including the now vacant old Albertson's Fresh Values grocery store building and the
Home Depot home improvement store. The Site is located on the southwest corner of 9400
S. Street and Highland Drive (2000 East).

The entire shopping center is zoned Special Development SD (Magna) CC Community
Commercial. A State Agency Liquor Store is listed in the SD(Magna) CC zoning district as a
Conditional Use, and therefore requires specific approval from the Planning Commission. A
Conditional Use application (CUP05242022-006334) has also been filed by the State and is
addressed in the accompanying staff report. The Planning Commission is the land-use
authority for these requested actions.

In addition to the consideration of the building's remodeling architectural materials,
designs and colors as proposed by the State's architectural consultant, the Planning
Commission must decide if the proposed changes to the building's east fa<;ade are
appropriate to be introduced into the existing shopping center's "Architectural
Theme" as established by the SD zone and by prior development.

BACKGROUND
The State Division of Facilities Construction and Management (DFCM) has entered into a long
term sub-lease with the parent company of Albertson's, Associated Fresh Markets, Inc, to
occupy the southern portion of the vacant food store building, and the adjacent surface
parking lot. Approximately 14, 657 Square Feet will be used for the liquor store operation.
The shopping center was developed, built, owned and managed by the local commercial
property development entity Magna Investment until recently, when the shopping center
was sold to other owners. While under the Magna management, strict adherence to the site
specific architectural design elements (established by the SD zone itself) were enforced upon
all of the various retail buildings built in the project. Thus, the center has an "Architectural
Theme" established which is reflected in most of the buildings present in the shopping
center.

ANALYSIS

Vehicle Access. Vehicle access to the shopping center will remain the same as it
currently exists. A traffic impact analysis has been conducted for the proposed change of
use, which compares the estimated trip generation and peak hour vehicle trips between
the porportionally sized grocery store and the existing trip genereation and peak hour
experiences of two other nearby state liquor stores. (Please see the attached traffic study
letter from Horrocks Engineering.) Vehicle access, parking and on-site circulation will be
adequate for this development.

Pedestrian Access. Pedestrian access to the shopping center will remain the same as it
is now. Pedestrian access will be adequate for this development.

City Departmental Reviews. All of the reviewing City departments and agencies have
reviewed the proposed new site plan development. Slight changes will need to be
made to fully meet the requirements of the various City Departments, as noted in their
review redlines and correction comments. All departments and agency redlines and
conditions will be incorporated into the final site plan prior to final approval
administration by City staff.

Zoning Review. The land use of the property is subject to Planning Commission approval
of a Conditional Use Permit, due to designation of such a State Liquor Store being listed
as a Conditional Use in the SD (Magna) CC zoning district.

Changes to the existing site are minimal from the original construction of the shopping
center. As part of the change in use/tenant, a demising wall is proposed to be
constructed to separate the new liquor store portion of the existing grocery store
building from the rest of the vacant building. Also, a small loading dock structure and
dumpster enclosure will be added on the back (west) of the building to facilitate
deliveries and waste disposal. The major change to the existing building is in the
form of a new fayade and entry point into the new store. This entry fayade change
is purposeful and deemed necessary by the applicant to focus customer's attention and
entry into the new liquor store, and helps with the DABS's desire to create a new
"branded" look to all state liquor store new buildings using strong, "Modern"
architecture. (Please see applicants letter attached addressing the fayade architecture
and why the applicant believes that this change is appropriate in the context of the
existing center.)

The proposed changes to the proximate parking lot are necessary to provide the
required accessible parking stalls and accessible routes at the new entry into the
building and to address slight changes to the storm water collection system.

Architectural Design Review. The proposed building fayade of the east side of the
building meets the Sandy City Architectural Design Standards for commercial buildings.
Because of the SD (Magna) CC zoning of the property, the zone includes certain design
and development standards that apply specifically to this unique SD district. The afore
mentioned letter from the applicant addresses the "Sandy City Architectural Design
Standard" adopted by the City in 2004, and which apply throughout the City to all
commercial development. This is not a consideration of the SD zone required
Architectural Design Standards, which follow. (Please see the attached building elevation
drawings and renderings attached to this report.)

Attached are three selected pages from those design and development standards that
are pertinent to the proposed fayade design change. The first is the cover page of the
design and development standards text. The second page is titled "13. Architectural
Design Statndards." Sub paragraphs a. through f. Subparagraph "a." may be the most
useful in the Planning Commission's analysis of architectural design change.
"Architectural concepts should reflect the stable character of an integrated
well planned commercial center".           This statement seems to encourage
consistency with prior development as to architectural materials and elements, but
does use the term "should" or "must" and is not specific as to design elements or
building materials.

The third page, under "17. Special Exceptions" provides that the Planning Commission
may grant special exceptions to the architectural design standards of the SD zone by
evaluating the following three criteria:

   1. Alternative solutions and/or changing standards make the exception reasonably
      necessary.
   2. Such exception meets the purpose and intent of the SD Magna/2000 East District.

In response to these three criteria, staff can state the following:

    1.The applicant makes a case for allowing "changes" to the design elements of the
      old development since that development is twenty plus years old and in need of
      updating. Support for the architectural design changes to the center was
      expressed by the citizens during the neighborhood meeting.
   2. The applicant makes the case that these design changes of the new fayade will
      meet the purpose and intent of the SD (Magna) CC zone, which is as follows:
           A. Purpose.         The purpose of the CC Planned Development
               Center/Community Commercial Sub-District is to allow for retail
               businesses and related uses to be grouped together into a well-planned and
               designed shopping center as a complimentary portion of the community
               center for the Alta Canyon Quadrant. The development is characterized
               by a shopping center which follows the current development trend by
               emphasizing several large tenants, with fewer smalleer tenant spaces
               being provided. This zone is intended to increase commercial investment
               in the City and encourages the location of new types of businesses needed
               in the area: not simply encourage the relocation of existing local busineses
               in a new commercial setting.
   3. The change of use and the fayade design change will not adversely impact the
      adjoining residential properties to the west, which are well buffered from the
      center's buildings and uses by building setbacks, mature landscape buffers and a
      masonry wall.

to allow the proposed east building fa�ade as proposed, based upon the following
