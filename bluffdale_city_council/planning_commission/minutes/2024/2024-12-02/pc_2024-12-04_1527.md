5.     PUBLIC HEARING. CONSIDERATION AND RECOMMENDATION on a
       proposed Text Amendment to Create a Definition for Micro Educational Facilities
       and Home-Based Microschools and to Allow Such as Home Occupations in
       Residential Zones per Utah Code S10-9a-305. Additionally, to Extend Reciprocal
       Recognition of Licenses for Mobile Businesses as Defined in Utah Code Sll-56-103.
       City of Bluffdale, Applicant. (Application 2024-43. Caitlvn Tubbs.Staff Presenter.

Planning Manager, Caitlyn Tubbs, presented the Staff Report and stated that staff continues to
work on the State Code updates. What is before the Commission is from the 2023 and 2024
Legislative Sessions. In 2023,the Legislature passed HB-408, which defined an enclosed mobile
business. It also required cities and counties to recognize business licenses for businesses that
had already been issued by other jurisdictions. It does not require municipalities or counties to
license these businesses; however, if a different jurisdiction does license them, the City is
required to recognize those licenses.

Ms. Tubbs reported that the 2024 Legislature passed SB-13 defined micro education entities and
home-based micro schools and awarded both uses the same accommodation as charter schools.
This means that they are allowed as permitted uses in all zones. Reference was made to the 2023
item wherein the definition put forth by the state for an "enclosed mobile business" is a business
that maintains ongoing mobility and the receipt of goods or services offered. Points of sale are to
occur within an enclosed vehicle, an enclosed trailer, or an enclosed mobile structure. Some of
the uses identified by the State as examples include a mobile barber, beauty, and cosmetics. She
noted that a lash technician can act as an enclosed mobile business. Other types of businesses
include such things as cell phone, computer, or small engine repair, and mobile pet groomers. It
does not include a food truck, food cart, or an ice cream truck, which have separate licensing
statutes per State Code and have separate requirements.

In response to a question raised, Ms. Tubbs stated that a few years ago food truck bills were a
popular topic that was addressed by the Legislature.

As part of more recent updates, the State has defined a micro-education entity or facility as a use
with up to 100 students and each of those hundred students must have at least 20 square feet of

Associate City Planner, Ellen Oakman presented the Staff Report and stated that the regulations
are similar to those that have been discussed recently. Feedback was collected from the
takeaways from previous meetings. The desire is to support business and address streetscape and
signage issues. While aesthetics are important, they wanted to be lenient in signage by allowing
for increased occurrences of temporary signage and increase the duration of vinyl barmers that
are affixed to buildings.


Ms. Oakman reported that banners of up to 24 square feet are permitted through a Temporary
Use Permit. Blade feather signs are prohibited as are inflatables and permants and any other
signs. The proposed amendment was presented. Two barmers are allowed per street frontage as
a temporary sign. Three feather signs or feather blade signs are allowed per street frontage. For
grand openings only, staff suggested allowing two inflatables and pennants. The proposed
change is to increase from two to three barmers, increase from three to five feather blade signs
per street frontage. Inflatables and permants are being proposed to be allowed during grand
openings only with vinyl banners allowed on buildings for 90 days from the date of Business
License issues. A time period of 10 days would be allowed for removal of violation. Staff has
conducted research and spoken to various sign companies, which was found to be adequate time.

Commissioner Swanson asked if violation language was included. Mr. Crowell stated that as
with anything in the Land Use Code, there are civil penalty procedures. There are items in the
zoning ordinance where signs might not comply but they may not warrant criminal prosecution.
Staff is also working on a simpler temporary sign ordinance.

Chair Cragun opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.


Commissioner Griflis moved to forward a POSITIVE recommendation of the proposed
Text Amendment as presented. The motion was seconded by Commissioner Kraupp. Vote
on motion: Erik Swanson-Yes, Tina Griffis-Yes, Michael Kraupp-Yes, Chair Cragun-Yes.
The motion passed unanimously.

7.     PUBLIC HEARING. CONSIDERATION                     AND RECOMMENDATION               on a
       Proposed Road Dedication Plat of the 14730 South Right-of-Wav. City of Bluffdale,
       Applicant. (Application 2024-40I. Grant CrowelK Staff Presenter.

Mr. Crowell presented the Staff Report and stated that on the east side of town,just off of Noell
Nelson Drive there is Quality Stone and an old private gravel road that goes around to 855 West.
The City's Redevelopment Agency("RDA")identified it as a good investment several years ago.
As a result, staff has been working to convert it to a public road. He identified 855 West on a
drawing displayed that comes down from 14600 South, which was identified as Phase 1. The
RDA participated as a partner with the developer of the Covent Garden Project. On the bottom
of the draw is 14730 South, which is Phase 2. It took time to acquire property there due to there
being numerous different property owners.


There was budget set aside in the RDA and BD Bush was awarded the bid. The Kings Cross
Project is currently under construction on the south side. The intent is revitalization of the
industrial park utilizing the Eastern Bluffdale RDA fimds. The City owns the parcels but is not
yet dedicated as a road. The utility providers will work inside the road.

Chair Cragun opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing
was closed.


Commissioner Swanson moved to recommend APPROVAL of the 14730 South Road

Dedication Plat, Application 2024-40 as presented. Commissioner Kraupp seconded the
motion. Vote on motion: Erik Swanson-Yes,Tina Griffis-Yes, Michael Kraupp-Yes, Chair
Cragun-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.
