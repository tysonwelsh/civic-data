3.4) Quarterly Finance Report
Dean Lundell, Finance Director, presented the city's quarterly financial report. He noted that the
early part of the year typically doesn't have as much activity, as many revenues don't come in
until later. Lundell explained that the city's sales tax receipts have been a bit behind budget and
lower than the previous year, though he said this isn't too concerning yet. He mentioned that
statewide, sales tax growth has been modest compared to the high growth the city has seen in
recent years. Lundell also discussed the city's general fund balance, which is projected to be just
above 35% of the budget. He said the city will work with the insurance provider to determine
appropriate reserve levels, including for things like natural disasters. Lundell provided an update
on the city's PARC tax, noting that about $3 million of the $4.3 million collected so far is
available for capital projects, with the first $5 million set aside for the Family Park project.

3.1) Parks and Recreation Master Plan Update

Councilor Condie joined the meeting at approximately 5:24 p.m.

The presentation outlined the development of Lehi's new parks and recreation system plan, a
long-range planning tool to evaluate service gaps and identify improvement opportunities.
Presenters shared initial analysis on Lehi's rapid growth and large youth population. They
discussed the community engagement process, including a highly successful survey that revealed
strong demand for amenities like splash pads, playgrounds, trails, and indoor facilities, as well as
a desire for more trees and youth programming. Based on this input, the presenters outlined five
key plan themes: engaging outdoor experiences, diverse play and programming, community
connection, matching resources to growth, and nature. The next steps will be developing specific
strategies and priorities to implement the plan over the next 10 years.

3.2) Point of the Mountain Chamber of Commerce Report
Marcy Gundrey- Chamber President presented a report for the Chamber of Commerce. She
highlighted the importance of community engagement. The Chamber has hosted several
successful events over the past year, including a state of the community event with the mayor
and a Halloween on Main Street event that attracted 1,500 kids. The chamber has also served as
stewards of the historic railroad depot, hosting events and ribbon cuttings for new businesses.
The chamber's membership has grown to over 400 members, with 50% being Lehi businesses.
Looking ahead to 2025, the chamber plans to focus on providing tailored resources for
businesses of all sizes, expanding educational and networking opportunities, and continuing to
engage with the city and promote local commerce. Ms. Gundrey expressed gratitude for the city's
partnership and support, and welcomed feedback from the community.

3.3) Discussion on Attainable Housing Overlay Zone
Brittney Harris, Lehi Planner, presented on the proposed attainable housing overlay zone. The
goal of the overlay is to focus on single-family housing for critical and essential workers by
setting a target price for homes and adjusting the density to meet that price point. The process
would be voluntary for developers. The proposal includes a 10-year deed restriction for owner-
occupancy.

5. Consideration of Resolution #2024-62 approving the City's Application to the Broadband
   Infrastructure Grant Program.
   Shaye Ruitenbeek Fiber Department Manager presented the resolution, which would approve
   the City's application to pre-register for the broadband infrastructure grant program. This is a
   federal grant program that the state of Utah has been awarded $317 million for. Ruitenbeek
   explained that this resolution does not commit the city to applying for the grant, but rather
   allows the city to pre-register and keep the option open to apply in the future. She noted that
   there are some requirements around low-cost options that the city is still evaluating as part of
   the pros and cons of potentially applying. The explained that the Council would have further
   discussions and approvals before any actual grant application is submitted. The council
   ultimately approved the resolution, with the understanding that more details would be
   brought back for further consideration before deciding whether to apply for the grant
   funding.

    Motion:     Councilor Albrecht moved to approve Resolution #2024-62 approving the City's
                Application to the Broadband Infrastructure Grant Program, with the understanding
                that more details would be brought back for further consideration before deciding
                whether to apply for the grant funding. Councilor Newall seconded the motion.

7. Consideration of Ordinance #67-2024, a Development Code Amendment to Chapter 37,
   adding an exception to the connectivity standards for vehicular safety
   Rick Magnus from AWA Engineering presented the development code amendment to allow
   an exception to the connectivity standards. This was related to a new grocery store
   development, where there were safety concerns about the grade difference and the orientation
   of the building.

    Magnus explained that the grade difference of 5-7 feet between the stub street and the back of
    the grocery store building posed a safety risk, especially for vehicles or entering from the
    residential street. Additionally, the large footprint of the grocery store and power line
    easements limited the building's placement options.

    The Council discussed the proposal and the applicant agreed to work with staff on addressing
    the safety and connectivity concerns.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to approve Ordinance #67-2024, a Development Code
                Amendment to Chapter 37, adding an exception to the connectivity standards for
                vehicular safety; also remove the word “however” from paragraph 5A and include
                all Planning Commission and Development Review Committee comments.
                Councilor Newall seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Hancock,
    Absent; Councilor Newall, Yes; and Councilor Stallings, Yes. The motion passed with 4 in
    favor and 1 absent.


8. Consideration of Resolution #2024-59 approving a First Amendment to the Interlocal
   Agreement for the Creation of a School District among the cities of Lehi, Cedar Hills,
   Highland, Alpine, American Fork, and Draper.
   Jason Walker, City Administrator, explained that the interlocal board would be granted
   extended authority until the school board is elected. This led to a lengthy debate, with
   Councilor Stallings expressing concerns that the amendment expands the city's role beyond
   what was originally intended and what is required by current state law. She argued that the
   division of assets and other responsibilities should be left to the future school board members.
   Mayor Johnson and other Council members countered that someone needs to take
   responsibility for the necessary work until the new school board is in place, and that the cities
   involved feel a sense of duty to ensure a smooth transition. Councilor Newall and Councilor
   Condie acknowledged the concerns but saw the amendment as precautionary. The discussion
   centered around finding a balance between the city's role and the need to prepare for the new
   school district's establishment, while also respecting the future school board's authority.
