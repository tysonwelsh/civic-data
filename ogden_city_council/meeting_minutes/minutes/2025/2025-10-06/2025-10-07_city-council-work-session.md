         The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
review the FlashVote Survey results for Public Safety; discuss the proposed Wall Avenue Corridor Plan; discuss the proposed Mt.

Agenda Review
        Chair White and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Proposed Wall Avenue Corridor Plan
          Jake Farnsworth representing Kimley-Horn and Associates, the project consultant for the development of the Wall
Avenue Corridor Plan, introduced the preferred street design concept for the downtown section of Wall Avenue, 22 nd to 27th
Streets. The preferred concept — Traffic Calming with Landscape Median and Shared Use Path — was developed through public
input, staff analysis, and coordination with the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT), and has been unanimously
recommended by the Planning Commission on June 4, 2025. He used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to review the concept,
it includes four travel lanes with a left turn lane, a landscaped median, street trees, on-street parking, curb extensions, mid-block
crossings, and an off-street shared use path along the west side. This concept is an important step for the future of downtown
Ogden because it will guide investment, development, and transportation decisions that shape how people experience the City’s
core. The final draft of the entire Wall Avenue Corridor Plan is expected to be submitted to and reviewed by the Council in the
next few months. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Farnsworth centered on the pros and cons of each of the optional concepts that
were considered by the public and the consultant/staff; potential impacts to travel time through the aera of 22 nd to 27th Streets on
Wall Avenue; drawbacks of mid-block pedestrian crossings; the construction project timeframe for improvements to the section of
Wall Avenue from 22nd to 27th Streets; the manner in which the preferred concept will benefit Union Station; on-street parking
opportunities; and the next steps in the process of approving the preferred concept and a Wall Avenue Corridor Plan.

FlashVote Survey Results – Public Safety
          Kevin Lyons, representative of FlashVote, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to review the results of the
FlashVote survey specific to Public Safety services in Ogden City. He reported 304 individuals participated in the survey; he
listed the questions that were asked and illustrated the filtering tools used to communicate the results of the survey. He concluded
that the public’s rating of the Ogden City Police Department is very positive. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the
information packet for the meeting.

        Chair White recessed the meeting at 5:00 p.m. to allow the Council to view a press conference regarding an event that
occurred in the community earlier in the day.

Proposed Mt. Lewis Community Plan Amendment; and
Proposed Rezone – 1514 Washington Boulevard
         Planning Manager Brierley explained Cooperstown Partners LLC has submitted a petition with two requested actions.
The first is a proposal to amend the Mt. Lewis Community Plan, specifically vision strategy 4.E of the plan to indicate the
property should be zoned R-2S, rather than R-1-6. The second requested action is to rezone the property from R-2 to R-2S to
allow for a townhome project. The property is along the north border of the city, near North Ogden and is adjacent to the
Washington Estates Mobile Home Park. Mr. Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the applicant’s
development concept; the Planning Commission recommends approval to amend vision strategy 4.E of the Mt. Lewis Community
Plan and rezone the two parcels at 1514 North Washington Boulevard from R-2 to R-2S/Conditional Overlay (CO) with a
condition that the owner enter into a development agreement limiting the dwelling units to owner-occupants for a period of 10
years after occupancy. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Discussion among the Council and Mr. Brierley centered on the history of the subject property and the purpose of the
proposed CO zoning; the Council concluded they were comfortable with the applications moving forward to a regular meeting.

Proposed Culinary Water Cross Connection Ordinance Amendments
          Public Services Executive Director Anderson explained Ogden City Administration is proposing that the Council
approve amendments to the city’s backflow and cross connection control ordinance. As required by state code, all culinary water
purveyors are required to implement and maintain a Cross Connection Control Program. Ogden’s ordinances regarding backflow
and cross connection control need to be updated to comply with state code as well as requirements of the Division of Drinking
Water and the Utah Plumbing Code. The proposal is to repeal the existing Title 9, Chapter 4, and replace it with the newly
proposed language. Mr. Anderson introduced the City’s Backflow Technician, Justin Royer, who used the aid of a PowerPoint
presentation to provide additional information regarding the proposed ordinance amendments, the purpose of which is to ensure
compliance with State and Federal requirements pertaining to safe drinking water. The regulations included in the ordinance apply
to backflow testers, contractors, citizens, the City, plumbing supply companies, and inspectors. For a copy of the presentation in
its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and Mr. Royer centered on the current rate of noncompliance with backflow regulations
and the specific types of construction projects or activities that require backflow testing. The Council concluded they were
comfortable with the proposed ordinance amendments moving forward to a regular meeting.

Fiscal Year 2027-2031 Capital Improvement Plan – Council Priorities
         Council Senior Policy Analyst Burton explained Council members have recently expressed interest in ranking capital
improvement projects to help inform the Administration of the Council’s priorities before the Fiscal Year 2026-2027 budget and
the 2027-2031 Capital Improvement Plan are put together. Council staff has compiled a list of projects from the most recent
(FY2026-2030) Capital Improvement Plan that was approved by the Council in June of 2025. Mr. Burton presented the system
that has been developed for ranking projects that are expected to be in the 2027-2031 Capital Improvement Plan; he indicated
Council staff will send an email with instructions and a link to the system to all Council members.

Council Business
          Union Station Plaza: Council member Myers stated he has heard of some recent changes related to planning efforts for
the Union Station Plaza; he asked that Administration provide an update to the Council in a future closed session if possible.
          Ongoing Construction Projects: Chair White asked Public Services Executive Director Anderson for a report of recent
construction activities around the Mt. Ogden Golf Course. Mr. Anderson stated there is work underway on Constitution Way in
front of the Mt. Ogden Golf Course; an old, corrugated metal storm drain line has deteriorated and the project will improve the
infrastructure in that area.
          Council member Graf also asked for an update on the project at the intersection of 20 th Street and Harrison Boulevard.
Mr. Anderson stated that asphalt and curb and gutter improvements are currently underway, and the estimated completion date is
October 20. The traffic patterns in the area will change when the intersection reopens and those driving through the area on a
regular basis will need to adjust to those changes.
          Council member Myers asked when the construction season will draw to an end. Mr. Anderson stated that all projects are
winding down, with some minor projects still underway through the end of October.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith asked for an update on the project on Taylor Avenue. Mr. Anderson stated many
residents have expressed concerns about the rate of speed on Taylor Avenue near the Weber State University (WSU) campus. The
City has participated in several community meetings regarding the problem and efforts have been undertaken to slow the speed by
installing a table-top speed hump. That project will be completed by the end of the month, along with a few other traffic calming
measures. The Council briefly discussed the temporary impacts associated with that project and thanked staff for keeping in touch
with the residents living in that neighborhood.
