         5. 2       Ordinance No. 21- 09 An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville approving Text
         Amendments to Taylorsville Municipal Code: Chapter 13. 21; Section 13. 36. 130; and
         Section 13. 36. 200, related to Private Lanes and Shared Driveways–       Jim Spung

 El Mr. Spung explained that although there was an applicant on this agenda item, staff had been
thinking about this very issue for quite some time.        The applicant had submitted some proposed
language for the ordinance, but staff felt the matter was more complex and was now submitting an
alternate text amendment to the   City Council. Currently, the city required every dwelling unit to
have direct access to a public or private street— regardless of whether it was a single- family home
or a multi-family complex. However, there was currently no mechanism in the code to allow more
than two dwelling units to share the same driveway. Mr. Spung listed various requirements that
would be in place should the Council approve the ordinance as staff proposed it. He also indicated
that the ordinance would create a mechanism to allow a particular type of development, but any
proposal would still need to comply with the underlying zoning in place and go through the
subdivision process.




Councilmember Barbieri inquired about flag lots. Mr. Spung responded that a flag lot would need
to have direct access to a street.


Mr. Spung explained that four other rezone and general plan map amendment applications for this
type of development pattern were currently being processed in the planning department.             He did
not feel this particular scenario was appropriate for every area of the city but pointed out that if the
ordinance was adopted, it would
                                      apply to all of Taylorsville— not just this particular parcel.

          5.3     Ordinance No. 21- 10 An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville approving a Zoning
          Text Amendment to Section 13. 08. 020; Section 13. 23. 170; Section 13. 24. 080; Section
          13. 36. 030; Section 13. 36.050 and Section 13. 36. 190 of the Taylorsville Land Development
          Code, related to Drive- Through Facilities–   Jim Spung

 e Mr. Spung summarized the following changes proposed in Ordinance No. 21- 10:
          Permissions in the land use table would be updated
          Drive-throughs would be permitted in all commercial zoning districts
          Review by the Planning Commission would no longer be required for drive-through
          facilities
          There would no longer be a 250- foot distance requirement in residential zoning districts
          Drive-throughs would be required to comply with the minimum building setbacks of the
          underlying zoning district
          There would be updates to the minimum number of stacking spaces required, with the
          most dramatic change to restaurants, which would go from nine spaces to five spaces
          Language relating to queuing lane placement would be strengthened
          Standards limiting the number of allowable queuing lanes would be removed
          Language requiring the building to be built at the front setback line would be removed
          The definition for" Business or Financial Services" would be updated to remove
          reference to drive-up facilities
          A new definition for" Drive-through" would be created
          A new definition for" Restaurant" would be created
