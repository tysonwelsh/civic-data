37          Wasatch Front Regional Council Presentation
38
39   Andrew Gruber, Executive Director, explained the role of the Wasatch Front Regional Council.
40   He acknowledged the residents' desire for biking, walking, and strolling paths and praised the
41   Council's focus on this issue. He also highlighted the increased funding opportunities from
42   organizations like WFRC and recent successes in securing new funding sources for regionally
43   significant trails. He introduced Hugh Van Wagenen, Active Transportation Planner, as an
44   expert on trails in the area and encouraged the Council to direct any questions to him.
45
46   Mr. Van Wagenen presented the transportation plan which is included in the packet. He said
47   that the regional transportation plan is reviewed and readopted every four years, with
48   opportunities for amendments during that period. He highlighted the importance of local active
49   transportation plans developed by municipalities, including Riverton's standalone plan from
50   2015.
51
     Page | 2 Minutes – City Council Meeting
     May 16, 2023




 1   Mr. Gruber emphasized that having projects on the regional transportation is a crucial initial
 2   requirement for accessing federal funds through organizations like WFRC, as well as state and
 3   county funding sources. The regional transportation plan serves as a means of assessing the
 4   feasibility of projects, even if their implementation is not anticipated in the immediate future.
 5
 6   Councilmember Pierucci inquired whether the planning primarily revolves around recreational
 7   purposes or if there is a more holistic approach that considers safe walking routes for children
 8   going to school and residents without access to cars.
 9
10   Mr. Van Wagenen said their primary objective is to create transportation options and facilitate
11   trips from one location to another. While there is overlap with recreational trips, he emphasized
12   the importance of providing transportation options for individuals who cannot drive, such as
13   older adults, people with physical limitations, and those who are legally too young to drive.
14
15   Councilmember Pierucci asked what Riverton City could do to enhance the effectiveness of the
16   trail planning process. In response, Mr. Gruber said they should consider regional connections
17   between Riverton and neighboring communities. He mentioned the Jordan River Trail as a
18   primary north-south trail spine that can be utilized by residents to connect to places in both
19   directions.
20
21   Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, said it would be wise to showcase existing active
22   transportation infrastructure in surrounding cities on the maps when identifying areas where new
23   trails or connections are desired. This could help secure funding as it presents a cohesive
24   regional perspective and emphasizes the value of creating a comprehensive and connected trail
25   system.
26
27   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal said that focusing on both maintaining existing trails and actively
28   pursuing the addition of new trails is a great idea. The Council needs to collaborate with Nathan
29   Page, Public Works grant writer, to leverage available funding opportunities.
30
31   Councilmember Buroker asked if cities moved to wider sidewalks could they incorporate them
32   into the trail system. In response, Mr. Van Wagenen said while expanding sidewalks can be a
33   practical approach in areas with limited right of way, it's important to aim for a width that can
34   accommodate both pedestrians and cyclists comfortably. An eight-foot width is often considered
35   the minimum for a trail-like experience, while a ten-foot width is preferable. Balancing
36   constraints and considering the anticipated usage can help determine the appropriate width for
37   sidewalks-turned-trails in different contexts.
38
39   Mayor Pro Tem McDougal said ensuring the safety and comfort of trail users is crucial to
40   encourage more people to utilize the trails. Simply marking a bike lane or trail with paint on a
41   major road may not provide the desired level of safety and comfort. The goal is to create an
42   environment where people feel safe and comfortable using the trails, thereby promoting active
43   transportation and a healthier, more connected community.

18            Engineering Standards Update
19
20   Matt Cassel, City Engineer, informed the Council about upcoming updates to the City's
21   engineering standards and specifications. He explained that these changes would be presented
22   at a future meeting for adoption and enforcement. The purpose of his presentation was to
23   provide a preview of the modifications being made to the standards. He clarified that although
24   there were 70 changes in total, they would only be discussing a select few during the meeting.
25
26   Mr. Cassel shared examples of the proposed changes such as the placement of a pipe in
27   relation to a box. Another example focused on streetlights, noting that the current standard
28   referred to a generation two LED light, whereas the new standard would reflect the use of
29   generation three LED lights.
30
31   Mr. Cassel offered to provide the full set of proposed changes in a PDF format upon request,
32   reiterating that the examples shared during the meeting represented the nature of the
33   modifications taking place.
34
35   Councilmember Pierucci asked how these standards are enforced. In response, Mr. Cassel
36   said that inspectors monitor the progress of construction to ensure adherence to the established
37   standards. If any deviations are identified, appropriate actions are taken, such as issuing a
38   notice of violation or implementing a stop-work order until the issues are rectified.
39
40   Councilmember Buroker asked Mr. Cassel to explain the change in the fire standard. Mr.
41   Cassel said that the previous standard called for a one-foot diameter call box, but the new
42   standard implemented by the International Fire Code requires a 96-foot diameter call box.

44            Implementation of New Flag
45
46   Ryan Carter, City Attorney, provided information about Senate Bill 31, which established the
47   new flag for the state of Utah. However, the bill does not take effect until March 9, 2024. He
48   mentioned that currently, the state recognizes the historic flag as the official flag. While there is
49   no explicit statute mandating the flying of the state flag, there is a description of the state flag in
50   the statutes referring to the historic flag.
51

 1   Mr. Carter discussed the potential public perception and reactions to flying the new flag before
 2   its effective date. They mentioned that the intention behind the delayed effective date was to
 3   allow cities and local governments to phase out the old flag and transition to the new one. He
 4   said that the City recently purchased new flags of the historic design, and it would not be cost-
 5   effective to replace them before they have been fully utilized. He recommended using the
 6   current flags until they are worn out and then transitioning to the new flag.
