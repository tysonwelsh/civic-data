2.    Sanitary Sewer Master Plan Update; Amend the West Jordan 2012 General Plan to
      include the Sanitary Sewer Master Plan Update; City-wide applicability; City of West
      Jordan (applicant).

Tim Heyrend, West Jordan Utilities Manager, gave a presentation of the proposed update to the
sanitary sewer system model for the city. The future land use plan helps with projections of population
and capacities. Future uses per house are projected at 100 gallons per person per day. Estimations for
occupants per house are 3.41. The CIP list is prioritized, and areas of deficiencies were identified. The
land use plan is always changing, so that can be difficult on projections. The seventeen projects
identified have a cost breakdown of $37M, which are meant to be through buildout of the City, so it is
a long-term plan. Changes and delays occur, which is why the plan is updated every five years.

Mr. Heyrend responded to questions from the Commission. He listed which projects carried over from
the last plan. He clarified the reasons for using a standard 0.013 roughness coefficient rather than
calculating each pipe separately based on type of pipe material, which would be a huge undertaking.
He explained that the projections of 100 gallons per day allows for things that do not go in the pipes
such as rain and high ground water tables. He said people are becoming educated with regards to
cutting back on water usage in times of high precipitation and watering only when it is necessary.

Matt Quinney opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

MOTION:     Jay Thomas moved to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council to
            update the West Jordan 2012 General Plan to include the 2019 Sanitary Sewer
            Update as presented. The motion was seconded by Corbin England and passed 6-0
            in favor.

3.     Active Transportation Plan; Amendment to the Master Transportation element of the
       West Jordan 2012 General Plan adding an Active Transportation section that will plan
       for pedestrian and bicycle facilities in the city; City-wide applicability; City of West
       Jordan (applicant).

Bill Baranowski, West Jordan Traffic Engineer, said the Active Transportation Plan helps us to
coordinate with neighboring cities and to identify missing links and special projects and to prioritize
the projects to get funding in the future from various sources.

Thomas McMurtry, Avenue Consultants, said this plan was a joint project with South Jordan. The top
project involving the two cities is 2700 West. He referred to the website JordanATP.com, which has
the full, interactive report. He reviewed the existing trails and bike lanes. West Jordan is already
starting in a good position for infrastructure. Collected Strava data gives a sense of where people are
biking and walking and where more facilities might be needed. They also have access to pedestrian
actuations at signalized intersections. Redwood Road has the most activity. They also review crash
data from the last 7 years. Improvements have been made to the 7800 South corridor for bicycle
infrastructure. Community outreach was conducted that contributed to the data and where the public
would like to see infrastructure. The idea for a 5.1-mile loop trail around the airport came from public
comment. He explained funding options.

Trish Hatch said she lives adjacent to the Jordan River parkway. It is very heavily used but is not
maintained very well on the West Jordan section. She asked if there were future plans to bring it up to
par and to either widen it or provide a separate bike or walking path for safety. The Jordan River
parkway draws people to West Jordan, and she would love to see it get more attention, which will
bring a more positive impression of the City to those who use the trail when comparing to other cities
along the trail.

Thomas McMurtry said the volume on the Jordan River trail is 100,000 – 200,00 people per year. He
wasn’t aware of funding for maintenance. The expectation is that each city is responsible for their own
section. Murray City dedicates a lot of money for maintenance of their portion of the trail.

Bill Baranowski said trail maintenance isn’t addressed in this study, but that is a matter of priorities
with the City. This plan looks at opportunities for filling in gaps and creating new trails and bike
facilities.

Kent Shelton referred to project 14 that appears to be on railroad property. He didn’t think that the
south end of the trail was feasible.

Thomas McMurtry wasn’t sure who owned the property, but the idea is to have a trail along the
railroad. He explained the potential timing of each project.

Ammon Allen asked if they found that activity was based more on destination based or recreational
travel. He also asked if the school districts and businesses were contacted.

Thomas McMurtry said the survey calls out that data, which has a good cross section of survey
participants. The vast majority are not commuters. He said they left survey cards at some bike shops.

There wasn’t a lot of interaction with the school district, because the study was completed mostly last
summer, but they looked at the situations around schools.

Ammon Allen said he would love to improve the destination traffic to businesses.

Thomas McMurtry said the major destinations is where the pedestrian and bike traffic is. Redwood
Road has the highest pedestrian traffic and is planned for 10-foot sidewalks on both sides of the street,
which would give people a safe space and where bikes can share with pedestrians.

Bill Baranowski said the plan for Redwood Road are 10-foot wide sidewalks as a compromise for not
having bike lanes. However, 1300 West is the better option for a commuter bike trail.

Matt Quinney opened the public hearing.

Matt Quinney said the Commission received a letter of support from Midvale City planning division,

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

Trish Hatch thought this is a fantastic plan and she is passionate about planning. It is key in attracting
businesses and development. She looks forward to seeing even more.

               Corbin England and passed 6-0 in favor.
