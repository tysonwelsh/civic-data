West Valley City
A zone text change to add artificial turf standards and amend several landscaping
standards
City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to add artificial turf standards and amend several
landscaping standards. While the City currently allows artificial turf as non-living landscaping,
there are no standards for the quality, installation, or maintenance of artificial turf. As a result,
the City has seen some poor applications of artificial turf. Given the ongoing drought, staff
anticipates more interest in this form of landscaping. In summary, the proposed standards
address the appropriate locations for, appearance, color, minimum height, minimum weight,
warranty, installation, borders, and maintenance of artificial turf.

In addition to setting minimum standards for artificial turf, the proposed ordinance would allow
artificial turf to count as live plant material for up to half of the required live plant material in all
zones. The proposed ordinance would also allow artificial turf as an alternative to lawn along
major arterial streets. This revision would address concerns the City has heard from several
parties about requiring lawn. It would also address HB 95, a bill currently being considered by
the Utah Legislature that, if passed, would prohibit cities from requiring lawn.

The changes proposed in Section 7-11-205 would amend how the amount of required live plant
material is calculated for the front yards of single unit dwellings. Currently, the amount of
required live plant material is 50% of the net landscaped area where the net landscaped area is
the area of the front yard less the driveway. Since the size of driveways can vary from lot to lot,
the amount of required live plant material changes from lot to lot. To simplify the calculation and
standardize the requirement for all lots, the proposed live plant material percentage is 30%. This
percentage was derived by removing the maximum allowable hard surfacing (40%) and then
dividing the remaining area by two.

Lastly, the proposed change would update the required irrigation controller for commercial and
multi-family residential developments to a Watersense labeled smart irrigation controller that
automatically adjusts the frequency and/or duration of irrigation events in response to changing
weather conditions.

During the Planning Commission study meeting, questions were raised about the need for a sub-
base material for artificial turf and the requirement to separate artificial turf from trees and
shrubs. The latest version of the ordinance adds language that requires a sub-base and clarifies
that the separation only applies to new plants to allow for growth.

3846 S 2200 W
Zone Change from R-1-8 to A
1.31 acres

West Valley City has submitted a zone change application for a 1.31-acre parcel located at 3846
S 2200 W. The current zone is R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000
square feet) and the proposed zone is A (Agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre). The current
General Plan designation is low density residential (3 to 4 units/acre).

Last year, City staff proposed General Plan and zone changes to several parcels as application
GPZ-4-2021. The purpose of the application was to address HB 1003 which was passed by the
Utah Legislature last year. Among other things, HB 1003, removed cities’ ability to regulate
most building design standards for a one to two family dwelling where no development
agreement exists. Most vacant properties in the City planned for residential use either have a
development agreement that includes building design standards or they are within an agricultural
zone where staff anticipates a development agreement upon a future zone change. The properties
included in GPZ-4-2021 did not have a development agreement and were zoned R-1-6, R-1-8, R-
1-12, and RM. The properties zoned R-1 could be developed as single unit dwellings without
many of the City’s building design standards.

Staff’s original list of properties affected by GPZ-4-2021 included the subject property. Upon
hearing about the proposed zone change, the owner of the subject property agreed to a
development agreement where any new homes would follow the City’s previous design
standards. Given the owner’s willingness to enter into a development agreement, his property
was removed from GPZ-4-2021. However, despite several attempts from staff, the owner of the
subject property has not executed the development agreement. For this reason, staff is proposing
to rezone this property once again.

RM Zone

According to the City’s zoning ordinance, zone changes to the RM zone can only be considered
by the City if the following four conditions are met:

   1. For properties without existing Structures which are proposed to be developed, the
      property must be a minimum of one acre and the Density must not exceed 12 units per
      acre. For properties with existing Structures proposed to be redeveloped, the property
       must be a minimum of 20,000 square feet and the Density must not exceed 20 units per
       acre. For properties that are west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West
       Valley Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station or the Redwood
       Junction light rail station, the Density may exceed 20 units per acre;

   2. The property shall meet any one of the following four requirements: (i) have access and
      Frontage on a Street with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet (for properties
      under two acres) or 100 feet (for properties two acres or larger) as indicated on the Major
      Street Plan; (ii) be adjacent to existing multifamily residential development on two sides;
      or (iii) be located west of Redwood Road and within one-half mile of the West Valley
      Central light rail station, the Decker Lake light rail station, or the Redwood Junction light
      rail station; or (iv) if the property is vacant, was vacant as of January 19, 2021, will be an
      exempt housing development under 24 CFR 100.304 or successor regulation with a
      density no greater than seven units per acre, and have access and Frontage on a Street
      with a planned Right-of-way width of at least 80 feet;

   3. A Development Agreement must be proposed with the zone change application that
      addresses Dwelling Unit sizes, exterior materials, architecture, Landscaping, and project
      Amenities; and

   4. A traffic impact study prepared by a registered traffic engineer is required for projects
      with 100 or more Dwelling Units.

This application meets the first condition in that the property is over one acre and has a proposed
density at 8.6 units/acre. For the second condition, the subject property has access and frontage
on 5600 West, which has a planned right-of-way width of 106’. On the third condition, the
applicant’s development agreement standards are addressed later in this report. The fourth
condition does not apply because the project is under 100 units.

       Dwelling Unit Sizes
       Each of the four proposed townhome units are 3-bedroom units above grade. Three of the
       four proposed townhome units will have a fourth bedroom in the basement. The
       minimum area set forth in the City’s ordinance for a 3-bedroom townhome is 1,400
       square feet and 1,700 square feet for a 4-bedroom unit. The units will range in size from
       1,560 to 1,796 square feet above grade and 2,291 to 2,519 square feet when including the
       finished basements. For reference, all of the homes along the south side of 4535 South to
       the north have basements. According to County Assessor data, the size of these homes
       range from 1,352 to 2,197 square feet including the basements (finished or unfinished)
       with the average area being 1,771 square feet.

       Exterior Materials
       City Code for multi-unit residential building exteriors states the following: “All multi-
       unit residential Building exteriors shall be brick, stucco, stone, tile, metal panels or fiber
       cement siding. With the exception of brick or stone which may be used on the entire
       Building exterior, no single material shall comprise more than 60 percent of a Building’s

       exterior. Where stucco, tile, metal panels or fiber cement siding are used, at least 20
       percent of the Building’s exterior shall be brick or stone.” While the elevations submitted
       by the applicant do not show 20% brick or stone, the development agreement does
       include this requirement so no exception is being requested. If this application is
       approved the elevations would need to be modified.

       Architecture
       The architecture will be subject to the City’s multi-unit residential design standards.
       Assuming this application is approved, a comprehensive review of the building plans will
       verify compliance with the design standards. Based on a preliminary review of the
       elevations, staff noticed the front elevations are short on the required 25% fenestration or
       windows. The development agreement includes the 25% fenestration standard so, here
       again, the elevations would need to be modified if this application is approved.

       Landscaping and Recreation Space
       The proposed landscaping and recreation space amount (51%) is above the required 50%
       specified for planned unit developments (PUDs).

Discussion:    Jody Knapp presented the application. She stated that they had discussed a few of
               the site plan comments during the study meeting, and she also went over those
               comments with the applicant. The applicant will modify the building layout;
               however, the site itself will remain the same. She noted that the applicant is still
               working out the shared access with CVS. Jody stated that the grading is still being
               evaluated for the landscaping. She explained that there is discussion in the
               development agreement about the north-south pedestrian connection from 4100
               South to the Sage Valley development. Jody said the applicant has been
               communicating with the primary developer regarding the pedestrian connection,
               and those comments will be part of the site review.
               Brock Richards, the applicant, stated that West Valley Family Medicine owns the
               subject property, and they have many locations throughout the City. He explained
               that the first floor of the proposed building would be Family Medicine Practice,
               and on the second floor, half will be used as a medical spa, and the other half is
               yet to be rented, but they are putting another medical tenant there.
               Mr. Richard stated that they had reduced the size of the building on their original
               site plan to meet the setback and road improvement requirements. He said they
               are two stalls short of meeting the parking requirement and asked the Commission
               for a 5% parking reduction.
               Mr. Richard noted that the physician running his practice in the subject property
               feels the shard access with CVS would benefit his patients. However, they are still
               waiting for approval from CVS.
               Chair Meaders asked Jody if she was aware of the applicant requesting a 5%
               parking reduction. Jody said she was not, but she could look into it. She said if the
               applicant is decreasing the footprint of the building, that will reduce the parking
               requirement. Jody stated that there are some shard parking and access easements
               in place that could also affect the parking. She noted that this would be discussed
               as part of the plan review.
              Commissioner Woodruff noticed that the site plan had parking spots for compact
              cars. He asked Jody if that was something the City allowed. Jody said that 25% of
              the parking for medical use could be for compact cars.
              Commissioner Winters asked Mr. Richard what a medical spa offers? Mr.
              Richards stated that the two primary uses of the spa would be laser hair removal
              and Botox.
