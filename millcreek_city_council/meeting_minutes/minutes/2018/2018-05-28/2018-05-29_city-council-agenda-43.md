1. Quarterly Report by Salt Lake County Animal Services
Carrie Sibert from Salt Lake County Animal Services provided a quarterly report for services
provided in January-March 2018. She started her presentation by recognizing Officer Melanie
Bennett who won the 2017 Utah Animal Control Association Officer of the Year. Officer
Bennett made valiant efforts by calling in a fire that would have spread to a chicken coop and
a house then used a fire extinguisher to eliminate the threat. She also saved a german
shepherd’s life in Herriman with the help of Unified Police Department.

Sibert then went over the quarterly report. From the Director’s notes she gave an overview of
the programs they offer. They have had a lot of success in the adoption follow up program,
which provides follow up calls and training to keep adoptions in the home instead of being
returned to the shelter. They are also revamping their court ordered community service to put
people into skilled positions. She then highlighted a high-profile case where Animal Services
was able to deal with over 200 animals, mostly reptiles, in an abandonment case. Animal
Services also started new advisory board committee that has representatives from the
jurisdictions that are provided service.

Salt Lake County Animal Services ended the first quarter in taking in over 1,500 animals and
ended at a 95% save rate on cats and dogs. Millcreek had 107 animals come into the shelter
and a 93% save rate for cats and dogs. Mayor Silvestrini asked if the live release rate is
determined by the condition of the animals that come in and the euthanasia services provided.
Sibert said if owners come in and request euthanasia, the shelter evaluates the animals first
and may not provide the service and those are not included in the live release rates because it
is out of their control. Animals Services sold 725 licenses to Millcreek, 64 microchips, 45
rabies vaccinations, 36 Bordetella vaccinations, 56 DHPP vaccinations and 39 FVRCP.
Animal Services held successful fundraisers at Dirty Johnson’s Dog Wash and Tats for Cats at
Fallen Angel Tattoo Shop. Mayor Silvestrini said he has received positive comments on
patrols.

2. Discussion on Light Pollution Ordinance Solutions
Francis Lilly said staff did research and Salt Lake City offers an elegant solution on light
pollution. From Salt Lake City’s code, he read, “On site lighting shall be located, directed or
designed in such a manner as to contain and direct light and glare only to the property on
which it is located.” He recommended the City add similar language into the R zones, C zones
or in the supplementary and qualifying regulations. Mayor Silvestrini said this discussion was
spurred by public comment at the last meeting. He said private street lights could arguably
resolve a budgetary issue for lack of street lighting funds and wonders if the proposed
ordinance would get in the way of that.

Council Member Uipi asked for details about the initial complaint. Mayor Silvestrini said the
lighting seems to be retaliatory in nature. He also received another complaint about trees
wrapped in lighting that was intrusive to some neighbors, but other people may consider the
lights to be attractive. Council Member Marchant hopes that the City does not have to regulate
every detail. Lilly asked for guidance from the Council for an ordinance. He said Salt Lake
City treats the lighting as a nuisance. He thinks there should be some sort of specificity that
empowers code compliance to enforce the code.

Council Member Catten asked if Lilly would propose regulations involving time limitations.
Lilly said there are two city code compliance officers, so resources in enforcing the code
could be an issue. The ordinance needs to be mindful of staff time in enforcing it. Council
Member Marchant said Christmas lighting does not stay on the property and asked where the
point between nuisance and non-nuisance was. Council Member Uipi asked about houses that
bring in hundreds of cars during the holidays and is also concerned about the City’s resources.
John Geilmann said ambient lighting is generally addressed in the commercial zones and the
nuisance concept is a private nuisance between neighbors. Geilmann said it is a private issue
that they must resolve themselves. Mayor Silvestrini said the City does not have to assume the

Detective Bettina Allen presented statistics for crime in Millcreek in April. She said drug

5.5 Report by the Unified Fire Authority

UFA Captain Aaron Nelson reported that UFA is approaching the end of their budget
process. The final budget will be approved next month on June 19. UFA just completed
the captains testing process and ended up with a list of 29 people numbered and ranked.
The list is good for the next two years. UFA is preparing for wildland season and a
normal fire season is anticipated. He said that UFA is beta testing a new staffing model —
the “one in one” model. Previously there were two paramedics on the fire engine, and
now they are putting one on the fire engine and one on an ambulance. The idea is that a
paramedic will get to the scene quicker. The department has gotten good feedback from
it. Mayor Silvestrini explained that there are two different ambulances, basic life support
and advanced life support.

A member of the public asked if there were any changes to the fireworks rules. Mayor
Silvestrini said there was a change in the State’s fireworks law so that the number of days
of lawful discharge has decreased. Millcreek also changed the unlawful discharge
designation area to include the property around the Jordan River. He said there were not
any other designated area changes. He said there will be a map in the stands where people
can buy fireworks showing the areas that are not allowed for discharge. The new law also
increased the penalty for unlawful discharge and allows for civil liability. He said parents
are financially responsible for their children who break the law. Another member of the
public asked if there were bans on aerial fireworks. Mayor Silvestrini said no, and that
the fireworks on the ground cause the most fires. Captain Nelson warned that sparklers
are dangerous too.

19.60-3(4) C-2 Zone — For building adjacent to residential uses, the side and rear yards
abutting single family residential uses shall be increased by one foot for every two feet that
the structure exceeds 30 feet in height.

19.44.050(1)(10) R-M Zone — For any development adjacent to an R-1, R-2, R-4, A-1, or
A-2 zone (“residential zone”), the maximum height for structures on the perimeter of the
development adjoining said zones shall be 28 ft.... maximum height allowed by increments
with each additional foot of height requiring an additional 15 ft. in set back from the rear or
side yard lot lines...

building could be further to the rear yard in the R-M zone than is being proposed.
Cleveland said staff met with the applicant a handful of times and there has been a lot of
give and take. She mentioned that there were concerns about tandem parking expressed at
the last Council meeting. The proposed parking meets the code and now with less units,
