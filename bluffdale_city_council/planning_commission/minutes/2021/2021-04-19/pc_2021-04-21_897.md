crossing would be designated to the private property. It has been approved by the City Engineer.

City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, presented the staff rep01t and stated
that the above item was considered at the past two Planning C01mnission Meetings. In response to
Chair Cragun's question regarding the need for a public hearing, Mr. Crowell stated that
Mrs. Robison listened to the previous meeting and confirmed that the public hearing was closed.
Therefore, a public hearing would not be necessary because that obligation had been satisfied.
Mr. Crowell reported that he received an email from Mac Brubaker that was to be read and
submitted into the public record. Chair Cragun could opt to re-open the public hearing to allow for
the reading of the email and to consider any other emails that might be submitted.

Mr. Crowell reported that the Planning Commission has had conducted two public hearings on this
agenda item. The staff report contained a bulleted summary of key issues and themes that the
Planning Commission requested be provided in summary form. Mr. Crowell drove the Valley to
take pictures of samples of building designs and materials that would inform tonight's discussion.
He also spoke with various business owners about their perspectives. The process of developing the
revised text amendments had been time-consuming and complex.

One area of focus had been the gateway into Bluffdale at Bange1ter Highway and 2700 West.
There has been extensive discussion on how that gateway should be designed to be visually
attractive without being cost-prohibitive to developers. Consideration of how one area should be
designed had proven to be difficult in terms of how other areas should be designed.

Commissioner Walston asked why the focus should be just on 2700 West because there are other
commercial corridors where the design standards should also be applicable. Mr. Crowell agreed
that design should be important throughout the City. The focus on 2700 West came about because
it has been an area of interest for developers. Mr. Crowell stated that a major point in the ordinance
is Section 11 .150, which addresses the general requirements for all commercial site plans
throughout Bluffdale. The ordinance will raise the bar for everyone. Commissioner Walston
opined that it would be much easier if the requirements are consistent.

Mr. Crowell indicated that there are areas in the General Plan that could be worthy of design focus.
Bange1ter Highway and Redwood Road were of particular concern. That intersection has its own
ordinance that was developed by Woodbury and is known as the Gateway-Redwood Zone. It is a
36-page document that includes design guidelines. That type of opportunity presents itself when
there is a master developer.

On the west side of Redwood and Bangerter Highway, Bluffdale City owns the property right at the
gateway, some of which is already zoned GC-1. The City owns the property on the north end, as
well. The City will be able to write its own ordinance for that property when it goes out for a
Request for Proposals ("RFP"). Mr. Crowell identified other areas, such as the Porter Rockwell
Boulevard, which is zoned GC-1 and Mixed-Use. In addition, Bringhurst Station and Rockwell
Ridge both have their own zones and Development Agreements. It was noted that it would be

extremely difficult to establish City-wide requirements when 2700 West is an ongoing p1iority and

Chair Cragun commented that there is a difference between industrial and other areas in the City
Bluffdale, so it may not be viable to have City-wide blanket requirements. However, Special
Districts provide a tool for developing specific requirements. Chair Cragun added that the Bluffdale
gateways do not all need to look the same. Mr. Crowell stated that design standards have become a
hot topic throughout the City. Commissioner Walston added that gateways can have variation.

Mr. Crowell stated that architects take some creative license, but some of the numbers specified for
things such as the use of glass might be high. The challenge is to determine an appropriate number.
He had received criticism for the numbers provided but was not getting definitive numbers from
anyone. The equation for balancing land and construction costs with commercial viability had been
problematic. Mr. Crowell commented that he does not have a vested interest in what the numbers
will be. He was more interested in allowing for partnerships and creativity.

Chair Cragun asked if regulations will place a more onerous workload on staff. Mr. Crowell stated
that ultimately, the Planning Commission will have to deal with the needs of developers. Chair
Cragun remarked that there needs to be reconciliation between the various requirements. Too much
flexibility results in unsightly building designs. The market and cost of construction were the
p1imary criticisms leveled by developers against the City in its design requirements.

Mr. Crowell commented that the number one industry in Bluffdale is construction. Therefore, the
City Council recently asked that contractor yards and offices be added to the Use Table. The
market would support designating land as HC Heavy Commercial and allowing that type of use.
The City Council was not ready to switch to that land use right away. Brick construction could be
eliminated but there was some interest in having brick and rock create a unifying design theme. He
noted that Woodbury has an Architectural Review Committee for properties it develops to ensure
that building designs are attractive.

Attention to detail will depend on the location of the building materials being used. Buildings that
are in the rear portion of the property and far removed from road frontage do not need stringent
design requirements. The front portions will potentially be the main focus of building designs with
more flexibility in the rear. Another recurring theme was the solid wall buffers imposed between
commercial and residential properties. In many cases, the walls fall under the Subdivision Code,
which was not being developed. It is a frequently heard complaint but is costly.

A c1iticism Mr. Crowell recently heard was that 20% landscaping is too much in a GC-1 zone.
Many options can fall within the buffer requirements within the Landscaping Code. Landscaping in
front of a business is a philosophical question with many opinions.

Mr. Crowell discussed the pros and cons of stucco versus brick or other materials. Building
materials are evolving and many options have been developed. It was noted that office and retail
buildings can be two stories. There are differing opinions regarding the merits of two-story
buildings.

With regard to the park, Mr. Crowell stated that it is a Salt Lake County facility and not a Bluffdale

efficiency will be largely out of the City' s control. Water rates will have an impact on the water
requirements that are imposed.

Mr. Crowell explained that there were no other new themes enumerated in Mr. Brubaker's email.
Referencing the survey conducted by the General Plan consultants, he opined that the citizens'
response to commercial development actually referred to a grocery store. He acknowledged that
other commercial development is important. To that end, the City is trying to provide incentives for
development in industrial parks.

With regard to 2700 West, Mr. Crowell had received feedback from people who have said that the
development taking place there was not what they had expected. It was noted that staff had been
contacted with ideas for the property along 2700 West.

Mr. Crowell suggested that the Planning Conunission consider the buffer and the way walls are
measured. They should also consider whether a solid wall should separate a business from a
residential area. Separating schools might be an exception since they are located in a residential
zone but are not residences. Context was detennined to be an important factor to consider.

Mr. Crowell stated that the discussion should detennine what constitutes the front and what would
be deemed as "visible." Chair Cragun stated that wall materials and landscaping are key issues to
address. Mr. Crowell added that context and creativity are also important considerations. Chair
Cragun stated that if people are going to see all four sides of a building, all four need to be
attractive. There could be more flexibility for walls with minimal public exposure.

Chair Cragun opined that 2700 West ought to have a brick theme. Using the proposed Arctic Circle
as a frame of reference would be a good place to start renegotiating the percentages of required
building materials.

Conunissioner Walston revisited the visibility issue and felt there was a gradation of visibility. As a
result, using the designations of ''back" and "front" might be too dichotomous. Mr. Crowell stated
that the gradation thresholds would have to be defined. Commissioner Walston agreed that the
requirements set for 2700 West should apply to other entryways into Bluffdale.

Timing issues were discussed. Chair Cragun stated that landscaping is a major sticking point so it
might not be viable to make a recommendation at the next meeting. Mr. Crowell stated that the
longer the process takes, the greater the risk the delay poses to the City because the current
standards would be applied rather than the new ones. Ways to expedite the process were identified.
With regard to landscaping, Chair Cragun hoped to see more pictures to illustrate the options.

Mr. Crowell suggested that the Planning Commission Members visit the Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy District's Garden Park in West Jordan where there are several examples of plants that
require less water. Mr. Crowell stated landscaping is challenging because the Water Conservancy
District will raise rates if the landscaping does not meet their conservation requirements.

Mr. Crowell noted that the longer the document is delayed, the more likely the developers will be to
submit site plan proposals on the basis of the current City Ordinance. If the document is not
presented to the City Council by next week, the City 's ability to apply the Model Ordinance will
expire. Mr. Crowell urged the Planning Commission to forward the matter to City Council as soon
as possible. Chair Cragun left the public hearing open.

Debbie Cragun moved to table the matter to the next meeting to give staff time to work on the
list of items identified by Mr. Crowell. Steve Walston seconded the motion. Vote on motion:
Kory Luker-Aye, Steve Walston-Aye, Debbie Cragun-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
