43   3.1) Public Hearing and recommendation of Justin Hamond’s request for review of the
44   Hammond Zone Change on 0.83 acres, located at approximately 600 West State Street,
45   changing the zoning from Mixed Use to Heavy Commercial.
46
47   Brittney Harris presented the item. It is in compliance with the General Plan. The current plan is
48   to build an office warehouse on the site. Commissioner Kunze asked why the previous plan for

     Lehi City Planning Commission                    1                                     July 10, 2025
49   the property was scrapped. City staff was uncertain. Commissioner Kunze asked if the area was
50   big enough for a Heavy Commercial project. Harris clarified that the property is zoned for Heavy
51   Commercial, but that they will still need to meet landscaping barriers and all other requirements.
52
53   David Stockinger was present, representing the applicant. He had no additional comments.
54
55   Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.
56
57   Corey Gurney owns the property to the west. He says that the location is currently surrounded by
58   houses, and due to that he doesn’t think the property should be rezoned. He thinks it may cause
59   more traffic. He also thinks that the planned project fits under the current Mixed Use zoning, and
60   thus there is no need to rezone prior to construction.
61
62   Mellissa Noaks owns adjacent property. She was unaware that the area was zoned for Heavy
63   Commercial in the general plan. She thinks the zone change should wait until official
64   constructions plans are submitted.
65
66   Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.
67
68   Commissioner Eyre asked when the last time the General Plan was updated. It was in 2022.
69   Previously the area was zoned as Mixed Use, but the Mixed Use zone was reworked to include
70   population densities and there was already many Heavy Commercial and Industrial projects in
71   the area, which is why it was rezoned.
72
73   Commissioner Eyre asked legal if the Planning Commission has any options if an applicant’s
74   plan is trying to align with the General Plan or if it must be approved. Craig Chambers explained
75   that the Commission would have to argue that it wasn’t arbitrary or capricious to deny the
76   change.
77
78   Commissioner Everett stated that there had already been a public process for the general plan,
79   and that someone needs to be the first to bring properties into alignment, but that he is also
80   worried about the adjacent residential properties and is inclined to question the timing of the
81   request, given that no site plan has been submitted. Commissioner Kunze mentioned that the
82   previous plan for the property was scrapped, so there would be nothing stopping the owner from
83   scrapping the current plan and selling the property, at which point it would be open to any Heavy
84   Commercial project. Commissioner Jackson agreed with her, mentioning that it is the duty of the
85   Planning Commission to consider the property as though there is no planned project.
86
87   Commissioner Eyre asked staff about buffers between Heavy Commercial and Mixed Use zones.
88   Kim Struthers explained that there likely would be no required buffers, as those are typically
89   only required if the adjacent zone is residential. Commissioner Lockhart argued that the zone
90   must have been residential at some point if homes were built there. She asked if landowners lost
91   the rights of a residential zone when the zone was changed. Kim Struthers explained that the city
92   can only base decisions on current zoning, and buffering rights are not grandfathered in.
93   Commissioner Jackson asked planning staff to look into making that possible. Commissioner
94   Lockhart clarified for those present that the job of the Planning Commission is to decide if things
95   are legal, City Council deals with more nuance.
96
     Lehi City Planning Commission                    2                                     July 10, 2025
 97   Brittney Harris clarified that approving the zone change wouldn’t introduce a new use to the
 98   area, as there are already several Heavy Commercial businesses in the area. There are also other
 99   areas in the city where Heavy Commercial and Light Industrial intermingle with residential
100   properties.
101
102   Commissioner Jackson shared that the area is heading towards Heavy Commercial, so even if
103   this zone change is denied, it likely will be approved in the future.
104
105   The Commission debated which version of code the applicant would be under, and if code could
106   be changed prior to approval. Brittney Harris clarified that as no site plan has been submitted, the
107   applicant wasn’t governed by the current code and would be held to whatever code is in place
108   when that is given to the city and approved.
109
110   Commissioner Eyre shared that he thinks all sides have valid arguments. Commissioner Everett
111   expressed frustration that the decision is essentially a dice roll for the community whether a site
112   plan is submitted or not.
113
114   Commissioner Lockhart asked if the City Council could negotiate buffering. Craig Chambers
115   explained that it would have to happen through a development agreement. She feels like they are
116   legally obligated to change the zone. Commissioner Jackson disagreed, as it is a legislative item
117   and should thus give the Commission more latitude. He didn’t see the change as a net gain for
118   the city and thus had no issue with delaying the zone change. Commissioner Lockhart argued
119   that a development agreement would fix the buffering issues. Commissioner Jackson agreed but
120   pointed out that if the zone were changed then developers would have no incentive to enter into a
121   development agreement, and the city would have no right to enforce one. He recommended
122   putting the issue on hold. Commissioner Lockhart preferred sending the issue to City Council as
123   they would be able to more effectively navigate the nuances of the issue as an elected body.
124
125                   Motion: Commissioner Everett moved to give a negative recommendation to the
126                   Lehi City Council with the finding that enacting the zone change would create an
127                   incompatibility and an inconsistency with the current use of properties
128                   surrounding it that are also still mixed-use.
129
130                   Amended Motion Commissioner Everett moved to give a negative
131                   recommendation to the Lehi City Council with the finding that enacting the zone
132                   change would create an incompatibility and an inconsistency with the current use
133                   of properties surrounding it that are also still mixed-use. He recommends that this
134                   scenario calls for thinking through additions to the code that would better protect
135                   residential properties in situations where commercial is abutting residential or
136                   legal non-conforming, specifically codifying circumstances where buffers are
137                   required before moving forward with this legislative item. He recommends that
138                   through a development agreement the property could be used for certain heavy
139                   commercial uses specific to the property in a mixed use zone. Commissioner
140                   Kunze seconded.
141
142                   Vote: Commissioner Lockhart, no. Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner
143                   Eyre, no. Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes.

225   3.5) Public hearing and consideration of Gardner Plumb’s request for approval of
226   exceptions for the Sy View West Anchor Site Plan located at approximately 240 West
227   Traverse Terrace Drive including exceptions for a drive aisle between the building and the
228   street, block length, front elevation glass requirement, and architectural wall variations.
229
230   Brittney Harris presented the item. The applicant feels the drive aisle and connectivity exceptions
231   are justified due to the grading of the property. Additionally, they would like an exception for the
232   amount of required glass on the front of the building, as well as an exception for the required
233   number of wall elevations.
234
235   Howard Cooke was present representing the applicant. He is under an NDA and cannot specify
236   what is being constructed. His client has a systematic approach to putting in new locations and
237   have whittled down the exceptions to the minimum amount presented to the Commission. He
238   reiterated that grading is the major concern for several of them and clarified that the limited glass
239   on the front of the building is a security measure.
240
      Lehi City Planning Commission                     5                                      July 10, 2025
241   Commissioner Everett asked for a more detailed justification of the drive aisles. Orienting the
242   building on the lot has been a challenge due to grading, and the drive aisle provides needed
243   access to the back of the store. The length exceeds 600 feet, but there is an access on one side
244   that helps to break it up. Cooke and his clients are worried about safety risks posed by the nearby
245   high school if they provide access in the back of the building.
246
247   Commissioner Kunze asked for the justification for the wall variation exception. Cooke
248   explained that the rest of his client’s buildings look like this.
249
250   Commissioner Eyre asked for the grade difference between the street and the parking lot. He was
251   concerned about a lack of pedestrian access in the back of the store. The applicant is worried
252   about liability and risk from how steep the stairs would need to be, especially in the winter.
253   Commissioner Everett suggested including pedestrian access in a specific area that would keep
254   pedestrians out of traffic.
255
256   Brittney Harris pulled up the grading plan. The Commission discussed the various exceptions
257   and issues they have with them.
258
259   Commissioner Jackson opened and closed public comment.
260
261   The Commission decided to work through the exceptions one at a time, beginning with the drive
262   aisle. Commissioner Lockhart shared that it makes sense with the grade.
263
264   Commissioner Everett thinks that a pedestrian access point would be a good thing to ask for in
265   exchange for connectivity. He recommends building a ramp if the applicant is worried about
266   liability with stairs. Commissioner Eyre likes the block length determined by code, and shared
267   frustration at the exception request. The applicant showed that there is a trail connection on one
268   side of the building. Commissioner Lockhart asked how high school students will get to the
269   location. There is some sidewalk connectivity. The applicant would like to direct people to the
270   front of the building rather than the back. The city had recommended trail access closer to the
271   front of the store, but grade once again made that difficult. The location of trail access is up to
272   the developer.
273
274   Commissioner Lockhart shared that she doesn’t think the burden of proof has been met to justify
275   the exception to glass on the front of the building. Cooke clarified that the applicant wants to
276   avoid having too much of the store visible from the outside. Commissioner Eyre established that
277   most visual sightlines are going to be on the south and east sides of the building and
278   recommended that the applicant focus on those. Commissioner Jackson agreed. The front of the
279   building is the only side that needs the exception, as no other sides will be visible. Faux windows
280   are not acceptable under city code. Cooke brought up the possibility of a skylight-style window.
281   He asked what the purpose of the glass percentage design standard was. Kim Struthers explained
282   that it is primarily an aesthetic standard. Commissioner Lockhart clarified that the commission is
283   not singling out this developer, and that there have been similar debates in the past, for example
284   the Latter-Day Saints temple construction project. Commissioner Eyre expressed discomfort
285   with setting a precedent for allowing less glass.
286
287   Cooke asked if the development would need to come back through Planning Commission once
288   the issues were fixed. Commissioner Eyre explained that the Commission would give a
      Lehi City Planning Commission                    6                                       July 10, 2025
289   recommendation, and the City Council could look at the adjustments that had been made in the
290   interim.
291
292                   Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to approve the exception for the drive aisle
293                   and the connectivity based on the challenges of the topography, to deny the
294                   request for the decreased percentage of glass with the findings that the applicant
295                   didn’t meet the burden of proof that they couldn’t meet the standard of the code,
296                   and to approve the exception for the glass being the third architectural variation
297                   on the applicable sides. He included all DRC comments.
298
299                   Amended Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to approve the exception for the
300                   drive aisle and the connectivity based on the challenges of the topography, to

303                   the code, and that it would set a precedent for other locations throughout the city

389   3.8) Public hearing and recommendation of Lehi City’s request for a review of a
390   Development Code amendment to Chapter 26, Accessory Uses, changing requirements for
391   Detached Accessory Dwelling Units.
392
393   Brittney Harris presented the item. There are two main changes. The first lowers the size
394   requirement of a lot to more accurately reflect a third of an acre. The second change deals with
395   slope concerns and an increasing number of ADU requests have been coming in from Traverse
396   Mountain.
397
398   Commissioner Jackson shared concern over the phrase “environmentally sensitive areas”. Harris
399   explained that it is defined in city code and in the general plan. Commissioner Eyre asked if the
400   code would be clearer if the word “grade” was included. The Commission agreed that it would
401   provide clarity.
402
403   Commissioner Everett asked about a DRC note from Gary Ellis recommending striking clause B.
404   Ellis explained that he thinks that greater grades should be allowed as long as the developers
405   have the budget and tools to engineer a solution. He said if pressed, he would extend the code to
406   allow up to 30% grade. Commissioner Everett asked if the city would be liable if an approved
407   ADU collapsed. The engineer would be liable.
408
409   Brittney Harris mentioned that ADUs as a concept are largely an affordability option. Creating
410   ADUs on steep slopes safely negate that affordability standard. She also shared concerns over
411   compounding issues on slopes that the city is already facing in the area.
412
413   Commissioner Everett clarified that the amendment will only change the required size and slope
414   of the lot, and that no other aspects of ADU code are being adjusted.
415
416   Gary Ellis agrees that there should be a limit on slope but recommends 30% rather than 20%.
417
418   Commissioner Jackson opened and closed public comment.
419
420                   Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to give a positive recommendation to the
421                   Lehi City Council with the addition for clarification to both b and c, identifying
422                   percentages as grading, and opening city council to consider a larger percentage
423                   than 20% based on DRC comments, with the findings that the proposed
424                   development code does seek to ensure the health safety and welfare of existing
425                   and future Lehi residents, the proposed development code amendment allows for
426                   detached ADUs on 1/3 acre lots, the proposed development code amendment is in
427                   conformance with the purposes, intent, and provisions of the general plan and it’s
428                   various elements. He included all DRC comments. Commissioner Everett
429                   seconded.
