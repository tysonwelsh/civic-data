TIP Modification - Utah Lake Shore Trail - Saratoga Springs Hot Pots (00:02:47) Bob Allen addressed the Committee stating this project has been around for a while. In the 2016 selection cycle Saratoga Springs was awarded funds for one project to fill in two gaps in the trail along the lake. In 2020 the funds were awarded to ACME Construction. In 2021 ACME Construction abandoned the project and demanded additional funds. In 2022 Saratoga Springs and UDOT separated ways with ACME Construction to seek a new contractor. There is quite a bit of work that will need to be done in preparation for paving the trail and to avoid settling. The proposal is to separate the one project with two sections into two projects for each section. The first would be the Hot Pots Section to be worked on with funds from UDOT and MAG and to defer paving. The second section is the Amanda Lane Section, which will be supported by funds from Saratoga Springs. The cost of paving will not be included in the proposed modification. If the City needs additional funding to pave the trail, it would be a new application. There was additional discussion related to the contingency funds. Brad Kenison moved to recommend that $1,233,000 be added to the Hot Pots Trail project and that the scope modification to construct the project through stabilization be approved. Page 2 of 4 Richard Nelson seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Corridor Preservation Provo 2230 North (00:16:01) Calvin Clark addressed the Committee explaining the request is from Provo for road designation H41. With the most recent Corridor Preservation Map adoption the Corridor Preservation Road Designations now match the RTP adopted map. The property owner is a willing seller and approached the City for the purchase of the property. Robert Mills moved to recommend that the MPO Board approve the Provo City Corridor Preservation Fund Request for $630,875. Brad Kenison seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. Title VI Plan Adoption (00:21:49) Kendall Willardson addressed the Committee and stated that the plan is out for public comment. At this time no public comment has been received. The Title VI Plan outlines how MAG will adhere to regulations as outlined by the FTA/FHWA. The ADA Accessibility Plan is how MAG will address the needs of people with disabilities as outlined in the American with Disabilities Act. The Limited English Proficiency plan addresses the needs of people with limited English proficiency for languages identified in the plan analysis. Vern Keeslar moved to recommend that the MPO BOard approve the Title VI, Limited English Proficiency, and ADA Accessibility Plans contingent on there being no significant comments that alter the plans. John Dorny seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. TIP Project Initiation and Management (00:25:33) Calvin Clark stated MAG staff is working to do a better job to keep TAC apprised and in the loop of how projects are being funded and moving through the process. It was clarified that the process is not changing, but each step is being formalized and integrated into Workflow Manager. Bob Allen stated this is to help to get projects started. MAG worked with Utah County and UDOT to develop a project management process. The process creates a uniform process and will be run through the Workflow Manager platform. The process includes steps in the following order:● Project Initiation Meeting● Contract● Consultant Selection● Kickoff Meeting The next steps are done concurrently:● Environmental Work Page 3 of 4 ● ROW Procurement● 30% Design - Geometric Review○ 60% Design - Plan -in-Hand Review○ 90% Design - PS&E Review Once the design is completed the next steps in order include:● Project Advertisement● Construction● Project Closeout It was emphasized to the importance of keeping MAG staff, and when applicable Utah County, in the loop throughout the process. MAG staff can help cities during this transition, deliverables will be uploaded to Workflow Manager for review and record-keeping purposes. It is also important to keep naming of the project consistent from project initiation to project closeout. TAG Cycle III Call for Projects (00:52:22) Daniel Wayne presented information for the Technical Assistant to Governments program. This is a competitive program for cities to apply for consultant services for projects such as small area plans, corridor plans, general plan updates, zoning updates, active transportation plans, transit oriented development plan, and station area plans. Over the last two funging cycles about $1.8 million has been awarded in technical assistance to 14 projects. The cycle III call for projects is open. Dan Wayne encouraged the cities to submit ideas of projects and the final section of the program will be in December. The projects that end up being awarded funds, and the ones most successful, are those that reach out early to MAG staff. It is beneficial to talk through the ideas so feedback can be given to make the proposal the most competitive and effective for the city.
