4. Public hearing and possible action regarding a proposed Conditional Use Permit 
for an expansion to the existing Summit County Animal Shelter; approximately 
1775 South Hoytsville Road; NS-131-D-X and NS-134-X;  Brian Bellamy, 
representing Summit County Animal Control, Applicant- Ray Milliner, Principal 
Planner

addition.  He showed the proposed plans that demonstrated where the addition would 
be constructed.  The purpose of the addition is to add kennels, office space, a kitchen, 
and storage areas for the building.  These areas were pointed out on the proposed plan.

exterior dog kennels being proposed will be used during business hours only.  The dogs 
will be put inside at night.   Staff does not believe there will be an increase of impact 
either visually or with the noise.   
 
Planner Milliner said Staff’s review of the application determined it meets the minimum 
criteria for approval.  Staff recommends the Planning Commission approve the 
conditional use permit according to the findings of fact, conclusions of law, and 
conditions of approval as outlined in the Staff Report.

1. Review of Eastern Summit County Zoning Maps – Planning Department Staff  
 
Director Putt distributed a copy of the possible zones they may use in the future.  This 
will give everybody an idea of what the potential of the zone districts could be.  He 
divided the zones into different sections.  The first section was the base zoning.   
 
The base zoning includes the AG-1 and the AG-6 zones which has been discussed at 
length.  Recently, the Planning Commission has considered an AG-20 zone.  This is a 
zone that might work in the right situations.  The other base zones would be the AG-40 
and the AG-80.  He explained that the Commission is proposing that the AG-80 zone 
take place of the AG-100.   
 
The next section has some new and some existing zones.  These zones would allow 
additional zoning opportunities.  A landowner could apply to be rezoned to the 
commercial, light industrial, industrial, and cabin zones.  The new zones are:    
 
1. The rural residential (RR) zone.  The density of this would be one unit per one acre.  
The minimum lot size might be as small as .75 acre.   
2. The residential subdivision (RS) zone would be a single family development with 
little to no agricultural uses.  This could be as small as three-units per acre.   
3. The recreation commercial (RC) zone.  The framework of that zone has been 
created, but not the last level of detail.  They haven’t decided how they would 
measure density.

Director Putt added the Commission is also reworking the subdivision processes.  
Currently, the minor subdivision process requires three lots or less.  They are proposing 
it be increased to five lots, or less.  This process would be an administrative process.   
 
Staff and the Commission are working on a draft for a major subdivision of six lots or 
more.  The major subdivision would go through the traditional review by the Planning

financing.

Chair Ure said he and Director Putt sat in on a County Council meeting last week.  He 
said Park City is trying to convince the County Council to allow hazardous dirt be 
brought to Three Mile Landfill.   
 
Director Putt said there are two separate issues going on.  The landfill was originally 
developed in 1985 through a CUP.  An additional cell is being added.  There is a desire 
by Park City Municipal Corporation to use a portion of that cell to bring in certain 
exempted types of hazardous soils.   
 
Director Putt said the Code contains a definition of a municipal landfill, which is how 
Three Mile Canyon is defined.  The current definition expressly prohibits hazardous 
materials of any kind.  The type of material being discussed is called bevill exempt.  It 
has varying degrees of hazardous materials.   
 
Director Putt said he brought to the Council’s attention that in order to utilize the 
landfill for that purpose, a Code amendment would be necessary.  Should Council want 
to grant this request, they would not be able to move forward as it is written.  The 
Council asked that this be brought to the attention of the Planning Commission as part 
of the Code rewrite.  This will come before the Commission in the future.    
 
Commissioner Vernon said he works in this business.  He said in the past, this soil was 
disposed of at Richardson Flats.  For the most part the materials could be left on site if it 
is handled properly.   Park City Municipal has decided they don’t want it on their side of 
the County.  It upsets him that they have taken it upon themselves to decide it will come 
to the east side of the County.     
 
Commissioner Vernon said that bevill exempt has low concentrates of lead and arsenic 
in it.  The chance of it hurting anybody is small.  His concern is if they allow this to be 
dumped at Three Mile Landfill, in some point in time, if the regulations are changed, 
Three Mile could be determined to be a superfund site.   
 
Chair Ure said at the County Council meeting it was pointed out that if there is any kind 
of leakage into the water system, the County has to maintain the landfill for 30 years 
after it is closed.   
 
Director Putt said it is clearly not the County Council’s direction to the Planning 
Commission to fix the Code so that it could be allowed.  The Council simply wanted to 
make sure the Commission is aware of the issue.  As the Commission works through the 
Code, they need to have this in mind and find a way to address it.
