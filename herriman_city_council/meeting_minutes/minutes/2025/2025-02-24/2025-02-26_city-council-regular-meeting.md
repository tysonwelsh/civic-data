      3. Administrative Reports
           3.1. Legislative Update
      City Attorney Todd Sheeran provided information on several bills:
      SB 337, a land use bill, would create a Beehive Development Agency allowing an override of
      local control for certain project areas. City Attorney Sheeran noted this was a troublesome
      bill for local governments as it would dictate what would happen without allowing cities to
      charge a fees for the impact to the community. City Manager Nathan Cherpeski added that
      there were concerns about cities having to provide services without receiving revenues,
      potentially burdening current taxpayers for future benefits.

      City Attorney Sheeran discussed amendment to HB 355, the gravel bill, stating that while
      amendments made it slightly better, it would still preempt a city if a gravel company owned
      property by May of this year. He indicated the City would likely oppose this bill.

      City Attorney Sheeran mentioned working on substitute language to make HB 444 more
      palatable, particularly regarding provisions requiring contractors to adopt certain privacy
      standards. City Manager Cherpeski brought up the state housing strategic plan, noting
      comments were due by March 19th. He highlighted concerns about additional studies and
      reports cities would need to produce, potentially requiring new staff hires. Mayor Palmer
      suggested the Council Members individually provide feedback on the plan.

      10. Public Hearing
          10.1. Public hearing and consideration of a Resolution amending the fiscal year 2025
                budget – Kyle Maurer, Director of Finance and Administrative Services
      Finance Director Kyle Maurer presented proposed budget amendments for consideration.

      In the General Fund, the Youth Council had raised $210 during the Night of Lights event, and
      the City received $13,650 in insurance reimbursements for damaged City property.
      Additionally, $30,000 was carried over for the economic development study.

      In the Public Safety budget, $4,000 was reallocated from general supplies to address higher
      administrative lodging expenses, and $5,000 was transferred from the emergency manager’s
      wage line item to cover increased Administrative Law Judge expenditures.

      Regarding Capital Projects and Equipment, the city received a development fee in lieu for
      Teton Ranch, along with $183,000 from vehicle sales and $17,000 from damage
      reimbursements. Councilmember Ohrn expressed concerns about the fee in lieu of Teton
      Ranch, questioning whether it was sufficient to cover actual costs. City Engineer Bryce Terry
      clarified that the fee was meant for the future connection to 6400 West. He acknowledged
      that it was challenging to cover costs with collecting a fee in lieu because of the difference
      between the contractor’s expense to build and the City’s potential costs.

      In the Enterprise Funds, it was revealed that the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)
      Grant Funds, which were intended to support water line replacements in Herriman’s historic
      area, could not be utilized for design or engineering costs, which was not anticipated. City
      Manager Cherpeski mentioned that grants from the EPA were often rigid, taking significant
      time to navigate, and only cover specific portions of projects. As a result, the City would have
      to use the general fund to manage the initial costs. Finance Director Maurer highlighted the
      importance of accurately coding the numerous funds involved, mentioning a reconciliation
      of impact fee funds which led to transfers between the water impact fee and water rights
      impact fee accounts to address previous codification errors.

          11.2. Public Works Facility Architectural Concept Design RFP – Justun Edwards,
          Director of Public Works
      City Manager Nathan Cherpeski explained that the City was looking to expand public works
      functions at Butterfield Park instead of the previously planned 5600 West location. The
      $80,000 Request for Proposal was to start outlining affordable concepts within a potential
      $12 million budget. City Manager Cherpeski emphasized that this process would be more
      realistic and constrained than previous attempts.

      Councilmember Shields expressed concern about the scope and potential budget, and wanted
      to ensure the project would be reasonable and materialize. City Manager Cherpeski assured
      that there would be constraints and opportunities for Council input throughout the process.

      Councilmember Shields moved to approve the architectural programming and schematic
      design proposal. Councilmember Ohrn seconded the motion.
