REQUEST
Architect Lyle Beecher, representing Beecher, Walker Architects and the Hale Centre
Theatre owner is requesting that the Sandy City Planning Commission review and approve
the architectural design, building materials and colors for a proposed building
addition to the existing Hale Centre Theatre (HCT), located in the Central Business
District-Office (CBD-O) Zoning District.  The Planning Commission is the land-use authority
for this requested action.  The proposed building addition will be enclosing the open-air
deck space on the second level of the building.  The HCT theater is located at 9900 South
Monroe Street.  On the north side of the Hale Centre Theatre building is an existing second
story open-air deck built above the porta-cochere structure that covers the patron drop-off
at the north entrance of the building.

The proposed 1,500 square foot building addition is to enclose the existing open-air deck into a
room that will be an extension of the existing "Legacy Room," which is used for private meetings
and gatherings.  The existing footprint of the theater building will not change from its present
configuration.  The site plan is not changing for the 2.366 acre Lot 103 in the Amended Sandy

NOTICE
Notices were mailed to property owners within a 500-foot radius of the subject parcel to notify
them of the Planning Commission meeting.  A neighborhood meeting was not held for this project
due to the lack of residential zoning in the area.

ANALYSIS
Architectural Design Review.  The building addition's design, materials and colors were
reviewed by the Sandy Planning Staff as to compliance with the Sandy City Architectural Design
Requirements.  The Sandy City Architectural Design Review Committee met on April 14, 2021 to
review the initial building addition design proposal.  During this meeting, a general concern was
expressed that the proposed addtion looked too "stuck on" to the existing building and that the
design and materials were compatible, but not necessarily enhancing to the existing structure
that has become iconic and is broadly identified as a key building in Sandy's emerging urban
center.  (Please see the attached minutes of the April 14, 2021 architectural design committee
meeting.)

Many questions and ideas for changes were exchanged in that meeting.  The committee finally
motioned to have the architect examine serveral modified optional designs for consideration by
the committee, which was done and these adddional options were then forwarded back to the
committee members.  At this point there were split votes from the committee to both support or
not support the original design proposal to the Planning Commission.  Further discussions
between City staff and the architects and the owner, resulted in the submittal of the final design
option, now before the Planning Commission.  The final design option received unamimous and
engergetic support from the committee for a favorable recommendation to the Planning
Commision for design approval. Planning Staff also recommends that the  Planning Commission
approve  the building architectural design, materials and colors as now proposed by the
applicant.  (Please see the attached transmittal memo and the proposed final design option
illustrations and plans.)

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission find that the proposed building addition's
final architectural design, building materials and colors as proposed be approved for the Hale
Centre Theatre, based upon the following two findings and the following three conditions:

FINDINGS.
       A.  That the proposed project meets or will meet all the applicable requirements of the
           Sandy City Development Code and the Sandy City Architectural Design Standards as
           to building architectural design, materials and colors.
       B.  That proposed building addition will enhance the functionality of the "Legacy Room"
           as needed by the theater and better utilize the open air deck area of the existing
           building.

4:00 p.m.    Hale Centre Theatre Legacy Expansion (9900 South Monroe Street)
             Lyle Beecher and Brad Beecher - Beecher, Walker and Associates
             Mark Dietlein – Co-Founder & CEO of Hale Centre Theatre

Lyle introduced Brad and Mark. He explained the immediate need that Hale
Centre Theatre has for more space in the Founders Room, also called the
Legacy Room which they are proposing to be extended.

Mark explained that the room they currently have has many functions. One of
the purposes of this room is used as a pre-show function for the Founders Club
who are the main donors who support the theatre. This club uses this room for
dinners before each show. Their goal is to grow this club. There are currently 220
club members, and they would like to grow it to 300. These donors help make up
the money for the theatre's yearly deficit. They want to expand this room in
anticipation of more donors that may come in. This room will also function better
for groups that may come for parties prior to the show. There is room now for 100
people to dine in. This expansion is very important for them. He believes this
space can be better utilized with the new expansion which will allow them to
hold more meetings, dinners, and other functions.

Lyle explained their proposal to add an enclosed addition to the existing room
above the porte-cochere on the northside of the building. They are capturing
the existing open air deck space into the interior space, which will double the
space of the existing room. This will help add to the function that Mark needs for
his Founders Club, as well as emphasize this being a cultural lantern to the
community. There will be the same RGB lighting that will coordinate with the
colors that go with the plays being held. The existing black granite will not be
altered in any way. They are adding a roof load and have been able to
translate that through the three columns and load that directly onto the existing
structures below. There will be a presentation wall on the west elevation wall,
and this will also help block some of the view to the parking structure.

Scott commented that he understands the need to improve the space, but it
looks "stuck on" from the exterior, but he likes how the interior flows. He asked if
the roof could be lowered, or the windows extended to the roof line, so that it
would not look so massive.

Lyle said they had a full rendering done in brick so it would tie into everything
else but found that it would be too heavy to be on the legs of the porte-
cochere.

Brad explained they worked with structural engineers, to see how the three
columns on the porte-cochere would work structurally to hold up the addition.
They found that the addition would line up with the existing columns. This
allowed them to keep the porte-cochere as is and this helps with keeping cost
down.

Scott asked if they tried something lighter with more glass.

Lyle explained they wanted to stay true to the materials on the building. So, to
extend the glass would cause them to have to wrap it around the pre-cast
panels. It would also get rid of an important stack of vertical support that frames
the windows.

Daniel asked about extending the glass height and would that change the
structure?

Lyle said the challenge would be the soffits with the interior because they are
matching that from the existing room and pulling that through with the addition.
Also, if the glass is extended too high, it will start to take on a modern look and
take away from its institutional style.

Scott asked if they could use the same granite that is above the Jester's head
(statue) instead of using the Terra Neo color?

Lyle said they had explored that but they did not have enough stock in that so
they would have to order that from China. It would take about a year to get,
and a match count could not be guaranteed.

Steve asked them to explain the switch with the barrel vault ceiling in the
existing room to switching to flat ceiling in the addition. Was this cost driven?

Lyle explained that it was driven by having the hip roof match the tower above
it. If they were to bring the barrel vault ceiling through the whole thing, it would
become very top heavy.

Cyndi asked what the band behind the addition was.

Lyle explained that it was the EFIS and put a collar on the tops of the towers
where they would up light them. This matches the whole band top in the fly loft.
This expansion is part of the step outs and not part of the tops. This addition
would not be lit.

Cyndi wondered if there are any other finishes that could make this look less
"stuck on" and more coordinated.

Doug asked if it would make more sense to have a flat roof like the stair tower
and mirror the subservience to the stair tower.

Brad explained that if you looked at the whole building, they thought this was
the best overall look.

Cyndi asked if other design elements from the existing building could be added
to this new addition as well as adding the recessed feature to it.

Lyle said to do that they might have to minimize the size of the windows and
that it would detract from it. He said he could explore that some more but
would have to be done on a smaller scale.

Scott asked if they could put the squares (window alcoves) spaced out above
the glass like they have in the fly loft.

Doug liked that idea of introducing the recesses.

Lyle said they could see how they lined up with the grids along the whole
building.

Scott said that maybe instead of scoring them as squares, you could score the
Terra Neo as rectangles like it is on the fly loft wall.

Daniel thought the more architectural detail that was added would make this
look more like an afterthought and not less.

Lyle agreed and said that was a challenge. He liked the look about the
cleanliness of the exterior and made sure to engage it with the ground and put
the notches into the stone at the plaza level and at the ground level.

Cyndi said it seems that this detail was a detail that would match.

Lyle worried about seeing the light source, and that they do not like to do it in
the backlit areas.

Cyndi asked if they were lit from above, below, or both.

Lyle said they are lit from above and you do not see the light source because it
is recessed.

Doug was wondering about the light band below the Jester's medallion which
lines up with the break in the glass. He asked what would happen if that band
extended around to separate the small pane and large pane of glass. Would
that help tie it all together.

Brad thought if they were to take the band across, it might make it feel like clear
story windows. They did explore this but decided not to do this because they
could not do it around the whole structure.

Doug asked if they could carry that line across on the glass with a window tint.

Lyle said he thought it would have to be a double mullion which could create
problems on the inside if they were to do that.

Scott asked if they had lighting in the soffit currently.

Brad said no, but they talked about adding soffit lighting there.

Doug asked if there was an LED equivalent of neon that could be along the
bottom of it and would light up instead of down. This way, you would not be
able to see the light source.

Mark said he thought that it should tie into the eaves of the two existing towers,
and to use that as your tie in.

Lyle said they wanted to keep with the smooth nomenclature of the building to
tie things together.

Steve asked about the hip roofs that sit on an element that is recessed back
from the stronger brick area and wondered if there was any way to echo the
underneath of the hip roof that goes around so you could get more of a direct
echo.

Lyle said they have talked about having a collar there but backed away from it.
They wanted the collar to take precedence and the lower to relate and to tie it
to the fly loft.

There was discussion and a consensus decision to have Lyle sketch up changes
and email the sketch to Doug by April 21, 2021. Doug will then email the
sketches out to the ARC Committee for their review and possible motion.
