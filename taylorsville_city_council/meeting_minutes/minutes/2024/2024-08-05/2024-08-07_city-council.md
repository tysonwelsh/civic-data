3.        REPORTS


          3. 1   Historic Preservation Committee— Susan Yadeskie



          IE Committee Chair Susan Yadeskie updated the City Council on various activities that
the Historic Preservation Committee had taken part in, and some they still planned.

They continued to work on organizing digital files and preserving oral histories. They had recently
taken a trip to Rush Valley in Tooele County to visit the Bennion Ranch, which was where the
Bennion, Harker and Sharp families grazed their cattle at one time. John Bennion' s great
granddaughter still owned the property and met with Ms. Yadeskie, Lee Bennion and other
committee members. She described it as an amazing experience that solidified how valuable the
committee' s work was.




Work on the museum grounds continued, including repainting some areas and doing important
repairs. She felt it was important to think about the future while continuing to share the past.
Because the number of schools visiting the museum for tours had declined, the committee was
revamping their school program. Among other things, they were creating new videos which would
describe the old dairy business. The video presentation would be followed by a " show and tell"
of the antique dairy equipment on site.

Upcoming events included the annual Day of Service and the Fall Festival. Ms. Yadeskie noted
that 2025 would mark the 30-year anniversary of Tombstone Tales and they were planning a great
event in conjunction with the Arts Council.

5.       PLANNING MATTERS


         5. 1       Ordinance No. 24-08 Approving a Zoning Text Amendment to Section
                    13. 11. 040 of the Taylorsville Municipal Code, Related to Regulations for
                    Vehicles and Trailers Associated with a Home Occupation— Jim Spung



      Senior Planner Jim Spung explained that the use ofvehicles and trailers associated with home
occupations had become more common in recent years. To support business owners and
entrepreneurs within the City, the City' s administration requested that staff research potential
amendments to the zoning code to allow for added flexibility regarding the parking of business
vehicles and trailers while also ensuring proper safeguards were in place to minimize potentially
negative impacts on surrounding uses.

The proposed amendments were intended to accommodate the parking of additional commercial
vehicles and trailers associated with home occupations in areas where such uses might be deemed
acceptable. The primary changes proposed as part of the Ordinance included:
       Within the R- 1- 20 or R- 1- 40 zoning district
         Primary access via collector or arterial street
         Vehicles and trailers parked behind primary residence and screened from public view
         Parking and maneuvering area limited to 35% of the rear yard area and located at least 50'
         from neighboring dwellings
         Maximum of 5 vehicles and 10 trailers at any given time
         Registered to the business owner or current resident
         Storage of equipment, materials, etc. not allowed
         New modified standard allowing a gravel surface for parking and maneuvering areas for
         residential lots that met the above criteria might be used when:
                o   Parking and maneuvering area had been designed and constructed to support weight
                    of vehicles.

                o   Gravel was clean, compacted, free of weeds, dust, etc.
                o   Minimum 30' paved track- out-pad was installed between the gravel surface and
                    public street

                o   Area was maintained to remain compliant with standards
Staff performed GIS analysis and identified 23 residential properties in the City that satisfied the
location criteria proposed( see Exhibit B). An individual review of each property would be required
during business license review to ensure proposed operations satisfied the standards.

On July 9, 2024, the Planning Commission reviewed the proposed draft and expressed the
following concerns:

         The potential creation of nonconforming conditions if adjacent lots were subdivided (e. g.
         flag lots)
         Compromising residential character and future residential development
         Potential noise, dust, and visual impacts to adjacent neighbors


The Planning Commission discussed increasing the minimum 50' setback proposed between
parking and maneuvering areas and adjacent dwellings as well as limiting the hours larger vehicles
could operate; however, the Planning Commission did not feel comfortable with the merits of the
proposed draft and forwarded a negative recommendation to the City Council. Despite that, staff
recommended approval based on the findings outlined.


Council Member Barbieri asked Mr. Spung why he felt this ordinance was a good idea. He
responded that the proposed ordinance would provide flexibility for entrepreneurs who were
unable to rent commercial space to store such vehicles. He also felt protections were built in so as
not to impact neighbors too much. He suggested the impact would be minimal since only 23
properties in the city qualified and of those, it was unclear how many were even interested in
storing additional vehicles in their back yards.

Council Member Knudsen asked how staff came up with the 5/ 10 ratio of trucks to trailers. Mr.
Spung responded there was no science to the number. He felt the number was flexible if the
Council wished to adjust his recommendation.


Council Member Barbieri asked what city code currently allowed. Mr. Spung indicated a resident
could have one truck and one trailer under certain conditions currently.

Chair Cochran felt that any business with five vehicles and ten trailers was not a" small" business.
He also did not wish to turn residential neighborhoods into commercial zones.

Council Member Burgess agreed with Chair Cochran and said he was not inclined to approve the
proposed ordinance.




Council Member Knudsen expressed concern for the property rights of nearby residents whose
ability to subdivide might be infringed upon if this ordinance were to be approved.

Chair Cochran expressed concern about such large vehicles moving through residential areas,
particularly with school starting soon.


Mr. Spung asked the Council if there was a specific number of vehicles that they would be more
amenable to. Council Member Barbieri indicated she was open to a different proposal from staff
but did not specify a number she would be comfortable with.

         7. 3    Resolution No. 24-19 A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville Forming a
                 Naming Committee and Appointing Naming Committee Members — Tracy
                 Cowdell


 El City Attorney Tracy Cowdell explained that city code outlined certain requirements that must
be met before the naming of a public place. In short, an ad hoc committee must be created to
consider potential names. Mayor Overson wished to name Ben James, John Taylor and herself to
serve as the" at large" committee members. Additionally, she wished to appoint Jen Andrus as the
required staff member and Council Member Knudsen as the City Council representative. The
committee was being formed for the sole purpose of considering the renaming of a public roadway
in   Taylorsville.     After    deciding   on    a    name,    the   committee   would   then present   their
recommendation to the City Council and the decision would belong to the Council.
