 1           A ceremony recognizing the Spring 2026 Provology Graduates (26-007)

Chief Administrative Officer, Scott Henderson, presented and congratulated the Provology Spring 2026
graduates. He stated the group demonstrated an unparalleled passion for Provo and expressed
appreciation for the opportunity to spend the previous 11 weeks with them. He thanked the
department presenters for sharing their enthusiasm and expertise throughout the semester and
recognized Shantel in the Mayor’s Office for her communication efforts and organization of the
program. Mr. Henderson explained that the program included more experience-based learning
opportunities this year, including visits and tours at the airport, wastewater treatment plant, Library,
City Hall, Recreation Center, and the Covey Center. He also noted the class had the opportunity to stand
on the tarmac and watch planes take off and land and experience the raw power of electricity. Mr.
Henderson then read the names of the graduates and jokingly remarked that he had told the class each
week they were the most intelligent and best-looking Provology class ever.

 Action Agenda

 3           A public hearing to solicit input on the 2026-27 Annual Action Plan (26-025)

Melissa McNalley, Community Grants Administrator, presented the public hearing regarding the Annual
Action Plan for the City’s U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) programs,
including Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) and HOME funding. She explained the purpose
of the hearing was to receive input from Provo residents and members of the Utah Valley Home
Consortium regarding the proposed use of funding for the 2026–2027 program year. Ms. McNalley
stated a draft of the Annual Action Plan was available on the Provo City website and could be accessed
through a QR code. She noted comments could be submitted through the website, by email, phone, or
mail, and stated the contact information for submitting comments was included in the plan.

Chair MacKay opened the public hearing. With none, and no council discussion, she moved to the next
item.

 4           A resolution amending the Council Audit Committee Charter and Internal Audit Charter
             (26-030)

       Motion:       An implied motion to approve Resolution 2026-11, as currently constituted, has been
                     made by council rule.

Tanner Taguchi, Council Policy Analyst, presented proposed amendments to the Council Audit Charter,
Council Audit Committee Charter, and Internal Audit Charter, noting the item had previously been
discussed during a work meeting two weeks earlier. He explained the changes were being made by
resolution and would not result in amendments to City Code. Mr. Taguchi stated the proposed revisions
would update terminology currently modeled after private business auditing practices by replacing
references to “external” and “internal” audits with “financial” and “performance” audits to better align
with governmental auditing structures and improve clarity for the public. He explained the term

“performance auditing” had been defined within the charter using an established auditing source to
ensure the City maintained broad authority to review city functions and operations. Mr. Taguchi further
explained the amendments would provide flexibility in the composition of the Audit Committee by
allowing adjustments to the number of public and Council members serving on the committee based on
qualified interest and participation. He stated the changes would also designate the Council Executive
Director as the Chief Audit Executive to serve as a liaison between the Audit Committee and the
legislative audit process. He noted committee members would be required to complete audit training
through the Office of the Legislative Auditor General and described the office as a highly respected
auditing agency that provides valuable guidance for effective auditing practices and recommendations.
Mr. Taguchi stated additional language had been added at the request of Council Members to explicitly
require committee members to comply with the City’s ethics and conflict of interest standards, including
disclosure of potential conflicts or family relationships involving audit subjects. He also reviewed
proposed changes to the Internal Audit Charter, including clarifying the audit planning process, allowing
for either outside contracted audits or limited internal reviews by Council staff, and adding explicit
procedures for responding to fraud hotline complaints submitted through the City’s online portal. He
concluded by stating the requested action was approval of the charter amendments as presented.

Councilor Garrett asked whether the proposed audit training for Audit Committee members was
separate from the training videos Council Members were already required to review through the State
Auditor’s Office.

Mr. Taguchi confirmed the training would be separate and explained it would focus specifically on the
audit function rather than general ethics standards. He added that Council staff would coordinate the
training with the Office of the Legislative Auditor General and provide scheduling information when
available.

Chair MacKay opened the public comment period. With no public comments or Council discussion, she
called for a vote.

       Vote:         The motion passed 6:0 with Councilors Bogdin, Christensen, Garrett, MacKay,
                     Whipple, and Whitlock in favor. Councilor Hoban excused.

 5           A resolution appointing public members to a council standing committee. (26-031)
