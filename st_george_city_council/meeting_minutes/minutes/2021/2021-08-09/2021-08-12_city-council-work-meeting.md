REVIEW THE PROPOSSED TECH RIDGE ZONE PLAN AND DEVELOPMENT
AGREEMENT:
     Community Development Director John Willis presented a PowerPoint presentation
     covering the following topics: Tech Ridge Zone Plan; PD zone plan; and
     Residential/commercial ratio.

      A discussion took place regarding the number of proposed residential units as there
      are concerns because what is being proposed is more than doubled from the original
      agreement. Isaac Barlow with Tech Ridge pointed out that the proposed office space
      is more than the original plan.

      Councilmember Larkin commented that with the additional proposed units and office
      space, there is not as much space at Tech Ridge. She asked what the capacity on
      the hilltop is, roads, parking, etc. Mr. Willis explained the building would go up or
      have smaller units.

      After being asked by Councilmember Hughes, Mr. Barlow explained the plan is to
      build higher.

      Mr. Willis continued with his PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
      Density intensification; Building setbacks; Building height plan and ridge edge
      setbacks; and Planning area 1.1-1.3 building heights section.

      Councilmember Larkin noted that height may not be an issue in this area; however,
      she asked if the ridge could handle that many residential units.

      Mr. Willis explained the applicant will be requesting to encroach on the ridgeline
      setback at a future date in some areas. After being asked by Councilmember
      McArthur, Mr. Willis explained this is the area where the stadium is proposed and
      may need a bit more room.

      Mike Bradshaw with Alliance Consulting explained the need for encroaching on the
      ridgeline setback. With the rim trail there is an opportunity to build some span
      bridges so it expands some of the ridgetop.

      Mr. Willis continued with his PowerPoint presentation covering the following topics:
      Ridgeline building height; Permitted uses; Parks, trails, and open space concept
      plan; Typical linear park connectors section; Vehicular and active transportation
      plan; Parking; Parklet options; Entry monuments, signage, and wayfinding; Signs;
      Stadium plaza; and Deviations from zoning regulations.

      Public Works Director Cameron Cutler explained staff will review the request for
      reducing the driveways and intersections as the construction plans are submitted.
      There is a bit of leeway when deviating from the assessment management policy.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard commented the zone plan that the Council received in
      advance of this meeting is the result of a lot of work. Staff is excited about what is
      being proposed tonight. The concept has support of staff. The main concerns relate
      to traffic, water, and utilities due to the number of units being proposed.

      Councilmember Larkin asked if there is a possibility to stick with original number
      with the idea to see how that plays out first.

      Mr. Barlow explained that they did a traffic study based on the proposed densities;
      therefore, traffic will not be a problem. Looking at the access roads, traffic will
      increase on the north side but the majority of the traffic should shift to the south
      side. He then provided and highlighted portions of the Tech Ridge newsletter from
      August, 2021. With regards to utilities, everything was designed based on what they
      are proposing tonight.

      Clint Reid with Zonos explained they started with about 34 employees, by the end of
      this year, they will be at about 130-140 employees; by the end of 2022 they propose
      to have 220 employees and at 1,000 employees in five years. Their biggest challenge
      is space as their current space is too small for them. It is very important to think
      faster and quicker. They are trying to bring talent to St. George; however, their
      prospective employees, who most of which relocated here, are finding a hard time
      with housing.

      Stephanie Sparks with Zonos noted the feedback from survey shows there are issues
      with the current housing situation.

      Mr. Barlow commented he hopes the newsletter will help to get the information and
      vision out there. He showed a video of the proposed Skyview Building.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard compared this proposal to Joule Plaza and the number
      of units and acreage. This proposal achieves a much more efficient use of the
      footprint.

      Councilmember Larkin noted her biggest concern is having the utility capacity to take
      care of the units.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard commented that water is an everyday conversation.
      He hopes the Washington County Water Conservancy District (WCWCD) will soon be
      able to give countywide numbers showing what the water resources will allow. He
      explained until final plats are recorded and the WCWCD signs them, water is not
      guaranteed.

      Councilmember Hughes stated this argument can be made for all of St. George with
      regard to the number of units, employees, and housing.

      Councilmember Smethurst noted he is concerned with traffic. If everything is built
      off the north end, everyone will exit off the same access. He stated Mr. Barlow will
      need to figure out how to have cars use the other access roads.

      Mr. Barlow commented that which access road cars will use depends on where they
      are headed.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard stated it is reasonable to expect that fewer cars will exit
      off the west side. He asked from the study, at buildout under this scenario, what
      level of service are the major intersections coming down off Tech Ridge.

      Mr. Bradshaw explained currently, the level of service for Tech Ridge Parkway and
      St. George Boulevard is almost an F. When traffic is added and things get worse,
      pushing traffic to a different location. He explained when Exit 5 was built, it
      completely alleviated the concerns with Bluff Street and Black Ridge Drive. People
      will take the easy access; however, if there is heavy traffic, they will use a different
      exit next time.

      Assistant Public Works Director Wes Jenkins stated staff read the traffic study
      approximately two years ago; however, he will review it again soon.

      Mayor Randall commented that staff needs direction from the Council.

      Mr. Bradshaw spoke about Councilmember Larkin’s question as to whether they can
      stick to the original number and see how things go. He explained that is a challenge
      with as they have to plan for density. It is difficult to return in five years to upscale
      the density. Mr. Barlow agreed.

      Mr. Reid stated they are willing do whatever they can over the next couple of years
      as long as they know it’s going to happen.

      Councilmember Larkin commented the density makes sense, but she is concerned.
      She asked if staff is confident the City can provide the necessary infrastructure and if
      the ridge can support this density.

      Councilmember Hughes mentioned he likes the vision, but the same argument can
      be made throughout the City – more housing is needed.

      Mr. Barlow noted one of their thoughts is that you cannot go back to low density. He
      commented that Tech Ridge has no ability to expand.

      Councilmember Larkin asked what will happen if things don’t go as planned.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard explained the zone plan is for entire property. There is
      an advantage to know upfront what is being committed to in the partnership with the
      developer. The best way to go about this is to decide what works, what is
      acceptable, and move forward.

      Community Development Director John Willis noted there are the ratios that will
      control the residential units. He explained if the office or commercial is not built,
      there will not be as many residential units. After being asked by Councilmember
      Smethurst, Mr. Willis stated staff is working on being comfortable with the proposal.

      Councilmember McArthur commented that density is a big concern in the City. He
      wants Tech Ridge to be successful; however, he believes the proposed density is
      extremely high for this area.

      Mr. Barlow stated they want this project done right and they know how important
      this is to the City. Their biggest issue is that you cannot go back from too low
      density.

      Mayor Randall mentioned it is hard when this proposal is compared to Joule Plaza.

      Community Development Director John Willis reiterated this proposal is different
      from Joule Plaza as it is a better utilization of the land. It is hard to compare density
      since the parking is underneath at Tech Ridge, omitting surface parking. Staff can
      analyze the utilities and get a better understanding of the traffic.

      Mr. Bradshaw explained the master plan design and all discussions have been based
      on this density. He believes it is still going to be some work, but the project is still
      viable.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman advised Energy Services Director Laurie Mangum sized
      the utilities based on the information they had. He asked if Mr. Barlow if he would
      be willing to pay the costs to upgrade the facilities that have already been installed,
      if there were any additional cost due to the changes in the plan. He noted that Ms.
      Mangum is confident her department could get power to the site.

      Mr. Barlow replied they would expect to pay the additional costs.

      Mr. Bradshaw noted their master plan with regards to utilities has been based on the
      2,400 units.

      Administrative Services Director Trevor Coombs inquired about churches and grocery
      stores have been considered. Mr. Barlow explained they considered this in their
      mixed-use areas.

      Councilmember McArthur inquired there are any examples of similar communities.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard noted a lot of effort was put into this model. He asked
      if they could create a web based 3-dimensional model. Mr. Bradshaw replied that it
      could be done, but it would not look as good as the video.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard asked the developers to get exact numbers on Dixie
      Tech and the Vaison building, and have the engineers review the traffic study.

      Mr. Barlow noted there are many models outside of St. George to show comparisons.

      City Attorney Shawn Guzman commented this area is contained with three exits
      down the hill.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard explained that staff is trying to gauge the concerns of
      the Council.

      Councilmember McArthur commented about Tech Ridge’s active transportation plan –
      it is a big thing to put stairways going up the hillside in four areas. He would like to
      see more on that as he believes the community will have concerns.

      Community Development Director John Willis noted that staff is working on
      developing those standards.

      Mr. Bradshaw explained they do not have a lot of detail on the proposals yet. It is
      important to capture the idea in a zone package or development plan.

      A brief discussion took place regarding the proposed stairways. The Council is okay
      with them as long as they lead somewhere.

      Assistant City Manager Deanna Brklacich asked about unit prices and attainable
      housing.

      Mr. Barlow noted they are not targeting the average wage of the county; rather,
      they are targeting the average wage of those employed at Tech Ridge.

      Assistant City Manager Marc Mortensen added what is being proposed is not
      attainable housing.

      Economic Vitality and Housing Director Shirlayne Quayle clarified that attainable
      housing is housing for all ages and income levels; however, this is a good definition
      of attainable house for Tech Ridge.

      City Manager Adam Lenhard commented another way to think about this is the
      biggest way to make a difference in the overall pricing of housing in the city is to add
      more units.

      Mr. Reid asked for any advice from the Council. If they don’t think something would
      work, he is open to ideas.
