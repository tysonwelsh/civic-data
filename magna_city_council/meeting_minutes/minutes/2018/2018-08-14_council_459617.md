Reports from Other Municipal Service Providers

Unified Police Department

                Chief Brent Atkinson, Unified Police Department (UPD), stated the Night Out
Against Crime was held August 6th. It was well-attended and a great opportunity to interact with
the community. He expressed sympathy for the tragic loss of Draper Fire Battalion Chief Matt
Burchett, stating out of respect and in solidarity with the Unified Police Authority, UPD officers are
going to wear mourning bands over their badges until the day of the funeral. Also, due to the
tragic death the West Valley code enforcement officer, the UPD Command Staff wants code
enforcement officers to have radios in their cars and will be discussing that tomorrow. Senator
Mayne is also going to try to get legislation to provide vests. Then, with Herriman leaving the
UPD September 30th, and the announcement from Riverton to leave, officers are feeling uncertain.
Two officers have already left the Magna Kearns precinct and accepted offers with Herriman and
others are considering leaving. There is a public meeting tonight at 6:00 p.m. at Riverton High
School on Riverton leaving.

             Council Member Hull asked if Herriman would still use some UPD services, such
as canyon support.

               Chief Atkinson stated the UPD would be open to Herriman contracting with it for
some things, but it has not asked for any of that yet. There will still be mutual aid when a call
goes out for help on both the police and fire sides.

                Council Member Prokopis reported that the UPD board will be meeting
Thursday. It is in the process of trying to strengthen the language in the interlocal agreement that
created the UPD. It is also looking into user-based costs for pooled services, as that was an issue
for both Herriman and Riverton, as well as weighting the votes by population. Right now, it is one
vote per entity.

Independent Audit

               Paul Ashton stated every government entity has to have independent audits
undertaken of all finances, and those have to be filed with the State Auditor. The Salt Lake County
Auditor who is assigned to the metro townships hired an auditor to do the independent audits for
the County as well as the metro townships and the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(GSLMSD) without consulting with them. The audits were done by a well-respected auditor and
they were clean audits, meaning all the money was accounted for, but the description language
on the GLSMSD and metro township audit is inaccurate. It failed to acknowledge the metro
townships and GSLMSD as independent subdivisions of the State. Bart Barker, General
Manager, GSLMSD, got the audit, but would not sign it without the independent auditor fixing the
descriptive units. As a consequence, the audit did not get sent to the State Auditor to meet the
deadline, so the metro townships and GSLMSD have received a 60-day notice. The State Auditor
has been notified of the problem, and a meeting has been set up.

              Council Member Prokopis asked if the State Auditor would recognize what has
already been done for this year.

              Mr. Ashton stated he thought the State Auditor would since it is a clean audit. The
State does not care as much about the description as the GSLMSD and metro townships do.

Resolutions with the Housing Authority of Salt Lake County

              Paul Ashton reviewed the following resolution authorizing the Magna Metro
Township to use the Housing Authority of the County of Salt Lake to deal with housing within the
Magna Metro Township’s boundaries:

RESOLUTION NO. 18-08-01                                                     Date: August 14, 2018

       A RESOLUTION OF THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP COUNCIL DECLARING
       A NEED FOR THE HOUSING AUTHORITY OF THE COUNTY OF SALT LAKE
       (THE “AUTHORITY”) TO EXERCISE ITS POWERS WITHIN THE BOUNDARIES
       OF MAGNA

      WHEREAS, the Housing Authority of the County of Salt Lake (the “Authority”) was created
pursuant to the provisions of Title 35A, Chapter 8, Part 4, Utah Code Annotated (“UCA”) 1953, as
amended (the “Act”); and

                                                4
DATE           TUESDAY                                AUGUST                              14, 2018


       WHEREAS, the Act authorizes the Authority to prepare, carry out, and operate projects
and provide for the acquisition, construction, reconstruction, rehabilitation, improvement,
extension, alteration or repair of any project within its area of operation; and

        WHEREAS, the Act provides that the Authority’s area of operation shall include the
municipality only if a resolution has been adopted by the governing body of the municipality
declaring that there is a need for the Authority to exercise its powers within the boundaries of the
municipality; and

        WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township is a municipality pursuant to UCA §§ 10-2a-401
et seq; and

      WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township Council is the local legislative body for Magna
pursuant to UCA §§ 10-3b-501 et al; and

      WHEREAS, it is to the mutual benefit of Magna and the Authority to enter into an
agreement.

    NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE MAGNA METRO TOWNSHIP
COUNCIL AS FOLLOWS:

       Section 1.      Words used in the foregoing recitals shall have the same meanings when
used in the body of this resolution.

        Section 2.     Magna hereby finds, determines and declares that there is a need for the
Authority to exercise its powers within the boundaries of Magna in order that the Authority may
provide rental assistance, and prepare, carry out, and operate projects and provide for the
acquisition, construction, reconstruction, rehabilitation, improvement, extension, alteration or
repair of any project within the boundaries of Magna.

        Section 3.     This resolution shall become effective immediately upon its adoption.

        APPROVED AND ADOPTED in Magna, Salt Lake County, Utah this 14th day of August,
2018.
