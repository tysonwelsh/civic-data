district” to the end of bullet number one on Objective 1.1. Ms. Garn said that neighborhood
watch does hold neighborhood meetings throughout the City. Councilmember McCay
suggested having a town hall meeting in each council district.

Councilmember McDougal expressed concern about staff being able to continue to provide
more and more activities. In response, the Council agreed to leave bullet number one in
Objective 1.1 as is.

Objective 2.1: Expand, improve, and maintain an active transportation system. The
Council discussed identifying and prioritizing trails and sidewalks to expand the current trail
systems and fill in the gaps in the trails.

Objective 2.2: Properly develop city infrastructure and facilities. Councilmember Buroker
suggested adding a bullet for supporting investments in transportation options, including

Mayor Staggs asked Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, to send the plans for the Edge

Mayor Staggs noted the bullet saying “explore the viability and cost of improving the Welby

Objective 2.3: Enhance the digital infrastructure of the city. Councilmember Buroker
asked to use a different word besides connectivity. No other comments on this theme.

Objective 3.1: Develop a proactive economic development strategy. The City will hit build-
out in about 5-6 years. Mayor Staggs would like to explore the viability and benefits of having a
dedicated economic development strategy. Councilmember McDougal said the City needs to
support the current businesses they have along with new businesses. Councilmember Pierucci
agreed. He said he knows other cities that meet regularly with the top businesses in the city

The Council changed the language about the sales tax revenue increase from “over the next
four years” to “2025.”

Objective 3.2: Attract and recruit business to the community. Councilmember McCay
asked that the bullet include “identify ways to support small businesses.”

Councilmember Pierucci wants to add “retain existing businesses” to this objective.

Objective 3.3: Enhance the Riverton Town Center area. No comments or changes to this
objective.

Objective 4.1: Improve and expand the city’s water resources and systems. Mayor
Staggs said there will be a budget amendment in the next meeting related to this item. The
13400 South Canal Filter Banks Project will cost about $300,000 more than what was budgeted.

Councilmember Buroker suggested adding local scape of 20% to the park strips recognizing
that the goal is to reduce water usage, not maintenance. In response, Councilmember
McDougal said he believes the cost is too expensive to flip the park strips. He suggested future
development is what the City can target.

         Long Range Plan Discussion

Mayor Staggs believes the City is in good shape. He said the two biggest things to consider are

Mr. Hicks showed the sales tax revenue and the long-range plan. He told the Council three new

Public Hearing(s)/Action Items

         Ordinance No. 23-05 – Amending Section 18.235, Water Efficiency Standards,
         Updating and Amending Existing Language, Amendments Proposed by Riverton
         City

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained the Riverton City Council has
previously adopted water efficient landscaping standards for development within the City. The
standards are based on similar standards put forward by the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy
District and others. The standards as currently adopted recommend methods of reducing water
use within landscaped areas, both residential and commercial. This can be accomplished both
by reducing or eliminating more water intensive landscape materials, and also by reducing the
overall landscaped area.

Mr. Lethbridge said this proposed amendment updates the standards for commercial and multi-
family development, and for front and side yard landscaping in new construction for single-
family residential development, from “recommended and encouraged” to required as part of the
site review process for such projects. This is consistent with development practices on recent
projects. The standards are based on both requirements of the Utah State Code and
recommendations from the Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District. The requirements do
include a limitation on the total percentage of landscaping that be lawn (sod) in new residential
construction front and side yards, and a prohibition on sod/turf in park strips in new construction.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember Wells MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 23-05 –
Amending Section 18.235, Water Efficiency Standards, Updating and Amending Existing
Language, Amendments Proposed by Riverton City.                      Councilmember McDougal
