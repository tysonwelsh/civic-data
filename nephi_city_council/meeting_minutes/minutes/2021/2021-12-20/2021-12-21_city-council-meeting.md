ORDINANCE 12-21-2021-ZONE CHANGE FOR GARDNER VILLAGE APPROVED:

    City Administrator Seth Atkinson reviewed with the council that In the summer of 2020, the Gardner
Village subdivision was recommended by the Planning Commission to the city council. Subsequently,
the subdivision was approved by the city council. The zoning that was requested by the developers was
R2-(Z) which allowed for duplexes and triplexes under the zoning code, but also allowed for zero lot lines
with the (Z) overlay. It was anticipated that approximately one half of the units would be rentals. When
the mylar was signed by the council this year, the plan for this development had changed to allow all
units to be purchased.
    When reviewing the building permits for the development, it was discovered that the subdivision did
not strictly meet the city code for either R2 or for the (Z) zero lot line overlay. With the homes being
sold separately as individual lots, a Planned Unit Development (PUD) zone overlay would be the most
appropriate zoning for the subdivision.
     Although approval for the subdivision has already been granted under the current zoning, Mr.
Atkinson stated that a rezoning would be most appropriate to have the subdivision match the zoning
with which it is most closely aligned, which would be the PUD overlay. The ordinance before the
council would rezone the Gardner Village subdivision be with the PUD overlay. The developers would
not be required to meet every aspect of the PUD code since the development was approved under a
different zone. However, the developers do have plans to meet many of these requirements as part of
their original subdivision plans. At the time this development was reviewed, the PUD zone overlay was
unavailable as an option due to a temporary land use ordinance being in place. This restricted any
subdivision applications to be submitted for the PUD zone overlay until the PUD code had been revised.
     Mr. Atkinson said that staff had also taken a critical look at the review process to determine how a
development could meet the current zoning. It would be imperative to gather as much information
about housing type/usage from the developers as early in the review process as possible. To do so, it
may be necessary to change the required information as part of the subdivision submittal process. Once
all information has been received, it will be easier to apply the appropriate zone to the development or
require the development to change to meet the current zoning. The Planning Commission
recommended that the zone change be approved to R2-(PUD) at their December 8th meeting. A public
hearing was also held on the zone change.
    Councilor Memmott moved to adopt Ordinance 12-21-2021 rezoning Gardner Village Subdivision to a
R2-(PUD) zone. Councilor Seely seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

POWELL YARDS SUBDIVISION DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT AND FINAL PLAT APPROVED:

  Mr. Atkinson explained to the council that the Powell Yards subdivision had been renamed and was
originally submitted under Powell Park in the vicinity plan. This is a unique subdivision submittal that has
been received in between the Sun Ridge Ranches and the Phase A & B of the Red Cliffs subdivision. The
previous owner of this property, Dean Powell, was approached about developing while the two phases
of the Red Cliffs subdivision were proposed. Negotiations were ultimately not reached between the
developers of Red Cliffs and the Powell property. This left a narrow parcel of land that was difficult to
develop. Mr. Powell tried various ways to develop the property but could not come to a satisfactory
proposal before his passing.

   Mr. Jordan Jones has now purchased the property and is proposing a subdivision that will enable
adjacent landowners to purchase parts of the property and expand their own lots. The DRC has
recommended the following conditions that would need to be recorded in a development agreement:
     No primary dwellings can be constructed on the auxiliary lots. (excluding Lot 1)
     No auxiliary exterior dwellings can be constructed on the auxiliary lots. (excluding Lot 1)

       The new subdivision lots can only be merged with lots that are contiguous with lots of plat A, &
        plat B of Red Cliff Subdivision, or Sun Ridge Ranches Subdivision.
       Animal rights could be reinstated to lots that have merged and are 0.5 acre minimum in size.
       No utilities shall be installed in the Powell Park property division. Utilities may be brought from
        an existing primary dwelling to the contiguous property.
       The 15' access easement shall be covered with 6" of road base.

 With this agreement the property could be developed in a unique way and only Lot 1 would contain a

REIMBURSEMENT RESOLUTION 12-21-2021 ADOPTED:

   Mr. Atkinson updated that council that the Hive project has been progressing due to the generous
donations received from community members and the donations that the city and school district had
already put in place. He stated that additional grants have been received for the project but if the city
and school district are to capitalize on the construction crews, expertise on site, and some materials
pricing, there is a funding gap that will need to be filled. It is estimated that the project will need
$1,515,000 to complete the facility.
  The City has explored options to meet this funding gap and will be looking at a potential sales tax
bond. This bond would incorporate the original $1.5 million donation from the city as well as the gap
funding. A resolution is before the Council would allow for the sales tax bond, once issued, to reimburse
any expenses the city has incurred to date on the project. The sales tax bond could eventually be paid
for with future sales tax revenue from events held at the Hive, PAR Tax, event revenue at the Hive, and
Parks & Recreation Impact Fees.
   Mr. Atkinson recommended approving Resolution 12-21-2021- Reimbursement Resolution and
assured that council that this does not obligate the city to pass a future sales tax bond resolution but
does allow for reimbursement of expenses from the bond if it is approved.
   Councilor Worwood moved to adopt Resolution 12-21-2021. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion.
The motion passed on a unanimous vote.
