1.      PUBLIC MEETING
        A Petition to the Planning Commission to Forward a Positive Recommendation to the
        South Salt Lake City Council for a Zone Change and an Ordinance Amendment for
        Property Located at 315 West 3300 South.

        ACTION ITEM
        Applicant: Shelter the Homeless

Chair Carter opened the public hearing. He informed those present that Staff will share
information, the applicant will present, and members of the public will be invited to comment on
the application. There will be an opportunity for the applicant to rebut and then the public hearing
will be closed. From there, the Commission will discuss the application, ask questions, and vote.

Ms. Halleck reported that the applicant is Shelter the Homeless and there is a two-part petition in
front of the Planning Commission: an ordinance amendment and a zoning map amendment. Shelter
the Homeless is seeking to develop and operate a 52,795 square foot Family Interim Non-
Congregate Children Housing Facility (“FINCH”) on 1.88 acres, located at 315 West and 3300
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                             4
September 5, 2024


South. Shelter the Homeless owns a number of other low-barrier-to-entry homeless facilities
within four cities. On August 21, 2024, a joint kick-off meeting with the Planning Commission
and City Council was held where the application was introduced. As discussed during the Work
Meeting, there are several entitlement steps associated with this application, which are as follows:

    •   Petition for a Zone Change;
    •   Petition for an Ordinance Amendment;
    •   Application for a Subdivision Plat (Preliminary and Final Plats);
    •   Application for a Conditional Use Permit;
    •   Application for a Building Permit;
    •   Application for a Certificate of Occupancy.

The Planning Commission is the recommending body to the City Council on the petition for a
Zone Change and petition for an Ordinance Amendment. The City Council is the Land Use
Authority and the final decision maker on those items. The two petitions are as follows:

    •   A petition for an ordinance of the South Salt Lake City Council amending South Salt Lake
        City Municipal Code Title 17.01.010 to update definitions, adopting Title 17.03.270 for a
        new Family Interim Non-Congregate Housing Facility ("FINCH") Land Use District and
        amending Title 17.05.040 to include additional regulations for conditional uses with
        specific standards related to homeless shelters;
    •   A petition for a zoning map amendment for the South Salt Lake City Council amending
        South Salt Lake City Municipal Code to amend the South Salt Lake Zoning Map from
        Transit Oriented Development (“TOD”) to Family Interim Non-Congregate Housing
        Facility ("FINCH") Zone for one parcel of land located at 315 West 3300 South.

Shelter the Homeless purchased the property in 2023. Prior to that, it was developed as a motel in
unincorporated Salt Lake County. In 1998, the property was annexed into South Salt Lake City.
State Code allowed it to be annexed with substandard development requirements. It was not a
subdivided parcel and did not comply with infrastructure requirements. Shelter the Homeless
began submitting applications in September 2023. Ms. Halleck stated that there is a lot of due
diligence tied to the entitlement steps. Those components were deemed complete in July 2024.

The current zoning is TOD and there is a mixture of surrounding uses. To the north there is office
and retail, to the south there is warehousing, retail, and single-family residential, to the east there
is office, retail, and a gas station, and to the west is the I-15 off ramp and I-15 corridor. Ms. Halleck
reviewed existing photographs of the area. Entitlement Step #1: Petition for a Zone Change was
further discussed. She reiterated that the current zone is TOD, but it is proposed to change to
FINCH. The zone would only apply to this specific parcel. The purpose of the rezone is to allow
for a FINCH facility. The following definition has been created for a FINCH facility:

    •   A facility that is owned, operated, or contracted for operations by a governmental entity or
        a charitable non-profit organization, which provides free, temporary, non-congregate
        housing to families – which must include at least one minor child or one young adult child
        who is still engaged in the K-12 public school system – who are experiencing homelessness

September 5, 2024


       while the parent(s) or guardian(s) obtain work, job skills, or otherwise take steps to stabilize
       the family’s long-term housing circumstances.

There is no other use proposed in this zone, so it would be limited to this facility. Ms. Halleck
explained that the reason for the rezone is that there is no zone within South Salt Lake City or
within the TOD that allows this type of use to occur. The second part of the petition is Entitlement
Step #2: Petition for an Ordinance Amendment. Modifications are proposed to 17.01 – Definitions.
The new definition for FINCH is listed as well as code cleanup that removes any potential
conflicts. She next discussed 17.03 - Land Use Districts and Land Use Matrix. Since a new district
is being created that does not currently exist, a standard needs to be created. The new zone
development standards for the FINCH District are as follows:

   •   A FINCH Facility is a Conditional Use in the FINCH Land Use District;
   •   A FINCH Facility is the only Primary Use permitted at the property:
          o Limits occupancy to existing structures;
          o 85 maximum bedrooms; Total overnight occupancy of no more than 400
              individuals;
          o Temporary length of stay;
          o Accessory Use: food service, sanitation/laundry, office/programming, fencing,
              parking.
   •   FINCH District Standards:
          o General Layout: minimum area, minimum frontage, minimum depth, maximum
              height, setbacks, etc.
   •   Subdivision Infrastructure:
          o Must comply with 17.06 infrastructure provisions, except minimum sidewalk,
              underground utilities, landscaping, parking demand, and parking stall
              configuration.

As discussed in the Work Meeting, there are existing sidewalks and those will be maintained. Ms.
Halleck discussed the undergrounding of utilities, specifically power lines. Power lines were
identified on 300 West and the current code requires power lines to be undergrounded. The
applicant is petitioning for an exception. Landscaping, parking, and parking stall configurations
are all related to the current site configurations. The current code has higher percentage
requirements than what exists on site. The proposal is to maintain what currently exists.

The next section that will be modified as part of the ordinance amendment is 17.05 – Conditional
Use Review. There will be new conditional use criteria specific to the FINCH, which include:

   •   Conduct all operations within enclosed structures;
   •   Serve only registered families who qualify for residency in a FINCH;
   •   Provide 24-hour on-site management;
   •   Provide licensed private security;
   •   Provide, implement, and maintain a comprehensive management plan;
   •   Work collaboratively for resources and reimbursement for disproportionate costs;
   •   Provide, implement, and maintain a security plan:

Finance Director, Crystal Makin, addressed one of the questions from Mr. Mortensen about
whether the shelter or City has provided documentation to show the true impacts of the shelters
and potential extra costs passed onto property owners. Ms. Makin explained that she cannot speak
to costs passed onto the property owners, but the City is eligible as a Tier 1 City to receive
mitigation funding to help offset the impacts of a homeless shelter in the City. For the current
shelter, approximately $3.2 million was received for the current budget year, and it is used to cover
the cost of 12 additional police officers who are assigned to the Homeless Resource Officer Team
as well as 12 firefighters/paramedics who provide medical services for the shelter as needed. The
calculation is based on several factors and it takes into account the number of beds available.

with mitigation efforts. The funding for the 2024/2025 year has already been allocated to the
qualifying cities, but in May or June of next year, there will be a new calculation and reallocation.
Ms. Makin explained that the funds are collected as an addition to sales tax from cities in the State
that do not provide homeless services, so taxpayers in other cities and counties contribute.

can to help the population and mitigate the impacts to the City. Within that department, funds are
budgeted each year to assist with cleanups, RV towing, and those sorts of matters. All an individual
has to do is report an area that needs to be addressed. This can be done to the Police Department
or Code Enforcement. From there, work can be done to address the issue and prevent it from
happening again. She noted that those resources have been in place for several years.

        Applicant: Shelter the Homeless

Ms. Halleck explained that this is the third step in the entitlement path that has been identified for
the new FINCH facility. This is a Preliminary Plat application. The Planning Commission is the
Land Use Authority on Preliminary Plats and will make the ultimate decision. On the other hand,
the Final Plat is a Staff level review. She noted that the Conditions of Approval will outline what
needs to be completed and identified in order for the Final Plat to be approved by City Staff.

Ms. Halleck noted that a Subdivision Plat application has an extensive checklist of items that must
be reviewed in order to show there is compliance with Title 17.10 of the South Salt Lake City
Municipal Code. The applicant hired third-party professionals and engineers to review the existing
conditions and compare those to the current code requirements. An ALTA Survey was completed.

There are a lot of utilities in the area, specifically on 3300 South and 300 West. The property
complies and meets all utility requirements from all the relevant companies, such as Jordan Valley
Water, Mount Olympus Improvement District, Rocky Mountain Power, and Enbridge Energy. All
of those companies have provided will-serve letters as part of this application. Ms. Halleck pointed
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                          12
September 5, 2024


out the red dashed areas on the ALTA Survey. When the title report was received, there were a
number of title exceptions that were existing. Those do not impact the development and will
continue to remain on the Preliminary Plat. There is a right-of-way dedication shown on the
Preliminary Plat for both 300 West and Archard Avenue. Those are small dedications and have no
impact on the existing structures. 3300 South is a UDOT road so there is no dedication there.

South Salt Lake Engineering has looked at the existing drainage system and paving, in
coordination with the third-party engineer hired by the applicant. There are no proposed changes.
Ms. Halleck reported that there are three vehicular drive approaches to the site that currently exist.

sidewalk, and replace some fencing. That will become the new pedestrian queueing area for the
intake office. That work will require permits from the City and UDOT. A Photometric Study was

not the best. South Salt Lake has applied for and received a Community Development Block Grant.

There are currently above-grade power lines on 300 West. If the City Council grants the exception,

this intensity of use. The utility providers have confirmed that the connections are adequate.
