A.    Self-Funded Health Insurance Discussion
HR Manager Derek Orth began the discussion for self-funded health insurance outlining how it
would be funded how the plan structure would change. Mr. Orth explained that Self-Funded Health
Insurance would save the City costs and would allow them to be rolled over year to year
maintaining a low or possibly no increase to costs in future years.

Stop Loss Insurance would reduce City exposure to high-cost years acting as a safety net. Stop Loss
creates a cap on how much the City would pay for any one individual for the plan year. Staff
recommended the cap be set at $125,000. Stop-Loss Aggregate creates a cap on how much the City
would pay across the plan year. The City would not pay more than 125% of expected worse-case
scenarios.

Responding to Council Member Zach Jacob, Mr. Orth explained that a second insurance provider
would cover anything over the $125,000 for a specific individual and the Aggregate would ensure
the city would not pay more that 125% of worse-case scenarios for the entire year.

Mr. Orth explained that the city was not able to keep the amount of unspent funds for health
insurance. With self-funded insurance the City would be able to build the unspent funds and it
would become quite rewarding. It would make a significant difference to be able to make benefit
increases to insurance plans.

Mr. Orth showed a Plan Cost Comparison and noted that they would be separating from the current
carrier to contract with PEHP. He mentioned presenting to the Employee Benefit’s Committee and
the majority wanted to go with PEHP. With the switch in carriers, the City would be sure to keep
coverage for Jordan Valley Medical Center. Coverage would be similar to Select Health Plans and

 of American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds. She explained ARPA interim rule and final rule concepts
 and explained proposed use of ARPA funding. Ms. Steck emphasized that ARPA funding was one-
 time money with a time limit for use. She expressed confidence that estimated impact fee revenue
 was conservative.

Ms. Steck reviewed interfund loans with the Council, and presented the following revised
infrastructure plan:

        Zone 6 Water Tank
              • ARPA Funds                           $1,694,189
              • State ARPA Funds                     $2,000,000
              • Interfund Loan – CIP Fund            $3,800,000

funds; and payments back to the CIP Fund would provide a reliable cash flow towards
improvements.

   Christopher Thomas explained that he worked for Salt Lake City, and was willing to answer any
   questions about the Community Renewable Energy Agency and program. Council Member Jacob asked
   what HB411 aimed to accomplish, and what the City’s role would be. Mr. Thomas explained that of the
   23 eligible communities, 17 had voted to join the Community Renewable Energy Agency since it was
   enabled in 2019 (including West Jordan). He said the purpose of the program was to give residents and
   businesses a choice of where their electricity came from. The goal of the program, once set up, was to
   match one-for-one all the kilowatt hours of electricity that participating customers used with
   renewable energy going into the grid. Mr. Thomas said the program was currently in the design phase.

   Council Member Bloom asked about the bottom line for residents. Mr. Thomas responded that,
   ultimately, those participating in the program would see a slightly different bill from those who did not
   participate. He explained that cost impact estimates had not yet been done. Mr. Thomas said it was
   unlikely that cost increases for participants would be more than 10%, and far more likely that increases
   would be lower than 10%.

   Chair Whitelock asked why Rocky Mountain Power would not be able to accomplish the same goal on
   their own. Mr. Thomas said affordability was at the top of the list in terms of making sure the program
   would work for residents and businesses. At the end of the process, residents would have the ability to
   make some of their own choices about where their energy came from. He said he believed it would be
   a wise financial move in the long run.

   Council Member McConnehey said he opposed the motion because he did not like the idea of a motion
   allowing one member of the public to speak without allowing everyone that opportunity. He said he did
   not disagree with what was trying to be accomplished but disagreed with the idea of having the cities
   foot the bill. He said he did not think it was appropriate to use local tax dollars for a regional issue.
   Council Member McConnehey said he was disappointed with the state for continuing to push the issue
   onto municipalities instead of dealing with something that should be a state issue.

   Vice Chair Green commented that the Blue Sky program was already in place for those who wanted to
   pay more to participate in renewable energy. He stated West Jordan had already been involved in
   renewable energy for eight years at the Trans-Jordan Landfill but did not get any credit for the fact. He
   expressed opposition to using tax dollars for the Community Renewable Energy program and said he
   would not vote in favor of joining any consortium in which West Jordan did not have its fair share of
   the vote.

   Chair Whitelock said the Council had discussed the issue before, and she did not see enough support to
   place it on a future agenda. Vice Chair Green expressed a desire to table the issue indefinitely, subject
   to reconsideration rules of the Council. Council Member McConnehey pointed out the support of two
   Council Members would be needed to put the issue on a future Council meeting agenda and suggested
   no formal action at that time.
