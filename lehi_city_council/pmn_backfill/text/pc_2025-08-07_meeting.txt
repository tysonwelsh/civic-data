Regular Work Session, 5:30 p.m.

1) Call to Order

Commission Chair Jackson welcomed everyone to the meeting.

2) Presentation of updated Starter Home Attainable Residential (SHAR) ordinance.

Brittney Harris presented the current iteration of SHAR (previously AHOZ, CHOZ).

The major changes to the ordinance since the last time the Planning Commission saw a
presentation include: the ordinance only being permitted in zones with half-acre lots or smaller;
requirements for the lots must be under 6000 square feet and the homes under 1200 square feet;
and deed restrictions for owner occupancy long-term. This current version has been through
multiple rewrites with input from engineering, the Lehi City Council, the Mayor, and others. The
city also would be able to use this version of SHAR as an alternative to ADUs by focusing on
adding density in detached single-family home neighborhoods that provide affordable ownership
instead of rental units.

Commissioner Eyre asked if deed restrictions ever fall off the properties. They do not.

Extra density for these developments is determined by taking the normal maximum allowed units
for a developer, multiplied by 1.5 and rounded up to the nearest whole number. Lot specifics
have also been determined, including side yard setbacks that accommodate for future garages;
unfinished basements; and maximum allowed square footage. The city is aiming to develop
homes that cost less than $400,000.

Commissioner Eyre asked if developers could charge more for finishing a basement. Harris
explained that finishing a basement likely wouldn’t be allowed under the development’s terms.
Commissioner Eyre asked if lot sizes can equal 6000 square feet. Lot sizes must be under 6000
square feet as that is the minimum lot size for an ADU.

Attainable homes in developments will be required to be constructed with similar design and
materials as other homes in the subdivision, to maintain high quality construction. A reduced
public right of way is a possibility that is being explored.

The process of approval for subdivisions will be as follows: developers create a concept plan,
which is gone over by the DRC. After that it goes to the City Council, who can write a
development agreement. The development agreement goes back to DRC, and then before the
Planning Commission, after which it goes back to City Council for final approval.

Commissioner Kunze asked how the concept functions as an ordinance as opposed to the overlay
zone it was previously proposed as. Harris explained that any units allotted by zoning could
follow normal code, and the SHAR requirements only applied to the bonus density granted by
the ordinance. Commissioner Jackson asked if there were any invested rights in the ordinance.
Harris explained that there are not, as the ordinance would be a legislative item, not an
administrative one, as the overlay zone previously was. The Planning Commission discussed
their role in the approval process. Kim Struthers explained that the process in whole is
discretionary at the level of the City Council. The Planning Commission wouldn’t have the
ability to stop an applicant or force it through. Struthers explained that he sees this as a tool that
developers can ask for, and that it must be voluntary. As such, it’s a development agreement with
no invested rights.

Commissioner Eyre asked to see a zoning map of the city so the Commission could see where
the ordinance could be implemented. He also asked how lot size was determined. Harris
explained that engineering suggested testing the ordinance on smaller zoning. Commissioner
Jackson asked for more distinction between this and an overlay zone. Harris explained that an
overlay zone would allow for by-right development, whereas an ordinance is discretionary.

Commissioner Eyre asked how the $400,000 figure was determined. Commissioner Jackson
asked if the figure was adjustable for inflation. Harris explained that the figure can be adjusted
through the City Council, so long as justification was provided. The figure was selected as it is
closer to an ‘attainable’ home than other prices, while still being possible to achieve by
developers. Commissioner Eyre suggested using a percentage of the AMI. Harris explained that
there are many factors that go into that percentage, and as such it’s difficult to determine.
Commissioner Jackson shared that he sees the price as more of a starting point for a conversation
with developers than a hard and fast rule. Kim Struthers explained that impact fees are variable,
but usually around $17,000-$18,000. Commissioner Lockhart asked if the city is offering a
discount on impact fees for the applicable lots. The city will not be offering any discounts.

Commissioner Jackson asked if there are any limits on how large unfinished basements were
allowed to be. Harris explained that enlarging basements would increase the price of the finished
project, so the limiting factor would be the $400,000 price point. Kim Struthers mentioned that

the Planning Commission could suggest capping the basement size at the footprint of the house.
Commissioner Eyre worries about forcing homes to be ramblers if this policy is implemented.

Brittney Harris clarified that the two concern points for the city were home price and utility
capacity.

Commissioner Lockhart asked who would manage the deed restriction. Harris explained that the
city could pursue legal action if renters are reported. Commissioner Lockhart asked if there were
any other owner-occupied deed restrictions. The city has not issued any, but they have been
implemented by HOAs or by individual owners. Commissioner Jackson shared that while he
likes requiring the home to be purchased by an occupant, he doesn’t like the idea of setting up
deed restrictions in perpetuity. Commissioner Lockhart asked if the city had any other tools to
achieve the same means. Harris explained that owner-occupancy could be written into the
ordinance, but that a deed restriction would give the city more teeth to pursue legal action if
needed. Commissioner Jackson shared fears of creating a nanny state by having perpetual deed
restrictions. Harris explained the purpose of the deed restrictions was to maintain the homes as
starter homes for more than one occupant. Commissioner Lockhart shared frustration that this
would minimize the value of the home as an investment. Harris explained that the city’s goal is
to develop homes as shelters rather than as assets. Commissioner Jackson was worried about
legislating for populations down the line.

Kim Struthers compared property rights to a bundle of sticks. Not all properties have all property
rights. A deed restriction is one aspect of property rights, and these homes would be purchased
with the awareness of that deed restriction. Commissioner Jackson shared fears over the current
state of the market, and that potential buyers are not as able to pick and choose properties as they
used to be. Commissioner Eyre shared his agreement with Struthers, and that he likes that the
deed restriction will keep the market moving. He sees purchasing the house with the deed
restriction in the same vein as buying a home for the view—it’s not a right the owner ever
possessed. Commissioner Lockhart feels like the ordinance is a major deviation from the General
Plan. Commissioner Roberts also worries about deviating from the General Plan. Harris
explained that density increases are happening anyway due to ADUs, and that the ordinance will
be better for development, as density will be planned for in utilities design. She also explained
that the General Plan is a snapshot of the city based off a certain point in time, and that changes
to the world can affect it. Commissioner Roberts worries that this is equivalent to buying
property in one zone and attempting to change it to another. Struthers and Harris explained that
there are rights and pathways to request and achieve zone changes.

Harris finished her presentation by explaining that cities are adjusted to a much slower rate of
development than Lehi is currently experiencing. Trying to keep neighborhoods at similar
densities has resulted in massive housing pressure in the city and the construction of density-high
housing in other areas. Commissioner Roberts asked if the ordinance would be an adjustment for
the master plan. Harris explained that while the density is increased, it will be accounted for in
regard to utilities and infrastructure.

Commissioner Jackson asked to end the meeting, as there is a historical marker unveiling.
