Agenda Item 3.1 is a request by Tom Stuart Construction for the Planning Commission to approve the site plan of
Lakeview Park located generally at 1600 South Geneva Road in the M1 Zone.

                                   Staff Presentation: The applicant previously was granted a rezone of this property
                                   in May of 2018. He applied for this site plan approval shortly after the rezone was
                                   granted. He has since been working with the Development Review Committee
                                   toward this site plan approval.

                                  Three new warehouse buildings are proposed. The largest building will be in the
                                  rear along the future Lakeview Parkway at a size of 69,857 feet2. The other two
                                  buildings will be near the front along Geneva Road and will be 54,696 feet squared
and 44,884 feet squared respectively.

Zoning Standards:
All setbacks are shown on the site plan to be met. The minimum south side setback is 20 feet from the property line
that is shared with the residential development. The building fronting Geneva Road will be 26 feet from it, and the
rear building fronting Lakeview Parkway will be 60 feet from it. The maximum height in the M1 zone is 48 feet, the
proposed buildings will be 38, 40, and 40 feet tall.

Architecture:
The proposed buildings will have painted concrete wall exteriors. These exteriors will be accented with aluminum
panel accents and awnings. There will be many docking stations that will all face inward. These doors will all be
aluminum.

Parking:
80 stalls are required for the 20,000 feet squared of office space that will be provided. 217 are required for the
remaining 149,438 feet squared of warehouse space. A total of 297 stalls are required. 297 stalls are provided on the
site. 30 bicycle stalls are required for the site. 30 are being provided.

Dumpster:
The dumpster will be enclosed with the required enclosures. These enclosures will be one foot taller than the height
of the dumpsters.

Landscaping:
Trees are being provided along the Geneva Road frontage and along the frontage of the future Lakeview Parkway.
Frontage trees are typically spaced at a distance of 1 per 40 feet, however, the M1 zone does allow for some flexibility
in the spacing requirement and does allow clustering if desired. In this case, the applicant is spacing the trees along
Geneva Road at 1 per 37 feet He is spacing them along Lakeview Parkway at 1 per 28 feet A total of thirteen (13)
non-frontage trees are required on site. These are being provided along the north and south boundaries of the site. An
eight foot tall screening wall will be provided along the southern boundary of the site.

Transportation/Engineering:
Access along Geneva Road is being provided. In addition, access is being provided on the future Lakeview Parkway
in the rear of the property. The applicant has committed to enter into a storm drain agreement with UDOT. Staff is
recommending that the site plan be approved with the condition that this agreement be completed.

Recommendation: The Development Review Committee determined this request complies with the Orem City Code.
Staff recommends the Planning Commission approve the site plan of Lakeview Park located generally at 1600 South
Geneva Road in the M1 Zone with the condition that a storm drain agreement with UDOT be completed prior to
obtaining a building permit for the project.

Staff Presentation: The City has found a need to clarify the language regarding the parking requirements for
commercial daycare services and private schools located in a residential zone.

Staff is proposing to base the parking requirements for commercial daycares in a residential zone on the maximum
occupancy of the facility. Parking will be required to be provided at a ratio of 0.24 parking stalls per child, plus one
parking stall per full time caregiver/teacher and 0.5 stalls per part time caregiver/teacher. All required parking will
need to be located on-site and no on-street parking may be counted towards the required parking.

In addition, reference to Private Schools, 22-6-9(H) has been added to the SLU Code 6813.

Recommendation: The Development Review Committee determined this request complies with the Orem City Code.
Staff recommends the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City Council to amend Section
22-6-9(G) Commercial Daycare Services, Section 22-6-9(H) Private Schools of the Orem City Code and Appendix
A by amending Standard Land Use Code 6813 Private Primary and Secondary Schools pertaining to commercial
daycare services and private schools in a residential zone.

Chair Cochran asked if the Planning Commission had any questions for Mr. Harding. There was a discussion regarding
commercial daycares approved in the past and requirements for future commercial daycares in residential zones.
     Daycares approved in the past would not be affected by these new requirements.
     On-street parking will be allowed in the future, but it may not count towards meeting their parking
         requirements.
     Mr. Earl said when this new requirement to the ordinance was being considered, on-street parking could be
         counted towards the required parking, that is unusual. That was the only case in the City Code where that
         was allowed. This will level the playing field and put them on the same level as every other use.
     Existing businesses will not be affected by this, just new ones coming in.

commercial daycare services and private schools in a residential zone. Mr. Spencer seconded the motion. Those voting
aye: Mickey Cochran, Carl Cook, Camille Jensen, Barry Roberts, Haysam Sakar, Ross Spencer and Mike Staker. The
motion passed unanimously.

Chair Cochran asked Mr. Harding to introduce Agenda Item 3.4:

Agenda Item 3.4 is a request by the City of Orem, Development Services for the Planning Commission to amend
Article 22-6-9(I) Accessory Apartments of the Orem City Code to clarify language in the accessory apartment
ordinance.

Staff Presentation: The City has found a need to clarify the language in the Accessory Apartments ordinance
pertaining to the definition of an “owner’ and “bedroom” and clarifying interior connectivity between units.

In working with the residents of Orem who have a desire to have an accessory apartment in their home, these
sections of the ordinance needed further clarification. This amendment to the accessory apartment ordinance will
help clarify the ordinance.

Recommendation: The Development Review Committee determined this request complies with the Orem City
Code. Staff recommends the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City Council to
amend Article 22-6-9(I) Accessory Apartments of the Orem City Code to clarify language in the accessory
apartment ordinance.

Chair Cochran asked if the Planning Commission had any questions for Mr. Harding. There was a discussion regarding
number of bedrooms allowed and parking requirements.
     Mr. Sakar wanted clarification on the definition of a bedroom and the number of bedrooms allowed. Mr.
        Bench said an accessory apartment is considered an accessory to the unit. It should not be as large as the unit
        and that is accomplished by limiting the number of bedrooms allowed to two. It will also keep the accessory
        apartment small and the rent less.
     Mr. Earl said accessory apartments only need one additional parking space, so if there are three or more
        bedrooms, there will also be a parking issue. There is not any standard definition of a bedroom.
     Mr. Staker said it would be better to create a higher standard for the parking requirement to make sure there
        is not overflow. Mr. Earl said that is an interesting thought, but accessory apartments are not designed to
        serve every type of tenant. Larger families may need to look at a different location or share a room. Mr.
        Bench said the majority of people doing accessory apartments are having a difficult time adding one stall, it
        would be a limiting factor to add two stalls.
     Ms. Jensen said there are so many residents that have illegal apartments with multiple people renting and it
        can ruin the neighborhood with on-street parking. It is the illegal rentals that don’t follow the rules that are
        the problem.

Chair Cochran then opened the public hearing and invited those who wanted to make a comment on this item to send
