Mr. Crowell reviewed the updated Significant Property Ordinance and stated that disposal of a
significant parcel of real property like this one, which is just under two acres adjacent to Redwood
Road, is something the public has an interest in. Mr. Crowell presented the purchase history and
highlighted the area not yet under City ownership. An Exchange Agreement has been approved but

determine the best way to dispose of property. The Exchange Agreement contemplates appraisals on

deems the property appropriate for surplus and approves the agenda item, the City Manager can
continue with the Exchange Agreement process.

       8.2     Ordinance 2023-19 - An Ordinance Modifying Water Share Dedication
               Requirements (StaffPresenter, Dan Tracer\ Modification of City Code Title 8 —
               Requiring Dedication of Welbv Jacob Canal Shares and Allowing Fee in Lieu of
               Sharing Dedication of Connections to City Pressurized Irrigation System.

Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer reported that the proposed ordinance is to modify water share
dedication requirements. The City has received numerous requests from residents to connect to the
City's pressurized irrigation system. As a result, they would like to make some Code changes to
clarify how people can connect and allow them to better serve those who want pressurized irrigation
service.


Currently, there are two pump stations on the Welby Jacob Canal with no other pump stations on any
other canal. They would like to revise the City Code to only accept canal shares for new customers
from the Welby Jacob Canal where the pumps are located and send the water into the system. Other
water sources are allowed as approved by the City Engineer on a case-by-case basis but they want
shares to come from the Welby Jacob Canal.


Properties located east of the East Jordan Canal have previously been allowed to pay cash in lieu,
which is money that can be used to acquire water shares from another canal. The proposal expands
that to allow any property owner in the City to opt for cash in lieu as long as the City has excess shares
available that are not already attributed to a property. This simplifies the process for a homeowner
who does not know how to find a water share on the open market. An amount is budgeted yearly to
buy up as many shares as possible as they are growing rarer and more expensive, and the City will be
able to allow property owners to pay cash in lieu in exchange for assigning unallocated shares to their
property. The price will be $2,800 per share ifthe Consolidated Fee Schedule Amendment,Item 8.3,
is adopted. That covers the cost to purchase shares as well as paperwork with the State Division of
Water Rights to convert it to municipal use and put it into the City's name.

In response to a question raised by Council Member Kallas, it was noted that the connection would
be separate. The cost had not been finalized.

Council Member Crockett asked if a share would be attached to the property itself and be transferred
to the next owner ifthe current owner sells the property. Mr. Crowell explained that once the property
owner connects, the share runs with the property under the City's name. The City is forbidden by
State law to sell canal shares.


Council Member Kallas asked if there will come a point where people will not be able to procure
water shares and if that would stop them from building. Mr. Tracer clarified that this wouldn't stop
people from building as it only applies to the City's Pressurized Irrigation System, which is separate
from drinking water. The Welby Jacob Canal Company has underlying water rights to Utah Lake
and there is a set amoimt to be allocated. Canal shares are increasing in price as the limit has nearly
been met. After shares run out, people can pay the fee in lieu, irrigate with culinary, or contract for
existing private irrigation services, which would be unaffected. Council Member Crockett pointed
out that offering irrigation services is unique and not available in every municipality.


The new pump station at 15000 South was nearing completion. It filters water twice using Amiad
200-micron filters to remove floatables, trash, and debris. It is pressurized at 50 Pounds per Square
Inch ("PSI") minimum out of the pump station, meaning property owners will not need their own
pumps or filters. The station is finished and in the final testing stage. Council Member Kallas pointed
out that the water improved after the same filters were installed recently at the Jordan Arrows pump
station. Mr.Tracer commended the Public Works Department for the effort they put into flushing the
secondary water lines. This led to a noticeable improvement and much less debris.


Council Member Crockett asked ifthe filters would help prevent E. coli, which has been an issue in
irrigation systems in nearby cities. Mr. Tracer reported that the filters are not intended to prevent
organics or viruses and he recommended against drinking the water.

Council Member Kallas conunented that a map ofthe different segments the system currently services
would be useful and asked that it include redundancies and areas where there is interest in being added
to the system. Mayor Hall noted that Mr. Tracer has been working on that type of map for months.
Mr. Tracer and staff have also been working on updating the Citywide Pressurized Irrigation Master
Plan. They analj^ed the entire existing system and explored what would be required to provide

Finance Manager, Stephanie Thayer presented a proposed ordinance to amend the Fee Schedule for

Staff uses the fee schedule to ensure that the actual costs are being covered for the different types of
add-on services the City provides. This is established in the annual budget but changes needed to be

Ms. Thayer reviewed the proposed changes to the fee schedule. The previous schedule did not
contemplate fees for inspecting fire sprinklers, fire suppression, and fire alarm systems, which is a
cost they would like covered that other cities charge for. Concrete Inspection Permits have been
moved under Encroachment Permits and costs have been adjusted, particularly driveway approaches.
There are differentiated connection fees for culinary and secondary water.

Council Member Kallas asked if there was a full-service option prior to this proposal or if they just
charge the $550. Mr. Tracer clarified that there has not been this type of fee in the Fee Schedule
before but this is for cases where there is a secondary water line in the road with nothing to coimect
it to the property. The connection involves digging up the street to find a main and installing
infi'astructure at an estimated cost of$4,000. In response to a question raised. Public Works Director,
Shane Paddock,stated that when they first expanded 2200 West,they ran into an issue with residents
trying to do the work themselves, which was time-consuming for Public Works and the City Engineer.
