         G. Action Item: Resolution RDA 2020-06, proposed Bacchus Highway Redevelopment
            Project Area, authorizing the preparation of a draft community reinvestment project
            area plan and budget and authorizing and directing all necessary action by agency,
            staff and counsel. (By Director of City Commerce/RDA Manager, Brian A. Preece)

City Commerce Director/RDA Manager Preece reviewed the background information on this
item (Attachment A). He said this is located on the northeast corner of Bacchus Highway and
11800 South. He said the property has been used by the South Valley Reclamation District to
store waste from the Sewer Treatment plant. It is expensive to remediate this property. The cost
becomes an impediment to the property. The landfill is north of this property. He said they want
to take the tax increment from the property tax and reimburse the developer. He said Doug
Young has obtained the purchase rights to this property. He said when you include remediation
costs on top of development costs, it makes the property undevelopable. He said this Resolution
authorizes staff to do studies so they can come back, hold a public hearing about what they are
doing, determine how much money is involved and give details on how it will be developed.
This is not a commitment to create the area. It just allows staff to move forward, do the studies,
and gather information to present to the City Council.

Board Member Zander asked if there is an expense for this? Mr. Preece said the RDA covers that
cost.

Board Member Harris said the alternative would be to let the property sit how it is and not let it
be used. Mr. Preece concurred. He said it is currently owned by a governmental entity. This
would also put it back on the tax rolls.

Board Member McGuire asked about the existing structures on the north end of the map. Mr.
Preece said that is where they do the composting.

Board Member McGuire said soon Daybreak will have homes developing on the border of this
property. It is better to do this project sooner than later so it does not disturb as many residents.

It was noted that Rio Tinto owns the property to the south. The future Bacchus alignment was
also noted.

Board Member McGuire clarified that property taxes only would be used. Mr. Preece said yes.
They will lay out all of the conditions so everything is post performance. There is no bonding
proposed. The developer would upfront the cost, and would be reimbursed over 15 years. Impact
fees, zoning, building permits, etc. are not part of this.

CM Whatcott said the city proposed an amendment to RDA laws that would include this kind of
sludge as blight, so they could redevelop this property someday. They already have 200 acres
that can’t be used because of the landfill. They did not want this parcel to be undevelopable also.
They proposed the modification of the rules so they could help remediate the cost of removing
the sludge so the property can be put into taxable value.

Board Member Marlor said he is excited about the opportunity for the land to be used in a
progressive way, to take it out of the sludge and have it meaningfully developed.

         H. Public Hearing: Zoning Ordinance 2020-06-Z, rezoning property located at the
            Cliffs at Jordan Station, 10448 S. Jordan Gateway from the C-F Zone to the MU-
            TOD Zone. (By Planning Director, Steven Schaefermeyer)

Planner Damir Drozdek, reviewed the background information on this item (Attachment B). He
said the land use map has this designated for mixed use.

Mayor Ramsey opened the public hearing. There were no comments. She closed the public
hearing.

Council Member Harris asked why is the lender making this request? Mr. Drozdek said he
believes they are trying to get the property refinanced. He told the applicant that even if this is
changed to Mixed Use, it will still be non conforming. It was noted that the applicant is not
present. CM Whatcott said he heard that the property has 2 different zones, and the lender likes
for it to be zoned the same.

Council Member Marlor said this would consolidate the same zoning for the entire area. Council
Member Harris asked why does the lender have an interest in that? Council Member Marlor said
if there was ever an issue, in the minds of the bank, they might have a harder time selling it with
different zones. CM Whatcott said they could possibly be looking at this from a value standpoint
as well.

Council Member Zander concurred that it reduces the risk for the lender. She asked if they pass
this, is there any downside for the city? Mr. Drozdek said he does not believe so. If the applicant
wanted to add residential to this property, they would have to come back for a rezone again.

Council Member Marlor asked if this change in zoning would create a change in taxation? Mr.
Drozdek said he does not believe so because it is still the same use.

City Attorney Loose said he does not see a risk in doing this. The biggest risk would be if the
city gave them a use that they didn’t intend to give them. But this property is built out. He does
not see the risk.

Council Member Harris asked if they are creating any unintended precedent? City Attorney
Loose said that is their legislative discretion. There is no precedential value. They do not know
of any other properties that are part CF zone and part another zone.

Council Member Zander made a motion to approve Zoning Ordinance 2020-06-Z. Council
Member McGuire seconded the motion. The vote was 4-0 in favor.

         I. Staff Reports and Calendaring Items

City Attorney Loose said they are reaching out to try to set things up so the City Council can get
some face time with the Legislators before the next legislative session.

Council Member Zander said some residents have approached her, concerned with public safety
in the city. Police Chief Carr said there have been about 150 demonstration events in Utah.
Almost all of them have been peaceful. If South Jordan has another one, it will be the 3rd event in
South Jordan. They take a significant amount of pre planning. In the past events, police have met
with the organizers and discussed the plans in detail. They develop operational orders for those
events to secure a safe and peaceful protest or event. They will have a plan in place when and if
it occurs.

Council Member Zander said when they are questioned by the public, and the public might be
fearful, is it safe to say that the police will be aware and in contact, and have expert plans to
make sure it is peaceful. Police Chief Carr said yes. Council Member Zander asked what if a
peaceful protest starts to turn non peaceful? Chief Carr said they would deem the individual(s)
unlawful and remove them from the area. He reiterated that they have had problems with very
few protests.
