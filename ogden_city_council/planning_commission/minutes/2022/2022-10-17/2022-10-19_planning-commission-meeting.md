1a. Setback reduction in NC Zone, 301 28th Street

    Mr. Simpson explained this is a vacant building and the applicant anticipates opening a restaurant,
    and due to its recent vacancy, the current NC standards are now applicable. The NC zone requires a
    20’ landscaped setback along both street frontages, which would eliminate any on-site parking. He
    stated the lot is relatively small for a commercial business, but currently has a paved area in front of
    the building for customer parking. He indicated the Staff had recommended approval of a reduced
    setback to 2’ on 28th Street which provides three parking spaces and an ADA stall. Due to some
    Engineering concerns relating to maneuvering and access, Staff has now prepared an alternate site
    plan which eliminates access on Grant Avenue and provides a setback of 4’ on 28th Street and 15’ on
    Grant Avenue but reduces parking to two stalls and one ADA stall and changes the angle of parking
    stalls. He stated all on-site utilities will be screened from public view and evergreen shrubs will be
    planted in the remaining setback area to screen parked vehicles. Staff recommends both plans be
    approved and allow the applicant and Staff to determine which will work best. He reviewed the NC
    Ordinance as well as parking standards, dumpster location and noted a vent for the restaurant use will
    be required.
    Commissioner Safsten asked about the property to the east of this site. Mr. Simpson stated while it
    appears as a paved parking lot, it is under separate ownership and not able to be used as part of this
    project without the owner’s permission. He indicated the NC Ordinance is intended to provide
    walkable neighborhood uses and actually allows zero parking spaces.
    Mr. Singh indicated either site plan is acceptable and is willing to satisfy City standards.
    MOTION:      A motion was made by Commissioner Blaisdell to approve the reduced setback, allowing
                 either site plan to be used based on the findings the requirement to install a 20’
                 landscaped setback would eliminate required parking on the site. Motion was seconded
                 by Commissioner Shinoda and passed unanimously with Commissioners Aaberg,
                 Blaisdell, Graf, Safsten. Schade, Shale, Shinoda and Sandau voting aye.

2. Residential Vacation Rental Ordinance clarification

   Mr. Brierley stated the Commission had made a motion to approve some changes to the short-term
 rental ordinance and desired to review those changes to assure the intent of the Commission was what
 is to be forwarded to the City Council. He reviewed both current regulations and proposed changes
 indicating owner-occupied units would be required to show three forms of residency but not be required
 to be on-site at all times during the rental period, but be available or have an agent available to respond
 within 30 minutes of a complaint. Owners would also be required to notify neighboring property owners
 of the vacation rental use and include contact information. The new regulations would also prohibit on-
 street parking of guests, with any citations going to the property owner rather than guests. Discussion
 continued relating to the use of on-street parking which is typically public parking and difficulty in
 enforcement. He noted the restriction for RV parking is eliminated. Commission asked if there is a
 restriction for RV parking on public streets. Mr. Tanner stated these are allowed up to 72 hours.
 Commissioner Shinoda felt this may appear to be unfair to existing small lots which would restrict the
 ability of homes to be used as short-term rentals. Discussion continued relating to both private property
 rights and the need for existing neighborhoods to be respected and maintained. Commission asked
 about how existing licensed uses would be handled. Mr. Brierley responded they would be considered
 nonconforming until their license is to be renewed and they would then need to meet these standards.

 Commission discussed the enforcement ability of these regulations. Mr. Brierley indicated while the
 State Legislature has prohibited cities from regulating these uses based on their advertisement of use, if
 there are complaints, Code Enforcement would be notified and would pursue enforcement of the
 regulations. He also noted the City has signed a contract with a private company to find these uses and
 notify them of the need for licensure for both owner-occupied and non-owner-occupied short term
 rentals, noting a small percentage of those operating are licensed. Commission asked if information is
 available to the public, indicating the best enforcement of these uses is neighborhood education and
 involvement. Mr. Tanner stated short-term rentals cause some wear and tear on the neighborhood
 community and contribute to the housing shortages. It was indicated neighbors in some communities
 are gathering photos on a daily basis to assist in enforcement. Commissioner Blaisdell suggested an
 article be written to include in the monthly water bill newsletter, as well as on the website as to the
 standards for short-term rentals.
 There were no additional comments relating to the proposed ordinance language and Commission felt
 their discussion appears to be satisfied with the language proposed by Staff.

3. Make Ogden Plan Implementation Ordinance
   Mr. Brierley welcomed consultants from Design Workshop attending via Zoom, Jessica Garrow,
   Mariana Stuck and Sam Richards, indicating they have been reviewing the existing code and have
   prepared new zoning classifications to implement the Make Ogden Plan and the 9-Rails Plan for the
   downtown area. He reviewed the history of the Make Ogden Plan, stating it was adopted in 2020
   after an extensive public process involving property owners and stakeholders of the downtown area.
   It is important that this vision be adopted by Ordinance as much new construction is anticipated in the
   downtown area as growth continues, including development of the Wonder Block Project, potential
   new construction along Electric Alley and expanding the Conference Center. The intent is to
   acknowledge Ogden’s history as well as recreational and natural resources in the development of
   underutilized parcels with both public and private investment, to increase land value and walkability in
   the downtown area and increase density. The Make Ogden Plan contains some design standards but
   as it is a plan and not part of the Code, they are difficult to enforce.
   Ms. Garrow reviewed the intent of the Ordinance revisions indicating their goals are to simplify the
   Code, make it easier to apply and understand for both citizens and government while implementing
   the vision of the Make Ogden Plan.
   Ms. Stuck reviewed update elements, including land use, public realm, parking, building height and
   articulation, and setbacks, service areas, public art and the administrative process. She indicated
   their team had reviewed the existing codes to determine how it currently meets with the Make Ogden

Plan and identify what changes are needed. She stated some information had been reviewed by the
Planning Commission in May. One item of discussion had been to limit the amount of residential uses
on the ground floor of buildings so there is adequate commercial and office space yet provide
increased density of residents in the downtown.
Ms. Garrow indicated the first topic of discussion is land use, where a simplified table of uses is
provided, with a focus on commercial and residential by creating new zone classifications including
multi-family vertical and multi-family horizontal, commercial mixed use, commercial-entertainment,
H25 and commercial-9-Rail while identifying areas where there is a minimum density requirement
with no maximum limit. The recommendation is that no ground floor residential be allowed in the 25th
Street Historic District and construction along Washington Boulevard and Wall Avenue be limited to
40% residential.
Commissioner Graf asked if there is a market for new commercial based on recent events and
increased use of internet sales. Mr. Brierley responded it is a challenge but it is felt commercial space
needs to be available and as density increases, the need for services and small shops also increases.
Ms. Garrow stated the commercial component is limited to Washington and Wall as those are the
areas where there is greatest potential for commercial uses, based on perceived reality, stating too
much residential could serve to degrade the downtown area. Mr. Brierley indicated commercial uses
include services such as a day care, gym, office, grocery or retail. Commissioner Graf asked about
public input relating to proposed zoning changes. Ms. Garrow indicated her team held some in-
person meetings in May and has worked with some focus groups, in the development of the
ordinances, and there had been public involvement with the Make Ogden Plan process. Mr. Brierley
indicated for the most part, the proposed revisions will expand the abilities of land use in the
downtown, but would eliminate auto repair and service stations.
Ms. Garrow stated the ordinance language also provides minimum and maximum building heights for
specific areas, with Historic 25th Street being limited to 2-4 stories, the CMU allowing 2-10 stories, as
well as a requirement that upper stories be stepped back from the building façade and articulation
requirements for new buildings. Variations to the specific height regulations in the CMU and C-ENT
could be approved by the Planning Commission. Commission members acknowledged there is
presently limited market for new office or retail space. Commissioner Safsten felt there should be
better incentives to increase residential density in the downtown which would then trigger more
commercial uses. Commission agreed it is important to retain a maximum height in the H25 zone but
asked why there is a height limit in the CMU zone, indicating while there is presently no market for
commercial uses, this may change over time and additional height of structures may be desirable.
Ms. Garrow answered it is important that a predictability be established, stating the character of the
area is important to preserve, and that Ogden has a small-town atmosphere and the mix of uses
currently available creates character which should be retained over time which then attracts people
into the City. Discussion continued relating to the 9-Rails District, outlining the boundaries to coincide
with the existing 9-Rails Master Plan in an effort to create an art-centered creative district.
Commissioner Blaisdell commented the height of buildings should be considerate of the view of the
mountains for those west of the downtown, and that the General Plan identifies the viewshed as being
important.
Commissioner Safsten commented the tax rate in the downtown is higher because there is
inadequate tax base and asked how the tax base can be increased in order to relieve the burden on
existing and potentially new businesses and offices.
Building setback and articulation were then discussed, with there being no minimum setback for
buildings in the downtown area. Both minimum and maximum setbacks for each zone are identified
in both horizontal and vertical situations with the use often determining the setback while also allowing
for access to surface parking. Ground floor entries are required in order to create a better pedestrian
experience, and buildings are limited to a maximum of 200’ in length along the street it faces.
Building transparency and materials are also important for this experience, particularly at the ground
level. Upper level windows are to be punched windows, with vertical design elements to match the
character of existing buildings in the downtown.

   The Make Ogden Plan identifies the public realm in three categories to define the streetscape,
   consisting of a 6’ sidewalk, an amenity area and a transition area between the curb and traffic lanes,
   including a tree canopy. Discussion continued relating to each zone as well as travel and parking
   lanes, with some concern one or more areas may not be able to exist given the existing sidewalk
   widths without reducing the travel lane. Commission was concerned sidewalks need to be walkable
   independent of whether there is sidewalk furnishings or not. Ms. Stuck clarified the existing asphalt
   would not change but the ability to use the sidewalk for public purposes.

   It was noted the City had recently commissioned a downtown parking study prior to their involvement,
   and its recommendations are considered as part of this plan and ordinance. Parking standards are
   revised in that all parking is to be to the side or behind buildings. While each use is not required to
   provide parking, it is anticipated parking is to be created in better locations with public parking
   garages. Commissioner Schade stated there has long been consideration relating to a parking
   agency being established so that Ogden City residents are not burdened with the cost of maintaining
   parking which is available to anyone and felt the users of parking should help pay the cost of
   maintenance by either parking meters or paid parking in structures.
   Screening of utilities and service areas is required and utilities are required to be located on private
   property and not the right-of-way or attached to the front of buildings. While it is acknowledged that
   utility companies have specific standards and requirements for their service, these should be
   addressed prior to construction. It was noted small cell cites are allowed within the public right-of-
   way, and efforts should be made to shield cell equipment or other utilities which may be placed on the
   roof of buildings so they are hidden from view.
   Commission then discussed the review process, stating some standards should be created.
   Discussion centered upon whether items could be administratively reviewed or require Planning
   Commission approval. Commission felt if there are adequate standards in the Code, administrative
   review is preferable, stating they are often frustrated as their abilities are limited if code requirements
   are satisfied. It was noted any decision by either Staff or the Planning Commission can be appealed
   to the Board of Zoning Adjustment and then to the District Court.
   Chair Sandau suggested an ability for a public forum at the Planning Commission level may be
   desirable. Discussion continued relating to whether this is best kept at the City Council level or if
   there would be a benefit to add an item to the agenda allowing public comment.
   Mr. Brierley stated the intent is to assure the elements of the General Plan are complied with, and the
   proposed ordinance language is an attempt to codify some of those items which are in the Make
   Ogden Plan. Ms. Garrow then reviewed some definition changes which are proposed, including
   façade, fenestration, primary and secondary materials, and public realm. Mr. Brierley announced the
   public hearing for the adoption of this Ordinance is scheduled for November 2nd.

There being no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at 7:35pm.
