clothing, games, and food for students in need. Ms. Ivins was praised for her enthusiasm and

        b.     Discussion on Retirement Health Savings Plan.
Mr. Chamness reported that last year the Council adopted an Employee Manual for City staff. At that
time, there was discussion regarding a benefit to assist employees in saving for retirement health care
costs and the long-term costs of accumulated sick leave hours. Staff recommended the addition of
language to the Employee Policy Manual related to this benefit.

All employees would be eligible to participate in the Retirement Health Savings Plan (“RHS”). Upon
implementation, employees with an accrued balance of 180 or more sick leave hours may be paid
50% of any hours above 180 hours at their hourly rate of pay. There was an estimated cost of $12,000
to $20,000 for the first year. Up to seven employees were currently eligible due to sick leave hours
in excess of 180 hours. Ms. Chamness reported that the annual costs would reduce in the future and
would likely be within the $5,000 to $6,000 range.

Council Member Fotheringham wondered what the sick leave policies were in neighboring
jurisdictions. He noted that often there was a ‘use it or lose it’ policy when it came to sick leave.
Ms. Chamness commented that Salt Lake City and Midvale offer a similar benefit to their employees.
Mayor Dahle believed 180 hours was fairly standard. He noted that it was important not to incentivize
accruing sick leave hours. That was the reason for the 50% buy out. Ms. Chamness offered to gather

        d.      Google Fiber Update.
Mr. Blakesley reported that there were ongoing negotiations between the City and Google to lay
Google Fiber within the City boundaries and City right-of-way. Google would be laying the fiber
and contracting with end-users for Internet service. Mr. Blakesley noted that telecommunications
fiber is often laid as a Franchise Agreement. As a License Agreement would avoid some of the
potential conflicts linked to telecommunications laws and franchise agreements. There were a few
points of negotiation:
    • Fee Negotiation;
    • Service Extension and Model; and
    • Terms of Excavation Work.

There had been prior agreements between Google and other municipalities in the State of Utah where
the fee was negotiated to be 5%. Those agreements occurred when Google provided video and
Internet service. Google does not currently offer video service and video would not be licensed in
the agreement. Google offered 2% of its gross revenues from Internet service. Mr. Blakesley noted
that Millcreek recently signed a similar License Agreement. He was attempting to negotiate language
that would permit the City to charge a higher rate, to a maximum of 5%, should there be a specific
statutory authorization that would allow a higher rate than the 2% that was negotiated.

Mr. Blakesley discussed the service level of Google Fiber. He noted that the City was not uniform in
the distances between homes and that would impact the provision of service throughout the City. An
attempt was made to insert a provision into the license agreement to ensure that Google would provide
service on an equitable basis. Mr. Blakesley believed Google would provide service that was
economical to them. The current License Agreement provisions state that Google will not
discriminate on an unlawful basis, but they reserve the right to provide service to areas in their
economic interest.

The final point of negotiation related to laying the fiber. It would need to be done in a way that does
not leave the City streets in worse condition after the fact. Mr. Blakesley reported that the Google
Fiber was laid down through a micro-trenching process. Some communities received complaints
from bicyclists because the micro-trenching process could affect the pavement and, in some instances,
catch the tire of a bicycle. To avoid creating unsafe conditions, negotiations were underway to ensure
that Google would conform to the City’s excavation ordinance.

        e.     Discussion on Storm Water Policy Manual.
Mr. Bunch discussed the Storm Water Policy Manual. He reported that the manual identifies what
the Storm Water Fee was, how it was evaluated, various classifications, the formula behind the fee,
and the adjustments, appeals, and waivers associated with the fee. There are different customers
classes including:

   •   Customer Class 1: Residential Area A (Typical Residential Property);
   •   Customer Class 2: Residential Area B (Estates); and
   •   Customer Class 3: Non-Residential.

Billing services would be provided by Rocky Mountain Power. There was a meeting with Rocky
Mountain Power and they stated that there could not be a decimal Equivalent Residential Unit
(“ERU”). For example, 2.3 or 2.5. Mr. Bunch explained that staff would work with Rocky Mountain
Power to define the ERU. In the Storm Water Policy Manual, one ERU was equal to the average
amount of impervious area found on the average single-family residential parcel of 5,200 square feet.

Council Member Gibbons asked for clarification about the different Customer Class distinctions. He
wanted to know if the owner of a Class 2 property would pay more or less per acre than the owner of
a Class 1 property. It was noted that the owner of a Class 2 property would pay more per household
but less per acre. The study used to determine the ERU was based on imperviousness. Mr. Bunch
explained that a consultant conducted a study of several hundred lots in the Residential Area A class
and sample surveys in the Residential Area B class. The average amount of imperviousness on the
estates was found to be 2.5 times higher. For instance, Residential Area A lots may have 60% to 90%
imperviousness where Residential Area B lots may have 20% to 30%.

Council Member Petersen noted that larger acre lots would have larger homes and driveways. They
could also have swimming pools or tennis courts. A larger lot does not necessarily mean that the
imperviousness difference was substantial. Mr. Bunch explained that sample surveys were conducted
and the calculations were based on those surveys. Council Member Fotheringham felt the study was
