City Attorney, Craig Bott, informed the public of basic rules surrounding public hearings. He
explained that the Planning Commission is not a policy-making or legislative body. They are
an administering body whose task is to apply the tasks to the law that has been established
by the State or the City Council. As such, they are bound by those laws. By law, a
conditional use request must be granted by the Planning Commission unless the Planning
Commission sees that it would lead them to believe that the conditional use requested would
have a negative impact on the community or the surrounding area. If they are unable to find
that the requested use has a negative impact, they may not impose conditions. If they find
that the conditional use request will have a negative impact on the surrounding area or
community, they may impose conditions but at the lowest level necessary to ameliorate the
negative impact.

They must also identify and enumerate how those conditions solve the problem identified.
If the Planning Commission determines that they are going to prohibit a conditional use in
its entirety they can only do so if there are no conditions that can be imposed that would fix
the negative impact that the proposed use would have on the surrounding area or
community. If they make that determination, it must be expressed in writing. If the claim is
made that the proposal will have a negative impact, the person speaking must specify how
that fact was determined rather than simply unsupported opinions.

The applicant, Ryan McDougal was now requesting a conditional use for a private lane. The

Mr. Prestwich provided background on the ordinances regarding private lanes. He explained
that the Planning Commission recently adopted a new ordinance that includes criteria for
evaluating private lanes and standards for how they should appear. The Criteria are A, B,
and C. C must be met, and either A or B must be met. The proposed project does not
impede any planned road on the Master Transportation Plan.

New items in the ordinance include item number 2, which allows for additional right-of-way
width to be added as determined during the Conditional Use Permit approval process.
Number four addresses the maximum number of units allowed based on criteria, which
includes minimum lot sizes and access management to be determined during the
Conditional Use Permit approval process.

Staff also included specific conditions for approval if the project is found to meet the criteria
for a private lane. These include following the City Codes and Riverton City Standards, that
the applicant obtain subdivision approval prior to any construction and that the Subdivision
Plan include provisions for mailboxes and garbage pick-up locations.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and explained that the item is for a conditional use
permit and amended site plan for property located at 3443 West 12600 South. The property
has been a church for many years. A daycare and preschool were operated within the
church as part of the congregation services. However, the church is moving to a new
location and wants to sell the building. Mr. Prestwich reported that a commercial business
now wants to open a preschool in the building. Since the property will potentially be zoned
CPO (Commercial Professional Office), a conditional use permit is required, as daycares
and preschools are conditional in that zone. Additionally, some changes are proposed to
the building itself and as a result, an amended site plan is required.

The applicant is Blooming Minds Preschool. Mr. Prestwich reported that the property is
approximately 0.84 acres, the building is 5,300 square feet and it is currently zoned RR-22.

CPO. In a few weeks, the item will move to the City Council for approval. Normally, staff

worked out so that the item could be presented to the Planning Commission ahead of the

inside the building will change, the site parking lot will continue to exist in its current state.
He explained that the applicant has requested to change the steeple and cross that are part
of the building. The steeple will be removed and stucco will be used on the front of the
building as the building is no longer functioning as a church.

        Subdivisions on private lanes or rights-of-way may be developed in any residential

            o A lot of record which is preexisting and has no frontage or adequate property
