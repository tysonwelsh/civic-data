VII.    PUBLIC HEARINGS
    a. RECEIVE PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING THE TRANSFER OF ENTERPRISE FUNDS
        PER UTAH CODE § 10-6-131
Danyce Steck explained that “…a city may transfer money in an enterprise fund to another fund to pay
for a good, service, project venture, or other purpose that is not directly related to the goods or services
provided by the enterprise for which the enterprise fund was created” (Utah State Code Section 10-6-
135.5). She explained noticing and public hearing requirements associated with the proposed utility
transfer.

Ms. Steck stated the proposed utility transfer was not pandemic-related and explained that General Fund
revenues had not been keeping up with expenses and demand before the pandemic. She summarized
expense reductions for FY 2021 and emphasized that some of the reductions would not be sustainable
beyond FY 2021. Ms. Steck explained that the City considered pros and cons of both a utility transfer
and a property tax increase and showed that a utility transfer would have less financial impact on
residents than a property tax increase with the same General Fund impact. She said it was anticipated
the utility transfer would happen every year and become a stable revenue source for the General Fund.

Ms. Steck reviewed modifications to the Water Rate Plan, and showed revenues, expenses, and reserves
for the Water, Sewer, Solid Waste, Storm Water, and Streetlight Funds.

Chair McConnehey opened a public hearing at 8:15 p.m.

Citizen Comments:
Dave Newton stated he was against a utility transfer. He stated it was a tax and said he did not believe
the Enterprise Funds should be used for taxing revenue. Mr. Newton suggested holding off on a utility
transfer for a year to see how things played out in the economy, with use of more City reserves in FY
2021 to cover the revenue/expense gap.

 Lance Fisher expressed concern that the proposed utility transfer would open the door to the City using
 Enterprise Funds to finance projects outside the original purpose of the funds.

 Jeremiah Tello referred to comments he sent previously to the Council, and said he echoed Mr. Newton’s
 comments.

 Chair McConnehey closed the public hearing at 8:21 p.m. He stated this issue would come before
 the Council again on June 24, 2020. Council Member Jacob thanked the community members who had
 reached out and shared comments with the Council. Council Member Pack encouraged citizens to
 continue contacting the Council.

 Council Member Lamb stated the proposed utility transfer was not something he wanted to do but said
 that the City had already had to reduce its work force and use what he considered to be the maximum
 reserves that should be used for FY 2021. He expressed hope that sales tax revenues would not be as low
 as anticipated, and said he would rather use funds already collected from citizens in the form of a utility
 transfer than implement a property tax increase to get more. He said he would not be in favor of a five-
 year utility transfer plan.

 Council Member Green emphasized that the City budget deficit was not a pandemic-related
 phenomenon. He pointed out the danger of relying entirely on reserves to fill the budget deficit and said
 he would lean toward the proposed utility transfer over a property tax increase because it would have the
 least financial impact on the citizens. He echoed the hope that sales tax revenues would not be as low as
 anticipated, and emphasized that this was a difficult decision for the Council.

 Council Member Whitelock agreed that the decision was difficult. She said that citizens seemed
 overwhelmingly in favor of the utility transfer when she was able to explain the situation to them more
 fully. She said the budget deficit issue was a long-term problem needing a long-term solution and said
 she would like to see the City in a better financial position. She encouraged citizens to continue
 contacting Council Members.

     XI.   WORKSESSION
       a. BUDGET AMENDMENTS FOR FISCAL YEAR 2019-2020
    Danyce Steck presented budget amendments for FY 2019-2020. A public hearing regarding FY 2020
    budget amendments was scheduled for June 24, 2020.

        b. BUDGET DISCUSSION FOR FISCAL YEAR 2020-2021
    Ms. Steck updated the Council regarding Federal Cares Act funding for COVID-19-related expenses and
    recommended creating a Grant Fund in the FY 2021 Budget to track eligible expenses. She explained
    anticipated use of the first $3.4 million in Cares Act funding to be received by the City.

    Ms. Steck reported that outside committees had each submitted FY 2021 budgets with a 10% reduction,
    as requested. Council Member Worthen suggested the Arts Council obtain outside funding or
    sponsorship that the City could potentially match. The Council discussed possible methods for
    distribution of funds to the Arts Council and discussed requesting proof of audit. Council Member
    Whitelock suggested proof of audit should also be required from the Historical Society. Rob Wall
    commented that proof of audit could be required with grant applications each year. Council Member
    Whitelock agreed with Council Member Worthen’s suggestion for the Arts Council to obtain outside
    funding with some matching from the City. Council Member Green asked about fees waived for
    committees and groups.

    A majority of the Council indicated approval for allocating $60,000 in the FY 2021 Budget for the Arts
    Council, with funds only distributed with proof of performance. The FY 2021 Final Budget would be
    presented at the June 24, 2020 Council meeting.
