II.    Holladay @20 Presentation and Recommendation – John Ashton-Chair and John
       Norton-Vice Chair – Holladay @20 Advisory Group.
Mayor Dahle welcomed members of the Citizen Advisory Group and stated that this is an important
matter because it addresses the City’s aging infrastructure when there are revenue challenges.
Following the presentation, a recommendation to the Council was to be made.

Assistant to the City Manager, Holly Smith introduced the group and reviewed the process for
Holladay@20 that included a citizen sounding board to ensure that recommendations were a
reflection of the community. The group also included essential staff and additional resources that
reviewed the City of Holladay’s budget and resources. During the project, there were multiple
communications provided to citizens as well as surveys for the work to be done.

Advisory Group Chair, John Ashton, addressed the Council and recognized Ms. Smith for her
assistance in the project as well as participating members of the Council. Chair Ashton discussed
the various aspects of the City of Holladay and the members of the committee, setting a backdrop
for Holladay@20. He explained that the City of Holladay has more needs than money with one of
the biggest challenges being the budget allocated to law enforcement and public works.
Additionally, revenues have remained fairly flat over the last 15 years.

Vice-Chair, John Norton, discussed instances that have resulted in unplanned expenses for the City.
Approximately 36% of Holladay roads are rated as poor or very poor and at risk of failing, which
presents a safety risk. Repairs and replacement of bridges, roads, and sidewalks comprise about
$4.5 million in budgetary needs. The City’s stormwater system drains and canals also require repair

and replacement. The group recommendation for financial stability in light of the City’s needs and
citizen input was for long-term bonds and incremental adjustments to taxes and fees. The group
studied the tax revenue and stormwater focusing on the following three scenarios:

   1. “Treading water” does not address unmet needs or additional road funding.

   2. Meet some maintenance needs for capital projects and adds more funding for roads.

   3. Keep City services running and meet all needs for capital improvement projects while
      allowing for bonding.   This option lowers road maintenance costs and has the most
      significant impact.

All three options have a Stormwater Fee of $6.50 per month with annual increases to repair or
replace the current system.

Based on the typical home value in the City of Holladay of $500,000 the increase in the total
property tax bill is 2 to 9 percent depending on the three options presented. This was determined to
be better than the initial estimate of approximately 20%.

Chair Ashton reported that the group’s unanimous recommendation was for the option with the
most impact. While a significant increase, it was important to note that the City did not keep up
with inflation increases for taxpayers. The City of Holladay also has not kept up with neighboring
jurisdictions in terms of tax increases and this option will bring the City in line with surrounding
cities.

Mayor Dahle informed those present that the presentation was boiled down and there is a great deal
of additional information that can be provided to those who are interested.

Council Member Durham thanked the group for their work. He asked about a third survey to
engage the community in terms of which course of action to take. He wondered if the group had
thoughts about boosting community input in a third survey. Mr. Ashton reported that they offered
to go to various groups to present information but there was no interest. The group made a good
faith effort to gather input. Mr. Norton explained that the marketing group conducted a statistically
significant survey of the population that was supposed to model the community. The surveys
successfully captured sufficient input.

Council Member Durham asked about survey replies to the three options, specifically between
“slightly better” and “biggest impact.” Mr. Norton reported that the surveys did not focus on the
specific three options as much as on the fiscal community tolerance. Ms. Smith stated that the third
survey was intended to be provided by the Council and not the group. Chair Ashton stated that the
survey addressed different models for increasing revenues.

Council Member Durham questioned why different inflation rates were used. Mr. Norton explained
that the use of a 6% increase in the first two scenarios was necessary to cover upfront costs. The
last option would provide greater revenue so that a larger upfront cost was not as necessary.

Council Member Gibbons thanked the committee for their work. He supported a third survey and

“new normal” is reached with respect to the Coronavirus. He asked about the survey regarding
bonds and taxes. Chair Ashton stated that the group did not spend a lot of time on bonds, but that
people support bonding. He commented that bonding is important because it is one of the most
popular options. It was noted that it is important to keep property taxes and fees as part of the
equation. Council Member Gibbons inquired about the recommended stormwater utility flat
monthly fee and if it should be based on lot size rather than a single fee for all. It was mentioned
that this could be of concern. Mayor Dahle added that proportioning a fee is logistically difficult

Brett Graham commended staff for their work. He reported that the group wrestled with the cost of
the proposal but recognized that the City must move in that direction.

Ms. Chamness reported that the Stormwater Fee provides more flexibility and can be approved at
any time by the Council. It would require an authorizing ordinance and a budget amendment to
recognize the revenue and expenses associated with the fee. A lead time of four to six months
would be needed to determine the billing process with Rocky Mountain Power.

Ms. Chamness added that alternatively, with property tax amendments there are statutorily defined
deadlines that require a rate change be incorporated into the budget the Council adopts in June. The
Council would also need to notify Salt Lake County that the City wants to begin the Truth in
Taxation process. The County would select a date for a hearing in August after which the Council
would either reaffirm the increase or make changes in August to the rate, which would be
incorporated into the property tax notice distributed in August and appear on tax bills in November.

Mayor Dahle reported that a fee is not as time-critical as it is a commitment that the City will move
forward with implementation. It may be referable and require a public hearing. A property tax
increase is time-sensitive and requires a commitment to the process. He suggested the Council
approve the current budget proposal but anticipate an additional revenue increase for infrastructure
and have that be a line item on the agenda. The Council could move forward with the process and
amend it as the Council moves toward a hearing based on public sentiment.

III.  Discussion on 2020-2021 Tentative Budgets.
Mayor Dahle asked the Council for feedback on their initial impressions of Holladay@20.

Council Member Durham was in favor of moving forward with the option with the most impact.

Council Member Quinn agreed and stated that the longer the Council waits, the more difficult it will
be to catch up later.

Council Member Fotheringham agreed that moving forward is necessary particularly because of a
proposed reduction in sales tax moving forward due to COVID-19.

Council Member Petersen reported that the Council never ignored the issue but there were other
issues at the forefront. She supported moving forward now recognizing that there will be
disgruntled taxpayers regardless of the decision. She suggested that messaging be basic and
educational.

Council Member Gibbons was ambivalent about moving forward with a tax increase due to the tight
time frame. He believed the populous was reeling and there is no time for a third survey.

Mayor Dahle asked if the Truth in Taxation hearing date is legislatively imposed. Ms. Chamness
explained that the date is not but the process is. It is designed to ensure that different jurisdictions
have hearings on separate dates, usually the first two weeks of August. She added that a hearing
will need to occur in August. A comment was made that there is more of a concern for the
increased impact on the unemployed as opposed to those on fixed incomes who still receive Cost of
Living Allowances (“COLA”) increases each year.

Ms. Chamness explained that Truth in Taxation hearings can be difficult for Council Members to
accomplish in a short time but it depends on the community. Mayor Dahle suggested that the
Council move forward with the messaging but back off if it does not go well. He was concerned
about delaying the message until next year. Council Member Gibbons suggested starting with the
Stormwater Fee.

Ms. Smith reported that based on research conducted on the City of Holladay’s workforce it was
determined that generally, the City of Holladay is a very educated community with higher-income
jobs and lower levels of unemployment. Just over half of the residents have bachelor’s degrees and
about 65% are in the workforce. The median income is about $83,000 and there is a low poverty
rate. The unemployment rate in the City of Holladay is historically lower than the State average.
Approximately one-third of City residents have an annual income of under $50,000, one-third have
incomes of $50,000 to $100,000, and approximately 40% have incomes of over $100,000.

Unemployment over the last 12 to 13 years tended to be lower than the State average. In March
2007, the State unemployment rate was 2.5% while the City of Holladay was at 1.5%. In March
2011, the State unemployment rate was 7.5% and 6.9% in the City Holladay. In March 2019, the
State and City were both at 3.0%. In February 2020, the State and City were both at 2.8%. In
March of 2020, the State unemployment rate was at 3.8% with Holladay at 3.9%. Ms. Smith
explained that the more vulnerable areas of employment are retail, hospitality, and food service.
Traditionally, stable areas such as healthcare have declined due to restrictions on elective surgeries.
Utah is a leader in terms of utilizing resources available to businesses, which will help with
recovery.

Ms. Chamness continued to gather information about online sales and the Growth Fund Balance and
would provide answers at a future meeting.

Council Member Petersen asked what other cities are doing in terms of COLA increases.
Ms. Chamness reported that most cities are pausing COLA and merit and market increases, similar
to Holladay, with some exceptions.

IV.     Discussion on Plaza Dining.
Ms. Smith asked the Council for policy direction on an issue raised by local dining establishments
in the Plaza. The establishments wanted to know if the Council would approve their ability to
extend outdoor dining into the Plaza public space. If Council approves, staff would work to enter
into agreements with the establishments to set forth the parameters. The Council was also asked to
consider whether outdoor dining establishments can extend into adjacent parking spaces for outdoor
dining.

Council Member Quinn asked about liability concerns. Council Member Durham asked about
density concerns in other portions of the Plaza in the event more public space is utilized. Ms. Smith
explained that the Plaza is fairly large and any issues should be addressed in potential agreements.
Council Member Petersen suggested the agreements include a time limit.

There was discussion regarding easing parking regulations, if needed, to include the ability to
extend dining space into parking spaces on a short-term basis.
