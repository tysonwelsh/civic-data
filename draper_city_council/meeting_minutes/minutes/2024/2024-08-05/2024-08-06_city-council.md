5.e   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1617, an Ordinance of Draper City
      amending the text of the Land Use and Development Code of the
      Draper City Municipal Code relating to the minimum lot size for
      detached accessory dwelling units in a specified portion of the South
      Mountain development
      City Planner Todd Taylor explained the request to amend the text of the Land
      Use and Development Code to allow owners of properties with existing
      detached accessory dwelling units (D-ADUs) in a specified portion of the
      South Mountain development to apply for an ADU permit. The Planning
      Commission reviewed the application and forwarded a positive
      recommendation.

      Responding to a question from Councilmember T. Lowery, Mr. Taylor said he
      believed approximately 40 carriage houses/above-garage apartments were
      built in the South Mountain development.

      Mayor Walker opened a public hearing.

      Ken Murdock, a Draper resident, said he attended the Planning Commission
      meeting and believed the Planning Commission came up with a good
      resolution to protect owners of properties with D-ADUs. Mr. Murdock
      commented that having regulations did not solve all problems, but said the
      proposed resolution was a good start for the owner-occupants who had
      made an investment in their properties and depended on the rent.

      Dave Bennett, a Draper resident, thanked Councilmembers for meeting with
      him over the last two and a half years. He said he owned one property in the
      subject area, which was not owner-occupied. Mr. Bennett said he rented
      both the main house and the garage apartment, and stated he believed he
      would be grandfathered with a legal nonconforming use. He encouraged the

                                                                         5|Page
Council to vote in favor of a modified resolution to allow non-owner-
occupied rental properties for those who had been using properties in that
way. Mr. Bennett spoke of difficulty for emergency response personnel if
called to an unpermitted ADU without an independent address.

Josef Bostik, a Draper resident, said he was in the same situation as the
previous commenter. He explained that renters had needed to move out of
the units and he had been without income from the property for some time.
Mr. Bostik asked the Council for guidance regarding whether he should sell
the property.

Josh Christensen, a Draper resident, thanked the Council for taking on the
issue. He expressed support for the proposed resolution as presented. Mr.
Christensen expressed the opinion that property owners who had been
renting illegal ADUs in the area for years should not be “grandfathered” into
an illegal use, and encouraged the Council to stay firm on the owner-
occupied requirements.

Preston Keller, a Draper resident, thanked the Council for addressing the
issue and expressed support for the proposed resolution. He said he did not
think the resolution went far enough with owner-occupancy, but was a great
start.

Vickie Christensen, a Draper resident, said she lived in the middle of the
subject area and had seen a big change in the last 20 years. She spoke of a
need for enforcement of parking restrictions. Ms. Christensen said there had
been issues with property managers, and a disconnect from owners. She
spoke of a sub-lease situation in which drug dealing occurred. Ms.
Christensen asked that whatever regulations were approved, were enforced.

Abby Shay, a Draper resident, said she had followed the issue since moving
into the area eight years ago. Ms. Shay said she found it disheartening to
attend meetings, hear individuals admit they did not follow established
regulations and had no intention of following them, and see no change in
terms of enforcement. She asked for Council help with enforcement and said
nothing would change without enforcement. She spoke of the need for
owner occupancy and investment in the community. Ms. Shay suggested
enforcement was the key to the bigger-issue problems.


                                                                    6|Page
      Shon Wettstein, a Draper resident, said he owned one of the homes in the
      subject area, which he rented as a single-family home. He said it was possible
      to make a good income with the property as a single unit, and expressed the
      opinion there was no reason to carve the properties up into several units.
      Mr. Wettstein said he believed enforcement was important, and said it was

      Dan Portwood, a Draper resident, said he understood the investor

      Cameron Daw, a Draper resident, said he was okay with the proposed

      Councilmember T. Lowery said it was her understanding and hope that

      Councilmember Roberts seconded the motion.

5.f   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1618, an Ordinance amending the official
      Zoning Map of Draper City for approximately 1.57 acres of property

                                                                          7|Page
from RA1 (Residential Agricultural, 40,000 square foot lot minimum
size) to RA2 (Residential Agricultural, 20,000 square foot lot minimum
size) for the property located at approximately 11511 South 700 West,
otherwise known as the Danduboyina Zoning Map Amendment
City Planner Maryann Pickering said the Land Use Map showed the subject
property as Residential Low-Medium Density. She said the property was
currently vacant. Ms. Pickering said the applicant had indicated a desire to
subdivide the property in the future. The Planning Commission reviewed the
request and forwarded a negative recommendation with a 3-2 vote. Ms.
Pickering said the Planning Commission felt there were not adequate
facilities and services intended to serve the subject property.

Sai Danduboyina, applicant, said he found the property desirable because it
was close to school, work, and the freeway, and fit his lifestyle well. He said
he planned to be there for at least 15 years. He expressed the opinion that
flexibility to subdivide the property into two lots represented a better use of
the land. Mr. Danduboyina said he did not intend to change the property in
a way that would make it stand out from neighboring properties. He said he
was told there were three options available to add sewer utilities for the
property. He said he did not personally have a problem with large animals
being allowed in the area.

Paxton Guyman, attorney for the applicant, asked to reserve his comments
until after the public hearing.

Mayor Walker opened a public hearing.

Jan Sharp, a Draper resident, said she lived two lots from the subject
property. She said the lots were developed so the residents could have large
lots and large animals, and said she was afraid Draper was losing the
agricultural aspect. She said she owned two horses, and her neighbors all
had large animals. Ms. Sharp said she would hate to see the subject property
developed and for the area to lose the purpose for which it was developed.
She expressed the opinion that it seemed the applicant wanted the property
more as a developer than as a member of the community. She said she
wanted the community to stay more rural.

Lane Hughes, a Draper resident, said he lived immediately across the street
from the subject property. He expressed concern that the zone change

                                                                     8|Page
would attach to the property, not to any specific project. He said the zone
change would be out of keeping with the neighborhood and would destroy
the character of the neighborhood.

Jennifer O’Neal, a Draper resident, said her property was directly north of the
subject property. She read aloud a letter on behalf of her neighborhood in
opposition to the proposed zone change.

Martin Orr, a Draper resident, said his property was down the street from
the property in question. He expressed a desire for the one-acre lots to
remain zoned as they were.

Spencer Humphrey, a Draper resident, said pocket zoning seemed opposite
to the intent of the area. He said he knew of newcomers to the neighborhood
who had an issue with the animal-related smells, and said it brought
disharmony to the neighborhood. He expressed concern that the proposed
zone change would result in a development with more people who did not
like the smell. Mr. Humphrey said he did not like the idea of pocket zoning,
and said the application felt sneaky. Mr. Humphrey provided a list of names
of residents opposed to the zone change.

Mayor Walker closed the public hearing.

Mr. Guyman, the attorney for the applicant, said there was nothing sneaky
or underhanded about the application. He said the application was for a zone
change from RA1 to RA2, consistent with the Land Use Map, and already
contemplated by the General Plan. Mr. Guyman said up to three lots would
be allowed on the 1.58 acres. He said the applicant proposed entering into a
development agreement in connection with the rezoning that would restrict
and prevent the concerns expressed by the residents, limiting development
to two single-family lots with no ADUs. Mr. Guyman said the applicant
already had the right under RA1 to build one single-family home and one
ADU, which he believed would have the same impact on the community as
two single-family homes. He said the applicant wanted to build one home for
his family to live in, and one home in the future for his children. Mr. Guyman
suggested the Council continue the matter to a date in the future to allow
time for the applicant to file an application for development agreement
approval.



                                                                     9|Page
Ms. Jastremsky said if the Council wanted to see a development agreement,
she recommended continuing the matter to allow consideration of the
development agreement and zone change together.

Responding to a question from Councilmember F. Lowry, Mr. Guyman said
the applicant desired the zone change and two single-family homes instead
of a single-family home and an ADU so that one unit or the other could be
sold separately in the future.

Mayor Walker said the applicant had followed the established public process,
which was the opposite of sneaky. Councilmember F. Lowry said the area
had been set aside for one-acre lots and large animals, and he wanted to
make sure any project was consistent with existing development.

Councilmember T. Lowery pointed out an ADU would have to be smaller than
the primary dwelling, and would have less of an impact on the area than a
second single-family home. Councilmember T. Lowery said she had concerns
about spot zoning, and said she believed the Council needed to respect the
history of the area. She said she was not in favor of the zone change.

Councilmember Roberts spoke in favor of a development agreement so the
City would have greater control to mitigate concerns. Ms. Pickering said a
flag lot would not be allowed on the subject property, so the property owner
would need to build a 26-foot-wide private road, which would not leave
enough acreage for three lots. A maximum of two lots would be possible.

Councilmember F. Lowry said the impact of the private road on the
neighboring properties would need to be considered.

Councilmember T. Lowery moved to deny                  Ordinance     #1618.
Councilmember F. Lowry seconded the motion.

A roll call vote was taken. The motion passed unanimously.

Council/Manager Reports (continued from Study Session)
  • Councilmember T. Lowery referred to an earlier discussion about fireworks
     and sparklers used at events and said sparklers were recently used at the
     reception center in SunCrest. Fire Chief Clint Smith said crews were able to
     visit with representatives of the venue and alert them that sparklers were not
     allowed. Chief Smith said the fire risk in Draper was still very high because of
     continued dry conditions.
  • Parks and Recreation Director Rhett Ogden asked if the Council wanted to
     change the operating hours of lights at pickleball courts. Councilmember T.
     Lowery said she would prefer not to change the current light schedule.
  • Linda Peterson said a limited number of Draper swag sold during Draper Days.
     She asked if the Council wanted to continue with the sale of Draper
     merchandise on the City website or save the products for community
     giveaways. Two Councilmembers expressed a desire to continue with both
     options.
  • City Attorney Mike Barker spoke of a request from a resident to purchase open
     space from the City, and a suggestion from a resident to enforce
     encroachments throughout the City as a way to raise money. He suggested
     future discussion with the Council. Mayor Walker expressed the opinion that
     the City should not sell open space, but should enforce all encroachments.
     Mayor Walker said selling open space had not gone well in the past.
     Councilmember T. Lowery said she agreed with the Mayor.
  • Fire Chief Smith provided an update on a deployed fire crew. The crew first
     deployed to Oregon and then shifted to California. He said four additional
     individuals would soon deploy to California.
  • Mike Barker provided an update on meetings regarding the remediation of the
     slide area. He reported the City was awarded a $117,000 Energy Efficiency
     Grant, and said staff would bring back ideas for potential grant projects.
  • Mayor Walker spoke of discussions with Wasatch Front Regional Council
     regarding Station Area Plans.

Adjournment
Councilmember T. Lowery moved to adjourn the meeting.

vote (4-0).
