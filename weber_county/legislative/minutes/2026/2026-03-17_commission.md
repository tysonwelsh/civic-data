           2. Presentation on American Correctional Association Accreditation.
              Chief Terance Lavely (Sheriff’s Department): Every 3 years we have to go through an on-site audit to
              maintain our accreditation with the American Correctional Association. That was scheduled for the first
              week in March. Well, back up a little bit back to April when we started working through those contracts
              with ACA. We also decided to go for a PREA compliance as well. [Prison Rape Elimination Act has
              standards set by the federal government of how facilities should operate with the goal of eliminating rape
              and sexual assault inside of jails and prisons]. In the first week in March, ACA sent out two separate
              auditors that were on site going through every nook cranny in the building, opening up behind the laundry
              area, down into the dock area, the maintenance area; just inspecting the in facility top to bottom; plus our
              procedures, policy manual, what our procedures are, and how we've been tracking those standards over
              the last three years since we gained our accreditation the first time in 2023. Out of 46 mandatory
              standards that apply to us, we gained 100% compliance on those. Out of 104 non-mandatory standards,
              we got a 96.2% rating. And the four standards that we missed are something that require a $500 million
              bond to build a new building to increase our cell size and our day room size. So those four standards are
            just things that we have no control over due to budgetary reasons and the how the facility was built 25, 26
            years ago. As part of the PREA compliance, the PREA auditor interviewed 12 random staff members and
            all of our specialty staff members such as our PREA investigators, as well as 30 randomly selected
            inmates. We gave her a housing roster and she picked names off of there who she wanted to interview.
            To be candid, I was a little bit nervous going into those interviews. There were multiple people that have
            been walking into that building, as long as I have, just on two different sides of the uniform. So, I was a
            little nervous what these guys were going to say. At the end the auditors do an exit interview with the
            sheriff and myself and our ACA team. Both auditors said most importantly not only were our staff
            professional and knowledgeable, but she said, "Not only did I interview these 12 staff members and find
            some very human people. They're knowledgeable, professional, they know what they're doing. They care
            about the people they work with." But those 30 inmates she interviewed and we weren't in the room with
            her, she was by herself, so they were free and open to talk candidly with her as much as much as they
            wanted. And she said there were no negative comments from the inmates. They also said the same thing,
            “The staff are knowledgeable. They're professional. They work very hard at treating us like human beings
            and trying to solve our problems with inside the facility while those people are spending time with us.”
            Not only did we pass all the standards and administrator side but we're actually doing it right because our
            staff are being complimented by the people that we deal with day in and day out, which isn't always an
            easy thing to do in the nature of the work that we do. Today’s Consent item number nine will be the last
            step to gain our PREA compliance certificate from the ACA and then in August we'll go to Pittsburgh to
            do our in-person interview to go over all of our standards with the ACA to finish off that part of the
            reaccreditation with the ACA for the facility audit.

            Commissioner Bolos: I heard this report in my last meeting with the Sheriff and Chiefs. I was very
            impressed with the work and leadership Chief Lavely and his staff do. Thank you for sharing.

            Commissioner Harvey: These numbers don’t lie. The results are a by-product of the policies and
            procedures in place.
