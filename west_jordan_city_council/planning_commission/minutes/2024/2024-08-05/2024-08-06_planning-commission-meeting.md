2. Dry Creek Highlands Amended Subarea Development Plan 1; 9000 South 6400 West;
Amend the Development Plan for Subarea 1; P-C Zone; Ivory Development/Ross
Dinsdale (applicant) [#32090; parcel 26-03-251-004]
Ross Dinsdale, applicant, said they are asking for an amendment to Subarea Plan 1 after receiving
comments from staff. Engineering had concerns with public utilities located in private streets in the
previous plan. All single-family homes in the new plan front on public streets, but there are still
some townhomes with private alleyways in the back. Another change is that the original
development agreement required that the swimming pool be built at a trigger of 200 units and the
clubhouse at 400 units with occupancy. They have discovered that the pool and clubhouse should
really be built at the same time, so they are asking that the trigger be delayed for the pool but moved
up for the clubhouse by making it at 300 units for both amenities. He showed a concept of what the
Club Ivory amenities may look like and where they are located.
Mark Forsythe gave an overview of the overall Jones Ranch (Dry Creek Highlands) development
occupying almost 600 acres with 2,960 proposed residential units in a mix of different home and lot
types and sizes. In addition, there will be active and passive open space and trails. The master plan is
the overall high-level plan for the area and the subarea plan provides the details. In addition to the
amendments explained by the applicant, the Barney’s Creek Wash and the crossing of the wash at
Albright Peak Road where it now stubs will be taken out of subarea 1 and will be a part of subarea 2.
He showed the proposed walls and fencing, which meet city code regarding column spacing, end
caps, and design.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation to the City

Council concerning the Amended Dry Creek Highlands Sub-Area 1 Development Plan and the

proposed amendment to the Jones Ranch Master Development Agreement.

Ammon Allen asked for clarification on why and when amenities are required.

Mark Forsythe said the requirement for, and types of, amenities are outlined in the P-C ordinance and

are generally performance-based meaning a development could receive more density by providing

more amenities. In this case, the only trigger was for the pool and clubhouse. Other amenities such as
trails and park areas are subject more to the developer’s timing.

Larry Gardner asked if the Commission would like to have a future presentation regarding how the

amenities for these larger developments were arrived at. He thought it might help them to understand

the background and what has already been approved for when these subareas come to them for review.

George Winn didn’t think he needed to have a special presentation, because that information will be

included with each application as it comes to them.

John Roberts asked if there was a liability issue with changing the trigger point as it relates to any

promises that may have been given to the homeowners who have already received certificate of

occupancy and who are counting on the amenity sooner rather than later.

Duncan Murray said unless the developer made some other contractual relationship with the

homeowners, the Master Development Agreement is between West Jordan and the developer. If both

parties agree to the amendment, then it is legal. He did not see any legal issues with making this
change, and the overall amenity will be constructed sooner than originally agreed to.

Trish Hatch thought it would be helpful for her and any other commissioners who want it, to have a

refresher class to remember the approved details of the larger developments.

George Winn opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

3. Text Amendments — Development Process; Amend the West Jordan City Code Title 14
Subdivision Regulations and Title 15 Permit Processing regarding changes to the
Subdivision Development Review Process to comply with House Bill 476; city-wide
applicability; City of West Jordan (applicant)

Tayler Jensen said the proposed amendments to Titles 14 and 15 are to comply with new state law in

House Bill 476. The Bill says a municipality may not require subdivision improvement plans to be

submitted at both preliminary and final reviews, initial reviews must be completed within 15 business

days, final subdivision review must be complete within 20 business days, and it limits the total number

of reviews a municipality may conduct. The amendment will also modernize the submission process

by removing the requirement for paper plan sets.

Staff recommended the Planning Commission forward a positive recommendation of the proposed

changes to Titles 14 and 15 of the West Jordan Municipal Code to the West Jordan City Council.

Trish Hatch asked what the issue was that caused the legislature to make these changes.

Tayler Jensen said the problems were not necessarily happening in West Jordan, but there were cities

that were not giving full comments, which prolonged the review process.

Duncan Murray said the legislature focused mainly on residential subdivisions that already have

entitlement to build according to the existing standards. The theory is that those types of applications

should be able to move through the process in a few months. The other issue was with the expense

involved with engineering plans, which West Jordan chose to require at the end of the process.

Scott Langford said the new legislation is effective on November 1, 2024. Cities have to accurately

review and provide the required corrections within four reviews. For the Planning Commission, they

may not see as much detail because that will now take place with the final review. However, there will

be a general condition of approval with the preliminary plat that says if the plans cannot be engineered

to city standards as approved at preliminary, then it would likely be remanded back to the commission

after redesign.

Trish Hatch said that this seems counterproductive by putting the onus on the city to get it right and the

developer gets what they want regardless. She understood that cities have the ability to slow things

down when it is required and is the best interest of city planning. She thought that this felt backwards.

John Roberts agreed. He did not think that there was a benefit for a city to slow development other

than wanting to get it nght for the community.

George Winn appreciated the comments but stated that they need to comply with state law.

George Winn opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.
