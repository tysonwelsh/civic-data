4, Discuss UFA Funding Request for 2025 Recreation Areas Contribution letter from
Salt Lake County Mayor

Council Member Huish reported that UFA Chief Burchett had received a letter from the
Salt Lake County Mayor denying UFA's request for the County to fully cover the costs of
providing emergency and medical services to specific unincorporated recreational areas. The
letter indicated that the amount offered was 20% less than the actual cost of servicing those
areas. Paul Ashton, Legal Counsel, clarified that these recreational areas were County-owned
and noted that, under State statute, the County is permitted to use general funds to cover the
costs of law enforcement and fire protection for County recreational areas, as they are used
by residents throughout the County as well as visitors from outside the County.

Paul Ashton, Legal Counsel, explained that the County Council had historically voted to
use general funds for covering costs related to unincorporated recreational canyon areas.
However, ongoing tension existed because SLVLESA and UFSA collected property tax
revenue from these areas. The Council and staff discussed issues related to tax collection,
the status of Kennecott land, the Legislative distinction between the terms “may” and “shall,”
and the potential for collaborating with lobbyists to address the matter.

5. Discuss Communications Policies

Communications Manager Maridene Alexander emphasized the importance of having a
privacy policy in place for the City website and demonstrated the privacy policy approved by
the Office of the State Auditor on the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Service’s website (MSD)
website. She explained that the policy would clarify how collected information is used and
noted that she had included a social media policy on the White City website. Ms. Alexander
also discussed website accessibility, First Amendment rights, and the use of artificial
intelligence (Al). In response to a request from Mayor Flint, she agreed to prepare a reference
outline of dos and don’ts for the City Council.

BUSINESS MEETING

1. Welcome and Determine Quorum

Mayor Flint called the business meeting to order and confirmed that a quorum was still

2. Unified Fire Authority Report

Chief Aldridge provided a recruitment update and reported that an additional ambulance

5.3 Ordinance No. 2024-0-06 amending Chapter 2.56 of the White City
Municipal Code Regarding Community Councils

Council Member Huish pointed out ‘vice chair’ needed to be added to Section 2-
56-060 to reflect four officers. Council Member Huish suggested language should be
changed to reflect that City Council members could serve on more than one
subcommittee. Mr. Ashton pointed out the City now operated on a fiscal year, and the
Community Council still operated on a calendar year. The Council and staff discussed
event funding and budgets. Mr. Ashton suggested changing the due date for the annual
report and budget request from the Community Council to January 31. Mayor Flint
emphasized that the annual insurance payment deadline was May 1.

The following changes were summarized: add a vice chair; change date to
submit annual report and budget request to January 31; and under 080b, change to
“members of the City Council may serve on subcommittees; however, members of the
City Council may not serve on the Community Council.”

Council Member Huish, seconded by Council Member Price, moved to
approve Ordinance No. 2024-0-06 with changes discussed. The motion passed by
unanimous vote.

Brent Potter, White City Community Council Vice Chair, asked for clarification
regarding an annual report, and Mayor Flint clarified that the Community Council would
present a report to the City Council.

Discussion Items

6.1. Discussion and Possible Action Regarding Title 12 of the White City
Municipal Code Regarding Code Enforcement and Community Preservation

Mr. Ashton said staff recommended moving away from existing Code
enforcement and toward relying on the Justice Court and criminal prosecution to
enforce Code. He said proposed amendments to Title 12 would create an administrative
law process. Mayor Flint said proposed amendments should be provided to the Chief for
review, and added that any agency that would be involved in enforcement should be
involved in the drafting process. Mr. Ashton expressed a desire for Title 12 amendments
to be ready for adoption by the February 2025 Council meeting.

Council Member Huish, seconded by Council Member Price, made a motion
to table Title 12 and send it back to administrative staff for input, to be brought
back no later than the February Council meeting. The motion passed by
unanimous vote.
