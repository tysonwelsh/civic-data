e Legislative Update
David and Ashley Spatafore updated Council on legislative bills pertaining to Midvale City.
Ashley Spatafore said it was a difficult session this year with COVID restrictions.
HB82 — ADU bill states it has to be allowed in 75% of the residential zones. Can require
internally within the home; one off street parking, can have separate utilities, can require
a permit, monitor for short term rentals. Bill passed.
HB98 — Applies to one and two family homes. Have 14 days for plan review, permit, have
three days for an inspector. After the three days, homeowner can hire an independent
inspector. Restrictions for external requirements.
HB347 — Homeless service amendments. Homeless Council that includes Mayors that
have homeless shelters. There will be a homeless coordinator for the state that will be
responsible for homeless services. Dollar amounts did not change, and the homeless
coordinator will oversee the funds to cities.
HB401 — Fee amendments. Did not pass. Would have been a requirement for new
system to track fees.

A. DISCUSSION OF CITYWIDE E-SCOOTER PILOT PROGRAM
Nate Rockwood said City staff was approached earlier this year by Bird, an E-Scooter
ride share company, about the potential of entering into a partnership to provide a ride
17

Proceedings of Midvale City Council Meeting

share pilot program to the Midvale Community. Bird’s proposed agreement would give
Bird exclusive right to operate in the City for a term of 24 months with automatic renewal,
unless there is written notice to not renew. In addition, the terms outlined that Bird would
dispatch 50 to 100 electric motor devices with the maximum speed of no more than 20
mph across the city. Customers can activate use of the escooter anytime between 4:00
am and midnight, using an app and the cost for use would be $1 to start and $0.15 per
minute. The agreement does state that Bird would provide the City with a share of $0.05
per ride to help fund bike lanes within the pilot areas.

City staff did conduct some preliminary research on other pilot programs in nearby cities
and found that Sandy City, who is currently working with Lime (another E-Scooter ride
share company) enacted a city-wide E-Scooter ordinance in 2020. This ordinance allows
any E-Scooter provider who complies with the ordinance to operate within Sandy City
limits. According to Sandy, Bird has recently connected with their business licensing
department to take steps to operate in Sandy. Other cities, like Salt Lake City initially
implemented a pilot program with two different E-Scooter companies, for the purpose of
gathering community feedback to create an ordinance based on public comments,
lessons learned, and best practices.

As part of the Council discussion, city staff will present additional information on the
proposed pilot program and recommended E-Scooter ordinance and related issues. City
staff would like to receive feedback from City Council on how they would like to proceed,
whether it be exploring other options, including a city-wide ordinance or a RFP to enter
into a pilot program with potential E-Scooter companies like that of Bird.

STAFF RECOMMENDS:

Based on current research of E-Scooter programs, City staff recommends a city-wide E-
Scooter ordinance which sets parameters for operators.

Background

e E-Scooters have been utilized as additional forms of transportation across the
United States and many cities have seen a rise in their popularity and demand.

e Midvale City was approached by Bird, an electric scooter ride share company,
about potentially entering into a partnership for a piolet program

e Bird provided a proposed MOU and agreement that had a cursory review by the
Attorney’s Office.

e City staff looked at surrounding cities and found that other cities have either
adopted a city-wide e-scooter ordinance or they have entered into a partnership
with an e-Scooter ride share company for the purpose of crafting an ordinance.

e In the state of Utah, e-Scooter ride share providers such as Lim, Bird and Spin
exist in Draper, Sandy, Salt Lake City, Provo, and Ogden.

How it Works

e E-Scooters will be dispatched to various locations across the City

e Customers can unlock and pay for the e-Scooter using a company app

e Customers can only ride anywhere that the company provides services
