      On the large monitors in the room Assistant City Administrator and Chief Financial Officer
Scott Harrington displayed a copy of the notice that had been published in the newspaper as well
as various other places. He explained that the average cost of a home in Taylorsville was
 432,000 and that if the council were to adopt the proposed tax increase, the tax on such a home
would go up by $ 16. 16 per year. He pointed out that Taylorsville businesses did not receive the

45%      residential   exemption     so    a   business   valued   at   the   same   amount ($   432, 000) and their
increase    would      be $ 29. 37   per   year.    Second homes in Taylorsville would be taxed like a
business. Essentially it was an increase of 10. 07% over the previous year' s revenue.


Mr. Harrington then displayed a bar graph showing Taylorsville' s certified tax rate from 2004
through 2021. Although many people assumed that as the value of their home increased, tax
revenue going to the city increased as well. That actually wasn' t the case. Instead, Taylorsville' s
tax rate went down as property values went up, resulting in the same amount of revenue as was
received when   property values were lower ( excluding new growth). In 2013 ( the last time
Taylorsville increased taxes) there had been a 29% increase which was a response to the

growing tax rate from 2009 through 2012 as depicted on the bar graph. The city was then able to
have a 50% tax reduction the following year when it joined Unified Fire Authority and the tax
rate had decreased steadily since then as property values rose. The white line on the graph
illustrated the steady rise of inflation since 2004. As inflation increased, the tax rate went down
which meant the city did not reap increased revenue.

Mr. Harrington then showed a " sample" property tax notice like the ones that Salt Lake County
had sent to each property owner the previous month. He had highlighted the line which showed
that even with a 10%+ increase, the city was below what its tax rate would have been the

previous year because house values had increased.


Councilmember Cochran pointed out for the residents that the figures Mr. Harrington was
referring to on the sample notice were based on the assessment types and market values at the
top of the notice. Mr. Harrington agreed and pointed out that there was a 45%                            residential

exemption, so residents were         typically only taxed on 55% of their house value.

He then went on to provide an explanation about Resolution No. 22- 26. The city' s General Fund
budget for the coming year was $ 26,000, 000. The previous year was approximately$ 30,000, 000.
The figure had gone done this year due to the funds that came from the American Rescue Plan
Act ( ARPA) funds provided by the federal government. However, inflation had resulted in
significant cost increases in many areas. He then summarized the budgets for the various city

          2. 2    Public Hearinif to receive public comment regarding Resolution No. 22- 24, a
                  resolution of the City of Taylorsville approving an increased property tax
                  rate for real property within the City of Taylorsville—Scott Harrington




 a. Chair Barbieri opened the public hearing at 6: 42 p.m.

Orin Randall appreciated that the city was trying to keep the tax rate down. But although he saw
the new Midvalley Performing Arts Center and improvements being made to the sports complex
down the street, he wondered what had been done in his neighborhood. There were streetlights
that had been out for 13 years. He could not enjoy his backyard because of the horrible smell
coming from his neighbor' s yard who fed feral cats. There was a court order in place ordering
him to do certain things and nothing had happened. The Health Department had visited the home
but because the wind happened to be blowing in the other direction, they could not smell the
problem. He did not believe he would ever be able to sell his home unless he sold it during the
winter and did not disclose the problem to the buyer. Mr. Randall also stated that he tried to keep
his yard looking nice and wished his neighbors did the same. He had to spend $ 3000 for a tall
fence on the other side of his home because of the condition of that neighbor' s backyard.

Chair Barbieri appreciated Mr. Randall' s comments and in response to his question about what
the city had done for him, it had invested in a new police department which was absolutely to
Mr. Randall' s benefit. There had also been a great deal of improvement done to the roads in
Taylorsville. And in response to his comment about street lights, she stated that the city had a
plan to replace the old and/ or broken lights with new ones.


Marc McElreath inquired
                 q      about whether Granite School District was fundingthe new school
resource officer.




Mayor Overson responded that the city administration was in the midst of negotiations with the
District on that very thing but felt that for the sake of safety, it was important to have the officer
on site as soon asPossible and not wait until an agreement with the school district had been
reached. She expected them to pay at least part of the new resource officer' s salary.

Linda Kerstein addressed the city council via an American Sign Language interpreter. She
indicated that she had spoken to realtors and compared prices of various homes. She stated it was
very difficult to find housing. She mentioned problems that she had noticed in Taylorsville as
well as other cities   in the valley— weather related problems, sidewalks covered with snow.      She

felt that many people were unable to afford an increase in property tax and felt that many
businesses had closed since Covid. She also indicated that many people were only working part-
time and therefore could not afford the proposed increase. She did not believe it made sense to
increase property taxes. She asked that the council consider approving a 5% increase rather than
10%.


There was no one else who indicated a desire to speak so Chair Barbieri closed the public
comment period at 6: 51 p.m.

Councilmember Cochran asked when a tax increase would take affect if it were adopted.             Mr.
Harrington responded that it would be in November.
