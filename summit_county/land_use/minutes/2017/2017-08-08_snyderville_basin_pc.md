Planner Strader said the Silver Creek Village Development Agreement (DA) was 
approved in 2015.  It consisted of 1,290 residential units and 50,000 square feet of 
commercial.  Of the residential units, 330 are required to be affordable unit equivalents, 
or affordable units.  One affordable unit equivalent is described as a 900 square-foot,  
2-bedroom unit.  The number of market rate units is directly related to the number of 
affordable units.  These are intended to offset the cost of the affordable unites.     
 
Planner Strader said it was determined that 35% (95 units) of the first 271 units built 
will be affordable units.  She said 271 units can be constructed before additional 
mitigation is required.  This number was chosen because it would not require a traffic 
mitigation review.  Once 271 units are constructed, the applicants are required to 
submit a traffic study.  Planner Strader said that Section 5.2.2.16 specifies that 
“affordable units will generally be located throughout the project and integrated within 
the community.”   
      
The approved master plan was shown.  It identified the roads, open parcels, and other 
specifics of the plan.  The applicant is requesting to construct 280 units on 5 parcels.  
Those parcels were outlined in red on the map.  Planner Strader pointed out the 
number of units being proposed for each of the outlined parcels.   
 
Planner Strader said the first phase of the DA contained 118 affordable housing units on 
two adjacent parcels.  Construction of these units would satisfy the 35% affordable 
housing requirement.  Another requirement was that the affordable units should be 
consistent with the character, design, and materials of the surrounding neighborhood.  
The newly proposed language failed to clarify if the market units have to be designed 
similar to the affordable units.   
 
Planner Strader said when the DA was written, the property owners said the affordable 
units would be integrated into the development.  There would be a variety of unit sizes, 
types, designs, and affordability.  Staff is of the opinion that equally important as the 
Snyderville Basin Planning Commission 
August 8, 2017 
Page 7 of 34 
 
number of units is ensuring that the design and function of the units results in a 
desirable living situation. 
 
The master plan was shown again.  The commercial area was pointed out.  Higher 
density is around that area and then the density begins to feather out.  There are single 
family lots along the edge of the village center.  Staff currently has four applications to 
develop parcels in the village center.   
 
Planner Strader said each of the plats should contain a proportionate share of 
affordable units.  She noted that under the current development code, each 
development is required to construct a minimum of 20% affordable housing.  Under the 
DA, it is 35%.   
 
Planner Strader went over the subdivisions and the proposed number of lots, along 
with the number of affordable housing units required.  There will be 347 total lots.  The 
20% requirement would equal 69 units.  The DA requirement of 35% equals 121 units.   
 
Planner Strader said this meeting is scheduled as a public hearing.  She has received 
additional comments since the Staff Report went out.  Those were delivered to each 
Commissioner.  She said the applicant has a presentation.
