3. 4Peaks Athletic Center; 7252 S. Jordan Landing Blvd.; Preliminary Site Plan; P-O Zone;
Uncommon Architects/Nathan Blei (applicant) [#33226; parcel 21-29-151-007]
Nathan Blei, Uncommon Architects, applicant, provided information regarding the proposed 43,500
square foot basketball facility in the P-O zone. The building is single-story at 36’ 9” tall. It is located
on the east side of the property, which will have a retaining wall. Parking is on the west side and will
connect an unfinished access road between the credit union and Hampton Inn to improve circulation.
He stated that the recreational use is a good buffer between the industrial and airport properties to the
west and the commercial uses to the east. The height is consistent with or shorter than many of the
surrounding buildings. Building placement is consistent with neighboring buildings along the road. A
bus stop is on the east side of the property, which also provides good access. Bump outs are provided

along the facade to break up the expanse and to provide more visual interest. Materials have metal
finishes and placement varies in orientation, thickness of the texture, and the colors, which are
burnished slate and ash gray. ADA access connection is provided from anywhere on the site to the

Trish Hatch said in her experience, these types of facilities are always short on parking because
everyone on the team drives separately.

Ammon Allen understood that the required parking calculation is based on six or seven people per
vehicle, which this plan complies with. However, he asked if they anticipate people parking along the
road and how that will be policed.

Nathan Blei said they do not anticipate parking on the side of the road because of fire access. For
certain days of the week the neighboring parking would probably be utilized for overflow before
people would park on the road. However, they do not anticipate that need.

Tayler Jensen said code requires one parking space per six people in the occupancy load. Based on that
formula the proposed 134 parking stalls would allow for 804 people. Total occupancy at full buildout
is 794 people, which is under the requirement. He stated that he had played in hundreds of these
facilities and it is common for the parents to bring the team and not stay, so the parking is under-
utilized. He did not think it will be an issue. Staffs review of the project within the P-O zone standards
included lot and bulk, landscaping, clear vision, etc. and have been found to comply. Final review by
staff will include detailed engineering. The Design Review Committee previously reviewed the project
and made a positive recommendation to the Planning Commission for approval.

Based on the analysis contained in the staff report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission
approve the Preliminary Site Plan for 4Peaks Athletic Center located on approximately 2.89 acres at
7252 South Jordan Landing Boulevard, with the requirements of approval listed in the staff report.
Trish Hatch appreciated that the parking requirement is one space per six persons, but there will be all
sorts of groups that will use the facility, so parking might be an issue at some point.

Ryan Wilkinson, West Jordan resident speaking remotely, asked if the developer was aware of or had
considered the impact of toxic lead emissions coming from the airport, since this will be an athletic
facility and there will be heavy breathing. Expansion of airport will at least double the lead emissions
that come from single-piston aircraft. He said that leaded fuel has been banned world-wide for at least
the last 50 years, and he did not know of any pushback from West Jordan with this situation.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.

4. Discussion - Senior Housing Overlay Zone; Discussion regarding current, pending, and

potential future use of the SHO (Senior Housing Overlay) Zoning District

George Winn said that this topic was thoroughly discussed in the briefing meeting and asked if there

were any additional comments.

John Roberts said with his experience in real estate he has seen that senior housing projects are strictly

regulated. He asked if there was potential liability for the city if there is no enforcement by the city and

an HOA decides to abandon the regulation. For example, what if a senior sues the city as a result of
lack of enforcement of the senior housing zoning that is established by the city.

Duncan Murray said abandonment of an HOA could be more of an issue as time goes by, and the city

could look at how they might handle that. For example, staff and city council are looking at the most

recent text amendment regarding what happens if an overlay zone is removed. He indicated that State
legislation has not identified any one governmental agency to deal with HOA issues. The city would
only have authority if an HOA document had regulations contrary to West Jordan code. Liability
generally transpires when you fail in a duty, but if the city does not have the duty to enforce the HOA,
they could not fail at it. The city could identify the areas where there is some authority and work on
them, such as the SHO.

John Roberts said that given it is actually a zoning regulation, could someone make a zoning complaint

through code enforcement.

Duncan Murray said that could be an area to look for solutions where the city might have authority for

enforcement.
