        A.1     Issue:        PARK PLACE SUBDIVISION PHASE 3, SUBDIVISION
                              AMENDMENT

                Location: 11065 South 1055 West
                File No:   PLPLA202000041
                Applicant: Nick Mason, Bach Homes

Planner Damir Drozdek reviewed background information on this item from the staff report.

Derick Wright, (Applicant) – said I live just south of this property, and this is a project we have been
working on for a couple of years. Myself, and my partner live in this area, and we love it here. This is kind of
an unconditional request, because usually you come to a City and request a private street so you can make
streets narrower, or smaller, or build them at normal specs like a public street, but the intent is usually to
create a PUD, or HOA that is denser. That is not our request tonight. Our goal was to have this circle gated,
but we understand that the Municipal Code does not allow gates. We work with staff and put in some islands
and things, and it was made a public street. What we are requesting tonight is to record this street as a private
street. Our application does not include gates of any sort, and the roads have been built to City specs. The
City code says the private streets are discouraged, but they can be approved in the right situation. This road
has been built to City specs, and we are simply asking to have it recorded as a private street. There are some
improvements in the front of the cul-de-sac that are in the right of way, so we would like to continue to
maintain and protect those are in the private street. We had a conference call with Assistant City Attorney
Todd Sheeran, and spoke with others, and we all did conclude that there is nothing in the code that should
prohibit this from being approved tonight.

Commissioner Catmull said could you go into more detail regarding the comment you made on made a
private-street. Do you have some projects that you would like to put in motion?

Mr. Wright said our intention is not to change anything. I know the City has been put in situations where they
have been asked to take on streets that are not built to the specs of a City street. We simply want this to be a
private street. We want to remove that burden. We will maintain the islands that are in the HOA, and we will
maintain the street. In the event, sometime down the road that the City becomes more favorable of the
potential of a gate, that would allow us to pursue it down the road. In the interim, that is really our motivation
right now.

Chair Hollist asked why just this one cul-de-sac?

Mr. Wright said because it is the only cul-de-sac that has islands. It was built differently. It is our intent to
make that one cul-de-sac private, not the rest of the neighborhood. It is a private right-of-way and we are
connecting to other projects, and it is a dead end circle. This street would be the only street that has the
potential to be gated in the future.

Commissioner Morrissey said by creating a private street you have the luxury of putting these planters in the
middle of the road, is that correct?

Mr. Wright said staff worked with us to put those planters within the public right-of-way.

Commissioner Morrissey said so what is the benefit to having a public street.

Mr. Wright said like I said, our intent from the beginning was to gate it. We know that is not going to happen
now, but in the future, there may be a change and the municipal code may allow that. It would be hard to
come to the City afterwards, and ask if we can have the street back. If it is a private street, we already have to
maintain those islands, so I don’t think we would be putting a burden on the City to have this a private street.

Chair Hollist said if this was made a private street who would be responsible for snow removal and street
maintenance.

Planner Drozdek said they would be responsible for the snow removal and street maintenance.

Mr. Wright said we know that, and we are ok with that.

Chair Hollist said how would the trash be handled.

Planner Drozdek said the same way every other street handles trash. This is not a gated street, so they could
get down the streets just like any other street.

Chair Hollist opened the Public Hearing to comments. There was none. She closed the Public Hearing.

Commissioner Catmull said I did some research on private/public roads, and there is a lot of talk about that
over multiple decades. I didn’t find a lot advocating not having private roads, but I did find a lot of reasons to
make roads private, as well as there were some Cities that had no problem having private streets. That is what
I found in my research, and to be candid, I am a little concerned with going too hard against a lot of opinions,
because I do understand the desire and how it could possibly put the applicant in a better situation in the
future, so I appreciate you taking the time to explain that.
,
Commissioner Morrissey said we have gone through the process and have had a discussion, so I am ok with
this going through. This meets all of the City standards and ordinances as well, so I wouldn’t want to keep a
private citizen from moving forward when it meets all of the criteria.

        C.1      Issue:        ORDINANCE 2020-05
                               TEXT AMENDMENT SECTION 16.04.200(B) FENCING BETWEEN
                               INCOMPATIBLE ZONES

                 Location:
                 File No:   PLZTA20200056
                 Applicant: City of South Jordan

Assistant City Engineer Jeremy Nielsen reviewed background information on this item from the staff report.

Commissioner Catmull said what would be replaced there if you don’t put a masonry fence.

Planner Sanderson said the simple reason for having a masonry fence is for farm animal rights, but there are
many other types of fencing that could secure animals, and work just as well. The circumstances for not doing
the fence would be substantial topography, so in those cases the City Engineer has the discretion to not
require a fence, or put up another type of fencing that is suitable to keep the animals in. In most cases, it is not
required to have fencing between residential uses; it is really just for the use of farm animals.

Commissioner Catmull said in the code, does this cover all zones, or only a subset of zones.
South Jordan City                                                                                          6

Planner Sanderson said you would have your agriculture zones and the R.1-8 zone. If you are in those zones
and you have a piece of property that is a ½ acre, that would qualify you to have farm animals. Those are the
circumstances for a masonry fence, or whatever type fence the City Engineer deems suitable.

Chair Hollist said if we send this on to the City Council would we find that they have a strong preference to
review things like this.

Assistant City Attorney Todd Sheeran said this text came up when we did a text for pending ordinance several
months ago stating that we may be making changes to the code. They allowed that to go forward on the notice
pending, and it is being codified, so they are well aware of it. I actually, drafted the language for the
ordinance and reviewed it with the City Engineer. It was presented to the City Council, and I think that most
of them do not want to get into the technicality of it.

Chair Hollist opened the Public Hearing to comments.

Derick Wright, South Jordan – said I did not know this was on the Agenda tonight, but I am familiar with
this, so I can give you a case that would apply. At the Park Place, they have a 40 Lot community and we just
talked about one phase of it. Our boundary on the south butts up to Parkway Palisades, and the Parkway
Palisades zone allow animals rights. There is one out of 200 some odd homes in there that has a ½ acre lot
with an animal larger than a dog, and those that butt up to us do not. Our property line is at the bottom of
Midas Creek, and they have a chain-link fence up on their bank, on their side, but our property line goes all
the way down to the bottom, and our zone does not allow animal rights. We can’t put a fence down in the
creek. We have talked to flood control and they do not want us to put a concrete fence up at the top of the
bank, because they want to be able to maintain the creek. When the Midas Creek turns and goes north, our
property actually crosses Midas Creek and goes up a steep incline to the circle that is up in Parkway
Palisades. It would be impossible to get equipment in there to put in a concrete fence. Therefore, you can’t put
it in the creek you would need to come on the east side of the creek, which means a person buying that
property would lose a third of their lot to comply with the ordinance if they put in a concrete fence. They
would not have access to their property if they wanted to beautify, or do something with Midas Creek.
Coincidently, our southbound property falls into this criteria. To the west, and the north, we will be installing
a 6ft. concrete fence, but on our west boundary against South Jordan Estates, they do not have animal rights,
so we don’t have a concrete fence there.

Chair Hollist closed the Public Hearing.

Chair Hollis said is a flood plain a unique circumstance.

Assistant City Engineer Nielsen said yes. We would need to consider that when putting up a fence, because
that could impact the flood plain.

        D.1      Issue:        DISCUSSION REGARDING WIRELESS COMMUNICATION
                               ORDINANCE

Planner Sanderson and Meadow Wedekind presented a training on Cell Towner Regulations; see (Attachment
A).

Craig Chagnon (Crown Castle) -said wireless is very complex and it moves very fast, and the South Jordan
staff is getting very familiar with the concepts and the needs of my request. Crown Castle is a very safe
company. We own real estate assets with a very long- term horizon. You asked if we loan or lease, we lease
from a landowner, and sometime we own the ground, but whatever the case may be, it is real estate. Our
needs in real estate are both vertical and horizontal. When Walmart came here they asked to build a Super
Center, they didn’t come and ask for 5 or 10 acres, they probably asked for 10, or 20 acres for what they
thought was needful for the community. That is what we are doing; we need the space to accommodate the
infrastructure, which is the poles, antennas, and other supporting equipment. The code that exists now is not
very flexible it was a one size fits all, so we were having a hard time meeting the requirements of the code.
There are four core locations, three are Verizon and one is AT&T. We prefer co-location. It is fundamental,
and universal in the wireless industry to get as many companies on the pole as possible. The towers may each
be a little different, but the more space that we have the better we can mount the equipment, and we can do it
more safely. I work in about four different states in the Rocky Mountain region, and I am not familiar with
any other jurisdiction that requires the flush mount only. Typically, it is in a residential zone, school, or an
area where aesthetics are not as important.

Chair Hollist said is each level a single carrier.

Mr. Chagnon said yes that is correct.

Chair Hollist said does the flush mount prevent you at this time from doing the (4) co-locations you have on
hold.

Mr. Chagnon said yes, it is prohibitive. There are multiple reasons. Typically, antenna rays are divided in to
three sectors (A, B, and C), and how many antennas can fit in those sectors. With a flush mount, there is one,
but it is not uncommon to put up three antennas in each sector. There is a safety issue on the flush mounting,
so having the antennas farther off the pole will allow us to put additional equipment.

Commissioner Catmull said does the overall range get affected by the 18 to 36-coverage area.

Mr. Chagnon said yes. Having more antennas up there, we will be able to get a better signal.

Mr. Chagnon said when we were here a few weeks ago at the City Council I had one of my colleagues with
me that is much better at explaining things about the antenna technology, but I will try to explain some things
that were explained there. Antennas speak to one another, so when you are driving through town you are
getting a signal off one antenna to a certain point, then you will get a handoff from another antenna. Multiple
antennas with make it so you get a more robust signal and you won’t be dropping calls, and having dead
spots. We have two ears to function, and they are not on the front of our face, they are spaced apart, which
allows us to have our optimal hearing. That is very similar in antenna technology, the spatial orientation is
very important. Carriers won’t do anything they don’t have to do, they are not going to go out and spend more
money on a taller, or more antennas, there sole objective is service to meet the demands. They are not asking
for way more than what they need, they just want to match the demand.

Commissioner Catmull said how does that fall into 6409.

Mr. Chagnon said 6409 is a FCC order and it was part of the 2012 tax relief. Basically, Congress, recognized
that wireless infrastructure, for a lot of reasons is very important to our nation. They wanted to enable us to
streamline the process, and get through permitting jurisdictions. I don’t think it is anything that you need to be
afraid of, but I think it is something that you need to understand. There a couple of gray areas, but it is not as
ambiguous as we are hearing. The main point of 6409 originally, was to simply not hold up some of the basic
maintenance the we do. If you read it, is says, “Removal, replacement and addition.” The majority of the work
that I do is upgrade the equipment from 3G, to 4G, to 5G. We are removing, replacing, and adding equipment
and antennas. They recognized that we shouldn’t be held up in deploying superior technology when we are
simply adding, or removing from the configuration.

Commissioner Catmull said what is the lifespan of the equipment?

Mr. Chagnon said it is hard to say, but we are always trying to improve it. I have worked with Crown Castle
for 7 years, and I have touched many of the same towers once a year.

Assistant City Attorney said last year we received about five requests on 6409, and only one was approved.
There was a lot of back, and forth regarding the ones that were denied, and whether they should have been
denied or not. That is a mechanism that is heavily used by the wireless community, but the language itself for
4609 is sparse.

Planner Brad Sanderson said Meadow is going to send out an email with the presentation we went over
tonight, so you can go over it. We covered a lot of information tonight, so this will allow you to go over the
presentation when you have time. We encourage you to go and look at the sites. They look very different in
person, because you will be able to see really how big they are. We will also be sending you a draft ordinance
so you can get yourself familiar with that. We will send you our contact information and you can reach out to
us if you have any questions, or suggestions. Of course, all of this will be a recommendation to the City
Council, and you may even want to talk to some of the Council Members that you are familiar with and get
their thoughts and feelings, as they have had additional training.
