Ogden Police License Plate Reader Update
         Police Chief Sube presented an update on the ALPR program. He emphasized that the program operates with support
from the Mayor while ensuring adherence to legal and constitutional requirements. The Chief provided background on the ALPR
program:
              ●    Started in 2008 with vehicle-mounted readers
              ●    Expanded in 2020 with fixed ALPR cameras in the Project Safe Neighborhood (PSN) zone
              ●    Currently operates 41 total cameras (32 in the PSN area with one non-operational, eight in Business Depot
                   Ogden (BDO) and one mobile)
         Chief Sube explained the legal framework governing ALPRs, noting that State law classifies license plate data as
protected information, limits retention to nine months maximum, and requires written policies and oversight. The Ogden Police
Department's policy (Policy 65) exceeds state requirements by:
               ● Restricting data retention to only 30 days instead of the 9 months allowed by law
               ● Requiring all searches to be conducted through a supervisor or the Area Tactical Analysis Center (ATAC)
               ● Mandating case numbers or CAD call numbers for all searches
               ● Implementing verification requirements
         The Chief clarified what ALPRs do and do not do:
               ● They don't use facial recognition, biometrics, or video recording
               ● They capture still images of license plates with date, time and location
               ● They help develop leads for investigations but don't provide complete evidence
         Chief Sube demonstrated the PSN area map showing ALPR locations and shared several case examples where ALPRs
were critical in solving crimes, including kidnappings, homicides, child abuse cases, hit-and-runs, and thefts. He noted ALPRs
have also helped exonerate innocent suspects.
         Council members asked several questions about the program, including:
               ● The history of Policy 65
               ● Audit procedures (biennial full audits with quarterly spot checks)
               ● Data sharing with other agencies (sharing with 32 local agencies but no direct federal access)
               ● Effectiveness of the 30-day retention period (has been sufficient for investigations)
               ● The cost-effectiveness of the program (funded primarily through PSN grants)
         The Chief confirmed that no federal agencies can directly access Ogden's ALPR data without making specific requests
through the department, which must include proper case information.

Consent to Annex unincorporated property at 2101 West Hinckley Drive
into Roy City
          Steward Land Company has requested the Council’s consideration of a resolution granting consent for the annexation of
approximately 3.96 acres of unincorporated property into Roy City. Utah Code §10-2-804(7) requires a municipality seeking to
annex property located within 5,000 feet of another city’s municipal airport to obtain consent from the city that operates the
airport. Approval of the resolution would allow the annexation process to proceed.
          Planning Director Brierley explained that the subject property located at approximately 2101 West Hinckley Drive lies
between Ogden and Roy city boundaries, and state law requires Ogden's consent since the property is within 5,000 feet of the
Ogden Hinckley Airport runway. The property owner wants to develop the site for residential use and is working with Roy City.
Planning staff has determined the annexation poses no concerns for airport operations as it is not directly in the flight path.
          Chair Hyer asked about the previously closed runway 7-25 and whether it might reopen in the future. Mr. Brierley stated
he was unsure of the status of that runway, but if it were reopened, it would not pose any issues for the subject property given the
terrain of that area of the airport. He concluded Planning staff recommends the City consent to the property being annexed into
Roy City.

Zone Ogden Update
          The Planning Division project began in summer of 2023 and is currently in the drafting stage. The Council previously
received updates on Zone Ogden during work sessions in March, July, August, and November of 2025. This is another
opportunity for the Council to discuss the new development code, to ask questions, and provide input as the code is being prepared
to go to the Planning Commission for review.
          Planning Manager Brierley and Senior Planner Rypien presented several topics related to the Zone Ogden development
code update:
              ● Private Streets
                       ○ Critical for infill development in Ogden's limited available land
                       ○ Proposed "minor private street" designation for 6 or fewer lots
                       ○ Would require maintenance by HOA or through private road maintenance agreements
                                 ■ Council member Lundell expressed concern about deteriorating private streets within defunct
                                     HOAs
              ● Home Occupations
                       ○ Proposed changes would allow use of garages/outbuildings for home occupations
                       ○ Would permit one outside employee, occasional gatherings of up to 6 employees, and customers by
                           appointment
                       ○ Certain uses would remain prohibited: vehicle repair, animal daycare, and high-traffic businesses
                       ○ Formula-Based Businesses (Chain Businesses)
                       ○ Proposed regulations would limit chain businesses in downtown to preserve unique character
                     ○    Would apply to eating/drinking establishments and retail with 10+ locations in the US
                     ○    Would exclude businesses that originated in Weber County
                     ○    Currently about four formula-based businesses downtown; proposal would limit to 10 maximum
                              ■ Council members discussed balancing preservation of unique downtown character with
                                   preventing vacant spaces.
             ●   Signs
                     ○    Presented issues with current signage and need for regulations
                     ○    Proposed maintaining content neutrality while limiting sign heights to 20 feet maximum
                     ○    Suggested three categories: non-permanent signs (6-month limit), portable signs (must be removed
                          daily), and event signs (allowed during holidays and elections)
                      ○ Proposal to eliminate distinction between on-premise and off-premise signs, while maintaining
                          billboard restrictions

             ● Parking (this issue was not fully covered due to time constraints of the meeting)
