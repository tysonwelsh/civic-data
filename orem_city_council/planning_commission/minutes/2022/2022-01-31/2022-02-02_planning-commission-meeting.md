Agenda Item 3.3 is a request by Development Services for the Planning Commission to amend Section 22-5-3(A)
and the Zoning Map of the City of Orem by changing the zoning of properties within the Hub District zones, The Arts
District zones, the City Center District zones, the Canyon Crossing District zones, and the North Village District zones
to the C1, C2, C3, R6, R6.5, R8, PD-1, PD-30, PD-37, PD-39, and PD-40 zones. And, amend portions of Section 22-
8-8 pertaining to zone development standards along the State Street corridor.

Staff Presentation: At the request of the City Council, Development Services proposes to rezone all State Street
Districts to the zones in place prior to June 6, 2018 when the first State Street District was adopted. The proposed
zones are mostly commercial zones, but also includes residential and planned development zones. The proposed
zoning map is included in the staff report.

In addition to the zoning map changes, staff proposes several amendments to portions of Section 22-8-8 (Zone
Development Standards) pertaining to standards along state street. These changes include:

1.   Height. Limiting the overall height in the C2 zone from 60 feet to 48 feet. The maximum height for structures
     which are setback less than 100 feet from a residential zone shall be 35 feet. The maximum height of non-
     habitable structures (chimneys, ventilators, antennas, etc.) shall be modified from 15 feet to 10 feet, with a
     modified maximum area from 50% to 30% of the uppermost floor area.

2.   Setbacks. The existing requirement of an 8 foot separated sidewalk with a 15 foot plantar area will remain as
     currently required. The proposed change to the design standards includes a minimum setback of 15 feet from
     the back of sidewalk. The additional 15 feet must be landscaped. There is no maximum building setback. A
     diagram is attached in the staff report for further clarification.

3.   State Street Districts. All references to the State Street Districts have been eliminated including but not
     limited to building access requirements along the street, space open to the public, and small building addition
     exception all specific to the maximum setback requirement which is no longer applicable.

Recommendation: The Development Review Committee determined this request complies with the Orem City Code.
Staff recommends the Planning Commission forward a positive reccomendation to the City Council to amend Section
22-5-3(A) and the Zoning Map of the City of Orem by changing the zoning of properties within the Hub District
zones, The Arts District zones, the City Center District zones, the Canyon Crossing District zones, and the North
Village District zones to the C1, C2, C3, R6, R6.5, R8, PD-1, PD-30, PD-37, PD-39, and PD-40 zones. And, amend
portions of Section 22-8-8 pertaining to zone development standards along the State Street corridor.

Dear Orem Planning Commission,
          Thank you for your service. I realize that this is a difficult moment to be making these decisions where
there are such strong feelings about the issues at hand.
         My thoughts are based on the following values: I like my single-family home neighborhood; I really like
walking and biking places and wish there was more opportunity to do this for me and my kids (not just biking for
recreation on the Murdock Canal Trail, which I love); we have for too long privileged cars in how we make
decisions about city planning to the detriment of the places we live.
         I appreciate that there was significant push back on the State Street Master Plan and adjustments need to be
made. However, I also believe prohibiting ALL multi-use, high density development on State Street is the wrong
approach. I would like to see more buffered zoning to project residential neighborhoods. There is a long spectrum
of housing density. Free standing, single-family homes are the least dense and multi story high rise apartments are
the most. These two ends of the spectrum should never be next to each other. But, duplexes or triplexes, court
apartments, could go between these two extremes. That would protect the existing residential neighborhood, but
also provide more dense housing. Under this system the disputed development at 1600 N and 400 West would not
have even been possible. That parcel, next to a single-family home residential lot would have to be something more
like duplexes or triplexes. However, facing state street, a taller building could be built.
          We need more housing options in Orem, and we need them along State Street. It makes most sense to put
higher density development along transportation corridors and hubs. Envision Utah’s Valley Visioning study found
this option to the best of all the models they presented to Utah Valley residents.
         With the increase in Utah Valley population, our traffic is going to get worse. What will help that is if we
put some of people’s daily activities closer together. Planning for more walkable communities is economically
sound and helps create a healthier population, according to Jeff Speck, the leading planner who studies this. Places
that encourage walking through design and policy are also safer for the residents who live there. I heard people say
about the State Street Master Plan that it is several miles long and therefore no one would walk it. That isn’t the
point. Are there pockets that could be more walkable? Could I go, or send my kids, to the intersection at 1600
North and State and there would be options for them to buy things from stores that would make it so I didn’t have to
get in my car and drive to get that thing? That would be lovely!
         I urge you to consider that some increased density in housing would benefit Orem and therefore not adopt
the proposed changes. We will have more and more competition for our shopping revenue tax dollars, and without
more local residents, our city will suffer in the long term. Work instead to develop new requirements that apply
principles of good planning and design that provide buffers between the highest and lowest density housing,
promote some areas of walkability, and build for density in a way that capitalizes on transportation corridors and
hubs (including existing and proposed public transit), and still preserves single family neighborhoods. This will
help our city!
Sincerely,
Laura Redford
Orem Resident
