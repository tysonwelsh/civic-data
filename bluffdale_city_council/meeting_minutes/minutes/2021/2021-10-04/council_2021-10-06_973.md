City Manager, Mark Reid, reported that at the last Council Meeting an individual spoke during the

14000 South. The Engineering Depattment was asked to prepai·e a concept and cost estimate.
Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer, repo1i ed that based on the comment received, the Engineering
Department looked into options to further deter left turns out of Summit Academy. A raised
median was proposed that would not be in the center of 14400 South. There is an acceleration lane
that motorists enter as they travel westbound on 14400 South. Staff was proposing to install a
raised median to separate the acceleration lane from the normal westbound traffic to prevent
motorists from turning left. This option, however, would not prevent the residents of the existing
homes sun-ounding the school from turning left. The median would be sho1iened to allow residents
to enter their driveways and motorists to turn left into the school as they normally would.

4.      Consideration and Vote on Ordinance No. 2021-19, Amending the City of Bluffdale
        Floodplain Ordinance and Accompanying Flood Insurance Rate Maps ("FIRM"),
        Staff Presenter, Daniel Tracer.

Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer, reported that the Federal Emergency Management Agency
(''FEMA") regularly updates its floodplain maps and conducts a review of the C ity's ordinance to
ensure that they comply with the National Flood Insurance Rate Program. He presented minor
Code changes involving the removal of old references and adding violation verbiage. He stated
that new floodplain maps were added to the City on waterways that did not previously have them.
The area is along Rose Creek and will become a regulated floodplain. There are a few areas where
there is the potential for development, but it will not affect existing structures. Mr. Tracer stated
that hazards have always existed there because of Rose Creek. The new map simply identifies the
hazards and creates a methodology for approving new development nearby.

Dave Kallas asked if the change will impact a project that was recently approved. Mr. Tracer
stated that it will not. In that section of Rose Creek, the channel can convey a 100-year event.
Council Member Kallas asked if the channel will be in backyards. Mr. Tracer stated that the
channel and the adjacent access road are on Salt Lake County property.

Mr. Tracer reported that there is also a new map in southern Bluffdale that is technically in
Hen-iman identified as the Wood Hollow drainage that impacts Redwood Road . Another area was
identified as well but had not been named. It was on property owned by McDougal who is a
prope11y developer. Mr. Tracer stated that the area has always been a natural drainage . The City
has had trouble getting anyone to take ownership and maintain it. They spoke to Salt Lake County
and Herriman City officials about it and Salt Lake County indicated that they do not have the funds
to take on new drainages. The existing ordinances, however, specify that all-natural drainages
must be kept and allowed to function as they did pre-development. FEM.A decided to issue a
floodplain for the area and Bluffdale City is the floodplain administrator for all floodplains within
the City. Salt Lake County simply maintains waterways and would not maintain or take it under
their jurisdiction.

To fill it in, a more detailed study would be needed along with additional permitting to make sure
that when it is piped, it is the right size. Mr. Tracer stated that to contest the County' s findings the
City would need to conduct an engineering study stating that they disagree and provide reasoning.

Dave Kallas moved to approve Ordinance 2021-19, amending the City of Bluffdale
Floodplain Ordinance and accompanying Flood Insurance Rate Maps ("FIRM"). Wendy
Aston seconded the motion. Vote on motion: Wendy Aston-Aye, Traci Crockett-Aye, Jeff
Gaston-Aye, Mark Hales-Aye, Dave Kallas-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.

5.       Consideration and Vote on Ordinance No. 2021-20, Prohibiting Certain Applications
         or Approvals of Non-City Public Uses in the GC-1 Zone, Staff Presenter, Grant
         Crowell.

City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, reported that a Temporary Zoning
Land Use Ordinance, also commonly referred to as a moratorium is requested, which can last up
to six months. Recently, it came to staff s attention that there may be some large-scale utility uses
to be located in one of the City ' s commercial corridors. That was of concern for a few reasons.
Staff wanted to make sure that utility uses do not become the default use along City frontage.
Mr. Crowell commented that public utility uses are very broadly approved.

Mayor Timothy suggested that the ordinance be City-wide rather than just the GC-1 zone. Mr.
Crowell stated that if it were City-wide, they would want to make sure that it does not inadvertently
apply to something already in the planning process.

Mr. Reid suggested that they also address setbacks and stated that a substation with a 100-foot
setback would be more palatabl e. Mr. Crowell recommended that they look at design criteria as
well. He stated that they were trying to facilitate the Sewer District that is having difficulty finding
land in the southern part of the community. There may be utilities relocated in the design and
analysis for the Bangerter interchange and they want to make sure that the table is establi shed in
advance.

It was noted that while utility companies must comply with the zoning, they have condemnation
authority and have rights that an average person does not. Mayor Timothy's preference was for it
to be City-wide in certain instances but expressed his support.

Dave Kallas moved to approve Ordinance 2021-20, prohibiting certain applications or
approvals of non-City public uses in the GC-1 Zone. Wendy Aston seconded the motion.
Vote on motion: Wendy Aston-Aye, Traci Crockett-Aye, Jeff Gaston-Aye, Mark Hales-Aye,
Dave Kallas-Aye. The motion passed unanimously.
