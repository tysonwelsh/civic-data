36   City Planner and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell shared a presentation from the
37   Utah League of Cities and Towns (“ULCT”), called “Moderate-Income Housing Deep Dive: HB-
38   462 – The Nuts and Bolts for October 1, 2022, and Beyond.” He reported that the Legislature
39   previously passed Senate Bill (“SB”) 34, which was an update to the moderate-income housing
40   requirements. Some of the first Legislation on moderate-income housing impacted Bluffdale. He

 1   explained that the City of Bluffdale was used as an example of a city that needs to further address
 2   moderate-income housing. As a result, the Bluffs and Vintage on the Bluffs areas were approved.
 3   Bluffdale was not the only city that was held as an example at that time, as other cities with
 4   historically large lots received additional attention from the courts as well.
 5
 6   SB-34 asked cities to plan for moderate-income housing in the General Plan and report on
 7   moderate-income housing on an annual basis. The reporting went through the Housing and
 8   Community Development Department of the Utah Department of Workforce Services. In House
 9   Bill (“HB”) 462, the requirements were amended to add further clarification and expand the
10   strategies or menu items that jurisdictions could choose from to create more flexibility.
11   Mr. Crowell discussed the purpose and objectives of the requirements, which were as follows:
12
13      •   To meet the needs of people of various income levels that live, work, or desire to live or
14          work in the community;
15      •   To allow people with various incomes to benefit from and fully participate in all aspects of
16          neighborhood and community life;
17      •   To determine how the jurisdiction will provide realistic opportunities to meet the need for
18          additional moderate-income housing within the next five years; and
19      •   To receive consideration for funding of projects based on compliance with the moderate-
20          income housing requirements.

     A RESOLUTION TO EXTEND THE TEMPORARY REDUCTION OF THE TRANSPORTATION
                                   IMPACT FEE

A.       On October 28, 2020, the City of Bluffdale, Utah (the “City”) adopted an impact fee
facilities plan (“IFFP”) and impact fee analyses (“IFA”) for parks & recreation, storm drainage,
water, public safety and transportation impact fees in accordance with UCA Section 11-36a-;
and

B.     On October 28, 2020, the City adopted Ordinance 2020-23 (the “Enactment”) which
enacted the impact fees for parks & recreation, storm drainage, water, public safety and
transportation;

C.     On July 14, 2021, the City Council adopted Resolution 2021-41 which temporarily
reduced the transportation impact fee by 40% for a period of 12 months or until the new IFFP
is enacted to encourage economic development;

D.     The City will not complete the IFFP update within 12 months of the reduction
resolution and desires to extend the fee reduction until the new IFFP is enacted; and

E.      The definitions in this Ordinance are same as those in the IFFP, IFA, and Enactment;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF BLUFFDALE AS
FOLLOWS:

Section 1. Findings. The Council finds and determines as follows:

1.1.    As a means to encourage commercial development within the City and to address the
current status of the IFFP update, the City Council reduces the transportation impact fee for
commercial development by 40% from $6,738 per PM Entering Trip to $4,042 per PM Entering
Trip. This reduction will remain in effect until a new IFA and IFFP are enacted.

CITY MANAGER AUTHORITY TO AMEND CONSOLIDATED FEE SCHEDULE

             The City Manager shall have authority to impose new fees, as the City Manager deems appropriate or
             necessary. Said fees shall be implemented by executive order. Any fee added by the City Manager
             under this section shall be effective as specified in executive order implementing the fee. In the regular
             City Council meeting immediately following any fee added pursuant to this section, the City Manager
             shall advise the City Council of said fee. The City Council shall then have 60 days from the date of the
             action taken by the City Manager to amend the Consolidated Fee Schedule to include the new fee.
             Should the City Council fail to amend the Consolidated Fee Schedule within the 60-day time period,
             the fee shall be void, and any fees collected pursuant to the executive order issued by the City Manager
             shall be refunded.

             The City Manager or Designee shall have the authority to waive, reduce or refund a fee in the interest
             of fairness and equity.
