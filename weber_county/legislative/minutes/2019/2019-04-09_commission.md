      2. UPDATE ON STORM WATER PROJECTION.
         Tage Flint, General Manager/CEO, Weber Basin Water, gave an overhead presentation, noting that they serve
         five counties in northern Utah, with over 680,000 people, and the Gardner Policy Institute’s staggering
         projections are that the population will almost double by 2065. Their service area includes seven reservoirs (of
         which Willard Bay is the largest fresh water reservoir) and their waters contribute to the late season agricultural
         irrigation. Mr. Flint showed Weber Basin’s average storage, for the 2018 year and currently. There has been
         a lot of water in the Ogden and Weber Rivers through this past winter. He outlined the spring runoff projections,
         which depends on many factors including snowpack stating that on April 1 we were over 130% of normal—we
         had been in a drought scenario for about the fifth consecutive year. He showed the inflow forecasts for some
         reservoirs and they are very high. We are already seeing over 1,000 cfs from the three stems of the Ogden
         River above Pineview. Because of extensive improvements through a cooperative project with Weber County,
         flood control has been greatly enhanced just off 12th Street near I-15. Commissioner Harvey took a tour last
         year with the District and noted how fascinating that water is stored in underground aquifers so it does not
         evaporate. Mr. Flint addressed Chair Jenkins’ question giving an update on the Bear River project.
