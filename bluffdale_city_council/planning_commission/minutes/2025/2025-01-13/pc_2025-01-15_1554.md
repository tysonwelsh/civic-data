       Proposed Update to the City's Oualitv Assurance Program. City of Blnffdale.
        Applicant. Dan Tracer,Staff Presenter.

Assistant City Engineer, Dan Tracer, presented the Staff Report and reported that the proposed
amendments would update the City Quality Assurance Program originally adopted in 2016 to
ensure that all infrastructure that will be dedicated to the City is properly installed.

Mr.Tracer explained why concrete can fail. Concrete consists ofsand,large aggregate, water, and
other additions based on installation conditions. When concrete is installed, the supplier submits
a data sheet indicating the makeup of the particular batch of concrete, as well as certification of
each compound and chemical analysis results to ensure that the batch meets requirements for
strength, weight, thickness, air content, etc. Concrete mix consists of many different parts, and
issues with any one component could cause the concrete to fail.

The Quality Assurance Program manual specifies the information the City requires for all types of
infrastructure including concrete, asphalt, water lines, streetlights, Americans with Disabilities Act
("ADA")ramps,etc. All items must be reviewed,inspected,and certified to ensure that the correct
materials are delivered. Once onsite, the manual defines required testing to ensure that the correct
material was provided and properly installed. It details what needs to be provided, who needs to
provide it, what types oftesting need to be done and by whom,required certifications, and testing
frequency.


Chair Cragun asked for a summary of the updates. Mr. Tracer stated that more detail has been
included on each item that needs to be provided to the City. For example:(type ofinformation)
needs to be providedfrom (type oftest), including (types ofcertification to be provided). It also
specifies the City's responsibility to review the information and the frequency of doing so. More
detailed information was included so that contractors know what to expect and when.


Commissioner Flynn asked about specifications regarding things like required minimum
temperature to install concrete. Mr. Tracer stated that temperature is not covered in the manual.

6.     OPEN PUBLIC MEETINGS ACT rOPMA"! ANNUAL TRAINING.                                    Fred
       Donaldson.Staff Presenter.


Mr. Donaldson presented the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act ("OPMA") training. The
OPMA requires that actions by political subdivisions and public bodies be taken openly, and
meetings be open to the public unless properly closed. The law favors openness, and exceptions
to holding open meetings are strictly construed.

The definition of "Meeting" previously included a list of things that a meeting is not, but was
amended to remove those items and provide a narrower description ofthe term.

A Meeting is defined as:

   • A gathering of a public body with a quorum present;
   • That is convened by the person with authority to convene the public body and following
       the process provided by law for convening the public body and for the purpose of acting
       as a public body;
   • For the purpose of receiving public comment on a relevant matter, deliberate about a
       relevant matter, or take action on a relevant matter.

   • A meeting can be in person, electronic, or hybrid.


Mr. Donaldson noted that a ribbon cutting or Christmas party would not be considered a meeting.

"Public Body" is defined in code as:

   • A body that was created by the Utah Constitution, statute, rule, ordinance, or resolution;
   • Consists oftwo or more persons;
   • Spends, distributes, or is supported by tax money; and
   • Makes decisions about the public's business.


Noticing requirements for open meetings were reviewed. They require at least 24 hours notice,
and the notice must include the agenda,date,time, and place. Mr. Donaldson noted that City Staff
does a great job of noticing meetings, and a yearly schedule of regular meetings is publicly
available. Notice is not required for emergency meetings. Minutes are required for all meetings
of the public body and must be made available to the public for review. Complete, unedited
recordings must also be made available, and members of the public are also allowed to record
public meetings.


Mr. Donaldson reported that, per State Code requirements, the Bluffdale City Council adopted
City Code §1.160.010 authorizing any public body ofthe City to hold electronic meetings as long
as all OPMA requirements are met. Fully electronic meetings, where there is no anchor location.

are not allowed unless the venue is closed due to public health or safety concerns. The public must
have the means to view and participate in the meeting, and all comments and voting must be by
roll call.


Individuals constituting a quorum may not get together outside of a meeting to predetermine an
action to be taken at a meeting. Members of the public body also should not transmit electronic
messages to other members during a convened meeting. Meetings should be orderly, and any
person who is willfully disruptive can be removed from a public meeting.

The requirements for closed meetings were reviewed next.

   • Closed meetings can only be held for specific reasons,and the decision to close the meeting
        is discretionary for local government meetings.
   • Two-thirds ofthe members present must vote to close a meeting.
   • An open meeting with proper notice must be held before closing a meeting.
   • The reasons why the meeting is being closed must be stated and the location ofthe meeting
        provided.
   • The closed part of the meeting must be recorded except in rare circumstances.

Reasons for closing a meeting include discussions of:

   • Character, professional competence, or physical or mental health of an individual.
   • Pending or reasonably imminent litigation.
   • Procurement and property purchase.

During a closed meeting, a public body cannot approve an ordinance, resolution, rule, regulation,
contract, or appointment; conduct interviews for elected positions; or take final action on a matter.

Violation ofthe OMPA is a Class B misdemeanor,but most violations can be cured in a subsequent
meeting and typically do not result in litigation or enforcement. It can be enforced by the attorney
general, county attorney, or a private citizen denied a right under the act.

Commissioner Swanson asked about communications to or from members of the public body to
citizens during a meeting. Mr. Donaldson stated that for purposes of decorum, members of the
public body should not be texting during an open meeting, but it is not specifically prohibited by
State law. Chair Cragun added that it would be a problem of perception because people would
wonder what you were discussing and with whom.


In response to a question raised by Commissioner Flynn, Mr.Donaldson stated that representatives
of the Police Department do attend City Council meetings to preserve decorum, but he has not
personally witnessed someone being removed from a meeting.


Mr. Donaldson then conducted training on Meeting Procedures and noted that the City's
procedures are semi-formal and follow the Roberts Rules of Order. Meetings are conducted by a
Chair or Mayor, and all comments should be recognized by the Chair. Actions are taken based on
motions, seconds, and then final votes.


Mr. Donaldson provided an overview of key points contained in Chapter 7 of the Policies and
Procedures Manual, which relates to the Code ofConduct for elected and appointed officials.

7.10: Code of Conduct


   • The City's public bodies are diverse,and members have differing points ofview that should
       be shared in a respectful way.
   • The Mayor or Chair is responsible for keeping comments focused on agenda items.
   • In discussions, free debate does not require nor justify members making belligerent,
       personal, impertinent, slanderous, threatening, abusive, or disparaging comments.
   • The public should be made to feel welcome,and members ofthe public body should avoid
       showing bias on topics.
   • Confidentiality should be maintained.
   • Every effort should be made to be cooperative and mutually respectful with City Staff.
   • Members who do not comply with the Code ofConduct can be reprimanded or censured.

Commissioner Flynn asked ifsomeone could be sanctioned by prohibiting them from voting. Mr.
Donaldson clarified that they could be removed from the meeting, which would prevent them from
voting, but they could not be restricted from voting ifthey were present. Ifan individual continued
to violate the Code of Conduct, they could be removed from the public body.

7.20 Parliamentary Procedures

   • A quorum is a simple majority ofa public body.
   • It is good practice not to text during a public meeting, especially when texting residents or
       other members.

   • The order of business is at the Mayor or Chair's discretion.
   • All members have the right to be heard on each agenda item.
   • The steps for handling a motion are:
           1. Addressing the Chair.
           2. Recognition by the Chair.
           3. Proposing a Motion.
           4. Seconding a Motion.
           5. Statement of Motion by the Chair.
           6. Voting on a Motion.
           7. Amending a Motion.
           8. Discussion After a Motion is Made.


Chair Cragun stated that the Planning Commission follows that procedure with the exception of
restating the motion and asked ifshe needed to begin doing so. Mr. Donaldson stated that it is best
practice. It can be helpfiil to restate motions, especially more complicated ones, but may not be
necessary for straightforward motions.

Mr. Donaldson reported that it is best to avoid phrases like "I so move." Motions should also be
stated in the positive. Rather than saying,"I move to deny," best practice is to say,"I move that
we vote on the approval of," or "I move that we approve." Chair Cragun stated that City Staff
often includes a draft motion in the Staff Report, and that draft motion is worded in the positive.
