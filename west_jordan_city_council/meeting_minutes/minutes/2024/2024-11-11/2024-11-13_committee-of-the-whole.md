 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Discussion regarding 2025 Western Stampede Plans and Budget
          Community Affairs/Events Manager Ashley Dupler noted that 2025 would be the
          platinum year for the Western Stampede (70th anniversary). She outlined the budget for
          2025 and explained plans to add an entertainment stage and breakaway roping in 2026
          with a requested budget increase. The Council and staff discussed options for a stage and
          where it might fit into the Veterans Memorial Park Master Plan. Public Services Director
          Cory Fralick expressed concern regarding the storage of a permanent stage and
          mentioned the option of renting a stage. Council Member Whitelock preferred to purchase
          a permanent stage rather than an annual rental and thought the Council had already
          approved funding a stage.

          Ms. Dupler shared cost estimates for stage options. Mayor Burton believed it would be
          possible to have a permanent stage built by July 2025. Chair Jacob preferred to find a
          location within the Park Master Plan for a permanent stage noting his second preference
          would be to purchase a portable stage. Mayor Burton commented that renting would
          provide flexibility. Council Member Bloom suggested staff find out the timing of providing
          a permanent stage and if the timing was not feasible for 2025, plan to rent a stage for
          2025. Council Member Bedore believed approval of the Master Plan was needed before a
          permanent stage was added.

          Council Office Director Alan Anderson said $200,000 was allocated in the FY2025 Budget
          for building a permanent stage at Veterans Memorial Park. Mr. Fralick was not sure

       b. Discussion of replacing the Board of Adjustment with an Administrative Law Judge
          Senior Assistant City Attorney Patrick Boice proposed dissolving the Board of Adjustment
          (BOA) in favor of an Administrative Law Judge (ALJ) noting that the BOA met quarterly at
          a minimum. Vice Chair Lamb reflected on his two years of service on the Board of
          Adjustment, questioning its necessity since it operates under strict, inflexible rules. He
          expressed support for dissolving the board. Mr. Boice said rigid regulations for the board
          were set by the State. Council Member Green expressed support for the process available
          with an ALJ.

          Council Member Bloom expressed interest in the experience of other cities that had made
          the same change, and asked the cost difference to the city for a BOA verses an ALJ. She
          wondered if an ALJ process would result in fewer legal challenges for the City. Mr. Boice
          did not know of any cities that had moved away from a BOA in favor of an ALJ, and
          subsequently moved back to a BOA. He also noted that West Jordan was one of the few
          cities in the state that used a BOA to make such decisions. Mr. Boice estimated an ALJ
          would be half the cost of the BOA.

       c. Discussion of a proposed budget amendment for Meadowlark Lane Traffic Calming
          Improvements
          Council Member Green reported that traffic had been a serious issue on Meadowlark Lane.
          He and Council Members Whitelock, Bedore, and Bloom proposed a budget amendment
          to use $30,000 from Council Contingency for traffic calming measures.

          Assistant City Administrator Paul Jerome shared data collected on Meadowlark Lane
          explaining that established warrants had not been met, however staff believed traffic
          speeds justified doing something. Mr. Jerome said staff prepared four options that could
          build on each other if necessary, noting that the fourth option was the only measure that
          would require a budget amendment. Public Works Director Brian Clegg presented a
          phased plan with four options: radar feedback signs, lane-narrowing with striping and
          bike refuges, plastic bollards with painted bulb-outs, and, finally, permanent solutions like
          mini traffic circles or speed tables. He suggested implementing one option at a time to
          gauge effectiveness. Mr. Jerome said the proposed options would be presented to the
          neighborhood.

          The Council and staff discussed a possible mid-year budget amendment if the fourth
          option became necessary. Mr. Jerome said staff could move forward with the first option
          following the neighborhood meeting. Council Member Bloom suggested identifying other
          high traffic areas that could benefit from any funds remaining. Council Member Whitelock
          asked for a follow up report and the outcome of the discussion after the neighborhood
          meeting took place.

       d. Discussion regarding strategies to address affordable housing
          Council Member Whitelock mentioned attending a class regarding affordable housing at
          the ULCT Conference where a graphic was shown with a carport. She commented that
          homes built in the 1950s were built with carports and not garages. She believed home
          ownership was vitally important for stability in the community and suggested homes
          built with carports could provide a more affordable option.

          Council Member Bloom felt that homes with carports could cater to demographics that
          included young professionals, single-parent families, or older adults looking to downsize.
          She spoke of potential benefits to removing the garage requirement, and suggested
          implementing a pilot program that could be reevaluated in a few years. Council Member
          Green expressed support for eliminating the garage requirement to allow carports, and

          suggested another possibility for improving affordability could be narrowing the bulk
          standard from 85 feet to 60 feet.

          Chair Jacob liked reducing regulations for the most part but did not think removing garage
          requirements would affect affordable housing. He felt smaller lots did not necessarily lead
          to more affordable houses. Council Member Shelton spoke in support of giving builders
          the option to build carports instead of garages.

          Community Development Director Scott Langford believed building homes with garages
          was a matter of image and aesthetics. He expressed the opinion that the aesthetics did
          have an impact on the image of a community. Mayor Burton believed the Utah League of
          Cities and Towns was looking at the option as something to take to the Legislature stating
          the idea was to retain the option of adding a garage at a later date. Council Member Green
          liked the idea of narrow lots with long driveways that would provide space to build a
          detached garage in the back if desired.

          Mr. Langford shared neighborhoods that could be used as case studies. He said a lot of
          code enforcement calls came from those neighborhoods that were directly tied to
          carports and things stored outside. From a planning perspective, Mr. Langford said many
          variances that had been approved over the years were related to carport-to-garage
          conversions that did not meet City setback requirements. Council Member Bloom
          commented that HOAs would play a big part in whether residents stored items in
          carports. Council Member Whitelock emphasized that off-street parking should be
          required.

          Chair Jacob was in favor of reducing regulation and letting the market decide what was
          desired. Mr. Langford said developers liked certainty and predictability. He said removing
          the garage requirement across residential zones would be simple. Council Member Bloom
          liked the suggestion to review setbacks.

          City Administrator Korban Lee said he had thought the purpose that evening was to
          discuss removing garage requirements for infill projects, and asked for clarification. Mr.
          Jerome commented that preserving the character of a neighborhood was an important
          part of infill projects. He suggested compatibility was not such an issue with new
          development. Chair Jacob suggested opening the option Citywide to see what would
          happen, with the intention to reevaluate. Council Member Bloom suggested adding
          guardrails.

          Council Member Green suggested staff take what they heard that evening and put
          together a proposal to reduce regulations with the goal to reduce house costs. A majority
          of the Council expressed support for implementing changes that would be applicable
          Citywide.

          Mr. Lee commented that some developments already had affordable housing
          entitlements, and asked if the change discussed would apply to those developments. Mr.
          Langford said such developments would need to reapply under the new Code.
