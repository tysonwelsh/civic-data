Study Session

         Update: Parks Capital Improvement Projects
         Parks and Recreation Director Rhett Ogden provided an update on Parks
         Capital Improvement Projects, and answered questions from the Council.

         Discussion: Emergency Management
         Fire Chief Clint Smith explained changes in administration of emergency
         management to improve efficiency and oversight. Chief Smith spoke of the
         possibility of adding a part-time Emergency Management Specialist position
         if funding were available. The Council and staff discussed the importance of
         having a position dedicated to coordinating community help in emergency
         situations.

         Authority (UTA) had requested feedback regarding the Point of the Mountain
         Transit Project and areas of historical impact. Ms. Jastremsky reported UTA
         proposed to mitigate the impact of removing historic track in Draper with
         placement of an informational plaque. She said the Utah State Preservation

5.b   Action Item: Resolution #24-55 approving and adopting a litigation
      settlement agreement between Draper City and Geneva Rock Products,
      Inc., resolving Case No. 220905804
      David Dobbins with Dobbins Advisory Group, LLC shared a timeline of
      Geneva Rock Products, Inc. intent to expand its claimed vested mining use
      to 142 acres acquired in 1999 (currently zoned A-5, which did not permit
      mining). In 2022, Draper filed a lawsuit in the Third District Court seeking a
      judicial determination that Geneva Rock did not have the vested mining use
      described in a 2021 Declaration and Notice of Vested Mining Use, and
      therefore was not entitled to expand its mining operation onto the 142 acres.
      A Motion for Summary Judgment filed by Draper earlier in 2024 was denied
      on March 20, 2024.

      Mr. Dobbins presented the following settlement proposal:



                                                                          3|Page
         •   Draper City will not challenge Geneva’s Declaration of Vested Mining
             Use.
         •   Geneva Rock will not leave highwall in Area “A” (area graded to meet
             Draper City development standards).
         •   Specified area in A not eligible for post-mining development.
         •   Geneva relinquishes and disclaims all statutory or equitable vested
             mining use rights it has claimed in the Declaration to expand
             extractive operations into Area “B.”
         •   Forego extractive operations in Area B (to be treated as a conservation
             easement area), notwithstanding the fact that the hatched portion
             within Area B is approved by DOGM for Extractive Operations under
             the 2022 RNOI.
         •   If the Vested Mining Use Statute were to be altered by the Utah
             Legislature, such alteration(s) shall not affect the efficacy or validity of
             this agreement.

      Councilmember Green said he believed the current proposal was the best
      the Council had seen, and the best the City could get. He expressed support
      for the settlement proposal. Councilmember T. Lowery said she agreed.
      Councilmember F. Lowry expressed support, and thanked staff and Geneva
      for working diligently. Councilmember Johnson expressed support for the
      settlement proposal, and said she hoped residents understood the many
      months of negotiation and work involved. City Manager Mike Barker
      recognized the role played by the City’s contract legal representative, Maria
      Windham.

      Councilmember Roberts moved to approve                     Resolution     #24-55.
      Councilmember Green seconded the motion.

      A roll call vote was taken. The motion passed unanimously.

                                                                               4|Page
      David Dobbins, consultant with Dobbins Advisory Group, LLC, explained the
      proposed sale of ten acres purchased from Canyons School District,
      currently zoned for half-acre residential lots. Mr. Dobbins reported no
      financially feasible proposals were received for a cemetery, and the City

5.d   Public Hearing: Ordinance #1626 amending text of the Land Use and
      Development Code (Title 9) of the Draper City Municipal Code relating
      to standards for Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs)
      Planner Todd Taylor presented proposed amendments to standards for
      ADUs to:

          1. Allow the property owner to occupy either the main dwelling or the
             ADU and rent, lease, or loan the other.
          2. Allow for more properties in the mapped area prohibiting internal
             accessory dwelling units (I-ADUs) to qualify for the deviation
             necessary to be permitted to have an I-ADU.




                                                                       5|Page
     Mr. Taylor reported the Planning Commission reviewed the proposed
     ordinance and recommended approval.

     Mayor Walker opened a public hearing.

     Victoria Neeshan, Draper resident, said she bought her home intending to
     welcome her parents to live with her. The intended situation did not happen,
     so she welcomed roommates to rent her basement. Ms. Neeshan said she
     received a Notice of Code Violation in September of 2024, and was glad the
     Council was considering the proposed amendments. She said she hoped the
     Council would respect property rights and trust that homeowners would be
     good stewards of their homes.

     Mayor Walker closed the public hearing.

     Councilmember F. Lowry moved to approve Ordinance #1626.
     Councilmember Green seconded the motion.
