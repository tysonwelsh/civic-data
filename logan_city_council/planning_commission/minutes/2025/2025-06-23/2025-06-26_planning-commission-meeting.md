Minutes of the meeting for the Logan City Planning Commission convened in regular session on
Thursday, June 26, 2025. Commissioner Peterson called the meeting to order at 5:30 p.m.

Commissioners Present: Eldon Peterson, Ken Heare, Sarah McNamara, Jennifer Duncan, Jessica
Lucero, Sara Doutre, David Lewis

Commissioners Excused:

Staff Present: Russ Holley, Aimee Egbert, Mike DeSimone, Mohamed Abdullahi, Darek Kimball,
Jeannie Simmonds (City Council), Mike Johnston (City Council).

Approval of the Minutes: Commissioner Ken Heare made a motion to approve the minutes from
the June 12, 2025 meeting. Commissioner Jennifer Duncan seconded the motion. The motion was
approved unanimously.

PUBLIC HEARING:

PC 25-033 Willow Lakes Rezone [Zone Change] Brett Jensen/Willow Lakes Holdings LLC,
authorized agent/owner, is requesting a zone change of Parcel K; TIN 02-345-9005 from Traditional
Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) to Mixed Residential Traditional (MR-9), Parcel D; TIN 02-338-
8001 from NR-6 to Mixed Residential Low (MR-12), Parcel |; TIN 02-345-9003 from NR-6 to
Commercial (COM), and Parcel J; TIN 02-345-9005 from NR-6 to COM, located at approximately
1100 South 1400 West in the Woodruff Neighborhood.

Staff: Russ Holley, Logan City Planner, reviewed the request for the rezone of approximately 8.51
acres from Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) to Commercial (COM), Mixed Residential Low (MR-9)
and Mixed Residential Medium (MR-12) describing the breakdown of specific acreage and location
for each requested zone.

R. Holley discussed the history of the Willow Lakes project and stated that this rezone application is
applicable to parcels in Phase 5. Russ covered the previous rezone request along with the Planned
Development Overlay process the applicant had been engaged in. R. Holley also discussed Logan’s
Future Land Use Plan and the recently adopted Woodruff Neighborhood Plan, both of which had
designated this location as Neighborhood Residential.

R. Holley covered the proposed range of uses that the applicant had discussed in the earlier Planned
Development Overlay including a fitness center, beach amenities, beachfront short term rental
homes, condominiums located along the lake, and an assisted living facility located adjacent to 1000
West. The Commercial rezone request is to enable public and commercial use of the fitness center
and beach amenities beyond just residents of Willow Lakes.

R. Holley discussed the range of uses, intensities, development standards, limitations and impacts
of each of the proposed zoning designations. R. Holley discussed access to the site, earlier
requirements to improve 1400 West, access limitations off of 1000 West, and the conservation
easement and future trail along the Logan River.


Commissioner Doutre asked about the proposed areas of rezone and asked for clarification on what
types of uses would come back to the Commission under a future design review. R. Holley discussed
what would be reviewed administratively by staff versus what would come back to the Commission
either as a design review permit or as a subdivision. There was further discussion on the sequencing
or timing of future development, and whether the Commission would see it all as a cohesive
development plan, or as individual projects. R. Holley stated that, if a rezone was approved, the
Commission would see future development in a piecemeal manner based on the developer's
schedule.

Commissioner Lucero asked about the extent of the required 1400 West improvements. R. Holley
stated that they were required to improve the roadway to 800 South as part of their earlier project
approvals. Commissioner Lucero asked about short term rentals and whether they would be subject
to the short term rental requirements similar to elsewhere in the City. R. Holley stated that if the short
term rentals went into the proposed Commercial zone, then they would not be following the STR
requirements applicable in residential zones. Commissioner Lucero asked about the MR-12 design
standards and also asked about a deed restriction to accompany a rezone request to limit the range
of uses. M. DeSimone stated that the City adopted the PD Overlay as the mechanism to use rather
than a deed restriction accompanying a rezone.

Proponent: Brett Nelson, described their project request, discussed the project history and their
request for the Mixed Use Zone in an earlier action. B. Nelson stated that the most important, and
most consistent factor in the overall project since the beginning was to have the fitness center, boat
house, club house, etc., open and available for public patrons beyond just project owners as their
main intended use. This is a recreational amenity that should be available for more than just owners.
B. Nelson discussed the CC&R’s, the allowance for short term rentals throughout the project, and
the concern/feedback from buyers about STR’s which then pushed those towards the commercial
location. The character of the parcel where the assisted living site is being proposed was significantly
changed due to UDOT access requirements of 1000 West so they felt that single family residential
wouldn't work in this location. Mr. Nelson stated that they didn’t believe commercial was appropriate
here either, so they landed on the assisted living use and needed the appropriate zone to make that
happen.

B. Nelson discussed the PD overlay elements and the limitations of the LDC on the condo layout and
placement. He also described feedback from prospective owners that there was a greater demand
for more single family development, and that any future multi-family development should be moved
towards 1000 West. B. Nelson suggested that their anticipated design would be “mansion homes” so
that they blend better into the community. He stated that the proposed MR-9 would enable them to
develop and that the PD overlay was no longer necessary as the MR-9 would enable them to develop
multi-family consistent with the overall neighborhood.

Public: Jeannie Simmonds discussed the fact that this is a spot zone and the PD Overlay was
created to move away from spot zoning and provide some predictability in the process. J. Simmonds
expressed her opinion that the PD process would be better suited for this project site as it had already
created public support for the project.

Commission: Commissioner Peterson expressed that the general concern is the fact that he had
participated in a number of the PD process meetings, felt that was an appropriate process, and that
this request is a spot zone. S. Doutre expressed concerns about being able to review future projects
in a cohesive manner, especially when this is in the middle of a residential neighborhood. J. Lucero
also expressed that the previous annexation brought this property into the City as Neighborhood
Residential (NR-6), and this is a divergent from the earlier request. K. Heare commented on the new


Land Use Plan, suggested that a PD overlay makes the project more cohesive, and asked the
developer why the change from the Planned Development overlay discussion and why step outside
that process, especially when we had three different groups in general agreement on the project
direction.

B. Nelson expressed that there is difficulty with the PDO that isn’t readily apparent. It provides more
certainty, but forces the developer to front load the project, meaning that every building has to be
fully designed and engineered and ready to pull permits even prior to PDO approval. B. Nelson stated
that this locks the developer into a specific building concept and/or design with little ability to change
based on a potential purchaser. He referenced the project's CC&R’s and they determined that the
PDO process was replaceable by the standard rezoning process.

E. Peterson initiated the discussion about what actually would be required to be reviewed in the PDO,
the level of detail for each component, where the neighborhood input fits into this process, and the
reluctance on the part of the Commission to consider a spot zone. B. Nelson stated that they believed
that the PDO required extensive drawings including architectural designs of the buildings in order to
proceed and obtain approval. R. Holley explained the PDO process which includes both the concept
review and then the more precise, refined review. He stated that they have completed the first phase.
There was additional discussion about how to build-in additional flexibility into the PDO process to
allow for options in timing and design. M. DeSimone explained that the PDO is very flexible in that it
can be approved with a phasing plan, can be tailored to meet specific nuances of a project or
neighborhood, can still provide certainty for the public and developer, and will still require some
additional information from the developer because they are asking for an increase above the base
zone.

B. Nelson stated that they believed that pulling back from the original project proposal in the concept
PDO and moving from 160 condo’s down to a number of triplexes, the requested zoning was
adequate to handle that change. The basic concern revolves around the costs of preparing
construction documents at this phase and not allowing them the flexibility to change to meet changing
demands.

S. Doutre outlined the benefits of the PDO process from the public’s perspective. Provides a more
cohesive review process that offers more guarantees, more certainties and fewer surprises. M.
DeSimone discussed the PDO procedural requirements that includes a text amendment which would
lay out what is being approved, how it is being approved, the timing, the certainty of future
development, timing, etc., within the PDO. B. Nelson asked about how the PDO could be structured
to allow the development group some flexibility in the design of the short term rentals. M. DeSimone
stated the City could approve some level of flexibility for specific project elements and everyone
works back from those upper project extents, but that everything in the PDO is negotiable, including
the review process for each project type.

J. Lucero asked the proponent about whether they wanted to continue with a rezone decision or
withdraw the application. B. Nelson stated that if they didn’t need to go back and redesign the project
with architectural drawings, then they were ok with going back into the PDO process. Rick Nelson,
Willow Lakes Development Group, re-affirmed B. Nelson’s concerns about front loading the project
with excessive design costs, and also stated that they were willing to work together to move the
project forward.

There as additional discussions about the Commission’s decision options: approve, deny or table the
rezone, or the applicant could withdraw the rezone request. B. Nelson asked about where they would
pick back up in the PDO process. M. DeSimone stated they are probably close enough to formally
submit their PDO application. Additional discussion continued about what would be considered in the


formal PDO, the level of detail required, the range of options for development alternatives, and
procedural options.

E. Peterson asked B. Nelson, proponent, if they were withdrawing the rezone request in which he
stated they were withdrawing the rezone request.

Item Withdrawn by proponent.

R. Holley workshopped the July 10, 2025 PC agenda.

Meeting adjourned at 6:30 p.m.

Minutes approved as written and digitally recorded for the Logan City Planning Commission
meeting on June 26, 2025.
