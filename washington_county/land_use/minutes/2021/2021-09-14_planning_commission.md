Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting
September 14, 2021

The Washington County Land Use Authority Meeting was held on Tuesday, September 14, 2021
in the Council Chambers of the Washington County Administration Building located at 197 E.
Tabernacle, St. George, UT. Also, via electronic means through Zoom. Chairman Brandon
Anderson opened the meeting at 1:30 p.m. He led the Pledge of Allegiance and then explained
meeting protocol.

Commissioners present: Frank Leuck, Reed Scow, Slade Hughes, Mark Owens, Kevin Jones, and
Brandon Anderson.

County Staff present: Scott Messel, Community Development Director; Victoria Hales, Deputy
Attorney; Laura Thurber, Planning and Permit Specialist; Kurt Gardner, Building Official; Kyle
Layton, County Building Inspector; Todd Edwards, County Engineer

1. PUBLIC HEARING/ ZONE CHANGE AND PRELIMINARY PLAT. Review a request
for a Zone Change and 13-lot subdivision of Parcel 7205-E-DVF from the OST-20 (Open Space
Transition 20 acre minimum lot size) zone to the RE-40 (Residential Estates 40,000 square foot
minimum lot size) zone. The parcel is located in the Pinion Hills Development in Dammeron
Valley. The applicant is Pinion Hills Development, LLC.

Brandon Anderson stated his company is doing the engineering for this project, but he has not
worked on the project.

Mr. Messel explained the project, location and current zoning of the parcel. He explained that there
are challenges with topography and layout with how and where the road will be located. The
adding of the road will turn the adjacent lots into double fronting lots. He stated the proposed
zoning and plat is consistent with the general plan.

Discussion was had about the drainage study submitted with this proposal. Ms. Hales stated the
detention basin needs to be removed from Lot 1, called out on the plat as common area/open space
and owned and maintained by a POA. The applicant explained that the plat was designed to
mitigate drainage issues and a lot was removed from the original design in an effort to allow better
drainage through the subdivision. Mr. Messel displayed photos of the area to give examples of
drainage. The applicant explained that the CC&Rs will require height restrictions for the homes to
allow for views and a natural look to the subdivision. Ms. Hales stated that the final plat will need
to reflect that the property is being annexed into the HOA/POA where the utility infrastructure,
like drainage is common to this property and other properties in the HOA/POA.

Chairman Anderson explained the public hearing protocol and opened the hearing to the public.
Comments were made by the public regarding concerns over water drainage, flooding and fire.
Concerns were voiced over infrastructure needs to mitigate flooding and weather issues.

After all who wished to speak were given time, Chairman Anderson closed the public hearing.

2. PUBLIC HEARING/ ZONE CHANGE AND AMENDED PLAT. Review and hold a
Public Hearing for a requested Zone Change of Parcel STON-AG-HV from the RA-1 (Residential
Agricultural 1 acre minimum lot size) zone to the A-5 (Agricultural 5 acre minimum lot size) zone
and requested amended plat. The parcel is located near Pintura. The applicant is David Morgan.

Mr. Messel explained that this subdivision was approved years ago and the applicant now wishes
to change the zoning of the parcel behind the residential subdivision lots into an agricultural area.
The parcel will become a flag lot and does meet all requirements for a flag lot. The common area
is for utilities and has been separated out on the plat and called out. The utilities on this parcel
support the adjacent lots in the subdivision. The applicant is requesting an amended plat for the
changes and zone change to agricultural. Mr. Messel explained that intended use of the property
is to build 17 large greenhouses, but is intended to be used by family so they may not be bothered
by the impact.

Staff is concerned about the amount and size of the greenhouses, visual impact on the freeway and
could be sold with possibly more businesses in the future. The greenhouses will require a CUP.
The common area supports the home’s utilities in the front, so the parcel could never be sold
without supporting the common utilities and infrastructure and needs to be legally tied to the other
lots.

The applicants explained the utilities would be in common area and the agricultural area called out
on the plat and that the parcel has a dedicated water treatment system for the subdivision.
Discussion was had about the appropriate ways to call out the common areas on the plat.

Chairman Anderson opened the public hearing. No comments were made pertaining to the subject
and the public hearing was closed.

3. PUBLIC HEARING FOR ORDINANCE AMENDMENTS. Review, discuss, hold a
Public Hearing, and recommend approval of the proposed amendments to various Title 10 Zoning
Regulations, including definitions, Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) and Short-Term Rentals
(STRs), and determining when a license for a STR is allowed, and deleting the term “Tourist
Homes,”, and deleting “Planned Development Rural Recreation Grounds and Facilities” from the
ordinances.

Mr. Messel explained that there was a previously noticed work meeting held with the County
Commission and Planning Commission a few weeks ago to discuss how the county would like to
go forward concerning short-term rentals and accessory dwelling units. The process would
streamline the policies for short-term rentals, zoning, changing definitions, and setting up a process
for applying for a short-term rental business license. Discussion was had in the work meeting about

atime frame if the ordinance was passed to help the public understand the process for short-term
rentals, which includes registering with the state and the county.

Chairman Anderson explained that this has been something that the county has researched and
discussed for some months now. Mr. Messel explained that we have been looking at other areas in
and out of state that are tourist based such as ours to pull research and examples from.

Individual Planning Commissioners spoke about their concerns, suggestions, and desires for this
ordinance. Ms. Hales explained that Staff has been tasked to draft an ordinance with suggestions
from the Planning Commission and County Commission from our various public meetings and are
presenting that draft at this time for comment from Planning Commission and the public to craft
the policy that the decision makers would approve of. Policies regarding owner occupancy and
STR and ADU size limits are based on the size of the lots in an attempt to keep ADU’s and short-
term rentals residential and not hotel-like. Staff has researched other short-term rental ordinances
throughout the county and have pulled examples from various jurisdiction. Ms. Hales stated that
Staff is tasked by decision makers to draft ordinances that implement the General Plan and those
ordinances and policies can be amended as needed. If changes are needed in the future, Staff is
available to assist with any changes.

Chairman Anderson opened the public hearing and allowed comments from those present and via
Zoom. Concerns were expressed by citizens about the taking of property rights and whether the
county has the right to change the zoning for this ordinance. The need for public notices was
discussed at length. Citizens expressed positive opinions for the ordinance and the need for
regulations. The fire department in Pine Valley voiced concerns over the ability to respond to fire
calls, UTV dust in the area and the lack respect to locals by visitors at short-term rentals. Citizens
spoke for regulations and the need to have help available for local property owners and that
property rights are needed on both sides of the fence. The Good Neighbor Policy, Inn-Keeper’s
laws and the need for local property managers was discussed. Numerous members of the public
commented for and against the proposed requirement for short-term rentals to be owner occupied.

Residents of Washington County and investors with short-term rentals both spoke for and against
the ordinance. Income and retirement concerns were expressed by those who use short-term rentals
as supplemental income. Homeowners expressed concern about needing to sell the properties if
not able to short-term rent them. Statements were made about the need for areas that allow for
families to have reunions, gatherings and the need for sites to host IRONMAN and various events
participants and their families. Many comments were made about the desire of short-term rental
owners to be the “best neighbor in the neighborhood” and that the negative stories are not the
norm.

Issues with the proposed square footage limitations were discussed at length and suggestions were
made to revise the limitations in the draft presented. Questions were brought up about how the
square footage requirements were determined. Suggestions were also made regarding the ability
for properties to be “grand-fathered” into the new ordinance, if passed. Comments were made that
the ordinance seems to be exclusive rather than inclusive and would make it difficult for
individuals to gather and that the ordinance seemed to be one-sided. Suggestions were also made
that would allow the Homeowner’s Associations to dictate whether short-term rentals are allowed
in subdivisions.

property manager that is able to quickly resolve any issues that may arise. Some members of the
public, including industry members from real estate and property management companies
expressed the desire to be good neighbors as short-term rental hosts.

Several individuals asked whether they would be allowed to apply for rental licenses at this time.
Ms. Hales explained that in accordance with State law the County Commission placed a hold on
the code concerning “tourist homes,” short-term rentals and tasked Staff to draft an ordinance on
the use of residential properties for short-term rentals and therefore, no new licenses are allowed
to be issued at this time. Chairman Anderson explained that the County Commission put the
moratorium in place because of concerns expressed by citizens.

Comments were made by citizens that short-term rentals are generally well-taken care of and add
to the appeal of the area they are in, and the main problem is the individuals who don’t get licensed
because there is no enforcement. Examples of other local jurisdictions were also commented on
and the need for regulations.

Public Notice requirements were discussed and Ms. Hales assured the audience that the meeting
was noticed properly and cited applicable code sections concerning notice requirements. Ms. Hales
corrected the code citations circulated by Mr. Crowe. Mr. Messel and Ms. Hales explained the
“grandfather clause” and that any existing and licensed short-term rentals will be able to continue
to rent if the ordinance passes.

Comments were made about the “silent majority” who agree with the proposed ordinance who
don’t want to live next to this commercial use, and that limiting home size and owner involvement
is key to regulating issues with short-term rentals. Suggestion was made to do an open house or a
special meeting before the ordinance was passed. Chairman Anderson stated that there was a public
work meeting before the ordinance was drafted. A property manager suggested that short-term
rental owners and property managers should govern themselves by respectful property ownership.
Commissioner Owens stated that many other jurisdictions have adopted ordinances regarding
short-term rentals and that the draft presented today is more lenient than many other jurisdictions.
Ms. Hales clarified that property owners are able to transfer legally existing licenses of existing
short-term rentals to new property owners. Commissioner Owens agreed that enforcement is
difficult and questions were asked as to how to enforce.

After all who wished to speak were given opportunity, Chairman Anderson closed the public
hearing. Further discussion was had on the topic by the Planning Commission about implementing
suggestions and ideas into the draft ordinance. Commissioner Hughes suggested addressing the
owner occupied and square footage requirements. He stated that he is not in favor of this ordinance.
Commissioner Kevin Jones thanked everyone for their comments and contributions to the
discussion. He explained his views that both sides of short-term rentals are valuable and that the
owner-occupied requirement needs to be addressed. He also stated the difficulty of the situation is
that we are dealing with a large area, rather than an incorporated smaller area. He suggested the
use of a local property management company. Commissioner Owens asked Staff how the county
will track compliance and Mr. Messel explained that the county will have records of licenses, and
that it is Staff's suggestion to work with the residents and property owners to come into compliance
