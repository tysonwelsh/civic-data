       Project Update – Public Works

Trace Robinson, Public Works Director, reported on various projects within the Public Works
department. This presentation is included with the packet.

Mr. Robinson explained that the bike lane on 13400 South was originally going to cost the City

and takes 5 feet out of the park strip, then they could create this bike lane. He asked if this was
something the Council would like to pursue. He said that he would budget $23,000 from next
year’s budget and this would be built by UDOT.

Mayor Staggs explained that the City had received a grant for $259,000 and the match is
$20,300. He asked Councilmembers to weigh in.

Discussion Items

         Roberts Rules

Mayor Trent Staggs explained that he had four points he wanted to address in regards to
following Roberts Rules during City Council meetings.

    1. He asked Councilmembers not to talk to or direct questions to the audience from the
       Dias.
    2. He reiterated that Councilmembers must be recognized by the Chair in order to speak.
       Each Councilmember has a limit and must not speak more than once on a topic until
       every other Councilmember has had a chance to speak.
    3. He explained that when a motion is presented, he will wait until there is a second to the
       motion. If there is no second to the motion then the motion dies. Councilmembers
       discussed how much time would be appropriate to wait for a second. The Council
       agreed that 15 seconds would be appropriate to wait for a second to a motion.
    4. Mayor Staggs explained that he had spoken to both David Brickey and Ryan Carter
       about substitute motions. Some councils do not allow substitute motions. He said that
       he would like to allow substitute motions as long as the substitute motion has a second.
       The substitute motion would be considered first and then if it fails the Council would
       revert back to the original motion.

Councilmember Stewart asked if a failure to vote is a no vote. Mr. Carter explained that a
failure to vote would be the same as voting no to a motion. Councilmember Stewart asked what
would happen if nobody made a motion on a particular item. Mr. Carter said that the Board
Chair could call for the next item on the agenda. The Board Chair can also decide not to allow
substitute motions.

Councilmember McDougal explained that instead of a substitute motion, the Council could just
let the motion fail, have a conversation and then do another motion. Mayor Staggs said that a
substitute motion may be valuable in some instances.

Councilmember Stewart said that he supports substitute motions. In response to
Councilmember Wells concern about a failure to vote, he said that he has seen councilmembers
walk off the Dias if there is a conflict and the chair can excuse them from voting.

Councilmember McCay agreed that the Strategic Planning Meeting was very good. She

particular event was the 8th event of its kind. He said that Craig Scott’s message of emotional

at the capitol building tomorrow and plans to try to advance infrastructure with these cities

money from the state rescue plan and the state will offer a 70% match. He does not believe the

will be working with the City’s lobbyist. HB 151 imposes a housing requirement which could

because the acreage in these reinvestment areas is so small.

Public Hearings

         Ordinance No. 22-06 – Scooters Rezone, Proposed Rezone of 0.51-acres located at
         3479 West 12600 South, from C-N to C-G, Kim Williford, representing Scooters
         Coffee, Applicant

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that this was a public hearing for
an application to Rezone 0.51-acres of land located at 3479 West 12600 South. Kim Williford,
representing Scooters Coffee, is requesting the rezone to accommodate a potential drive-
through coffee shop on the property. The property is currently zoned C-N, Commercial
Neighborhood. The applicant had initially requested a rezone to C-PO, Commercial
Professional Office. However, during the Planning Commission hearing, the Commission
discussed the appropriateness of that zone, and recommended that the Commercial Gateway
zone is also consistent with the General Plan and more compatible with the proposed use of the
property. The Commission made that formal recommendation to the City Council, and the
applicant made the change in their requested zoning, as well.

Kim Williford, applicant for the rezone, from Scooters Coffee commented that her coffee brand
is really awesome. She said she is a big proponent of Riverton and appreciates the support
Riverton City gives for mental health.

Councilmember McCay explained that she has not received any communication from residents
and asked if the City had received any comments. Mr. Lethbridge said there were no comments
for this public hearing but did receive a couple of clarifying phone call, not objections. Ryan
Carter said there were no comments at the planning commission meeting.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember McCay MOVED that the Riverton City Council adopt Ordinance No. 22-06 –
Scooters Rezone, Proposed Rezone of 0.51-acres located at 3479 West 12600 South,
from C-N to C-G. Councilmember Stewart SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for
discussion on the motion. There being none, he called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as
follows: Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, Stewart-yes and Wells-yes. The motion
passed unanimously.
