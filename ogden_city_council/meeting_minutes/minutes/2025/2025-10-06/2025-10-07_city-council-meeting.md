Proposed Resolution 2025-17 certifying that prior action was taken by the
City, as documented on Exhibit A, meets the objectives of Utah Code 10-
9A-403.1(7)(A) regarding a fixed guideway station area plan (FrontRunner
Station)
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the proposed FrontRunner Station
Area Plan came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City Administration is asking the City Council to consider
a resolution that would allow the FrontRunner Station Area Plan to be certified under state law. In 2022, the Legislature passed
House Bill 462, which requires cities with major transit stations to plan for the land within a half-mile of those stations. These
plans must advance four goals: affordable housing, sustainability, access to jobs and services, and more transportation choices.
Instead of writing a new plan, Ogden can use the bill’s “previous actions” option, which allows cities to show that plans and
projects already in place before 2022 are still moving these goals forward. The law is meant to ensure state transit investments are
supported by thoughtful city planning—so stations are connecting people to housing, jobs, and opportunities.
          Senior Planner Rypien summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to highlight key components
and objectives of the Station Area Plan; for a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council member Richey asked that City Administration consider the City’s ability to meet some of the housing metrics
and requirements of the Station Area Plan. Mr. Rypien stated he will continue to evaluate those matters and will confer with
representatives of the Regional Growth Committee (RGC) of the Wasatch Front Regional Council (WFRC) to ask questions about
those matters.
          Chair White introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2025-17, entitled:
                   “A resolution of the Ogden City Council certifying that prior action taken by the City, as documented on the
                   attached Exhibit A, meets the objectives of Utah Code 10-9A-103.1(7)(A) regarding a fixed guideway station
                   area plan.”
          Chair White then called for public input regarding the proposal.
          Linda Sanchez stated she reviewed the packet materials regarding this matter, and she noticed many references to high
density housing projects very close to the Union Station campus. She stated she thought the City Council had already made the
decision to prohibit high density housing in that area and she asked for clarification on that matter. She also noted that Wall
Avenue Corridor Plan has not been approved yet and she wondered with the Council would proceed with action on the
FrontRunner Station Area Plan before approval of the Wall Avenue Corridor Plan. She added that she is also confused about the
plans for bridge reconfiguration on 24 th Street and she asked for additional information about that issue.
          No additional persons came forward to be heard
          Chair White asked Mr. Rypien to address Ms. Sanchez’s questions. Mr. Rypien stated that the framework plan that has
been adopted for the Union Station Campus limits building heights in the surrounding area; while the FrontRunner Station Area
Plan does include allowances for high density housing, the Union Station framework limits tall housing buildings. He then
addressed the question about the 24th Street viaduct reconfiguration, which is addressed by the MakeOgden Downtown Plan; plans
are to lower the bridge to Wall Avenue, but that must be approved and funded by the Utah Department of Transportation and no
progress has been made to that end. He concluded by noting that any action to approve the Station Area Plan must highlight any
progress made towards adopting the Wall Avenue Corridor Plan; although that Plan has not yet been adopted, the City has been
making progress towards adoption. Mayor Nadolski added that there has been a conscious effort to focus residential density in
downtown Ogden and around major transit hubs and corridors as this is considered to be responsible planning. If those decisions
are not made now by the City, they will be made for the City by a higher government power.

Proposed Resolution 2025-18 adopting the Middle-Income Housing
Program Guidelines
           A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed Moderate Income Housing
Guidelines came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Ogden City Administration is proposing that the Council
adopt program guidelines to allow developers of multi-family housing projects to utilize the Middle-Income Housing Program that
is part of the Federal Housing Administration’s FHA 221(d)(4) multifamily programs. This program allows an applicant to utilize
special underwriting and financing with FHA if the proposed development meets certain criteria. A project using the program
must have at least 50 percent of the units reserved for households earning no more than 120% of the area median income for at
least 10 years. Rents for these units must be capped to ensure the units meet the requirements of the program. In order for a
developer to utilize the program, the local government must adopt guidelines and thereafter the Administration must track and
certify compliance on an annual basis.
           Community Development Manager Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
expound on the proposed program guidelines; for a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the
meeting.

Public Comments
           Gina Shafer presented the Council with a petition from the Edgehill Neighborhood Coalition; she noted she represents 91
percent of the households on her street who have signed the petition opposing the proposed traffic detour through their street –
Edgehill Drive. She spoke of the nature of the neighborhood and the presence of young children and indicated the residents there
are being asked to absorb commuter traffic that cuts between Weber State University (WSU) and the Dee Events Center that was
deemed unsafe for Taylor Avenue. Taylor Avenue benefits from sidewalks, two bike lane shoulders, and multiple speed monitor
signs; Edgehill has none of these protections. She expounded on the concerns of herself and other residents and stressed that
public safety is not negotiable.
           The following individuals echoed the sentiments communicated by Ms. Shaefer and asked that the Council take action to
protect their neighborhood: Janetta Kramer, William Shafer, Sean May, Brianna McMullen, Kalie Copenhaver, Chandler
Copenhaver, Logan Iverson, Phil Nastoriak, Autumn Iverson, and Royce McMullin.
           Heath Satow stated he and many others were devastated by the news of the deadly shooting near the Ben Lomond
campus today. He then stated he is a strong supporter of local businesses, which includes the local newspaper – the Standard-
Examiner. He also fully supports the Mayor’s efforts to better interface with Ogden residents, and he genuinely believes the new
newsletter is a step in the right direction. However, he has been concerned about the execution; a City publication should be
clearly defined as just that and calling it Ogden City News gives the impression of independent journalism where none exists. The
line is blurred when the company paid to print and distribute the newsletter is the very same company the public relies on to be an
independent watch dog. He stated his concerns are fundamental and lie with the erosion of the public trust in the government and
in the press. There must be a clear separation between government and press and the current arrangement will cause residents to
wonder if there is a financial motivation for the Standard-Examiner to keep in the City’s ‘good graces’. He then referenced
Council member Myers’ comments during tonight’s work session regarding the Union Station campus project; he noted there is
still a lingering lawsuit regarding the project and he advised the Council to look into that issue.
           Linda Sanchez stated she listened to the recent City Council candidate debates and she was pleased by the questions that
were asked of the candidates. She thanked the Council for what they do and indicated they are not thanked often enough. She
stated that she will miss Council member Choberka greatly; she reaches out to the Council with questions on a regular basis and
Council member Choberka consistently responds to her. She also thanked Mayor Nadolski for responding to a question she asked
him over the weekend. She also thanked Chair White for answering her question today as well but noted she does not always
answer questions as consistently as others.
           Theresa Bramwell stated she is very sorry to hear that a student was shot and killed near Ben Lomond High School. She
then noted the State of Utah had a homelessness convention last week and she asked if any representative of Ogden City attended
that event and will be making any changes in terms of how the City deals with its homeless population. She also referenced the
action tonight regarding middle income housing program guidelines; she doubts that there is a single person who earns a $102,000
per year that wants to live in a rental apartment. She believes that person would rather live in a home, something they own, and
she wondered if the program includes any incentive or funding for home ownership.
           Chair White asked Mayor Nadolski to respond to the public comments regarding the traffic detour on Edgehill Drive.
Mayor Nadolski thanked the residents who spoke regarding this issue and indicated he is committed to finding solutions to ensure
the safety of children in the area. He emphasized the current traffic detour is temporary in nature and the improvements being
made to Taylor Avenue will potentially impact traffic flow in the area. He then stated the purpose of the City newsletter is to serve
as a communication tool, not a form of independent journalism; other cities do something similar to what has been done in Ogden
City and that is because they are pursuing transparency and community engagement. The newsletter is a fully staff-written
document printed by the Standard-Examiner with a contract in place. He has nothing to hide and is concerned about the negative
rhetoric surrounding the matter; he will ignore this rhetoric and prioritize people and progress. He then discussed recent challenges
faced by the City that have highlighted the need for responsible decision making; the City is committed to responding to crises
that are occurring and wishes to provide safe and supportive environments for Ogden’s residents. He noted his thoughts are with
the family of the young person who was tragically killed after school and with the officers who responded to the scene. He also
addressed the negative comments about the City’s response to homelessness and middle-income housing issues in the community;
he expressed frustration with the lack of support from other cities in addressing homelessness in the community and hopes to see
them step up in providing resources and support in the future.
