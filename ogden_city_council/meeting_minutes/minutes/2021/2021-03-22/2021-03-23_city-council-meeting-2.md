        Autism Awareness Month
                  A Joint Proclamation declaring April as Autism Appreciation Month in Ogden City came before the Council for
        consideration. Council member Nadolski read the joint proclamation, after which COUNCIL MEMBER NADOLSKI MOVED
        TO APPROVE THE JOINT PROCLAMATION. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA,
        ALL VOTING AYE. Council member Stephens was not present when this vote was taken.
                  Chair Blair then presented the proclamation to Stacy Bernal, Managing Director of Awesome Autistic Ogden. Ms. Bernal
        thanked the Mayor and Council for this action; when she first began working to organize an autism walk in Ogden, she did it for
        her 12-year-old son. She did it because no one else was doing it and she knows the struggles that families of autistic, neurodiverse,
        and disabled people face. She wanted to help create awareness of those living with these struggles and to combat bullying that
        autistic children face in school; she was hopeful that her son and others like him would be able to make friends. Her 12-year-old
        boy is now a 16-year-old young man who is insanely smart, socially awkward, and a talented artist who can often be found
        wearing noise cancelling headphones and talking softly to himself. The mission of Awesome Autistic Ogden, while still a
        celebration of the beauty that is neurodiversity, has taken on a sense of urgency; autistic children grow up to be autistic adults and
        research shows they are four times more likely than neurotypicals to experience depression over the course of their lives. More
        than 70 percent of autistic youth have mental health conditions, including depression and anxiety, and their rates of depression rise
        with intelligence and with age. In a 2018 study in the United States, autistic people had a higher likelihood of gender diversity,
        with 6.5 percent of autistic adolescents and 11.4 percent of autistic adults saying they wished to be the gender opposite of what
        they had been assigned at birth. Autistic people have three-fold higher rates of suicide and suicide attempts than the general
        population, and autistic girls and women have a notably higher risk. Researchers also found that high functioning autistic people
        are at higher risk of suicide as they are more likely to get less support, but the most alarming statistic is that disabled individuals
        make up a third to one half of all people killed by law enforcement. Last week while providing a diversity equity and inclusion
        training, she posed the question "have you ever had a conversation where you had to tell your child to be careful around police".
        One attendee answered no and indicated their kids were good kids. She stated that her son is also a good kid; neuro-disabled kids
        are good kids, but sometimes the world is not good to them. She issued a challenge to all listening to try to be a part of the solution
        rather than the problem and to try to create a better world for people who are different than them; she encouraged everyone to try
)       to recognize what a sensory overload looks like rather than assume something different. Most people are aware of autism, but the
        community needs to go a step farther in building bridges of understanding, appreciation, inclusivity, and kindness. She again
        thanked the Mayor and Council for this proclamation and invited them to follow the Awesome Autistic Ogden Facebook page.
                  Council member Choberka thanked Ms. Bernal for the great service she provides to the community and for continuing to
        host events promoting autism awareness, even throughout the COVID-19 pandemic.

Utah Transit Authority (UTA). Beth Holbrook, a memberofthe UTA Board
of Trustees, will present the UTA Five Year Service Plan
           Utah Transit Authority Trustee Holbrook used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize UTA's Five Year
Service Plan. The Plan, developed during the COVID-19 pandemic does the following:
                •   Seeks to align UTA's service network with new projections of available revenue.
                •   Aims to achieve higher ridership long-term by aligning service with emerging travel patterns.
                •   Works to strengthen customer confidence in UTA by ensuring that public transit is available when and where
                    people need it.
           Vice Chair White asked if route frequency will be increased over the next five years according to this Plan. UTA
Planning Director Hanson stated different services are designed to meet different markets and demands. She stated there are
express routes and commuter routes, both of which will be improved upon in the long term. Some routes have been adjusted to
provide aU-day service based upon demand, but overall, the hours of service and frequency has increased for most of the routes in
the City. She added that UTA will continue to work with City planners to ensure they are providing service responsive to the
growth in the community. Vice Chair White then asked for a brief synopsis of why the Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) project is
important for Ogden City. Ms. Holbrook stated that the BRT project will connect to the Front Runner project and create a transit
loop in the community; the frequency of six-minutes between buses is incredibly attractive to students who do not live near the
Weber State University (WSU) campus. The five-year service plan calls for investment in infrastructure and increased
connectivity; these are key components that will send a message to landowners and businesses that they can rely upon that
investment for their own investments in the community. A dedicated lane is one of the things that makes the BRT project so
successful and attractive.
           Council member Choberka stated she appreciated the opportunity to take a tour of the BRT project in Utah County; she
noted there are many design elements for the Ogden BRT project that she greatly appreciates, and she feels that the project will
benefit the community. She then asked if the BRT pathway will be maintained in the winter months to keep the dedicated lane
clear and to ensure that riders can easily access the bus at designated stops. Ms. Hanson stated the project will include a snow melt
system that is intended to keep the dedicated lane and stops clear.
           Council member Lopez asked Ms. Holbrook to talk about improvements in electric technology for public transit. Ms.
Holbrook stated there will be multiple charging stations throughout the City; electricity will connect to the top of each bus to
facilitate fast charging. Electricity will be generated outside of the Wasatch Front to maximize air quality. UTA Regional General
Manager Colman added that the buses UTA is currently acquiring have large 400-kilowatt batteries that will enable them to
operate for 200 miles on a single charge. The fast overhead chargers will make it possible to keep the batteries charged at all
times; these charges will be placed at strategic locations to allow for expansion of the battery electric bus network and to serve the
BRTroute.
           Council member Choberka asked if students will receive a BRT pass with their tuition to WSU; she also asked if there
will be incentive programs to try to get people to use the system a bit more. Ms. Holbrook stated that a free fare time period will
be implemented to incentivize use of the system once it is available.
           Council member Hyer asked if electric buses will eventually replace the entire bus fleet. Ms. Holbrook stated that the
majority of the current fleet is diesel powered, with a portion of the buses being CNG powered. UTA continues to explore options
and is working to determine a strategy regarding increasing the number of electric buses in the fleet; however, flexibility is also
important and that is why it is important to maintain a diverse fleet that can offer a high level of service.
           Ms. Holbrook then thanked the Council for their attention to her presentation this evening and for their support. She
invited them to reach out to her and her team if they have any further questions about the services provided by UTA.

    Proposed Ordinance 2021-15 amending the Fiscal Year 2021 budget in the
    amount of $1,973,650
              A memo from the Management Services Department regarding proposed wage and travel appropriations adjustments
    came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Council will consider setting a public hearing on a proposed
    budget amendment that transfers ($1 ,973,650) from respective Fund balances to payroll accounts in the General Fund, Enterprise
    Funds and Internal Service Funds to cover a 4 percent wage adjustment for full-time employees ($1,830,650). The wage
    adjustments are proposed to become effective July 1, 2020. The amendment also appropriates funds to travel accounts ($143,000)
    for costs associated with staff training. Salary increases and travel costs were not funded in the adopted FY21 budget due to
    budget uncertainties arising from the Covid-19 pandemic. A separate, related proposal amends the FY21 Salary Schedule to add a
    4 percent increase to each salary range.
