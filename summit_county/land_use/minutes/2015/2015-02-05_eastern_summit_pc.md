4. Public Hearing and possible action on a proposed Development Agreement Amendment 
regarding ridgelines at the Promontory Development – Amir  Caus, County Planner 
 
Planner Amir Caus said in 2001, the Promontory Development Agreement was approved.  In 
order to protect the ridgeline, certain design and architectural guidelines were created as part 
of the Development Agreement.  The Development Agreement requires the ridgeline to be 
surveyed from certain vantage points; however, those vantage points were never identified.  
There is a conservation easement on the property.   
 
Planner Caus said the applicant is requesting they specify the vantage points from where the 
survey will take place.  Per the Development Agreement, the vantage points will be from 
U.S. 40, I-80, Brown’s Canyon Road, and Highway 248.  Photographs of the Promontory 
Ridgeline were displayed from the different proposed vantage points.   
 
Planner Caus said the Promontory Development is a unique project.  Staff felt this 
application should also be reviewed by the Snyderville Basin Planning Commission (SBPC) 
because the ridgelines are viewable from their area.  Their only recommendation was to have 
these spots identified by global positioning satellite (GPS) coordinates so they are specific.  
This will help avoid any issues in the future.  A map of the existing and future development 
was shown.  Staff recommended the Planning Commission forward a positive 
recommendation to the Summit County Council.   
 
The applicant, Rich Sonntag, said the one point he would like to make is that Promontory has 
been around long enough that no one remembers what was agreed to 15 years ago.  That is 
why Director Putt recommended they put this in writing.     
 
Commissioner Henrie said this is an exception from what they normally do.  If the 
Commission should approve this as written, would it be exclusively for the Promontory 
Development?  They don’t have GPS locations for any other development on the eastern side 
of the County.  The usual requirement is if it is viewable from a major highway, such as SR

Question #1: 
What impacts does the proposed transmission line upgrade present to current agricultural 
operations?   
 
Planner Lewis said he visited the entire line along with RMP officials.  He identified three 
areas that the line will cross that are key agricultural areas.  The first was an accumulation of 
properties that has been identified as the Keyes/Sargent Property.  The second was the West 
Hoytsville Road near Geary Gravel Pit.  This has been identified as the Pace property.  Both 
of these properties were part of the site visit.  The third property was near Wanship (Bates 
Land Corporation Property).     
 
Planner Lewis said on the first two properties, there are already poles in the middle of the 
agricultural property.  On the Bates property, most of the poles are at the edge of the 
agricultural activity.  Staff did not observe any other active agricultural operations along the 
corridor.     
 
Question #2:  
What visual impacts are created due to the increased height of the poles? Where do the poles 
break the skyline?   
Planner Lewis said the Code specifically exempts transmission poles from the skyline 
ordinance.  There were three areas identified where the poles break the skyline.  These were:

is not conclusive, but Staff leans toward the belief that it is probably safe.

What impacts will the proposed upgraded transmission lines have on the potential change in

The line crosses over some houses in the West Hoytsville area.  Most of the existing lines 
stay away from the prime development areas.  There will be some impacts, but because 
language hasn’t been finalized, he can’t quantify what the impacts will be.

4.3 Height and Massing of Structures 
Promontorys building height restrictions are set to comply with County ordinances, the Development 
Agreement, the Governing Documents, and these Guidelines.  While these building height restrictions may help

are fully explained in this section) because a large range of views from U.S.-40 and/or I-80 concentrate

Generally speaking, Promontory homes cannot exceed a true vertical height of 32 feet, except in 
Retention or Ridgeline Areas where the maximum is 28 feet or whatever height may be achieved without 
breaking the ridgeline when viewed from the designated locations on U.S.-40 and I-80, whichever is less.

when that point is measured from the original natural grade directly below it.

ridge projection which sits at the downslope terminus.  Subject to County pre-approval, the Committee will 
make this allowance on a case-by-case basis after it has carefully considered factors such as the slope of the

3 Definition of Stray Voltage
Stray voltage is a steady-state voltage resulting from the normal delivery or use of electricity
which may be present between two conductive surfaces that can be simultaneously contacted
by members of the general public or their animals. Stray voltage is not related to power
system faults, and is generally not considered hazardous.
Neutral-to-earth voltage (NEV) is a voltage between the neutral conductor and the earth when
measured to an electrically remote ground reference point. Stray voltage is a special case of
NEV , as it is defined at a specific location. NEV is a normal result of operating any grounded
electrical system, including the customer’s electrical system and PacifiCorp’s system. NEV is
the result of normal return current flow through the impedance of the grounded neutral
conductors and connections.
Stray voltage is not synonymous with contact voltage. Contact voltage results from power
system faults, and is not related to the normal delivery or use of electricity. Contact voltage
can exist at levels that may be hazardous. If a tingling sensation or painful shock is
