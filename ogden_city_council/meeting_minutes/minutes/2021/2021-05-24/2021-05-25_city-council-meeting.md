Fiscal Year 2021 Budget Amendment- Corner Plaza, Public Art. and
Various Other Funding
           Comptroller Stout explained the proposed budget amendment will increase the FY2021 City budget by $1,110,785; it
recognizes revenues in the General Fund from fund balance ($525,075) and appropriates those funds to the Council ($7,100),
Legal ($80,000), Management Services ($38,000) Fire ($304,975), and Public Services ($95,000). The proposed budget
amendment also transfers funds from various Community and Economic Development Departmental accounts to the CIP for
Amphitheater improvements ($12 7,1 00). Community and Economic Development is also requesting additional funding for The
Comer art project ($30,000). A refund from a loan program and funds from an Airport CIP project are proposed to provide
additional funding for the Zoning Ordinance updates ($227, 100). The Golf Course fund will receive funding from miscellaneous
revenues and donations for building repairs and maintenance ($16,935). Public Services and Community and Economic
Development have received RAMP EZ grant funds for the Golden Hours Center ($2,000) and Union Station ($2,000)
respectively. Donations from the Union Station Foundation and UTA will fund the Union Station Professional and Technical
account ($209,975). And, fmally, the Strike Force is receiving grant funds for general expenses ($127,700). She concluded the
proposed budget amendments will be funded by the General Fund ($525,075), Capital Improvement Fund ($227,000), Golf
Course Fund ($16,925), Miscellaneous Grants ($213,975), and Strike Force Funds of $127,700. The total fiscal impact is an
increase in the FY21 budget of $1,110,785.
           Discussion among the Council and Ms. Stout centered on planned public art projects and a brief focus on the need to
include the expenditures in the FY21 budget rather than include them in the FY22 budget. Council Executive Director Eller-Smith
stated if there are no objections, Council staff will include an item on the next meeting agenda to set a public hearing regarding the
proposed budget amendments.

    Director Brown used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the proposed budget for her Department. She expressed

    Divisions located in her Department include Human Resources/Risk Management, Comptroller, Treasurer, lnfonnation

    increasing the City's social media following and reaching/engaging residents and visitors to the community. This Division has had

    pursued teleworking innovations. The Comptroller effectively managed all general ledger accounts in the City; received the

    study; implemented a program for advanced scheduling in the Fire Department; managed COVID leave policies and tracked
    teleworking; posted 128 jobs and hired 196 individuals; interviewed over 250 individuals; processed 4,946 applications,

    Judicial Council restricting in-person court hearings. This Division oversaw the creation of the OCJC Pandemic Response Plan.
    The Justice Court remains open and operating from day one of the pandemic while others were closed; the safety of all patrons
    and personnel is paramount and enforced through the implementation of new service methodology. This Division has effectively
    adapted to the pandemic and made sure that service levels did not suffer. The City Recorder's Office has also played a vital role in

          Police Department: Police Chief Young and Department Administration used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
discuss the FY22 proposed budget for his Department. He presented the mission statement and organizational chart for the
Department, noting that the City currently employs 252 total personnel in the Police Department. He then discussed the Project
Safe Neighborhoods program, which focuses on violent career criminals and federal level crimes. It is a collaborative effort with
many other law enforcement partners in the State and nationally. The program has seen 50 defendants sentenced for a total of
145.7 5 years in federal prison. Ninety-nine individuals are pending sentencing or trial and 191 firearms and great deal of narcotics
have been seized. He discussed the Area Tactical Analysis Center (ATAC), which saw a 23 percent increase in activity over 2019.
Requests for information (RFis) processed by the ATAC in 2020 included proactive intelligence bulletins, cell phone extractions,
real-time intelligence products, person profiles, and statistical reports. Weber County Sheriff Ryan Arbon has committed a full-
time sworn position to the ATAC; the center provides support on homicide investigations, manages ongoing threat assessments,
and assists in child kidnapping cases. The Department also manages AIR-TAC UAS operations, which utilizes drones to perform
surveillance. Over the past year there were 91 total flights aiding in accident reconstruction, search and rescue, SWAT overwatch,
community event patrol, overwatch on structure fires, training, and monitoring unsheltered persons. The Department also
introduced the use of automated license plate readers (ALPR) and he cited a2020 child kidnapping case and homicide case, both
of which were solved with the assistance of the ALPR. The ALPRhas also been very helpful in addressing vehicle burglaries and
property thefts throughout the City and at local businesses. He then presented a chart illustrating the decrease in part one crime
from2019 to 2020 and noted thisreduction took place as population increased by .38 percent. The decrease from 2016 to 2020 is
even more dramatic at a total of 30.3 percent, though crime nationwide continues to increase. He then congratulated the traffic
bureau of the Police Department for being recognized during the Zero Fatalities Virtual Safety Summit for the #DriveSafeOgden
campaign and video series. The City has seen an overall3 .01 percent increase in enforcement activity, which has a direct impact
on the 7.57 percent decrease in traffic accidents. He also reported on community policing activities and homeless related activity,
which has increased by 26.97 percent from 2019 to 2020. He relayed success stories associated with the work that has been done
by the Department's Homeless Advocate and stated it is very important for the City to continue to focus on improving the lives of
people experiencing homelessness in the community. He then reported one of the goals of the Department is to be the best trained
in Utah, if not the United States, and the employees are working towards meeting that goal by engaging in many different types of
training opportunities, including: de-escalation, mental illness, diversity, force on force, firearms, less lethal and VirTra simulator
training, unconscious bias training, and defensive tactic control training. Recruiting and hiring is also a top priority for the
Department and a civilian recruiting position was added to the Department in January 2021; this employee began quarterly new
hire testing, implemented new background investigation software, which will speed up the process by 50 percent, attended
recruiting events at EOS gym, Law enforcement expo in SLC, TAP program at HAFB for retiring military members and multiple
POST academies. In the future, the employee will attend the Ogden City Career Fair, Ogden City Farmers Market, and visit the
Marshall White Center. Recruiting Officer John Thomas works closely with Diversity Affairs Officer Diana Lopez to attract
applicants that reflect the diversity of the community. He then discussed new position requests for the Department in the coming
FY. The Department's funding for crime victim advocate positions was increased as the grant funds used for that purpose were
reduced. Additionally, the Department will hire one new full-time sworn police officer using the grant award from the Utah
Department of Human Services for Homeless Mitigation. Before concluding his presentation, Chief Young summarized the
Department's COVID-19 response, peer support and wellness efforts, and future community outreach plans. Challenges facing the
Department include the continuing pandemic, bail reform, and the backlog of criminal cases in the justice system. Department
