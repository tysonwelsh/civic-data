 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Discussion of Council Compensation for latter half of Fiscal Year 2026 Budget
          Council Member Jacob said annual Council compensation was last set in 2015, effective
          January of 2016, at $18,000. He pointed out that a lack of increase over time equaled a
          pay cut and said the $18,000 was comparatively worth $13,000 in 2025 with inflation. If
          the compensation had been adjusted annually to account for inflation, Council
          compensation would be $23,978 in 2025.

          Council Member Shelton said he had a problem with the optics of an automatic annual
          COLA for the Council but did not have a problem with what a COLA would effectively
          accomplish. Council Member Jacob said he liked the idea of aligning a COLA for Council
          compensation with that of staff compensation, because the Council was not involved in
          recommending the COLA for staff compensation.

          Responding to a question from Council Member Green, City Administrator Korban Lee
          said West Jordan used COLAs and an annual market study and market adjustments in
          setting staff compensation. Mr. Lee emphasized that pay ranges needed to be adjusted
          every year to keep up with inflation and remain competitive.

          Vice Chair Whitelock commented that for her time on the Council was a service, not a job.
          She expressed the opinion that current Council compensation was adequate and said she
          would not vote in favor of an increase at that time. Council Member Bedore expressed the
          opinion that relatively lower Council compensation, compared to other communities in
          the area, would result in less qualified individuals willing to serve. Vice Chair Whitelock

       b. Discussion of possible revisions to the Good Landlord Program
          Senior Assistant City Attorney Patrick Boice explained that with the current Good
          Landlord Program, landlords received a reduction in fees when program requirements
          were met. He said the Council had discussed simplification of business licensing and
          discussed landscaping and beautification of the City as priorities at the recent legislative
          retreat. Mr. Boice proposed the Good Landlord Program as a nexus of the two priorities.

          Council Member Bloom asked if the program produced measurable outcomes and asked
          the ultimate goal of the program. Mr. Boice said the fees charged were meant to offset
          costs incurred by the City. A good landlord with good tenants and well-maintained
          property typically resulted in lower costs for the City, and therefore warranted lower fees.
          Mr. Boice said almost all rental landlords in the City participated in the program.

          Council Member Green said the program provided the City with a point of contact for
          rental properties. Council Member Green asked the State Legislature’s current view and
          said he would not want to use background checks and credit checks to make rentals
          exclusionary in the City.

          Community Development Director Scott Langford said staff had not had the resources to
          manage the program as intended. Code Enforcement Director Brock Hudson said there
          were 8,206 rental units in West Jordan (1,484 not including apartment complexes).

          Council Member Bloom suggested it was important to define the intention of the program.
          Vice Chair Whitelock commented that Salt Lake City and South Salt Lake City were the
          only other two cities in the valley with the Good Landlord Program. Vice Chair Whitelock
          asked when the rental unit fee was last studied and how the market study was done and
          asked if the City provided good landlord classes and whether the City charged for the
          classes. She expressed the opinion that in her neighborhood the Good Landlord Program
          did not work.

          Mr. Boice said the City did not have the resources to offer classes. He said it was his
          understanding that fees were last studied in 2018. Mr. Boice suggested an increase in fees
          would provide an increased amount to fund enforcement. Mr. Hudson said staff
          conducted a test of 101 properties participating in the Good Landlord Program. He said
          50% of the properties failed and received notices, and 49% had multiple violations. Mr.
          Hudson said all but three homes were in compliance by the time the process was done.
          Vice Chair Whitelock pointed out the City could accomplish the same thing through Code
          Enforcement without the Good Landlord Program.

          Council Member Bloom asked if a hybrid program that would balance landlord incentives
          with stricter enforcement measures and reduced administrative load would be possible.
          Mr. Langford said the discount could be adjusted to compensate for additional
          enforcement. He asked that the Council consider adding more requirements if they
          wanted to continue the program. He reported that some landlords in the City had started
          charging tenants to park on the rental property, resulting in tenants parking on the
          streets.

          Council Member Green commented that many rental properties in West Jordan were
          actually functioning as boarding houses. Council Member Green suggested the Council
          should comprehensively address residential real estate rentals in general, including
          short-term rentals, boarding houses, and rental agreements. He said the Council also
          needed to address who was responsible in rental situations for maintaining yards and
          keeping sound levels down.

          Mr. Boice commented that a majority of rental units in West Jordan were owned by
          corporate investors with management companies. Council Member Green said he
          suspected the City’s current Good Landlord Program was designed for mom-and-pop
          rental situations. Council Member Green suggested the City needed to throw out the
          current program and start over with a new philosophy and code.

          Council Member Jacob suggested uniformly applying better standards to all rentals with
          a uniformly applied fee (close to the current Good Landlord Program discounted fee).
          Council Member Bloom added that financial sustainability for the program and desired
          community outcomes would be the ultimate goal. City Attorney Josh Chandler said the
          Good Landlord Program was intended to provide a method to obtain desired outcomes.
          He compared the program to a carrot and enforcement to a stick and said the City would
          be left with only the stick if the program were removed. Council Member Shelton said it
          seemed the intent was to save the City money. Council Member Bloom said another
          intention of the program was to collect data.

          Council Member Green said the Council needed to decide what they could require
          landlords and tenants to do, who was responsible for what, and what the expectations
          were. The Council discussed where responsibility would fall in different potential rental
          situations. Mr. Hudson commented that a landlord not in compliance could be bumped
          from the program and subject to the higher fee structure. He said a lack of Code

          Council Member Bedore said the City was currently collecting less money and not getting

          to make data-driven decisions.

          Chair Lamb said he had the impression that the Council wanted to revise the existing
          program to be sufficient for both the City and the rentals. He suggested two or three
          members of the Council work with staff to begin a revision. Council Members Shelton and
          Whitelock said they were leaning more toward getting rid of the Good Landlord Program.
          Mr. Hudson responded the City would have one less Code Enforcement tool. Council
          Member Shelton said he did not believe the current program was saving the City any
          money. Council Member Bloom repeated her suggestion of a hybrid program. Mr. Hudson
          said the threat of losing the fee discount had proven to be a good incentive for compliance.

          Four members of the Council indicated support for restarting with a new program. Mr.

          Vice Chair Whitelock expressed the opinion that the current fee discount was too
          generous. Council Member Bloom said she agreed. Mr. Lee suggested that if the
          discounted rate were increased by $20 per door, the City would have enough to add a new
          position to administer the program. He emphasized that if a new program were going to
          work well, it would need to be administered well and would need funding.

       c. Discussion of possible amendments to West Jordan City Code Section 13-13-5 Water
          Efficiency Standards
          Vice Chair Whitelock said she and Council Member Shelton took the opportunity to ride
          along with a member of Code Enforcement. She compared a section of State Code with
          West Jordan City Code Section 13-13-5, where State Code language required water
          efficient plants to cover 50% or more of a project area at maturity, and no more than 35%
          of a front and side yard with lawn, but West Jordan City Code only used the word
          “suggested”. Vice Chair Whitelock proposed altering City Code to match State Code to
          require instead of suggest. She emphasized that a large area with only rocks really
          changed the ecosystem.

          Council Member Green expressed the opinion that requiring staff to measure properties
          to ensure 50% of a project was covered with water efficient plantings would be too much
          of a burden. Council Member Bloom said it was her understanding from reading the staff
          report that landscaping requirements needed to be revisited, some possibly loosened, to
          address enforcement and aesthetic concerns. Council Member Green said he would agree
          with language stating no more than 50%, but not a requirement of 50% or more. Council
          Member Bloom said she wanted a win-win situation for both staff and homeowners.

          Council Member Shelton said he liked the idea of 50% coverage. He said he planted sage
          brush and scrub oak in his front yard that would not require irrigation at maturity. Council
          Member Green pointed out most landscaping standards only applied to new development.
          Council Member Bloom asked if a change to the Code would make a difference with so
          many new developments having HOAs. Mr. Langford suggested requiring a certain
          percent of landscaping in planter beds would be easier to measure.

          Vice Chair Whitelock said the areas of concern seen on her ride along were existing
          neighborhoods with properties where landscaping had been let go, with only dead plants
          and dirt. She said she was told by Code Enforcement that staff could not do anything
          because of the current landscaping standards. Mr. Hudson commented that the cut-off for
          being considered new development was unclear. Council Member Shelton asked to table
          the discussion and move on to the next agenda item out of consideration for others in
          attendance.

          Mayor Burton said he believed staff supported requiring people to landscape yards. He
          referred to the concept of desert landscaping and suggested the term “landscape” needed
          to be defined. Council Member Bloom commented that some of the open space in the City
          had “desert landscaping”, and suggested if the City were to require a certain level of
          landscaping, the open spaces would need to match the standards.

          Vice Chair Whitelock said she would like to require some level of landscaping because a
          lack of landscaping brought down property values in a neighborhood. Council Member
          Green stated it was not the Council’s role to maintain property values. Vice Chair
          Whitelock said the Council kept talking about wanting to improve aesthetics in the City.
          Council Member Green said aesthetics needed to be balanced with property rights.
          Council Member Jacob said the government had an interest in requiring trees along the
          street and walkways to be trimmed to a certain height but emphasized that the
          government needed good reasons for such requirements.

          Council Member Jacob suggested City Code Section 13-13-10 was more applicable to the
          discussion. Council Member Bloom said she would like to see suggested changes from staff
          that would improve the situation for both staff and homeowners. Mr. Langford said staff
          would bring back suggested changes to the Committee of the Whole. Council Members
          Shelton, Whitelock, Green, and Bloom indicated agreement.

       d. Discussion of 4 Dimonds Development – 3009 West 7800 South
          Vice Chair Whitelock said she and Council Member Green had seen the subject property
          on 7800 South as an opportunity to test getting community input earlier in the

            development process. She said a town hall was held with about 20 individuals in
            attendance. Vice Chair Whitelock said residents with yards adjacent to the property
            reported occasional flooding because of a retention basin that did not always retain. She
            said residents present at the town hall indicated they would prefer to see homes on the
            property than commercial, indicated they had a problem with the proposed density, were
            intrigued by the live-work idea, and were not necessarily opposed to townhomes, just the
            proposed quantity. She said residents in attendance expressed concern for open space on
            the subject property.

            Vice Chair Whitelock pointed out that once something was built, it was not likely to go
            away. She said she wanted the Council to think about how the City should look long-term,
            and said recreation areas needed to be provided. Vice Chair Whitelock said she knew the
            developer was interested in knowing what the Council would like for the property.

            Council Member Bloom pointed out the property was currently zoned SC2, which did not
            allow residential. She asked if the proposed concept aligned or deviated from the
            Council’s vision for the area in the long term. Vice Chair Whitelock said Economic
            Development Director David Dobbins had expressed the opinion the subject location was
            a viable commercial spot. Council Member Bloom said the Council needed to keep in mind
            that allowing an exception would set a precedent.

            Council Member Green said he would want to wait to move forward until after the current
            Legislative Session, because House Bill 37 dealt with housing. Council Member Bloom
            added that infrastructure needs and requirements would also need to be understood. She
            said she was a big fan of live-work situations, but would want to make sure it could work.

            Council Member Shelton said the property was designated commercial in the City’s long-
            term plan, and expressed the opinion it should stay commercial. Council Member Bloom
            commented that things changed over time. The Council discussed water ERUs (equivalent
            residential units). Council Member Jacob said he would be willing to consider a plan that
            could work.

            Responding to a question from Mr. Langford, five Council Members indicated a desire to
            keep the property commercial. Council Member Green said he could change from
            commercial with a valid plan, but the ERUs were a big issue.
