        1.2      Mayor’s Report

     Mayor Kristie Overson reported that a series of videos highlighting the city and all that it had
to offer businesses, visitors, and residents. The videos allowed viewers to learn more about
Taylorsville and had been created at no cost to the City.

Taylorsville had recently been recognized for assisting Salt Lake County during the COVID
pandemic. In June the City would host an additional COVID vaccination and testing clinic just
outside city hall.

A Meet and Greet event had recently been held for residents to get to know the police department.
The K-9 officer was a particular hit with local children and Mayor Overson said she had enjoyed
watching the children speak to the officers and get their pictures taken.

The Unified Fire Authority Board had approved new patches for various fire station personnel to
identify which community they worked in as well as their affiliation with Unified Fire Authority.
(For example, those assigned to Taylorsville stations would wear a UFA patch on one sleeve and
a Taylorsville patch on the other.)

Taylorsville High School’s Jr. ROTC program had recently held a change of command ceremony,
wherein the graduating senior cadet passed command of the unit to the incoming cadet commander
for the upcoming school year. Mayor Overson was honored to attend the event and present an
award from the National Society of the Daughters of the American Colonists.

3.      REPORTS

        3.1      Senior Center Report – Mike Potter

      Mike Potter, Manager of the Taylorsville Senior Center, explained that attendance at the
center had grown steadily each month with an average of 47 patrons each day. Meal service had
also been steadily increasing.
    • 2,727 take-home breakfasts had been provided since the beginning of the year
    • 3,900 lunches had been served during the same time frame
    • There would be a COVID vaccination and testing clinic for seniors on July 7 from 11am
        to 2pm
    • Volunteers at the Center would be celebrated on July 14th with a special luncheon

       3.2       Historic Preservation Committee – Susan Yadeskie

    Committee Chair Susan Yadeskie briefed the City Council about the hard work that the
committee continued to do.

Tombstone Tales, a play that would take place in the Taylorsville Cemetery was scheduled for
Aug 25-27. Although this was an annual event, there would be some changes in this years’ version.

Ms. Yadeskie had spent a great deal of time thinking about the direction she felt the museum was
taking. She listed three specific priorities that she had for the Gerard Home/Jones Dairy and they
had been identified back in 2018.
    • The deteriorating gingerbread woodwork
    • The condition of the masonry of the entire home
    • The envelope of the building

Costs of both labor and materials had increased considerably since 2018. The retail dairy store and
the pump house were included in a recent application to place the structure(s) on the National
Register of Historic Buildings. Once they were on the register, they would be eligible for a $10,000
matching grant.

In the last six months, committee members had been changing the way they reached out to the
community. Ms. Yadeskie outlined several projects that the committee wished to move forward
on but their budget was not sufficient for all of them. She pointed out that it was a city-owned
property, but the committee was made up of volunteers, and they were unclear what money was
available to them to maintain the facility. The committee was very grateful for the City’s previous
support, but she asked for more communication in the future so that the committee could have a

         3.3      Taylorsville City Cemetery Report – Lee Bennion

       Cemetery Sexton Lee Bennion expressed his appreciation to the Council for their support of

    • Cemetery Fund: $226,000
    • Redevelopment Agency Fund: $3.24 million
    • Capital/Parks Fund: $1.2 million

    • Storm Drain Fund: $2,076,000

Challenges to Overcome in the Coming Fiscal Year:
   • 10% inflation rate
   • 36% increase in fuel costs
   • Rising price of oil
   • Supply chain issues
   • Employee Retention

New Items to Be Aware Of:
  • An additional School Resource Officer (SRO) at Taylorsville High School (for a total of
       2)
  • GRAMA Coordinator for the police department
  • Transfer of $396,198 from the General Fund to Capital Improvements
  • A new roof for the senior center
  • 5% decrease in health insurance
  • Continued support for the Arts Council ($20,000)
  • A property tax increase yielding $337,000 in new revenue

Mr. Harrington explained the history of property taxes in Taylorsville, displaying a graph which
illustrated the City’s certified tax rate over the last several years. In 2012 there had been a 15% tax
increase with an additional increase of 29.5% in 2013. There was then a 50% reduction in 2014.
Despite what many people believed, increasing house prices did not bring in additional revenue to
the city. Instead, as house prices went up, the certified tax rate actually went down.

With a 10% property tax increase, a home with an assessed value of $340,000 would cost the
property owner an additional $15.44 over the course of a year. A home valued at $461,000 would
cost an additional $20.95. These were property values as determined by the tax assessor which
was different than what the home would likely sell for in the current market.

Councilmember Harker wished to confirm that the Capital/Infrastructure Fund was separate from
the funds that would be used for museum maintenance. Mr. Harrington indicated that was correct
but pointed out that $30,000 had been budgeted in FY21-22 for museum maintenance and the same
amount was included in the tentative FY22-23 budget, plus an additional $10,000 to match an
anticipated grant award. Councilmember Harker indicated that she would love for the Council to
earmark an additional $10,000 to be put towards critical museum maintenance/upkeep projects.

Councilmember Burgess was interested in getting specific cost information for those projects so
the Council could have a better idea of what was needed to bring the museum into the condition it
needed to be.

Chair Barbieri expressed appreciation to city administration for recommending a second police
officer at Taylorsville High. She felt it was very necessary. Councilmember Burgess asked Mr.
Harrington to explain for the public some of the reasons the administration felt a second officer
was needed. Mr. Harrington responded that calls for service at the school were higher than they
had ever been. Moreover, 9th graders had been added to the school and student numbers were
expected to increase even further in the future. The City was currently negotiating with Granite
School District to hopefully share the costs of the SROs. The District currently contributed about
$20,000 for a school resource officer but the actual cost of just one officer was $150,000—so it
was $300,000 for two. Mayor Overson explained that it was too important to wait for GSD and
she recommended moving ahead with the expenditure for safety reasons. Chair Barbieri agreed,
but reiterated her desire for the District to step up and contribute more to the security of the school.

Marc McElreath explained that he was speaking as a citizen and not as a member of the Budget
Committee. He appreciated that the tentative budget had been published for the public’s review

    • The public safety budget appeared to have been reduced by $44,000 from the previous year
    • He understood that the public works contract budget appeared to have been reduced but it
        was only because some funds were moved over to the storm drain fund. It was his
        understanding that there would be no reduction in services
    • He expressed concern about the City taking ownership of Taylorsville Park. He was not
        convinced it was wise, considering the perpetual costs necessary to maintain it.

    •   It appeared that wage increases were not equal across all city departments. He hoped that

Councilmember Burges responded that there had been many discussions about the park, and he

        6.2      Public Hearing Receive public input regarding Resolution No. 22-15,
                 approving the Year-End Amendments to the 2021-2022 Fiscal Year Budget

      Mr. Cowdell asked Mr. Harrington to present the highlights of the current budget (FY21-
22). He reported the following:
    • $4.6 million in amendments to the budget ($4.3+ million of that was simply recognizing
       the second half of ARPA money (American Rescue Plan Act)
    • Increasing sales tax revenue by $300,000
    • $4.5 million in administration was being moved to the “transfers” line item
    • $8,880,000 was being moved to capital projects, to be spent the following year
    • $125,000 was being added to the police department’s fuel budget

    • Various housekeeping items

attached and could only be spent on certain types of items. Councilmember Harker asked for
examples on approved spending for that money. Mr. Harrington responded that the administration
planned for it to be spent on parks, broadband infrastructure, roads, etc.

High pressure sodium vapor streetlighting would be changed over to LED which would bring the
City a return on its investment by reducing power costs.
