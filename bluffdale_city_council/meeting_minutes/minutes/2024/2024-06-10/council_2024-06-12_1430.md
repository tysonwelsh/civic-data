        4.1    Utah Taxpayers Association Overview. (Presenter Rusty Cannon,Presidents,


Utah Taxpayers Association President, Rusty Cannon, gave a presentation on the Association, its
history, and views on property taxes. He expressed his appreciation for the City Council's invitation to
speak. The Utah Taxpayers Association was founded in January of 1923. He found it humbling to see
that the issues they wrote about in 1923,such as government growth and taxes, are the same ones they
face today. In 1923, Governor Charles Mabey wrote,"I observed that the directing forces ofthe Utah
Taxpayers Association are comprised ofthose who can speak with authority on the different elements
of the tax problem: the farmer, the banker, the railroad operator, the manufacturer, the merchant, the
public utility operator, the mine operator, the homeowner, et cetera. This in itself guarantees the
independence of the organization from undue influence by any one group." The Association's current
membership has essentially the same makeup. They are a 501 (c)(4)organization with over 1,000 dues-
paying members.


Mr. Cannon reported that the Utah Taxpayers Association was instrumental in crafting Utah's Truth in
Taxation law in 1985. When their former president, Howard Stephenson, started with the Association
in the 1970s, local budgets, and tax rates were based on what the governments felt was appropriate.
There was very little accountability. Truth in Taxation was a result of high inflation in the late 1970s
and early 1980s, which Mr. Cannon compared to present-day inflation. They crafted a policy where
taxing entities would get the same property tax revenue they received the previous year plus new
growth, but it would be a set rate. If property values increase, tax rates go down,and vice versa.

The main drivers of higher property taxes over time are growth and local government spending.
Approximately 60% of property tax bills fund schools, 20% to 30% goes to the County and City, and
the remainder typically goes to special districts such as water, sewer, or mosquito. Based on these
logistics, the Association suggests that taxing entities look at Truth in Taxation, which is the process
of raising taxes every five to eight years. That generally creates a modest increase and avoids the
extremes of either raising taxes yearly or waiting 10 or 20 years until a large increase is necessary. He
understands that all decisions will leave someone unhappy, but believes that the correct long-term
decisions will diversify the tax base and help pay the City's bills.

The Association understands Bluffdale's position. It is a bedroom commimity without a large tax base
ofhigher taxpayers. As an example,a 0.10-acre residential lot might generate $2,000 in property taxes,
whereas the same size commercial lot could generation $10,000. It would behoove any city to ensure
that there is a large enough commercial tax base to pay the bills, and the Association does not fault
municipalities for trying to attract those taxpayers. He cautioned the City to not begin creating fees.
The Association does not believe it is the correct route and is concerned about potential legal
ramifications.


Council Member Aston asked for Mr. Cannon's thoughts on the previous referendums in Bluffdale and
the current proposed tax increase. Mr. Cannon stated that Bluffdale has not had a tax increase in five
years, so it is likely time for a modest increase. The Association understands that you cannot take the
position to never raise taxes. Ifthe Council feels that they have made cuts where possible, they are in
the correct timeframe to consider an increase. Mayor Hall thanked Mr. Cannon for the presentation.

       7.2     Ordinance 2024-14 - Adopting City Code Establishing an Administrative Code
               Enforcement Program. (StaffPresenter Fred DonaldsonX

Mayor Hall commented that the City Council and Staff have been discussing this program for years
and she was pleased that it is finally being realized.


City Attorney, Fred Donaldson, presented the draft Ordinance to establish an administrative process to
enforce Code violations. Previously, violations were enforced through the criminal justice system.
Residents who received Code violations would be required to appear before a judge and make a plea.
They would be prosecuted and possibly convicted of a criminal offense. The new program allows
Bluffdale to do a similar process that is administrative and civil as opposed to criminal. Many cities
have had success in obtaining compliance with City ordinances through these programs.

There are two parts to establishing this Code. First, the Administrative Code Enforcement Program
will be established in Title 1. A new part ofthe Code will then be adopted. Title 15, which details the
Administrative Code Enforcement Program.

Mr. Donaldson emphasized that the criminal process can still be used if needed. This does not replace
that process but rather supplements it. In response to a question from Council Member Wilding, he
stated that typically the first step would be the administrative process. If compliance is not achieved,
it is then escalated within the administrative process. If compliance is still not achieved, at that point
it could be elevated to a criminal proceeding.

Mr. Reid provided the example of a lot with out-of-control weeds. The City would first send the
landowner a letter stating that they will be fined ifthey do not cut the weeds. Ifthe landowner ignores
the letter, the City will administer a fine. Typically a second and third letter will then be sent. If the
landowner still does not address the problem, the City can cut the weeds and charge the homeowner a
fee for doing so. Currently, if there is a violation, the City cites the owner, who then must go to court.
If they are convicted, they will have a Class C misdemeanor on their record. Mr. Reid believes that
having a Class C misdemeanor on your record for a small violation is too severe, and he was glad the
administrative process is being established.

Council Member Lord asked how charges will be determined for violations. Mr. Donaldson stated that
the next item on the agenda addresses fees for violations ofthe City Code. For Notices of Violation, a
period is provided within which the violation must be corrected. If it is not corrected within that
timeframe, there is a $100 per day penalty until the violation is corrected. For citations, the fee is $50
for the first citation, $100 for the second, and $200 for the third and any additional citations.

Council Member Austin noted an error in the Ordinance. On Page 1, Section 2, "Saratoga Springs"
needs to be replaced with "Bluffdale."         He then asked for clarification on Title 15, Section
15.20.010.040,Inspections, which states, "Civil penalties accumulate daily until the property has been
inspected and the Notice of Compliance is issued." He asked if daily fees would continue to accrue if
a property owner requests an inspection but it cannot be scheduled for multiple days. Mr. Donaldson
confirmed that the daily fees would continue until the Notice ofCompliance is issued. The owner could
then go back to the administrative law judge and request that the additional fees be removed.

spike in revenues because the City requested a tax increase. Property values increased, and an
additional tax increase was requested. Since then, property values have continued to increase, and the
Certified Tax Rate has continued to drop to its current level.
