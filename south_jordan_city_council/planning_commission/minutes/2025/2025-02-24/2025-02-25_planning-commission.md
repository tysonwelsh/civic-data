South Jordan’s General Plan could not be clearer about the City’s commitment to
protecting its remaining natural areas. These precious tracts of land—designated as NA
(Natural Area) and AP (Agricultural Preservation)—serve as crucial havens for wildlife,
preserve our community’s scenic beauty, and uphold the City’s own vision of harmonious
growth. However, the push to expand the Mixed-Use Property (MUP) boundary at 515
Ultradent Lane threatens to encroach upon what the General Plan itself recognizes as
essential green space. (please see attached maps at the bottom of this document). This
proposal undermines the City’s responsibility to safeguard open land and blatantly
contradicts the guiding principles set forth in the General Plan.

Ironically, the very cover of the South Jordan General Plan displays the VERY PLOT OF LAND
IN QUESTION!

Please consider the following reasons why South Jordan City Management and related
departments must preserve and protect the general plan and current zoning with respect to
the NA & AP designated areas associated with the 515 Ultradent Plot:

   1. Upholding the General Plan’s Mandate
      The General Plan states unambiguously that Natural Areas are to be set aside for
      “habitat and riparian corridors in continuity to allow for animal migration, hydraulic
      flows, and visual breaks in the built environment.” These areas may include only
      minimal improvements such as restroom facilities, shade structures, and small
      outdoor classrooms. This guidance is not a casual suggestion; it is an integral
      element of the City’s long-term vision. Allowing high-density development—or any
      expansion of MUP—into this NA/AP zone would amount to a direct breach of the
      very plan that is supposed to guide responsible, sustainable growth in South Jordan.

   2. Preserving Wildlife Habitat and Riparian Corridors
      South Jordan’s natural areas are havens for deer, foxes, coyotes, and other wildlife
      seen daily in these corridors. Once lost, these habitats cannot simply be recreated
      elsewhere; wildlife depends on uninterrupted swaths of natural land for shelter,
      foraging, and migration. Fragmenting or converting these lands to accommodate
      mixed or high-density development would endanger the creatures that have called
      this area home for generations. Our stewardship obligation extends beyond human
      considerations, recognizing that these ecosystems and riparian corridors provide
      incalculable benefits to the environment and the community’s quality of life.
   3. Protecting Our Community Character and Heritage
      Rapid development can erode the very essence of a community if not carefully
      planned. South Jordan is proud of its balance between modern conveniences and
      preserved open space. By holding to the General Plan’s clear designations of AP and
      NA—especially for “visual breaks in the built environment”—the City honors its
      promise to current and future residents. These small, undeveloped parcels not only
      maintain ecological diversity but also enrich the character of our neighborhoods
      and provide our families with natural retreats for recreation and education.

   4. Recognizing the Value of AP (Agricultural Preservation) portion of the plot.
      Agricultural lands foster local food production, promote self-reliance, and preserve
      a crucial piece of South Jordan’s cultural identity. By maintaining the AP designation,
      the City not only upholds the General Plan’s clear intent to safeguard farmland, but
      also ensures a sense of continuity and balance between nature and development.
      Allowing high-density construction to overtake these agricultural zones would
      irreversibly erase a historically and environmentally significant piece of our
      community, dismantling an important resource that has long connected residents
      to the land and its bounty.

   5. Ensuring Accountability and Public Trust
      The City must stand firm against private interests that seek to ignore or modify
      established land use boundaries purely for profit. When residents supported the
      General Plan, they placed their trust in city leadership to safeguard these final green
      enclaves. Approving a land-use change for 515 Ultradent Lane that expands the
      MUP into AP/NA territory would betray that public trust. It would set a dangerous
      precedent, signaling that even the most sacrosanct designations in the General Plan
      are vulnerable to outside pressure, further eroding the City’s ability to protect other
      valuable natural spaces in the future.

   6. A Legacy for Future Generations
      We owe it to our children and grandchildren to preserve the last vestiges of South
      Jordan’s natural landscape. Our community is already experiencing the pressures of
      urbanization; open spaces continue to shrink, and the calming influence of nature
      becomes more precious every day. If we allow the present proposal to push forward,
      we forfeit a piece of our collective heritage—a piece that can never be reclaimed
      once lost to concrete and asphalt.

Conclusion: Honor the General Plan and Protect Our Natural Areas
South Jordan’s elected and appointed officials have an obligation to uphold the City’s
General Plan, which explicitly prohibits the incursion of high-density or mixed-use
development into designated AP and NA lands. That plan was created with foresight and
reflects the will of the community—to ensure responsible growth while protecting essential
green spaces for wildlife, ecosystem health, and future generations.

We urge the City to uphold its own guiding document and preserve 515 Ultradent Lane and
its surrounding AP/NA land for the valuable riparian corridors, wildlife habitats, and scenic
vistas that the General Plan so fervently promises to safeguard. South Jordan must stand
by its stated principles and reject any push to prioritize short-term development interests
over the long-term well-being of our community and the fragile ecosystems that remain.
Let our City demonstrate true leadership by protecting these precious natural areas—
proving that growth can and must be balanced with the preservation of the land that
sustains us all.
