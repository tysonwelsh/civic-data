1. Discussion on Utopia Fiber; Roger Timmerman, Executive Director/CEO

Mayor Silvestrini said the Council heard a presentation from Jeff Christensen, another fiber
provider, and has heard from a lot of residents interested in fiber.

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 11 February 2019 Page 2 of 17

Roger Timmerman said UTOPIA was not a business, but an interlocal partnership between
entities. The founding members are Brigham City, Layton, Midvale, Orem, Perry, West Valley
City, Centerville, Lindon, Murray, Payson, and Tremonton. He highlighted a map that
illustrated the fiber network along the Wasatch Front. Timmerman presented a timeline of
UTOPIA and UIA. Phase 1 of UTOPIA began in 2004 and it was the core backbone, but
revenues lagged, there were higher expenses and few service providers. UIA is Phase 2 and
began in 2010. The revenues exceeded debt service, there were sustainable buildouts, lower
material and electronic costs, and strong service provider competition. Timmerman said
Millcreek’s only obligation would be to itself, not the other two phases. He said some smart
city applications would be available, including PurpleAir air quality sensors. UTOPIA is an
open-access fiber which means that the operator of the network and the service provider are
separate entities. Municipalities provide fiber infrastructure that can be shared by competing
telecommunications companies. With UTOPIA, there are 10 Gbps available for homes and 100
Gbps for businesses. He said UTOPIA has the highest customer satisfaction and is the number
one rated provider in the region.

Timmerman went over the financial stability of UTOPIA. He said Phase I is covering all of its
operational expenses and UTOPIA cities are paying for the original bond obligations. Phase II
UIA is covering all of its operational expenses and debt service, the net revenues continue to
grow, UIA is paying dividend back to member cities, and there is rapid growth with 1,000-
1,500 new homes added each month. Council Member Marchant asked about outstanding debt
from Phase I. Timmerman said it was $185 million. Mayor Silvestrini asked about the debt in
Phase II. Timmerman said Millcreek would piggy-back on investment from other cities. Mayor
Silvestrini asked about the take rate to pay debt back. Timmerman said the number of
households multiplied by thirty-three percent is what the contract would be for Millcreek. He
recommended sending a survey out to residents to see how many people would sign up.
Council Member Marchant asked about the extra take above 33%. Timmerman said the
additional take would cost UIA more to expand but would use the revenue to fund itself
without asking for anything from the City. Council Member Marchant clarified that this
partnership is not revenue generating but provides a service. Mayor Silvestrini asked about the
commitment to UTOPIA. Timmerman said they used to require a 20-year commitment from
residents. Since then, the structure changed to a 1-year commitment with a higher monthly rate.
Council Member Marchant asked what the average take rate is among all the cities.
Timmerman said the number is always changing, but about a 28% take rate across the board.
Timmerman illustrated to the Council with graphs that revenue and demand are up.

Timmerman went through what the potential partnership with Millcreek would include. He
said the only obligation is to the 33% take rate for the City. He went through example
subscriber fees and benefits of partnering with UTOPIA Fiber. The next steps would be:
community survey, Millcreek issues an RFI/RFP, UTOPIA Fiber would offer a proposal, the
City Council votes, the project is built, and a 2-3-year maturity of marketing to target take-
rates. Council Member Uipi asked about the mediums used to distribute the community survey.
Timmerman said whatever the City wanted to use. Mayor Silvestrini asked if the fiber would
be buried or would be above ground on poles. Timmerman said they would use what the City
had. Council Member Jackson asked if Millcreek would have a position on the interlocal
board. Timmerman said not initially because founding cities have taken on a collective risk
which differs from partnering cities. He said the agreement would act as a partnership, not a
membership.

e S.B. 129 Public Safety and Firefighter Tier II Retirement Enhancements — The Senate Retirement
Committee was in favor of the bill. The Utah League of Cities and Towns would like the
funding stream to change. Currently, the State picks up the cost the first year then the cities in
subsequent years. The insurance premium tax is generated by 2.5% that everyone pays on all
lines of insurances except medical. He would like that stream to go towards this fund.

e S.B. 139 Motor Assisted Scooter Amendments — is moving forward on the Senate floor. Mayor
Silvestrini said this bill proposes to conform provisions in state law that conflict with speed
limits on shared mobility devices.

e H.B. 31 Water Supply and Surplus Water Amendments - went through the Legislative Water
Development Commission in the summer and is waiting to be heard on the Natural Resources
Committee. Mayor Silvestrini said the bill requires entities that deliver water to be more
transparent on reporting for surplus customers.

e HB. 79 Interlocal Provision of Law Enforcement Service — allows the Unified Police Department
Board to hire a police chief for UPD that is not the Sheriff. This bill is on the House floor.
Mayor Silvestrini said it passed in the House last year and failed in the Senate due to time
constraints. Mayor Silvestrini said he does not have concerns about Sheriff Rivera’s
performance, but it is a good bill.

e H.B. 245 Community Reinvestment Agency Revisions - will eliminate the word “blight” and
change it to “development impediment” and the development impediment designation can now
be made by the development agency instead of the Taxing Entity Committee. The Economic
Development Committee will hear it Wednesday.

e S.B. 77 Tax Increment Amendments — Salt Lake County Auditor has considered tax increment
funding on projects significantly different that other county auditors in the State. It deals with
the value of taxes raised by entities when the taxes were raised and what the increment is. The
bill reconstitutes where the initial development year is, what increment is being developed, and
how it is to be determined and it is all based on the certified tax rate.

e S.B. 98 Community Reinvestment Agency Amendments - deals with the administrative fee Salt
Lake County has been charging. The project area would pay a one-time fee at creation.
Spatafore said there are negotiations and the bill will not pass in its current form.

e H.B. 5 Retirement and Independent Entities Base Budget - Ashley Spatafore said the fire
insurance premium has a hole, so appropriations are trying to fill it. There will be $12 million
ongoing and $17 million one-time. The bill passed. There will be another bill being put together
to fix state code to calculate that premium appropriately.

e S.B. 34 Affordable Housing Modifications - passed 2™ reading calendar in the Senate. There is a
$20 million one-time and $4 million ongoing appropriation. It provides cities options on how to
deal with affordable housing and they will go in the annual moderate-income housing plan.
Once cities do it, they will be eligible for TIF funds. It may undergo a change in the House.

e H.B. 164 Local Boards and Councils Structure Amendments - requires at-large members on
boards and councils. Mayor Silvestrini said it would be counter-productive to representation in
Millcreek and would double the cost of running at-large elections every two years.

e H.B. 59 Government Enterprise Amendments — requires a market study for competition with
private enterprise. Mayor Silvestrini said it cripples the ability of local government to hold
community events to have to pay for expensive feasibility studies.

Mike Winder brought up a Jordan River appropriations bill sponsored by Senator Harper.

Lilly presented the Council with an option for regulating residential dog boarding. He

proposed the following definition and standards for dog boarding.

“Residential dog boarding:” means a home-based operation where an occupant of a single-family

residence in an R-1 zone temporarily cares for no more than four (4) household dogs for a fee, dogs

being not less than four months old, and owned by others not residing at same address. Temporarily
cares for includes overnight boarding and/or care.

Business License Required

Residential dog boarding businesses shall maintain a Millcreek Business License, which is predicated

on meeting all the requirements below. Failure to adhere to these requirements or any other applicable

law or regulation may be grounds for suspension or revocation of the business license, pursuant to

MKC Chapter 5.07.

Area Limitations

limitation on number of dogs boarded based on minimum lot area:

a. Boarding of dogs on lots less than 6,000 square feet is prohibited.

b. For residential lots between 6,000 square feet and 8,000 square feet, no more than two dogs may
be boarded at a time.

c. For residential lots 8,000 square feet in area or greater, no more than four dogs may be boarded
at a time.

Pick up and Drop Off. Pick up and drop off of dogs must occur between the hours of 7:00 am and
7:00 pm. No more than two clients can pick up or drop off dogs within a 30-minute period.

Kennel Licensing. Residential dog boarding businesses shall maintain all licenses required Salt Lake
County Animal Services.

Animal Code Compliance. Residential dog boarding businesses shall continually comply with the
Millcreek Animal Code.

Insurance. Residential dog boarding businesses shall have in force business liability insurance,
separate from a homeowner’s or renter’s insurance policy, adequate to protect third parties from
the consequence of bodily injury or property damage arising from the dog boarding business.

Nuisance Abatement. Dogs that are boarded shall not constitute a nuisance as defined by 8.01.010 of
the Millcreek Code. If any dog in the care of a residential dog boarding business is deemed a
nuisance, such nuisance may be grounds for suspension or revocation of the business license,
pursuant to MKC Chapter 5.07.

Dog Licensing Required. Dogs must be licensed within the origin of their jurisdiction. Operator shall
have record of license and current vaccination records of all dogs in its care, as required by Salt
Lake County Animal Services.

Doggie-Doo Disposal. Residential dog boarding businesses shall dispose of all solid animal waste
daily and place the waste in an enclosed container.

Dogs are Allowed in a Rear Yard Only. With the exception of pick up, drop off, or walking on
public rights-of-way, outdoor activities for dogs while being boarded shall be limited to rear yards
only.

Fencing Requirement. The rear yard shall be fully enclosed with an opaque fence material that is not
incompatible with designs in terms of fencing materials used by residential properties within 300
feet of the applicant’s property. Fencing must be secure, including self-closing gates, to prevent
escape.

Shelter Requirements — In Residence or in An Accessory Structure. Residential dog boarding
businesses shall provide an enclosed shelter, with climate control, and smooth walls to protect the
dogs from weather conditions and minimize noise. Shelters shall be constructed of materials that
are water-resistant and can be readily cleaned and sanitized.

Standards and Limits for Rear Yard Shelter. Shelters may be within the primary residence in an
accessory building in a rear yard only, provided that the accessory building is set back at least 40
feet from any human dwelling, school or church located on an abutting property not owned by the
licensee, and 1,000 feet from any other accessory building used as a shelter for a dog boarding
business. Distances are to be measured from the edge of the shelter to the nearest edge of any
human dwelling, school or church.

Dogs to be Supervised while Outside. While outside of an enclosed shelter, dogs must be supervised
by either the licensee or designee, so as to prevent nuisances caused by the dogs, including, but
not limited to, noise and odor.

Humane Treatment Requirement. Dogs shall be treated in a humane matter, including, but not
limited to, adequate opportunity for exercise, freedom from restraints, hydration, food, protection
from weather and extremes of temperature, and protection from other animals.

Limited Number of Residential Dog Boarding Businesses in Millcreek. The total number of
residential dog boarding business licenses issued within Millcreek shall be limited as follows:

a. The maximum number of residential dog boarding businesses shall be calculated for each
Community District within Millcreek. Each Community District shall have a minimum base of
four (4) residential dog boarding businesses plus one (1) permit for every 300 single-family
detached dwellings within each Community District Boundary.

b. The total number of available licenses shall be recalculated biennially based on an estimated
number of single-family dwellings within Millcreek derived by the Community Development
Department.

c. Ifa complete application meeting all other requirements for approval is received after the
maximum number of licenses has been issued within the Community District the proposed
residential dog boarding business is located within, the application shall be placed on a waiting
list in order of the date of receipt of a completed application. The list shall be reviewed on an
annual basis. No fees will be due until a license becomes available. An application is deemed
complete by the Business License Official upon completion of all information required by the
provisions of this chapter, the Millcreek Animal Code, and the Millcreek Business License
Code.
