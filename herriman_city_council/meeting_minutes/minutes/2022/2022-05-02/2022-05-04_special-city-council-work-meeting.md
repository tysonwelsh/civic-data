                                   SPECIAL CITY COUNCIL
                                     MEETING MINUTES
                                     Wednesday May 04, 2022
                                    Approved October 12, 2022
The following are the minutes of the special City Council Meeting of the Herriman City Council. The
meeting was held on Wednesday, May 04, 2022, at 5:30 p.m. in the Herriman City Hall Ft. Herriman
Conference Room, 5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as
required by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council,
media, and interested citizens.

Presiding: Mayor Lorin Palmer

Councilmembers Present: Steve Shields, Teddy Hodges, Jared Henderson and Sherrie Ohrn.

Staff Present: City Manager Nathan Cherpeski, Assistant City Manager Tami Moody, Assistant City
Manager Wendy Thomas, Records Specialist Ashley Glines, Community Development Director Blake
Thomas, Communications Specialist Jonathan LaFollette, Public Works Director Justun Edwards, Planning
Manager Clint Spencer, Economic Development Director Sandra Llewellyn, Chief of Police Troy Carr, and
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi.

5:30 PM:
1. Call to Order
Mayor Lorin Palmer called the meeting to order at 5:33 p.m.

2. Administrative Reports
    2.1.    Discussion pertaining to the proposed Automall Development
City Manager Nathan Cherpeski summarized this meeting was scheduled the request of the Developer to
bring information to the Council for the process to move forward. He encouraged Council to engage in
discussion and provide direction to staff and the developer. Legal counsel representative Bruce Baird
detailed the four parcels displayed on the Parcel Exhibit Map. The sections discussed were the “Automall”,
“Office Flex” (North of Miller Crossing), “Garden Plots”, and “the Crescent”.

Legal Representative Baird started the discussion with the Automall/commercial section and asked the
Council of their understanding. He relayed his thought a Public Infrastructure District (PID) would be

   initiated to fund the infrastructure for the infrastructure as well as incentives paid through the Tax
   Increment Financing (TIF). City Manager Cherpeski clarified the TIF was the incentive for the end users.
   City Attorney Chase Andrizzi indicated the City received a letter of intent from the developer to initiate a
   PID. Councilmember Ohrn requested the information be brought to the Council for discussion.

   Legal Representative Baird offered a brief statement relating to the Crescent section, stating his team would
   like 121 residential units on the parcel which may need to be rezoned. He suggested this was the number
   one issue and hoped they could come to consensus if the density could be negotiated. He relayed their
   desire to also rezone the property North of Miller Crossing to allow for flex space for office and
   warehousing with the potential to allow retail stores. He concluded his clients proposed to construct a multi-
   family development on the Garden Plots. He acknowledged previous discussions regarding some interest in
   having a hotel be constructed on the site; however, noted his team had not been contacted requesting the
   use. Legal Representative Baird relayed willingness to place the development of the Garden Plots on hold if
   the group could come to terms pertaining to the development of the other parcels.

   Mayor Palmer suggested to discuss each section separately and asked if the Council had comments
   specifically pertaining to the flex space request on the North of Miller Crossing development.
   Councilmember Hodges expressed his willingness to consider the proposal as the developer looked to keep
   commercial space; however, would like to see more retail and tax-based uses in the area. He opined his
   would consider auxiliary services and wanted to maintain focus on the zoning and preliminary concepts.
   Councilmember Ohrn expressed concern with the idea of creating flex space, noting the concept seemed
   dependent on creating as much retail as possible. She paused with rezoning the parcel unless the group
   could come to terms with zoning conditions to require a certain percentage of the development be a tax
   generating use. Councilmember Ohrn opined there were areas throughout the City which would
   accommodate flex space in a more beneficial manner and suggested this location was prone to retail
   development. Councilmember Shields opposed to any change zoning from commercial based on location.
   There needed to be a focus on tax generating commercial space and noted there was no other commercial
   space which fronted Mountain View Corridor until reaching Porter Rockwell. Councilmember Henderson
   iterated his position had not changed and stated no new information had been presented.

   Mayor Palmer moved to the Crescent property and asked for feedback. Councilmember Shields suggest all
   the sections being discussed were not specifically part of the automall development and questioned why they
   were being discussed in conjunction with one another. He felt as though the automall was being used as
   leverage to get residential zoning approved.

   Legal Representative Baird disagreed an acknowledged the automall was a wonderful concept and
   highlighted the time it would take for development to occur. He indicated the development of the projects
   were linked together, not leveraged. Legal Representative Baird explained his clients were viewing the
   developments as single sets of property complementing each other. In response to a question posed
   regarding commercial zoning, Legal Representative Baird explained commercial development would come
   to fruition based on market demand. Councilmember Ohrn relayed her position it would behoove the
   Council to not plan for future developments appropriately, even if it meant the parcel would be vacant
   longer. She relayed the importance of maintaining long-term visions.

   Councilmember Henderson reiterated his position of not being in favor of the proposal. Councilmember
   Hodges highlighted some developments would take longer and was willing to wait for a better product. The
   City zoned the area specifically for an automall and was aware it would take time to come to fruition. Mayor
   Palmer understood initially, the Garden Plots and Crescent properties were not part of the discussion.
   Councilmember Henderson confirmed. Councilmember Ohrn suggested this proposal was more due to a
   financial need for the developer. Councilmember Henderson indicated an end user in the automall would
   not have any need for residential planning. The main goal was to maintain the commercial space.
   Councilmember Henderson stated the City helped the developer along through the initial phases of this
   project including extending purchasing contracts. He indicated it was never the intent of the City to change
   the development to residential.

   Councilmember Henderson concluded the deal would be the land and zoning, knowing it would take time
   and stated the City had gone to great lengths to help the developer on this project. Councilmember Shields
   questioned if the Council would consider approving concessions to have the automall be developed. He
   recalled a previous discussion where the outcome was concessions would be considered if the automall
   would come to fruition. Councilmember Shields opined the automall should be developed prior to receiving
   residential concessions. He didn’t want to agree based on a hope something would be developed.
   Councilmember Ohrn commented it didn’t make sense to build residential as there was still a need for tax-
   based development. The area being considered was zoned commercial for a specific purpose.

   Legal Representative Baird thanked the Council for the feedback offered and indicated he clearly
   understood their perspectives. Mayor Palmer emphasized the desire for the automall to be developed.
   Councilmember Shields clarified, on the crescent piece, he would not be opposed to residential being
   constructed if it had similar density to the surrounding neighborhood. Councilmember Hodges added
   resident response was aligned with the Council. Councilmember Ohrn opined residential was not a good fit
   for the area. Legal Representative Baird concluded with Council consensus to consider the residential
   development separate from the automall. The Council agreed. Legal Representative Baird expressed his
   appreciation to the Council even though they disagreed.

   3. Adjournment
   Mayor Lorin Palmer moved to adjourn the Special City Council meeting at 6:09 p.m. Councilmember Shields seconded the
   motion, and all present voted aye.
