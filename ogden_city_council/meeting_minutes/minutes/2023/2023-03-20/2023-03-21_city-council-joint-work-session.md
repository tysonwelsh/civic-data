Presentation — Weber County Treasurer John Bond
John Bond, Weber County Treasurer, used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide information regarding the
growing disparity between the values of residential properties, commercial, personal, and agricultural properties, and centrally
assessed properties as a percentage of all property tax revenues. He emphasized key trends over the past five years, including
dramatic increases in residential property values, while the same was not true for commercial property. He also discussed the
State’s tax laws, which are aimed at providing revenue neutrality for taxing entities year after year unless a taxing entity follows
the truth in taxation process to increase its certified tax rate. He left with the Council a packet of information that provided
comparisons of valuations and total taxes for like properties. This led to discussion among the Council and Mr. Bond about efforts
to adjust the State’s taxation laws, including allowances for tax deferral under certain circumstances, and the impact that higher
property tax rates have on development in a community.

} Proposed Zone Text Amendment — Height Limits in R-3 Zones
Planning Manager Brierley explained the R-3 zone currently does not have a base height limit, though it does have some
limits based on proximity to other properties. The City Council recently considered potential uses on a vacant R-3 property at
3841 Jackson Avenue, which the Ogden School District had declared as surplus. The Council expressed concerns that a tall
building could be built there with the current zoning, and consequently, Administration asked the Planning staff to propose a
reasonable height limit. Planning staff proposes to set a maximum height of four stories, or 50 feet, for the R-3 zone and to modify
setback standards adjacent to residential/open spaces and for nursing homes. He summarized maximum residential densities
currently allowed in the R-3 zone and referenced existing developments as examples of the types of projects that have been
allowed based upon current regulations. He added that the R-3 zone also limits heights based upon existing development of a .
block within the R-3 zone; ‘ if seventy five percent (75%) of the linear block is developed with dwelling structures, (a new)
building shall not be taller than the tallest dwelling structure on that linear block regardless of the zoning height allowances.’ He
referenced the Ogden School District property that was the catalyst for this discussion and provided a rendering to highlight the
types of buildings and building heights that could be built on the subject property. He concluded the Planning Commission has
recommended that the Council amend the R-3 zone as follows:
e Limit height in the R-3 zone to four stories or 50 feet.
e Modify setback increases for tall buildings to apply only to buildings adjacent to single-family and open space
zones.
e Apply the same height limits to nursing homes in the R-3 zone as for other uses.
Council Executive Director Eller-Smith noted consideration of the zone text amendment has been scheduled for April 4 if
there are no objections from the Council.

Proposed Airport Community Reinvestment Area Plan and Budget
Community and Economic Development Director Cooper reviewed the proposed plan and budget for the Airport
Community Reinvestment Area (CRA). The Board approved the survey area in February 2019. The area consists of all airport
property within Ogden City as well as properties north of Hinckley Drive and east of Airport Road. The proposed project area
collection period is 25 years commencing on January 1, 2023. The total proposed budget for the project area is $119,316,079 with
Ogden City, Ogden School District, Central Weber Sewer Improvement District, and Weber County participating. He used the aid
of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the budget for the CRA, the timeline of actions to be taken on the CRA, key projects ,
anticipated within the CRA project area, development objectives, use of tax increment financing (TIF), and the public benefit of :
the CRA activities. He emphasized that eminent domain will not be authorized within the Airport CRA.
Discussion among the Council and Mr. Cooper centered on whether the projects pursued within the project area will be
aeronautical related; and efforts to work with Roy City leadership on projects that will benefit both communities.
Council Executive Director Eller-Smith noted the public hearing on the project area plan and budget can be scheduled for
May 9. Council member Nadolski asked if City Administration will seek input from the Airport Advisory Committee. Mr. Cooper
stated that will be done within the 30-day public hearing notice period.
