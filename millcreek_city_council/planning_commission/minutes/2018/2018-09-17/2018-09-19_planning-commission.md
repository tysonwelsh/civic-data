1.1 Consideration of ZT-18-009, Amendment to Chapter 19.73 of the Millcreek
Code, to allow the Planning Commission to Approve Additional Height Subject to a
Conditional Use Permit Location: 3089 S. Richmond St., 1291-1315 Gunn Ave.
Applicant: Cottonwood Partners & Solstice Development Planner: Francis Lilly
Francis Lilly said this is an amendment to the town center overlay zone, to allow the
Planning Commission to approve additional height subject to a conditional use permit for
potential development located on Gunn Avenue between Highland Drive and Richmond
St. This item also involves a recommendation to adopt a development agreement between
Millcreek, Solstice Development, and Cottonwood at Brickyard LLC regarding the
development of projects located at 3089 S. Richmond St. and 1291-1315 Gunn Avenue. |

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 19 September 2018 Page 2 of 19
These changes would allow the Planning Commission to consider a request for additional
height, subject to a development agreement. A development agreement is proposed as
part of the ordinance change to ensure that the City gets something of value in exchange
for the grant of additional height and the incremental effects of the proposed changes.
The zone would listed requirements for buildings with at least 100 feet of frontage along
Gunn Avenue which include ground floor commercial frontage, front yard setback, and
maximum building length and facade articulation requirements.

Lilly went over the development history which included a city center stakeholder meeting
and Mountair Acres neighborhood meeting. The Planning Commission granted a
conditional use permit in December 2017 for Project 1 on 3098 S. Richmond St. which
was subject to a building height of 40 feet. The developers approached the City to obtain
greater building heights. Staff encouraged the developer of Project 1 to consider waiting
to develop so the City could develop a better street standard and an open space plan that
could better accommodate the density and mix of uses that was already allowed by the
conditional use permit in the underlying zone. The developer of Project 2, located at
1291-1315 Gunn Avenue, was asked to delay applying for a conditional use permit for
the same reasons. The general plan shows the brickyard area being a redevelopment area.
The general plan notes on the city center say primary uses are commercial and secondary
multifamily and gathering spaces, the building heights range from 2-6 stories, and the key
characteristics of the city center include a diverse mix of land use, space for local
community events and daily socialization, designed as walkable, encourages
redevelopment and infill, and provides employment opportunities. Lilly said the City is
making great strides on the city center master plan.
Lilly said the City retained a traffic consultant to consider ways to mitigate increased
traffic on Richmond St. and Highland Dr. Staff anticipates an increase in traffic on both
Highland and Richmond St., an increase of up to 5,441 trips. Both roads can

, accommodate an additional 17,500 daily trips. The traffic consultant is considering other
measures, including roundabouts and pedestrian activated stoplights on Elgin Avenue at
Richmond and Highland. Traffic improvements will be installed as part of the overall
improvements to the area. Project 1 (Cottonwood at Brickyard) will change by an
increase of 142 dwellings, 1,819 square feet of commercial space, twice the commercial
frontage along Richmond Ave., angled parking along Gunn Ave., and 27 feet in height on
the east end and 35 feet on the west end of the project. Mike Winder said the height
would be lower in height if the structure did not have commercial on the bottom floor.
Lilly said Project 2 (Solstice Multifamily) on 1291-1315 Gunn Ave. would change by an
increase of 90 dwellings, 8,500 square feet of commercial space, twice the commercial
frontage along Highland Ave., angled parking on Gunn Ave., and 12 feet in height on
Highland Dr. and 35 feet in height on the west end of the project. The summary of the
changes is an additional 232 dwellings, 35 feet in building height, twice as much on-
street parking, 10,319 square feet of commercial space, and an additional 50% of
Highland and 1300 E. frontages. The developers would get these benefits in exchange for
the development agreement conditions, which include a revised Mountair streetscape
including a barrier that would be installed by the developers but owned by Millcreek, a
public plaza on Gunn Ave. owned by the developer with a city easement on it, a complete
street with angled parking and an 8-foot sidewalk, parking limits to prohibit resident and

All four community councils reviewed the proposed ordinance changes along with the
development agreement conditions. Canyon Rim Citizens’ Association, Mount Olympus
Community Council, and East Mill Creek Community Council unanimously
recommended approval of the proposed ordinance change and development agreement.
Millcreek Community Council voted four for the proposal and four against it. The four
against expressed concern about building heights being out of character with the pattern
of development in the area, as well as concerns about traffic and pedestrian safety,
particularly along Richmond Ave. Staff recommended approval of the proposed text
change and development agreement. Specifically, the development agreement provides
for the following:

1. An improvement to the Mountair Streetscape, to be constructed by the developer

with the input of community members. The Mountair Streetscape improvements will be

turned over to the city for ownership and maintenance. The developers agree to pay for

the improvements and pay for the maintenance for several years.

2. The developers will construct a public plaza at the south end of Project 1, to owned

and maintained by the developer. The plaza will be publicly accessible by virtue of a

public access easement. The design of the plaza will be approved by the City.

3. A Gunn Avenue streetscape that accommodates angled parking, which would

provide twice the parking than a parallel parking arrangement allows for. If the city is

to require commercial spaces on the ground floors of mixed-use buildings, the parking

needs to be designed to accommodate those uses. The City will ensure that the parking

is limited to 2 hours use during business hours.

4. The developers will construct the angled parking and dedicate it to the City.

5. The developers agree to impose parking limits in rental agreements, prohibiting
residents and guests from parking east of Highland Drive.

6. The developers agree to commercial space in excess of what is required by ordinance
— 100 percent of the ground floors of their buildings facing Richmond Street and
Highland Drive, respectively.
7. The developers agree to building designs that exceed what is required by ordinance.
In response to resident concerns about timeless materials and quality design, the
developers are proposing a series of materials that are durable and reflect the area’s
brick-making heritage. Moreover, the developers will not be using stucco or EIFS for
the visible portions of their buildings — these are materials found commonly on
multifamily buildings in Utah.

1.2 Consideration of ZT-18-010, Amendments to Millcreek Code Regulating Tattoo
and Massage Establishments Planner: Francis Lilly
Francis Lilly said that earlier this year, staff received requests from several concerned
licensed massage therapists (LMTs) and tattoo artists about the restrictions our current
zoning regulations imposes on the operation of their businesses. Among the issues they
raise is a concern that “tattooing and body art” and “massage” are not defined in the land
use code (although ‘massage’ is defined in the Business License Code), although these

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 19 September 2018 Page 15 of 19
uses are called out and regulated in the commercial and multifamily zones. Staff is also
proposing clarifying and modifying the separation requirements found in the commercial
zones for both massage businesses and tattooing and body art establishments. In light of
a series of meetings staff held with licensed massage therapists and a majority of
community councils recommending against the initial staff proposal, staff is proposing
two options to the Planning Commission.

Lilly said the restrictions apply to massage/tattoo businesses as a principal use. Massage
therapists affiliated with day spas, gyms, chiropractors, medical uses, hospital uses, or
other associated uses are not subject to the restrictions. Currently, massage and tattoo
businesses are not allowed in a C-1 zone or within 300 feet of an arterial/major
intersection in the C-2 or C-3 zones, and not within 500 feet of an established
substantially similar businesses. Massage businesses are also permitted in the RM zone,
subject to a conditional use permit, and limited by the following standards:

1. Hours of operation shall be between 7:00 a.m. and 10:00 p.m.

2. Each practitioner who is not an employee of the business licensee shall have a

Millcreek business license.

3. Neither clients nor practitioners shall appear on the premises in a state of nudity or

semi-nudity, as defined in the Sexually Oriented Business Chapter of Title 5 of this

Code; and

4. The premises shall not be used for any conduct that violates Section 58-47b-501 of

the Utah Massage Therapy Practice Act (2013) or sexual conduct that violates Title 76

of the Utah Criminal Code.
He said that tattoos also have existing use limitations. Tattoo establishments have been
permitted in the RM zone under the category of ‘personal service.’ Massage
establishments and tattoo studios are subject to greater restrictions in the commercial
zones than in the RM zone. Moreover, because Millcreek ordinance treats every licensed
massage therapist as a business entity, they must be separated from each other by at least
500 feet — this prevents single-use massage studios housing multiple therapists from
occupying a space in Millcreek, unless the space was grandfathered for the use.
Lilly said the effect of the ordinance is that unless you are grandfathered in, new massage
therapists cannot be within 500 feet of another. Lilly said there is a state code definition
that deals with the practice of massage. There is no city definition for massage, tattooing,
or tattoo studio. There are 6 tattoo establishments and 60 active commercial massage
business licenses in Millcreek. He presented a map of the City with the location of these
massage business licenses identified. Staff asked research questions of Unified Police
Department on incidents of prostitution and human trafficking and is waiting to hear
back. Lilly said there is an unfair perception of massage and that is what the restrictions
are based on. He went over the two options for the Planning Commission to consider.
Ordinance option 1 is to update definitions, adding massage establishments and tattoo
studios to office use, and adding limitations for each. Lilly said 3 of the 4 community
councils recommended adoption of the definition changes and eliminating all the
proposed restrictions and limitations for massage and tattoo establishments. This would
serve as ordinance option 2.
