Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report and stated that the request is for
an America First Credit Union Subdivision located at 12653 South 3600 West on 7.9 acres
of land. A property rendering was displayed. The zoning is Commercial Professional
Office (“CPO”) and requires a minimum of .50-acre development site as a stand-alone
parcel. The applicant is requesting a commercial subdivision and is proposing to split the
property into two pieces ahead of potential development. The Engineering Department
confirmed that no additional right-of-way is required on 3600 West or 12600 South prior
to plat approval. Because it is a subdivision, staff recommended approval with the
condition that they may slightly adjust the boundaries of the parcel if they get to the point
it needs to be moved for a potential user. The adjustment could be done administratively
so long as the lot still meets the minimum requirements. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that the
matter was noticed to neighbors within 300 feet. A comment was received from a resident
asking that the property be developed as commercial rather than residential.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the request involves a
Commercial Site Plan Amendment for Big O Tires located at 2154 West 12600 South.
The building is currently vacant and was previously occupied by Firestone. The building
architecture and site reflect the early days of Riverton City. A rendering of the property
was displayed. The parking lot is lined with an eight-foot vinyl fence along the east
property line shared with commercial and eight-foot masonry on the north line shared with
residential. Big O Tires has acquired the property and intends to make this their second

improvements and changes to the building to bring it more in line with the zoning and
current requirements in that zone. A rendering displaying exterior materials was
reviewed.

Mr. Prestwich stated that the current Landscape Plan does not include a sidewalk and
the Americans with Disabilities Act (“ADA”) requirement will not allow an ADA route
through the drive approach if there is another option. Alternatives were presented. He
reported that the site is legal and was approved at some point when it complied with the
ordinance. Because the proposal is to upgrade an existing building and the legal non-
conforming status, not all items are to be brought into full compliance with the Code. He
explained that this does not prevent the Commission from discussing issues they feel are
necessary. Staff reviewed the site plan, landscaping, and building elevations as shown
and recommended approval.

Commissioner Brown asked if staff was agreeable to the exterior details and colors.
Mr. Prestwich indicated that the ordinance requires masonry components and different
elements. Although what is proposed will be an improvement to the existing building, the
proposed details do not match what exists in the area currently.

Chair Rushton asked if the turn of the century elements are being built in. Mr. Prestwich
stated that if this were a new commercial property, the applicant would be required to
have three of those elements. He did not see any of those elements in the proposal other
than the brick.

Big O Tires Owner, Cory Coleman, stated that the rendering shows a darker image but
the actual product will be lighter. The exterior has been duplicated from another Riverton
building and the brick will be similar to several surrounding structures. Red brick was
considered, however, because the building sits behind the building next door, he felt it
would cause the building to blend in and get lost. He was comfortable with the ADA
addition to the front of the building and confirmed that he will re-asphalt the entire site
including the back area. He reported that he and his brother own several Big O Tires
stores and they strive to maintain a clean and aesthetically pleasing building to create a
positive customer experience.

      d.     ZONE TEXT CHANGE: RIVERTON CITY IS PROPOSING CHANGES TO
             THE LAND USE CODE FOR NOTICING PUBLIC HEARINGS.

Chair Rushton opened the public hearing.

Mr. Prestwich presented the staff report and stated that the above item involves a City-
initiated change to the zoning text. The State Land Use Code changed during the most
recent Legislative Session and they were no longer required to public notices in the
newspaper. This change will bring the City Ordinance in line with the State Code and
remove the newspaper requirement from the City Code. It came to his attention that the
note card notice has several different standards and this will harmonize it within the
Ordinance and create fluency. Rezone notices will remain for those who are 1,000 feet
from the edge of the property with all other site plans and subdivisions. Conditional uses
will remain 300 feet from the property edge. Staff will continue to provide notice on the
Utah Public Noticing website and on the City’s website. Mailings will be sent out 10 days
prior to the meeting. He noted that the Ordinance states that the applicant is required to
obtain place a notice on the property. That process has been problematic and
inconsistent and has created confusion for those visiting the site. He confirmed that they
would like to remove that requirement and rely on mailers and the City’s webpage to
provide public notice.

Commissioner Lefgren expressed support for the proposed text change and believed it
was long overdue.

There were no public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Lefgren moved to recommend APPROVAL of the proposed
amendments to Sections 118.135.020, 18.190.050, and 18.215.050 Noticing for
Public Hearings, as described. Commissioner Cluff seconded the motion. The
motion passed with the unanimous consent of the Commission. Commissioner
Breinholt was not present for the vote.
