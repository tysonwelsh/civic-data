   1.3. Presentation and discussion of the proposed Panorama Master Development Agreement
        (MDA) and amendments to the South Hills MDA and Rosecrest MDA – Michael Maloy,
        Planning Director
Commissioner Ferguson arrived at 6:12 pm.
Planning Manager Spencer informed the commission that representatives from Panorama (DAI),
Rosecrest, and South Hills Master Development Agreements (MDA) were attending the meeting to
discuss their proposed amendments and agreements. Staff are currently working on the staff
reports to have the amendments ready for the October 2nd meeting agenda. The location map was
shown. There are a few pods and areas that are a little different than shown in the packet. The
current land use was shown for Rosecrest with the number of pods and units labeled. The South
Hills land use map was shown with the number of pods and units listed. The unentitled land that
was previously owned by many different landowners was pointed out. Those areas are zoned in a
variety of ways but not entitled to any MDA’s. The unentitled areas are now owned by one of these
three groups. The three different proposal map changes were shown, the backbone roads will
remain the same with the access points to Mountain View Corridor fixed. An exhibit was shown to
detail how the developments work together but will not be part of any of these amendments. The
development groups have worked together to make the areas more developable. Commissioner
Rypien expressed his appreciation with the groups working together instead of creating a lot of cul-
de-sacs. The Panorama representative noted working with the other developers has been very
productive. Commissioner Sickles clarified the large white box on the map is Jordan School District
land and the two smaller spots are the new water tanks.
The Panorama development area map was shown. The site conditions might change the layouts
slightly as they are developed. Commissioner Sickles pressed if there was a time frame for the
Juniper Crest Road and Real Vista Drive connections. The representative reported once their two
items are approved it will take off. Community Development Director Thomas divulged the
anticipation is the City Council will require the road to be developed before any of the houses above
can be approved. Manager Spencer questioned what would happen if both development groups
were not ready for the same area at the same time, how are they coordinating with each other? The
Panorama representative conveyed they have agreements with each other to make sure they all
have the property agent authorization to create the lots. Conversation continued detailing the
agreements the developers have with each other.
The Rosecrest remaining land map was shown. A portion of their land included in the MDA is now
owned by the Panorama development. The proposed agreements for the three developments were
shown. Panorama will have 4 agreements including: general plan amendment, zoning map
amendment, new MDA agreement, and Public Infrastructure Districts (PID). South hills will have the
same 4 agreements but will be an Amended and Restated Master Development Agreement
(ARMDA). Rosecrest will have a 4th amendment ARMDA.
Commissioner Ferguson pointed out there are a few cul-de-sacs with more than 30 lots which is
against fire code. The Panorama representative disclosed there is another access road that connects
the two cul-de-sacs for emergency use that doesn’t show on the map.

The South Hills representative showed their land use map. These new changes have addressed all
the Council’s concerns. They wanted more units in pod 20 but Council wanted to cap it at 48 units
but allowed clustering to keep everything below the Camp Williams factors. The land swap was
pointed out for pod 5A. The Council had requested more commercial areas to be added to the pod,
so it makes more sense to put in apartments instead of townhomes. The decision was made to
request to update from the 2008 general plan to the new 2022 general plan. They agreed with the
city’s request to do the ARMDA to make it easier to read and execute. The community plan map was
shown. The Pod 20 units have been condensed, as you wind up the backbone road there is a mix of
different product types. Part of this development is an active mining area. The Zone 2 and Zone 3
water tanks were shown on the map.
Commissioner Anderson sought if the city had the tools to help coordinate with the developers to
create the roads to other properties. Manager Spencer briefed that the city has an ordinance that
prevents land locking up property. They must show a plan to allow access to the property. Staff
also have phone numbers and emails for the landowners to help communicate and work together.
Commissioner Sickles pressed if Staker would continue to mine during the developing process.
Director Thomas briefed they will continue to mine using the haul road to the south end that comes
out on Porter Rockwell Drive. The South Hill representative detailed the Hill Side Overlay protects
30-degree slope, because the area is being actively mined everything will be mined down to less
than a 30-degree slope before they build. They are asking for an exception of the overlay since it is
a mining site. Commissioner Jacobson requested they bring a map with the overlay zone shown to
the next meeting.
The parks, trails, and open space maps were shown. They have defined the locations for the
smaller parks on the map. There are two larger parks that will be developed by both Panorama and
South Hills developments: one around 9 acres and the other around 14 acres. The map showed 6
different kinds of trails including single track hike or mountain bike, a corridor for Bonneville Shore
Line Trail, and hard scape trail will be added to the development. Manager Spencer implored with
the huge elevation changes how they are connecting the trails. The South Hills representative
communicated they will be using switch backs or stair connections for the trails. They are working
on creating a master parks, trails and open space connection with Panorama, Rosecrest, and South
Hills. The master plan will be the first amendments to their agreements once approved. Manager
Spencer raised the question of revegetation of the native open spaces when they do all the grading
for the developments? The South Hills representative disclosed they will replant with native
vegetation and make it look like it has never been mined or graded. Commissioner Anderson
pointed out the bottom left of the map has no trails. Director Thomas informed they are waiting for
the parks and trails master plan, the Bonneville Shore Line trail will need to be installed in the area
somewhere, and they need to keep people safe from the continuing mining issues.

   4.1. Consideration of a Preliminary Plat for the Malibu Laguna Subdivision at approximately
        4700 West Juniper Crest Road in the FR-2.5 Forestry Recreation Zone and Hillside
        Overlay Zone. (Public Hearing)
        Applicant: John Lindsley, IV Development Partners (authorized agent)
        Acres: ±822.25
        File No: S2023-059

   https://www.herriman.org/uploads/ﬁles/4893/Item-4.1-Staff-Report.pdf
Manager Spencer displayed the Malibu Laguna subdivision vicinity map. The proposal included 65
single family lots, on 822 acres, and provided off-site improvements, easements, and access
documents. The lots are FR-2.5 zone with the minimum lot size of 2.5 acre and include the
construction of two water tanks. Engineering review shows water service is outside of the
current water service ability. There are plans for 2 new water tanks for zones 2 and 3. The lots
range from zones 3 to 8. There will be a process involved to get the water in place for the
subdivision to take place. One of the provisions was no plats can be recorded before water is
available. Zoning compliance issues included access being largely built by others, the plans
provided for off-site improvements for grading, and they have acquired easement for access
across the other properties, The lot width of 250 feet is required and being meet except for 5 lots
that will need adjustment. Slopes, grading and utilities are provided. The road grading and hill side
grading map was shown. The FR-2.5 zone and the Hillside Overlay are similar. They require access
to recreation, sensitive lands/hazards, steep slopes, grading/drainage, ridgeline preservation,
landscaping & natural vegetation, wildlife habitat, access/circulation, street design and utilities.
For the sensitive lands hazards the applicant will continue working with staff during the final plat
approval to identify areas of impact. For the landscaping and natural vegetation, they need to
provide landscaping plans for the road dedication areas. The pedestrian access circulation is
required for both sides of the dedicated driveway. Staff recommends approval with the conditions

   5.1. Consideration and recommendation of amending Chapter 10-15 Article E “Sports and
        Entertainment Mixed Use Overlay Zone” of the Herriman City Code (Public Hearing)
        Applicant: Herriman City
        File No: Z2024-108

   https://www.herriman.org/uploads/ﬁles/4891/Item-5.1-Staff-Report.pdf
Commissioner Ferguson excused himself from the meeting.

Commissioner Rypien moved to appoint Commissioner Jacobson as Pro Tempore Chair, Seconded
by Commissioner Garcia, and all voted aye

Community Development Director Thomas presented the proposed Sports and Entertainment
Mixed-Use Overlay Zone text amendment to chapter 10-15 Article E. The zone was created in 2017
with RSL coming into town. The approved zone was specific to what they wanted to create at the
time including a single facility for viewing and training, accommodate no fewer than 5,000 people,
minimum of 100 acres and included sign allowances. The proposal addresses council’s direction to
invite sport focused development, provide flexibility to allow complementary land use, support the
development of destination facilities/venues to encourage tourists to come to Herriman. The
summary of the amendment is to identify permitted uses, provide height allowances for
specialized structures, identify specific development standards (lighting, access, walkability,
parking, materials, architectural), and create specific conditions where special allowance for
signage may be applied. Staff suggested the commission also include an allowance for accessory
use for personal care services to a primary permitted use. Staff recommends a positive
recommendation to council. Commissioner Jacobson verified the exact location of the zone would
be determined later. Director Thomas relayed this item is just the text approval. The zone could
be applied anywhere in the city if the criteria is met.

Pro Tempore Chair Jacobson opened the public hearing.

No comments were offered.

Commissioner Garcia moved to close the public hearing, Seconded by Commissioner Oberg and all
voted Aye.
Director Thomas clarified for the commission the minimum is 5 acres. The Commission could
recommend a smaller acreage allowance. The intention in the code is 5 acres minimum.
Commissioner Sickles admitted in his experience with tournaments if they don’t have the acreage
they can’t provide the necessary parking.
Commissioner Oberg moved to forward a positive recommendation of approval to City Council of
item 5.1 Consideration and recommendation of amending Chapter 10-15 Article E “Sports and
Entertainment Mixed Use Overlay Zone” of the Herriman City Code and include an allowance for
accessory use for personal care services to a primary permitted use.

        applying the SEMU Sports and Entertainment Mixed Use Overlay Zone for property
        generally located between Miller Crossing Drive (on the south) and Midas Creek (on the

        (on the east). (Public Hearing)
        Applicant: Herriman City
        Acres: ±16.98
        File No: Z2024-109

   https://www.herriman.org/uploads/ﬁles/4892/Item-5.2-Staff-Report.pdf

Director Thomas outlined the proposal for north of Miller Crossing Drive to apply the Sports and
Entertainment Mixed-Use Overlay Zone. The current zoning is AMSD, the whole site is about 17
acres, 12.33 acre is under contract with the Auto mall development group, 4.65 acres is owned by
MLM Holdings. There is access from Main Street and Miller Crossing Drive via Mountain View
Corridor (MVC). The development on the north has supporting development with the Anthem
development, a hotel and pickleball. To the south will be the Mountain View Plaza development.
The concept plan was shown including baseball fields, basketball training facility, a potential hotel,
indoor entertainment, and potential signage for MVC. Staff recommends a positive
recommendation to the Council. Commissioner Sickles sought if the hotel would be allowed in the
new zone. Director Thomas relayed it is one of the potential considerations. Commissioner Rypien
pressed if a height maximum would be applied to a hotel. Director Thomas responded if the zone
was applied it could go up to 60 feet permitted and up to 125 feet with a conditional use approval.
Commissioner Oberg recognized this is still concept plan are there any connection plans over Midas
Creek. Director Thomas communicated there are plans for two footbridges but no vehicle bridges.
Commissioner Anderson queried would the detention basin be landscaped. Director Thomas stated
it would be required to be landscaped. There has been discussion about putting it underground for
