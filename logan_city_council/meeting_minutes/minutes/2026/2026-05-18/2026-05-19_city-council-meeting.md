Chair Johnson explained that any person wishing to comment on any item not otherwise on the agenda
may address the City Council at this point by stepping to the microphone and giving his or her name
and address for the record. Comments should be limited to not more than three (3) minutes unless
additional time is authorized by the Council Chair. Citizen groups will be asked to appoint a
spokesperson. This is the time and place for any person who wishes to comment on non-agenda items
and items that are germane or relevant to the authority of the City Council. Items brought forward to
the attention of the City Council will be turned over to staff to respond to outside of the City Council
meeting.
Logan resident Joshua Molitor expressed concerns regarding the proposed data center project in Box
Elder County and its potential impacts on Cache Valley. He discussed concerns about possible
environmental effects, projected population growth associated with construction and operations, and
the potential for increased development and housing density in Logan and surrounding communities.
He noted concerns regarding air quality and regional weather patterns that could affect Cache Valley.
Mr. Molitor encouraged consideration of ways local governments and neighboring communities could
work together to address impacts and ensure future development does not negatively affect the
community. He also shared broader concerns about corporate influence and growth-related impacts on
local systems.
Logan resident Gail Hanson expressed concerns regarding proposed data center projects, stating
concern that growth in data center development may be temporary and could result in significant
investments in projects that may not provide long-term value.
She also commented on decision-making considerations, commending the Council for evaluating who
benefits from proposed actions and considering associated prices. She encouraged additional focus on
evaluating broader costs and impacts, including long-term community and environmental effects.
Drawing on personal experience, Ms. Hanson shared observations regarding changes in community
landscapes and the effects of infrastructure decisions on the environment. She encouraged
consideration of the long-term impacts and costs associated with current projects and proposals,
including the proposed data center and Logan River-related considerations. Regarding the data center
she feels it’s a bubble and a lot of resources going into something that will stand empty.
Logan resident and former councilmember Herm Olson expressed appreciation for the Council and
City staff, reflecting on his prior years of service on the City Council and noting that he had come to
miss participating in and attending Council meetings. He acknowledged the work and effort involved
in serving the community, including the responsibilities and challenges that accompany public service.

continued efforts on behalf of the community.
Logan resident Linda Johnson commented on traffic conditions within Logan and noted concerns
regarding travel times within the city. She expressed appreciation for the Council’s work with various
boards, commissions, and departments, including reporting and communication efforts.
Ms. Johnson asked about communication and collaboration between Logan City and neighboring
communities within Cache Valley, including whether city councils or community leaders meet
regularly to discuss regional issues and planning efforts. She referenced topics such as land use,
development, and long-term planning, and asked how information and coordination among
communities are shared and communicated.
Chair Johnson responded to Ms. Johnson’s question regarding regional coordination; he explained that
Logan City participates in both formal and informal collaboration with neighboring communities and
regional organizations. Examples discussed included participation on shared boards, committees, and
advisory groups involving representatives from municipalities throughout Cache Valley and the
surrounding region.
He noted that interactions occur through organizations and committees focused on areas such as
recreation, transportation planning, housing, airports, and homelessness. It was also noted that Council
members and local officials regularly meet and communicate with leaders from neighboring
communities to discuss issues and collaborate on matters of regional interest as they arise.
Mayor Anderson added that City representatives also participate in regular meetings with regional
leadership groups, including mayoral associations and intergovernmental organizations, providing
opportunities to discuss shared issues and exchange information. He stated that efforts are ongoing to
strengthen relationships and communication with neighboring communities and county representatives
through both formal meetings and regular collaboration. He noted that while not all discussions are
reported individually during meetings, coordination and relationship-building efforts with regional
partners remain an ongoing priority.
Ms. Johnson expressed appreciation for the discussion regarding regional collaboration and
communication efforts. She suggested that brief updates during Council meetings regarding meetings
with neighboring communities, boards, and regional organizations could help provide additional
transparency and keep the public informed about ongoing regional discussions and partnerships.
There were no further comments or questions for the Mayor or Council.

Finance Director Richard Anderson provided an overview of recent action by Cache County adopting
Ordinance 2026-23, which increases the fire levy while reducing the general fund levy by an
equivalent amount. It was explained that Logan City residents currently contribute to the County fire
levy but receive limited direct fire services from the County due to Logan City’s own fire department
providing those services locally.
It was reported that the net effect of the County action would reduce taxes on Logan City residents by
approximately $1.3 million. He noted that this development occurred after initial budget workshops
and was not available at the time the Mayor’s proposed budget was prepared.
He explained that the Council now has the option to consider whether to adjust Logan City’s property
tax levy in response. It was noted that any adjustment could potentially offset the County reduction
and allow the City to redirect funds toward operational needs, including potential funding for a future
fire station in the northwest portion of the City.
It was further explained that funding considerations include:

   •   Supporting future operational costs for a northwest fire station
   •   Reducing reliance on intergovernmental service agreements
   •   Balancing operating and capital funding priorities
   •   Managing potential impacts to service levels and response times if no adjustment is made

Mr. Anderson emphasized that multiple options exist, including maintaining current levels, adjusting
the levy to redirect funding internally, or continuing reliance on existing agreements and capital
planning strategies. It was clarified that no decision was being made at this time and that the

Chair Johnson reiterated that the purpose of this discussion was to determine whether the matter
should be placed on a future agenda for further consideration during upcoming budget workshop on

Councilmember Lee-Koven requested additional context for public understanding regarding the City’s
budget proposal. She noted that the Mayor’s proposed budget includes an approximate 3% increase. It
was also discussed that in recent years, approved budget increases have generally ranged between
approximately 3% and 7%, often reflecting inflationary pressures and service level adjustments rather
than purely inflation-based changes. It was her understanding that approximately 12% of the total
property tax paid by residents is attributable to Logan City’s portion of the tax rate.
Mr. Anderson provided additional clarification regarding the potential property tax implications of the
proposed adjustment. It was explained that the referenced approximately $83 per household is
currently collected through Cache County. If Logan City were to adjust its levy to capture an
equivalent amount, it would represent an approximate 20% increase in the City’s portion of the
property tax rate.
It was further noted that Logan City accounts for approximately 11% of a resident’s total property tax
bill, and that a 20% adjustment to that portion would translate to an overall impact of approximately
2% to 3% of a resident’s total property tax bill, depending on final calculations and distribution.
Mr. Anderson reiterated that Cache County has adopted a resolution indicating its intent to reduce its
levy by the corresponding amount, though it was noted that implementation is anticipated rather than
fully finalized at the time of discussion.
Councilmember Simmonds stated that the Mayor’s budget has already been formally proposed and
cannot be amended at this stage; any changes would require Council action. The Mayor has indicated
support for whatever direction the Council chooses.
The Council Chair emphasized that the timing of the discussion is critical due to statutory
requirements governing property tax processes, including required deadlines, public notice provisions,
and findings related to proposed adjustments. It was noted that these procedural requirements
necessitate timely Council consideration if further action is desired.
Councilmember Lee-Koven recommended placing the item on a future agenda for further Council
discussion, citing the timing of the recent Cache County fire service levy reduction and the need to
evaluate both immediate and long-term fire service funding considerations. It was noted that the
proposed discussion would not constitute a public hearing at this stage, but rather an opportunity for
Council review and deliberation ahead of formal statutory hearings later in the budget process.

Consideration of a proposed resolution designating a Closed Area to Ignition or Use of
Fireworks – Resolution 26-14 – Fire Chief Nate Thompson

Fire Chief Nate Thompson addressed the Council regarding the proposed resolution regarding
fireworks restrictions within the City. It was reported that there are no changes to fireworks boundaries
from the previous year. The eastern boundary remains along 1600 East through North Logan and
Logan, extending down through Canyon Road. No changes were noted to boundaries affecting
contract cities.
It was further noted that areas along 100 West and 600 West remain unchanged. Chief Thompson
stated that the newly appointed Fire Marshal, Aaron Walker will review certain developed areas as
part of ongoing evaluation efforts, noting that boundary determinations must account for required
separation distances from wildland and agricultural lands.
Chief Thompson also provided an update on current fire conditions, noting increasing regional fire risk
and unusually early fire restrictions in parts of Utah, emphasizing the importance of public caution due
to dry conditions.
An interactive online fire restriction map was presented as a public resource, allowing residents to
search by address to determine whether fireworks are permitted in specific areas. He noted that this
tool will be made available on the City’s website and promoted to the public, including at fireworks
sales locations.
Chief Thompson confirmed that boundary signage will be posted approximately one week prior to the
legal fireworks period on both the east and west sides of Logan City.

CODE AMENDMENT – Consideration of a proposed ordinance amending Section 5.02 & 5.20
of the Logan Municipal Code Regulating Mobile Food – Ordinance 26-09 – Russ Holley,
Community Development Director
Community Development Director Russ Holley addressed the Council and presented the proposed
code amendment to Title 5 of the municipal code related to business licensing.
He explained that the ordinance updates definitions and licensing categories by adding classifications
for commercial Level 1 and Level 2 businesses. The amendment also removes the bonding
requirement section, noting that this requirement has not been actively used or enforced for
approximately 15 years.
Additional updates include revisions to food vendor regulations to align with recent changes in state
law. These updates expand the scope of regulated vendors beyond mobile food vendors to include
other types of vendors and clarify where and how they may operate.
Overall, he characterized the ordinance as a housekeeping update to modernize and align the
municipal code with current practices and state requirements.
The proposed ordinance will be an action item and public hearing at the June 2, 2026 Council meeting.
