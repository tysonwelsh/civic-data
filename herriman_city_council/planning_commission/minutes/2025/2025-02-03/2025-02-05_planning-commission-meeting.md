    1.3. Review and discuss Administrative Referral Policy updates for Minor Subdivisions –
         Michael Maloy, Planning Director

Planning Director Maloy discussed updates to the Administrative Referral Policy for minor
subdivisions. He explained that the City Council had approved the framework of the policy, allowing
the Planning Commission to defer administrative items to staff. The policy covered lot splits, lot
consolidations, lot line adjustments, and single lots of record to reviews by staff.

Planning Director Maloy proposed expanding the policy to include developer surveys and
preliminary minor residential subdivisions of up to five lots. He also suggested including flag lots in
this category. Street dedication plats were discussed as a separate item.

Commissioners expressed concerns about including minor residential subdivisions and flag lots in
the administrative review process, preferring to keep these items in public meetings for
transparency. They were more comfortable with street dedication plats being handled
administratively.

The commission also discussed the possibility of including commercial subdivision amendments in
the administrative review process, particularly for financing purposes. Planning Director Maloy
agreed to bring back revised language on commercial subdivision amendments at the next meeting.

Assistant City Manager Thomas provided an update on the 2025 Utah State Legislative session,
which was on its 16th day. She reported that 427 house bills and 234 senate bills had been
introduced, totaling 661 bills so far.
She highlighted several bills of interest to the Planning Commission:
   1.   HB 88: An ADU bill allowing ADUs anywhere and permitting modular homes in any
        residential zone. The city currently opposes this bill.
   2. HB 90: A minimum lot size bill, now proposing 6,000 square foot minimum lots in all
      residential zones, an improvement from the initial 4,000 square foot proposal.
   3. HB 256: A short-term rental bill allowing municipalities to use Airbnb listings as proof of
      short-term rental use and provide notice of violations and obtain transient room tax.
   4. HB 318: A turf bill limiting the amount of turf in landscape areas.
   5. HB 355: A gravel pit bill requiring municipalities to hold public hearings on gravel pit
      operations, despite having no authority to act on them.
   6. HB 368: A comprehensive bill covering annexations, boundary changes, and development
      review processes.
   7. SB 152: A bill prohibiting requirements for garages in single or dual-family residences and
      restricting certain design elements.
   8. SB 181: A bill restricting Deeds as owner-occupied for at least five years, parking. Restricts
      requirements on building design elements and parking for one or two-family dwellings.
Assistant City Manager Thomas also mentioned the First Home Investment (FHIZ) bill, which had
passed the House and was expected to pass the Senate. She noted that Herriman was working
towards a FHIZ in Olympia with specific requirements for owner-occupied units and mixed-use
development.

   4.1. Review and consider approval of a Preliminary Subdivision Plat Amendment to create
        commercial condominium units in Studio Business Park located at 5101 W 11800 South in
        the C-2 Commercial Zone. (Public Hearing)
        Applicant: Casey Forbush (authorized agent)
        Acres: ±2.76
        File No: S2024-135

Planner Howa presented a subdivision plat amendment for the Studio Business Park located in the
Northeast corner of the city. The applicant proposed to create four units per floor in an existing
building under construction on Lot 102, totaling 2.7 acres zoned C-2. The proposal included
condominium declarations and shared common spaces through breezeways.

Staff recommended approval with the condition that the condominium declaration be recorded with
the plat.
