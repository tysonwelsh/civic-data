          Fee Changes: Comptroller Stout stated that as part of the FY21 budget, Administration is proposing three fee schedule
amendments: fire bum permit fees increasing from $5 to $10; planning review and petition fees increasing; and airport lease rates
increasing to allow discretionary rate adjustments in line with market rate and to add a new lease rate and defmition for raw land.
          Fire Chief Mathieu provided more detail on the proposed fire bum pennit fee increase. This rate has not been increased
in the past 20 years and department analysis concludes that each permit requires approximately 40 minutes of staff time and costs
about $21.98 in fuel and staff time. The current fee is $5; however, the department does not want to make the permit cost
prohibitive and is proposing the fee increase to $10.
          Planning Manager Montgomery reviewed the proposal regarding planning review and petition fees. The intent of the
proposed increase is to cover more of the existing costs, particularly with cost increases incurred because of modifying the
noticing approach. Currently, fee revenues have accounted for 50-60% of actual costs. The Planning Division has completed a
benchmark of comparable entities' fees, mostly with other cities in Utah. This proposal also removes the fee structure for
expedited applications as these often were not feasible given the time requirements for noticing.
          Airport Manager Garrett talked about the proposed amendments regarding airport leasing rates. This fee modification is
intended to reduce the amount of General Fund subsidy to support airport operations. The Administration has evaluated zip code
data for current airport badge-holders, which identifies that 12 percent of those frequently utilizing the airport live in Ogden City.
This proposal includes allowing the Airport Manager to use discretion to set non-aeronautical land lease rates, at a minimum of
fair market value and with maximum rates included in the ordinance. This also adds a new raw land rate that was not previously
included, raw land is defined as airport land with no established pavement improvements.
          Council member Hyer asked Mr. Garrett to provide an explanation of the difference between aeronautical and non-
aeronautical land at the airport. Mr. Garrett replied the Federal Aviation Authority (FAA) defmes those tenns. If an item is
manufactured on the installation and is installed on an aircraft that will test fly out of the airpmt, the land on which the item is
manufactured can be defmed as aeronautical. If the item is manufactured and shipped off the property, the land on which the item
is manufactured is defined as non-aeronautical. Council member Hyer stated it was his understanding that non-aeronautical
activities were not pennitted at the Ogden Airport and asked if that has changed. Mr. Garrett responded that the FAA does not
want the City to push an aeronautical use out of the installation in favor of allowing a non-aeronautical use to locate at the airport.
They reserve the right to scrutinize the allowed land uses at the airport. However, virtually every airport has a non-aeronautical
use on-site and the City's lease of airport land to non-aeronautical uses is not problematic in his opinion.
          Chair Choberka said she is not aware of another fee in the City's fee schedule for which a Department Director has
latitude to detennine a rate. Mr. Garrett expressed his understanding that may be a concern but noted the fee increase will address
losses at the airport. The key is to find a place of equilibrium where the lease rates can be absorbed in the market while also
supporting the airport and helping to move in a direction of being self-sufficient. He indicated the proposal is based upon a
benchmark study of lease rates charged at comparable airports.
