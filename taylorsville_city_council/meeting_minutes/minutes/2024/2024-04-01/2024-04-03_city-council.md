          1. 2   Mayor' s Report


         Mayor Kristie Overson reported that Tita' s Restaurant ( just west of city hall) had held a
ribbon-cutting recently. She thanked the council members and Chamber West for helping to
welcome Tita' s to Taylorsville.


The Mayor mentioned that she and several other city representatives were invited to celebrate
Ramadan with the local Islamic community. She described it as an extraordinary evening, and was
very appreciative of the opportunity to share a delightful meal and get to know these neighbors
better.


Congressman Burgess Owens recently met with Mayor Overson via Zoom along with other local
mayors. She looked forward to continuing to touch base with him about the needs of the
Taylorsville community.

April was Child Abuse Prevention Month and each year the Youth Council and Exchange Club
planted pinwheels in front of city hall in remembrance, which they had done earlier in the day.
Mayor Overson loved sharing the message that child abuse prevention was extremely important.
The City was happy to share their support via these pinwheels.

A localscape presentation was recently held for approximately 30 residents interested in learning
how to conserve water through their landscape choices. She hoped the program continued to
inspire residents to flip their strips and to be more cognizant of water conservation.

The music of Stephen Schwartz was recently celebrated by the Arts Council. This involved a
lovely evening of music and dance at the MidValley Performing Arts Center, and the Mayor
enjoyed the great talent available here in Taylorsville.


Beginning on July 9, a Farmer' s Market would be held every Tuesday evening at Centennial Plaza.
An outside organization would coordinate the event with various vendors, and Mayor Overson
looked forward to enjoying the market. It would continue weekly through September 24.

The Mayor gave a shout out to Unified Fire who had stepped up their social media game. She
recommended that everyone follow them on various platforms.


On March 23- 24, TVPD' s K-9 unit joined 50 other teams from across the region at the 32nd Annual
Las Vegas Metropolitan K-9 Trials. Taylorsville was represented by Sgt. Palmer and Koda, Officer
Moore and Turbo, and Officer Hoffman and Ghost who competed in events involving Agility,
Area Searches, Building Searches, Tactical Obedience, Handler Protection, and Narcotics
Detection. TVPD was named " Top Agency," the most prestigious and sought-after award in the
competition. This means their three teams had the highest average score across all categories.
Additionally, Moore and Turbo placed 1St in Building Search; Hoffman and Ghost took 2nd place
in Handler Protection as well as 2nd place in the" Top Dog" competition.

          1. 3   Citizen Comments


El Chair Cochran invited any members of the audience who wished to address the City Council
to step forward and do so.

Resident Bret World expressed concern about what he felt was the poor condition of sidewalks in
the city. He referenced the fact that city code made it clear that sidewalks were the responsibility
of the homeowner living adjacent to the sidewalk. He wanted to know then why the city had to
bear the cost of repair and why the code was not being enforced. Mr. World referenced a recent
tragedy in Salt Lake City where a woman on a scooter died after crashing into a damaged sidewalk.

3.       REPORTS


         3. 1    Citizen Advisory Board— Dean Paynter


 El   Board Chair Dean Paynter appreciated the opportunity to report to the City Council. He
explained he had genuinely enjoyed having the opportunity to serve. He explained that the Board' s
duty was to" review all matters relating to uses of force by the TVPD— primarily vehicular pursuits
and internal affairs investigations of police officers."


He explained that the original five board members were appointed in March 2022 and soon began
weekly training sessions to familiarize themselves with a little bit of what officers did in the course
of their work. They had a session on the driving range as well as with a " Shoot, Don' t Shoot"
simulator which was very intense. They met with Chief Cottam, the Patrol Division, and the Crisis
Intervention Team. They also visited a gun range, learned about how records and evidence were
retained, and received some legal training. They reviewed their first incident that September.

Mr. Paynter explained that when the Board reviewed use of force incidents, Lt. Cheshire typically
presented a report to the Board and then the committee would review any body camera footage of
the incident. He appreciated that the lieutenant and others were always happy to answer any
questions. Each board member made his/her own determination if they felt the use of force was
within policy or not. Thus far the Board had reviewed 228 incidents with just four cases which
were determined to be outside policy.

Mr. Paynter made the following observations:
      A vast majority of the cases reviewed by the Board involved alcohol, drugs, or mental
         illness
         Many of the cases involved domestic violence
         Residents and especially the youth were less respectful of law enforcement than they once
         were


         There was some frustration with the legal system in Salt Lake County

After a recent discussion, various board members used the following words to describe the
Taylorsville Police Department— professional, respectful, patient, determined, and effective.


Mr. Paynter wished to thank the police department for their support, and Mayor Overson for
appointing them to serve on this board. He also wished to thank HR Director Stephanie Snow who
attended their monthly board meetings as well.

Captain Rich explained that UFA' s 2024-2026 Strategic Plan had been adopted by the Board of
Directors. In preparing the plan there had been 90 meetings and 2, 100 data points collected. The

Several retirements had taken place and those retiring had an average of 25 years of service. As a
result of these departures, there were numerous promotions to Captain, Engineer, HazMat
Technician, Public Information Officer, Investigator, Emergency Management Staff Captain, and
Emergency Management Deputy Director. Three experienced firefighters had been hired with five
more plus four paramedics currently in the hiring process. There were an additional 27 recruits
currently in week# 5 of their training.
