41           1.2      Mayor’s Report
     Taylorsville City Council Minutes

 1
 2         Mayor Kristie Overson had attended ribbon cuttings at both Cookie Cutters and Rockler
 3   Woodworking and Hardware recently. Cookie Cutters was a fun and colorful place for children to
 4   get their haircut while Rockler was a national business that had chosen Taylorsville for their first
 5   Utah store. The Crossroads Shopping area continued to be a vibrant place to be.
 6
 7   The Taylorsville Food Pantry was flourishing under the guidance of the Intermountain Baptist
 8   Church. The church had recently held a Breakfast in the Park event where plenty of food was
 9   available for all who attended.
10
11   Granite Credit Union had recently invited Mayor Overson to attend Juntos Avanzanos where the
12   credit union was recognized for its strong relationship with the Latino community. She appreciated
13   the invitation and was delighted to attend the event.
14
15   The mayor had been a guest of Mountain America Credit Union at the Sutherland Institute Gala,
16   along with Councilmembers Harker and Barbieri.
17
18   She had recently attended a demonstration of a weapons detection system that had been installed
19   at Hunter High School on a trial basis. She was joined by City Administrator John Taylor, Chief
20   Cottam and Sgt. Scott Lloyd and found the demonstration quite fascinating. She appreciated the
21   city’s partnership with the Granite School District.
22
23   Mayor Overson pointed out that winter weather had delayed several city projects.   Once the
24   roadways were sufficiently dry, residents should see some patching of potholes taking place
25   around 4700 South and 2700 West.
26
27   Many residents had contacted city hall to inquire about sand bags to help mitigate potential
28   flooding as the snow melted. There were a few locations in the city where sand would be made
29   available for residents to fill bags, although specific details were not yet available. The information
30   would be publicized via social media the following week. In the meantime, residents could go to
31   604 West 6960 South or several other locations in unincorporated SL County.
32
33        Starry Nights at the Plaza would begin May 5 and continue every Friday night through the
34   summer. Fifty-seven bands had expressed interest in performing at these events. Mayor Overson
35   was very excited for this summer-long program.
36
37   The Youth Council and the Exchange Club had jointly acknowledged Child Abuse Awareness
38   month in Taylorsville at a pin-wheel planting event.
39
40                    1.2.1   Proclamation Recognizing the Month of April as “Child Abuse
41                            Awareness Month”
     Taylorsville City Council Minutes

     Page 4



 1
 2   Mayor Overson invited Susan Gartell of the Family Support Center to step to the podium. She
 3   presented Ms. Gartell with a copy of a proclamation declaring April 2023 as Child Abuse
 4   Awareness Month.
 5
 6   Ms. Gartell spoke briefly about the 9,695 instances of child abuse that were reported in Utah in
 7   2022. She explained the five protective factors that could help alleviate the root causes of child
 8   abuse and neglect were:
 9      • Resiliency
10      • Social Connection
11      • Knowledge
12      • Education
13      • Concrete Support Systems
14
15   The Family Support Center worked to give parents access to these five important factors. Ms.
16   Gartell pointed out that society could not continue to demonize abuse and neglect while also
17   stigmatizing those who sought help.

34           6.2      Resolution No. 23-05 A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville approving the
35                    First Amendment to the Commercial Development Incentive Agreement with
36                    the Carriage Square Property Owners’ Association – Wayne Harper
37
38        Community and Economic Development Director Wayne Harper explained that the Council
39   had just approved a resolution which included $100,000 in funds for Carriage Square
40   improvements. He was now bringing to the council an addendum to an agreement with the
41   Carriage Square that had first been approved about two years before. Due to a variety of factors,
     Taylorsville City Council Minutes
     April 5, 2023
     Page 7



 1   the project costs had increased significantly. The city’s contribution increased to nearly $1.2
 2   million, all of which would come out of CDBG funds. The Carriage Square Property Owners
 3   Association was requesting a loan in the amount of $250,000 to be repaid over six years.
 4
 5   Councilmember Harker inquired about an updated project timeline. Mr. Harper responded that if
 6   the resolution was approved, construction could begin the following week.
 7
 8   Councilmember Cochran wondered what these funds would provide. Mr. Harper indicated they
 9   would cover resurfacing the parking lot, repairing sidewalks, irrigation, and landscaping. There
10   was an architecture review committee and a property management firm would be hired to help
11   ensure property owners did their part as well.
12
13   Councilmember Barbieri inquired about vacancies at Carriage Square. Tuet Nguyen, one of the
14   property owners’ boardmembers responded from the audience that there were currently just two
15   spaces without tenants out forty-eight spaces.
16
17   MOTION:          Councilmember Burgess moved to approve Resolution No. 23-05, approving
18                    the First Amendment to the Commercial Development Incentive Agreement
19                    with the Carriage Square Property Owners’ Association. The motion was
20                    seconded by Councilmember Cochran.
