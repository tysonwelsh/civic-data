       A.     ZONE TEXT CHANGE - PLZ-23-5001, PROPOSED CHANGES TO THE
              CITY'S LANDSCAPING AND IRRIGATION ORDINANCE. APPLICANT -
              RIVERTON CITY.

Chair Gilchrist opened the public hearing.

Community Development Director, Jason Lethbridge, reported that some time ago the City
adopted a Water-Efficient Landscaping Ordinance. It included water-efficient landscaping
standards and irrigation systems and applied predominantly to commercial and large-scale
residential users. It was based on model ordinances that came from the Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy District (“JVWCD”) and elsewhere. At the time they chose to adopt it as a
recommended standard as opposed to a mandate. The purpose was to allow the City to
ease into the process of review and enforcement. It has worked well and there has been
voluntary compliance. It is an encouraged standard for most, if not all, commercial users.
They have seen existing businesses also look to convert to more waterwise landscaping.
The proposed amendment is based on an ongoing review and evaluation with JVWCD and
other agencies. Additional mandates are coming from the State for cities to pursue more
waterwise and water-conserving systems.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that the amended ordinance mandates compliance for designs on
all landscaping and commercial projects and required developer-installed landscaping in
residential areas. It is not mandated for single-family yards but for multi-family housing,
which must meet certain conservation standards. It allows for “active recreation areas”
within a multi-family project but everything else requires that the plans submitted meet the
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                   2
February 9, 2023

waterwise standard. A maximum percentage of turf is set within the development at 20% of
the landscaping that is installed. The intent is to minimize water use and waste. The
amended ordinance will help the City’s relationship with JVWCD and other agencies to meet
the required thresholds to qualify for grant money for different rate tiers on water use. Some
of that pertains to funds that go toward “flip your strip” and similar programs where
homeowners and businesses are compensated for converting park strips to more waterwise
landscaping.

Staff is confident that the mandate will not overburden City businesses or the development
community and will help conserve water and allow the city to work more closely with outside
agencies to qualify for grants and other funding. Mr. Lethbridge reported that otherwise, the
standards in the ordinance are the same as adopted previously. The verbiage was simply
changed to be required rather than recommended and encouraged. The Planning
Commission was asked to make a recommendation to the City Council who will make the
final decision.

Commissioner Cannon reported that he has heard that a bill at the house level puts teeth
into the existing ordinance. He had heard that if the incentives such as grants are used to
change landscaping, and these changes are later removed, the grant must be paid back.
He commented that it is a well-written bill. Mr. Lethbridge stated that it will position the City
to be compliant with state mandates and qualify for grants and other funding.

Commissioner Cluff asked for clarification on the 20% turf allowance. Mr. Lethbridge
explained that most developers are eliminating turf almost altogether but there is currently
no mandated amount of turf allowed. This helps staff in reviewing the plans and the Planning
Commission when approving them to know what that threshold is. He did not expect
commercial projects to come close to that. It was noted that active recreation areas will not
count against the turf allowance.

In response to a question raised, Mr. Lethbridge explained that in instances where a
developer is required to install front yard landscaping, they will be required to comply with
the ordinance. If a builder, as part of their sales package, offers to install lawn for buyers,
they are encouraged to do so. One of the options is to install a waterwise landscaping
system but it is not mandated.

Because this is a new requirement, Chair Gilchrist asked if there are resources on the City’s
website that developers and homeowners have access to. Mr. Lethbridge explained that
because it has been in place as an encouraged standard, it has been made available. When
it is adopted and codified by the Council, they will make it and other resources available on
the City’s website.

Commissioner Park supported the idea but was not supportive of being asked to recommend
adoption of an ordinance that they have not been provided the text for. It was noted that a
link was provided to the full document prior to the meeting.

There were no public comments. The public hearing was closed.

additional employee. Staff recommended that a condition be added requiring her to use the
