  Nicole Cottle, Acting City Manager, informed a public hearing had been advertised

  Filed by Erik Moreno, Requesting Final Plat Approval for the Moreno Subdivision-
  Amending Lots 15-17 Block 2 East Magna Plat A Located at 7055 West Gates
  Avenue

  Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
  information as follows:

         Erik Moreno is requesting final approval for the Moreno Subdivision. This
         application will also amend lots 15, 16 and 17 Block 2 East Magna Plat
         A. The subdivision will consist of 1 lot on 1.3 acres, is located at 7055 West
         Gates Avenue and is zoned Manufacturing.

         The subdivision is being proposed to consolidate and reconfigure the
         existing 3 lots into 1 lot for a new commercial business. The consolidation
         is a requirement of the conditional use application approved by the Planning
         Commission.

         Access will be gained from Gates Avenue. Full dedication exists along
         Gates Avenue, but formal improvements are lacking. As part of the site plan
         for the pending development, the applicant will be required to install curb,
         gutter and sidewalk.

  Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing. There being no one to speak in favor or
  opposition, Mayor Lang closed the Public Hearing.

RESOLUTION 23-29: APPROVE AN INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT BETWEEN
WEST VALLEY CITY AND THE SALT LAKE COUNTY CLERK'S OFFICE,
ELECTIONS DIVISION, FOR THE PROVISION OF ELECTION SERVICES FOR
THE 2023 PRIMARY AND GENERAL MUNICIPAL ELECTIONS
Nicole Cottle, Acting City Manager, discussed proposed Resolution 23-29 that would
approve an Interlocal Agreement Between West Valley City and the Salt Lake County
Clerk's Office, Elections Division, for the Provision of Election Services for the 2023
Primary and General Municipal Elections.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       This agreement authorizes the Salt Lake County Clerk’s Office, Elections Division,
       to provide election services to the City Recorder’s Office. These services include:
       •       Ballot layout and design
       •       Ballot ordering and printing
       •       Machine programming and testing
       •       Delivery of supplies and equipment
       •       Provision of all supplies
       •       Election vote center/early vote locations
       •       Vote by mail administration
         •    Updating state and county websites
       •       Tabulating, reporting, auditing and preparing canvassing election results
       •       Conducting recounts as needed
       •       All notices and mailings required by law (except those required by Utah
               Code Ann. Ch . 11-14, Part 2 and § 20A-9-203)
       •       Direct payment of all costs associated with elections to include vote center
       workers, training, polling places, rovers.
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING MARCH 14, 2023

       The provision of these services will assist in conducting the elections in an efficient
       manner. The fee for these services is estimated at $160,072.00 and will be based on
       the City’s pro-rata share of actual costs of the elections.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City
Council, and she called for a motion.

       Councilmember Harmon moved to approve Resolution 23-29

       Councilmember Fitisemanu seconded the motion and noted that while the City is
       not choosing Ranked Choice Voting this year, they are still committed to
       researching and evaluating the program for the future.

RESOLUTION 23-31: AWARD A CONTRACT TO STRATTON AND BRATT
AQUATIC DIVISION FOR REPLACEMENT OF THE OUTDOOR POOL
BOILERS AT THE WEST VALLEY FAMILY FITNESS CENTER
Nicole Cottle, Acting City Manager, presented proposed Resolution 23-31 that would
award a Contract to Stratton and Bratt Aquatic Division for Replacement of the Outdoor Pool
Boilers at the West Valley Family Fitness Center.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       Parks and Recreation publicly advertised for proposals for the Pool Boiler
       replacements at the Centennial Outdoor Pool. The original boilers were installed
       in 2001. Extensive preventative maintenance and general work has been performed
       over the years to keep the boilers functional. Recently there have been significant
       and consistent issues keeping them operating. One of the two boilers is non-
       functional.

       The RFP was publicly posted and several companies were contacted by staff. The
       proposals were evaluated and compared against each other in a matrix format by a

RESOLUTION 23-32: AUTHORIZE THE EXECUTION OF AN AMENDMENT
TO A DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT WITH BRAD REYNOLDS
CONSTRUCTION FOR PROPERTY LOCATED AT APPROXIMATELY 3554
SOUTH 3200 WEST
Nicole Cottle, Acting City Manager, discussed proposed Resolution 23-32 that would
authorize the Execution of an Amendment to a Development Agreement with Brad
Reynolds Construction for Property Located at Approximately 3554 South 3200 West

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:

         The City entered into a development agreement with Brad Reynolds
         Construction on December 14, 2021 for a 33-unit townhome development at
         3554 South 3200 West. One of the requirements in the agreement is that each
         townhome unit must have a finished basement. According to Mr. Reynolds, the
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING MARCH 14, 2023
                           -8-

         finished basement requirement, which was included in his proposal, was an error
         on his part. He is requesting that the development agreement be amended so that
         the basements are not required to be finished. In exchange, Mr. Reynolds is
         proposing to install a masonry wall along the south and west sides of the property
         where vinyl fencing was required and to install larger trees along 3200 West. The
         amended agreement also corrects the minimum unit areas that were submitted in
         error by the applicant. The corrected minimum unit areas result in a larger
         finished, above grade space and a smaller basement space with the total area
         remaining the same.

         During the Council’s study meeting on February 7th, the Council directed staff to
         prepare this amendment for their consideration.

      Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the
      City Council, and she called for a motion.

              Councilmember Harmon moved to approve Resolution 23-32.

              Councilmember Nordfelt seconded the motion.
