Study Session

1.0   Discussion: Ranked Choice Voting

1.1   The Council discussed Ranked Choice Voting. A majority of the Council
      indicated they were not in favor of Ranked Choice Voting for the 2023
      municipal elections. The Council discussed the possibility of changing the
      format to have candidates state which council seat they are running for,
      without having districts.

2.0   Discussion: Water Wise Landscaping Text Amendment

2.1   Staff provided possible water-wise landscaping text amendments. The Council
      requested that staff put together a basic plan with options for Council review
      and discussion.

3.0   Update: Draper Days

3.1   David Wilks and Kellie Challburg updated the Council regarding plans for 2023
      Draper Days. The Council and staff discussed possible locations to stage
      fireworks, an integrated parking plan, planned festivities/activities, and what
      would be new in 2023. Of the options presented, a majority of the Council
      indicated a preference for Option B.

6.1   City Manager David Dobbins thanked Public Works staff for their response to
      the recent flooding. Mr. Dobbins and Public Works Director Scott Cooley

      updated the Council on flood response and answered questions from the
      Council. Mr. Cooley said it was unknown at that time where the water was
      coming from.

6.2   Responding to a question from the Council, City Attorney Mike Barker said he
      had not looked into the City’s legal authority or responsibility with private land
      drains. He said he suspected City legal responsibility was minimal.

6.3   Staff commented that flood insurance usually did not cover groundwater
      flooding.

6.4   Councilmember Roberts commented that it was understandable to use public
      resources in emergencies, but expressed the opinion that more information
      was needed before using public resources to address private property issues.

6.5   Mr. Cooley spoke of researching past plans and finding field drain systems that
      conflicted with land drain systems, and design plans that conflicted with as-
      built construction. He said a lot of manpower was required to sort through
      and make sense of the information. Mr. Cooley said he believed the
      information should be compiled to help homeowners and HOAs better
      maintain the private systems.

6.6   Councilmember Green asked Mr. Cooley what he needed from the Council
      moving forward, and asked if Emergency Management had been involved.

6.7   Councilmember Green asked if the City could require the homes to have a
      sump pump, with annual inspections of the systems. Mr. Cooley responded
      that some questions were difficult to answer in the heat of the moment, and
      said he had been careful not to overstep bounds with the private systems.

6.8   Mayor Walker said he did not believe the City should be the keeper of all of
      the private system maps. He said he believed the City could provide general
      information, but should not give specific directions about what to do.

6.9   Mr. Cooley said there were interesting interfaces between private systems and
      public right-of-way. He said locating the lines and finding the access points was
      key to the maintenance that needed to occur but emphasized that he did not
      think the City necessarily needed to facilitate that work. Mr. Cooley stated to
      get an accurate read of these private systems could take a year.
