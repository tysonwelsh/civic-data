       THE UTAH VALLEY DISPATCH SPECIAL SERVICE DISTRICT TO LEASE COPPER AND

Commissioner Graves outlined his concerns with this agreement, which he stated greatly benefits the Utah Valley
Dispatch Special Service District but is not balanced for Utah County. Commissioner Graves mentioned he also
serves on the Utah Valley Dispatch Special Service District (UVSSD) Board, and reviewed the costs as submitted
by the UVSSD. He noted copper cabling is expensive, and it is problematic that Utah County as the supplier must
pay for all services but only after UVSSD as the customer has performed their own inspections. Additionally, any
interruptions to service during repairs and maintenance lead to discounts for the UVSSD. Utah County Public
Works Director Richard Nielson noted the copper is used by the entire county, and Commissioner Graves mentioned
he would like to see some of the costs recovered. Commissioner Ivie suggested continuing discussion on this item
for one week to address the issues laid out today.

3.     ADOPT A RESOLUTION UPDATING THE DUTIES OF THE UTAH COUNTY
       INVESTMENT/BENEFITS COMMITTEE
(Track A, 15:00)
Commissioner Lee talked about the creation of this committee in 2007. This update includes using job titles rather
than individual names of county representatives, and the involvement of the Utah County Commission. Utah
County Human Resources Director Cammie Taylor added the committee will be referred to as a recommending
body rather than a selection team. The committee meets quarterly to make recommendations on issues such as
failed investments, and currently relies heavily on the county broker for training as most members are new within
the last two years – this resolution will help prevent issues and clarify the intent by placing more responsibility on
the commissioners to delegate funds.

7.     APPROVE AND AUTHORIZE COMMISSION CHAIR TO EXECUTE AGREEMENT WITH
       VINEYARD CITY FOR LAW ENFORCEMENT SERVICES
(Track A, 29:15)
Commissioner Lee summarized the contract is written to allow for adjustments based on actual annual costs.
Lieutenant Yvette Rice of the Utah County Sheriff’s Office confirmed, mentioning city growth has impacted needs
very similar to expansion in Eagle Mountain. Commissioner Lee asked how the number of officers is determined,
and Lieutenant Rice explained the national standard is one deputy per 1,000 residents; the city must formally request
any increase in officers. Lieutenant Rice outlined the staffing plan and budget processes, and the number of current
deputies and plans for 2018. Commissioners Lee and Graves discussed city credits being issued for citations, and
both expressed the rebate should be removed from the agreement. When Lieutenant Rice pointed out Vineyard
would be the only city not to receive credits based on how other county law enforcement contracts are written, both
commissioners stated the clause should be removed from all of the agreements.

(Track A, 40:35)
Commissioner Lee asked about county liability if an officer were to suffer a long-term disability, or even death,
while serving in a contracted city. Lieutenant Yvette Rice stated Vineyard City would be paying for a portion of
insurance costs; she deferred questions about potential payouts to the County Attorney’s Office. Commissioner
Graves noted while no price can be placed on the loss of life, Commissioner Lee pointed out the agreement can be
written in a way for the financial burden to fall upon the contracted city. Commissioner Graves agreed the city
would be responsible for payments if the disability or death took place through their own police force.

(Track A, 42:30)
Lieutenant Yvette Rice summarized the Commission request thus far to update contracted city law enforcement
services to remove Justice Court credits, and all three commissioners agreed. She reiterated Vineyard City would be
the only entity to not receive the credit at this point. Commissioner Graves rebuked larger cities such as Eagle
Mountain which brag about revenue and assets while also claiming they cannot afford minimal budget increases for
necessary services such as law enforcement. He stated they can either pay full costs of the services provided by the
county, or create their own police agency. Commissioner Lee added it is unfair to expect county citizens to pay to
subsidize some of these cities when they may already be paying for services in their own towns.

Lieutenant Mike Brower of the Utah County Sheriff’s Office returned to the earlier question regarding long-term
disability and death in the line of duty. Lieutenant Brower mentioned Cory Wride and Greg Sherwood (two officers
killed and severely injured in January 2014, respectively), clarifying Deputy Sherwood was not serving Eagle
Mountain at the time of his injury. The commissioners went on to discuss mutual aid, with notes from Utah County
Attorney Jeffrey Buhman regarding previous Commission decisions not to charge cities for prosecution or public
defenders.
