Ogden School District Surplus Property Purchase

A memo from Council staff explained on December 13, 2022, the Ogden City Council passed a resolution declaring the
City’s intent to purchase four properties deemed surplus by Ogden School District. Per state law (§53G-4-902), once the Council
passes the resolution declaring intent to purchase, the City has 90 days to deliver to the School District an earnest money offer to
purchase the surplus property at the purchase price. The initial proposal from the City’s Administration was to purchase three of
the four properties for the purpose of developing housing. However, the Council felt that further discussion should be had in order
to explore all options for the future use of the property. If the Council determines that not all properties should be purchased and
the City does not deliver an offer to purchase the property, the City forfeits its exclusive right to purchase the property and the
property will be sold by the District on the public real estate market.

Chair Nadolski and Council member Richey facilitated discussion among the Council regarding the topics of funding
sources for building new and maintaining existing public park space and exploring opportunities to partner with other community
entities to accomplish desired improvement projects. The discussion involved some review of the goals and directives of the
Parks, Recreation, and Trails Master Plan which was adopted in 2019. Council member Hyer commented on the fact that the
School District may also be declaring other properties as surplus in the near future, and he would like to understand where those
properties are located as they may be more viable to a City park improvement project that would align with the Master Plan. He
stated that a joint work session with the School District is scheduled for February, and he would like to delay a decision on the
four properties currently available for purchase until questions about other properties can be answered. Chair Nadolski agreed,
after which he continued his facilitation of discussion of the highest and best use of the four properties currently being disposed of
by the District. There was a focus on opportunities for expanding City and private youth programs and the amount of
programmable space needed to accommodate that expansion; ideas for passive open space; consideration of adjusting fees for
usage of park space; and funding sources for the desired improvements. Chair Nadolski stated that the information and ideas
shared tonight will help Council staff and leadership in their efforts to work with Administration and the School District to get
answers to the questions regarding planning and use of the properties.

Utah Transit Authority Bus Rapid Transit OGX Project Update

Utah Transit Authority Trustee Beth Holbrook will update the Council on the progress of the Bus Rapid Transit—or
OGX— project. Planning for the project began in 2004. Construction started in April 2021 and will continue through 2023.

Trustee Holbrook and other UTA representatives used the aid of a PowerPoint to provide an update regarding the
progress of the OGX Project; the presentation included background information for the project, including communication with
businesses and residents who have been impacted by the project and changes in the timeline of the project responsive to concerns
expressed by businesses; development of a marketing budget for the project and the creation of a business toolkit aimed at driving
business to 25" Street during the project; and coupons given to riders during the marketing campaign to incentivize patronize of
downtown Ogden businesses. The presentation also provided the current status of the project; construction along Washington
Boulevard is substantially complete and canopies have been installed. The same is true for 25" Street. Public art pieces are yet to
be installed in those areas but will be installed as soon as they are completed. The work around Weber State University (WSU)
and McKay Dee Hospital is complete, with just glass to be installed at the transit stops. Harrison Boulevard construction is also
wrapping up, though this area of the project was much more difficult to complete. Construction at the Intermodal Hub began at the
beginning of 2022 and the next phase starts at 23" Street at The Junction; this phase involves four zones and public access to
points surrounding the transit line will be preserved during the project. Trustee Holbrook stated that this phase will begin in
January of 2023 and there will be disruption, but UTA will do its best to minimize it. Grand opening of the OGX line is slated for
August of 2023.

Council member Choberka asked if the business coupon program will continue through final phase. Ms. Holbrook
answered yes. She added that riders can ride on the new transit line for free for the first three years.

Discussion among the Council and UTA representatives centered on efforts to preserve walkability of this area of
downtown Ogden throughout the project; access to the parking facilities in The Junction during the OGX project; the long term
benefit the OGX project will create for the entire community; traffic impacts of the project, namely at points along Harrison
Boulevard; transit signal prioritization for OGX buses; and the wear and tear on existing infrastructure associated with heavy
construction equipment working on the project. In conclusion, there was a brief discussion about legislation that will be
considered in the 2023 Legislative Session that would impact funding for UTA and plans for extension of the Front Runner transit
line to Pleasant View, including a transit stop at Business Depot Ogden.

WonderBlock Development and Pay Parking System

Chair Nadolski noted that the Redevelopment Agency met last Tuesday, December 13, to conduct a fact-finding session
regarding the WonderBlock Development and Pay Parking system; due to the late hour of that meeting, the pay parking system
was not discussed and this meeting is intended to be a continuation of that discussion. He noted that Community and Economic
Development Director Cooper provided a presentation regarding the WonderBlock project and the Board and visitors in the
meeting were given the opportunity to engage in a question and answer session with representatives of JF Fisher. He invited
Administration to discuss the pay parking system.

Deputy Business Development Manager Sawyer used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the pay parking
system; Administration has considered the best way to address current challenges for existing businesses and prepare for
additional opportunities associated with needed MAKE Ogden development. He discussed his personal observation of parking
difficulties in downtown Ogden, specifically on and directly around 25" Street, and noted that in his role with the Business
Development Division of the City, he has worked closely with many local business owners in the community and is familiar with
their concerns related to parking. His presentation included the same information shared during the November 15, 2022 joint work
session meeting, such as: future demand concerns, results of the commissioned parking study, implementation of a paid parking
system, financing of the project, experiences of other communities that have implemented a paid parking program, and review of
public feedback regarding the project.

Discussion among the Council and Mr. Sawyer centered on the costs included in the operating expenses for the paid
parking program, specifically employee costs and the number of employees that will be needed to manage the system. Mr. Sawyer
indicated that at the onset of the program, three employees will be needed to manage it, but that number may increase in future
years. Discussion among the group then centered on possible disadvantages of a pay parking system; the impact a pay parking
system could have on private lots; bonding actions associated with the paid parking system and bond repayment sources; potential
zone text changes to reduce parking accommodation requirements for certain uses responsive to implementation of a paid parking
system; the collection of data at certain times and different days of the week as part of the parking study; the possibility of an
over-projection of revenues associated with the paid parking system; phased implementation of the paid parking program and
whether it is appropriate to build the parking structure before first introducing the mechanisms to facilitate paid on-street or
surface parking; and the history of City Administration’s consideration of a paid parking system for downtown Ogden.

Chair Nadolski then opened the question and answer portion of the meeting for residents.

Al Jones stated that he is very supportive of paid parking but suggested that a system be implemented to address on-street
and surface parking before moving forward with the parking structure. He stated that the occupancy of an existing parking
structure on Grant Avenue is typically around 30 percent, and he believes that is because people do not want to park in a structure
and walk to points of interest. There is a need for public transportation to help people circulate around the downtown area. The

then asked who will manage security of the structure and how will parking regulations be enforced. He stated that as downtown

for parking, and he suggested the City get a handle on its capacity to bond to pay for more parking structures. He noted that he

paid parking system; security of the structures will be the responsibility of the City and the City will likely hire a third-party
security company to handle that service. Parking enforcement will be handled by City employees. In terms of the structure at the
WonderBlock, JF Capital will have an on-site management company that will help with security and enforcement at that structure.

Chair Nadolski inquired as to the increased burden that will be placed on the Police Department associated with the paid

for enforcement. Chair Nadolski asked if there are concerns that people will not visit downtown Ogden when they learn they will
be required to pay for parking, resulting in lower than projected revenues and insufficient funding for enforcement staff. Mr.
Cooper stated that there are many unique offerings in downtown Ogden that cannot be found in other communities that do not
have a paid parking system; people will continue to patronize the businesses they value, and he believes they will be willing to pay
a minimal fee for that opportunity.
