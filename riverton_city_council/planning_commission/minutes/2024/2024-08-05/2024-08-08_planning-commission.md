       A.     WALMART, PLZ-24-8011, AN AMENDED SITE PLAN APPLICATION FOR
              MODIFICATIONS TO PARKING STALLS FOR THE LOCATION AT 13502
              HAMILTON VIEW ROAD. APPLICANT – SID BIDDLE.

Lisa Halversen, Planner, presented the Staff Report and stated that the application is for
parking lot modifications to add additional online pickup stalls. There were no proposed
changes to the building or landscaping. The original Site Plan was approved in 2005 as part
of the Master Site Plan that includes other pads facing 13400 South. The site is 22.3 acres
and is zoned Regional Commercial (C-R), as are the properties on the north, east, and west
sides. To the south is Rose Creek Lofts, which is zoned Multifamily Residential (RM-14).

Ms. Halversen presented the site map showing the existing and proposed pickup areas. The
applicant proposed converting the existing 18-stall pickup area to standard parking stalls
and adding a larger, 36-stall pickup area on the northwest side of the building. She noted
that the site will still meet the 10% minimum landscaping requirement. Notice was sent to
neighboring property owners, and no comments were received. It was noted that the
application originally included changes to the southwest side of the building, but the
applicant was able to create the pickup stalls without modifying the building's exterior.

Commissioner Cluff asked about any proposed changes to address safety issues with
workers crossing the busier section of the parking lot if they will be adding extra striping and
markers. Ms. Halverson stated that new labeling examples were included in the meeting
packet. If needed, additional safety signage could be added at a later time without
Commission approval.

       B.     BELCHAK PRIVATE LANE, PLZ-24-2025, AN APPLICATION FOR A
              PRIVATE LANE TO PROVIDE ACCESS FOR UP TO FOUR RESIDENTIAL
              LOTS TO BE LOCATED ON 1.1 ACRES AT 3807 WEST 11800 SOUTH.
              APPLICANT – THOM BELCHAK.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the Staff Report and stated that the subject property
is on the border of Riverton and South Jordan. The property was rezoned in 2024 and the
application before the Commission was the next step in the development process. It is
zoned Residential, Four Units Per Acre, Special Designations (R-4-SD). The special
designations were added in anticipation of a private lane, and cover side yard and internal
setbacks. Only the Planning Commission has the authority to review and approve private
lanes.

Mr. Prestwich presented an aerial view of the subject property and noted that the high-
density housing on the opposite side of 11800 South is in South Jordan. Mr. Belchak's
property is surrounded by a subdivision and currently has no other access from 11800
South. The private lane will be on the west side of the subdivision and will include a fire
turnaround. Notices were sent to all property owners within 300 feet of the subject property.
Additional notices will be sent when the Preliminary Subdivision Plat is presented to the
Commission for approval. Staff will review the final plat prior to construction.

Mr. Prestwich reported that when evaluating a private lane, the Commission must consider
two questions:

       1.     Does the property qualify for a private lane?
              18.35.070(1) indicates that the property must meet conditions A or B, and C:
              (a)   A lot of record which is preexisting and has no frontage or adequate
                    property to construct a public street; or
              (b)   It can be demonstrated by the applicant that the property cannot be
                    physically subdivided with public streets and that development on a
                    private lane would not impede the extension of any adjacent public
                    right-of-way either now or in the foreseeable future; and
              (c)   The development does not impede the necessary access from
                    adjoining properties as required by the Master Transportation Plan.

Staff believed the property qualifies under all three conditions.

       2.     What will the private lane look like?
              18.35.070(2) includes the following requirements:
              (b)   Three or more lots; 25 feet of paved surface width.
              (d)   Where development includes more than four lots and/or lane width
                    exceeds 150 feet in length, additional right-of-way width, including
                    sidewalk on at least one side, may be required by the City up to the
                    minimum pavement and sidewalk width of a standard public residential
                    right-of-way.

Mr. Prestwich stated that item D will make the lots unbuildable and the ramifications would
need to be discussed if it was something the Commission wished to consider.

Fire Code influences private lanes, both the required turnaround and "No Parking" areas.
The proposed private lane will have no parking on both sides. Parking will be in the
driveways of individual homes only. Mr. Prestwich noted that the Fire Department had not
considered the conceptual plan but would review the private lane during the subdivision
review process.

Mr. Prestwich stated that a neighboring property owner asked about the process and if the
existing house will remain. They discussed the approval processes for both the private lane
and subdivision. Mr. Prestwich informed the neighbor that it is unlikely to remain given the
proposed layout.

       D.     BURGER KING, PLZ-24-8010, AN AMENDED SITE PLAN FOR EXTERIOR
              CHANGES TO A COMMERCIAL BUILDING LOCATED AT 1856 WEST
              12600 SOUTH. APPLICANT – BRIAN RUSSELL.

Ms. Halversen presented the Staff Report and noted that the Concept Plan for the building
was discussed at the June 27, 2024, Planning Commission Meeting. The applicant then
incorporated some of the Commission's suggestions and came forward with an application.
The building is located on the north side of 12600 South on Lot 3 of the IFA Country Square
commercial subdivision. The approximately 7,350-square-foot building was built in 2004 on
0.7 acres and is zoned Downtown Commercial (C-D). The applicant is affiliated with Burger
King, and they propose to make significant exterior modifications with all new building
materials, architectural details, trim, lighting, etc. Notices were sent to neighboring property
owners, and no comments were received.

Ms. Halverson presented drawings of the current and proposed elevations and reminded
the Commission that the C-D zone has its own architectural requirements. The zone was
established to preserve the City's historic downtown as a marketplace and covers the area
from South Jordan Canal to 22000 West and approximately 12500 South to 12800 South.
The intersection of Redwood Road and 12600 South is the area with the most stringent
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                                     6
August 8, 2024


requirements. Red brick and turn-of-the-century features are common elements in this part
of the zone. The plan was reviewed by City Staff and presented to the Commission to
determine if they felt the architectural design was appropriate for the C-D zone.

On the west side of the building, the applicant proposed to reduce the height of the current
middle section. A softer roofline variation will remain. All exterior materials will be changed.
Arches will be removed from the windows and main playground area. The south side will
change from an arched theme to a square theme. The exterior materials will also change,
but some red brick accents will remain. The plan includes stucco, wood, tile, and metal.
There will be a tower with a modern Burger King sign. The square corners are highlighted
with black trim. The east side will incorporate the same elements as the west side. On the
north side (rear) of the building, the primary change will be in the exterior materials, which
will change from mostly brick to mostly stucco.

Downtown Commercial Code Section 18.65.060, Architectural Standards, requires a
minimum of five turn-of-the-century characteristics but indicates that as many as possible
should be used. The closer to the intersection of 12600 South and Redwood Road, the
more of those elements should be used. These features include:

              (i)      High-pitched roofs.
              (ii)     Arched doorways and windows.
              (iii)    Decorative block or brick quoins.
              (iv)     Columns, either brick, rounded wood, or colonial.
              (v)      Clock towers and other decorative vertical features.
              (vi)     Keystones over doorways or windows.
              (vii)    Decorative wood window shutters.
              (viii)   Brick accent walls.
              (ix)     Other features as recommended.

Ms. Halverson reported that because the building is in the IFA Country Square subdivision,
it requires IFA approval and the IFA approved the plan. She presented photographs of other
updated buildings in the area including the Subway/UPS building and several fast-food
restaurants. She reminded the Commission that reasonable minds may come to different
conclusions regarding whether the Site Plan modifications are appropriate for the Downtown
Commercial District. The item was previously discussed, and the minutes of that meeting
were included in the meeting packet. The building proposal is clean, modern, and
harmonious but it was not clear if the changes were compliant with the C-D zone. As with
prior applications, Staff urged the Commission to proceed with caution because the next
exterior remodel will point to this remodel as the baseline for what the City is willing to accept.

Commissioner Beck asked if the turn-of-the-century requirements were previously reviewed
or if it was up to the Commission to determine if the plan met those requirements.
Ms. Halverson stated that Staff reviewed the plans and the case could be made that all the
elements are there but the Commission would need to make the final determination.

Commissioner Rushton stated that no real guidance is given in the standards regarding what
"closer to the intersection" means; it is a subjective term. "More elements" is also subjective.

Chair Matheson reviewed the requirements listed in Section 18.65.060. He noted that the
building does not have a high-pitched roof, but none of the referenced buildings had a high-
pitched roof so that seems to no longer be a requirement. It does not have arched doorways
and windows, decorative blocks, or brick quoins. In response to a question from
Commissioner Cluff, Ms. Halverson confirmed that the requirement is for five or more
separate elements.

The applicant, Brian Russell, referenced 18.65.060.1(b), which states, "Large developments
should be able to use all of these features. Smaller scale developments are encouraged to
use as many of these features as possible." He stated that it is not a large-scale
development, and he used as many elements as possible without being overly gaudy. There
are no arched windows in the development, so they were not included in the building design.
That is acceptable to IFA. There is also no rock wainscotting in the development. The
building will include brick and concrete columns. Towers, or vertical features, are present
on both the front and at the entry. There are no decorative wood shutters, but there are
decorative wood accents. Brick accent walls are also present. He stated that previously the
Planning Commission accepted cornices and heavy fascia as typical turn-of-the-century
features, and there will be cornices in the playland area. Based on the Commission's
comments at the previous meeting, they increased the amount of brick on the façade. The
front columns will be brick, not stucco as shown on the previous design. They have included
four of the required items: decorative wood (not decorative shutters), brick accent walls,
towers and vertical features, and brick columns.

Chair Matheson stated that he likes the design, but it does not have the five required
elements. Mr. Russell restated his opinion that 18.65.060.1(b) allows for less than five on
smaller developments.

Commissioner Cannon referred to 18.65.060.1(ix) and indicated that the Commission should
be able to recommend another feature for approval. He stated that the applicant had made
an effort to beautify the building and include as many elements as possible. He believes

with the design was to do a modern interpretation of the turn-of-the-century look, and he
believes they accomplished that goal.

Commissioner Rushton asked if the "Home of the Whopper" element could meet the

       E.     ROCK RIDGE SUBDIVISION, PLZ-23-8003, AN APPLICATION FOR A
              COMMERCIAL MASTER PLAN OF 6.7 ACRES LOCATED ON THE
              SOUTHEAST CORNER OF 12600 SOUTH AND 3600 WEST. APPLICANT
              – GRAYSON SMITH, ENGINEER.

Mr. Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that it is for a new subdivision that has
not yet been reviewed by the Planning Commission. The 6.7-acre parcel has been vacant
for a number of years. The America First Credit Union ("AFCU") is under construction on
the corner, which has generated a lot of interest in the parcel. The LDS Church is to the
south and is zoned RR-22. Because of the zoning, it is an incompatible use. South of the
church is the Homestead Phase 2 Subdivision that was approved by the Planning
Commission at its June 27, 2024, meeting and is zoned RM-6-SD. The two lots behind the
LDS Church and subject property are zoned RR-22 with animal rights. Scooter's Coffee is
to the northeast of the subject property and is zoned C-G. Notices were sent to property
owners within 300 feet of the subject property.

Mr. Prestwich presented a rendering of the AFCU building that is currently under
construction and noted that the developer knew the entire site would be developed, but the
credit union needed to begin construction immediately. They came to the City with a site
plan that laid all the groundwork for the Master Site Plan. The AFCU Site Plan was approved
by the Planning Commission on May 2, 2023, and that plan will not change. Mr. Prestwich
believes it will be open for business in fall 2024.

The proposed subdivision will have eight total lots, including the AFCU. Three phases have
been identified. Phase 1 is the backbone infrastructure and roads for the other pads on the
site, and Mr. Prestwich anticipated a final Site Plan for Phase 1 to be presented to the
Commission within the next two months. The individual lots will then come in any order. An
eight-foot precast wall will be installed by AFCU along the south property line to the Scooters
south boundary. He noted that Commissioner Rushton asked many questions about the
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                              10
August 8, 2024


site, and Mr. Prestwich copied all Planning Commission members on his responses.
Questions regarding lighting and grading will be addressed with the Master Site Plan, but
the fence installed by AFCU will protect the residential lots.

Two entrances are proposed, one from 3600 West and one from 12600 South. Truck
movements are shown on the site plan for the Fire Department review, and delivery trucks
will follow a similar path. All drive aisle widths are set by City Code. Details such as how
close the parking can be to sidewalks and the required landscaping between them, parking
counts, and stall widths are also set by Code.

Mr. Prestwich indicated that the Site Plan shows two drive-through type businesses and a
tire or automobile repair shop on 12600 South, but that is subject to change. Each pad's
site plan will be reviewed by the Commission prior to construction. A preschool is shown at
the rear inside corner, with play areas behind it. Lot 7 may be a hotel, although concerns
have been expressed about the 35-foot height limit, which is the maximum allowed by City
Code with no possibility for variations. Another lot may be a multi-tenant medical building.
The AFCU will be on the corner, with mixed uses on the remaining lots. There will also be
a connection to Scooter's Coffee. Access from 12600 South will be restricted; the islands
required by UDOT will ensure a right-in, right-out approach. The southern access from 3600
South has full entry/exit access. Mr. Prestwich noted that if the uses for any of the pads
change in a way that impacts access, it will be addressed with the final Site Plans.

The Master Site Plan includes signage for the development. The subdivision qualifies for
pole signs as long as they are inclusive of the entire development.

Grading is from west to east. The development will have underground stormwater storage
chambers, not above-ground ponds. The chambers function like a pond, but they are
underground so the real estate above them can be used. There are standards regarding
how they work, and design engineers know how to use them. The water collected in the
chambers either leach into the ground or seeps out slowly through pipes into the City system.
Stormwater Engineer, Tom Beasley, and City Engineer, Matt Castle, make sure that they
treat the water before it goes into the tanks so the porous space in the tanks does not fill
with sediment and prevent the water from leaching into the ground. Annual maintenance
and inspections are also required.

Mr. Prestwich noted that Staff from multiple departments reviewed the Site Plan and
recommended approval.

Commissioner Rushton thanked Mr. Prestwich for answering the questions he posed via
email and asked for clarification on the approval process regarding the businesses shown
on the Site Plan. For example, Lot 3 and Lot 4 show fast-food restaurants, but the final
tenant could be a different business type. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that the business types
could change based on the specific tenants for each pad. The developer and engineer
waited until they had a good idea of what types of businesses would be in the development.
He noted that they did a similar thing with VSM. There were a few modifications to the final
Site Plans, but not significant ones. If Lot 7, for example, becomes two separate drive-

through restaurants, a Master Site Plan amendment may be required.              The Planning

Commissioner Rushton asked if a tire store is an approved or conditional use in the C-G
zone. Mr. Prestwich clarified that the Commission was not approving any conditional uses
with the Master Site Plan. If anything is a conditional use, they will either apply for a
Conditional Use Permit concurrent with the Site Plan or independently prior to submitting
the Site Plan. Hotels and preschools are conditional, and drive-throughs might also be

Chair Matheson asked where the left-in access was located. Mr. Prestwich stated that a left

the uses change, it is possible the City could require an island to be installed on 3600 West
to prevent left-hand turns.

Chair Matheson asked for a more detailed explanation of the treated water in the
underground tanks. Mr. Prestwich stated that a box is installed with a lid that is similar to a
sewer lid. Water flows into the tank. Garbage and oil float to the top of the tank and sediment
falls to the bottom, which are removed during regular maintenance. The water then flows
through baffles and into the underground system. The water going into the underground
system is cleaner than the water that enters the tanks, but it is not chemically treated. He
noted that manhole access into a concrete box is easy to maintain with a vacuum truck.

The applicant's engineer, Grayson Smith, thanked Mr. Prestwich for his presentation and
stated that he had nothing to add, but was happy to answer any questions.

be made for a median on 3600 West to ensure that there is no conflict at the northern
driveway, but the traffic study did not identify any issues.

be to make the entry wider and if it would need to be part of this approval. Mr. Prestwich

the final Site Plan for Lot 5 is presented because that access will not be open until that time.
It was noted that that specific access was not included in the traffic study, but there is an
agreement between the properties regarding cross-traffic. Any widening on 12600 South
would be at UDOT's discretion.

Commissioner Cannon stated that, if both In-N-Out Burger and Starbucks open in the
development, traffic will be an issue. Vehicular traffic from the main corridors is a concern,
but pedestrian traffic should also be considered. He asked if it was appropriate to give
blanket approval on the Master Site Plan without knowing which individual businesses would
go into the development. Mr. Prestwich confirmed that that is the process. The Master Site
Plan is approved after considering the perimeter impacts and backbone infrastructure and
with a general idea of what businesses will be included, and then the specific details are
worked out with each final Site Plan. The uses can change, so the specific information is
reviewed when the final Site Plan applications are considered.

The example was given that Lots 7 and 8 were originally going to be an office complex. A
traffic impact study was done that indicated the possibility that it would create internal trips
and reduce the number of trips that would impact 3600 West and/or 12600 South. Since
that time, it has changed to two lots containing a possible hotel and possible school, which
changes traffic movement. The traffic study was redone to account for those specific uses.
If the uses change again, the traffic study will again be updated to reflect the new
occupancies.

where there is a central, anchor tenant like Lowe's or Walmart, that business carries the flag
for the rest of the project, but it is all one commercial site. When Walmart came to Riverton,
