Annexation Plan

                Council Member Stitzer stated the annexation policy was drafted on time to be
publicly noticed for the planning commission to review. They met last night to get that information.
There will be a 10-day period where people can submit comments or revisions. After those
comments are heard, the planning commission will hold a public hearing. If there are no revisions,
it will be recommended to the metro township. The metro township will then hold a public hearing
and will be able to adopt a policy. If it is approved and a landowner wants to annex, they will have
a solid way of handling those petitions.

              Nathan Bracken stated the annexation plan policy process dictates that the
planning commission must take public comment for ten days before it can hold a public hearing.
The planning commission and the Council can hold public hearings on the same night. The public
hearings can be published, and public comment period can be completed in time for the planning
commission and Council to hold a public hearing in March.

ARPA Update

              Mayor Clayton stated Copperton has not spent any of its ARPA funds and will get
another tranche in the summer. There will be approximately $100,000 in ARPA funds to use.

               Council Member Olsen stated the main thing being considered is storm drains.
He has learned about permeable pavement that allows water to penetrate through the surface
and back into the ground. This could replace storm drains; however, this type of pavement is not
recommended for high traffic areas.

              Nathan Bracken stated he loves permeable pavement, but in Utah, the freezing
and thawing can damage the pavement, so it does not last long. If the technology changed, it
might be beneficial.

               Council Member Stitzer stated she researched permeable pavement for the
cemetery and the information she was given is the same as what Mr. Bracken said. It also does
not work well with snowplows.

               Council Member Olsen stated he will look into it further.

              Mayor Clayton stated the money could also be used for high-speed internet
access like Google Fiber.

Street Signs in Copperton

               Council Member Stitzer stated she and Council Member Olsen have discussed
the street signs in Copperton and agreed to remove all the signs that say, “Salt Lake County”.
They would also like the Copperton Township sign, coming into town, and the northeast corner
park sign replaced. There is concern over replacing the flashing school bus signs on the highway
because the highway is owned by UDOT. She asked Mayor Clayton if anyone had provided
options on the type of sign that does not cross the highway.

             Mayor Clayton stated he priced two HAWK crossings that are approximately
$8,000; however, there is not enough traffic to install them.

               Council Member Stitzer asked what the next step is in having that reevaluated.

               Mayor Clayton stated he will contact Crystal Hulbert, Salt Lake County
Engineering, to get the road reevaluated. There are funds in the budget for two flashing crosswalk
signs. He suggested the possibility of having a contest among the citizens to design the signs.

               Council Member Stitzer stated she liked that idea.

              Council Member Bailey stated they should also reserve the right not to choose
any of the submissions.

               Mayor Clayton stated the quote to purchase weatherproof reflective stickers to go
over the tops of all the existing signs would be $3,500.

                Council Member Stitzer, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved to
authorize Mayor Clayton as point of contact to purchase the stickers and instruct the contractor
to install them. The motion passed unanimously.

Committee/Board Updates

    Legislative Research Committee

               Nathan Bracken stated this has been a busy legislative session and there are
several land use bills to be aware of. One of them is HB303, which requires a 30-day notice to
be mailed to every property owner in town any time there is an amendment to the land-use code.
This would be quite costly and slow the Council down in doing ordinances. There is also HB151
which amends provisions relating to incentive payments for retail facilities.

               Mayor Clayton asked if it looks like HB303 is going to pass.

does not know if it will move forward in the Senate. It is being vigorously opposed by The League
of Cities and Towns. It enables property owners to lock in their rights before the code changes.

               Council Member Olsen stated Council Member Prokopis suggested that there be
a UPD 101 training for all incoming board members. They want to reach out to Harry Souval,

helpful.

    Council Member Bailey

       Unified Fire Authority (UFA)
       Unified Fire Service Area (UFSA)

              Council Member Bailey stated the fiscal year is starting and they are looking at
the budget. They are especially concerned with the inflation rate right now. It is well above 10
percent in some areas and is having an impact on a lot of things. They are not going to add any
FTEs but are concerned about overtime costs. Retirement costs for Tier 1 employees have gone
down. They will probably ask for a fee increase for all the members of UFSA.

    Council Member Stitzer

       Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District (WFWRD)

                Council Member Stitzer stated WFWRD is still working through the Salt Lake
County Council to become a local district. There have been revisions, but they have support from
the board of trustees.

    Council Member Severson

       Salt Lake County Animal Services

               Council Member Severson stated the January meeting was cancelled, but he
does have the 2021 annual report. Animal Services provided care and assistance to 14,000 pets.
Their field department responded to 17,200 service calls. Animals in the shelter were provided
with 15,000 pounds of food. The pet pantry distributed 32,000 pounds of food to 4,000 pets in
need. The in-house clinic provided 3,700 spay/neuter surgeries and 4,000 microchips. Their
amazing volunteers provided 3,000 service hours. Foster homes provided 71,000 foster care
hours. Animal Services is getting a new committee chair and vice chair at the next meeting.
