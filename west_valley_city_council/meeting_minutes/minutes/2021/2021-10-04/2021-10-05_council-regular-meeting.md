COMMENT PERIOD
A.  PUBLIC COMMENTS
    Alan Naumann stated that he participated 2 years ago in regard to HB411. He stated
    that while clean energy may cost a little more money that most people would be
    willing to spend more to have clean air. He is asking the city to take a huge leap
    and take part. He mentioned that he has been in the solar power business for 12
    years. He would like to invite the council to an event this Saturday regarding
    electric vehicles. The event will take place in the parking lot of city hall. Mr.
    Naumann also would like to see a sustainability department within the city.

       Kirk Lowe stated that this is the first time participating in a council meeting. He is
       concerned about the city’s stance on zero scaping in yards. He took an online class
       at Jordan Valley Water Conservator and was told that West Valley City is the only
       city that has not been out to take a tour or participate. Second, he is concerned
       about the lack of police presence on the westside of city, therefore would like to
       request more police patrolling in the neighborhoods.

       Darrell Curtis stated he lives close to Mountain View Corridor. He would like to
       have a 4-way stop sign put on Marsha Drive. He is also concerned about the engine
       brakes noise. He understands that this is not just a city issue, however a county
       issue as well. Would like to have better policing or cameras installed to record the
       sound so proof of the noise could be turned into the county due to it affecting the
       neighborhood. He mentioned signage stating engine brake noise enforced would
       even be helpful. He doesn’t want to move and doesn’t want this to drive people out
       of his neighborhood which he believes will happen if we don’t respond.

       Jim Vesock stated that we should get a seat at HB411. He stated that the last food
       truck event on Wednesday was very disappointing with less than 50 people in
       attendance. He mentioned that 2 of the 4 vendors left early due to lack of business.
       He would like to see Parks and Recreation do something better next year. He stated
       that city parks are not just used by West Valley City residents, but that people come
       from Magna, Draper, and West Jordan, therefore they need to be maintained. He
       stated that there is nothing worse than seeing sprinklers on when it is raining. He
       also recommended that we should insist our builders take some of the burden off
       the city financially. He attended the West Valley City Family Fitness Center VIP
       event. He believes that the fitness center is a crown jewel in the city therefore needs
       to be taken care of better. He is disappointed in the delay of timeline for some of
       the Parks and Recreation projects. Also has noticed that the men’s locker room at

PUBLIC HEARINGS
A.   ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING RE-OPENING THE FY 2021-2022
     BUDGET
     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled October 5, 2021, regarding re-opening the FY 2021-
     2022 Budget.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              State Statute Title 10, Chapter 6, Utah Code Annotated 1953, as amended,
              allows the City of West Valley to amend its budget during the year. The
              West Valley City holds public hearings on budget amendments on a
              quarterly basis each fiscal year.

              A Public Notice was posted September 23, 2021 in public view at City Hall,
              the Family Fitness Center and UCCC; also, posted on the Public Notice
              Website and West Valley City’s Website. Notice was given that a public
              hearing is to be held October 5, 2021 at 6:30 p.m., West Valley City Hall,
              3600 Constitution Blvd., West Valley City, Utah.

              City staff recommends approval of the Ordinance amending the budget of
              West Valley City Corporation for the fiscal year beginning July 1, 2021 and
              ending June 30, 2022, to reflect changes in the budget from increased
              revenues and authorize the disbursement of funds.

       Mayor Bigelow opened the Public Hearing.

       Jim Vesock expressed gratitude for whom prepared the budget. He stated it was
       very informative and he appreciated the rollover information. He did look for
       clarification regarding the donations for the Veterans Memorial Project.

  Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
  information as follows:

        The Meng Chhour, Sreng Chhour, Ov Lim Chhour, Siv Meng Chhour and
        Eang Eng Heng parcel located at 4914 West 4700 South is one of several
        parcels affected by the 4700 South Reconstruction Project (4000 West to
        5600 West). This project is being constructed in cooperation with Kearns
        Metro Township. This project will reconstruct the pavement, curb, gutter,
        sidewalk and decorative street lighting on both sides of 4700 South and
        4715 South to improve safety and ride quality of 4700 South and 4715
        South. Compensation for the purchase of the Warranty Deed, Perpetual
        Easement and a Temporary Construction Easement and improvements is
        $72,400.00 based upon the appraisal report prepared by Van Drimmelen &
        Associates, Inc.

        The project is being funded under the Surface Transportation Program
        (STP) with federal funds. Kearns Metro Township executed the Federal
        Aid Agreement with UDOT. West Valley City has signed an Interlocal
        Agreement with Kearns Metro Township. The city is responsible 6.77% of
        all project costs, including right of way, within West Valley City
        jurisdictional limits. The total value for the Warranty Deed, Perpetual
        Easement and a Temporary Construction Easement is $72,400.00, making
        the West Valley City share of the acquisition $4,901.48.

  Upon inquiry by Mayor Bigelow there were no further questions from members of
