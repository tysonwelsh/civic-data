3. Public Hearing: Seidel Residence Plat Amendment (Administrative Action)
A request by Stephen & Diana Seidel for approval of a Plat Amendment in the RA2 (Residential
Agricultural, 20,000 square foot lot minimum) Zone to vacate a portion of an access easement to
allow for a new home construction. The property is located at 1888 E 12200 S. Application
SUBD-890-2020. Staff contact, Pete Kane, 801-576-6335, pete.kane@draper.ut.us.
Pete Kane gave a brief description of the amendment. There were 11 lots, most of which had
already been built on. The property was zoned RA2 which meant it had a 20,000 square foot lot
minimum. Lots 10 and 11 had shared access and were flag locked. There were hammerhead
turnarounds on both properties. The one change was taking out the hammerhead turnaround on
lot 11, because the sprinkler system would not require this. He showed staff pictures of the
property.
Commissioner Van Hoff asked what the purpose of the hammerhead turnaround.
Chairman Adams replied it was for fire safety to allow for the fire truck to back up.
Don Buckley, Draper City Fire Marshal explained the house was already built, and so it was hard
to add the hammerhead now. The driveway would be strengthened and widened to allow for a
fire truck to back up. The owner was also adding sprinklers to the house.
The applicant, Stephen Seidel, stated staff did a good job at explaining the agenda item.
Chairman Adams opened the floor for public comment. There was no one who wished to speak,
so he closed the public comment.
Motion: Commissioner Traci Gundersen made a motion to approve the application with
the stipulations.
Second: Commissioner Kent Player
Vote: A roll call was taken with the Commissioners voting in favor of the motion. This item
passed with a vote of 5 to 0. Commissioners Hawker, Gundersen, Hoff, Player, and Tonks
voted “Aye” and no commissioner voted, “No”.
The motion passed unanimously.

Road). Applications DA-955-2020, TEXTMAP948-2020, & TEXTMAP-949-2020. Staff
contact: Pete Kane, 801-576-6335, pete.kane@draper.ut.us.
Mr. Kane explained the request. He showed pictures of the site. The request did not include the
land between Lone Peak and the plot. The neighborhood was zoned RA1, residential agriculture,
and the request was to change it to M1, lightening manufacturing. The next request was to
modify the allowed uses for the M1 zone for their property. Most of these modifications were
restricting them further. The last request was to amend the land use map to make all 22
properties business and lightening manufacturing.
The applicant, Ryan Dickson, commented they were trying to meet the demands of Draper City.
He was open to any questions.
Commissioner Van Hoff asked the size of the docking trucks. He was concerned about running
big trucks over a residential street.
Mr. Dickson replied they would-be full-size semi-trucks. He felt they could address the road
concerns.
Chairman Adams opened the public comment.
Colleen Hawker at 123 Golden Harvest Grove commented this issue had been going on for over
one year. Everyone was ready to move on and felt the plan worked.
Chairman Adams closed the public comment.
Commissioner Hawker believed they needed to have the engineer address concerns about the
road holding semi-trucks.
Brien Maxfield, an engineer, stated they would have to change the road to allow for holding
semi-trucks.
Commissioner Van Hoff asked how they could incorporate the road needing to be reengineered.

6. Public Hearing: Highline Signage Text Amendment Request (Legislative Action)
On the request of Justin Grubb with YESCO LLC to amend Draper City Municipal Code
(DCMC) Section 9-18K-070 Signage. The applicant is requesting that a Text Amendment be
approved to increase the allowed sign area in the CSD Highline Zone. Staff Contact is Travis
Van Ekelenburg, 801-576-6522 or travis.vanekelenburg@draper.ut.us. Application number:
TEXTMAP-952-2020
Mr. Van Ekelenburg stated the applicant was requesting an increase in sign area allowed for
businesses. The office buildings would now be allowed to have 600 square feet rather than the
previous allotted 200 square feet. Residential signs would now be allowed 200 square feet rather
than the previous allotted 100 square feet. Current signage was too small to help these
businesses. It would only affect Highline CFD.
Commissioner Van Hoff thought that was a significant increase in signage.
The applicant, Justin Grubb, clarified the 600-foot signs would be the maximum. This was
talking about buildings that were very large. A 200 square foot sign was tiny on a 1200-foot
building.
Chairman Adams opened up the public comment. There were no comments made and he closed
the public comment.
Motion: Commissioner Craig Hawker motioned they forward a positive recommendation
for the Highline Sign Text Amendment to the City Council.
Second: Commissioner Kent Player
Vote: A roll call was taken with the Commissioners voting in favor of the motion. This item
passed with a vote of 5 to 0. Commissioners Hawker, Gundersen, Hoff, Player, and Tonks
voted “Aye” and no commissioner voted, “No”.
The motion passed unanimously.

9. Public Hearing: Big Willow Creek 2nd Amendment Development Agreement Request
(Legislative Action)
On the request of Bryon Prince, representing Ivory Development, LLC, for a development
agreement on 20.9 acres of property located approximately at 491 West 11400 South. The
agreement will authorize various deviations to the Draper City Municipal Code Title 9 Land Use
and Development standards in regard to density allocation and minimum lot size. Application
number DA-931-2019. Staff contact is Jennifer Jastremsky, 801-576-6328,
jennifer.jastremsky@draper.ut.us.
Senior Planner Jennifer Jastremsky gave a presentation. The property is 20.9 acres and it is in
two different zones. The development agreement would incorporate the new units in Big Willow
Creek and requested the density to be spread across the property rather than having densities
placed based on the zoning district boundaries. The development agreement would allow the
applicant to build infrastructure improvements in Phase 2 before approval and before the
completion of a secondary access, at their own economic risk.
Mr. Maxfield, staff engineer, stated that engineering is opposed to the language as it is currently
written forwarding phase 2 to construction without having a secondary access component within
phase 2 or a secondary access built to public standards. .
The applicant, Bryon Prince, commented he would be happy to answer any questions. They were
trying to create a cohesive subdivision. Spreading the density would allow for more family
homes and less town homes.
Commissioner Player thought it was a good idea to mix family homes and town homes in the
subdivision.
Commissioner Van Hoff was concerned about the flexibility the developer would have with
building the units.
Mr. Prince replied the Commission would get to approve the plans for the units and where they
were to be located. They were willing to address the language concerns the engineering
department had.
Commissioner Player stated he was concerned about the additional houses and traffic the street
would receive.
Chairman Adams opened the public comment. There were no comments and so he closed the
public comment.
Commissioner Van Hoff was concerned about the secondary access and the flexibility the
developer had.
Ms. Jastremsky stated the flexibility allowed for different types of housing in the subdivision.
She showed possible motion language.
Motion: Commissioner Craig Hawker made a motion to give a positive recommendation
with City Council with the amended language that approval subject to the City Council
amending the language to its satisfaction regarding the limiting of the secondary access
with the approval and record action of the plat and the issuance of building permits for the
Big Willow Creek development agreement.
Second: Commissioner John Van Hoff
Vote: A roll call was taken with the Commissioners voting in favor of the motion. This item
passed with a vote of 5 to 0. Commissioners Hawker, Gundersen, Hoff, and Tonks voted
“Aye” and Commissioner Player voted, “No”.
The motion passed unanimously.
