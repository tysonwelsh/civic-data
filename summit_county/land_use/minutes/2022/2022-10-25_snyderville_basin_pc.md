Background
The applicant, Wasatch Crest Treatment Facility, Jim  Huff man representative, is seeking
approval of a Residential Treatment Facility at 241 South Highland Drive. The property is zoned
Rural Residential (RR).  Section  10-2-10 of the Development Code states that Residential
Treatment Facilities in the RR zone are a conditional use.  Section  10-11-1 of the Development
Code defines a Residential Treatment Facility as:
''RESIDENTIAL TREATMENT FACILITY: A twenty-four (24)  hour group  living environment
for four (4) or more individuals unrelated to the owner or provider that offers room or
board and specialized treatment, rehabilitation, or habilitation services for persons with
emotional, psychological, development, or behavioral dysfunctions, impairments, or
chemical dependencies.  In  residential treatment,  individuals are assisted  in acquiring
the social and  behavioral skills necessary for living independently in the community."

The proposed location of the use is an existing building with sixteen (16) bedrooms, seventeen
and a half (17 %) bathrooms, a living room, dining room, kitchen, laundry, and office area. The
use was originally approved in 2011 as a group home/assisted living center.
The proposed facility would be used to house up to 28 (previously 32) adult men and women 2
persons in each bedroom. Half of the bedrooms would be used for group living and the other
half for residential treatment living. Patients in the residential treatment program would stay
on site the entire time for approximately 1-7 days, after which they would be transferred to a
facility in Heber. Residents in the group living side would stay anywhere from 30 to 90-days.
These patients would leave the site during the day to go to jobs or receive off site treatment.
Comments from the Park City Fire District and County Building Department state that the
proposal would require minor internal remodeling of the building, but no building expansion is
proposed. The applicant would like to build an enclosure for a dumpster, a privacy fence on the
rear property line and a dedicated smoking area in the front of the building. None of these
structures require any special review other than a building permit.

October 11, 20Z2, Public Hearing
At the October 11, 2022, Snyderville Basin  Planning Commission (`'Planning Commission'')
meeting, a public hearing was held on a conditional use application for a proposed residential
treatment facility, known as the `'Wasatch Crest Treatment Facility."  Members of the public
requested further clarification of the following:
3
Wasatch Crest

1.    Whether the Summit County Code (`'Code'') would bar approval of the proposed
residential treatment facility based on a Code provision that prohibits operation of a
residential treatment facility for the handicapped or elderly within three-fourths (3/4)
mile of another such residential facility for the handicapped or elderly persons. There is
currently a group home located approximately .4 miles from the proposed location for
the Wasatch Crest Treatment Facility. The Commission requested that staff provide
analysis of whether this Code section would apply to bar the conditional use application
for the residential treatment facility.
2.    Whether the proposed circulation plan for deliveries and trash pick-up at the proposed
site are compliant with engineering and fire requirements.
RESIDENTIAL FACILITIES FOR THE ELDERLY OR DISABLED
With respect to the first issue, a group home is currently operating at 150 East Highland Drive.
Its function is to house students and provide therapy, training, and life skills for young women
to grow and recover from past circumstances. The group home is located approximately .4
miles from 241 Highland Drive, the proposed location of the residential treatment facility, and
therefore, within % of a mile of the proposed facility.
The remaining issue is whether the group home fits within the definition of ``residential facility
for elderly or handicapped persons" as set forth in Section 10-8-6. a. of the Summit County
Code. This section states, in relevant part, that, ``No residential facility for elderly or
handicapped persons shall be constructed or operated unless in compliance with the following
standards: 5. The facility is not located within three-fourths (3/4) mile of another residential
facility for elderly persons or residential facility for handicapped persons."
`'Residential treatment facility" is defined Summit County Code Section  10-11-1 as, "A twenty-
four (24) hour group living environment for four (4) or more individuals unrelated to the owner
or provider that offers room or board and specialized treatment, rehabilitation, or habilitation
services for persons with emotional, psychological, development, or behavioral dysfunctions,
impairments, or chemical dependencies. In residential treatment, individuals are assisted in
acquiring the social and behavioral skills necessary for living independently in the community."
The Code does not include a specific definition of '`residential facility for elderly or handicapped
persons.''
"Handicapped persons" is only used and defined in the Summit County Code in Section 10-11-1,
within the definition of "Group Home." ``Group Home" is defined as ``A dwelling shared by four
(4) or more persons, including resident staff, who live together as a single housekeeping unit
and in a long term, family like environment in which staff persons provide care, education, and
4
Wasatch Crest

participation in community activities for the residents with the primary goal of enabling the
resident to live as independently as possible. As used herein, the term "hand/.capped" shall
mean having; a) a physical or mental impairment that substantially limits one or more of such
person's major life activities so that such person is incapable of living independently; b) a record
of having such impairment; or c) being regarded as having such an impairment. The term
"handicapped" shall not include current illegal use of or addiction to a controlled substance, nor
shall it include any person whose residency in the home would constitute a direct threat to the
health and safety of other .ind.iv.iduals. The term "group home" shall not include residential
facilities for the handicapped, residential facilities for the elderly, alcoholism or drug treatment
centers, work release facilities for convicts or ex-convicts, or other housing facilities serving as
an alternative to incarceration''. (Emphasis added.)
The definition of ``group home" specifically excludes residential treatment facilities for the
handicapped and elderly.  Therefore, the existing group home cannot qualify as a ``residential
facility for the elderly or handicapped." The definition also specifically excludes ``drug treatment
centers."  Therefore, the proposed residential treatment facility cannot qualify as a ``residential
treatment facility for the elderly or the handicapped" for the purposes of Summit County Code
Section 10-8-6. 8 (5).
Staff concludes that Summit County Code Section 10-8-6. 8 (5) is inapplicable to the proposed
application for a residential treatment facility.
Trash and Service Deliveries
Concerns were raised that the proposed dumpster location would cause trucks to back into the
street and create a hazard to traffic. The Engineering Department reviewed the proposed site
plan and stated that although the situation is not ideal, it is not uncommon in the Snyderville
Basin, and can be made to work. They stated that the driveway width could be expanded to
provide more space for the truck to turn around, they could require that the dumpster be
wheeled out of the enclosure to a location where the trash could be picked up safely, or they
could require that the garbage truck have a `'spotter" to guide the truck when backing up. This
spotter would always be with the driver.
Staff received a letter from the trash service company:
"Republic Services services the current trash container at 241 Highland Drive in Park City. We have
reviewed the planned location for the new trash container and feel comfortable that we will be able to
safely and efficiently service the location as planned. We plan to utilize an 8-yard container, serviced no
more than once per week on Wednesdays. The driver is usually in the area between the hours of 1200 pin
to 300 pin, which is a relatively low traffic time. The truck is staffed with two individuals, driver, and
helper. Approximate time to service this container takes 10-15 min within the driveway of Wasatch Crest
Treatment Center. The container is within a parking drive that is not wide enough for the truck to make
turns within. This would require the spotter to be out of the truck to help the driver safely back onto the
frontage road when exiting. This process would slow or stop traffic on Highland drive for no more than 2

Having worked in residential treatment with children and adolescents, which require some of the most

important factor for patient safety.
For this area of my analysis,I reviewed the company staffing plan and evaluated patient-to-staff ratios
relative to patient census, acuity, and time of day. I also considered staff competency and levels of training
to ensure the proper support for the proposed type of patients.
Detox Staffing  Levels
These patients require more care and supervision due to the acuity of their symptoms. In order to meet
state licensing standards, detox staffing oversight must include a minimum patient-to-staff ratio of 8:1
during daytime operating hours, and 12:1 during the overnight shift. The current staffing plan exceeds
these levels.   By comparison, child and adolescent residential programs require the lowest patient-to-staff
ratios in the industry (4:1 in the waking hours,12:1 overnight), and I recommend something more in line
with these ratios to ensure the highest quality of care and safety.

Wasatch Crest Policies and Procedures
Security and Patient Oversight
To maintain safety for our patients, staff and community the following security measures will be
followed. The policies below are specific to safety and security. There are additional policies and
procedures to address the medical and clinical needs of our patients.
Visitation
Wasatch Crest requires all visitors to be approved prior to arriving at our facility. Patients must
complete a `'visitation request form." Once completed the request must be reviewed by our
clinical and administrative team. Visitors will be limited to individuals that are found to be
beneficial to the patient's recovery and the safety of the milieu. The number of visitors at any
point in time will be limited to available parking and designated visitation times. Wasatch Crest
reserves the right to deny visitation requests for any individual that may threaten the wellbeing of
a patient, or the group. Visitors will not be allowed for patients receiving detoxification services.
Curfew
Patients receiving detoxification services must always remain onsite. Patients residing in our
recovery residence are expected to follow a curfew of 10:00 PM on weekdays and 12:00 AM on
weekends. Patients will be unable to enter the building using their assigned key card after the
curfew. Any patients missing curfew must be escorted into the building by a staff member. This
allows us to monitor and ensure curfew is followed, and to discuss rule infractions where
appropriate.
Staffing
Wasatch Crest staff will be onsite 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Staff will include a minimum of
one registered nurse and additional qualified staff depending on the number of patients currently
receiving services, and the time of day. In addition, Wasatch Crest will maintain on-call medical
and clinical staff at all times.
Rounds and Patient Monitoring
Patients will be appropriately supervised at all times. Patients receiving detoxification services will
be visually monitored at a minimum interval of 30 minutes. Documentation and additional
oversight will occur in accordance with state requirements and medical best practices. Patients
residing in recovery residence are required to provide weekly schedules as well as notify staff and
peers of daily activities.  24-hour staff at the facility allows for the effective monitoring of these
patients, and their daily schedules.

Random Drug Screening
To ensure a safe and 100% sober environment, patients are required to submit to random urine
drug screening tests. Recovery residence patients are tested a minimum of 2 times per week. If
staff observes concerning behaviors the patient will be tested immediately. In the event of a
positive drug screen, staff intervention will occur immediately. Intervention may result in referral
to a higher level of care or discharge from the program depending on the circumstance.
Security Cameras
Security cameras will be placed in all critical areas of the facility to provide added patient
oversight. Cameras will be accessible for both onsite and remote staff, including a camera LCD
monitor located in the 24-hour nurse's office. Recordings will be maintained for a minimum of 30
days, and the administrative staff may review camera recordings for security or safety concerns at
any time.  Critical camera areas will include all entrances and exits, smoking areas, driveways,
parking, backyard, side yard, medication room, and all common areas of the building.
Facility Electronic Keycards / Door Locks
Staff and patients will be issued individual key cards that are linked to their staff or patient
number. All exterior doors, bedroom doors, staff areas, and gates are secured with the keycard
system. This allows Wasatch Crest to monitor the use of each individual keycard at all times.
Keycards are restricted based on patient or staff access. Patient keycards will allow entrance and
exit of the building and assigned bedroom during the hours not restricted by curfew. A patient
wishing to enter the building after curfew will be required to be escorted into the building by a
staff member. Keycard use may be reviewed by administrative staff at any time to determine who
and when someone has accessed any area of the property. All exterior doors, bedroom doors, staff
areas, and gates are secured with this system, requiring a valid keycard for entry/exit.
Privacy Fences and Property Use
Privacy fences and/or screens will be built to provide privacy for patients and neighbors. The
outdoor common areas will be located within these screened areas. Patients will be restricted to
designated areas depending on their level of care. Patients are not allowed to use the neighboring
residential streets for congregating, walking or recreation. The walking path located along
Highland Drive will be the only permissible outdoor walking area for patients.
Patient Vehicles
Patients receiving detoxification services are not allowed to park at the facility.
Patients residing in the recovery residence may be allowed a vehicle based, in part, on availability
of parking spaces. All patients must complete a ``Personal Vehicle Request Form" to be reviewed
by administrative staff and the clinical team. Vehicle approval is based on treatment progress,
program engagement, and individual needs. Vehicles will not be permitted unless approved by
program staff, and sufficient parking is available to accommodate the vehicle.

Wasatch Crest Policies and  Procedures
Security and  Patient Oversight
To maintain safety for our patients, staff and community the following security measures will be
followed. The policies below are specific to safety and security. There are additional policies and
procedures to address the medical and clinical needs of our patients.
Visitation
Wasatch Crest requires all visitors to be approved prior to arriving at our facility. Patients must
complete a '`visitation request form." Once completed the request must be reviewed by our
clinical and administrative team. Visitors will be limited to individuals that are found to be
beneficial to the patient's recovery and the safety of the milieu. The number of visitors at any
point in time will be limited to available parking and designated visitation times. Wasatch Crest
reserves the right to deny visitation requests for any individual that may threaten the wellbeing of
a patient, or the group. Visitors will not be allowed for patients receiving detoxification services.
Curfew
Patients receiving detoxification services must always remain onsite. Patients residing in our
recovery residence are expected to follow a curfew of 10:00 PM on weekdays and 12:00 AM on
weekends. Patients will be unable to enter the building using their assigned key card after the
curfew. Any patients missing curfew must be escorted into the building by a staff member. This
allows us to monitor and ensure curfew is followed, and to discuss rule infractions where
appropriate.
Staffing
Wasatch Crest staff will be onsite 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Staff will include a minimum of
one registered nurse and additional qualified staff depending on the number of patients currently
receiving services, and the time of day.  In addition, Wasatch Crest will maintain on-call medical
and clinical staff at all times.
Rounds and Patient Monitoring
Patients will be appropriately supervised at all times. Patients receiving detoxification services will
be visually monitored at a minimum interval of 30 minutes. Documentation and additional
oversight will occur in accordance with state requirements and medical best practices. Patients
residing in recovery residence are required to provide weekly schedules as well as notify staff and
peers of daily activities.  24-hour staff at the facility allows for the effective monitoring of these
patients, and their daily schedules.
53

Random Drug Screening
To ensure a safe and 100% sober environment, patients are required to submit to random urine drug
screening tests. Recovery residence patients are tested a minimum of 2 times per week. If staff observes
concerning behaviors the patient will be tested immediately. In the event of a positive drug screen, staff
intervention will occur immediately. Intervention may result in referral to a higher level of care or discharge
from the program depending on the circumstance.
Security Cameras
Security cameras will be placed in all critical areas of the facility to provide added patient oversight.
Cameras will be accessible for both onsite and remote staff, including a camera LCD monitor located in the
24-hour nurse's office. Recordings will be maintained for a minimum of 30 days, and the administrative
staff may review camera recordings for security or safety concerns at any time.  Critical camera areas will
include all entrances and exits, smoking areas, driveways, parking, backyard, side yard, medication room,
and all common areas of the building.
Facility Electronic Keycards / Door Locks
Staff and patients will be issued individual key cards that are linked to their staff or patient number. All
exterior doors, bedroom doors, staff areas, and gates are secured with the keycard system. This allows
Wasatch Crest to monitor the use of each individual keycard at all times. Keycards are restricted based on
patient or staff access. Patient keycards will allow entrance and exit of the building and assigned bedroom
during the hours not restricted by curfew. A patient wishing to enter the building after curfew will be
required to be escorted into the building by a staff member. Keycard use may be reviewed by
administrative staff at any time to determine who and when someone has accessed any area of the
property. All exterior doors, bedroom doors, staff areas, and gates are secured with this system, requiring a
valid keycard for entry/exit.
Privacy Fences and Property Use
Privacy fences and/or screens will be built to provide privacy for patients and neighbors. The outdoor
common areas will be located within these screened areas. Patients will be restricted to designated areas
depending on their level of care. Patients are not allowed to use the neighboring residential streets for
congregating, walking or recreation. The walking path located along Highland Drive will be the only
permissible outdoor walking area for patients.
Patient Vehicles
Patients receiving detoxification services are not allowed to park at the facility.
Patients residing in the recovery residence may be allowed a vehicle based, in part, on availability of
parking spaces. All patients must complete a "Personal Vehicle Request Form" to be reviewed by
administrative staff and the clinical team. Vehicle approval is based on treatment progress, program
engagement, and individual needs. Vehicles will not be permitted unless approved by program staff, and
sufficient parking is available to accommodate the vehicle.

Proposal
The  purpose  of the  amendments  is to  clarify and  define setback  language  regulating  building
setbacks from lakes, ponds, and streams.
Background
The Rural  Residential (RR),  Hillside Stewardship (HS), and  Mountain  Remote (MR) zones
require:
1.    The minimum setback from a naturally occurring year-round stream (other than East
Canyon Creek) shall be one hundred feet (100') from the centerline of the stream.
2.    The minimum setback from a lake, pond, or reservoir shall be one hundred feet
(100')-from the high-water mark.
There are a few problems with this language:
•     Neither naturallyoccurringyear-round stream, lake, pond, nor reservoirare defined in
the Code.

LAKE: A large body of still water formed  naturally that is surrounded  by land.
POND, ARTIFICIAL: A small  body of still water created  by excavating and/or diking dry land  and
used exclusively for such  purposes as stock watering, irrigation, settling basins, recreation
purposes, aesthetic ornamentation or as a  landscape/architectural feature.
POND, NATURAL: A small  body of still water that is surrounded  by land.  Natural  ponds may
arise naturally in floodplains, wetlands, as part of a river system, or may be created specifically
for habitat restoration.
RIVER: A natural stream of water of fairly large size flowing in a definite course or channel or
series of diverging and converging channels. A river is fed along its course by converging
tri buta ries.
STREAM, EPHEMERAL:  Ephemeral streams or spring flows are channels that do  not flow on a
regular annual  basis but flow only during maj.or storm  events. Their channels lack continuous

These definitions are identical to the definitions approved by the County Council in the Eastern

10-11-1 Terms Defined
LAKE: A large body of still water formed  naturally that is surrounded  by land.
POND, ARTIFICIAL: A small body of still water created  by excavating and/or diking dry land and
used exclusively for such purposes as stock watering,  irrigation, settling basins, recreation
purposes, aesthetic ornamentation or as a  landscape/architectural feature.
POND, NATURAL: A small  body of still water that is surrounded  by land.  Natural  ponds may
arise naturally in floodplains, wetlands, as part of a river system, or may be created specifically
for habitat restoration.
RIVER: A natural stream of water of fairly large size flowing in a definite course or channel or
series of diverging and  converging channels. A river is fed along its course  by converging
tributaries.
STREAM, EPHEMERAL:  Ephemeral streams or spring flows are channels that do not flow on a
regular annual  basis but flow only during major storm events. Their channels lack continuous
