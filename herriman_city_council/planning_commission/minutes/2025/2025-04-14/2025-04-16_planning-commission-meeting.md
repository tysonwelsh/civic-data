    1.2. Review of Agenda Items – Planning Staff

Planner Howa began the review of the agenda items by mentioning Item 4.1 the natural gas
regulation station for Enbridge, a project brought forward due to the need for additional regulation
capabilities to accommodate current demand and future growth in Herriman. He highlighted that the
site was in the Hidden Oaks development area and included plans for a 15x15 foot building meant to
house necessary equipment. The station would be surrounded by an 8-foot precast fence with a
wrought iron gate, designed to blend with the local aesthetic through thoughtful landscaping choices
meant for low maintenance due to the site's unmanned status.

Attention then shifted to item 4.2 the 9-lot residential subdivision proposed for Sky Ranch Phase 4,
located off Rose Canyon Road. The subdivision met the maximum density requirements of 1.8 units
per acre pursuant to its recent rezoning. Planner Howa emphasized plans for a singular access point
to the subdivision from Rose Canyon Road and noted the use of precast walls as per ordinance
requirements. He acknowledged minor landscape concerns due to the configuration of future drive
approaches but assured that these would be resolved during final planning.

Planner Hoadley elaborated on item 4.3 the Mountain View Plaza subdivision amendment, explaining
the adjustments involving lots 5 and 6, and changes to common areas to enhance maintenance
responsibilities and eliminate confusion among future property owners. She described the intention
behind clarifying common area obligations and the re-organization of the buildings within the lots to
sustain a cleaner and more straightforward management plan for the area.

Planning Manager Spencer provided details about item 4.4 the Auto Mall subdivision amendment,
which proposed increasing lot 3 by adding 2 acres to facilitate expansion by a car dealership. The
modification aligned with zoning requirements reflecting a straightforward procedural approval
given its compliance with existing zoning codes.

Subsequent discussions touched upon items 4.5 and 4.6 on the agenda, including amendments for
the Midas Crossing retail subdivision and the Garden Plaza subdivision. The commission noted no
significant concerns, highlighting that these proposals predominantly focused on administrative
adjustments to existing plans for clarity and improved management of the respective spaces.

Further discussion of items 5.1-5.3 focused on amendments to the Master Development Agreement
(MDA) concerning Land Reserve's request to modify their plans, such as reducing commercial
acreage and allowing flexibility within designated areas. These requests involved potentially
increasing residential capacity consistent with city planning goals and adjusting land use as part of
negotiated agreements balancing development interests with community needs. Both the Midas
Crossing and Garden Plaza subdivisions were seen as minor amendments conforming to existing
regulations, with no significant concerns from the commission regarding these proposals.

    1.3. Review and Discuss Land Use Code Regulations, Standards, Policies, and Best
         Practices for Herriman City – Michael Maloy, Planning Director

Planning Director Maloy discussed updates to the referral policy, mentioning the inclusion of a
recommendation from Planner Howa regarding preliminary subdivision approval extensions that
would not exceed one year. The commission agreed with this addition, recognizing the importance
of defining constraints to maintain a structured development timeline. He detailed the commission's
ongoing interest in simplifying procedures for development partners. However, he acknowledged
persistent challenges due to mandatory public hearing requirements, which often extended the
process. These hearings required a period of public notification and feedback, subsequently
elongating the timeline for approvals. He proposed that future text amendments could address and
potentially eliminate public hearing requirements for more straightforward subdivision requests,
arguing that these procedural changes could lead to significant improvements in efficiency for both
staff and applicants. He suggested that removing these requirements for simple amendments or
small subdivisions could streamline approvals and reduce the administrative burden on staff,
aligning processes more closely with those of cities that have successfully removed similar barriers.
He noted this as a potential point of discussion for future amendments to the city's land use codes,
prompting the commission to consider how similar changes might be applied in Herriman City.
