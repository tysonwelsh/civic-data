         3.1     Legislative Session Update – Senator Karen Mayne

       Senator Karen Mayne explained she enjoyed coming to the different communities she
represented to explain in person how the legislative session had progressed. She explained that
she sat on the Criminal Code Evaluation Task Force wherein various situations were brought to
their attention if it was thought that a modification to state code could bring about positive change.
She gave an example involving people who came forward with information regarding sex
trafficking of young people in Utah. They wished to help put an end to the problem and Senator
Mayne indicated that with their help, important changes were made to the criminal code in this
area. This was just one example of work done by the committee.

     Safety inspection processes were mentioned. She noted many people want safety inspections
for vehicles. She stated she was planning to research the mechanical inspections of vehicles and
see what can be done.

Senator Mayne stated that two years ago it was brought to her attention that there were non U.S.
citizens who wished to be police officers but were prohibited from serving in that capacity. She
pointed out that there was no citizenry requirement to serve in the U.S. military. Her legislation
was designed to change the criteria to allow non-citizens to serve as law enforcement officers. She
felt diversity was needed in police departments and this legislation would increase diversity in the
ranks.

       Sen. Mayne brought up public notice requirements and the challenges that were faced as
“newspapers of general circulation” became more limited. She sponsored a bill that eliminated
the requirement of a newspaper notice in many situations.

     Senator Mayne mentioned transportation changes and commented that Senator Harper was
deeply involved in such issues and she would defer to him on those topics.

There had been multiple pieces of mental health legislation that Senator Mayne hoped would lead
to some answers about the high rate of suicide in Utah. She mentioned a multitude of other bills
that had been passed in the most recent legislative session, including involving affordable housing.
She also responded to questions from councilmembers.

     Chair Cochran expressed an interest in talking to the Senator about what appeared to be a lack
of regulation of what mobile home park property owners could charge tenants for utilities. Senator
Mayne stated she was aware of the issue and would be happy to speak to him outside the meeting.

Senate Bill 61 – Lighted Billboards: He noted the bill was defeated by a narrow margin and that
the outdoor sign industry indicated they would be stepping back and contacting individual cities
to better understand their concerns.

      Bill 144 was also related to billboards and was also defeated, again by a narrow margin.

HB 82, Auxiliary Dwelling Units (ADU) - This legislation was a threat to local control of housing
issues. He recounted the history of the bill and explained that elements of the Taylorsville City
ordinance were incorporated into the bill. Mr. Hiskey also commented on short-term rentals (i.e.
Air B&B) and legislation that would have limited local control on such rentals was defeated.

Councilmember Harker asked for clarification on HB 82. Mr. Hiskey deferred to City Attorney
Tracy Cowdell for more detailed information. Mr. Cowdell stated it was a very complicated issue
which was difficult to enforce. While Taylorsville did not issue licenses for short-term rentals,
people providing short-term rentals often did so without concern for licensing limitations. City
staff and Council continued to discuss the challenges of ADUs.

Building Inspections (HB 98) - Mr. Hiskey explained that the original version of the bill would
have allowed builders of single-family homes as well as commercial buildings to choose a private
inspector rather than a city inspector. The final bill (which was passed) still gave the City 14 days
in which to review a plan for a single-family home. If the City failed to do so, it would be eligible
for an additional 14-day extension if the developer agreed. Or the developer could opt to have its
own engineer or architect certify the plan, relieving the City of its inherent liability. It appeared
that most cities had no difficulty meeting the 14-day deadline but if a builder ended up using an
outside inspector, the building code would need to be followed.

As for building inspections, Mr. Hiskey explained that cities would have three days in which to
complete an inspection. It was his understanding that was not typically a problem. If the City
failed to meet that three-day deadline, the developer would then have the option to hire an outside
inspector who would need to be accredited and certified through the State of Utah.

      Mr. Hiskey then explained various pieces of legislation that related to law enforcement
entities. He stated these entities came together to discuss the following:

SB 13 – Required law enforcement agencies to report certain investigations to the Peace Officers
Standards and Training (P.O.S.T.) It also required that documented information about police
employees be provided to prospective police employers upon request and would provide immunity
for the entity which provided the required information.

HB 59 – Provided for criminal penalties for the misuse of evidence in certain types of cases.

        3.4     Y2 Survey Report – Kyrene Gibb

      Kyrene Gibb provided information regarding the survey of city residents conducted in
November 2020. It was a follow up to one conducted in 2019 and she pointed out that although
the level of satisfaction appeared to be reduced from the initial survey, she felt the reduction was
largely in response to the pandemic and not necessarily specific to services Taylorsville was or
was not providing. She noted residents gave the City’s health and quality of life a very positive
rating and indicated there were five specific findings she wished to share with the City Council.
     Overall, the respondents were happy with the way the city was being run
     Residents’ major concerns were focused on traffic, growth, and crime.
     Most respondents were pleased with the way Taylorsville had handled the pandemic
     Respondents had increased the amount of time spent recreating in public parks and their
        satisfaction with City parks and open spaces had increased slightly from the previous year
     Most of the respondents still wished to receive their information about the City from the
        Taylorsville Journal (37%), an email from the City (26%) or social media (6%).

Ms. Gibb explained that the survey results were based on 517 respondents which was a roughly
14% response rate. Ms. Gibb explained that was considered a fairly good rate in the
survey/research industry.

        8.5     Councilmember Barbieri – Discussed the Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling
District (WFWRD) moving from being a special service district to a local district. She noted the
City would need to adopt a resolution to approve this change and that the Salt Lake County Council
would need to do so as well. Councilmember Barbieri stated she had spoken with County
Councilmember Aimee Winder Newton regarding this, and she expressed interest in keeping
control at the local level as much as possible.

With the current status of an SSD, most decisions needed go through Salt Lake County for approval
which could cause delayed responses and communication issues. Ms. Barbieri stated this change
would allow for WFWRD to function more expeditiously. Salt Lake County needed to agree to
allow it to become a local entity before it could happen, and every single member agency would
need to do so as well.

     Tracy Cowdell stated that some cities had already passed resolutions in favor of the change.
He indicated there did not seem to be a downside to approving it. WFWRD’s status as an SSD
allowed Salt Lake County to have more power than perhaps the cities would like. This change
would create an autonomous entity.

WFWRD director Pam Roberts was in attendance and was asked to speak on the subject. She
pointed out that it was a fee-based business. They operated on cash that was received each quarter.
She felt that was a great way to operate a business. Ms. Roberts provided some history about the
creation and evolution of WFWRD before stating that there were five other municipalities that had
already adopted a resolution supporting the change.

Councilmember Armstrong asked about the voting process on WFWRD’s governing board. Ms.
Roberts responded that each member city had one vote—the votes were equal and not dependent
upon the size of the cities. Mr. Armstrong asked if the resolution could be approved without 100%
approval from the municipalities. Ms. Roberts explained that every single member city needed to
approve the change for it to take place. Discussion ensued regarding appointing the board.

Councilmember Harker asked if there would be differences felt by the residents and Ms. Roberts
responded that there would not be.
