Mr. Spung conducted the briefing session.

          He opened it up for suggestions and concerns by the Commissioners on ways to improve the function of
the Commission. Commissioner Burggraaf addressed the possibility of holding extra Planning Commission meetings
if there were a reason presented to do so. Commissioner Quigley suggested having updates on what is going on in
the City more often, such as what new businesses have opened and which are planning to come into the City. He
would also like to have more training available for the Commissioners. Commissioner Wendel said she had received
a flyer recently advertising some available training in March and said she would forward to those interested.

Commissioners an update of what is going on, i.e., the Beltway Development is in progress. That Staff is in meetings
with the developers of the K-Mart project to try and get them refreshed on the process. The development
agreement has not been signed yet, so not much is happening at this point. He announced that the Carriage Square
project is going to get some CDGB money to pay for the anticipated upgrades there. Staff is currently working with
consulting groups on architectural designs for that.

          Mr. Spung returned to review of tonight’s agenda saying that the first item on tonight’s agenda File #43C21,
is a conditional use amendment for improvements to Valley Regional Park and that Salt Lake County has applied for
a building permit to replace the current Valley baseball complex with a new facility. The Planning Commission is
hearing this item since it involves open space and the Planning Commission will end up conducting a review of the
plan. Commissioner Burggraaf said the review tonight will be the conditional use permit hearing. Mr. Spung advised
the second item is File 8S21, for consideration of a 33-lot residential preliminary subdivision plat for 8.089 acres of
land along 4800 South in Taylorsville, Utah. This item has been heard previously in the conceptual stage and is back
for the full subdivision review of this very large project.

  3.       Public Hearing and Consideration of a Conditional Use Permit Amendment for Improvements to Valley
           Regional Park Located at 5135 South Jordan Canal Road, Taylorsville, Utah. (Jim Spung, AICP/Senior
           Planner)

3.1     Mr. Spung presented this item.        He advised that Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation is requesting
Planning Commission review and approval of a conditional use permit amendment for improvements to Valley
Regional Park located at 5135 South Jordan Canal Road, Taylorsville, Utah.

         3.1.1     The softball complex is an existing facility that was constructed around 1975. This has been a well-
used facility and has served as a great asset to the community and Taylorsville. Due to its’ age, current deterioration,
and extensive use, Salt Lake County is requesting to replace the facility with a new one in the same footprint. The
updated facility would comply with ADA accessibility standards, provide new concessions, and improved seating.
The improvements are intended to serve the next several generations of facility users. The proposed improvements
include resurfacing of the south parking area; replacement of the pedestrian accessway from the south parking area
to the sports complex; reconstruction of the sports complex building; and various utility upgrades.

         3.1.2    The property is in the Open Space (OS) zoning district. Pursuant to Section 13.10.010 of the
Taylorsville Land Development Code, all development in the OS district is subject to Planning Commission review
and approval. In addition, the “recreation, outdoor” land use requires non-administrative conditional use permit
review and approval in the OS zoning district.

          3.1.3    Staff found no contradictions with City Code; the General Plan shows this site as parks and open
space; it is the same footprint being upgraded with a new facility in its place. He showed the planned site plan and
the proposed architectural elevation. That as the current use and size of structure is not changing, the proposed
construction is not expected to change the traffic or parking demand from the current use. There are no detrimental
impacts anticipated with this change.

         3.1.4    Mr. Spung said the proposed upgrades are consistent with the current use and structures on site.
Staff does not recommend additional conditions or modifications be made to the submitted proposal.

         3.1.5   The applicant has filed a building permit application with the City (CBLD-301998-2021). Condition
Use Permit and Planning Commission approval are required before the Community Development Department can
approve the building permit. Construction activity is not permitted on site until applicable permits have been issued
by the City.

  4.       Public Hearing and Consideration of a 33-Lot Residential Preliminary Subdivision Plat for Approximately
           8.089 Acres of Real Property Located at 4701 S. 1065 W.; 1040 W. 4800 S.; 1050 W. 4800 S.; 1054 W.
           4800 S.; 1066 W. 4800 S.; 1078 W. 4800 S.; and 1088 W. Turnberry Way, Taylorsville, Utah. (Jim Spung,
           AICP/Senior Planner)

4.1      Mr. Spung presented this item. Brad Reynolds Construction is requesting Planning Commission review
         and approval of a 33-lot residential preliminary subdivision plat, “Meadows on 48th” for approximately
         8.089 acres of real property at aforementioned properties. Approval of the preliminary subdivision plat
         would allow for final subdivision review and approval of 33 new single-family residential lots as well as new
         public street infrastructure. The existing dead-end street (Turnberry Way) would be continued eastward
         and transition into Turnberry Cove, terminating with a cul-de-sac. A new public street (Taylors View Lane)
         would extend southward from Turnberry Way and connect to Murray Taylorsville Road (4800 South). The
         new public street infrastructure is necessary to provide adequate ingress and egress needed to serve the
         lots of the proposed subdivision. The proposed street configuration has been reviewed and approved by
         the City Engineering Department.

         4.1.1     According to the Salt Lake County Assessor data, the subject property is made up of ten individual
                   parcels with a cumulative area of 352,364 square feet (8.089 acres). The developer has purchased
                   the property within the proposed subdivision plat boundary except for the southern portion of
                   Parcel 21.020377-030-0000. On October 26, 2021, the Taylorsville-Bennion Improvement District
                   (TBID) Board met and approved the developer’s request to purchase the southern portion of this
                   parcel; which is necessary to continue Turnberry Way eastward. The developer is coordinating
                   with TBID to finalize that transaction.

         4.1.2     On Aug 20 and 31, 2021, demolition permits were issued authorizing the demolition of existing
                   structures, sheds and fences within the boundary of the proposed plat. The single-family dwelling
                   at 1050 West 4800 South remains and will be removed prior to construction of the new public
                   street.

         4.1.3     The developer is proposing to vacate several existing easements in conjunction with this plat as
                   they will no longer be necessary. Several new easements are proposed to be established and
                   shown on the plat.

         4.1.4     The irrigation canal will be piped, having received permission from the Jordan Irrigation Company
                   to do so. There is a TBID pump house on the site, which the developer is working on obtaining
                   access thereto.

         4.1.5     The developer is requesting approval of a reduced park strip throughout the development. The
                   developer is requesting a 5-foot park strip and a 5-foot sidewalk in lieu of an 8-foot park strip and
                   5-foot sidewalk. The proposed arrangement is consistent with adjacent development and satisfies
                   the minimum requirement established in the Land Development Code. Staff supports this request.

         4.1.6     The developer is proposing a stormwater detention area at the southern end of the proposed
                   subdivision (Parcel A). At this time, it is assumed that the City will own this parcel; however the
                   developer is exploring options related to the ongoing maintenance. If Parcel A will be maintained
                   privately, a Home Owner’s association (HOA) must be established, declaring the HOA responsible
                   for Parcel A, including ongoing maintenance and repair of all improvements thereon. A note must
                   be placed on the plat including language to clarify the responsible party. As of the time of this
                   meeting, an agreement between the developer and the City has not been formalized.
