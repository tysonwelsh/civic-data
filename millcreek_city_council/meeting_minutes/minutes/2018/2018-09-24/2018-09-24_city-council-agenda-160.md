1. Discussion on Citizen Cash Contributions Towards Infrastructure Upgrades; John
Miller, Public Works Director
John Miller said he as has had complaints from constituents stating that they would contribute
funds towards infrastructure upgrades if they had the opportunity to do so. The City needs to
decide if that is something the City is willing to accept. The City currently does a 50/50
sidewalk program to help enhance sidewalks with half residents’ participation and half city
participation. Mayor Silvestrini said some neighborhoods would be able to afford things more
than other neighborhoods. Miller said five residents did the 50/50 sidewalk program last year
and three residents have this fiscal year. David Baird, Mount Olympus Community Council
Chair, said he would like to speed up speed control. He said there are current conditions that
exist that need to be addressed sooner than the Council can get to it. He said he does not see
any problems with private contributions funding city standard infrastructure. Council Member

2. Discussion on Canyon Rim Splash Pad Study; Rita Lund, Communications & Programs
Director
Mayor Silvestrini said the City did receive a grant for a splash pad at Canyon Rim Park.
Wayne Johnson, Salt Lake County Parks & Recreation, introduced Eric Powell with ArcSitio
Design who did the feasibility study. Mayor Silvestrini said the City has an arrangement with
the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints to share some parking to the north of Canyon
Rim Park. Powell said the design shows 88 parking stalls with 38 more than what is currently
there, though 120 parking stalls is what is needed at a minimum for this park. Cost estimates
were provided for a reuse system, $1,270,353, and a recirculation system, $840,483. Council
Member Catten asked how full the parking lot currently gets. Johnson said if there is a game
or event, the parking lot is full. Council Member Uipi asked how many trees would be
removed. Powell said about a half dozen for the parking expansion. Council Member
Marchant asked if funding for the pad was from the grant to the City. Mayor Silvestrini said
the grant is for $350,000 for the pad plus the cost of the study. Council Member Uipi asked
the Council where the continual funds for ongoing costs would come from.

Council Member Uipi said there are not a lot of neighboring splash pads, so this will draw
more than just residents. Council Member Marchant asked if the splash pad should be in the
new city center. Mayor Silvestrini said the City needs to do public engagement with the
neighbors around the park to see if they want the increased impact. Council Member Uipi
asked what the public process is for grants. Rita Lund said staff was waiting on the study first
to see what that would be. Council Member Jackson cautioned doing public engagement if the
City is not going to follow through with it. Council Member Catten would prefer to get the
input first. Council Member Marchant asked when the splash pads are open for use. Johnson
said in Bluffdale it runs from April 1 to September 30. Council Member Uipi asked if the
grant was for Canyon Rim Park or for anywhere in the City. Johnson said at Canyon Rim
Park. Council Member Catten asked about the life expectancy for the splash pad. Johnson said
it is about 10 years before parts are replaced.

Council Member Catten said she would not vote to approve the splash pad without more
parking. Mayor Silvestrini asked if the bathrooms would need to be expanded for the new
amenity. Johnson said no. Council Member Uipi asked how attendance is at other County
splash pads. Johnson said it is a come and go amenity, so there is not a good way of counting
attendance but the first few years after installation are the busiest. Council Member Jackson
asked when construction of this would take place. Johnson said it depends on funding, but it
could be as early as next year. Mayor Silvestrini said he is not sure the City can come up with
$2 million for parks this year. John Geilmann said the City is working on purchasing new park
property elsewhere and the City Hall expansion is happening this fiscal year, so he is not sure
there is another million dollars somewhere in the budget. Mayor Silvestrini said the best thing
to do is to ask the TRCC Board for a year extension. Francis Lilly said the Planning
Department is also working on a CDBG grant for Sunnyvale Park and it may require a series
of applications over several years and that type of funding often involves some monetary
matching on the City’s part.

3. Business Matters
3.1 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-53, Amending Code Chapters
5.01, 5.02, and 5.22 to Provide that Rental of One or More Residential Dwellings is
Included in the Definition of Business and Thereby Requires a Business License
Mayor Silvestrini said this ordinance requires single family rentals to obtain a business
license and to be subject to inspection. This requirement, in addition to the adoption of
the maintenance code, would improve the rental property situation in the City. Kurt
Hansen said the Council discussed this in a prior work session. Hansen said the ordinance
has technical changes that remove the County from code, changes the business license
requirement from 3 rental units to 1 unit, and adds a requirement for an affidavit. Hansen
said the idea is to inspect rentals the same as every business license application. The
owner of the property can sign the affidavit after completing the required items with the
stipulation that the City could inspect it as time-permitted or as was driven by complaints.
Council Member Marchant asked if the ordinance would apply to all existing rentals as
well as future rentals. Hansen said that was correct. Hansen said the effective date would
be January 1, 2019, so October, November, and December would be a grace period for
owners to get a license. Council Member Jackson asked how the City was planning on
getting the word out to property owners. Hansen said staff can run a search for all owners
whose primary address is not the Millcreek address. He can get the data from the County
Assessor’s Office. The assumption is that this would show what homes may be rentals.
Council Member Uipi asked if staff knows how many there are in the City. Lilly
anticipates in the hundreds. Council Member Marchant asked about the cost for the
license. Hansen said it would be $150 annually.

Mayor Silvestrini asked for public comment.

Kumar Shah, 4484 Parkview Dr., said he wanted to know what would happen if people
were noncompliant when the City does random inspection and where in the code it says
that. Hansen said there is a courtesy notice for 14 days to comply, then notice of
violation, then after 15 days there would be a fee. Mayor Silvestrini said it is in the
business license ordinance.

Council Member Uipi moved to close public comment. Council Member Catten seconded.
Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. The motion passed unanimously.

4.4 Unified Police Department Report

Chief DeBry said he has spoken to Mayor Silvestrini about an initiative called “Chat with
the Chief” to be held at City Hall. It is an open forum for the residents to speak to him
about police operations and delivery of services. He hopes to start the first week in
November.

Chief DeBry announced Officer Tracy Creno as Officer of the Month for August 2018.
She was dispatched on a domestic violence call and came to the scene of a crime. She
advised dispatch that she needed assistance, cleared the scene of threats, and administered
medical aid to the victims. She was able to get the suspect’s name and vehicle description
out to other officers. She served as the first responder and lead shift officer as the acting
supervisor. Mayor Silvestrini said this was a horrific event and thanked her for her
presence of mind to handle the situation and said he was glad to have her work in
Millcreek. The Citizens Advisory Board presented her with a gift.

Detective Prescott said he will send August crime statistics out later. He reported that two
weeks ago UPD did the Operation EASY (eliminating alcohol sales to youth) program
for three days. He said there are 31 businesses that sell alcohol in Millcreek and six
businesses failed. The six stores sold alcohol to underage buyers. The violation process is
that a citation is written to the clerk, the store is put on notice with the City and the State,
the second violation is a fine, and the third violation the business gets shut down from
selling alcohol for thirty days. They do these operations once in the spring, once in the
summer, and twice in the fall. Council Member Marchant asked why Detective Prescott
does not report the failed business’ names to the Council. Detective Prescott said he gives
the report to the State. Mayor Silvestrini said Millcreek received an award of beer sales
tax money from the State to be used on alcohol education and enforcement. Those funds
support this program and the City will be purchasing mountain bikes for enforcement on
trails. Detective Prescott provided contact information for the precinct, civil division,
dispatch, and gang/tip hotline to the audience. Chief DeBry said the public should go
through dispatch in non-emergency situations. Detective Prescott showed images of
transient camps near Grandeur Peak and said they are coming back along the Jordan
River. Council Member Marchant shared a story in which victims to a crime were
grateful for the help they received from the Unified Police Department.
