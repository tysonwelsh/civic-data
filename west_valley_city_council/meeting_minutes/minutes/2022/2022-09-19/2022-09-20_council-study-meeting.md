      RESOLUTION 22-144: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO ENTER INTO A REAL
      ESTATE PURCHASE AGREEMENT AND ACCEPT A SPECIAL WARRANTY
      DEED WITH AND FROM THE ALAN FRED BROCK FAMILY LIVING TRUST
      FOR PROPERTY LOCATED AT APPROXIMATELY 4435 SOUTH 4400 WEST
      Nancy Day, Parks and Recreation Director, presented proposed Resolution 22-144 that
      would authorize the City to Enter Into a Real Estate Purchase Agreement and Accept a Special
      Warranty Deed With and From the Alan Fred Brock Family Living Trust for Property Located
      at Approximately 4435 South 4400 West.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
      follows:

             West Valley City has owned a small parcel of land dedicated for the purpose of a
             future park. This land is located on 4325 W Maple Meadows Dr. (4420 S.). In
             reviewing the locations of park properties within West Valley City neighborhoods,
             it was discovered that the district where this parcel is located is in need of additional
             park space. A vacant piece of property located at approximately 4435 South 4400
             West is directly adjacent to the small parcel listed above. The West Valley City
             Parks and Recreation Department contacted the property owners of this property to
             see if there was interest in selling the property to the City. The addition of this land
             will allow the city to create a nice neighborhood park. The property owners had
             interest in this and an appraisal was obtained. A purchase price of $1,538,000 was
             agreed upon by the landowners and West Valley City.

      Councilmember Nordfelt asked if the park property can be accessed from the west. Nancy
      replied no and noted that the Brock’s will maintain ownership of several outbuildings and
      some farming property on the west. She indicated that there will likely be parking stalls as
      well as access points on the east and added that there will likely be restrooms as well for a
      park of this size. Councilmember Nordfelt asked if there will be pickleball courts at this
      location. Nancy replied that this is something that staff would take into consideration but
      noted that too many courts would not be recommended given the increase of potential
  MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – SEPTEMBER 20, 2022

traffic and on-street parking in the neighborhood since these courts draw residents from
other areas. Nancy indicated that lights, type of playground equipment, etc. is all taken
into consideration when parks are constructed in established neighborhoods such as this
one. She stated that neighborhood involvement will be important and both the City and
architects working on the project will get input from residents on what they’d like to see.
Councilmember Harmon stated that he would agree and would like to see pickleball courts
at this location. He asked if the Brock’s would be interested in keeping the small section
of property on the northwest side of the project so that there is an even cutoff for the park.
Nancy replied that this was discussed but the Brock’s didn’t want to maintain that much
property. She noted that the City will ensure that the design prevents any potential for
creating a problem area at this location. Mayor Lang asked how long before the park could
begin construction. Nancy replied that there are several parks being worked on now and
the development of this park will be dependent on funding. She noted that the City would
like to maintain a “farm” theme and that may provide for potential grant funding that can
help fast-track the process. Mayor Lang suggested that the property be leased to the
Brock’s or others who may be interested in continuing to farm the land. Nancy agreed and
indicated that the City would like to see this as well and noted that this would help with
construction as the soil would be in good condition. She indicated that the Brock’s are not
opposed to it and those conversations will occur as this purchase moves forward. Wayne
asked how many projects are currently on the Parks development list. Nancy replied that
there are several being worked on now but there are an additional 5 or 6 awaiting funding,
design, or development. Councilmember Whetstone expressed concern about the ratio of
parks maintenance workers to acreage. Nancy agreed that this is something that will be
taken into consideration as more parks are developed. She indicated that additional
personnel will need to be considered but noted that technology is helping reduce some of
the man hours needed.

The City Council will consider Resolution 22-144 at the Regular Council Meeting
scheduled September 27, 2022 at 6:30 P.M.

A.   CONSIDER APPLICATION SD-2-2022, FILED BY DR HORTON,
     REQUESTING CONSIDERATION TO DEDICATE A PORTION OF 4100
     SOUTH AT APPROXIMATELY 4100 SOUTH 8400 WEST
     Steve Lehman, CED, discussed proposed Application SD-2-2022, Filed by DR
     Horton, Requesting Consideration to Dedicate a Portion of 4100 South at
     Approximately 4100 South 8400 West.

      Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
      information as follows:

             DR Horton is requesting consideration to dedicate a portion of 4100 South
             west of U-111. The proposed dedication is necessary to provide access to
             a future project within SL County to be known as Little Valley.

             Property west of U-111 at this location is mostly in Salt Lake County,
             Magna Township. This area is undergoing a major transformation with a
             variety of residential, community use and other uses planned on the west
             bench of the valley. The extension of 4100 South, as shown on the Wasatch
             Front Regional Transportation Plan, will provide needed access not only for
             the Little Valley project but for property in West Valley City located south
             of 4100 West.

             Discussions about this access have been ongoing between the City and
             developer for quite some time regarding an acceptable alignment at the
             intersection of 4100 South and U-111. There are two proposed plats needed
             for the dedication. One for that portion lying within the boundaries of West
  MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – SEPTEMBER 20, 2022
                          -5-


             Valley City and the other plat for that portion lying within the boundaries
             of Magna Township. The portion within West Valley City occupies an area
             of approximately 1.7 acres.

             In summary, this application dedicates right-of-way located in the City’s
             boundary west of U-111 for a future project in Magna Township.
             Construction of said right-of-way will need to comply with City standards
             and review and approval of the City’s Engineering Division.

      Wayne asked if West Valley City and Magna will split the cost of street
      maintenance. Steve replied yes.

      Upon inquiry, there were no further questions or concerns expressed by members
