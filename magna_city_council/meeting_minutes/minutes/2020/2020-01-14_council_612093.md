                                           Staff Meeting

Election of the Mayor and Mayor Pro-Tempore

              Council Member Peel stated he would be comfortable keeping things as they
were now for continuity.

                Council Member Pierce stated she thought there had been discussion in the past
about rotating the Mayor position among the Council Members

             Council Member Peel stated that was the intent, but the title of “Chair” was
changed to “Mayor,” which came with new regulations. The Mayor has to sit on the Greater Salt
Lake Municipal Services District now.

               Paul Ashton stated the Mayor seat is also responsible for other administrative
tasks, such as signing documents, ceremonial things, and attending other meetings.

                Council Member Prokopis he would not be able to commit to the kind of time the
position of Mayor took. Rotating that would also upset the continuity on the other boards. In order
to sit on the Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, he would have to give up sitting on the
Unified Police Department Board of Directors because committing to both boards was unrealistic.

Citizen Public Input

                Jessica Mecham stated she runs a daycare with a maximum of 12 children
because that is what the County’s business license requirements allowed, but now that Magna is
a Metro Township, the Council can change the Municipal Code to allow 16. She asked that it
consider starting the process to do that.

               Council Member Hull asked what the ratio of service providers had to be with 16
children, and there was an age ratio required.

               Ms. Mecham stated to be licensed for 16 children, a provider had to have two
caregivers, with the ratio being 8 to 1, and only 4 of those can be under the age of two. She has
two caregivers now, and her business is in compliant with the County’s ordinance.

               Paul Ashton stated the next step is for the Council staff to put it on an agenda for
the Council to discuss.

               Greg Schulz stated he could put in on the January 28, 2020, agenda.

                Council Member Peel suggested Ms. Mecham bring information on this topic for
the Council to look at.

Council Business

Unified Fire Authority (UFA)

                Council Member Hull stated Assistant Chief Mike Watson has retired. Riley
Pilgrim became the new Assistant Chief on January 1st. The UFA Board of Directors is finalizing
the interlocal agreements and working on new buildings and funding for the buildings. It is also
working on the new building policy, which will include using the stations that have meeting rooms.
Until the agreement is worked out, nobody can use the rooms.

                  Council Member Prokopis stated the existing language is no known
governmental use. The board is looking to loosen that language to whatever the local community
approved, and that local community here is the Council. He also announced that 33 new
firefighters will be starting at the training grounds on the first Monday in February.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Unified Police Department (UPD)

                Council Member Prokopis stated this Thursday, the UPD Board of Directors will
be electing new leadership. Two new board members from the most recent election and a
member from the newly incorporated Brighton will be added to the board. The UPD is also
updating its interlocal agreement, which will be similar to the UFA’s. It will include a weighted
voting system, whereby 50 percent will be based on an entity’s percentage of the budget and 50
percent by its population. That weighted voting system could only be implemented when two
members of the board asked for it and could only be used for budgetary items. Magna makes up
5.8 percent of the UPD’s budget, and it has about 10 percent of the population served making its
total weighted vote 8.14. The board will probably approve the interlocal agreement as written,
after which members can take it back to their entity for ratification. Then, it would come back to
the UPD Board for final adoption. That will probably take a couple more months.

Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD)

                Mayor Peay stated Brighton is also part of the GSLMSD now. He reviewed the
Olympia Hills Development proposal, which is part of unincorporated Salt Lake County and will
fall under the GSLMSD’s responsibility. He was concerned the new development would tap the
GSLMSD’s funds unless it was annexed into a city.

                 Council Member Prokopis stated he had a hard time with the GSLMSD building
all the infrastructure only to have the property annexed.

               Paul Ashton stated the developer is supposed to build the infrastructure and the
development is subject to a master development agreement. There are a lot of safeguards in the
agreement, so if the property did annex into a city, the city would have to reimburse the GSLMSD
a certain amount. He anticipated the developer would form an infrastructure district, which would
give it taxing power with which to build that infrastructure. That was just authorized by the
Legislature last year.

             Mayor Peay stated a number of intersections would be impacted, which the
developer would have to take care of. The development is broken into four segments, and after
each segment, the developer would review everything to see if any changes were needed.

Special Event Fee Waiver

                Greg Schulz reviewed the request to waive $1,500 in special event fees for the
Salt Lake City Track Club to host the 2020 Winter Series races at Saltair. If approved, he will
send a letter to Salt Lake County’s Public Works Engineering Division.

               Council Member Peel stated the Council needs to be prudent about waiving fees
and look at each request individually rather than blanketly approving them.

               Council Member Prokopis stated approving this might set a precedence for the
next organization that requests a fee waiver.

               Mayor Peay asked if there was a cost to the Magna Metro Township.

                                         RECITALS

       A. The Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) is a municipality pursuant to Utah Code
             Annotated (“UCA”) §§ 10-2a-401 et seq.

       B. The Magna Metro Township Council (the “Council”) is the municipal legislative body
          for Magna pursuant to UCA § 10-3b-501.

       C. The Utah Local Governments Trust (“ULGT”) is a Nonprofit Interlocal Corporation that
          provides insurance coverage to Local Governments pursuant to UCA 11 Chapter 13a
          – Governmental Nonprofit Corporation Act.

       D. ULGT provides General Liability and Workers Compensation Insurance to Magna.

       E. ULGT provided Magna with a Bid for Insurance in December 2019 for the year 2020.

       F. Magna is required to accept the bid prior to payment of the insurance premium.

                                       RESOLUTION

       THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, the Council hereby approves this resolution, accepts
the Bid for Insurance from the Utah Local Governments Trust, hereinafter known as Attachment
“A,” and approves the payment of the insurance premium for Magna upon receipt of the invoice.
