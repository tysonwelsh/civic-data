                                    Regular Council Meeting

Community Input

Recognize Visiting Officials

                Representative Anthony Loubet reported on the end of the legislative session.
The most significant legislation was HB 374, which requires the dissolution of the Unified Police
Department (UPD) over the next two years. Other metro townships besides Copperton have
expressed concern about their lack of taxing authority. Magna Metro Township has also
expressed concern about ensuring police officers will keep their current positions and pay grades.
Complying with HB 374 will require multiple conversations, and the bill is far from ideal.
Representative Loubet has submitted an interim bill to establish taxing authority of the metro
townships. Bill files can be opened as early as May, and Representative Jordan Teuscher has
given his support. UPD will officially expire June 30, 2025, and the County will play a more active
role than it has in the past.

              Mayor Clayton requested that Representative Loubet give a quick summary of
HB 374 for the benefit of a youth group that was in attendance.

               Representative Loubet summarized the bill.

Kennecott Conveyor Belt

              Mayor Clayton stated Laura Ingersoll and Brendan Murphy were in attendance to
discuss a problem with the conveyor belts at Rio Tinto.

                Laura Ingersoll, Senior Advisor- Community and Social Performance, Rio Tinto,
stated Rio Tinto experienced a failure the previous week with one of the conveyor belts that
transports ore from the mine to the concentrator. Rio Tinto was still investigating what happened.
In the interim, starting Friday, it would be transporting ore using mine trucks that would travel by
mining roads. There would be approximately five haul trucks per hour, 24 hours a day. This could
result in some noise and dust issues for Copperton residents. Rio Tinto would be doing some
spot noise monitoring and will continue with usual dust suppression methods. Rio Tinto would be
providing updates on its Facebook page but did not have a specific time frame.

                 Brendan Murphy, General Manager – Minerals, Rio Tinto, stated Rio Tinto may
not be able to run its conveyor at full speed for a while. This was done previously in 2013 with a
fleet of 40-ton trucks. The plan, currently, is for the truck use to be supplemental.

               Mayor Clayton asked Council Member Bailey to make sure this information was
posted to the Copperton Facebook page.

       Unified Police Department (UPD)
       Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

                 Council Member Olsen stated the finance committee met and discussed the
appropriation of the general fund balance. SLVLESA’s portion was $1.228 million, and the
precinct fund balance was around $1.7 million. There was a deficit resulting from the 2021/2022

get it paid off prior to the dissolution of UPD. There has been a lot of discussion on the dissolution

       Salt Lake County Animal Services

                Council Member Severson delivered the 2022 annual report as he was not
present to do so in February. Animal Services had been working on a no-kill program since 2008.
It achieved that goal in 2013 and has been a no-kill shelter since then. Much of this is due to
fundraising efforts that continue to garner donations. Salt Lake County won the National
Association of Counties (NACo) award in 2022 for its Hounds Around Town program. It also won
Best in State for the 14th time. It is purchasing some hand scanners to send to various
municipalities so stray animals can be scanned and immediately returned to their owners before
being sent to a shelter. It is also working on a mobile spay and neuter unit. Dustin Gettel was
elected as chair, and Arlyn Bradshaw was elected as citizen chair.
