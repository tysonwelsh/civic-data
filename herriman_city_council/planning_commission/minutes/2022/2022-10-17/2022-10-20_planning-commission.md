Legislative items are recommendations to the City Council. Broad public input will be taken and considered on each

Council meeting.
5.1 Consideration to Recommend new language in the City Code Regarding Action Levels in
Contaminated Soils (Public Hearing)
File Number: C2022-074
Community Development Director Blake Thomas presented the recommendation from the Utah State
Interstate Technology and Regulatory Council requesting Herriman update City Code to conform to the
action levels for lead as suggested by the Record of Decision (ROD). He presented the table showing the
proposed reduction to the lead and arsenic levels and provided a summary of options for the
Commission to consider.
Chair Berbert identified the recommended lead level change for non-residential uses being 4,000 ppm
and 300 ppm for arsenic levels. He asked about the buffer zone between residential and non-residential
uses. Director Thomas explained the regulation only specifies the requirement for uses and did not
provide for an additional buffer zone between the two.
Chair Berbert opened the public hearing.
Andrew Lawrence indicated he lived behind a berm which contains a vast amount of contaminated soil
and suggested the tables outline a range of levels and suggested the range be conditional dependent
upon the type of business being placed at the location. He opined the research being conducted by the
EPA was trending to lower limits and the state toxicologists suggested a level closer to 2,000 ppm
adjacent to residential.
Chair Berbert closed the public hearing.
Chair Berbert asked about the recommended range and questioned if additional research needed to be
conducted. He questioned if the level was reduced to 1,200 ppm, would mediation be required for
finished developments. Director Thomas responded in the negative and agreed there still was questions
needing to be answered by the consultant. Commissioner Jacobson requested for additional information
to be solicited and observed the Auto Mall Crescent Parcel would fall under this standard. Director
Thomas highlighted the Engineering Standards specified the lead level to be 4,000 ppm and it was only
a higher level in City Code. He anticipated seeing this item be brought back after the first of the year
for further requested reductions. Director Thomas explained staff would file an environmental
covenant on the property specifying the clean-up requirements. Planning Manager Spencer indicated
he would investigate options for safe development options on affected properties. Director Thomas
indicated the Council may adopt 1,600 ppm lead level based on a University of Utah study.
Commissioner Powell moved to recommend approval to reduce the nonresidential property action levels
