    2. INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT WITH WEBER BASIN WATER CONSERVANCY DISTRICT FOR THE 3300
       SOUTH CHIPSEAL & ROAD STRIPING PROJECTS – RESOLUTION 26-2016
         Chad Meyerhoffer, of County Engineering, stated that the District put in a secondary water line this year
         and the county just chip sealed that road last year. This contract is for the county to receive the funds
         from the District to restore the road to the condition that it was. Ricky Hatch, County/Comptroller noted
         that Scott Parke, County Comptroller, of the Clerk/Auditor’s Office, had not received this contract for

    3. CONTRACT WITH ALPHA COUNSELING & TREATMENT, INC. TO PROVIDE MENTAL HEALTH SERVICES
       TO INMATES AS REQUIRED BY STATE AND FEDERAL LAWS AND REGULATIONS

       Steffani Ebert, with the County Sheriff's Office, stated that the contract with Weber Human Services
       (WHS) to provide mental health services to inmates in the county jail expired this year and WHS
       exercised the clause not to continue. The county issued requests for proposals and subsequently selected
       Alpha Counseling. She addressed Commissioner Ebert’s questions stating that the annual amount with
       WHS was just over $200,000 and the new contract is $184,000, that it is a 3-year contract and is subject
       to annual review by both parties.
       Commissioner Ebert moved to approve the contract with Alpha Counseling & Treatment, Inc. for mental
       health services to inmates as required by State and Federal laws and regulations; Chair Bell seconded.
       Commissioner Ebert – aye; Chair Bell – aye
    4. CONTRACT WITH THE STATE OF UTAH DISTRICT COURT TO PROVIDE BAILIFF AND COURT SECURITY

    7. CONTRACT WITH WADMAN CORPORATION FOR GENERAL CONTRACTOR - RENOVATION OF MAIN LIBRARY
       Lynnda Wangsgard, County Library Director, presented this item. The contract includes the Standard
       Form of Agreement Between the Owner and Contractor and the General Conditions of the Contract for
       Construction. Both parts have been certified by Tom Brennan, Principal Architect with EDA Architects,
       and approved by the County Attorney’s Office and the county’s insurance provider. The contract sum
       stipulated for payment is $12,398,000.
       Completing the project in fewer phases than originally planned will be less expensive for the contractor,
       and the savings will be passed along to the county. The original target date for completion and
       rededicating the Main Library was 4/7/2018; the new construction completion date is 9/21/2017—almost
       six months ahead of schedule, and the goal now is to have the library ready for rededication on 1/1/2018,
       still three months ahead of the original schedule. Wadman has agreed to liquidate damages in the amount
       of $2,000/day for any delays in reaching benchmark goals in the timeline. Wadman also agreed to
       benchmark the quality of the Main Library against their recent work in completing the Payson LDS
       Temple, which quality workmanship will result in a new Main Library that will be a source of pride. This

    2. PUBLIC HEARING TO RECEIVE COMMENT WITH RESPECT TO (A) THE ISSUANCE OF APPROXIMATELY
       $10,150,000 OF SALES TAX REVENUE BONDS & (B) ANY POTENTIAL ECONOMIC IMPACT THAT THE
       PROJECT TO BE FINANCED WITH PROCEEDS OF SAID BONDS MAY HAVE ON THE PRIVATE SECTOR.
       John Bond, County Treasurer, stated that there is opportunity to accelerate road projects for 12th Street
       and 3500 West (the construction crews are currently there) that are scheduled to come from a
       transportation fund. The county will borrow —just over $6 million for 12th Street and under $3 million
       for 3500 W.—f or the projects and then be reimbursed by the transportation fund over the next 3-5 years.
       It will be a 10-year note that is to have a clause to allow paying it off early without penalty. He addressed
       the commissioners’ questions stating that the plan is also to use the 2016-2019 available funds.
    3. Public comments: Chair Bell invited public comments and none were offered.

       steps. He recommends the 10-year note to allow for some flexibility. Mr. Bond noted that the
