The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agenda City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
discuss the following areas of the Fiscal Year (FY) 2023 Budget: Fire/Medical, Community and Economic Development, and Arts
Grants proposals; review the Redevelopment Agency FY 2022-2023 budget; and discuss Council/Board business.

Agenda Review

Acting Chair Lopez and members of Council staffreviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 City Budget

Department Budget Review, Fire/Medical: Fire Chief Mathieu used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize
the budget proposal for his Department including administration, emergency management, medical services, operations,
paramedics, prevention, and training. He identified notable proposed changes to the Department’s budget for Fiscal Year (FY)

2022-2023:

e Fire Department:

14% wage increase for employees;

Reallocation of $309,875 from small tools and equipment to fund four new firefighter positions. Note
that this is a reallocation, not a budget increase;

$11,025 in the New Hire Academy to train approximately three new hires annually; and

Lease charges budget increase of $108,025 to allow for the replacement of the new ladder-truck in 10
years. This was budgeted in FY2022 and is estimated to be placed in service in January 2023.

e Medical Services Fund:

14% wage increases for employees;
$143,000 budget increase to fund two new EMT/Firefighter positions.

o The Fire Department plans on establishing a new truck company, comprised of four firefighters and

The Department is also responsible for managing the Airport Enterprise Fund and the Community Development Block

Arts Grants Proposals: Arts Coordinator Buckley then discussed FY2023 Arts Grants proposals; $150,000 has been
allocated for arts grants in FY2023 and the City Council’s role in the grant process is to make the determination that the value of
the projects and programming that the public receives from the proposed arts grants are equal to or exceed the monetary value of
the grants. She noted that 24 grant application were received, and the Arts Grants Review Sub-Committee recommends 21 grant
applications merit funding; this is broken down as six general support grants and 15 project support grants. Total funding in
FY2023 is $139,989. Grants are reviewed with a rigorous scoring process including the following:

Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Redevelopment Agency Budget

Administration is requesting the Board adopt the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2022-2023 Redevelopment Agency (RDA)
budget totaling $14,026,800. The is an increase of $3,367,825 or 31.6 percent over the FY2021-2022 budget. The RDA is funded
primarily through tax increment received from the various redevelopment districts. Contributions from Business Depot Ogden
(BDO) lease revenue are also included in the Agency’s revenue. A contribution from the Agency to the General Fund covers
services provided to the RDA by City administrative staff.

Community and Economic Development Director Cooper used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
details of the RDA budget; there are 18 redevelopment areas in the City and the proposed budget including all areas is
approximately $12 million. Transfers to the RDA from BDO lease revenue total $5 million and will be used to fund the following:

e Continental Community Reinvestment Area (CRA) — Wonder Block ($2.2 million)
Continental CRA — GWCU Loan ($60,200)
Continental CRA — DL Evans Loan ($81,331)
Trackline EDA — Swift ($500,000
Ogden River — Property Purchase ($170,000)
Union Station — Catalyst Campus ($318,000)
DL Evans Loan — Rite Aid ($80,125)
Quality Neighborhoods Funding ($1 million)
CBD Mall Debt Service ($250,000)
e 550 24" Street Infill - RDA not CIP ($314,500)

Mr. Cooper then concluded by noting he plans to recommend the creation of four new redevelopment areas in FY2023;

more information regarding those areas is forthcoming.

Council/Board Business

Flip the Strip Program: Council member Hyer asked Planning Manager Montgomery to provide the Council with an
update regarding the timing of ordinance amendments that will allow residents to receive a rebate for a ‘flip-the-strip’ project. Mr.
Montgomery stated that the Planning Commission has made a formal recommendation to the City Council and Council staff has
been working to calendar action on the ordinance amendment. Council members expressed support for holding a special meeting
to take action on the ordinance amendments. Acting Chair Lopez stated Council leadership will review the meeting calendar with
Council staff and determine when the item can be scheduled.

The meeting adjourned at 5:49 p.m.
