 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Copper Rim – Preliminary Development Plan / Master Development Agreement
          Amendment; a petition by Matt Dean/C.W. Land Co. requesting a major variation to
          the Preliminary Master Development Plan for Copper Rim at approximately 7516
          South Copper Rim Drive by (1) removing the requirement for 204 age-restricted
          senior housing units in Phase 9 and replacing it with 133 market-rate multi-family
          residential townhomes, (2) changing the location and classification of Phase 9 road
          from a Public Road to Private Driveways, and (3) reconfiguring phase boundaries,
          relocating lots, changing street connections, removing private alleys, and making
          other revisions to the adopted Preliminary Development Plan.
          Senior Planner Ray McCandless explained proposed amendments to the Copper Rim
          Preliminary Development Plan and Master Development Agreement and answered
          questions from the Council.

          Council Member Green commented that four-story residential buildings were not allowed
          by ordinance and asked how the proposed change from 204 senior housing units to 133
          multi-family townhomes would be in the best interest of the city. Council Member Jacob
          liked the lower density. Council Member Worthen expressed the opinion that West Jordan
          needed senior housing options and she did not want to cut out the senior housing. Council
          Member Green asked for the final density of the development. He was willing to look at
          modifying the ordinance, but not as proposed. He felt the proposal was premature
          because it did not take into consideration how the PC Zone was written.

          Responding to a question from Vice Chair Bloom, Matt Dean with C.W. Land said they had
          looked at several economic analyses and found that an age-restricted project would not

          work at the subject location. The current proposal was to do something different and
          avoid a failed project. Mr. Dean said the proposed townhome product had been successful
          in the market. Vice Chair Bloom expressed the opinion that 14-foot-wide driveways
          would not work. Mr. Dean responded that Daybreak successfully delivered 12-foot-wide
          driveways. He said the 204 age-restricted units had been planned as rentals, and the 133

          Council Member Whitelock was opposed to removing the senior housing overlay, and not
          interested in changing the balanced housing ordinance. She felt the proposed change
          would lower the number of units but increase the number of vehicles. She was not
          comfortable approving the current proposal.

          Chair McConnehey was hesitant to remove the senior housing overlay and would not
          want to set a precedent that would undermine the balanced housing overlay in the future.
          He appreciated the reduction in units with the proposed housing change and preferred
          adding additional green space. Chair McConnehey appreciated the direction of the
          proposal and wanted to hear more. He asked why the applicant felt the proposal was an
          improvement over the original Development Plan, why the relocation of units was not a
          concern, and why standard lots would be less costly for homeowners to maintain. Mr.
          Dean suggested the proposal was an improvement in the reduction of overall units and
          amount of open space, and said alleys were less costly for a homeowner’s association to
          maintain.

          Mr. McCandless reported that phasing lines did not work the further they got into the
          project and the currently proposed phasing lines offered two points of access for looping
          of roads for each phase. He said original lot widths were found to be incorrect in northern
          Copper Rim, requiring a shift in lot lines.

          Council Member Pack spoke of trying to find a balance between low density, decent price
          points, and senior housing opportunities. He appreciated the proposed reduction in
          density but would be hesitant to completely remove the senior housing overlay. Council
          Member Pack expressed interest in hearing more about the commercial phase. Mr. Dean
          repeated that they had looked at the senior housing component for the last year, and
          found it would not work. He said C.W. Land had also analyzed the possibility of small
          detached housing units, and found the 50-55 units possible would not create the
          necessary critical mass for the economics to work. Council Member Whitelock said the
          Council already gave the applicant a big concession in the form of reduced commercial.

          Council Member Jacob liked the reduced density, and believed the proposed residential
          would provide as good a buffer to the higher density across 7000 South as the originally
          proposed senior housing. He did not believe it was the Council’s responsibility to dictate
          the price of homes developed. He asked about future plans for 7000 South, and made
          suggestions for concessions in the form of increased road width. Vice Chair Bloom said
          she would have liked to see statistics backing the decision to move away from the senior
          housing.

          townhomes as an exempt housing type for the PC Zone. Five Council Members indicated
          they were in favor, with two opposed.

       b. Discussion of Transportation Projects
          East/West Connectivity/9000 South – Assistant City Administrator Jamie Davidson noted
          that transportation was at the forefront of citizen concerns in relation to sustainability
          and livability in the city and emphasized that staff took the responsibility seriously. Public
          Works Director Brian Clegg provided an update regarding transportation planning
          relative to 9000 South and east/west connectivity. He emphasized that decisions would
          be made following an environmental impact study process with public feedback
          opportunities.



Committee of the Whole Minutes             Wednesday, October 18, 2023                            Page 3
          Council Member Green asked why the Council was receiving the presentation, since
          decisions for such a project were the responsibility of the Executive Branch. He
          commented that if UDOT wanted a road to go through, it would go through whether the
          City wanted it or not unless the City could offer a good alternative. Council Member
          Worthen noted that it was the Council’s job to listen to and have conversations with the
          residents. Assistant City Administrator Davidson reported that the City was in the
          business of building neighborhoods and make them thrive. In contrast, UDOT was in the
          business of building roads. He explained that the intent of the presentation was to prepare
          those present for future conversations with UDOT regarding transportation planning, to
          help positively affect the outcome since the City and UDOT had different focuses. Chair
          McConnehey noted that residents had recently reached out to Council regarding plans to
          realign 9000 South from 6100 West to U111.

          The Council and staff discussed development and transportation plans and needs. Council
          Member Jacob questioned how the current alignment of 9000 South had taken place,
          however he understood that plans change over time, and master plans were not always
          followed. Council Member Green expressed the opinion that the established process
          needed to play out without premature Council involvement. Chair McConnehey believed
          it was important for the Council to be aware of what was happening. Council Member
          Jacob expressed a desire to look at the situation with a fresh view and decide what would
          be best for moving forward. Council Member Worthen encouraged individuals to keep
          suggesting ideas to improve safety moving forward.

          Transportation Utility Fee – City Administrator Korban Lee explained that most cities
          subsidized maintenance of roads through the general fund. Eleven or twelve years ago, a
          couple cities in the State implemented a Transportation Utility Fee, a monthly fee on
          homes and commercial properties to assist with the cost of maintaining roads. The
          legality was subsequently debated in court, and the Utah Supreme Court determined the
          Transportation Utility Fee to be a tool that cities could use. Mr. Lee asked the Council to
          keep the Transportation Utility Fee in mind as a possible tool to use in the future, and
          commented the issue may be discussed by the Legislature during the next session. Vice
          Chair Bloom thanked Mr. Lee for keeping the Council informed.

       c. Discussion of Council Rules related to Council Seniority
          Chair McConnehey explained Council seniority was currently defined by length of
          consecutive years served. He felt the definition was no longer pertinent due to changes in
          Council Rules, and suggested the definition could be changed or removed. Council
          Member Whitelock was not comfortable altering the language but would be comfortable
          removing the definition. Council Member Green expressed a desire to remove the
          definition. Council Member Pack was willing to continue the conversation if seniority
          mattered to Council seating on the dais, in light of the potential need to fill a vacancy in
          coming months. Council Member Jacob said he believed seniority needed to be spelled out
          if seniority mattered. Chair McConnehey stated seniority did not affect Council seating,
          and the definition did not drive any action. Responding to a question from Chair
          McConnehey, Council Members did not appear to object to including deletion of the
          definition on a future agenda.
