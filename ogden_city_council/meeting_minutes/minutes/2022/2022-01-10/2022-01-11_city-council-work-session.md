The purpose of the work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.;
discuss proposed rezone at 3432 Ogden Avenue; discuss proposed rezone at 975 Washington Boulevard; discuss Council Business
pertaining to Council Rules and Norms and the Marshall White Center; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review

Chair Nadolski and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Proposed rezone at 3432 Ogden Avenue

Deputy Planning Manager Brierley explained Lindquist Mortuaries is seeking to rezone 3432 Ogden Avenue for the
purpose of expanding their existing crematorium/columbarium building. The property currently has a home on it, and the
petitioner is planning on demolishing the home and extending the existing mortuary to the south. The property is under the R-1-6
zone, but it will be necessary to rezone to C-3/CO in order to accommodate mortuary services. The C-3/CO zone would match the
zoning of the mortuary to the north, and the development agreement has been written as an amendment to the existing
development agreement that the mortuary has with the City. The Planning Commission recommended approval of the rezone,
citing consistency with the General Plan and the community plan.

Council member Richey asked if there has been any public opposition to the petition. Mr. Brierley indicated the Planning
Commission did not receive any public input during the public hearing regarding this matter.

Council member Hyer asked if there is a potential for any nuisance noises or smells associated with the use. Mr. Brierley
answered no.

Proposed rezone at 975 Washington Boulevard

Planning Manager Montgomery explained Wasatch Peaks Credit Union is petitioning to rezone their property from C-2
to C-3/CO to allow for an impound/repossession lot at 975 Washington Boulevard. The planned impound lot is located just west
of the credit union and would be developed as an accessory use to the main building. Repossession lots are not allowed in the
current C-2 zone. While rezoning to C-3 is technically spot zoning, the Planning Commission found that placing a conditional
overlay on the property would prevent any unwanted uses from taking place on the property. Because of the conditional overlay, it
would be necessary to also approve a development agreement. The proposed development agreement would help ensure that the
property can only be used for an impound lot connected to a financial institution. Additional visual buffers—such as a six-foot
fence and landscaping—would be placed around the impound lot to lessen any negative visual impacts to the homes across the
street.

Council member White inquired as to the location of existing impound lots in the City. Mr. Montgomery identified one
other impound lot associated with credit unions, but most impound lots are located in manufacturing zones in the City and are not
associated with financial institutions. Council member White asked if there is a higher crime rate associated with impound lots.

Mr. Montgomery stated that there have been no reports of increased crime near impound lots; the existing impound lot associated
with another credit union is under surveillance and the lot is isolated and screened to prevent public view. The site design is
important in reducing any potential crime. Council member Blair added that he worked for a credit union in the past and is
familiar with the repossession process; it is his experience that most individuals who have their vehicles repossessed are made
aware of the action and usually submit because they can no longer pay the payment. There were very few occurrences when
individuals tried to retrieve their vehicles from the repossession lot.

Council Business
Rules and Norms: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the proposed amendments to the Council Rules
and Norms are as follows:
1. Modify the Regular Meetings section to update meeting schedule.
2. Modify the Regular Meetings section to add Acknowledgements to the Opening Ceremony.
3. Modify the Civility and Decorum section to clarify the reason and purpose for limiting outward expressions of
support or disagreement with a given position or statement.
4. Adding a Signage section to provide guidance for meeting attendees.

Discussion among the Council centered on the intent behind the proposed amendments and the individual or individuals
that will be responsible for enforcing the amended rules. There was a heavy focus on the amendment dealing with decorum and
using signs or other forms of outward expression to indicate support or disagreement regarding an item being considered by the
Council. The Council ultimately concluded to reduce the size of a sign permitted in the Council Chambers and restricting larger
signs to the back of the room to avoid obstructing the view of any participant in the meeting. Ms. Eller-Smith indicated she will
adjust the Rules and Norms document responsive to the feedback provided before presenting it to the Council for action in a future
meeting.

Marshall White Center: The Council briefly discussed the implications of the proposed joint resolution regarding the
Marshall White Center, which is scheduled for action during tonight’s meeting. Council member White stated that her only
concern is that the term ‘major’ in the first section of the resolution is subjective and she suggested that it be defined in order for
any reader of the document to understand the Council’s intentions. Other Council members indicated they did not share the same
concern and that removing the word does not change the intent of the document.

Vice Chair Lopez indicated that upon adoption of the joint resolution, it will no longer be necessary for the Council to
include a standing item on each work session agenda to discuss the Marshall White Center. Chair Nadolski agreed.

Council member Choberka asked if a member of the Council or Council leadership will attend future Marshall White
Center Advisory Committee meetings. Chair Nadolski answered no; those meetings will be conducted by the Committee and
Administration and the Council will receive monthly reports regarding the content discussed in those meetings.

Council Schedule and Zoom meetings: The Council briefly discussed the schedule for the remainder of the month of
January, with a brief focus on the length of time during which the Council will continue to meet virtually.

The meeting adjourned at 5:10 p.m.
