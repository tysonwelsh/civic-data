Proposed Ordinances 22-29 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 22-29, AMEND SECTIONS 7-1-103, 7-9-106, 7-
9-107, 7-13-704, 7-13-705, 7-13-807, 9-4-102 AND 9-4-105 OF THE WEST
VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN
REQUIREMENTS CONCERNING STREETS
Steve Lehman, CED, discussed proposed Ordinance 22-29 that would amend
Sections 7-1-103, 7-9-106, 7-9-107, 7-13-704, 7-13-705, 7-13-807, 9-4-102 and 9-4-
105 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Amend Certain Requirements
Concerning Streets.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
information as follows:

       The City Council has expressed concerns about the financial burden private
       streets place on the residents of planned unit developments (PUDs) with
       private streets and directed staff to prepare an ordinance revision that would
       prohibit private streets. This ordinance is in response to that direction.

Councilmember Nordfelt verified that the two smaller cross section options apply
only to multi-unit projects. Steve replied yes. Councilmember Nordfelt stated that
the Planning Commission was concerned about industrial subdivisions and
questioned why. Steve replied they felt there should be more flexibility for
industrial projects. He referenced a private road for CR England and Godfrey that
is made of concrete and works well for those businesses. Councilmember Nordfelt
asked if the Code would allow for larger roads. Steve replied that the proposed
standard cross-section is plenty wide for maneuverability. He stated that staff hasn’t
discussed a need for larger roads with commercial developers. Councilmember
Harmon asked what other issues the Planning Commission had with the proposed
ordinance. Steve replied that they were concerned that one size doesn’t fit all. They
indicated that there were unique properties that may require smaller roads to be
developable. Steve noted that overall, the Planning Commission just wanted there
to be more options for flexibility. Mayor Lang asked if this ordinance would
prohibit private streets in a single-family home PUD. Steve replied yes. Steve
briefly discussed different subdivisions in the City and some of their unique layouts
with private and public streets.

     Requesting a Vacation of a Perpetual Easement and Right-of-Way Located at

     Proposed Ordinances 22-30 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
     Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

     ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 22-30, APPROVE THE VACATION OF A
     PERPETUAL EASEMENT AND RIGHT-OF-WAY LOCATE AT
     APPROXIMATELY 7000 WEST 5400 SOUTH
     Steve Lehman, CED, discussed proposed Ordinance 22-30 that would approve the
     Vacation of a Perpetual Easement and Right-of-Way Locate at Approximately 7000
     West 5400 South.

     Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
     information as follows:

            Katherine Pasker, representing Northrop Grumman Innovation systems Inc.
            is requesting a vacation for a perpetual easement and right-of-way dedicated
            to Salt Lake County in 1932. Subsequent to West Valley City’s
            incorporation in 1980, this easement and right-of-way was transferred to the
            City. The purpose for the vacation is to clean up title records.

            According to County records, Arnold and Areva Petersen dedicated 33-feet
            of their property to Salt Lake County for a future public highway. This deed
            was recorded in November 1932. The highway was obviously not built,
            and the property was subsequently developed by Hexcel.

            Although the unimproved street will be vacated by ordinance, the ordinance
            will have no force or effect of any franchise rights of any public utilities,
            nor would they be impaired thereby. The ordinance will also protect any
            holders of existing franchises, water drainage pipelines, or other such uses

            as they may presently exist under, over or upon said property or as are or
            may be shown on Salt Lake County records.

     THE 4100 COMMONS OFFICE CONDOMINIUMS
     Steve Lehman, CED, discussed proposed Ordinance 22-31 that would approve the
     amendment of the 4100 Commons Office Condominiums.

     Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
     information as follows:

            Brook Hintze, representing Back Development, is requesting consideration
            to amend the 4100 Commons Office Condominium Plat. This plat was
            recorded with the office of the Salt Lake County Recorder in December
            2021. The property is located at 3745 West 4100 South.

            The purpose for the original condominium plat was to re-purpose the old
            Granger Medical site by converting the interior space for individual
            condominiums. The original plat divided interior spaces into smaller units
            which allow businesses the opportunity and affordability to own a piece of
            a much larger, nicer facility for business operations. According to the
            applicant during the original approvals, “It creates synergy within the
            building with like-minded ownership”.

            Subsequent to the plat recording in 2021, Back Development has
            experienced more growth and interest in the project. They have been asked
