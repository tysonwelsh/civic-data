2. Presentations and Reports
1.1 Point of the Mountain Transit and Central Corridor Transit Presentation – UTA
Patty Garver with UTA gave an update on the studies being conducted for better transit systems
in Utah County. She said they looked at light rail and BRT options.

Mary Delmar-Shaffer with UTA explained the Central Corridor Transit study. She said that they
are looking at using the State Street bus routes and noted that UTA and UDOT have a good
working relationship because this project will involve both agencies.

Eric Rasband stated that he really appreciates how involved local leaders have been. He further
explained that Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) could utilize the UTA right of way in the freight
corridor. They could also construct stations at 2100 North, south Triumph, and SR-92. He stated
that MAG helped to fund the environmental study.

Patty Garver stated that BRT is easier to construct and operate and it’s quicker to implement with
less construction.

1.2 Metropolitan Water District Report- John Peterson
John Peterson gave an update on the Metropolitan Water Districts. He reported on some of the
trades and purchases that were made in the last year. He noted that they are always looking to
purchase more culinary water sources and one way to do that is to acquire well water rights from
private property owners.

1.3 Ranked Choice Voting Discussion
The Council discussed if they should remain in the State pilot program for RCV and will make a
final decision at the next Council meeting. There was discussion about holding a Primary
election with RCV. Council Koivisto stated that she feels a primary election is needed to help
narrow the candidates. If the Council decides to use RCV then they will also make a final
decision about holding a Primary election or not.

1.4 Recreation, Arts, and Parks Tax Discussion
Dean Lundell, Finance Director, explained what a RAP tax is and how it works for a city and its
residents. He noted that many other cities in Utah County have a RAP tax. He estimated that the
city could collect $1.3-1.5 million in revenue from the tax which could then be spent on parks
and other amenities. The Council agreed that they would like to initiate the first steps to consider
adding this to the November ballot.

    Renee Cortese inquired about the irrigation water concerns. Mr. Gutierrez then explained how

    staff could investigate Mr. Hadfield’s concerns.

    stated that he has already gone above and beyond what is required.

    Motion:     Councilor Koivisto moved to grant Final Subdivision approval for Pioneer Heights,
                a 45-lot residential development located at 1452 West 900 North; including all
                Development Review Committee Comments and Planning Commission comments;
                and also include that staff will work with the developer and neighbors to address

                motion.

    Council Southwick suggested that the verbiage be amended to state that staff investigate the

    Amended Motion: Council Koivisto agreed to amend her motion to Councilor Southwick’s
