 2.        Resolution 2020-38 authorizing the consolidation of two Utah State Infrastructure Bank
           Loans to create a new consolidated Bank Loan from the Utah Department of Transportation
           to the Airport Fund. (20-234) (0:13:00)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Resolution 2020-38 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, presented. On October 29, 2019, the Council authorized a
$5,000,000 Utah State Infrastructure Bank Loan to the Airport Fund to be repaid at 2.1% interest over five years to
partially fund the cost of infrastructure construction for the new airport terminal. The Provo Public Works
Department has secured a second Utah State Infrastructure Bank Loan for $5,149,553. This resolution would
consolidate the two loans for the airport terminal. The new consolidated loan of $10,149,553 will be paid back at a
0.79% interest rate over five years starting in Fiscal Year 2023 and ending in Fiscal Year 2027.

Chair Handley opened public comment, there was no response. He called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                          Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.

 3.        Resolution 2020-39 authorizing an interfund loan of up to $9,100,000 from the Energy Fund
           to the Airport Fund. (20-135) (0:16:17)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Resolution 2020-39 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

John Borget, Administrative Services Director, presented. The Public Works Department was requesting
authorization of an interfund loan of up to $9,100,000 from the Energy Fund to the Airport Fund to help finance
the completion of the new airport terminal. Interest on the interfund loan will be calculated monthly at a variable
interest rate equal to the monthly State Pool interest rate. Principal on the interfund loan would be paid back in
the following years and amounts: $807,792 in Fiscal Year 2022, $1,798,962 in Fiscal Year 2023, $3,470,858 in Fiscal
Year 2024, and $3,022,388 in Fiscal Year 2025.

Mr. Borget shared a document with the Council showing the Energy Department’s days cash on hand, this was an
important factor for measuring liquidity, particularly for utilities. This loan, along with two other recent loans,
would cause a temporary dip in this factor over the next few years, but he still recommended approval of this
interfund loan. (0:17:43)

Councilor Shipley asked how this would impact the City’s bond rating. Mr. Borget said interfund loans are not
treated the same as other debt. The rating agency would see an impact to liquidity, and they were sensitive to this,
but they were committed to meeting the loan schedules.

Chair Handley opened public comment, there was no response.

Councilor Harding said they needed to exercise caution with interfund loans. He hoped they could find other ways
to fund these gaps, but in the meantime, he was supportive of this loan.

Chair Handley called for a vote on the implied motion.

 4.        Resolution 2020-40 to place approximately 1.69 acres of vacant land at 1100 West Center
           Street on the surplus property list. (20-134) (0:29:00)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Resolution 2020-40 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Tara Riddle, Ombudsman and Property Manager, presented. Starting in 2000, Provo City and the Provo
City Redevelopment Agency had acquired several parcels of property at approximately 1100 West
Center Street. The intent of these property acquisitions was to control property for a project that would
enhance the entrance onto the Center Street corridor.

Over the years, the City had received many offers to purchase the property for uses that have ranged
from storage units to higher density housing. Recently, the City had received viable inquiries about the
property and determined that it was the right time to add the property to the surplus property list.
Having the property surplused would ease the process in negotiating the sale of the property.

Ms. Riddle noted that there was a Community Garden located on the parcel. Community Action, the
City’s partner for the management of the Community Garden, recognized that the Community Garden
was a temporary use and that the garden would need to be removed and possibly relocated at the time
of the sale.

Chair Handley opened public comment, there was no response.

Councilor Ellsworth was concerned about the development of this land. This was an entrance to the City,
and she wanted to ensure that whatever got developed there would be representative of the City’s
beauty. Councilor Harding responded that approval of this item would only make the property available
for sale on the surplus property list; it was not actually approving the sell of the land. He viewed this as
an added layer of protection because the City would have the opportunity to select the right buyer later.

Chair Handley called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                          Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.

 7.        Ordinance 2020-46 amending Provo City Code 6.20 Special Events and Expressive Activities.
           (20-223) (0:44:10)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2020-46 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

John Magness, Policy Analyst, presented. In the Council Meeting on July 7, 2020, there was a discussion
about the permitting process for public gatherings and demonstrations in response to the
demonstration in Provo that led to the shooting of a driver when the vehicle became surrounded by
demonstrators. The Council created a subcommittee to look at Provo City’s current ordinance regarding
the permitting of such activities to see if more could be done to help regulate them to protect the
general public’s safety. After several meetings, the committee had proposed changes to the ordinance.

Chapter 6.20 of the Provo City Code regulates the use of traditional forums. The committee looked at
the use of these spaces and decided it would be best to treat expressive speech, such as political speech,
different than commercial speech, such as the Freedom Festival. What was before the Council was an
enhanced section guaranteeing the rights of expressive speech while balancing the need for public
safety. Included is a definition of a spontaneous event, a ban on protesting in front of private residences,
and a definition of what types of expressive speech events need a permit before they can take place.

Councilor Fillmore had been the Chair of this committee. He said working on this project had been very
rewarding. He felt all constitutional issues had been addressed and this followed supreme court
precedents. He thanked Mr. Magness and Camille Williams, Assistant City Attorney, for their hard work.

Councilor Sewell inquired about events in front of private residences. Ms. Williams referred to section
6.20.090. The supreme court previously found it was not unreasonable to have restrictions for private
residents, specifically things that would prevent egress or ingress and prevent someone from having
privacy in their own home. She said it was unlaw to march and obstruct traffic, but it might be possible
for someone to obtain a permit for a march in a residential area as long as they did not interfere with
traffic or target a specific residence. Councilor Fillmore added this would protect the privacy and
sanctity of a public official’s home.

Chair Handley opened public comment, there was no response.

With no further discussion from the Council, Chair Handley called for a vote on the implied motion.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                          Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.

 8.        Ordinance 2020-47 amending Provo City Code regarding Civil Infractions and Repealing
           Requirements Related to the COVID-19 Pandemic. (20-118)

       Motion:            An implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2020-47 as currently constituted, has been made
                          by council rule.

Clifford Strachan, Council Executive Director, presented. On August 25, 2020, the Council approved an Ordinance
2020-36 creating Provo City Code Chapter 9.25, which set requirements for facial coverings and public gatherings
in response to the COVID-19 pandemic. It was upheld on August 27 after a mayoral veto and amended on
September 19, 2020. Chapter 9.25 was originally set to expire on November 15, 2020. The Council agreed to
discuss renewing it in their meetings on October 20, 2020.

Mr. Strachan explained this had been discussed in the work meeting and Council felt the State’s new requirements
satisfied the intent of the original ordinance and it was no longer needed. The State’s mandate was more
restrictive than the City’s ordinance and included a much larger geographical area, not just Provo.

Councilor Harding asked for the proposed ordinance to be displayed, emailed, and reviewed by Council. (1:00:53)
Mr. Strachan suggested a short break to allow the Councilors time to review the ordinance at their own pace.

The meeting resumed at 6:45 p.m. and the Council discussed and made several changes to the language of the
proposed ordinance. (1:13:26) The changes were meant to emphasize the shared intent of the Council to simplify
multiple mask mandates, but more importantly to protect the public health of the community by defaulting to the
State’s more restrictive mandate. Council Staff would prepare a press release to explain the intent of the Council’s
action.

Chair Handley opened public comment, there was no response.

Chair Handley expressed the seriousness of the pandemic and emphasized the importance of mask wearing, social
distancing, and all other precautions. He was grateful for the State’s action and appreciated that it covered a much
larger geographical area because Provo was such a mobile community with so many different touch points along
the Wasatch Front.

       Motion:            Councilor Harding moved to ratify the changes made to the ordinance on the screen.
                          Councilor Sewell seconded the motion.

Chair Handley called for a vote on Mr. Harding’s motion.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                          Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.

Chair Handley called for a vote on the implied motion to adopt Ordinance 2020-47.

       Vote:              The motion was approved 7:0 with Councilors Ellsworth, Fillmore, Handley, Harding,
                          Hoban, Sewell, and Shipley in favor.
