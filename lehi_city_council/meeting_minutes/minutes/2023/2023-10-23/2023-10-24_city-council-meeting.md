128   7. Consideration of Ordinance #61-2023 an amendment to the Lehi City Master
129      Transportation Plan, adding a new master planned roadway alternative connecting
130      Clubhouse Drive to Redwood Road.
131      Mayor Johnson stated that the road line on the map is not a fixed line. The city needs to
132      designate the area in which they recommend a road so that UDOT can move forward with
133      their study. There will be multiple opportunities to comment on the exact road alignment in
134      the future.
135
136       Ryan Hales presented the updated plan. There was discussion on the various transportation
137       corridors, including the recently built Porter Rockwell and the planned 2100 North freeway
138       corridor. The 2100 North corridor, originally planned for 2029, may be moved up to 2025
139       due to increased traffic and growth. Questions were raised about the need for additional
140       roads, such as the clubhouse drive extension, considering the accelerated timeline for the
141       2100 North corridor. The discussion also touched on other projects like Pioneer Crossing and
142       the challenges of balancing development and road infrastructure. The status of bills for
      Lehi City Council Meeting                        3                                   October 24, 2023
143       environmental impact studies and funding for construction was clarified, emphasizing the
144       ongoing process and the need for careful planning in transportation development.
145
146       There were discussions on the alignment of Clubhouse Drive extension. Council Koivisto
147       questioned the necessity of the extension.
148
149       The discussion also delved into the potential consequences of showing different routes on the
150       master plan and the importance of being cautious in conveying the city's intent. Concerns
151       about the impact of the proposed connector on property values were discussed.
152
153       Mayor Johnson noted that even if the route was modified, it would not guarantee its
154       implementation, as further studies and public input would be required.
155
156
157       Mayor Johnson asked for comments
158
159       Travis Walter, an attorney representing Paul and Laura Hardman and Westerly Properties,
160       expresses frustration on behalf of his clients. The Hardman family, a five-generation
161       ranching family in Lehi, had planned their property development in alignment with the
162       general plan, receiving city entitlements and promises. However, they only learned about the
163       proposed road connector a month ago, after significant investments in time and money. He
164       emphasizes the disruptive impact on landowners and Lehi residents who have dedicated
165       efforts to enhance the community and plan their financial futures. He urged reconsideration
166       of the current road proposal, citing potential legal challenges.
167
168       Jeremy Baker, a resident of Holbrook Farms and former Lehi City Council candidate,
169       expressed dissatisfaction with the proposed clubhouse drive connection, considering it more
170       beneficial to Eagle Mountain and Saratoga Springs than Lehi. He asked for the proposal to be
171       tabled or denied, suggesting it be revisited with the Hartman family's concerns in mind. He
172       views the road cutting through their property as a significant overstep by the city,
173       emphasizing the importance of involving landowners in decisions about developments on
174       their land.
175
176       Colonel Venswolf, Director of Engineering and Environment for the Utah National Guard,
177       stated the challenges related to the proposed road. He stated that the red line on the map
178       represents government-controlled land, and while efforts have been made to gain control, it's
179       a slow process. He suggested that 3600 West might be the best option for now.
180
181       Paul Raymond stated that the passing of House Bill 265, emphasized that any land use
182       change within 5000 feet of military installations in Utah must go through the Department of
183       Veteran and Military Affairs. He highlighted that the city will need to consult with Camp
184       Williams for any roads or land use changes.
185
186       Lora Hardman expressed concerns about the proposed road affecting her 60-acre property
187       negatively. She highlighted the family's history of contributing land for roads, annexation
188       efforts, and ongoing development plans. Mrs. Hardman urged the council not to approve the
189       master plan amendment, suggesting alternative solutions for handling traffic if Clubhouse
190       Drive is built.

202       Kyleen Pace expressed frustration and concern about the proposed road. She challenged the
203       Mayor's statement about avoiding developed property and questions the city's decision to
204       include her property in the master plan for corridor preservation. She highlighted that
205       corridor preservation funds cannot be used for eminent domain, and she made it clear that her
206       family will not agree to sell their property. She criticized the planning commission's
207       approval, stating that they did not recommend the route, and emphasizes the stress and
208       anguish her family has experienced over the past month.
209
210       Starley Basinger stated her concerns as a resident of Holbrook Farms, particularly regarding
211       the ongoing discussions about 3600 West. She emphasized her role as a mother and
212       expressed disappointment in the perceived prioritization of other cities' needs over the safety
213       and well-being of the established neighborhood. She highlighted the importance of a
214       walkable community, especially for families with children who attend school on 3600. She
215       feels that the city's decisions may negatively impact their way of life.
216
217       Howard Cook, representing the Gartner company, acknowledged the contributions of the
218       Hardman family to Lehi and expresses a willingness to be part of a solution to reduce or
219       alleviate the impact on the Hardman's property. He emphasized their desire to work
220       collaboratively with the city, highlighting their positive past experiences working with city
221       staff.
222
223       Michelle Stallings attended the planning commission meeting and noted the discrepancy
224       between the presented option for approval and the information about multiple options,
225       including a "no build" option, being considered by UDOT. She expressed confusion about
226       the urgency to select a specific route, emphasizing the potential impact on property once a
227       decision is made. She questioned the necessity of voting on an alignment that hasn't been
228       thoroughly studied, suggesting that the decision could wait until after a more comprehensive
229       examination of alternatives.

232       for safety. He emphasized the need to make difficult decisions and protect the city's future.
