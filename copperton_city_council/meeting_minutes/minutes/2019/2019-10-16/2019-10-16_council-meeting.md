Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

SLVLESA Tax Increase

                 Frank Nakamura, District Administrator, SLVLESA, stated he was here to give
information about the proposed tax increase, not to advocate for it. Copperton Metro Township
Council will not be imposing a tax increase. A vote today would give the SLVLESA board the
ability to increase taxes after it goes through a Truth in Taxation process that ends in December.
There will be a public hearing. Many notices will go out and comments will be received.

Secondly, SLVLESA is the funding arm of the Unified Police Department (UPD), which passed its
budget in June. All UPD members, which includes Council Member Pazell representing
Copperton, were involved in the UPD budget process. When the budget is determined the costs
are set.

              Council Member Pazell stated he voted against the UPD budget. He noted that
most of the SLVLESA board members are also on the UPD board.

               Mr. Nakamura stated compensation and staffing are driving this increase. Eighty
to 85 percent of the budget is for personnel. The district also needs to be competitive in its hiring
practices. Other area police departments have also raised salaries. Additional officers are
needed in Kearns and Magna.

              Chief Jason Ackerman, UPD, stated staffing in Magna is better than Kearns,
which has five vacancies. Hundreds of people used to apply for police jobs when they were
posted. Today, there are 12 positions open and only 38 applicants.

                 Council Member Pazell stated he disagreed with the tax increase. Copperton is
fairly happy with the service it gets. This would be taking taxpayers money that they deserve and
giving it to police officers we feel deserve it. The other reason for his opposition is the way the
budget had been managed in the past. Improvements are being made, but the budget is reeling
from the Herriman and Riverton departures.

               Mr. Nakamura stated he could not speak for the prior ten years. The UPD budget
adopted this year is very transparent with much detail. When problems were discovered with the
budget, the matter was brought to the State Auditor for an audit and problems were corrected.

                Council Member Pazell stated the SLVLESA board is biased towards the
municipalities. Riverton has saved $1 million by leaving SLVLESA and was able to give raises to
its officers.

UFA Weighted Vote

               Council Member Bailey stated the Unified Fire Authority (UFA) has not yet
implemented the weighted vote. Salt Lake County does not want the weighted vote done by
population because there are very few people in unincorporated Salt Lake County, but the County
contributes a lot of funding to the UFA. They would prefer the weighting be done by amount of
funding. Either way, the weighted vote would only be used for the budget. An alternative proposal
is to have a 50/50 weighted vote, whereby 50 percent of the vote is by population and 50 percent
by funding. In addition, there was discussion about putting all metro townships in a group for a
vote. She opposed that idea because the needs of Copperton are different than those of other
townships.

               Nathan Bracken stated he has reviewed the proposal and it is definitely legal but
strictly the Council’s call. He noted that the proposal indicates “any two board members
representing separate parties may call for a weighted vote.” That is different from making a motion
and second. A motion and second is procedurally more proper than just saying “any two may
call.”

               Mayor Clayton asked if the Council could propose that language change.

               Mr. Bracken stated yes. In addition, the motion needs to be in an open meeting,
on the record, with a motion/second. Otherwise it could be an email, phone call, behind closed
doors, etc.
