B.      ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION GP-1-2022,
        FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING AN AMENDMENT TO
        THE WEST VALLEY CITY GENERAL PLAN TO UPDATE THE CITY'S
        MODERATE INCOME HOUSING PLAN
        Mayor Pro Tem Huynh informed a public hearing had been advertised for the
        Regular Council Meeting scheduled September 20, 2022, in order for the City
        Council to hear and consider public comments regarding application GP-1-2022,
        Filed by West Valley City, Requesting an Amendment to the West Valley City General
        Plan to Update the City's Moderate Income Housing Plan.

        Proposed Ordinance 22-36 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
        Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

        ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 22-36, ADOPT THE 2022 MODERATE
        INCOME HOUSING PLAN AS PART OF THE WEST VALLEY CITY
        GENERAL PLAN
        Steve Pastorik, CED Director, discussed proposed Ordinance 22-36 that would
        adopt the 2022 Moderate Income Housing Plan as Part of the West Valley City General
        Plan.

        Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
        information as follows:

               During the 2022 general session, the Utah legislature passed HB 462 that
               modified requirements for municipalities’ moderate income housing plans
               as well as reporting. The purpose of this General Plan amendment is to
               update the City’s Moderate Income Housing Plan to bring it into
               compliance with State code. Compliance is required by State code by
               October 1, 2022.
  MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – SEPTEMBER 13, 2022
                          -6-


              The most significant State code changes include:
                  requiring municipalities with a fixed guideway public transit
                    station to implement five or more of the 24 updated moderate
                    income housing strategies listed in State code,
                  requiring an implementation plan,
                  requiring municipalities with a fixed guideway public transit
                    station to adopt station area plans for each station, and
                  modifying the reporting requirements to include more detail on
                    municipalities’ efforts to encourage moderate income housing.

       Councilmember Nordfelt asked what specifically has changed in the plan. Steve
       replied that all the tables and figures have been updated to include more recent data.
       He stated that strategies were also changed beginning on page 12 to reflect State
       requirements. Councilmember Fitisemanu asked how transit relates to the plan.
       Steve replied that any community that has a commuter or light rail station must
       have a plan within a half mile radius of the station. He indicated that the
       requirements for this plan are very specific, so the State has given City’s until the
       end of 2025 to come into compliance. He noted that if someone were to make an
       application within a specific transit area, the City would need to adopt a plan for
       that area within 12 months. Steve stated that Fairbourne Station already meets
       requirements.

       The City Council will consider Ordinances 22-36 at the Regular Council Meeting
       scheduled September 20 at 6:30 P.M.

B.      JUSTICE COURT REPORT
        Nicole Cottle, Assistant City Manager, presented a PowerPoint presentation
        summarized as follows:
               - What Is a Justice Court?
                      o Utah’s trial court system includes the district, juvenile, and
                          justice courts. Each court has a different responsibility. District
                          courts are courts of general jurisdiction that handle civil cases,
                          criminal felonies, some misdemeanors, and domestic relations
                          cases. In general, juvenile courts are civil courts that handle
                          issues relating to minors (individuals under age 18). District and
                          juvenile courts are state courts.
                      o The justice courts are established and funded by municipalities
                          and counties.
                      o Justice courts have authority to deal with class B and C
                          misdemeanors, violations of local ordinances, small claims, and
                          infractions cases within their jurisdiction. Justice courts’
                          jurisdictions are determined by the boundaries of the
                          municipalities and counties that established them. There are 137
                          justice courts with 108 county and municipal judges serving in
                          them. A justice court nominating commission recommends
                          candidates to the local appointing authority. The chosen judicial
                          candidate is then ratified by the local legislative body. The
                          appointee must also be certified by the judicial council. The
                          judge then stands for retention election every six years.
               - Quick Facts
                      o The West Valley Justice Court is the 2nd largest Justice Court in
                          the state.
