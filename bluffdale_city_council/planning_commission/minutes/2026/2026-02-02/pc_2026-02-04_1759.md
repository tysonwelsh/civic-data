5. PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on the
Ordinance Amending Chapter 9.10.020 Related to the Adoption of the Wildland
Urban Interface Building Standards, City of Bluffdale, Applicant. Fred Donaldson,
Staff Presenter.
City Attorney, Fred Donaldson, presented the proposed ordinance and indicated that the item is
required to participate in the Utah Wildland Fire Program. If certain conditions are met, the State
will pay all wildland fire costs incurred by the City. One requirement is to adopt the Wildland
Urban Interface Building Standards.
Chapter 9.10.020, Building Codes Adopted, will be amended to read:
A. Adoption: The City of Bluffdale adopts the State Construction Code as adopted in
Utah Code Annotated title 15A, "State Construction and Fire Codes Act" and the .
building standards in the 2006 edition of the Utah Wildland Urban Interface
Code, issued by the International Code Council, with the alternatives or
amendments approved by the Utah Division of Forestry, Fire, and State Lands
and subsequent amendments thereof, as the Building Code of the City of Bluffdale.
Mr. Donaldson reported that a current bill before State Legislature would amend State Code, and
the language would automatically update City Code to the 2024 edition of the Wildland Urban
Interface (“WUI”) Code.
The updated WUI Map was reviewed. Fire Chief, Matt Evans reported that the map was created
by the Utah Department of Natural Resources. High risk areas are identified in red, and those
cities will be required to perform neighborhood assessments. There are no risk high areas in
Bluffdale. The Bringhurst Farm area in yellow is at mild risk, but that risk will subside once the
property is developed.

6. Training on Open and Public Meetings Act and Procedures. Fred Donaldson, Staff
Presenter.

Mr. Donaldson reported that the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act (“OPMA”) and Government
Records and Access Management Act (“GRAMA”) are the State’s sunshine laws, which promote
transparency in government. The OPMA provides that meetings and deliberations of public bodies
are to be open to the public unless properly closed. It is the legislature’s intention that political
subdivisions take actions and conduct deliberations openly.
The OPMA applies only to meetings, which are defined as:

e A gathering of a public body.

e With a quorum present.

¢ That is convened by the person with authority to convene the public body following the

process provided by law.

e For the purpose of acting as a public body to receive public comment on a relevant matter,
deliberate about a relevant matter, or take action on a relevant matter.

e A meeting can be in person, electronic, or hybrid.
Mr. Donaldson indicated that in the past, ribbon cuttings or other informal gatherings could
potentially invoke the requirements of the OPMA. That had been clarified as stated above.
A “public body” is an administrative, advisory, executive, or legislative body that:

e Was created by the Utah Constitution, statute, rule, ordinance, or resolution;

e It consists of two or more persons;

e Spends, distributes, or is supported by tax money; and

e Is vested with the authority to make decisions about the public’s business.
The following are public bodies:

e City Council

e Planning Commission

e City Redevelopment Agency

e Local Building Authority

e Historic Preservation Commission

e Arts Advisory Board

e Healthy Bluffdale Coalition
At least 24 hours’ notice is required for public meeting. Notice must include the agenda, date,
time, and place of the meeting, and the agenda must be reasonably specific. Matters that are not
on the agenda may be discussed, but final action cannot be taken if the item is not listed on the
agenda. Notice must be posted at City Hall and on the Utah Public Notice Website, and a schedule
of regular meetings must be posted yearly. Although emergency meetings are rare for the Planning
Commission, they do not require 24-hour notice.

Detailed, written minutes must be kept for all meetings and include the following:
e Date, time and place of the meeting.
e The names of all members present and absent.
e The substance of all matters proposed, discussed, or decided.
e Individual votes on the matter.
The approved, written minutes are the official record of the meeting. However, the requirement
can be satisfied by taking video. Minutes are classified as public under GRAMA and are
distributed through the public notice website and City Hall.
Open meetings are recorded, and the complete, unedited recording is public record. Members of
the public can also record meetings. Bluffdale public meetings are livestreamed on the City
. website.
Per Bluffdale City Code § 1.160.010, any public body can hold electronic meetings. An anchor
location is required so that members of the public can participate in the meeting. However, that
requirement can be suspended for certain public health emergencies like COVID-19. Electronic
meetings must follow the same procedures as regular meetings, but all votes must be by roll call.
Mr. Donaldson reported that the legislature recently added the following language regarding
predetermining public body action to the Code:
Individuals constituting a quorum of a public body may not act together outside a meeting
in a concerted, deliberate way to predetermine an action to be taken by the public body at
a meeting on a relevant matter.
Members of a public body should not transmit electronic messages to each other during a convened
meeting. However, they may do so outside of meetings. Meetings should be orderly. If someone
is disruptive, they can be removed.

Meetings are open to the public unless closed for a specific reason, and no local government
meetings are legally required to be closed.
If a meeting is closed:

e A quorum of the public body must be present.

e Two-thirds of members present must vote to close the meeting.

e Must first hold an open public meeting with proper notice before closing a meeting.

e The reason for closure must be publicly announced, and a roll call vote must be taken.

e The closed part of the meeting must be recorded except in very specific situations.
Reasons for closing a meeting include discussion of:

e The character, professional competence, or physical or mental health of an individual.

e Collective bargaining.

e Pending or reasonably imminent litigation.

e Security personnel, devices, or systems.

e An investigative proceeding regarding allegations of criminal misconduct.

e Certain issues related to procurements and trade secrets.

‘¢ A loan application if the discussion would disclose personal financial information or

nonpublic business financial information that could result in competitive injury.

e The sale of real property, water rights, or water shares under certain circumstances.
During a closed meeting, a public body may not:

e Approve an ordinance, resolution, rule, regulation, contract, or appointment.

e Interview a person to fill an elected position.

e Discuss character, competence, or physical or mental health of an applicant to fill a mid-

term vacancy.
e Take final action on a matter.

Intentional violation of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act is a Class B Misdemeanor, and a
court can void any action taken in violation of the act. However, violations can also be cured in
subsequent meetings. Violations may incur court and attorney fees.
The following entities enforce the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act:

e The Attorney General

e The County Attorney

e A private citizen denied a right under OPMA may also commence a lawsuit.
In response to a question raised by Commissioner Swanson, Mr. Donaldson clarified that the
character, competence, or physical or mental health of an applicant for a mid-term vacancy would
have to be discussed in an open public meeting. Discussions should be on professional
qualifications.
Commissioner Swanson asked what constitutes a meeting outside of a public meeting. Mr.
Donaldson reported that three Commissioners should not meet together to discuss Planning
Commission business because that would constitute a quorum. If two members meet to discuss a
matter, that is not considered a meeting. Deliberations must be conducted on the record at a public
meeting, but it is acceptable to share your opinion with another Commissioner. In response to a
follow-up question from Commissioner Swanson, Mr. Donaldson reported that members of the
public are always allowed to record public meetings.
Mr. Donaldson reported that the OPMA was changed significantly in the 2024 legislative session,
but no substantial changes were made in 2025 or so far in 2026. Chair Cragun asked Mr.
Donaldson to provide an update on the session once it concludes.
