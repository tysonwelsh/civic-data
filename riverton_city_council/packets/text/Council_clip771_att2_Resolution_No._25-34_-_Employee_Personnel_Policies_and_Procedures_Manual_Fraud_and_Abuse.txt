                                  RIVERTON CITY, UTAH
                                  RESOLUTION NO. 25-34

    A RESOLUTION APPROVING AN AMENDMENT TO THE RIVERTON CITY
            PERSONNEL POLICIES AND PROCEDURES MANUAL

        WHEREAS, the Riverton City Personnel Policies and Procedures Manual (the
“Manual”) is used to set acceptable parameters for employment operations, and define employee
benefits; and

       WHEREAS, the Manual is reviewed and updated from time to time as changes in law or
in employment practices arise; and

       WHEREAS, City staff has evaluated the manual and determined some terms need to be
updated to improve the standards in the Manual for reporting fraud and abuse; and

        WHEREAS, the Riverton City Council finds and determines that an amendment to the
Manual as proposed by City staff would encourage employees to report fraud and abuse, which
in turn protects City held property, funds, and other assets; and

        WHEREAS, the City Council of Riverton City, Utah, does hereby determine that it is in
the best interests of the health, safety, and welfare of the citizens of Riverton City and City
employees to adopt revisions to the Manual which describe the standards for reporting fraud and
abuse;

        NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED by the City Council of Riverton City, Utah,
as follows:

       1. The Riverton City Personnel Policies and Procedures Manual is hereby amended to
          added Section 6.16, as provided in Exhibit A of this Resolution. The Mayor is hereby
          directed to ensure that the Manual is accordingly revised and made available to all City
          employees within a reasonable time from adoption of this Resolution.

       2. This Resolution shall become effective immediately upon passage.

6.16 REPORTING FRAUD AND ABUSE

All employees are responsible for reporting any improper employee action which an employee observes
or reasonably believes to have occurred. An improper employee action is defined to mean any action
undertaken by an employee that the observing employee reasonably believes amounts to a violation of:
any federal law; any state law; any City ordinance; or City policy.

City employees who become aware of improper employee actions should raise the issue first with their
supervisor, the City Attorney, the City Manager, Human Resources, Administrative Services Director, or
any director within the City. The employee may be required to submit a written report stating in detail
the basis of the employee’s belief that an improper governmental action has occurred. The City shall
take prompt action to investigate the report of improper governmental action. All Riverton City officers
and employees involved in the investigation shall keep the identity of the reporting employee(s)
confidential to the extent possible under law unless the employee authorizes the disclosure of his or her
identity. After an investigation has been completed, the employee reporting the improper governmental
action shall be advised of a summary of the results of the investigation, except that a final personnel
action taken as a result of the investigation may be kept confidential. This policy shall not be construed
to limit the rights of investigated employees to receive information during formal predetermination or
disciplinary proceedings provided in this Manual or by statute. If the reporting employee does not
believe that an adequate investigation was undertaken, insufficient action has been taken to address
the improper government action, or that for other reasons the improper governmental action is likely to
recur, the employee may report the information to the City Attorney, City Manager, or Mayor. The City
adheres to all federal, state, and local laws at it pertains to whistleblower protection.


ANONYMOUS COMPLAINTS
Anonymous complaints are discouraged; however, if a complaint is submitted anonymously, it shall be
directed to the City Attorney for a recommendation on processing the complaint. Upon review of the
complaint, the City Attorney will recommend to the City Manager (or Mayor if the complaint involves
the City Manager) whether a formal investigation should be undertaken or that the complaint has no
merit. A review of anonymous complaints will be provided to the Audit Committee on a regular basis.


FAILURE TO REPORT
Failure to report improper employee actions, cooperate in an investigation, or provide knowingly false
information may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination.
