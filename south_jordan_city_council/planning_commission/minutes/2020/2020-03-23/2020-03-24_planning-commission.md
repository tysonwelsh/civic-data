A. FÖHN BOUTIQUE AND EVENT CENTER, CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT
Location: 1098 W. South Jordan Parkway, Suites 103 & 104
File No: PLCUP202000016
Applicant: Jinkee McFadden

Chair Hollist said I wanted to remind everyone that is here tonight for the Fohn Boutique and Event
Center Conditional Use Permit that we had a Public Hearing at the last meeting, so we will not be taking
comments tonight. We are here tonight to have the staff present to us what they found over the last 2
weeks concerning the parking situation.

Planner Brad Sanderson said I sent out an email and I apologize for the tardiness of it, but you should
have received it about 45 minutes ago. If you haven’t seen it yet, please get into your email so you can see
the updated information (Attachment B).

Planner Brad Sanderson reviewed background information on this item from the staff report. He said we
can only condition this based on what the parking demand would be that is above and beyond what the
permitted use would be, so based on the operation plan that the applicant gave us called for roughly 100
guests, we figured they would need 13 extra parking stalls for their portion of the businesses. We have
added a condition that if they can find 13 extra stalls above and beyond of what they parking center
already has, then the rest of the conditions would apply. Since the staff report went out to you originally,
the applicant has been diligently working to get those stalls. As you know, there is a site plan approved to
the north for an office complex and they are over parked by about 20 stalls. They are under construction
right now, but I asked the applicant (Jinkee McFadden) and the land lord (Bob Wager) to approach them
and see if they could get a shared parking agreement. I think they are still in the process of having that
conversation, we can ask the applicant where they are in the process. If in the event, they can’t get the
stalls that is where the email I sent you will come in play. They are willing to reduce the number of guest
to fall into the number of stalls required for a permitted use

Chair Hollist said so they are 36 stalls short of the current use for all of the businesses combined, and
because of the space that this business has you are requiring them to have 13 additional stalls, is that
correct?

Planner Sanderson said that is correct. The reason is, we can’t require a Conditional Use applicant to
solve all of the conditions.

Commissioner Catmull said at the last meeting I asked if you can require that the shared parking
agreement be in place as part of the conditional use. I didn’t see that in your conditions.

Planner Sanderson said I am not opposed to that, but I would think at some point you would want to have
a discussion with the applicant and see where they are at in the process.

Megan Taufa, (Applicant) – said we have been in contact with the construction people that are doing the
office building complex to the north of us. They have some excess parking, so we are looking for a shared
parking agreement, or if we can rent some parking spaces from them. We are still in the discussion phase
of working on things with them, but because of everything that has been happening things are moving
much slower. We did discuss stripping the back side of the building, and that would give us an additional
8 stalls, but that would still leave us trying to find 4 more spaces, at least until we could get those 13

anything in writing yet.

Planner Sanderson said the Planning Commission will not, but it will be reviewed by the Planning and

Chair Hollist said is there a reason that we don’t remove the second meter now. I wasn’t entirely clear on

Planner Sanderson said we as staff recommended it and pushed that it be removed, but the applicant

Brandon Aymes – said I am representing Brian Rindlisbacher on the subdivision amendment. There are
absolutely no intentions for the garage to be anything else but a garage. The reason we felt that it was a
good idea to keep the meter there, was so that there wouldn’t be a need for any construction or digging if
we leave it there, and the water line does run across the street into the neighbors park strip. The water was
always just intended to supply the garage with water for the recreation room, and game area for the
grandkids. It just makes more sense to run water from both directions on this particular lot and the way it
lays out, rather than running a line across the entire lot.

Chair Hollist opened the Public Hearing.

Mike Wilcox, South Jordan - said I am curious how the code applies to the fencing, because it is a
corner lot impacting the front yard, and they proposing to make it a rear yard, is that going to impact the
neighborhood? I would also, like to know why this was done as a subdivision amendment, and not a lot
line adjustment to consolidate the lots?

Chair Hollist closed the Public Hearing.

Assistant Attorney Sheeran said I can address the reason for the subdivision amendment. The Utah Code
dictates whether things should be done by a subdivision amendment, or a lot line adjustment. The codes
states that anything that changes the boundaries of the subdivision, or any changes in a subdivision need
to be processed by a subdivision amendment. There was an attempt at this last Legislature Session to
include an easier process to do lot consolidations. They are fairly simple, and there are not as many
concerns as there would be with a subdivision amendment, but that ultimately wasn’t brought forth at the
Legislature, so until we have some clarification to the Utah Code we will be processing a lot
consolidation in a subdivision through a subdivision amendment.

Planner Sanderson said if and when this lot gets approved and consolidated it is intend to all become the
rear yard, in which case the applicant could run a fence along the adjacent sidewalk around the home to
the north. That being said and based on the concept that you have there is a 30 x 30 clear vision site
triangle on the corner that needs to be maintained so traffic can see there. There is also a clear vision site
area next to the driveway that is 10 x 10 triangle, and that is there to allow a vehicle to be able to pull out
and see a pedestrian coming down the sidewalk.
