Public Comment No comments Transportation Improvement Program Modification Process Amendment - Shawn Eliot This is an amendment to the TIP modification process and the proposal will bring the MPO in line with UDOT STIP process. When a sponsor requests additional funding equal or less than 25% or up to $250,000 it is staff modification. A Board modification is equal or over 25% and over $250,000. The amendment would change the dollar value to $500,000 for both Staff and Board Modifications. Current Staff Modification Suggested Staff Modification Current Board Modification Suggested Board Modification Additional Funds < = 25% or up to $250,000 Additional Funds < = 25% or up to $500,000 Additional Funds > = 25% or over $250,000 Additional Funds > = 25% or over $500,000 The MPO sets aside 10% of funding for a contingency fund. If a Staff Modification for a project is needed then staff would use the contingency funds following the above policy process. Also, MPO policy is if a sponsor has multiple projects and a completed project has cost savings those savings can be moved to a project needing additional funding. This would not impact the general funds or any other sponsors funded projects. And the funding process needs to be followed for both Staff and Board modifications. Mayor J.H. Hadfield moved to have the Regional Planning Committee approve the proposed amendment to the Transportation Improvement Program Modification Process to extend the Staff Modification amount from $250,000 to $500,000, to get the process in line with the UDOT process. Mayor Bert Wilson seconded the motion. The motion carried unanimously. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning November 5, 2015 Page 3 Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) Modification - Shawn Eliot The Elk Ridge Drive Extension Project (Salem Parkway) was funded in 2009 for $8m. The project extends the Elk Ridge Drive from SR-198 in Salem to UC 8000 South near the I-15 Benjamin interchange. The Army Corp of Engineers approved the project and currently right-of-way is being purchased. Construction is planned in 2016. The project is underfunded and the estimated to cost is now $12.5m. The additional costs occurred because of the additional time needed to obtain a permit by the Army Corp of Engineers, the additional changes need to avoid wetlands, and for unforeseen right-of-way. North County Blvd is complete, the project had a cost savings of $4,285,611. The MPO TIP Policy and Procedures states that a sponsor (Utah County) with multiple projects can move funding between projects when there is cost savings on one project can benefit another. MPO staff can approve changes up to $250,000. Since this change is over $250,000, this change is being brought before the MPO Regional Planning Committee for their approval. Mayor Brian Wall move that the MPO Regional Planning Committee approve the recommendation of the MPO TAC by modifying the Transportation Improvement Program to add $4,285,611, the cost savings from North County Blvd, to the Elk Ridge Drive Extension project. Mayor Hadfield added the County needs to guarantee that a sidewalk will be put in. Mayor Chris Pengra seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. UDOT Federal Funds Exchange Program - Shawn Eliot UDOT has an exchange program which allows a MPO to exchange federal STP dollars for state dollars on projects. Staff has been working with UDOT on a pilot program that would allow the MPO to exchange their STP dollars for state dollars at the rate of $.85 to the dollar. The federal requirements would be removed and there could be a cost savings of 15% to 30%. UDOT requires a 10% local match, currently the local governments pay 6.77%. The funds would be exchange annually. With the federal requirements removed the MPO would be allowed to manage their projects with UDOT in an advisory role. The MPO would probably need to add an engineer position to staff to manage the projects. UDOT wants to wait until a federal transportation bill is passed and in place for at least one year. The TAC Committee supports staff continuing to pursuing the pilot program of exchanging federal dollars for state dollars. Mayor Chris Pengra moved to have MAG staff continue to explore this opportunity and return to TAC and Regional Planning Committee when appropriate. Mayor Jim Miller seconded the motion. The motion carried unanimously. Transportation Improvement Program Selection Process Amendment Item #6 - Shawn Eliot Currently, TIP projects are programmed by the MPO committees using the MPO’s federal funds and the Utah County Sales Tax funds. Utah County sales tax funded projects are recommended to the County Commission for their approval. Federal funds require a 6.77% local match; there is no local match on Mountainland MPO Regional Planning November 5, 2015 Page 4 county funds. When a sponsor requests TIP funds they know that they are required to have a 6.77% match. A local match on the county funds would make all funding sources equal, sponsor wouldn’t be able to request one fund over the other. Local match would bring more funding to the MPO. Currently MPO has oversight on MPO funds. If the local match for county sales tax projects takes place staff would request to have oversight on the county projects also. Staff is recommending that TIP Selection Process be amended to require the County Sales Tax projects have a 6.77% local match. Also, staff will recommend to TAC which funds should be used with each proposed project based on the available MPO federal funds and Utah County Transportation Sales Tax funds with required match be paid by project sponsor. And let MPO have oversight on county sales tax projects. SUGGESTED RPC MOTION 1: I move that the MPO Regional Planning Committee on the recommendation of the MPO Technical Advisory Committee, approve the amendment to the Mountainland MPO TIP Selection Process to require Utah County Sales Tax funds have the same local match as the MPO federal funds. It is found that this change would help equalize all funding programmed by the MPO committees and require project sponsors to have some financial responsibility. Which includes a provision for UDOT to reimburse the county for rights-of-way on state corridors preserved with local funds. In addition, the Eligible Corridors Map which determines where Corridor Preservations Funds may be allocated was last adopted in 2007.
