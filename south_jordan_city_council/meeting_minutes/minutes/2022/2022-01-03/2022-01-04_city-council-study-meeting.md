Mayor Dawn Ramsey mentioned she may need to ask for one of those budget meetings to be
rescheduled as she is attending National Conferences in Washington, DC in early February. The
two budget meetings are scheduled for January 26 and February 9, so she will get back to staff
with any changes she may need.

Council Member Tamara Zander mentioned she spoke earlier with City Attorney Ryan Loose
about three residents on the north side of 11400 South asking to do a three minute presentation in
relation to an item on tonight’s agenda. She asked Mayor Ramsey when she would like that done
during the regular council meeting.

Mayor Ramsey said she will give them public comment time for their presentation when we get
to that item on the agenda. She has had some updates on the retail incentives piece; they are not
quite there yet, but she asked for an additional League board meeting tomorrow. There is a lot of
work happening and the League has their own draft legislation, including one from the
subcommittee; they are a little bit different, but she thinks the one from the League is much
cleaner, more targeted and just better policy. A third proposal came up yesterday on possibly just
limiting no retail tax incentives on anything over 20,000 square feet. There are a lot of decisions
to be made in the next few days and she will keep everyone updated as things come about.

City Attorney Ryan Loose mentioned Local Officials Day and Youth Day on January 19.
Regarding the amount of time they should plan to be there, it depends on how involved they plan
on being. If they want to do anything up at the Capitol they will need to block out more time, but
if they will just be at the Salt Palace for the lunch with the Youth Council and our legislators he
recommended setting aside 11:30 a.m.– 1:30 p.m. The main program is 12:00 p.m. – 1:00 p.m.,
but they want to be there a little early and there is always chatting afterwards.

Mayor Ramsey said they have the students going to the Capitol that morning; the Governor is
giving his State of the State Address that night. The governor is actually coming to speak to the
kids at the Capitol that day, but they haven’t invited him to come speak at the Salt Palace. They
did ask the speaker and the senate president to come, and she believes they will be having a
dialogue where she will be facilitating a conversation and questions with both of them. She also
thinks they are going to invite a Youth Council member or two, potentially from our city to ask
some Youth Council perspective questions. Earlier today they discussed getting excused
absences for any Youth Council members who attend, so she is going to see what she can do to
make that happen.

Attorney Loose mentioned that day is always a great time to touch base with our legislators
during their first week and see the youth; the League always does a good job with presenters and
keynotes.

Attorney Loose explained that a few years ago, the legislature changed the law that certain

                  E.1.       Bangerter project update. (By Bangerter Highway Environmental Studies
                             Project Manager, Brian Allen)

Brian Allen reviewed his prepared presentation (Attachment A) regarding the preferred
alternative for 9800 South and Bangerter Interchange.
South Jordan City                                                                                   5

Council Member Patrick Harris asked if UDOT had done a study on the impact of a 9-12 month
closure of 9800 South on businesses and schools.

Mr. Allen said they plan to do a traffic analysis for impact, but they like to get messaging out
ahead of that so people can start planning alternate routes.

Council Member Tamara Zander asked how many months we would be without the pedestrian
bridge during construction.

Mr. Allen said they have been working with Jordan School District, but typically when that safe
route to school is blocked they do bussing which will be worked out with the school district.

Council Member Zander asked if those bussing costs go to UDOT or the school district.

Mr. Allen responded that UDOT would take on those costs as a part of the project.

Mayor Dawn Ramsey mentioned that we are working really hard for this to go under, and to
keep the pedestrian bridge in the same place. She asked how long it will be before the final
decision is made by UDOT as to whether this interchange goes over or under.

Mr. Allen said the two alternatives have some differing right of way impacts, and those
acquisitions take a lot of time. UDOT is planning on that decision being made right after the
legislative session. They won’t know who is constructing this until Winter of 2022.

Council Member Marlor asked if Mr. Allen had a slide that shows which areas are being taken
out, whether they go over or under. He is interested in seeing the impacts on neighbors and
businesses for each option.

Mr. Allen said they have detailed scroll plots, however he did not bring those tonight. On
January 10 and January 12 they are having the public open houses where they go through and put
dots on each house to show which ones are full relocations, etc. There will be some minor right
of way acquisitions from the businesses on the east and temporary construction easements to
reconstruct driveways. The Dollar Tree will be a full acquisition, however other businesses close
by may be able to be saved. They may have to demo the Dollar Tree to provide access for other
businesses. On the map you can also see that there are a lot of homes that will be purchased and
residents relocated. As they get further north, it gets more subjective and they look more at
purchasing backyards and other pieces of land. They also do hardship appraisals to help property
owners determine if they want to stay or be relocated.

Mayor Ramsey said there has been a lot of conversation with Elk Ridge Middle School and
Jordan School District, they are fully on board with the plan to go under. If this preferred
alternative is what we are able to make happen, the cell tower will have to be relocated but that
can be done on their property without cutting into the school.

Council Member McGuire asked if construction starts in Spring of 2023, when does UDOT
anticipate completion.
South Jordan City                                                                                    6

January 4, 2022



Mr. Allen said typically these interchanges take one and a half to two years, depending on the
challenges that arise during construction. He pointed out where the school would like to relocate
the cell tower, to a park strip on the west side of the school; this placement aligns with city code.

Council Member Zander mentioned that with the school and pedestrian bridge situation, this
interchange is unique and unlike other interchanges. The city’s deficit is $24 million and
obviously, everyone here is working on lobbying for more money from the state. She asked if
UDOT has any responsibility financially in this situation, or do they just come in and give the
cost without any discussion. Are there any allowances or variations where UDOT comes up with
some of the deficit when there is a school involved, as well as a pedestrian bridge which is a
safety issue.

Mr. Allen said the deficit is $24 million, but UDOT is planning to participate with $12 million of
that deficit. UDOT will be purchasing significant right of way from the school, and Elk Ridge is
also willing to donate some of the property to help with some of the cost, but only if the
interchange goes under.

Mayor Ramsey said it is exciting to be at this point, having this conversation about 9800 South.
One big reason for the excitement is that this has received priority funding, and been moved up
from the funding model and the timing that were originally planned for years down the road.
This will be a lot of work, a lot of money, and a lot of closures and inconvenience but 10400
South will be fully done and functional by the time everything starts.

Mr. Allen noted that there is contact info (hotline number, email and website) on the last page of
the presentation (Attachment A) for anyone who has questions or concerns on this proposed
interchange.
