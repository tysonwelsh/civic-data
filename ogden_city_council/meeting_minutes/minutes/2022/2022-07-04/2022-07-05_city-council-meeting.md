requested the approval of a boundary line adjustment to bring Ogden City parcels 11-014-0034 (1512 North Washington
Boulevard) and 11-014-0035 (1514 North Washington Boulevard), a total of approximately 1.7 acres of land into North Ogden.
The request is made to accommodate a housing development approved in North Ogden. The resolution directs that a public
hearing be scheduled to allow a sixty (60) day comment period prior to adoption of an ordinance authorizing the boundary
adjustment. The Planning Commission recommended approval of the boundary adjustment with a vote of 4-1, subject to requiring
that a 10-foot landscaped setback along the north property line of the Washington Mobile Estates development. The vote against

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated the developer is proposing to build a 142-unit apartment
complex with 92 units in the City of North Ogden and 50 units on 1.7 acres currently in Ogden City. Property at 1512 North
Washington Boulevard has a home on it. The property at 1514 North Washington Boulevard is vacant. The properties are located
just north of Washington Mobile Estates, a mobile home development. While the developer could build across municipal
boundaries, the permitting process and utility connections would be simplified for the developer by adjusting the boundaries and
placing the entire development within North Ogden’s boundaries. The matter has been discussed by the Council during two
different work session meetings and directed staff to contact North Ogden City to determine if North Ogden would be willing to
exchange another parcel of property similar in size to the one in question or whether North Ogden would be willing to enter into a
revenue sharing agreement to allow Ogden to receive the tax revenue from the property. The general consensus among Council
members was that there did not appear to be any benefit to the City in agreeing to a boundary adjustment without these additional
agreements. Assistant City Attorney Tanner contacted North Ogden City’s administrator and attorney, Jonathan Call. Mr. Call
indicated that North Ogden would not exchange property for the boundary adjustment. Additional concerns that were expressed
by the Council include the loss of property tax revenue of approximately $23,000 per year and the project not conforming with
Ogden City’s density, open space, and setbacks standards. He concluded that if the Council adopts the resolution before them
tonight, a public hearing will be set for at least 60 days from today to allow for public input and formal consideration of the
boundary adjustment.

Chair Nadolski asked if the lost revenue of $23,000 per year is based upon the current value of the property or the
projected future value of the property upon completion of the development. Mr. Brierley indicated it is based upon the future
projected value of the property.

Council member Hyer stated he spent some time talking with the stakeholders in this arrangement, the developer, North
Ogden Mayor, and North Ogden Council members. North Ogden elected officials were not willing to consider a land swap and
they also communicated they would not agree to the boundary adjustment if the situation were reversed. The developer contacted
him and indicated he had not been able to reach an agreement with North Ogden to support modifications that would make the
boundary adjustment beneficial to Ogden City in some way.

Proposed Ordinance 2022-34 regarding water-wise landscaping
requirements

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed waterwise landscaping
requirements came before the Council for consideration. ‘The memo stated in an effort to reduce the amount of water used to
sustain landscaping in Ogden, the Administration is proposing amendments to several sections of landscape-related code. These
amendments place a limit on how much landscaped space may have turf grass in new commercial, industrial, and multi-family
developments. The proposed changes also allow more rock and mulch to be used than was previously allowed. This report also
outlines other various changes to the city’s landscaping requirements. If approved, these amendments would qualify Ogden City
residents to apply for the Flip-Y our-Strip program with Weber Basin Water Conservancy District offered in conjunction with the
City’s own park strip conversion program.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the
proposed amendments to the Ogden City Code relating to waterwise landscaping; most notably, a new chapter is being added to
the zoning ordinance that specifically addresses and provides standards and definitions for waterwise landscaping.

Proposed Ordinance 2022-35 regarding revising parking requirements for
dedicated low to moderate income housing

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a zone text amendment pertaining to
low/moderate-income housing parking requirements came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the Ogden City
Planning Division is proposing a change to the zoning ordinance to amend the minimum and maximum required parking stalls for
dedicated low- and moderate-income housing in residential and commercial zones. The proposed amendment would reduce
required parking for dedicated low- and moderate-income housing projects in residential zones from 2 stalls per unit to between |
and 1.4 stalls per unit. For dedicated low- and moderate-income housing projects in commercial zones, the proposal would reduce
the parking maximums from 1.75 stalls per unit to 1.4 stalls per unit but would maintain the minimum 0.8 stalls per unit. The
proposed change was based on a parking study commissioned by the City. The change to residential parking standards is being
proposed as a follow-up to the amendments to design standards for multi-family residential development in commercial zones that
were approved in October 2020.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated the Planning Commission reviewed the proposal at its May
4, 2022 meeting and forwarded a recommendation of approval to the Council. The recommendation was based on the finding that
the amendments are consistent with the General Plan in encouraging better utilization of parking to reduce the parking
requirements for dedicated low- and moderate-income housing.

Proposed Resolution 2022-15 approving an Interlocal agreement between
Ogden City and Weber Basin Conservancy District for the creation of a joint
park strip water conservation program

A memo from the Public Services Department came before the Council to consider a resolution authorize an interlocal
agreement with Weber Basin Water Conservancy District (WBWCD). The memo stated the proposed agreement is for joint
participation in a park strip conversion program—more commonly known as the “flip your strip” program. Under the terms of the
agreement, Weber Basin will review applications, provide training to qualified applicants, and contribute $100,000 annually to the
program. Ogden City will administer the program, including permitting and inspection services and provide matching funds
(50/50) to the program.

Public Utilities Manager Herd summarized the memo and noted the purpose of the program is to:

e Promote water efficient landscaping and assure sensible use of the City’s water

e Enhance the visual environment

e Allow for increase in population, while ensuring adequate water supply within Ogden City
e Assist in meeting state and local water conservation goals

e Provide education and awareness.

The City is collaborating with Weber Basin’s Park Strip Lawn Conversion Program (Flip Your Strip) and proposes a City
incentive of $1.25 per square foot of conversion, which will be matched by Weber Basin Water Conservancy, making residents
eligible for an incentive of $2.50 per square foot. The average park strip size is 600 square feet and with the amount of funding
available for the program, Administration estimates that 133 park strips could be converted. Mr. Herd then summarized program
qualifications:

Residential customer within the Ogden City’s service area (Single Family Dwellings)
Must have a Park Strip
Attend Lawn Conversion class through WBWCD
Park strip must be well maintained lawn
e Remove all the lawn in the park strip and replace it with water efficient landscaping
e Converted park strips will be watered with a drip irrigation system or hand watered
e Converted park strips must be covered by at least 3 inches of mulch or accepted ground cover permeable to
water and air
e Trees must be planted or left in the landscape as required by the City
e Must complete within 10 months
e Park strip must not be returned to lawn
e All work must comply with City regulations
In conclusion, Mr. Herd summarized the planning and approval process for incentive applications.
Chair Nadolski introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2022-15, entitled:
“A resolution of the Ogden City Council approving an interlocal agreement between Ogden City and the Weber
Basin Water Conservancy District for the creation of a joint Park Strip Water Conservation Program.”
