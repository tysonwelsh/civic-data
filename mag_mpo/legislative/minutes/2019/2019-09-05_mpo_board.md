Corridor Preservation Policy and Eligible Corridors Map Review - Jim Price, MAG In 2006, Utah County enacted the Local Option Corridor Preservation Fund, a program paid for by a $10.00 annual vehicle registration fee. Statute requires that money from the Fund be spent according to locally adopted policies, and within corridors that are included on county, municipal, and MPO Regional Transportation Plans. (UC 72-2-1-117.5) Historically, the MAG Highways RTP has been adopted as the list of eligible corridors in order to comport with statute. Recently the state legislature made certain transit rights of way eligible for corridor preservation. Jim Price asked for feedback from the Committee regarding adopting a new Eligible Corridors Map, including possible transit elements, and about a process of amending the Eligible Corridors Map. There were concerns as to whether transit would make it too competitive for Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee September 5, 2019 Page 4 cities to apply for funds. Jim Price stated that it will be a good tool to have if needed and that RPC has the final say for approval. In addition, purchases made by UTA that are not used, will be put back into the fund. The final recommendation will be given January 2020. DRAFT Federal Reauthorization Principals- Andrew Jackson The current federal surface transportation authorization legislation, the Fixing America’s Surface Transportation Act (FAST Act), expires on September 30, 2020. Utah’s transportation agencies and partners have jointly developed key principles for a multi-year reauthorization that reflect priorities to keep Utah moving. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee September 5, 2019 Page 5 Discussions have taken place regarding how it will be of benefit to the members of Utah's Congressional delegation, and others, as it will provide a high-level set of shared principles for transportation reauthorization. These principles would be supplemented by more specific policy positions for each mode and from each agency, similar to the coordinated approach agencies within Utah have taken together in the past. The DRAFT Federal Reauthorization Principals was reviewed with the Committee. Tracking Housing Types and Associated Costs – Mark Christensen Saratoga Springs presented data taken from their GIS that is tied to the overall theme of managing growth and trying to develop a financial model to create a fiscally sustainable model. This topic crosses many fields of development, planning, theory and practice, and how GIS technology can be used in day to day management. There is a myriad of issues that span the range from referendum limiting growth (2013) to average unit-size and high-density development issues, in addition to water and transit planning.
