         The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00
p.m.; discuss the Fiscal Year 2021 budget specific to employee groups, Management Services, Police Department, City Attorney,
Schedule A, and Salary Schedule; discuss Council business; and hear Council comments.

Agenda Review
         Chair Choberka and members of Council staff briefly reviewed the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to
begin at 6:00 p.m.

Fiscal Year 2021 Budget
          Employee Groups: Fire Employee Group Representative Atkinson indicated the group has seen the presentation
regarding the Mayor's proposed budget and understands there will be no merit or cost of living adjustments (COLAs) this year;
they understand that the City is trying to continue to operate in these challenging times. He noted Administration has offered other
benefits to the Fire Department and this is much appreciated, but the group is hopeful the City will pursue benchmarking and
reinstate merit increases as soon as possible to ensure that the City's Fire Department is competitive with other agencies. He noted
the Group is curious to learn the results of the City-wide salary survey and any plans to implement new policies responsive to the
results. He concluded by thanking the Council for their support of the Fire Department during the COVID-19 pandemic.
          Council member White asked when the findings of the salary survey will be available. Management Services Director
Buxton indicated the project was put on hold as a cost saving measure, but once funding is again available, it can be completed
within one month.

           Travis Kearl, Ogden Police Benefits Association (OPBA), repmted on a survey the OPBA completed in the Police
Department; he has received 69 responses to questions intended to gauge the respondents' level of employment satisfaction.
Ninety-seven percent feels an annual four percent pay increase is extremely important. They are also eager to learn the results of
the City-wide salary survey and hear any associated wage adjustment recommendations. He asked for more infonnation regarding
the time line for that survey. Human Resources Manager Judkins indicated the study is ongoing and is in the job description phase;
at present, the City does not have funding to implement the recommendations of the study once it is completed.
           Mr. Kearl then continued his review of the fmdings of the study completed in the Police Department; employees have
communicated they feel becoming fully staffed in the essential departments of the City is very important. Regardless of the
COVID-19 pandemic, the Department is still losing officers to other agencies and the decision to forego merit increases in Fiscal
Year 2021 will only make that problem worse. He asked that Administration and the Council reconsider this issue and provide a
one-percent increase for the time being, with the commitment to evaluate the City's fmancial condition quarterly to determine if
additional merit increases can be implemented throughout the year. The OPBA would also like to have the opportunity to
participate in future budget discussions with the Council to keep them informed of the climate of the Police Department. Wage
expectations via a defmed pay scale are helpful in recruitment efforts for the Department. He asked the Council to thoughtfully
consider what level ofattrition they are willing to accept in the Police Department; Officers are being recruited to departments that
pay more and have lower call volumes; given the workload of the Ogden Police Department, it is important to preserve wage
expectations and any benefit program possible.
           Council member Lopez asked how the OPBA assesses or evaluates the loyalty of police officers to Ogden City. Mr.
Kearl stated that the Ogden City Police Department is attractive to younger police officers because of the level of training and
experience they will be able to garner; eventually, the 'newness' of that experience dulls and the day to day grind of the job begins
to wear on officers. Officers are loyal only to a ce1tain point; they hear from their friend in other agencies about the amount of
money they are making and the workload they carry and eventually they are enticed to pursue similar opportunities. He noted that
some officers have commented that they are as loyal to Ogden City as Ogden City is to them in the form of their paycheck. He
stated the lack of clear information and transparency about the salary study project and the associated timeline has created lack of
loyalty in the Police Department.
           Council member Stephens inquired as to the viewpoint of police officers regarding their benefit package, in addition to
the defmed pay scale. Mr. Kearl stated that some of the City provided benefits, such as the Own in Ogden housing assistance
program, will not be accessed by police officers. They are appreciative of their benefits, but wage increases are much more
important because an officer's retirement pay is based upon the highest three years of pay during their years of service.
           Management Services: Management Services Director Buxton used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize
the proposed FY 2021 budget for his Department. He named the Divisions encompassed within his Department, after which he
briefly discussed the accomplishments of the Department during the FY 2020 year and, more specifically, the accomplishments of
the Department during the City's response to COVID-19. He then reviewed the timeline for the Enterprise Resource Planning
(EPR) project, which is scheduled to be completed by the end of2020; proposed equipment purchases in the coming FY; FY
2021 recommended Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) projects; and programmed Information Technology IT equipment purchases.
He noted his Department is responsible to monitor City-wide travel and funding for travel activities has been removed from the
budget responsive to COVID-19; this resulted in a $283,825 budget reduction. He concluded by summarizing the ongoing
challenges facing his Department, including employee retention; staff workload and burnout; reducing employee injuries and
liability to the City; personnel pay keeping up with private companies; and IT hardware failures.
           Council member White asked for a list of facilities improvements to be completed under the FL007 General Facilities
CIP project. Mr. Buxton stated he can provide the scope of the project but noted that it generally encompasses vital improvements
or repairs in buildings throughout the City.
           Police Department: Police Chief Watt used the aid ofa PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed FY2021
budget for his Department. He discussed goals for the Department as defined by the Strategic Plan and reported that the goals
were accomplished as evidenced by measurable metrics. Part One crimes have been decreased by 27. 7 percent in the community
for the timeframe 2016-2019. He provided several graphs to illustrate the fluctuation in various Part One crimes, noting that some
calls for service have increased dramatically over the past several months during stay at home orders responsive to COVID-19. He
then reported on the status of the Project Safe Neighborhoods program; 24 defendants have been sentenced- 23 to prison and one
to probation. Eighty-three individuals have been charged and another 116 are under review or declined. One hundred forty-four
firearms, 480 pounds of methamphetamine, and 5 .1 pounds of heroin have been seized. He also touched briefly on the work being
done in the Umnanned Aircraft System (UAS) drone policing program. He then discussed new position requests in his
Department, including an Audit and Inspections Supervisor, Cmmnunity Policing-Homelessness Officer, and a Homeless
Outreach Advocate. He then stated the Depa1tment has dedicated approximately 1,094 hours to dive1t cmmnunity activities and
 training opportunities aimed at cultivating police/cmmnunity relations.
           Deputy Chief Young then repmted on the impact COVID-19 has had on the Police Depaitment; there have been no
significant interruptions in police operations. However, there has been a loss of productivity due to reduction in proactive police
work. There has been an overall reduction in calls for service and crime, but there has been an increase in violent crime. Costs to
respond to COVID-19 have been minimal and the City has accessed State and County resources to assist in purchasing personal
protective equipment for Department employees. The depaitment is pursuing available grant funding to support programs in

FY2021. School resource officers and crossing guards have remained working at local schools throughout the COVID-19 closure;
some have been staffed for breakfast and school lunch and have been paid throughout the closure. The Department will maintain
employee incentive programs in FY202 l, including the loyalty program, education incentive, and shift differential pay. Chief

opportunities for improving morale. Chief Watt stated that at some point the City will need to be proactive in addressing
compensation of the Department in order to remain competitive; he is comfortable with where the Department is at present and
believes that the Department, Administration, and City Council can work to incrementally address outstanding issues.
          City Attorney: City Attorney Williams used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the proposed FY 2021 budget
for his Department; the budget has not changed dramatically when compared to the FY 2021 budget. Travel expenditures have
been eliminated as have membership funding. This means that he and his staff will accomplish ongoing education requirements
through available online training programs.
          Schedule A: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith explained changes to the Tentative Budget are included in a
Schedule A which is attached to and adopted with the Tentative Budget. Proposed amendments outlined in the Schedule A
document often correct inadvertent errors in the Mayor's proposed budget. Schedule A corrects an error in the IT Fund by
decreasing the use of fund balance by $155,850. Prior to completing the proposed budget, expenditures in the computer
replacement program were meant to be reduced by $155,850. However, this change was not included in the proposed budget.
Additionally, changes in the Major Grants funds related to the COVID-19 CDBG funds are required to align with the Annual
Action Plan Budget. Funds in the amount of $100,00 are proposed to be moved from the Small Business Loan Program to the
Microenterprise Loan Program. An additional $30,475 increase in the Quality Neighborhood fund is also required to align with
the Annual Action Plan.
          Salary Schedule: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith presented the City's four proposed salary schedules for non-
merit special employees, merit employees, classified employees within the Police Department, and classified employees within
the Fire Department. She summarized the reclassifications and new positions included in the schedules, after which she reiterated
no COLA and merit increases are included in the proposed salary schedules. Health Insurance will increase eight percent;
increases to the traditional employee health plans will be passed on to employees. The City will continue to pay 100 percent of the
cost of the high deductible health savings plans, including the increased amount. Finally, tier two retirement increases will be
covered by the City. She concluded that action on the tentative budget is scheduled for June 9; if the Council would like to suggest
additional amendments to the budget, they should be submitted to Council staff to be included in a Schedule A-1 document.


         The meeting adjourned at 5:50 p.m.
