5.     PUBLIC HEARING.CONSIDERATION AND VOTE on a Proposed Amendment to
       the Centrum Development Agreement to Add a Fifth Story and 66 Dwelling Units to
       an Approved Apartment Building located at Approximately 14676 South Porter
       Rockwell Boulevard. Including a Reduced Parking Ratio of 1.6 Stalls Per Dwellinp
       Unit. (Application 2025-08L L.H. Perry Investments. LLC. Applicant Represented
       by Jake Finlinson. Caitlvn Tubbs,Staff Presenter.

Planning Manager, Caitlyn Tubbs reported that Items 5 and 6 would be presented together but
would require two separate motions. Item 5 was an amendment to the existing Development
Agreement for The Centrum Apartments, and Item 6 was a Text Amendment to Bluffdale City
Code Section 11.110.130, SD-X The Centrum at Bluffdale Zone.


The request would add 66 dwelling units and one story to the apartment building, which would
increase the number ofunits from 252 to 318 and building size from four to five levels ofdwelling
units with two levels ofstructured parking underneath. The maximum height would be increased
from SO to 90 feet. The Applicant also proposed reducing the parking ratio. City Code currently
requires two stalls per unit, and they proposed a reduction to 1.6 stalls per unit.

The Site Plan was reviewed indicating that the subject property is located at the southwest
intersection of 14600 South and Porter Rockwell Boulevard, directly west ofthe Rockwell Ridge
Business Park and in the same parking lot as Starbucks, Domino's Pizza, and the future Jersey
Mike's Subs.


The Centrum Development Agreement and SD-X Zone were adopted by the City Council on
January 12, 2022. The original approval included 236 dwelling units. The Development
Agreement and SD-X Zone were then amended on October 12,2022 to adopt architectural design
guidelines and increase the unit count to 252. Original and revised elevations were reviewed, and
Ms. Tubbs indicated that no changes were proposed to the parking levels or overall style and
materials.


Ms. Tubbs reported that the Applicant reviewed their other projects throughout Utah and Idaho
and tabulated the current parking demand versus the amoimt ofparking that was provided for those

projects. At the current ratio of two parking spaces per dwelling unit, the Applicant would be
required to provide 504 parking spaces for the existing units and 626 stalls for the proposed 318
units. With the proposed parking ratio of 1.6 stalls per unit, only 509 stalls would be required.

The approved Parking Plan was reviewed indicating some surface parking around the perimeter of
the building that was designated as guest parking. The remainder of surface parking on the site is
reserved for retail uses.


In response to a question from Commissioner Swanson, Ms. Tubbs reported that the 16 additional
units were approved in October 2022 based on a change in the unit mix. Both the original and
amended plans met the requirement for two parking stalls per unit. The Applicant requested a
reduction in the parking ratio to accommodate the 66 additional units. The new unit count would
include a mixture ofstudio, one-bedroom, and two-bedroom apartments.


The Applicant, Jake Finlinson stated that Perry Commercial has been working on the design for a
few years, but the project had been delayed due to market conditions. After working extensively
with their capital partners and design team,they identified this solution that will enable the project
to move forward. He reported that a large part of making a podium project feasible is analyzing
the ratio ofunits above to parking structure below and ensuring that the cost is in balance. In order
to achieve that balance, their request was to add one additional floor of units but maintain the size
ofthe parking structure.


Mr. Finlinson stated that Perry Commercial typically holds its properties for over 25 years. As
the owner and operator of both the retail and residential portions of the project, it is in their best
interest to provide adequate parking. He reviewed an analysis of their current properties that
considered how many parking stalls were provided at each property versus how many were
actually utilized and noted that many of the developments they considered are one- and two-
bedroom suburban projects. The total average was 1.14 cars and 1.57 adults per unit.


The unit mix was reviewed. Mr. Finlinson explained that the Development Agreement includes a
total of 317 units to account for any potential future changes but a total of308 units was planned

to consist of73 studio, 143 one-bedroom, and 92 two-bedroom apartments. When that data was
applied to the unit type and count,they determined that 308 units would need 483.56 parking stalls.
The Applicant felt that the requested 492.8 parking stalls would be more than adequate.

Mr. Finlinson reported that Perry Commercial will be posting signage onsite identifying surface
parking that is for retail use only and no overnight parking is allowed. All apartment parking will
be assigned, and all tenants will be required to register their vehicles with the leasing office.
Additional overflow surface parking will be available near the leasing office to accommodate
prospective tenants and guests.


A Site Map ofThe Centrum was reviewed, and Mr. Finlinson indicated that the Utah Department
of Transportation ("UDOT") will be purchasing a 39-foot section of Lot 6 to accommodate the
new right-of way on 14600 South. That will not impact parking availability, but it will reduce the
building size from 6,000 square feet to 2,300 square feet. Mr. Finlinson stated that Perry
Commercial is excited to begin construction on the apartments, and they hope to be able to move
forward.


Commissioner Kraupp did not understand the concept of using fractions for parking requirements
and noted that the request was for over 100 fewer parking stalls. He understands that the
Applicant's opinion is that it will not cause problems but expressed concern that they may be
ignoring a potential parking problem. Mr. Finlinson explained that the proposed 1.6 parking stalls
per unit was indicative ofthe unit mix. Most studios have one adult with one car, but some people
who rent studios rely on public transportation. Most one-bedroom units have one vehicle. Two-
bedroom units typically have at least one car, but the average is closer to two per unit. Averaging
those factors resulted in the requested ratio of 1.6 parking stalls per unit.

In response to a follow-up question from Commissioner Kraupp, Mr. Finlinson stated that the
actual building height will be 87 feet.


Chair Cragun asked if limits will be placed on how many people can live in each unit.
Mr. Finlinson confirmed that both the number of occupants and vehicles would be limited, and

additional vehicles would incur fees. Their goal is to reduce the number ofcars on the site. They
will require tenants to register their vehicles and potentially enforce towing of vehicles parked
overnight in the retail parking stalls. In response to a follow-up question, he stated that they are
obligated to provide ample parking to the retail tenants so they will proactively enforce parking
restrictions.


In response to a question raised by Commissioner Kraupp, Mr. Finlinson explained why the
additional floor was necessary to make the project viable. Revenue is only collected on a portion
ofthe building. The determining factor in whether a project is financeable is the ratio ofhow much
ofthe building generates revenue versus how much ofit does not.

Commissioner Swanson recalled that a lot of concem was expressed by residents in 2022 about
loss of parking on Rising Star Way. His recollection was that they discussed not enforcing the
retail parking as a way to alleviate those concerns. Ms.Tubbs stated that to her knowledge,nothing
to that effect was recorded. Chair Cragun agreed that business owners in Marketplace were
concemed that overflow from this project would park on their streets, which are already crowded.
Community and Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell stated that Staff worked with the
developer on the intersection design, and some diagonal spaces were added. No other offsite work
was required, but he did recall the discussion during both public hearings.

Commissioner Swanson stated that a lot of people park on the west side of The Centrum parking
lot, and that does not appear to be related to the retail. He asked ifthere were any written or verbal
agreements with residents of Marketplace. Mr. Finlinson stated that he was not aware of any
agreements. They were obligated to widen the right-of-way and provide some diagonal parking
to help alleviate those parking concems.


Chair Cragun remarked that it is an interesting area because parking is not allowed on Porter
Rockwell Boulevard or 14600 South. She asked where people would park if there are more cars
than anticipated. The Centrum is next door to a community that currently has a parking challenge.
Mr. Finlinson stated that they must ensure that their tenants' parking needs are met. They are
willing to accommodate the neighbors to the south during the day, but nighttime parking may be

enforced to help alleviate potential concerns. They believe the residential parking needs will be
met within the structured parking.


In response to a question from Commissioner Kraupp, Mr. Finlinson stated that they considered
other options including adding a third parking level, but the only feasible solution was the one they
presented. Ifthe amendments were not approved, the project likely would not move forward.

Commissioner Swanson asked about current overnight parking. Mr. Finlinson stated that a
maximum of 15 cars park on the property overnight,so it is not currently an issue. Looking ahead,
ifit becomes an issue, they will enforce overnight parking restrictions to resolve it.

Commissioner Swanson noted that the area of Bluffdale does not have adequate public transit; the
only current options are rideshare via commercial providers or the Utah Transit Authority("UTA")
subsidized service. He asked if the availability of public transportation was considered and if so,
how it impacted their parking calculations. Mr. Finlinson stated that all the developments included
in the calculations are suburban style walkup products that have access to more bus routes, which
could be reflected in the average of 1.4 cars per unit. That is why they put more weight on the
average number of adults per unit and increased the proposed stalls per unit to 1.6.

Commissioner Swanson's understanding was that there was no commitment regarding how
pedestrian and bicycle friendly the new intersection would be. Mr. Crowell stated that the Active
Transportation Plan team has spent some time on the issue. The Point will have trails on the east
side and Bluffdale's trails are on the west side, so they need to determine how to safely move
people through the intersection. The power lines prevent an overhead solution. In response to a
follow-up question, Mr. Crowell reported that currently a circulator route and extension of the
Blue Line are planned, but those lines do not have studies into Bluffdale. The Fresh Look Transit
Plan considers routes up to 20 years in the future. There has been a lot ofdiscussion, but currently
there is only one bus route in the City.


Commissioner Swanson stated that western Draper has a number of apartment buildings that rely
heavily on external street parking and asked about the number of parking stalls provided per unit

at similar developments in surrounding communities. Ms. Tubbs indicated that Staff did not have
that information but could obtain it at the Commission's direction.       An analysis of parking
requirements was conducted in 2016 when the parking counts were adopted, and at that time
Bluffdale's requirements were similar to other cities in the area. However, some cities have
reduced parking requirements in recent years.


Mr. Crowell indicated that there is no set standard for parking stalls. Some municipalities in the
country have abolished all parking requirements for various reasons including availability of
public transit and allowing the market to decide. Suburban communities without public transit or
that want to avoid street parking have stricter requirements. Bluffdale is urbanizing, and the 2016
study did not factor in projects with four different unit types and podium parking versus townhome
projects or ones with surface parking. Staff could study the matter in further detail and provide
more information at a later time. Commissioner Swanson stated that it would be good to have that
information, as well actual parking counts for nearby projects.


Chair Cragun reported that Perry Commercial also controls the retail space on the subject property
and is willing to enforce parking requirements, so they would also need to consider if those other
sites had enforcement and whether street parking is allowed. Parking is prohibited on Porter
Rockwell Boulevard and 14600 South, so residents will not have alternative parking.
Commissioner Swanson agreed that the developer being able to control permitting and
enforcement was an interesting mitigation, but impermitted cars could be a concern. Chair Cragun
agreed and noted that there would be nowhere else for those cars to park other than possibly across
the street in Rockwell Ridge, which could cause problems for that property owner.


Commissioner Swanson lives near the site and stated that apartment complexes like Beacon Hill
have issues with overflow parking. Mr. Crowell reported that that project was approved prior to
the two stalls per unit requirement and they were also allowed to include street parking in their
count.



Chair Cragun expressed concern about adding 66 units. She understood that they intended to
reduce the number of vehicles allowed per unit. However, it is very difficult to turn left into

to turn left. The intersection is already a problem. She asked if UDOT had indicated when they

Mr. Finlinson stated that the engineer who created the original traffic study indicated that, due to

Chair Cragun agreed that a new traffic study was not necessary due to the reduced amount ofretail
