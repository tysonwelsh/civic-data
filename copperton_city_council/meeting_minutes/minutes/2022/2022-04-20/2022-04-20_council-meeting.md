Council Business

Collecting Rainwater

               Council Member Stitzer reported on the 2022 rain barrel sale with Utah Rivers
Council. The deadline to request a barrel is April 24th. The normal cost is $83 but if Copperton
registers as a municipality, citizens of Copperton can get a rain barrel at either a rebate or a
discounted price. She does not see any downside to Copperton registering. Barrels are a good
way for users to know how much water they are using.

               Mayor Clayton asked if there would be any liability to Copperton if the Council
were to register.

              Council Member Stitzer stated that when a citizen orders a barrel, it is ordered in
their name, and they are responsible for everything that entails

               Mayor Clayton stated he would put a notice on the Facebook page.

               Council Member Stitzer, seconded by Council Member Severson, moved to
approve registration of the Copperton Metro Township so residents can receive rain barrels at a
discounted rate. The motion passed unanimously.

West General Plan

                Council Member Stitzer stated she, Mayor Clayton, and Mr. Bracken had met
with representatives from Salt Lake County to discuss some “think tank” options. They talked
about the Bonneville Shoreline Trail being realigned and also about the Oquirrh Corridor Blvd
aligning directly through Copperton. They were assured that the precipitation ponds in Rio Tinto
would be moved. They are trying to meet more with the individual municipalities on the West
General Plan. It was a good meeting, and they will be taking it back to the Salt Lake County
planning commission.

                 Mayor Clayton stated the meeting was later in the process than he would have
liked, but he is hopeful that the changes they were able to make will be beneficial in the future.

                                  −−−−−−−−−−−−−−

Zoo Arts and Parks Board

                 Mayor Clayton stated he received an email from the Zoo, Arts, and Parks (ZAP)
board and they would like to know if someone from the Copperton Metro Township Council would
like to sit on the board.

                Council Member Stitzer asked what this would entail, and Mayor Clayton stated
it would involve attending meetings and helping to decide how ZAP funds are disbursed.

                Council Member Bailey stated she would be willing to sit on the board but is
unsure if it would be a conflict of interest with her employer, Salt Lake County Parks & Recreation.
She will check tomorrow and follow up.

               Mayor Clayton stated Council Member Bailey might have to abstain from a few
votes, but he thinks she would be a good match.

               Mr. Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, stated as far as he is concerned, it is not a
conflict with Copperton but Council Member Bailey will have to check with her employer and
should probably also check with legal counsel for that board.

Arborist Park Update

               Council Member Bailey reported on implementation of the parks plan from the
arborist. Work is getting started. Salt Lake County is likely to contract out the work as usual. The
arborist has marked 45 trees that he thinks need to come down. Some of them have black mold,
which will require special precautions. He is putting together a cost quote for tree removal and
wants to make sure Salt Lake County Parks and Recreation does a similar bid that accounts for
the black mold factor. One of the trees affected is particularly iconic in the park and it may fall.

    Council Member Olsen

       Unified Police Department (UPD)
       Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA)

                Council Member Olsen stated Undersheriff Peterson previously noted that the
mobile command unit is outdated and forecasting the possibility of using ARPA funds to address
the issues. Copperton’s percentage is around 2%. The issue is that if one council in UPD doesn’t
agree, the other councils would need to absorb the cost. A UPD 101 training was held, explaining
how UPD operates. Council Member Olsen has personally requested a meeting with Frank
Nakamura with SLVLESA. There will also be a training on March 12th to discuss how UPD
distributes the money once they receive it.

             Mayor Clayton asked if they would be willing to record the training and if the tax
increase made it to the officers’ pockets in the form of pay.

               Council Member Olsen stated they could record the meeting. As for the tax
increase, he has not seen the budget numbers, but he was assured that it did make it to the
officers. The goal was to get UPD into the fourth position in the State, but Cottonwood Heights
ended up beating them. UPD is now fifth. There is a little bit of surplus and fund balance, but Mr.
Nakamura reports that the money in gone.
