Animal Control Ordinance

                Nathan Bracken stated he read the County’s animal control ordinance and
flagged some items that might warrant Council consideration. He asked if the Council wanted to
revise the ordinance to meet Copperton’s needs. The effort to standardize the policy for all areas
served by Animal Control was an effort to make things easier for Animal Control.

               Mayor Clayton stated some things in the standard ordinance do not align with
Copperton, such as the sections on fowl and ducks. He noted the Council could write a resolution
and note its changes to the ordinance. Then it could also adopt this ordinance.

              Mr. Bracken stated the Council could amend this ordinance or come up with
something else. Either way, it needs to be done as an ordinance.

                Council Member Stitzer stated she agreed with creating an ordinance that
actually reflects Copperton. The issues that concern her do not involve emergency action. Animal
Services would have the time to familiarize themselves with Copperton’s ordinance before starting
an investigation.

               Mr. Bracken stated it would not be hard to put together a summary document that
refers them to your unique provisions.

              Mayor Clayton suggested that Council Members start a “track changes” version
of that document and send it to Mr. Bracken to have it coded the same way as other Copperton
ordinances. He will put the matter on the April agenda.

Legislative Update

              Nathan Bracken reviewed the following pertinent legislation from the recently
concluded state legislative session.

     • HB 273 – Rep. V. Peterson - Property Rights Ombudsman Amendments
This bill enacts fines of $250 per day if a city or land use applicant disputes a finding from the
ombudsman’s office. His concern is that this bill weaponizes the property rights ombudsman’s
office by turning it into a quasi-court.

     • HB 35 - Rep. C. Pierucci – Impact Fee Amendments
This bill was introduced because of the Olympia Hills development and requires counties of the
first class to consult with a city or town if they are approving a development in that city or town’s
expansion area. He would like to see it changed to say “municipality” instead of “city or town.”

   • HB 359 - Rep. C.R. Musselman – Municipal Annexation Revisions
This bill is of the most interest to Copperton. It will allow a municipality to annex land that would
be an island or a peninsula if certain factors were satisfied. This could make it easier for
Copperton to annex land and may expand that possibility a little bit more.

   • SB 150 - Sen. W. Harper – Transportation, Governance and Funding Amendments
This bill changes how the percentages of funding to Class B and C roads are allocated. This
would result in less revenue to the MSD. The amount the MSD would lose far exceeds any gains
