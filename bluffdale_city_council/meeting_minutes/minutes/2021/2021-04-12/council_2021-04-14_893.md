        b.     Discussion on Findings of Council Member Aston's Request: Employee
               Survey, Job Descriptions, and Wage/Compensation Comparison, Staff
               Presenter, Bruce Kartchner.

Administrative Services Director, Bruce Kartchner reported that staff continues to compile
together information for descriptions of all jobs within the City. The City has made an effort to
hire people for new positions. There is a 3% unemployment rate in Utah, which creates a tight
market. It is particularly noticeable in certain positions. With the description of the job and pay
range provided and advertised, the City has been unable to attract a sufficient number of applicants.
The potential for employees to leave positions is also high. Mr. Kartchner found that the market
rates for certain positions are high and exceed the City's pay scale. This gives employees no ability
to advance. Another issue was salary compression. The salaries required by new hires is close to
the salary of their supervisors. All of the salaries must then be adjusted to be relative. The salary
structure of the City does not currently represent or address that issue.

It was noted that every position in the City has a job description. It also is assigned a numerical
pay grade ending at 29. Every pay grade has a range with overlap to reflect what happens in the
market. Mr. Kartchner presented the Hour Pay Plan Scale to note that there are two components
to the yearly pay raises consisting of the Consumer Price Index ("CPI") component based on
inflation, and the merit component. The merit component has been around 1%. The CPI amount
is designated in the budget each year and the entire pay grade range will move accordingly for
inflation. It does not take into account specialized inflation for specific positions. The merit
portion moves the worker up within their range.

Via a subscription service, the City can see the pay ranges for specific positions in different cities.
Mr. Kartchner used this and presented it as a report to illustrate averages along the Wasatch Front.

Mayor Timothy asked Mr. Kartchner why he is not focusing on a smaller market area.
Mr. Kartchner explained that the market area for workers includes the entire Wasatch Front. He
also explained that they looked at cities that are comparable in size. Salary ranges were partly
based on the size of the city and the experience City employees have. Mr. Kartchner suggested
that Bluffdale look at these examples for new salary detenninations.

Every position in the City was analyzed and put on a chart with a scale. An example of a pay grade
15 employee was shown. It illustrated that the individual was being paid below the Wasatch Front
average. Certain positions were identified that should receive salary increases to reflect the current
market. Mr. Kartchner explained that although he is the primary point person, he has also
discussed options with department heads. One of the areas of concern for salary compression is
the Fire Department where nearly everyone is being paid the same rate despite some workers
having supervisory responsibilities. Expansion in salary rates was discussed.

Benefits was another item Mr. Kartchner was asked to look at. He explained that they looked at
other municipalities for comparison and did not look at private entities. The City contributes to
the Utah Retirement System ("URS") as most municipalities contribute to a government-type
retirement program. The City also elected to be Federal Insurance Contributions Act ("FICA")
exempt, which means the employees do not have Social Security deducted, however, they also do
not receive benefits such as Social Security Disability ("SSD"). To make up for it, the City offers
long-tenn disability. It also offers a 401k, matching contributions reflective of Social Security.
Certain positions also contribute 7.5% to URS which is comparable to Social Security. All cities
are different in their retirement and disability benefits. Therefore, a straight comparison of
percentages is not feasible.

The City also offers medical insurance which it pays into and a life insurance subsidy. Because
the City switched to high-deductible medical plan years ago, the City now contributes to a Health
Savings Account ("HSA"). These benefits are reflective of similar cities. Some specific positions,
such as the Mayor and City Manager also receive a car allowance. This impacted the overall
percentages in the benefits comparison. Mr. Kartchner used the report displayed to illustrate that
some benefits are a percentage of what the worker is earning, and some are fixed costs. Therefore,
the ratio of benefits varies by worker.

       Amendment Application from the R-1-43 Zone to the R-1-10 Infill-Overlay (I-0)
       Zone on 15.54 Acres, located at Approximately 14563 South Camp Williams Road,
       Sage Homes, LLC, Applicant, Staff Presenter, Ellen Oakman.

Ms. Oakman presented the staff report and displayed an aerial map of the planned subdivision.
She highlighted the four parcels that are part of the subject property. One parcel is in the R-1-43
Zone and three are in the R-1-10 Zone. Ms. Oakman explained that the property zoned R-1-43 has
to be rezoned to R-1-10 and then to infill-overlay (1-0). The developers are proposing a 15.54-
acre subdivision with 23 lots. There is 1-0 zoning nearby. The topography is difficult, which
makes development a challenge. Ms. Oakman explained that Quail Ridge Phase 1 was rezoned in
the past. The three R-1-10 lots are low-density residential land use. The R-1-43 is very low-
density residential land use. The 1-0 zone is not specific with respect to density. The applicant
believes they are fulfilling the purpose of 1-0 Zoning.

The proposal includes modifications and meets all applicable Code standards. The front setbacks
will be 25 feet rather than 30, the side setbacks will be 10 feet rather than 12, and the rear setbacks
will be 25 feet rather than 30. The applicants are proposing a long cul-de-sac that is a maximum
of 1,700 feet long with the only access at the north. They will remove the access to the east on
Canal Road. The Fire Chief previously approved similar lengths exceeding 1,000 feet. The private
road will be serviced by the HOA. There will be an entry gate at the north entrance. A proposed
streetlight design that meets City standards will be paid for and maintained by the HOA according
to conditions imposed by the Planning Commission. However, if the City pays to maintain it, it
will be a City design. The lot plan includes a maximum of 23 lots with minimum 12,000 square-
foot lots. Ms. Oakman presented the concept plan and explained that Lots 9 and 10 will have a
shared driveway because they do not have frontage. The driveway will run alongside Lot 5. The
majority of the lots are larger than one-half acre in size.
