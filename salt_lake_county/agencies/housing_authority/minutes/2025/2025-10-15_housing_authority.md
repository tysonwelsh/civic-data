• Resolution #2025-1136: Housing Choice Mobility Plan:  
o Chief Operating Officer Timmerman presented the Resolution. Housing Connect was awarded $60,000 for

a planning grant from HUD in July, 2024, which was part of a larger initiative attached to additional 
funding. The larger initiative did not come through, but the planning grant did . With the planning grant 
money, we did go through an extensive process to work with residents and determine what they would 
like and what it should look like in the context of our mission, our strategic plan with resident services, 
and the opportunity to give choice and support to residents who choose to move. COO Timmerman noted 
while HUD‘s funding focuses on moving households to opportunity areas, Housing Connect’s focus is on 
empowering households to have a choice in where they live by creating a family preparation plan and 
working with program staff to determine what areas they will be most successful in.  
o COO Timmerman outlined the seven-phase plan, including focus groups on finding out what the residents 
want, developing a mapping tool for participants to use when deciding where to move, and developing a 
full housing mobility plan that can be used should HUD release more funding.

Choice Mobility Plan with none opposed. The motion has passed, and the Housing Choice Mobility Plan is 
approved. 
• Resolution #2025-1137: Housing Choice Voucher Payment Standards (Annual Adjustment):  
o Chief Financial Officer Aniban presented the Resolution. This is a joint effort between Finance and the 
Housing Choice Voucher Program. It’s an annual review we normally perform. We are asking the board to 
increase the payment standards for the studio units to  101% of Fair Market Rent (FMR). The rest of the 
payment standards for the remaining bedroom sizes stay substantially the same but will increase to reflect 
the new Utility Allowance . This would be our recommendation for the new payment standards, which 
would capture the increase in the Utility Allowance.  
 HUD published the Fair- Market rents for 2026, and this triggers the review of the payment 
standards currently in place. There are factors we consider in deciding where we should be on the 
scale. The Payment Standards range from 90% -110% of the FMR for that year. We can not go 
above that, above 110%, as it requires HUD approval, but if we’re within the range of 90%-110%, 
it just needs to be internally approved by the Board of Commissioners. 
 Some of the factors we consider when we analyze the payment standards: First, we look at the 
number of participants currently i n a rent burden situation and this is where participants are 
paying more than forty percent of their income towards rent. We also look at the increase in Fair 
Market Rent from 2025-2026; the number of voucher holders having difficulty finding or renting 
units; projected federal funding for 2026; prevailing rental market conditions; increase or 
decrease in vouchers that could be leas ed up with the change in Payment Standards; and an 
increase in utility cost that might impact total tenant portion.  
 Commissioner Akerlow asked if there is a four percent (4%) decrease, does that also mean that it 
is a four percent (4%) decrease in administrative fees the agency receives? CEO Kimball answered, 
no, as we get a certain amount per unit leased. If we are not able to lease up units because of 
higher payment standards, we have less admin fees coming to Housing Connect.  
 Commissioner Ramos asked, I’m noticing the payment standard went down; how does that affect 
the families currently renting at the higher rate? Does this mean they will be picking up the 
additional cost? CFO Aniban replied, we’re not adjusting the current payment standard, we’re 
keeping it as is (excepting studio sized units), even though the Fair Market Rent decreased. The 
utility allowance also increased to compensate for increases in utility costs. It will help the voucher 
holders, assuming they are at the same current income levels. 
o HCV Director Brady spoke on the Utility Allowance  (UA). We did a study this year and also looked at the 
last five years and did a review. We are proposing to increase the Utility Allowance slightly. But with that, 
we’re proposing to keep our payment standards the same. We didn’t want people to lose  their buying 
power because the utility allowance changed. We are proposing to only increase the payment standards 
the exact amount the Utility Allowance is increasing, and it basically stays the same for our participants. 
o Commissioner Akerlow made the motion to Accept and Approve the Housing Choice Voucher Payment

MONTHLY FINANCIAL REPORT 
• Prior to Chief Financial Officer Aniban’s presentation, Chair Bernal stated the summary of programs is very good 
as it divides everything up well, even by property (or property categories), and it has started making things clearer 
on why this property is treated this way, and that property another way. CFO Aniban thanked Chair Bernal for the 
feedback and stated we’re trying to address all readers of the Monthly Financial report. 
• CFO Aniban, this is eight (8) months of operations as of August 31, 2025 . Our grouping of entities is  presented 
based on accounting standards. We consider ownership, control, fiscal dependency , and when we talk about 
ownership, it’s not percentage per se, it’s also if you’re a managing member or not. We don’t present properties 
we just manage and do not have an ownership in. Our total combined balance sheet (Liabilities and Equity) is 
~$292mm, which is a slight decrease from the previous month, but it is not anything to be concerned about. We 
need to look at this in terms of what programs we should be focusing on as a board, and I would say, overall, we’re 
okay. In terms of liquidity, we are on a one -to-one basis – that’s not the ideal, but it’s reasonable f or an agency 
and a non-profit. That’s a balancing act we need to be mindful of. And we need to be aware of our solvency as 
well. 
For a more in-depth look, please see the monthly Finance Reports included in the October 2025 Board materials.  
There’s no change from last month; our cash balances are strong and there are no significant or material changes.
