DESCRIPTION OF REQUEST
The applicant, Mandy Taft, is requesting a special exception for a retaining wall with a height
exceeding five feet along her rear property line adjacent to Falcon Park. The proposed wall at its
highest point will be 11 feet tall.

BACKGROUND & SITE CONDITIONS
The subject property is elevated well above Falcon Park which borders the property on the
rear. The elevated grade had been retained on the property, but the applicant, not realizing
they needed Planning Commission approval for the new retaining wall, removed the existing
retaining structure prior to obtaining the appropriate approvals creating a potentially
hazardous situation.

The neighboring properties bordering Falcon Park also are at a similar grade as the subject
property and are well above the grade of Falcon Park. The height of the retaining walls are
not as tall as the one requested by the applicant, nor are their backyards level, as the
applicant is proposing to do to her rear yard. The rear yards of the neighboring properties
are sloped, but still useable space.

NOTICE
Notices were mailed to property owners within a 500-foot radius of the subject parcel as per Sandy
City Land Development Code requirements, to notify of the Planning Commission meeting.

ANALYSIS
Sandy City Ordinance limits the height of retaining walls along property lines to a height of five
feet. It also contains a provision allowing the Planning Commission to grant a special exception
for walls exceeding the prescribed height limit. The following is the ordinance relating to this
request:

     Sec. 21-28-8. - Retaining Walls.
           Where a retaining wall protects a cut below or a fill above the natural grade and is
     located on the line separating lots or properties, such retaining wall shall not exceed five feet.
     For cuts/fills to be retained that exceed five feet, retaining walls shall be stepped and
     separated horizontally by a minimum of five feet. The uppermost retaining wall may be
     topped by a fence, wall, or hedge of the height that would otherwise be permitted at the
     location if no retaining wall existed. The Planning Commission may grant a special
     exception to this criteria where it can be shown that this provision would cause an
     unreasonable hardship to the property (e.g., where the stepping would eliminate the
     side or rear yard leaving the property owner with a strip of unusable yard space (less
     than five feet)), or where it may not be practical due to adjoining uses.

The plan provided by the applicant shows a wall 11 feet in height at its tallest point. (See
Exhibit 1 – Retaining Wall Plan)

The applicant has provided a memo detailing how she meets the criteria for a special
exception. (See Exhibit 2 – Applicant Memo to PC)

The home is setback approximately 25 feet from the rear property line, potentially allowing for the
wall to be stepped as prescribed in the above ordinance, and still have useable back yard. (See
Exhibit 3 – Aerial View of Property)

STAFF CONCERNS
The primary reason for the stepping regulation is for aesthetics. The intent of the ordinance is to
break up massive retaining walls with some attractive landscaping in the separation of the walls.
Staff’s primary concern is whether the request meets the criteria stated in the ordinance for the
granting of a special exception.

Another concern regarding this application is that when the previous wall was removed there was
some damage done to the parks property. Parks and Recreation staff is recommending that the
property owner/contractor be responsible for repairs to the park as per Sandy City Standards and
Specifications. This will include grading, sod and irrigation, or any other damage that the
contractor caused to the park.

STAFF RECOMMENDATION
Staff recommends that the Planning Commission determine whether the request outlined by the
applicant adequately meets the criteria for an exception to be granted.

      Should the Commission choose to grant the special exception, staff recommends that it be
      approved subject to the following conditions:

      1. That the applicant complies with each department’s comments and redlines throughout the
         building permit process and that all issues be resolved before the wall can be built.

      2. That the wall is built in strict adherence to the plans provided by the applicant.

      3. That no additional construction shall take place until a proper building permit has been
         obtained.

      4. That the property owner/contractor be responsible for repairs to the park as per Sandy City
         Standards and Specifications. This will include grading, sod and irrigation, or any other
         damage the contractor caused to the park.

              Taft Retaining Wall - Falcon Park - Sandy City, UT


To whom it may concern,

Our house borders Falcon Park in Sandy City, Utah. We originally had a railroad tie retaining
wall that ran along our property line. Recently, our retaining wall gave way and was being held
up by a chain-link fence that ran parallel to the wall. This wall's failure has been caused due to
high-pressure water, from Falcon Park’s sprinkler system, spraying directly into the wall. The
sprinklers were not adequately adjusted and directly impacted the wall with a pressure that
would hurt if you stood in front of it as a person.

We urgently brought in a professional retaining wall company to excavate and replace our wall.
Our wall has been designed and stamped by a professional engineer to show that it is
structurally capable of retaining our yard, and was designed to Sandy City specs. These plans
have been submitted to the city, and were withheld due to the height of the wall. We want to
replace this wall to level our yard back out. Our property is not very large, and to take away five
feet of usable space would be detrimental. Not to mention, we have a deck in our backyard. Per
our contractor with the way the retaining walls are built, and the required use of geo-grid
underground between bricks, would require them to excavate back to the house and would
require them to remove our deck entirely. With the deck removed, we now lose our access to
our back yard from the house and have a door that leads to nowhere.

From my understanding of the code, the issue against anything over 5 feet is related to the idea
of visibility. In our case, we do not hinder the visibility of neighbors, vehicles, or anything of the
sort. This wall is simply to replace and continue to retain our house that sits above the park. The
wall is designed to withstand this height and has been stamped by an engineering firm. We
spoke with Ryan Kump (Sandy City Engineer), and he stated that with the city being our
“neighbors,” in this situation, they would not have any objections against the height of the wall.
Our next-door neighbors are entirely on board, and we have had countless people in the park
telling us how glad they are we finally got rid of the previous wall. Spectators are excited to see
the finished product.



Sincerely,



       Mandy Taft
