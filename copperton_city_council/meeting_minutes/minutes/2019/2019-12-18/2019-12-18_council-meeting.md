Renewable Energy Resolution

               Nathan Bracken stated this is a new program that requires the Council to pass a
resolution by the end of this month. Adopting the resolution does not obligate Copperton to
continue with the program. The Public Service Commission will create the rules for this program
and then Copperton would have to enter into an agreement with a service provider.

               Council Member Pazell stated that although there was a lot of support for this
program, he had concerns about costs incurred if Copperton opts out, and small communities not
having effective votes and voices.

             Loren P. (“Lucky”) Morse, Director, Customer and Community Management,
Rocky Mountain Power, explained the details of the program.

                Council Member Pazell stated he would vote “no” if a resolution was presented
today. However, he would like to work with Rocky Mountain Power so that the opt in programs
are a little more accessible and well known to the community. Right now, there are too many
unknowns.

                Council Member Pazell, seconded by Council Member Bailey, moved to not opt in
at this time but to work with Rocky Mountain Power to make sure the alternative energy solutions
they are working with are available to Copperton residents. The motion passed unanimously.

               Mayor Clayton asked Mr. Morse to provide additional information to the Council.

Franchise Agreements

  Questar, Century Link, Rocky Mountain Power & Comcast

               Nathan Bracken stated before Copperton incorporated as a municipality there
were franchise agreements entered into for the utilities that serviced Copperton. They were made
between the utility companies and the County. Copperton Metro Township now owns the rights
to it. The agreements authorize utilities to use municipal property to provide utility services. He
has revised the agreements to indicate they are with the Metro Township, not the County. The
terms are functionally the same. The Council was fine with the agreements and authorized Mr.
Bracken to send the drafts to the utilities.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Legislative Update

              A cleanup bill for the Metro Townships is being considered for the next legislative
session. One of the restrictions placed on townships is the inability to collect franchise taxes.
There are three options on the table that could change that.

   1. Remove the reference in code that metros cannot have franchise taxes.
   2. Reduce or modify the requirements to become a city or town.
   3. Pass legislation changing how cities and towns are classified to include the Metro
      Townships.

The Council expressed its desire to remain a Metro Township and simply remove the prohibition
on franchise tax.
