PUBLIC COMMENT PERIOD
Jim Vesock commended American Preparatory Academy for its after-school programs. He
reported hearing frequent public concerns about poor road conditions, particularly on 3500
and 7200 West. Jim raised ongoing concerns about inconsistent code enforcement and the
lack of a finalized standard operating procedure. He also noted deteriorating conditions at
the bike park following Westfest cleanup. Finally, he shared his support for the BWF
program after hearing an impassioned County Council speech, emphasizing its inclusive
mission to uplift the community.

Janzelle Tudor shared two meaningful experiences highlighting the impact of West Valley
Arts. She attended the Hunchback of Notre Dame preview with her children and described
a powerful scene where townspeople embraced the outcast Quasimodo, which resonated
deeply with her oldest child, fostering hope and belonging. She also spoke about Moana,
daughter of former Councilmember Jake Fitisemanu, who connected with the actress
playing Esmeralda over shared Brazilian heritage and representation. These moments
inspired both children to pursue their passions, demonstrating the importance of arts in the
community.

  consider public comments regarding Application GP-3-2025, Filed by West Valley
  City, Requesting a General Plan Change to Update the Fairbourne Station Vision.

  Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
  information as follows:

         The current version of this plan was adopted in 2012 and addresses the
         area between 3500 S and Lancer Way (3650 S) and between 2700 W and
         3200 W. This plan was originally called the City Center Vision, which
         was adopted in 2004 in effort to steer development that helps create a
         downtown for the City.

         Staff is proposing this update for several reasons listed below:
             • Much progress has been made in accomplishing the goals stated in
                  the current plan.
             • Reference data such as population and traffic counts were updated.
             • The property between 2700 W and I-215 and between 3500 S and
                  3800 S was added to the plan with a future land use of commercial
                  along the 2700 W frontage and Costco and mixed use on the
                  balance. This area includes the mall, Costco, The Opal
                  Apartments, and the shopping center west of The Opal
                  Apartments. This area was included in the original City Center
                  Vision in 2004; however, it was removed in 2012 because the mall
                  had just seen significant reinvestment. This area is being added
                  back to the plan for two reasons. First, the mall once again needs
                  reinvestment. Second, the State requires that station area plans for
                  the areas surrounding light rail stations be certified by Wasatch
                  Front Regional Council (WFRC) by the end of this year. Staff
                  requested a courtesy review of the current Fairbourne Station
                  Vision by WFRC staff before formally submitting the plan for
                  certification. The feedback received was that the plan should
                  include the mall area. As the Commission may recall, Wasatch
                  Guaranty Development, LLC recently requested a mixed use
                  designation on the mall site, which the Planning Commission
                  approved. This change would have effectively added the mall site
                  to the Fairbourne Station Vision. However, Wasatch has since
                  tabled their application with no indication as to whether they will
                  proceed with the application to City Council. Hence, to meet the
                  State’s certification deadline, staff is now proposing to move
                  forward with the update.

  Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing. There being no one to speak in favor or

D.     ACCEPT PUBLIC INPUT REGARDING APPLICATION ZT-3-2025,
       FILED BY EMANUEL SHAOULIAN, REQUESTING A ZONE TEXT
       AMENDMENT TO SECTION 7-6-301 AND ADD SECTION 7-7-117.5 TO
       PERMIT MEDICAL AND DENTAL OFFICES IN THE M
       (MANUFACTURING) ZONE ON PROPERTIES THAT ARE PARTIALLY
       OR ENTIRELY WITHIN 500 FEET OF A RESIDENTIAL USE OR
       RESIDENTIAL ZONE
       Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
       Meeting scheduled August 12, 2025 in order for the City Council to hear and
       consider public comments regarding Application ZT-3-2025, Filed by Emanuel
       Shaoulian, Requesting a Zone Text Amendment to Section 7-6-301 and Add Section
       7-7-117.5 to Permit Medical and Dental Offices in the M (Manufacturing) Zone on
       Properties that are Partially or Entirely Within 500 Feet of a Residential Use or
       Residential Zone.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

              The applicant owns several office buildings along Decker Lake Drive
              between 2770 South and 3100 South. These buildings are experiencing
              significant vacancy due to changes in the office market. The applicant is
              proposing this change to provide more tenant options for his office
              buildings. Included with this report is supporting information from the
              applicant. For reference, the office market data from CBRE shows an even
              greater office vacancy rate for the West Valley/Lake Park submarket than
              what the applicant is suggesting.

              Prior to the comprehensive update to the Zoning Ordinance that occurred in
              March of 2017, medical and dental offices were allowed in the M zone and
              commercial zones. At the time, staff recommended that medical and dental
              offices be prohibited in the M zone for at least two reasons. First,
              commercial zones tend to be located next to residential zones where medical
              and dental office patrons come from while manufacturing zones tend to be
              located further from residential zones. Second, at the time, staff was
              concerned about the State forcing a permanent homeless shelter in an
MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING AUGUST 12, 2025
                       -10-

         industrial location within the City, which would have significantly impacted
         our police and fire calls for service. While medical and dental offices aren’t
         homeless shelters, shelters do at times have nearby medical services tailored
         to transient populations. Hence, the City was trying to make an industrial
         location less desirable for a homeless shelter in that support services could
         not be located nearby.

         The applicant’s proposed requirement that medical and dental offices in the
         M zone be located within 500 feet of a residential use or zone addresses
         staff’s first reason to prohibit this use in the M zone because medical and
         dental office patrons would be close to new medical and dental offices
         within the M zone. On the second reason, the City is still concerned about
         having a permanent homeless shelter forced on the City. However, in staff’s
         view, the proposed ordinance change is unlikely to increase the chances of
         having a permanent homeless shelter in the City because there are so many
         factors in play besides the proximity of supporting medical services.

  Mayor Lang opened the Public Hearing.

  Chris Rowe discussed challenges with high office vacancies in Decker Lake due
  to tenant bankruptcies and shifts toward remote work, particularly among former
  call center tenants. After exhausting traditional leasing options, the team explored
  opportunities with medical and educational tenants, finding interest in flexible
  medical and light industrial space. Rowe proposed a targeted approach within 500
  feet of residential areas, focusing on community-benefiting uses such as dental and
  orthodontic offices. He emphasized that the change would supplement, not replace,
  the property’s primary use, helping revitalize the site and surrounding area.

  Mayor Lang closed the Public Hearing.
