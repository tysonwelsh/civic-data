Public Comment No comments UDOT TIP Modifications - Bob Allen, MAG Staff SR-68 Village Pkwy. UDOT Region Three requests approval to add $3.0 Million to the SR-68; Village Pkwy. to Grandview in Saratoga Springs Project (PIN 15275). The $3.0M in additional TIF funding is needed for an increase in right of way costs for the project. Appraisals have been done on all the properties and some parcels have doubled or tripled in price from the original estimates. The budget has been updated to reflect the appraisals and the project does not have enough contingency to cover the increase. Mayor Westmoreland moved that the TIP be modified to add $3.0 Million to (PIN 15275). Commissioner Millington seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee March 1, 2018 Page 3 American Fork Main & State Street Intersection UDOT Region Three requests approval to add a new Transportation Solutions Project to the TIP. The new project is the American Fork Main Street & State Street Intersection Area Project (PIN 13669). The project would construct a free right turn for westbound to northbound traffic on US-89, and modify the location of the south leg of intersection to line up with the north leg, which will allow the signal to operate more efficiently. The project value for this project is estimated to be $4.0 Million. The Region has these funds available in their unprogrammed balances. Council Member Baertsch moved that the TIP be modified to add (PIN 13669) American Fork Main Street & State Street Intersection project to the TIP at the project value of $4.0 Million. Commissioner Millington seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. SR-92 Bike Ped Structure Funding Adjustment UDOT Region Three requests approval to add $1.2 Million of Transportation Alternative Program (TAP) funds to the Design for Bike Structure over SR-92 in Lehi Project (PIN 14702). UDOT Region Three has an existing TAP project funded at $120K to help provide the match for the First Mile/Last Mile TIGER grant being managed by UTA. Adding an additional $1.2 Million to this project will help move up the schedule for this project, which will provide a vital link to the trail systems in this area. UDOT Region Three also suggests that with the addition of funds that allow the project to move to construction, that the project name be changed to Bike/Ped Bridge Over SR-92 in Lehi. Mayor Johnson moved that the TIP be modified to add $1.2 Million of Transportation Alternative Program (TAP) funds to the Design for Bike Structure over SR-92 in Lehi Project (PIN 14702). Commissioner Graves seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Federal Safety Performance Measures Letter of Intent - Bob Allen, MAG Staff The Fixing America’s Surface Transportation Act (FAST Act) was enacted in 2015. The FAST Act is the latest federal transportation act that funds transportation and dictates transportation planning policy. A new element of the FAST act is performance measures. As an MPO, MAG is required to report on specific performance measures and the targets set for those measures. Safety is the first set of performance measures that the MPO must either: 1. Set our own targets for the required performance measures, or 2. Agree to the statewide targets set by UDOT. Mountainland MPO Regional Planning Committee March 1, 2018 Page 4 MAG staff recommends that RPC accept the statewide safety targets for the required federal performance measures and authorize the chair to sign a letter of intent to UDOT stating it. Mayor Pray moved that the MPO accept the statewide safety targets for the required federal performance measures and authorize the chair to sign a letter of intent to UDOT stating it. Mayor Brunst seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. I-15 Lehi Tech Corridor Up-Date - Bob Westover, UDOT Beginning April 2018, UDOT will reconstruct the I-15 Technology Corridor between Lehi Main Street and S.R. 92. The scope of the project is to widen I-15 to six lanes to make it consistent with the cross-sections running north and south, and includes the reconstruction of S.R. 92 and 2100 North Interchange, constructing the Triumph Blvd. bridge over I-15, conversion of frontage roads between S.R. 92 and 2100 North, and make significant enhancements and improvements to the bike/ped facilities in the area. The Lehi Tech Corridor is the last section of I-15 in Utah County to be reconstructed. The contract for the project is signed and design details have been finalized. The contractor will begin with construction activity, utility relocation, temporary paving and replace barriers. Contractor will keep four 11’ lanes and two shoulders open in each direction during peak hours to minimize congestion. Early in the project, the contractor will convert the section to five lanes. The Triumph Blvd. bridge will be built on sight and is projected to be completed by the end of this year, immediately relieving some of the congestion on S.R. 92. UDOT’s project website provides detailed project information and updates. Individuals can request updates via email, text, and twitter for specific areas to accomodate their travel needs. http://www.udot.utah.gov/techcorridor/ Provo City Corridor Preservation Application for Funds - Jim Price, MAG Staff Provo City has applied for funds for advance purchase of Right of Way (ROW) along the Lakeview Parkway Corridor. Because the property is not under immediate development pressure, the city cannot use planning and zoning powers to acquire it at this time, and use of Corridor Preservation funds to prevent development is not a consideration. There are concerns because Provo City already has a final design for the road and are in the process of finalizing the 404 permit with the Corps of Engineers including any related environmental issues. By purchasing the entire alignment now, this will set the final road alignment to conform with the completed design and avoid any costly redesign expenses. A request to fund the Provo City Corridor Preservation Application was presented at the February 26, 2018 TAC meeting. MAG staff reported at that TAC meeting that there was $13.2 M available in funds. Unaware that the actual fund amount was approximately $5.7-9 M, it was recommended by TAC to bring it to Regional Planning Committee to fund the $1,809,257 request from Provo City. Utah Legislative Update - Andrew Jackson, MAG Staff SB 136: Transportation governance amendment will change UTA and some of the quarter cent sales tax. At the last RPC meeting, the Committee passed a resolution asking for the legislature to change the 4th quarter cent that’s available from sales tax to .10 transit, .05 county, .10 cities/towns. The amendment allows cities/towns to impose a full .25 of the 4th quarter city by city, town by town if the county has not acted by June 30, 2020 to impose it. If the county does not do it, then the cities can enact it. If the cities/towns adopt it, half of the quarter goes to the municipality and half to UTA. Going to the vote of the public is optional to enact the tax. HC 14 Sub1: Concurrent resolution recognizing Utah’s robust economic growth and urbanization and the need for forward thinking planning and zoning, appropriate infrastructure investment, and a talented workforce in Utah’s metropolitan areas. Proposing to have SLC/Ogden Metropolitan statistical area and Provo/Orem statistical area combine into one. When they competed for Amazon, they didn’t make the top 10 because they had to combine SLC and Ogden. Problem is when Provo and Orem compete for best small community in the country, they would be competing against larger areas, not small areas like they do now. HB 416: Traffic flow amendments allows a vehicle operator to proceed through a red light on a highway with a speed limit of 55 mph or lower during times of extremely low traffic levels when no other vehicles are at or near intersection or no pedestrian or bicyclist is at or attempting to cross the intersection.
