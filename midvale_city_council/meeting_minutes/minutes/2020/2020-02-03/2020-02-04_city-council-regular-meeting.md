schedule to address a minor issue in the rate structure. The third is to add and modify the

Midvale has been using the same Building Permit Fee Schedule for more than a decade.

While the fees have been fixed, we have not included them in the Midvale City municipal

fee schedule which is updated annually as part of the budget process. The municipal fee

schedule currently states “Contact Building Official” rather than containing a table
enumerating the fees. Staff is proposing to add more building permit fee detail into the
municipal fee schedule.

Staff is also proposing to make some minor adjustments to the building permit fee

schedule to address a minor formula issue. The City’s building permit fees are based on

a tiered valuation system. For example, a rate of $14 per $1,000 of valuation is applied

between $100,000 and $125,000. The rate between $125,000 and $150,000 is $10.10

per $1,000 of value. The current schedule shows a small reduction in the fee between
tiers as projects become more expensive, which is not the intent of the fee structure.

Instead there is supposed to be a rate reduction between tiers which is intended to reduce

the fee per dollar of value as a project becomes more expensive, but not reduce the total

amount of the fee between tiers. Staff is proposing to adjust the schedule so that the total
fee amount does not decline between the tiers of valuation.

Staff is also proposing to make modifications to the fee schedule to address costs of
working in the ROW. These include increasing the permit amount from $100 to $250
| dollars and adding a $0.25 per square foot fee for excavation and a $0.50 per linear foot

fee for boring projects. Staff would also like to add a $5,000 bond requirement in the fee
schedule for excavations within the City’s right-of-way. The current bonding requirement
has been included in Title 12 of the Midvale City Municipal Code, rather than the fee
schedule. Staff is proposing to remove the requirement from Title 12 and include it in the
fee schedule where it will be more transparent and will be considered annually as part of
the budget process. The increases in the ROW fees and bonding requirements are
intended to address actual costs to the City. The current fees have not been adjusted in
decades and there for do not account for increases in the cost of labor and materials.

REQUIREMENTS RELATING TO MEDICAL CANNABIS AND RETAIL
SPECIALTY TOBACCO BUSINESS USES IN TITLE 17, ZONING

Alex Murphy said UT HB 3001, adopted in December 2018, and UT SB 1002, adopted in
the 2019 first special session, provides for the licensing and regulation of Cannabis
Production Establishments (including cannabis cultivation facilities, cannabis processing
facilities, and independent cannabis testing facilities) and Medical Cannabis Pharmacies
throughout the state of Utah. Included in these legislative acts are requirements and
limitations imposed on municipalities governing the establishment of cannabis-related :
uses.
Staff approached the City Council in July 2019 seeking direction on how to incorporate
cannabis- related and, because of their similarities in State regulations, specialty tobacco
uses into the City’s Zoning Ordinance. In response to that discussion, Staff prepared a
summary of the proposed land use regulations and sought input from the Planning
Commission in October 2019.
Following those workshop discussions, Staff prepared draft language to consider. The
Planning Commission held a public hearing on the proposed language on January 8,
2020 and forwarded a positive recommendation with minor changes to the Council.
After the Planning Commission public hearing, the City Attorney recommended
modifications to the text to better reflect and relate to the State’s language. The changes
include referencing State definitions and proximity requirements instead of copying them,
which allows for the City’s ordinances to change as the State code changes and removing
certain zones from the retail tobacco specialty business use list due to their inclusion of
residential options. The proposed ordinance accounts for the Planning Commission’s
recommendation with the City Attorney’s most recent recommendations.
The proposed language includes new use categories for cannabis production
establishments, medical cannabis pharmacies, and retail tobacco specialty businesses |

A. DISCUSS VECC 911 ADDRESSING
Matt Hilderman said the Salt Lake Valley Emergency Communications Center (VECC) is
a 9-1-1 police, fire, and medical emergency services dispatch agency located in West
Valley City, Utah and was formed under Utah law in 1988. Per a signed agreement
between this agency and Midvale City, we (the City) are required to establish and maintain
our portion of the VECC road centerlines. A road centerline is a line that represents the
physical center of the roadway between road shoulders and data is attached/attributed to
these road centerlines. If VECC encounters address discrepancies or errors within our
boundary, Midvale is required to review and rectify these issues within a reasonable
amount of time.
On December 23, 2019, data was received from the VECC GIS Coordinator identifying
185-existing addressing points within Midvale City that had discrepancies within the 911-
services database and recorded data from the Salt Lake County Recorder’s Office. After
further review of the data, a total of 179-addressing points was corrected, however; it has
been determined, that to rectify the issue of the remaining six (6) properties, it will require
changes to their long-standing numerical address through an address affidavit procedure.
An example of this situation is illustrated on the next page to help explain the issue.
Discussions with UFA Chief Brad Larson and UPD Chief Randy Thomas concluded
changing the addresses to rectify the discrepancies is important for public safety
purposes. First-responder agencies are discussing plans of moving towards an automatic
vehicle location (AVL) system in the future which is intended to automatically determine
[ and transmit the geographic location of emergency vehicles and emergency responses.
This type of system relies heavily on accurate, systematic numerical addresses.
Staff has agreed it is necessary to change property addresses that are incorrect within
the recognized addressing grid utilized by VECC and the State. In some cases, this will
mean a property owner being required to change the address they have been using for a
long time. We want to explain this issue and discuss an appropriate procedure to resolve
these and future situations, that are likely to arise.
Council Members Quinn Sperry and Dustin Gettel suggested contacting these residents
personally to discuss the reasonings behind changing the addresses.

. GC. DISCUSS CITY RECOGNITION MEMORIAL POLICY
Matt Dahl said Midvale does not have a policy for the creation of monuments or for the
commemorative naming of city assets, and recent requests have highlighted the need to
develop such a policy. Staff researched common practices used in surrounding
communities in an effort to gather sufficient information to facilitate a discussion with the
City Council on this topic. Staff will use the feedback provided by the City Council during
the meeting to begin crafting a memorial and commemorative naming policy for Midvale
City.
Research conducted by staff shows a wide range of policies for memorializing individuals,
groups, or events. While many communities have no policies, several have lengthy
regulations covering a wide range of topics. Based on the policies used in other
communities the following are some issues that need to be considered as part of our
discussion:

e How should a request for a memorial be initiated: Established application process
or case-by-case legislative process

e Whocan request a memorial: Midvale City, Resident, Non-Resident?

e Who or what can be memorialized: Individuals, groups, or events?

e What standards should be used to determine the appropriateness of
memorializing an individual, group, or event: posthumously only, no political or
religious groups, etc.

e What assets can be used for a memorial: Street signs, buildings, park benches,
etc.

e Who will pay for the design, construction, and maintenance of the memorial:
Midvale City or the requesting party?

e What process needs to be followed to evaluate a memorial request: background
check, evidence of accomplishments, public comment period, etc.

The following are some of the common policies that we have seen in our research that
address the issues listed above:

e Memorials require a high level of community support and approval by the City
Council and/or Mayor.

e To avoid confusion, names should not duplicate those of nearby existing
memorials.

e Names should not be derogatory or offensive in long or short form and should not
be associated with tobacco, alcohol, firearms, obscenity, a sexually-oriented
business, a controversial situation, or a convicted felon.

e Several cities restrict names of and references to religious or political

| organizations/leaders unless such people are being recognized solely for their civic
contributions.
