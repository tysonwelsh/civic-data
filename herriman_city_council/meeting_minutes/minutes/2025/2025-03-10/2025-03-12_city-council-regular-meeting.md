         3.2. Discussion and Review of the City’s Position on the Utah Housing Strategic Plan
              – Nathan Cherpeski, City Manager
      City Manager Nathan Cherpeski presented the Utah Housing Strategic Plan for Council
      feedback. He highlighted several concerns with the plan including:
                1. The goal of 150,000 homes by 2028 seemed unrealistic given the current
                   building rates and resource constraints.
                2. Some metrics, such as measuring time from completed development application
                   to building permit issuance, seemed unclear in their purpose and potentially
                   misleading.
                3. The plan requested data collection that might not provide meaningful insights.

      The Council discussed the need to provide individual feedback to the Utah League of Cities
      and Townes and expressed concern about how the data might be used and interpreted in
      relation to legislative actions.

      Mayor Palmer suggested focusing comments on the data collection aspects rather than
      broader policy issues. The Council agreed to submit comments by the March 19th deadline.

          3.3. Discussion regarding options for allowing additional uses in commercial zones –
               Blake Thomas, Community Development Director
      Community Development Director Blake Thomas presented options for allowing additional
      uses in commercial zones, particularly focusing on "micro flex" spaces. These spaces,
      typically around 2,500 square feet, would have served as a middle ground between home
      occupations and larger industrial spaces. He discussed the need for spaces to accommodate
      growing home-based businesses and entrepreneurs, potential locations for these spaces,
      including areas with challenging access or visibility for traditional retail, design
      considerations ensuring aesthetic appeal while maintaining functionality, and the possibility
      of including limited storage options as part of the developments.

      The Council expressed support for the concept but raised concerns about ensuring
      appropriate uses and preventing an overabundance of storage facilities. Director Thomas
      noted he would draft preliminary guidelines for a potential new zoning category, tentatively
March 12, 2025 City Council Meeting Minutes                                            Page 4 of 9


      called C3 Zone, to be discussed at the April 30, 2025 joint meeting with the Planning
      Commission.

          3.4. Discussion pertaining to the 2025 Herriman City Election – Jackie Nostrom,
               Deputy Director of Administrative Services/City Recorder
      City Recorder Jackie Nostrom provided information regarding the upcoming 2025 Herriman
      City election, which would encompass the mayoral race as well as races in Districts 2 and 3.
      She outlined Salt Lake County's projected cost for election services had increased
      significantly from $51,000 in the prior mayoral election to potentially reaching $213,000 for
      2025.

      City Recorder Nostrom noted only $85,000 had been allocated for the election, resulting in
      a budget shortfall. Additionally, the County implemented a $5,000 non-refundable
      administration fee due in July. The methodology for these increased costs was unclear, and
      Recorder Nostrom noted she would solicit additional information. The Council voiced
      concerns about the considerable increase in costs and the lack of transparency in the pricing
      estimates. Councilmember Hodges asked about potentially handling the election internally or
      considering alternative options. Recorder Nostrom noted the election could be conducted
      internally, but not without overcoming challenges. The Council tasked staff with obtaining a
      more detailed cost breakdown from the county, investigating other election options, and
      connecting with other cities to formulate a coordinated response to the county's pricing
      approach.

      11. Discussion and Action Items
          11.1. Consideration for adoption of a Resolution of the City Council of Herriman City,
                Utah authorizing and approving a preliminary official statement, an official
                statement, an official notice of bond sale, and all other related documents required
                in connection with the issuance and sale of not more than $20,000,000 aggregate
                principal amount of Sales & Franchise Tax Revenue Bonds, Series 2025. – Kyle
                Maurer, Director of Finance and Administrative Services
      Finance Director Maurer noted the Council previously approved the sale and issuance of
      Sales and Franchise Tax Revenue Bonds to fund infrastructure projects outlined in Master
      Development Agreements and other reimbursement agreements. The next step before
      proceeding with the bond sale would be to approve several key documents, including the
      Preliminary Official Statement, Official Statement, Official Notice of Bond Sale, and other
      related materials. The Official Statement will be made available to the public and interested
      parties before the bond sale would occur. The preliminary official statement would be sent
      to the investment community on March 25, with a tentative bond sale scheduled for April 10.
      Director Maurer noted that the closing date could be pushed back depending on market
      conditions.

      Director Maurer clarified the funds in the bond would be used for various road projects
      specified in Master Development Agreements, with the largest being 6400 West. City
      Manager Cherpeski added that some of the obligations dated back to 2017 and needed to be
      completed.
