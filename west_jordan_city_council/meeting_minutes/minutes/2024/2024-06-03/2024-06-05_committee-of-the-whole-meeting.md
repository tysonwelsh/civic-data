 2. DISCUSSION TOPICS
       a. Fiscal Year 2025 Tentative Budget Discussion
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck reviewed budget changes for the Utility
          Funds, requesting a full-time facilities inspector and succession training funds due to
          Utilities Engineer Manager Dave Murphy's upcoming retirement. Steck also proposed
          adding a dump truck for the Water Fund to be shared between construction and repair
          crews, costing $95,370. The funding would come from the Water Fund, with the Fleet
          Fund purchasing and amortizing it over seven years. Additionally, she suggested adding
          a vehicle for the Water Fund supervisor, reallocating his current truck to the crew.

          Steck proposed adding a full-time apprentice electrician to the Street Light Fund to
          support a transition from a two-man to a three-man crew. No personnel costs had
          previously been appropriated in this fund. Vice Chair Lamb highlighted the fund's
          growth without new lighting installations due to copper thefts. Steck mentioned that
          reserves had been used for new lights over the last two years, but thefts delayed further
          installations.

          Council Member Green opposed raising the street light fund again, questioning the use
          of last year's increased funds. Steck explained that the funds were redirected to cover
          personnel and theft-related costs. Council Member Shelton noted that copper thefts
          hindered new light installations, emphasizing the need for additional electricians to
          address maintenance and new installations.

          The council discussed vehicle needs and the potential transition to electric vehicles to
          reduce costs and environmental impact. Council Member Whitelock and others debated
          the necessity and logistics of adding vehicles for the Public Works supervisor. Council
          Member Green reiterated frustration over the lack of new streetlights despite increased
          funds, while Council Member Bedore supported last year's fund increase, stating it was
          necessary to cover theft-related expenses and maintain service levels.

          A majority of the council supported funding personnel and material costs with a
          potential rate adjustment to support the Streetlight Fund effectively. The council
          discussed reallocating resources, managing copper theft impacts, and ensuring adequate
          support for city services while being mindful of budget increases.

          Steck next reviewed the Utility Fund fee changes, highlighting that cost increases are
          controlled and passed through from vendors. Council Member Whitelock inquired about
          the city's limited voting power on the sewer board, given its significant financial
          contribution. The current agreement, expiring in 2028, determines voting rights based
          on contributions.

          Assistant City Administrator Paul Jerome mentioned pending legislation that might alter
          board compositions for more equitable representation. He and Utilities Manager Greg
          Davenport plan to engage more actively in board operations. Davenport and Public
          Works Director Brian Clegg stressed the need for a comprehensive capital agreement to
          manage future costs effectively.

          Council Member Whitelock asked about the feasibility of the city having its own sewer
          district. Steck responded that while legislation might explore this, it would be financially
          burdensome for the city. The negotiation of future agreements will be crucial, the city
          will investigate every option they have. It was noted that despite a 6% rate increase by
          Jordan Valley Water, no tax increase is planned for this year.

          Council reviewed the Solid Waste Fund. Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck
          reported that the budget was $125,000 short due to underestimated landfill costs. To
          address this, a 7% fee increase was proposed, with an additional 5.5% increase over the
          next five years to maintain a $1 million balance for emergency cleanup. It was also
          reported that tipping fees are rising from $24 to $26 per ton, impacting overall costs.

          Council Member Kayleen Whitelock inquired about the costs of the dumpster program.
          Clegg explained that conducting dumpster days is cheaper than distributing them across
          neighborhoods. He also mentioned that separating and recycling debris and green waste
          helps offset some costs. Council Member Lamb reminded Council of the free dumpster
          program, though it is more expensive.

          Council Member Green asked why the solid waste fees were being raised by 10%
          without a corresponding increase in dumpster rental rates. Steck suggested a $5
          increase in rental fees. Clegg added that using a compaction machine from ACE has
          reduced the number of loads needed, helping to manage costs. Despite concerns about

          disincentivizing neighborhood clean-up efforts, the council debated balancing fee
          increases to cover rising costs without overly subsidizing the program. Some members
          noted that frequent free dumpster usage by some residents needed addressing.

          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck reviewed the Water Fund, noting a
          concerning decrease in revenue. Chair Jacob suggested creating a revenue stabilization
          fund to serve as a reserve or "rainy day" fund. Utilities Manager Greg Davenport
          discussed monitoring wells and the AMI program, which logs into the system for better
          management.

          The Sewer Fund review included an added line for the SWQ expansion, which started in
          2022 and spans two fiscal years. Capital projects will dip into reserves, and costs for the
          lift at Jordan Valley Water Conservative District have significantly increased. Steck
          outlined revenue, distribution, and treatment costs, with 35% dedicated to the city’s
          system and the rest to the sewer plant. Due to state regulation changes, costs have risen,
          and a bond payment is required. Sewer capital projects total $2,325,532, and the South
          Valley Water Reclamation Facility Capital Plan is $2,750,000. Council Member Green
          questioned the completion delay of a project between 9000 to 9200 S. Steck explained it
          was a master plan project affected by higher UDOT bills. Davenport mentioned
          successful cost containment efforts, although a 10% annual increase over the next ten
          years is needed to maintain the fund.

          The Storm Water Fund review outlined FY2025 revenues and expenses, with reserves
          set for the Barney’s Creek projects. A 7% fee increase is proposed, with a 7.5% increase
          year over year, depending on capital project needs. Storm Water Capital Projects total
          $3,819,280. Davenport indicated that the Barney’s Creek project costs would be
          approximately 10% of the total construction costs.

          The Street Light Fund was briefly reviewed where early that meeting an in-depth
          discussion took place. An electrician position is being added, and personnel are being
          transferred to the street light fund. A 6.75% increase this year and a 6.5% fee increase
          annually for the next five years are anticipated.

          Staff reviewed Parks Capital Projects, proposing bond proceeds to expand multipurpose
          fields. They received a $200,000 grant from Utah Outdoor Recreation, with the city's
          portion coming from impact fees. An interfund transfer of $5.9 million in park impact
          fees was explained, noting that no reserves are held in this fund—they are in the CIP
          fund.

          Various parks bond scenarios were discussed. Council Member Whitelock advocated for
          a 25-year or longer bond term and questioned the omission of Big Bend as a priority.
          Public Services Director Cory Fralick explained that federal grants for rerouting the
          river and park development don’t require matching funds. However, permanent access
          to the park remains a hurdle.

          Council Member Whitelock asked about the financial impact on households. Steck
          explained that the costs would come from CIP reserves and not affect the general fund

          or ongoing operational costs. She outlined the pros and cons of bonding, emphasizing
          the necessity for a road maintenance program and noting that bonding for parks is
          easier than for roads. She also noted that while impact fees could cover 60-100% of the
          annual debt service, the interest costs and debt commitment are significant concerns.

          Council Member Shelton asked about the cost of interest versus savings from avoiding
          inflationary costs. Steck noted a 50% cost increase in a short period, suggesting that a
          larger bond now could save money in the long run. City Administrator Lee discussed the
          process for multipurpose fields, stating that costs are high, and bonding seems more
          practical than using park impact fees.

          Council Member Green raised concerns about replacing the 15-16 fields needed,
          suggesting artificial turf fields as a more efficient option. Vice Chair Lamb noted that not
          all fields are fully utilized, and the focus should be on starting the project. Lee and
          Fralick emphasized the importance of maintaining the fields and keeping the turf
          healthy for various sports. They suggested Ron Wood fields as the best location due to
          lower construction costs and potential collaboration with the County for a recreation
          center.

          Chair Jacob highlighted the need to maximize field usage, while Council Member Green
          emphasized the need for fields for sports other than soccer. Assistant City Administrator
          Jerome mentioned how teams were already trying to prioritize fields, and Fralick noted
          that staff was looking into opening facilities to adult leagues which could help.

          Council Member Shelton inquired about artificial turf, which Fralick would love to do
          although the cost could be $1 million per field but realized they would see long-term
          water and maintenance savings. The council discussed bonding options, with a
          consensus leaning towards bonding for $12 million but trying to spend $10 million,
          using any savings for other park improvements. The majority supported bonding for
          park upgrades.

          Council reviewed Parks Capital Projects, outlining construction costs for multipurpose
          fields, Ron Wood Wheels Park, the cemetery columbarium project, Sycamores Park,
          9000 S entryway landscaping, and the Dog Park, totaling $13,750,000 and the Big Bend
          Project total of $730,620.

          General Capital Projects Fund was reviewed and Steck explained that the General
          Capital Projects Fund holds all reserves, totaling $23,745,058, which can be used
          flexibly. Brian suggested earmarking these reserves for road maintenance. She
          Reviewed maintenance projects totaling $683,000 and construction projects totaling
          $7,975,000. No additional funds were requested for these budgets unless discussed at
          the meeting.

          Chair Jacob asked what more was needed to move forward with the Public Art Program.
          City Administrator Lee discussed the challenges of impactful use of a $25,000 budget
          and proposed forming a committee to select art projects. Chair Jacob advocated for
          increasing the budget, but Council Member Green wanted to see progress before any

          increase. The council supported carrying the fund forward, emphasizing community
          engagement and emotional connections through art.

          It was noted that equipment costs totaled $298,359.

          Council requests from budget discussions were outlined. Meeting Software: $29,490
          ongoing and $18,317 one-time funds were unanimously supported for implementation.
          The funding approach regarding Firefighter Turnout Gear was debated. Council Member
          Shelton preferred phasing the $290K cost over several years. Council Member Green
          and Chair Jacob supported a mix of one-time and phased funding. The majority,
          including Council Members Whitelock, Green, Jacob, and Bloom, favored a full expense
          for a second set of turnout gear, with a rough estimate of $50K per year for ongoing
          costs.

          Council Member Green inquired whether the funds for the entertainment stage were to
          cover the stage itself or the performers. Public Services Director Cory Fralick explained
          that renting the stage would cost $45,000, which would cover AD costs. When asked
          about fitting the stage on a semi-trailer, Mr. Fralick recommended renting rather than
          building a permanent stage. He mentioned that a concrete amphitheater would cost
          approximately $200,000. Council Member Whitelock and Bedore expressed a
          preference for investing in an amphitheater instead. City Administrator Lee reminded
          the board of the existing amphitheater at the Viridian. Council Members Green, Bloom,
          and the Chair also supported the idea of an amphitheater. Administrative Services
          Director Steck suggested adding a CIP project for an amphitheater and removing the
          entertainment stage request, which received majority approval.

          Regarding firefighter turnout gear, there was confusion about phasing the expense.
          Council Members Whitelock, Green, Jacob, and Bloom favored covering the full expense
          for a second set of turnout gear.

          The Council unanimously agreed to fund Council Office meeting software.

          When discussing an additional code enforcement officer, Assistant City Administrator
          Paul Jerome conveyed Code Enforcement Officer Brock Hudson's support for the
          position. Although Mayor Burton was hesitant to hire another officer, Mr. Lee
          emphasized the importance of improving community aesthetics positively. The majority
          of the Council supported adding the officer.


          Council Member Green proposed removing the Healthy City Committee Title and
          increasing the budget for Title 2 committees from $5,000 to $10,000. He noted that only
          two committees had been active recently and opposed the budget increase. Council
          Members Shelton, Bedore, Bloom, and Chair Jacob agreed to keep it at $5,000.

          Mr. Lee explained that the Community Arts Council requested an increase to $75,000,
          which was initially overlooked in the budget. Mr. Lee supported this request and
          provided further details. Council Member Whitelock inquired about fundraising efforts,

          and Mr. Lee read from reports provided. Council Member Bedore supported the arts but
          wanted assurance that the additional $8,000 for a lease was necessary and also
          expressed a desire to sit on their board. The majority of the Council supported the
          additional funds for the Community Arts Council.

          Council Member Kayleen Whitelock expressed the desire to discuss business licensing
          and lot lane changes before the meeting adjourned.

          Ms. Steck reviewed several items still pending discussion within the General Fund,
          Roads Capital Projects Fund, Parks Capital Projects Fund, Sewer Fund, Solid Waste
          Fund, and Streetlight Fund. She outlined revenue to offset expenses, including budgeting
          lights at the rodeo grounds and completing the 9000 South betterment project next
          year. The Fleet Management Fund required one-time money for vehicle lifts for safety
          issues. The IT Management Fund needed $25,000 for maintenance and three computers
          for additional employees. Additionally, there were discussions about events, utilities,
          and an extra $10,000 for a Code Enforcement employee.

          Ms. Steck noted that they would prepare the tentative budget with the changes
          supported by the Council. She acknowledged that while some items could be rejected by
          the Council, it would be challenging to find alternative funding if those items were not
          approved.

          Ms. Steck responding to a request from Chair Jacob agreed to provide a slide showing
          one-time funds and the necessary tax increase to cover them. Vice Chair Lamb
          requested further detail regarding new budget requests and specify whether they were
          requested by the City Council, Mayor, or supplemental requests. Ms. Steck confirmed
          that she would provide that detail.

          A correction for the Consolidated Fee Schedule was needed to correct a cemetery charge
          that was incorrectly listed as $600 instead of $60. A work session was requested for late
          fall to discuss the Western Stampede. Additionally, UDOT indicated that $250,000 would
          be needed for the pedestrian bridge project in Fiscal Year 2026, which would be added
          to the five-year plan.

          It was reported that the Consolidated Fee Schedule discussion would take place on June
          12th and would be led by Budget & Management Analyst Rebecca Condie in Ms. Steck’s
          absence. Chair Zach Jacob asked the council to email any other items of discussion for
          that session and indicated he would be including a roads fee discussion topic.

          Ms. Steck asked if Council Members wanted to allocate extra funds in the CIP to start a
          roads maintenance program exceeding the B&C funds. Mr. Lee commented that while
          more was being invested in roads, maintenance required a long-term approach and
          significant structural changes. Public Works Director Brian Clegg added that as the
          program grows internally and with contracting costs, it would ultimately require more
          staff for a concrete crew and materials for maintenance.
