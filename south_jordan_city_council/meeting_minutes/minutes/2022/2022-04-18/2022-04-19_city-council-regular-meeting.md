Mayor Ramsey opened up the public comment portion of the meeting.

Bob Paxton (Resident) thanked the mayor and council for all they do. He discussed the
paperwork he received with his water bill and said that he doesn’t necessarily like 35 parks,
that’s a lot of parks; when he moved here he thinks there was one park and that was City Park.
There have obviously been a lot of changes but the beauty, value and investment is what caught
his eye. He thinks that for those who have lived in this city for a while, and who have larger
yards, they have been concerned about water for the last few years. He has drastically dropped
the amount of water he uses by 25%-30%. Green is very important, and we know the biological
values of green; there is a lot of that, and we need to consider all of that when we come to this
discussion with cities, counties and state with water. He thinks many here have sizable
investments with the green they have, but we are coming to the point where our state and county
have, he thinks, over utilized the natural resource of water. This isn’t a magic wand where we
can wave it and say we are going to get another 20% of water; our growth has exceeded the
natural resource which we have. He would like to know at some point, and hopefully it can be
published in something like the resident newsletter, the plans that our city, state and county have
for water in conjunction with the growth that we have. He has been told that our culinary water is
7.5% of our total water, our irrigation is 11% and that farmland water is 82%. He doesn’t think
we can continue until we start having some really hard discussions on where water is coming
from as 7.5% for all the growth we are experiencing is not enough water for these people. He is
hoping we can think outside the box, and that we don’t put blame on the individual landowners
that are participating with trying to control water, but still have an investment in that area.

             H.2. Resolution R2022-15, Sagewood Ranch Barn Land Use Amendment.

Mayor Ramsey asked if it would be appropriate to have Director Schaefermeyer present on

issues, Items H.2., H.3., and even Item I.1. as they are all inter-related; you can’t have the
rezone without the land use, and you can’t have that particular zone without a development

specifically, the law allows one public hearing for multiple items that are related, however he
has never seen that in the land use. He asked Director Schaefermeyer if he had ever seen that,

would feel more comfortable having two hearings as that is what they have always done. There
is a specific rule for that under the RDA, but he is not aware of any specifications in the land
use arena so he would suggest having the whole presentation from the planner, information
from the applicant, then opening the public hearing as to the two items knowing that in the
past what typically has happened is that everyone will speak on the first item; the second item

how they would handle these items: They will go from Item H.2. to H.3., then I.1., then vote
on I.1. because the agreement has to be in place before any rezone is done. After that, they
would come back to H.2. and H.3., in that sequence, to vote. Knowing they are going to do
that, he thinks they can open the public hearing with the knowledge that at the end of the one
public hearing another will be opened, but only to accept new comments.

Dear Mayor and Council Members,


In advance of the City Council meeting April 19th, we wanted to share some information
with you about my family's proposal to preserve our parents' barn and create a beautiful
space for the community to gather and celebrate.


Our family is eager to be good neighbors and good community partners. We have lived in
this community for several generations now and this property has become not only a
special place full of memories for our family, but the community at large. People share
stories with us regularly about how much they enjoy the barn and seeing the buffalo and elk
in the field. We want to honor that history and community impact in a meaningful way.


In an effort to be good neighbors and community partners, we have met and communicated
with neighbors and heard the concerns shared in the planning commission. We are working
towards some modifications to help address those concerns. We have also done significant
research to ensure we will not cause negative impacts to our surrounding neighborhoods.
We want to share some of these with you in advance of the meeting to get your feedback.
We appreciate that you know your constituents well and we can benefit from your
experience and insight. After reading this information, we would love to hear your thoughts
and ideas.


First, traffic was brought up several times in the planning commission meeting. We worked
with Wall Consultant Group, the traffic engineers frequently used by South Jordan City, to
do a deeper dive into the traffic impacts. What they found was:
- During the PM peak hour, approximately 154 trips are expected to be generated by the
proposed reception center. **Keep in mind this is not daily, only when events are being
held.
- The daily traffic volumes on 3200 West are approximately 3,945 vehicles. The maximum
desired daily traffic volume is 10,625 vehicles per day. Therefore, 3200 West has
sufficient excess capacity to easily accommodate the proposed project and
associated traffic volumes.
- According to UDOT, the 2019 AADT on 400 South is approximately 22,000 vehicles per
day. Five-lane roads like South Jordan Parkway should be able to accommodate 30,000+
vehicles per day. Therefore, there is sufficient excess capacity to easily accommodate
the
proposed project and associated traffic volumes on South Jordan Parkway.


We also heard concerns about safety. Through a GRAMA request, we obtained information
on all calls for service to the two other reception venues in South Jordan. These venues
share some similar characteristics with our proposed venue, primarily that they also abud
residential property. The response we received from Patrick Black, Records Technician for
South Jordan City was, "After some searching, we were unable to find any calls for service
at either venue last year."


In our initial proposal, we included a wall to address privacy and sound concerns expressed
by a couple neighbors. We were a little taken aback in the planning commission when we
heard others disliked that idea. In an effort to appease neighbors’ concerns of noise and
privacy, we are looking at revising that component and including a precast concrete fence
only along Alexander and the development property line. Along 3200 west, we have
changed the fence to wrought iron which will allow a more open feel to accommodate those
hoping for that. We are open to other ideas and suggestions as this is a very expensive
element and was intended solely as a way to make the neighbors feel more comfortable
with the plan.


We have spent a great deal of time working with city staff to ensure the development
agreement includes solutions that address the concerns we have heard. The agreement
also specifies that the barn cannot change use outside the parameters outlined in the
agreement without going through the planning commission and city council hearing process
again.

their input on our proposal. We have been overwhelmed by the positive responses we have
received. Over 700 South Jordan residents have responded saying that they want to see
the barn preserved and the reception venue to move forward. You will receive a second
email with some of their comments and signatures for you to see.


This project means a lot to our family. This barn has been at the center of so many family
and community memories. We would love to be able to preserve this piece of history for
future generations to enjoy and make their own memories in. If you still have any questions
after reviewing this information or have suggestions on how to improve it, we would love to

forward to seeing you then.

Dear Mayor and City Council,

I was present when Megan Visser, her husband Matt, and a few others presented their idea in the public meeting back
on December 7th, 2021 for the rezoning of the land to create an event center and preserve the Jones barn. There was
no public comment allowed, so I sat quietly and listened and really thought about what transpired.

A few years back, when we, as neighbors, had sat down with the Jones family, the city planning and zoning commission,
and then ultimately with each of you, we mutually came to an understanding that the land would be rezoned from R1.8
to R2.5 with a very specific development agreement in place. This agreement was approved by each of you as the best
development that fit the general plan for the area and met the needs of the city and the surrounding neighborhoods.
This development agreement was built on trust and integrity and as a source to ensure that with the rezoning from R1.8
to R2.5 that the developers of the future, or anyone else for that matter, wouldn't come back and alter that plan. It
included homes to complete the neighborhood and a right turn lane for the city on 3200 W as well as other benefits. It
seemed that all parties walked away from the deal pleased with what had been agreed upon. I even sat, at a future
date, as the only neighbor, in a Planning and Zoning meeting with one of the developers, Mr. Freiss, as final sign offs
were made, splitting this into a two phased development and the ok to move ahead.

However, as I sat in the public meeting, where the Vissers presented their plan, I learned something. I learned that
each of you, minus Councilman McGuire, began to show signs of the crumbling foundation of every community that
leads to distrust and a lack of integrity. I listened as some of your approvals to move forward were based on Pride (it's
about the history of South Jordan and preservation of the barn- which by the way once retrofitted and expanded will be

                                                            1
hard pressed to receive historical status and therefore can be changed at any time), others built your approval on Riches
(tax revenues would be a benefit for years to come from this becoming a commercial endeavor) and added approvals
based on Power (this is a wonderful plan and a need for South Jordan, we have the ability to make this happen... just
make sure you cover/address most of the concerns of the surrounding neighbors, so we can vote yes and point to the
fact that the neighbors' concerns were considered and addressed where possible.) It was almost as if the original
development agreement had never existed. Pride, riches, and power overshadowed the integrity and trust of upholding
an agreement already in place. I naively had gone into that meeting with the belief that our city representatives' word
was their bond and they would stop even the most well intended changes to that agreement from happening. I came
out of that meeting with the knowledge that the term "development agreement" meant nothing and was merely a
stepping stone to whatever the city wanted to really do in the end.

In the end, I will directly be impacted and get to live with whatever you all decide. I am sure I will make the best of
whatever that decision is. That is who I am. It may be all houses as the original development agreement states. It may
be houses and an event center which a new development agreement would state. It may be a gas station or smoke
shop as an even future development agreement may state. I understand that the vote is 100% in your hands and I have
no control over this outcome.

However, what I do control is the future narrative I get to share with any and all those I come in contact with in South
Jordan and the surrounding communities. I would like that message to be that our mayor and council upheld their
original development agreement and should be trusted with future decisions in our community.

Thank you for listening.

indicating that the area will be open green space and have influenced many to believe rezoning is the right thing to
