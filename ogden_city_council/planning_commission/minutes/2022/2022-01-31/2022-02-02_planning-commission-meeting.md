   a. CBD Site Plan, for site and building revision at 457 26th Street. (Attachment 2a)
      Ms. Corbridge reported this is a request to remodel an existing building in the Central Business
      District to allow both retail and office uses. New windows are proposed on the sides of the building,
      new doors on the north, south and east sides and new gray panels added to the façade. In addition,

  existing building material but will also introduce metal panels and stone to better define the entryway.

  indicated ADA parking spaces can be added to meet the City’s regulations.

  the conditional use considerations, stating the addition matches the existing building and will not create
  any negative impacts to surrounding uses. She stated handicap parking is not provided and two ADA

  patterns, but an airspace study will be required at the time building permits are issued. Staff
  recommendation is for approval subject to all necessary permits being obtained, including a septic
  permit from the Weber-Morgan Health Department, and two additional handicapped parking spaces are
  added to the site plan.
  Mr. Lucas Love stated he represents IHC and was available if there are any questions relating to the use
  of the addition. He commented this is the busiest WorkMed facility in their system and its expansion
  will better accommodate employers of the area.

  a benefit to the surrounding community.

4. Request for Amendment, for North Pointe Village Phase 2 PRUD at 801 Century Drive. (Attachment 4)
  Mr. Mike Flood indicated while not the original developer, Nilson Homes is now working on this project.
  He stated Jeff Simon from Peterson Development is available if there are any questions about the
  previous approvals. He stated as construction for Phase 2 was contemplated, it was noted the plans
  called for the relocation of the stream, and its relocation also had been approved by the Army Corps of
  Engineers. This proposal is to retain the stream in its current channel, which forces the relocation of
  two residential units which are proposed to be moved to the north. This also exposes garages to
  Century Drive, and the proposal is to screen their view with additional landscaping. The trail will be
  continued along the streambed. He expressed support of Staff’s recommendations, but suggested the
  relocation of the dumpster proposed would cause difficulty with trucks backing either in or out in order
  to collect trash and felt the current location allows better access.
  Ms. Corbridge stated this project had received final approval in July 2019 and building permits were
  issued in October and construction of Phase 1 has begun. The revisions proposed are for Phase 2,
  which would avoid disturbing the existing Millcreek natural flow path which requires the relocation of
  two buildings which had been proposed to encroach onto the stream channel. The two buildings being
  relocated provided a screen to the rear of one of the townhome buildings, which is now proposed to be
  mitigated by the installation of 14 new coniferous trees. She stated preserving the existing stream
  channel encourages better use of land and creates a better sense of community. She felt natural
  vegetation along the river should be preserved as much as possible.
  She further reviewed the proposed plan, stating buildings face each other or the public street and
  walkways connect to the individual units. However, walkways are missing between buildings and
  leading to open space within the project. A financial guarantee also is required for Phase 2 prior to
  permits being obtained. She stated while most dumpster locations are the same as previously
  approved, one is closer to the street, indicating dumpsters should be to the rear or side of buildings.
  The location proposed by Staff, however is 100’ from the street, but its proposed location does not meet
  Ordinance requirements. Staff recommendation is for approval, subject to the following conditions:
    1. More walkway connections be added between the buildings within the open space.
    2. The landscape cost estimate be updated and a financial guarantee submitted for Phase 2 prior to
        pulling building permits.
    3. The dumpster closest to the street be relocated to the rear of the property next to the additional
        ten (10) parking stalls.
    4. All department staff review comments be satisfied.
  Commissioner Schade expressed concern with the distance garbage trucks would have to back in order
  to collect trash. He commented so long as the dumpster is enclosed, its location is not as critical,
  clarifying it would be required to be enclosed no matter where it is located. Commissioner Blaisdell
  expressed concern with the safety of tenants crossing to get to the dumpster. Commission agreed its
  existing location is preferable due to safety concerns.
  MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to approve the revised plans based on the
                findings the proposed PRUD amendment will be in full compliance with zoning regulations

5. Petition to Vacate, the east 82’ of Franklin Street at approximately 171 E Franklin Street. (Attachment 5)
   Mr. Curtis Groft stated Young Ford of Ogden has purchased properties to the east of their existing car
   lot in an effort to provide better parking for their employees who sometimes now park along the street.
   Their hope is to combine these parcels along with this portion of the Franklin right-of-way and add the
   space to their existing property.
   Mr. Simpson stated this request is to vacate 82’ of the existing right-of-way. He stated the car lot has
   its main access from Wall Avenue and Lincoln remains a single-family neighborhood. He stated this
   petition is tied to the upcoming petition requesting property be rezoned, stating property to the north is
   already zoned commercial. The request would eliminate 5,000 square feet of right-of-way and allow an
   expansion of space for the existing car lot.
   He reviewed both the Jefferson Community Plan and the Wall Avenue Corridor Plan, stating the Wall
   Corridor Plan supports commercial and auto-related uses along Wall Avenue. The Jefferson
   Community Plan and the Zoning Ordinance would require screening and a buffer area to be created to
   protect the existing single-family neighborhood. He stated two of the homes have been vacant for a
   time, while the third is occupied. The plan is to demolish these homes to create more space for vehicle
   storage. He stated without the vacation, the lot at 171 Franklin would become isolated, and indicated if
   the street and the three lots were combined with the existing car lot, it would appear as one project. He
   also stated the vacation would be dependent upon the properties all being rezoned to commercial. Staff
   recommendation is for approval, subject to the parcels being combined with the existing car lot
   property, all properties as well as the vacated street property be approved as commercial zoning and all
   departmental staff comments be satisfied.
   Commissioner Safsten asked if the remaining portion of Franklin would become another accessway to
   the dealership, indicating there is a gated access on 34th Street which should be adequate to service the
   property. Mr. Simpson indicated he has not yet received improvement plans and is unaware of the
   intent of the dealership. Commissioner Schade added he felt there should be no additional vehicular
   access into the adjacent neighborhood from the auto dealership. Mr. Simpson indicated the
   Commission could add a condition of approval that there be no vehicular access from Franklin, but as
   this is a new and used car lot, it is a permitted use in the CP-3 zone and the site plan would not again be
   reviewed by the Commission. Chair Graf expressed concern with the effect of vehicular access on the
   residential community along Lincoln Avenue.
   Mr. Groft responded the dealership desires to have an access at Franklin, indicating it could be gated
   and locked except for emergency access. He stated he has contacted some neighbors and is making
   efforts to upgrade the community as the vacant homes have become a nuisance. He stated a large
   landscaping buffer will be created between the dealership and the homes. He stated access from
   Franklin would be helpful for vehicle haulers and delivery, indicating trucks often park on 35th Street and
   deliver to the rear of the property and could then enter the property to unload. He stated fencing and

6. Public Hearing, Petition to Rezone property from R-1-5 to C-3 property located at 171 Franklin Street.
  (Attachment 6)

  Mr. Curtis Groft stated the vehicle dealership has acquired additional properties to the east and desires
  to expand their lot. While some of the property is zoned commercial, this request is to extend the
  commercial zoning to the south to 35th Street which squares off the property boundaries.
  Mr. Simpson indicated the request is to rezone R-1-5 property to CP-3, including the previous the
  potentially vacated Franklin right-of-way. He stated when reviewing rezonings, the Commission is to
  consider the General Plan and policies. The Wall Avenue Corridor Plan and Jefferson Community Plan
  both recognize Wall Avenue as a commercial corridor, with emphasis on auto related businesses in this

                                                 Page 6 of 12
  area. The Jefferson Plan also indicates commercial uses should not be allowed to extend east to
  Lincoln Avenue. The Ordinance requires buffering and screening between commercial and residential
  uses which is recommended to be required before the dealership is allowed to expand. The applicant
  also will need to complete the Franklin Street vacation and subdivision plat to combine the properties
  with the existing dealership prior to the rezoning becoming effective. The rezoning also should extend
  to include the vacated Franklin Street right-of-way.
  He stated a 10’ landscaped setback and screening fence is required to buffer the use from the
  neighborhood to the east, which should contain coniferous trees to create a noise buffer. He felt the
  expansion would reduce some issues identified by neighbors in the emails received by the Commission.
  He also recommended the completion of the vacation of Franklin Street as requested previously should
  be a condition of approval for the rezoning. He stated the rezoning will allow the existing business to
  continue while providing a buffer between the use and the residential neighborhood to the east. He
  also indicated the Commission could add a condition to restrict access to the dealership from Franklin
  Street in order to be consistent with the previous action. He also clarified thee dealership cannot use
  the vacant property until the street vacation and rezoning has been approved the subdivision process
  completed and recorded, and the required buffering and screening is installed. He noted some emails
  have been received by Staff and the Planning Commission which will be helpful in the establishment of
  the landscape buffer design in order to reduce noise. He also indicated Code Enforcement would be
  notified of the noise problem and keep a watch on the use. Citizens also may report their concerns to
  Code Enforcement. Staff recommends approval of the rezoning as requested, to extend to include the
  Franklin Street right-of-way once it is vacated, subject to the street vacation, subdivision plat and site
  plan process be completed and approved by Staff prior to the business expands onto these lots. An
  updated legal description of the additional property will be required as part of the subdivision and
  rezoning process.
  Chair Graf noted it appears the dealership has expand to the north of Franklin on existing commercial
  lots. He wanted to know whether buffering is required for this area as well. Mr. Simpson responded as
  he reviewed the property lines of the dealership, it appears they may have given the 10’ buffer area to
  the adjacent neighbors to the east, and the landscaping is on the residential property. He stated he has
  found no records of previous approvals for expansions to the east. Commissioner Schade noted there
  are often trucks parked along 35th Street to service the dealership which also contribute to noise in the
  area.
  Ms. Julia Ortega felt the existing home at 171 Franklin should be restored as it is the original family
  home of the Charlesworth family and was built at the time the property was subdivided and made a part
  of Ogden City.
  A QA comment from Jennifer11 expressed concern with the appearance of vehicles in her back yard
  and large trucks next to existing residential fences, stating existing buffering is inadequate.
  Commissioner Blaisdell noted as the residential property at 171 Franklin has already been purchased by
  the dealership, the Commission has no ability or authority to declare it as historic. Commissioner
  Southwick indicated Young had purchased the property from Russo Investments, and it appears the
  previous owner had owned the residential properties for some time.

As there were no additional comments from the public, a motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to
close the public hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Sandau and passed unanimously.

7. Petition to vacate a portion of 13th Street west of Gibson Avenue. (Attachment 7)

  Mr. Brett Barton stated the main entryway into Fresenius is now available at 12 th Street and is
  requesting the vacation of 13th Street in order to provide better security into the site and allow the ability
  to lock down the campus.
  Mr. Barton stated the petition is for the portion of 13th Street between A Avenue and Gibson Avenue and
  the Commission is to consider whether there is good cause for the vacation as proposed and whether
  any other property would be materially injured by the vacation. He stated the main access for deliveries
  is now available on 12th Street, and the request would restrict future access into the site from A Avenue
  via 13th Street. He stated Fresenius intends to construct a cul-de-sac at the terminus of 13th Street and
  the portion not being requested to be vacated would remain open in order to serve existing property
  owners to the east while providing a convenient turn-around. Fresenius owns both sides of 13th Street
  of the proposed street to be vacated and intends to consolidate lots. This area will then be available to
  provide employee parking and a man gate will allow access outside the lot. The purpose to provide
  security for deliveries is a good cause and the expansion of Fresenius is supported by the Gibson Plan.
  No other businesses will be affected by the vacation as access remains available to the properties to
  the east, and Fresenius owns both sides of the property adjacent to the petitioned property. Staff
  recommendation is for approval of the petition, noting as the street was not dedicated as part of any
  subdivision, the property will be required to be purchased by the applicant and utility easements will
  remain in place.

8. Final Approval of the Villages at the Pointe Group Dwelling at 360 West 12th Street. (Attachment 8)
   Mr. Derek Terry stated this request is for preliminary approval for the proposed group dwelling project,
   indicating he has been working with Staff to assure ordinance requirements are satisfied.
   Mr. Brierley stated preliminary approval for this project had occurred in June, and the applicant is now
   requesting final approval. The Commission is to determine whether the issues identified at the
   preliminary stage have been satisfied and to assure all requirements of the Development Code are
   satisfied. The proposal is for the development of Lot 3 in the Pointe Subdivision as a multi-family group
   dwelling project. As the Ordinance requires buildings to either face a public street or open space,
   several buildings have been reoriented to either face the street or the clubhouse and open space.
   Amenities and open space have been relocated to be more accessible to all tenants, with walkways
   provided, and the applicant is working with the City Urban Forester to determine what trees should be
   saved, indicating some are on the adjacent mobile home property and will not be removed. There is an
   existing grove on the northwest corner of the property which will remain and be incorporated into open
   space for the project. He reviewed the proposed building designs and stated brick or stone is required
   on the main floor levels, with brick extending to the roofline in some areas. Front entry doors have been
   added and the entry areas defined with a covered porch. Staff recommendation is for final approval,
   subject to the following conditions:
    1. Details showing ADA parking designs meet standards.
    2. Obtaining SWPPP permits.
    3. Financial guarantees for public improvements and landscaping.
    4. Storm water quality reports and additional details on detention.
    5. Water flow tests.
    6. Additional amenities within the common area.
    7. Five-foot wide park strips adjacent to the street.
    8. Preservation or planting of sufficient number of trees to meet City standards.
    9. Easement to Century Drive for a second access
    Commissioner Schade asked about the Century Drive right-of-way and whether the developer owns
    the property. Mr. Brierley answered while it is under a separate ownership, the developer is working to
    obtain an easement for access. Mr. Terry stated they have agreed to purchase this property.
    Commissioner Schade noted the elevation shown does not include brick for the entire ground floor as
    required by Ordinance. Mr. Brierley answered these areas are small and are recessed, but the
    Commission has the ability to require brick if they desire. Commissioner Schade then asked about the
    requirement for commercial development when a property exceeds a certain size and as there is no
    commercial proposed, he questioned how this is being accomplished. Mr. Brierley answered the
    development project includes the three lots, and Lot #1 along 12th Street is proposed as commercial as
    a later phase which would be 25% of the overall project. Commissioner Schade noted once
    subdivided, he understood each lot would need to meet this regulation and expressed concern the later
    phase may be done by a different developer and it would be difficult to enforce the requirement. Mr.
    Brierley stated the subdivision is recorded is to be recorded with a deed restriction which would require
    commercial development on Lot 1. He stated while typically all phases would be accomplished at the
    same time, but as the Century Drive right-of-way will likely be affected by future development proposed
    by UDOT along 12th Street, As the exact alignment is now unknown, it is difficult to require the
