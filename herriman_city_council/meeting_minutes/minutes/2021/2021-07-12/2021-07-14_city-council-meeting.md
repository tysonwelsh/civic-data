     3.4. Discussion pertaining to City water conservation efforts - Justun Edwards, Public Works
          Director and Anthony Teuscher, Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events
Public Works Director Justun Edwards offered information about the communications with relation to the water
restrictions. This included the prohibition of water utilization between 10:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m. He explained
staff did reach out to high water users to help mitigate excessive water use and help determine if a water leak
was present. Director Edwards presented additional public outreach education and information methods being
utilized. This included newsletters, weekly social media messages, kiosk signs, a podcast, and other methods.

Director Edwards presented the City-wide water usage comparison between 2020 and 2021, and he highlighted
the pretty substantial reduction. Director Edwards and Council discussed weather changes between the two
years as well. Director Edwards talked about the 17-percent reduction seen in residential, with the inclusion of
new growth. There was also a significant school reduction of 15-percent for June.

Deputy Director of Parks, Recreation and Events Anthony Teuscher presented the best management practices
put into place for City parks. He relayed staff performed weekly visual checks of all irrigated areas, performed
monthly audits to ensure distribution uniformity and to mitigate excessive overspray, reduced irrigation in
passive recreation areas to no more than three times per week, reduced irrigation on sports fields and cemetery
to no more than five times per week, turf would be mowed no shorter than three-inches on sports fields and
3.5-inches on passive recreation areas, replaced malfunctioning controllers with new, had more water efficient
controllers, ensured all flow sensors are operating properly so the flow would be shut off in the case of a broken
system, and soil improvement methods would be used to amend existing soils and allow for better water
penetration. He stated that aeration, top dressing and fertilization and dethatching were examples of soil
improvement methods. A cycle and soak irrigation method would be used for better water absorption.

Deputy Director Teuscher presented a map outlining what park and open space had been considered a high or
low watering priority. Mayor Watts recommended implementation of a watering schedule to water every three
days rather than three times a week for the landscaping at City Hall. The Council iterated the saying that yellow
was the new green, and further discussed areas of concerns with watering. The consensus was that sports fields
and the cemetery should continue to be watered consistently, however for all other areas they simply wanted to
water enough to ensure that it did not die. It was also noted that wear and tear of the different areas needed to
be considered as well, as areas such as parks received significantly more traffic. Deputy Director Teuscher
presented a comparison of water usage between 2020 and 2021.

of trails owned by the City but not used. City Manager Cherpeski explained the City did not have a process to
deal with this type of issue. Staff looked for guidance relating to developing a policy for future requests.

Councilmember Henderson relayed his position as this had become a kind of common construction practice,
and the City’s two options were to either let it happen or to actually enforce code and make them move it. He
further noted the situation was not the same in unintentional situations. Mayor Watts was supportive of
enforcement. He noted the issue was not necessarily with the specific request by the resident, but rather it was
whether or not the City would allow them to make that request. Mayor Watts opined a general rule he did not
see a reason for the City to give up right-of-way. He had concerns with the creation of a policy that allowed
requests to vacate right of ways, as that would never be his personal intent.

to see them split the two issues out, and he was happy to consider this specific issue because they already had a
request for this specific issue. However, he requested it come back as a separate item in regard to a general
policy. He further said he wanted to make a policy that they did not look at the removal of right of way or other
City assets.

    9.4. Southwest Salt Lake County Transportation and Solutions Development Study Analysis -
         Megan Townsend, Community and Economic Development Director for Wasatch Front
         Regional Council
Wasatch Front Regional Council Community and Economic Development Director Megan Townsend
presented on the Southwest Salt Lake County transportation project team which included representatives from
Bluffdale, Copperton, Herriman, Riverton, South Jordan, and West Jordan, Salt Lake County, Utah Transit
Authority, Utah Department of Transportation, and the Wasatch Front Regional Council to analyze
transportation needs. The process involved close collaboration to identify transportation solutions to match
current and future needs.

Director Townsend briefly outlined the history of this effort. She explained how it started with the Southwest
Salt Lake County Vision, and through they realized the transportation issue was big enough that it needed to be
highlighted and studied independently. She noted it was an expedited process that kicked off in January, and
outlined how the process unfolded up to the current point. She explained they had developed three scenarios:
a connectivity focus, a freeway focus, and a transit focus. She said this all led to the development of their
preferred scenario. The result of the process would be considered into the TRP to offer recommendations
eligible for funding.

Director Townsend turned the time over to Fehr and Peers Transportation Engineer Natalia Brown who
presented on the preferred scenario. Engineer Brown reviewed how they started with the original transportation
plan (RTP), then layered over the projects for the local transportation master plans for the participating cities,
and finally layered the new ideas. She noted there was a lot of demand for some sort of freeway type facility to
connect the Mountainview Corridor, and stated the estimated cost for the area they chose was roughly $800M.
She expressed the project would substantially impact the community and budget.

Engineer Brown explained what the freeway facility would do in that area, and discussed what it meant in terms
of the metrics they had looked at. She further explained how they followed the same process for the transit
portion as well, and talked about the various factors which were tested and considered in the creation of the
preferred scenario. With the aid of the presentation, she reviewed which lines denoted what aspect on the
provided visuals. Engineer Brown talked about active transportation. She said it was different from roadway
and transit because they did not have specific metrics for active transportation, other than how much more
households could be served by the facilities. She explained the facilities talked about in regard to active
transportation mainly referred to bike facilities.

Engineer Brown communicated they had also looked a little bit at land use, although it was not the focus of the
study. She presented the access to opportunity or jobs that would occur because of two factors: the additional
July 14, 2021 – City Council Minutes                                             Page 12 of 20
workplaces and households would be attracted to the study area, and the increased performance level of the
transportation system. Both factors improved in the preferred scenario due to the proposed additional roadway,
transit, and active transportation project investments. She summarized it took into effect both where the jobs
were and how hard they were to get to. A brief discussion ensued about the cost to get to jobs in relation to
how far out people lived.

Director Townsend offered the recommendations of the transportation and solutions development. First was
to plan and prepare for the transportation systems, and how and where the growth occurred. Second was
concepts not yet in the regional transportation plan, they should conduct feasibility studies to validate the need.
Councilmember Henderson relayed the importance of the need to have a freeway on the west side of the valley.
The flex lane would be the best option available once it was in the plans and received the appropriate funding.
There was further brief discussion amongst the Council and Director Townsend about the project and the

scenario was in place for roughly 2050. Director Townsend commended Herriman City for their regional
collaboration in these efforts.

    11.1. Discussion and consideration of a resolution authorizing the issuance and sale of not more
          than $11,500,000 aggregate principal amount of sales and franchise tax revenue bonds; and
          related matters - Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Alan Rae explained that during the legislative session, Herriman City had been awarded $800K
per year for the next 15-years. He further explained a proposal was made to bond for that amount for 15 years,
which would amount to about an $11M bond with a payment of $800K. He stated the consideration was a
parameters resolution to would allow staff to begin the process of moving forward with the bond. He outlined
that the parameters resolution set the outward ranges of the parameter, and this particular one allowed for up to
a 5-percent interest rate (which was currently at 1.4-percent). It would allow up to 16 years, but it would only
be expected to be a 15 year.

          Attorney
City Attorney Andrizzi presented a zoning text amendment to adopt language into Title 10 of the Herriman City
Code regarding Large Project Master Planned Communities where the Planning Commission recommended
approval. City Attorney Andrizzi briefly reviewed the zones, and the previous discussion that had been had
about why the tool made sense in Herriman City.

City Attorney Andrizzi offered a scenario for the application process if a resident requested to utilize this land
use tool. Councilmember Ohrn asked why the process was to have the developer bring a master development
agreement rather than have the City provide the base standard for the said agreement. City Attorney Andrizzi
explained it was just part of the proposed procedure. Councilmember Ohrn asked if it would make more sense
for the City to write the MDA. Mayor Watts questioned if that would result in the City having to do work for
things that would never happen. There was a brief discussion among Council about how it may be smart to
provide a base MDA to show what the City viewed as acceptable.

The consensus among Council was that they wanted there to be a template MDA or a list of things that must
be included. Councilmember Smith agreed that they needed to find a way to ensure that the protections the
City wanted would be included in an MDA, however he expressed that they needed to make sure those
protections were standard.
