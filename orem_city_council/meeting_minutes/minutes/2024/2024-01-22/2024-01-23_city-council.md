MGT Study Updatesd for Police and Finance Presenter: Keri Rugg, Management Services

Director; Carson Hardy, Management Analyst; Bruce Cowan, MGT Consulting; and Jerry Wolf,

MGT Consulting

Mr. Hardy introduced the MGT Consultants attending on zoom, Mr. Cowan and Mr.
Wolf. He explained that the City Council will be hearing about the MGT report in smaller
chunks. The full report will be published for the public when it is completed. Today’s
presentation is on the Police Department and the Purchasing Division in the Finance Department.

Mr. Cowan explained that the purpose of this report is to see if the City is running at its
highest efficiency. He continued to say that his report didn’t find any serious problems within the
City, instead he has found some opportunities to address the levels of service the City provides.
He began the report by explaining the definition of efficiency, which is to deliver the same

service at a lower cost, to deliver the same cost but faster, to spend a little more but get a lot
more, and to spend much less but without as much impact on services.

Mr. Cowan described the things that the City asked. First, to serve the public with
transparency and accountability and to review most of the departments of City government.
Second, the review was limited to specific questions posed by the City with input from both City
management and the departments. Finally, engage in discussion with City staff about findings
and implications, resulting in refinement of the results. The departments that were studied were
311 Customer Service, Development Services, Facilities, Finance (Procurement), Fire,
Information Technology, Justice Court, Legal Services, Library, Management Services, Police,
Public Works, Recreation and Senior Center. Mr. Hardy explained that MGT met with each of
the department heads and they learned about objectives and priorities.

Mr. Cowan explained that the police department had two questions: should the Police
offer 24/7 lobby service and is the supervisory structure appropriate? As to the lobby service
issue, there is currently an emergency assistance button outside of the Police Headquarters
building, but lobby service and non-emergency matters happen during day shift only. The report
concluded that the cost would be relatively the same because the day shift employees would be
assigned to night shifts and would not require the addition of more staff members. The report
also found that the demand for this type of 24 hour service has no precedence in this area. West
Jordan is the only city who has a service close to this and it is run similar to how Orem is
currently running it. West Jordan was questioned and they reported that their after hours button is
rarely used. The findings were that a 24/7 lobby would not be hugely beneficial and the appeal of
night time work for staff would be ideal. They do not recommend the 24/7 lobby.

Mr. Cowan moved on to the question of supervisory structure? Is it fitting to have a
Deputy Chief? And, are the use of supervisory titles appropriate and are the spans of control or
staff per supervisor suitable? The report applauded the City’s decision to have a Deputy Chief
and believed that a clearly designated second in command for the department is a good idea. The
report found that the supervisory titles that the police department are using are industry standard
and common in the area. They found that the structure within the department matches cities in
the area. The one area that raised some questions was on the span of control. The report found
that the staff to supervisor ratios are consistent with peer practice, except in the Investigation
Department where supervisors oversee fewer people than other Orem police divisions and their
peers. This is not necessarily wrong, but a formal policy is recommended.

Mr. Bybee asked about the review that was done of the Citygate Master Plan Study. Mr.
Cowan believes that is an outstanding report with a particular expertise in understanding the
communication and technology that runs the department. Mr. Bybee asked Mr. Cowan to explain
how that helped to create the questions that were studied. Mr. Cowan said that when he looks at
efficiency of different types of municipal departments, some of them have workloads that are
directly proportional to the population served. Police is not one of those organizations. Police is a
coverage strategy.

Mr. Hardy explained that finance is an internal department and feedback for them was

Impact Fee Study Update Presenter: Brandon Nelson, Finance Director and Fred Philpot, LRB

Mr. Nelson introduced Mr. Philpot to speak about the impact fees. Mr. Philpot is with
LRB Public Finance Advisors and has come to explain impact fees. He explained that before
imposing an impact fee, each local political subdivision or private entity shall prepare an Impact
Fee Facilities Plan (IFFP) and an Impact Fee Analysis (FA). There is a specific process that
needs to be followed, including noticing, presentation, public hearing and approval. The last
time the City updated the impact fees was in 2018. There are six steps in crafting a working IFFP
and IFA. These steps are 1) determine demand, 2) provide inventory of existing facilities, 3)
establish existing and future level of service, 4) identify existing and future capital facilities
necessary to serve new growth, 5) consider all revenue resources to finance system
improvement, and 6) conduct proportionate share analysis.

Mtr. Philpot explained that when looking at the demand, it is important to define a specific
demand relative to a service area. The analysis should identify existing levels of service (LOS)

and identify proposed LOS. The proposed LOS cannot exceed the existing LOS. New
development can only be charged for the proportionate cost of the new facilities that maintain the
LOS. Mr. Philpot stated that when looking at the existing facilities there needs to be an inventory
on existing facilities based on original value, type and useful life. This helps identify excess
capacity. Once that is determined, then we can determine the facilities necessary to serve new
developments and process the impact fee facilities plan. The City will need to develop a Capital
Improvement Plan. The financial strategy portion will consider all revenue sources, it will
illustrate existing and future public facilities funding mechanisms and the purpose is to show that
impact fees are necessary to achieve equity. Mr. Bybee explained that right after impact fees are
completed then the whole process starts over. Mr. Philpot confirmed that this process takes time.

Mr. Phipot continued into the proportionate share analysis summary. He spoke about how
the calculation of impact fees is determined by demand. Each city's demand looks different, in
Orem it may look like redevelopment where there is existing development. The impact fees
under review are: water, water supply and water re-use, wastewater, storm water, roadways,
parks & recreation and public safety. Mr. Philpot will present the results in a future City Council
work session. He concluded with the impact fee noticing.

living environment, and expanding economic opportunities, principally for persons of low or

makes sure they are compliant and then it goes to our commission to decide where the funding
goes. Ms. Delora assured the council that they check carefully that Orem funds are being used

for Orem residents.

Ms. Delora explained that along with the non-profit organization, there are Orem City
services funded every year, including victim services, homeless services, neighborhood
revitalization and code enforcement. The anticipated funding will be $625,000 for FY24. In
April, the CDBG Commission will give a presentation and funding recommendations to Orem
City Council.

Victims’ Advocate Division Presenter: Josh Adams, Police Chief

Mr. Adams is here to speak about the increase in domestic violence. He introduced Sgt.
Glenn Giles and Lt. Karalee Tracy. They began with a domestic violence call that came into the
911 dispatch. This happened in 2021 in Orem. They spoke about a 31 year old female who was
killed in Orem by domestic violence. They continued with the domestic violence calls that they
received just this week. There have been 8 fatalities from domestic violence in Orem from
2014-2024. There was a spike in domestic violence during Covid and now there is another spike.
Sgt Giles explained that cases are then given to victims' advocates. The victims advocate division
spends on average 10 hours per victim and in 2023, they had 570 new domestic victims.

In July of 2023 the State of Utah began requiring that each victim of domestic violence in
an intimate relationship be evaluated by the case officer. Assessments must be performed
immediately upon response and reported to the state if the victim is at high risk. Immediate
services must be offered to any victim of domestic violence that is high risk. These cases carry
great liability with them if not handled properly by the Police Department. Sgt. Giles says that
pecialized training is needed for Orem police officers.

Sgt. Giles introduced the members of the victim advocates team, which include 3
advocates and Hoku the new victims advocate service dog. Each advocate averages over 100
cases per year. The average for other agencies comparable to our size is just over 50 cases per
year per advocate. Victim Services Statistics for the past 7 years are: 2017 - 287 victims were
given resources, 2018 - 345, 2019 - 334, 2020 - 422, 2021 - 495, 2022 - 491 and 2023 - 570. The
Victim Service Team has been getting VOCA Grant for many years. This grant originally started
at over $160,000 per year, but over the years this grant money has been cut. In 2023, the City of
Orem was awarded $56,000. This grant is labor intensive and takes up about 50% of one
advocate’s time. This grant may not be worth the effort. They have found that the CDBG funds
are easier to maintain.

Chief Adams expressed to the City Council that this area is of great concern and priority
for him. He proposed the need for a Domestic Violence Investigator. Currently, the department
has to take officers from the Community Outreach Programs to fill the need. He believes they
