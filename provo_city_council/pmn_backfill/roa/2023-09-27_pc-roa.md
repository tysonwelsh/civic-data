The text of the proposed amendment is attached as Exhibit A.
STAFF PRESENTATION
The Staff Report to the Planning Commission provides details of the facts of the case and the Staff's analysis, conclusions,
and recommendations.
NEIGHBORHOOD MEETING DATE
 • Citywide application; all Neighborhood Chairs received notification.

NEIGHBORHOOD AND PUBLIC COMMENT
 • This item was Citywide or affected multiple neighborhoods.
 • One member of the public commented. They expressed the opinion that the proposed amendment seemed overly
    broad. They have neighbors with artificial turf and have not seen a problem.
PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
 • Jeff Whitlock thought the proposed amendment was overly broad and infringed on personal property rights.
 • Lisa Jensen suggested that artificial turf should be permitted to make up the up to 60% of non-vegetative coverage
     permitted in landscaping. She also expressed the concern about the imbalance that the proposed amendment permits
     artificial turf in large athletic fields but prohibits it in small residential patches, which would be less environmentally
     harmful than the field. Would prohibiting the small patches be enough to make a measurable difference?
 • Raleen Wahlin said that her home has artificial turf. They have not had a problem with heat. The only problem is
     small pieces of bushes get stuck into the artificial turf.
 • Melissa Kendall agreed with Lisa Jensen. She also expressed that artificial turf could be a good solution for places
     where other plants could not grow.
 • Andrew South said that he had done some research and thought that artificial turf was environmentally harmful,
     despite the aesthetic benefits. He expressed that if the City has tried it for four years and it has not been beneficial,
     then perhaps the prohibition should be considered.
 • Barbie DeSoto agreed with Andrew South and added that she agrees with the proposed amendment because of the
     negative effects of artificial turf on native flora and fauna.
                                                          Page 1 of 3
 •   Jeff Whitlock said that some new versions of turf are marketed as non-toxic, and the technology is catching up with
     the problem. The largest concern is those who have old artificial turf, not those installing new, better versions.
 •   Dan Gonzales agreed with Lisa Jensen and wondered what other alternatives could be considered. They both agreed
     with removing artificial turf from (6)(a) which counted it toward vegetative coverage.
 •   Jonathon Hill suggested limiting it further if it were permitted, maybe to 40% coverage.
 •   Jeff Whitlock suggested adding restrictions like prohibiting low-quality types with greater pollution shedding and
     requiring certain installation standards for drainage, which are currently in code.
 •   Dan Gonzales asked staff to address installation and enforcement. The responses are summarized below:
     • Despite the installation standards in code, most artificial turf has not been installed correctly. The City does not
          require a permit for installation and staff are not trained in how to recognize incorrect installations, so there is
          no means of enforcing the standards.
     • Development Services, Public Works, Parks and Recreation, and Energy unanimously recommended the
          amendment. When asked about other alternatives or compromises, the departments’ recommendation was not to
          consider other alternatives and to simply prohibit it.
     • Public Works said that it had not had the desired effect on water use. The Stormwater Division also expressed
          concern about microplastics and the lack of permeability in incorrectly installed turf.
     • Parks’ primary concern was the heat and how that affected people and animals who used the artificial turf.
     • Energy, especially the Forestry Division, had seen increased tree damage and death due to the heat islands,
          especially in parkstrips. They shared Public Works’ concern about the lack of permeability.
 •   Lisa Jensen suggested that the Planning Commission request staff to return with a new proposal that treated artificial
     turf like other non-vegetative ground covers and found a middle ground between the benefits of artificial turf and its
     negative environmental impacts.
 •   Jonathon Hill requested more data about recorded negative effects on tree health within Provo. He also suggested
     reconsidering the allowance for athletic fields.
 •   Jeff Whitlock and Dan Gonzales noted that artificial turf has been used well on rooftop gardens and dining patios,
     and so staff should also consider that in the revision.
 •   Lisa Jensen and Dan Gonzales asked staff to revise the proposed amendment and consider the following:
     • Addressing artificial turf like other non-vegetative ground coverage and considering a percent limit,
     • Installation standards,
     • And a provision for areas that would not allow for other plants.
 •   Jonathon Hill also asked for more information from the four departments recommending the prohibition, especially
     if they agree that allowing artificial turf in small areas would still create problems.

15.20.060 Design Standards.

…

(6) Xeric landscaping and water wise landscaping is encouraged in all yards if the landscaping plan for yards
adjacent to a street meets the following criteria:

       (a) Living vegetation including turf grass, shrubs, perennials, or ornamental grasses or artificial turf
       grass shall cover a minimum of forty percent (40%) of the unbuilt surface area within three (3) years of
       planting. Tree canopy and weeds may not count toward this percentage;

       (b) The remaining unbuilt area not covered by living vegetation or artificial turf grass shall be covered
       by bark or wood mulch, decorative rock, hardscape or any combination of these materials. Living plants
       should be grouped aesthetically throughout the yard;

       (c) Living plants in the parkway area shall not be included in the minimum coverage requirement.
       Landscaping requirements for parkways shall be followed as defined in Section 15.20.120, Provo City
       Code.

       (d) Artificial turf grass is prohibited except in athletic fields and public recreation spaces. may be used
       in landscaping so long as it meets the following requirements:

              (i) Artificial turf grass in yards adjacent to a street must have a blade length between two (2)
              inches and three (3) inches, have a natural green color, and be water permeable;

              (ii) Artificial turf grass must be installed on top of a minimum of three (3) inches of sand, rocks,
              gravel, or similar product for drainage. Rocks, gravel or similar products shall range in size from
              three-eighths (3/8) inch to three-fourths (3/4) inch;

              (iii) Artificial turf grass used for landscaping may not include rubber infill in a yard adjacent to a
              public street;

              (iv) Outdoor green carpet shall not be used in landscaping;

              (v) Upon request, specifications for installed artificial turf grass shall be provided to the
              Development Services Department.

       (e) Outdoor carpet shall not be used in landscaping.

14.23.090 – Building Height.

The total maximum building height within the ITOD zone shall not exceed one hundred (100) feet
according to the following:

(1) Building height shall be measured in number of stories, excluding basement areas;

(2) Each building shall not have less than two (2) stories. The fifth story and each story above it shall
have an additional setback of no less than fifteen (15) feet from the first story building elevation
adjacent to any street;

(3) Each story within the ITOD zone shall not exceed fourteen (14) feet except for first story
commercial functions may be up to twenty feet (20);

(4) Parapet walls and cornices shall not exceed five (5) feet, except as otherwise provided in Section
14.34.090, Provo City Code;

(5) Residential main floor level shall not exceed three (3) feet in height above street curb elevation;

(6) Commercial main floor level shall not exceed one (1) foot in height above street curb elevation; and

(7) Mechanical equipment shall be screened. Only the minimal amount of screening necessary to fully
screen such equipment shall be used. Mechanical equipment shall be setback in such manner that the
required screening cannot be seen from the pedestrian street view, if possible.

 CONCERNS RAISED BY PUBLIC
 Any comments received prior to completion of the Staff Report are addressed in the Staff Report to the Planning
 Commission. Key issues raised in written comments received subsequent to the Staff Report or public comment during
 the public hearing included the following:
     • Barry Finch expressed concerns with the proposal, namely security and hours of operation.
     • Judy Finch shared concerns with the proposal, specifically hours of operation, security, and distance requirements
         that could make businesses difficult to succeed.
     • Scott Christofferson has concerns about what types of events would be permitted in the overlay zone.

APPLICANT RESPONSE
Key points addressed in the applicant's presentation to the Planning Commission included the following:
   • Staff expressed support for the proposed language that would mitigate the concerns that would come with event
       centers in agricultural and residential areas, and codes that could be administered by staff in a reasonable manner.
   • Staff agreed to research distance requirements for events adjacent to animals.
   • Staff stated the importance of security on uses granted through a zone change, which is different from a
       conditional use permit that could be revoked.

PLANNING COMMISSION DISCUSSION
Key points discussed by the Planning Commission included the following:
   • The Commission asked for clarification of standards in Section 14.08A.100 of the proposal, staff responded that
       security would need to be on site for the entire event when those conditions exist.
   • Commissioner Wahlin expressed concern that the proposal is too restrictive.
   • Commissioner Knudsen expressed his support for the language as written.
   • Commissioner Jensen confirmed parking standards for the overlay would need to be paved, how the design
       standards would be verified (through the DRC), how the proposed hours of operation correspond to other rules in
       the city, and where the distance requirements came from.
   • Commissioners discussed possible changes to security requirements, noise levels, and types of events that could
       be permitted.
   • Commissioners discussed options for security, what live music is defined as, and whether alcohol is provided or
       brought to the premises.
   • Commissioner Whitlock suggested more specificity to the design standards in regard to permitted materials, and
       suggested that gravel parking be permitted in the overlay zone.
   • Commissioner Hill suggested removing the time requirement for security and further defining the distance
       requirement and live music standard.

FINDINGS / BASIS OF PLANNING COMMISSION DETERMINATION
The Planning Commission identified the following findings as the basis of this decision or recommendation:
   • That the regulations of the overlay need to be reviewed by staff, specifically in the following areas:
           o Metal Façade definition
           o Distance requirement research and regulations
           o Lessening of security requirements by limiting use allowances and defining noise limits
           o Investigating if gravel can be a parking surface for permitted uses
           o Defining permitted uses and noise levels with uses
