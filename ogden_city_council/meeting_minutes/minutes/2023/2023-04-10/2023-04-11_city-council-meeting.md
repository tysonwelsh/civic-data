A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed MAKE Ogden downtown
zoning ordinance and zoning map amendments came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed
amendments are the next step in the implementation of the MAKE Ogden Downtown Master Plan and the Nine Rails Creative
District Master Plan. The proposal includes major updates to the zoning code including the elimination of the CBD and CBD-I
zones and the introduction of two new residential zones which are the Residential Multiple-family Vertical (R-MFV) and '
Residential Multiple-family Horizontal (R-MFH) and four new downtown commercials zones which are the Commercial
Entertainment (C-ENT), Commercial Mixed-Use (C-MU), Historic 25th Street Commercial (H25), and the Nine Rails Creative
District Commercial zone (C-9). The proposal also includes various minor code text amendments updating terminology,
definitions, and review criteria throughout the City code. The code changes are accompanied by six proposed rezones
reclassifying the entire CBD and CBD-I zoned areas as well as some downtown adjacent properties to the new residential and
commercial zones.
Planning Manager Brierly summarized the memo and introduced Jessica Garrow of Design Workshop, the City’s partner
in creation of the MAKE Ogden Plan, who used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed ordinances that
will be used to implement the MAKE Ogden Plan. She first reviewed the process that was followed to get to this point, which was
guided by goals that were identified for the project as follows:
e The new code should be simple, easy to understand and apply. ,
e People will want to visit downtown and say great things about the area. }
e The code should clearly explain the process so most applicants can successfully navigate it and understand what
is expected of their application.
e The code should strive to protect Ogden’s character.
e The code should result in design and form that does not reflect a cookie cutter design process.
e The code should encourage development patterns in downtown that create small, “15 minute” neighborhoods.
e Incorporate code language to reflect the recommendations of the Make Ogden Plan.
Ms. Garrow presented a map of the study area, which also highlighted existing zoning within the area; the MAKE Ogden
Plan proposes zoning adjustments for several areas within the district and she offered a comparison of the current zoning map with
the proposed zoning map if the adjustments are implemented. She then discussed community engagement; Design Workshop
facilitated small group meetings, a roundtable luncheon, provided notices to downtown businesses and owners, and checked in
with staff and various boards. The goal of these meeting was to get input on the following questions:
e What aspects of the Downtown character need to be preserved?
e What are current frustrations and challenges of the code?
e What are barriers to development?
© What works well?
Key takeaways were related to building height limits, streetscape improvements, land uses, and the need to clarify the
land use code. This feedback was used to inform the draft documents that were first presented to the Planning Commission in
work sessions and public hearings; the Planning Commission made refinements to land uses, provided design criteria for small
cell developments, addressed the interaction between the Code and other adopted plans, provided color and mural standards for
the Nine Rails District, and reviewed view and shade studies.
Ms. Garrow then expounded on the input received throughout the public engagement process and discussed the manner
in which this input influenced recommendations for revisions; key changes deal with height limits, development standards,
parking regulations, land use updates, streetscape improvements, definition revisions, and a streamlined review process. She then
provided detailed explanation and offered examples of each of the revision topics listed above and engaged in discussion with the
Council regarding the potential implications of some of these revisions. There was also discussion among the Council and Mr.
Brierley regarding the manner in which property owners in the MAKE Ogden Plan study area were notified of public meetings
and the number of opportunities they had to be involved in the planning process.

Chair Choberka invited City staff to respond to the questions raised during the public comment period.

Community and Economic Development Director Cooper first addressed the comments about Union Station; the
perception that zoning Union Station campus as mixed-use will devalue the Station or impacts its preservation/restoration is
inaccurate. The campus is a mixture of Central Business District (CBD) zoning designations, which is one of the most liberal
zones in the City. Therefore, a commercial/mixed-use zone would be more prescriptive and restrictive than the current zoning.
Currently, someone could develop the campus according to the CBD development guidelines and the resulting uses would be out
of line with the projected future use of the area. All of the physical buildings on Union Station are on the National Historic
Register, which means there are restrictions placed on the exterior and interior of the buildings. The campus is much larger than
the building itself — between approximately 22" and 27" Streets on Wall Avenue — but most of that land is vacant and it is not
appropriate to place a historic designation upon vacant land. Staff feels that there is adequate protection of the buildings
themselves due to the fact that they are included on the National Historic Register. He then noted that the MAKE Ogden Plan
