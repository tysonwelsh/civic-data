III) REGIONALLY SIGNIFICANT PROJECT STATUS UPDATES

Sustainable Tourism Plan – Park City Chamber:
Jennifer Wesselhoff, Executive Director of the Chamber, reviewed the progress being
made in sustainable tourism. She indicated the Global Sustainable Tourism Council
performed an assessment and the results helped the Chamber as it started a long-term
sustainable tourism plan. The Chamber hired a consultant and a plan was created.
Months ago, a survey was distributed to learn the priorities of the community. Seven
themes were addressed in the plan: The Board and staff were engaged, excited and
prepared to take on the challenges ahead, degradation of Summit County’s key natural
assets were a looming threat, Park City residents had tourism fatigue and were
approaching a breaking point, the community was about to lose itself to the destination,
the destination needed more stewardship and not necessarily more promotion, the
current and future tourism levels on top of the area growth were testing the community’s
infrastructure, and there was a shared vision across the County and City and a desire to
achieve a more sustainable future for the community.

Wesselhoff indicated the Destination Stewardship Committee was reviewing the report.
This committee was made up of 22 people, including representation from the City and
County governments, resorts, restaurants, the Historic Park City Alliance (HPCA),
Olympic Legacy Foundation, Recycle Utah, Mountain Trails, Basin Rec and at-large
members. They hoped to have a final plan to present to the Chamber Board and both
Councils and she requested a resolution be passed in support of the plan. The public
would have opportunities to hear about the results of the analysis and the strategies the
committee was thinking about on April 11th and 27th. The Chamber would also be going
through a long-range strategic plan to make sure they were prepared to take on the
sustainability plan.

Council Member Doilney stated it was a big step for the Chamber to get the assessment
when it didn’t know what the outcome would be. The theme and commitment that
marketing should be reevaluated in order to take care of Summit County and Park City
was impressive and he appreciated the Chamber for embracing that. Chair Robinson
thought it would require a lot of courage as the Chamber began its transformation.
Council Member Armstrong asserted Wesselhoff saw the need and stepped up to move
the community forward.

Mayor Worel asked if any of the marketing strategies would be altered during this time.
Wesselhoff indicated they were trying to educate visitors on the sustainability initiatives
through their media while strategies and tactics were formalized. She realized the
Chamber was behind since the City, County, and resorts had been working on
sustainability for a while. Council Member Toly discussed the natural assets of small
business, and noted if they were pushed out, the big businesses would come in. She
thought it was essential to keep the small businesses in town.

Council Member Doilney was impressed with the committee members and thought they
represented the community well. Council Member Wright stated it was a good study. He
thought Park City would remain the tourist area of the County, but he thought tourism
would change due to the climate - from a winter destination to a summer destination. He
noted thought should be given to how the City and County would transition.

Silver Summit Campus Subdivision - Summit County:
Derek Radke, County Engineer, reviewed a County project in the Silver Summit area,
referred to as the FJ Gilmor properties. The County acquired this parcel for future growth,
development, and the preservation of part of the area. The County worked with the
Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) for the past two years and noted the large
amount of metals in the soils. The upper part of the parcel was not in the soils overlay
zone. They planned a new High Valley Transit facility for bus storage and maintenance.
Mountain Regional Water requested one of the parcels. Radke noted the parcel was
being planned for public works uses. One issue identified was traffic in the Silver Summit
area. By creating a spine corridor through the parcel, it would allow some of the traffic to
bypass the existing roundabout. He indicated the roundabout would also need to be
expanded or changed to handle the future traffic load. To create the spine, the County
worked out a land swap to accommodate the plan. The road system was scheduled to
begin construction this summer and he anticipated it would take two summers to
complete.

Council Member Gerber clarified there was 10 acres of the parcel that belonged to Park
City Municipal to which Radke affirmed. Council Member Gerber asked what restrictions
were tied to the land. Chair Robinson stated the parcel was still zoned rural residential
and that needed to change. Council Member Rubell asked if the City and County were
working together to plan some of the uses, such as the High Valley Transit (HVT) facility,
to build something that would benefit everyone. Chair Robinson stated there might be a
way to combine uses in the future, but HVT was new and they were just trying to get
established. Council Member Armstrong stated it would primarily be a storage facility and
an administrative office. Summit and Wasatch Counties were in discussions right now
since this was a regional service.

SR224 Bus Rapid Transit - High Valley Transit District:
Caroline Rodriguez, Summit County Transportation Manager, reviewed the City and
County partnered to fund and implement a bus rapid transit (BRT). In 2016, a Transit
sales tax was passed by the voters. This revenue source provided for Electric Xpress,
Kimball Junction Circulator, and the Summit Bike Share. She noted the grants received in
recent years for Transit purposes. The BRT lanes were planned to end at the SR248 and
SR224 intersection. She anticipated construction for BRT would begin in 2024. Chair
Robinson asked if the construction was dependent on grants, to which Rodriguez stated it
was not, since there were many sources of funding.

Council Member Gerber stated BRT was the preferred alternative and asked Rodriguez to
review all the alternatives. Rodriguez stated it was a twostep screening process to
determine the locally preferred alternative. They looked at local bus, heavy rail, maglev
trains, a gondola for the entire way, etc. It looked at the return on investment, cost of the
capital improvements, cost of ridership, flexibility, and scalability as well. Screening Level
Two looked at the qualitative analysis around cost per mile, environmental impact, and
public sentiment. The BRT was the best option.

Council Member Toly asked what would happen to the shoulder when BRT was
implemented. Rodriguez stated it was a dedicated lane next to the shoulder. Council
Member Toly asked if the private property would need to be acquired, to which Rodriguez
affirmed. She also stated there would be four stations along the route and noted the
stations would be more than a shelter, but it was not finalized. Council Member Armstrong
thought this should be a bus stop that was enclosed to protect riders from the elements.
Council Member Toly asked if microtransit would be able to use the stations, to which
Rodriguez indicated the stations could be used by all public transit. Rodriguez indicated
BRT was not the solution but it was part of the solution, along with microtransit, bike
share, and other options. She stated each element would do its part to be a living
network.

Quinn's Junction Park and Ride – Park City Municipal:
John Robertson, Park City Engineer, reviewed the park and ride was part of the City's
Transit goals. The City would capture the number of vehicle trips since it was located off
of SR248. It would have 465 parking stalls, electric vehicle charging and bus charging
stations, bus shelters, and restrooms. Council Member Wright saw big problems with
traffic if this park and ride was built. Council Member Armstrong thought there would be a
total of 900 spaces between the Ecker Park and Ride and Quinn's Park and Ride and he
hoped outreach could be made with businesses to utilize the parking lots. He gave an
example of the resorts offering shuttles from the park and rides.

Council Member Gerber stated over 8,000 commuters came from outside Summit County
per day and this lot would provide a great opportunity to get vehicles off the road. Council
Member Stevens thought this would be well used because it was in a convenient location.
Chair Robinson thought it would be hard to get into the parking lot. It was a good attempt
and he hoped it would dovetail when UDOT began its project. Council Member Clyde
didn't think the project design made sense. Council Member Toly agreed it would be
difficult to turn left onto the frontage road. Chair Robinson thought it could provide some
relief. Council Member Clyde asked if a traffic study was performed. Robertson stated it
was performed and it worked, but it would be a challenge during peak times.

Chair Robinson was excused at 10:00 a.m.

IV)   OLYMPICS PLANNING DISCUSSION

Mayor Worel asked what could be done to open the Olympics conversation with the
community. Council Member Stevens reviewed she met with local facilitators, and they
stated this should be an unbiased process with the residents. The bid would be sent in
the summer and she thought outreach should be in May and June, both in person and via
Zoom. She suggested having weekly meetings with facilitators and having a panel to give
information. She wanted feedback across the spectrum with regard to support or
opposition to having the games here. Council Member Armstrong stated the school year
was wrapping up in May and he wanted to get the word out now about the dates for these
outreach initiatives. Council Member Toly suggested working out the costs for the County
and City for facilitators and other outreach measures so it could be approved as soon as
possible.

Council Member Rubell asked what the outreach would determine. Mayor Worel stated it
would determine at what level Park City would participate in the games. Council Member
Stevens expounded it would determine help with traffic mitigation, if housing should be
built, shutting everything down on Main Street, etc. She knew the City and County had a
voice, but with momentum at this point, she wasn’t sure they could expect this level of
help. Council Member Armstrong stated he was surprised to hear at the Olympic meeting
that nothing new would be built. The City and County expectations might not be the same
as the Olympic Committee’s expectations. He thought some of the sustainability goals
could be covered. He wanted more clarification on Frazier Bullock's “no build” comments
so there was no miscommunication.

Council Member Clyde stated an explanation needed to be given to the public on what
was going to happen, and one thing that needed to be communicated to the public was
that the businesses would do 25% less business during those 18 days of games. At the
last Olympics, businesses got reimbursed for lost revenue. Council Member Doilney
reviewed the Olympic presentation by Myles Rademan and that he stressed we needed
to have a seat at the table. He stated it was true they might not build anything for the
community, but there was an opportunity to work with the Legislature and good
relationships could be built with the Olympic Committee, UDOT, and members of the
Legislature. The City and County could be part of how the games happened instead of

Council Member Rubell agreed the narrative should be created by Park City and Summit
County. He wanted the discussion to move away from asking if we should have the
Olympics to managing our role in the Olympics.

V)     REGIONAL PLANNING DISCUSSION

Mayor Worel asked that the group meet again to have a deep dive discussion into
affordable housing and the possibility of creating a regional housing authority. Council
Member Clyde stated in the interim, staff could get together to frame the issues. Council
Member Stevens also noted Wasatch County had a housing authority and she thought
collaboration could include them to have a Wasatch Back Housing Authority.

Mayor Worel thanked Matt Dias, City Manager, and Tom Fisher, County Manager, for
their work and asked them to plan a future meeting.

VI)    CITY TOUR

Matt Dias, City Manager, stated City Tour would be the third week in May and he hoped
some City and County officials and staff could come to Boise, Idaho. He would send
additional information out.

VII) ADJOURNMENT

With no further business, the meeting was adjourned.
