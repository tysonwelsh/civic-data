Chair Healey called the meeting to order.
1. Discussion
1.1 City Center Overlay Zone
Francis Lilly said he wanted to go over the proposed ordinance with the Commission. Mike
Winder said the City Center was within a community reinvestment area so as to capture tax
increment to reinvest back in the area. He assured the Commission that the tax increment
would not be used for developer subsidies, but for public infrastructure. The plan is to invest
in a park, dubbed Mill Park. He handed out a potential timeline for the City Center from
August-Summer 2020 and went through each item.
August: Fehr & Peers traffic study modification made
August: Bond for property acquisition issued
August/September: City Center Overlay Zone presented to Community Councils, Planning
Commission & City Council for approval
August 2019 - September 2019: Mill Park Master Plan
August 2019 - April 2020: Property acquisition for proposed Mill Park

Lilly said Fehr & Peers was doing a traffic study on potential impacts from the City Center
with desired streetscape changes. Winder highlighted the boundaries of the City Center on a
map and said Phase 1 would be 3300 South to Woodland Avenue, then north of Woodland to
the City boundary would be Phase 2. He said Millcreek closed on a $20 million bond the
prior week with a low interest rate. That bond is for the property acquisition, demolition,
master planning, utility relocation, and construction of Phase 1 of Mill Park. Lilly said
design architects will be selected through an RFP process. He said he will short-list three
firms to have them give the City a concept up front and the City will see which they like best.
Commissioner Mumford pointed out that the $20 million bond was only for Phase 1. Winder
said he was working to relocate businesses in the area and the purchase of properties would
start in the next month. Commissioner LaMar asked about the condo occupancy in the area.
Winder said the residents would be out by March 1. Lilly mentioned that Millcreek has a
parks and recreation impact fee that will also generate money for the City Center. Winder
said the timeline was aggressive, but Millcreek wants to go fast because the tax increment,
which pays the bond debt payments, is limited to 20 years in the community reinvestment
area. He said in the first two years, Millcreek has lower bond payments. The City can borrow
from the West Millcreek Redevelopment Area to make the bond payments until tax
increment in the City Center comes in. He said when the park goes in, it will boost
development surrounding it. He said there is also a federal opportunity zone with
development opportunity and tax benefits with 10-year window for the area. Winder said he
wants to move the project along while the economy is good. Lilly said he had previous
experience with this process in South Salt Lake City. He said the overlay zone provides
specifics for developers to develop in the City Center.
John Janson then went over the draft ordinance with the Commission. He said the three
districts of the City Center are Neighborhood District, Mill Center, and City Market Place.
There are different requirements in the code for each district. He said there are application
processing standards in the code and asked the Commission whether projects should be
permitted or a conditional use. He said he had received input that the conditional use permit
process usually produces a better product though the draft ordinance allows most uses as
permitted. Winder said he was willing to have projects be a conditional use from an
economic development standpoint. Janson suggested conditional uses for multifamily and
mixed-use projects. The pros of having projects be a conditional use are that it provides
additional oversight, triggers community engagement at the Community Council and
Planning Commission, and could allow the Planning Commission to propose mitigations to a
particular project based on detrimental effects identified in code. The cons are that it is slows
the approval process, and with a detailed code there may not be any additional detrimental

effects that could be mitigated so the Commission could be setting a public expectation that a
project could be modified or denied. Lilly said some developers are willing to be flexible
with changes to plans made in public meetings.

Janson said there was a table of bulk and yard regulations in the ordinance. He showed a
depiction of a building with step backs to the Commission. He said there were height
exceptions in the draft ordinance different than the regular ordinance and a definition of a
story (height). Commissioner LaMar suggested using a specific height instead of stories to
allow flexibility for the developer. Lilly suggested listing stories and a not to exceed height.
He offered that the heights be between stories so as to be able to add building variation.
Janson reviewed the building height map which depicted greater height in the City Center
and less height closer to the neighborhoods. Lilly said there was a 30-foot elevation change
from Richmond Street to Highland Drive, so that is why the height is loaded on the west side.
Winder offered that additional height be allowed to the immediate south of 3300 South
(Option B Map). He said the Economic Development Department was working to plan for a
variety of uses there. Chair Healey said he would like to see the plan allow for greater
development on the east side of the 3300 S. and Highland intersection. Winder said he could
reconfigure the map to allow more height on both sides of the intersection.

Janson said in form-based codes, the place is described as much as possible. He showed
depictions of the road cross sections for Highland Drive, Richmond Street, 3300 South,
neighborhood Streets, one-way Mill Park Street, laneway for Mill Park Street, and woonerfs.
Lilly said the City Center Master Plan describes what the streets should look like and this
code creates enforceable standards for them. Commissioner Mumford suggested using
different themes for the different streets. Janson said he would work with Public Works on
what trees to put in. He showed a depiction of the corner plazas and setbacks. Lilly said the
corner plaza and setback standards would help to preserve view corridors. Janson said the
parking ordinance was slightly modified from the standard parking requirements in code.
Commissioner LaMar expressed concern on three-bedroom units not having enough parking
with the proposed 1.5 stalls per unit. Commissioner Booth said the parking may depend on
the type of building. Chair Healey said the younger generations were limiting their car
ownership and use. Elyse Greiner said she was part of a younger generation and she does not
go places where there is not enough parking. Lilly said the plan differs from surrounding
communities because it offers angled parking, which allows for more. He said the City would
have to regulate how the on-street parking is used through a parking management plan.
Commissioner Mumford said that where people are from that are using the City Center will
dictate what they are accustomed to for parking. Winder said the City Center will be urban
and there could be opportunity to build underground parking underneath the park. Lilly said
the City should attract the right businesses to the City Center.

Janson said the proposed ordinance describes different building designs and building types.
He showed depictions of what the buildings may look like. Winder said the mixed-use
buildings would be broken up by requiring variation with certain building lengths. He also
pointed out a requirement for awnings on Mill Street as refuge from the weather. Lilly said
having plazas along the park will help the space feel larger. Commissioner LaMar asked
about the 15% open space requirement. Janson said the open space would be public or
common. Janson said developments could participate in shared open space or substitute the
requirement by paying cash in-lieu of the open space.

Janson said the ordinance sets building design standards for street lighting, signs,
sustainability elements for rooftops and charging stations, and overhead utility lines. Winder
said there is currently a billboard at the entrance of Mill Park that would need to be relocated.
He asked the Commission to be creative with billboards. Lilly said the City Council has not
heard the new proposed billboard ordinance. He said there are 6 billboards in the City Center.
Commissioner Claerhout suggested putting an electronic billboard on the side of a building.
Winder brought up that the City would require developers to bury the utilities. Lilly said the
intention is to harness the market forces into something of value. Janson asked about tax
increment with the developments on Gunn Avenue. Winder did not know those figures off-
hand. Lilly said the building permits would be expensive, about 2% of the project value.
Commissioner Booth asked about the uses for the park. Lilly said the uses would come up as
part of the work from the consultants retained through the RFP process. Commissioner
LaMar suggested the south end of the park be able to be blocked off from vehicle access to
allow for festivals and the like in the park. He asked that the Commission be involved in the
development approval process. Commissioner Claerhout asked about having two
Commission meetings a month. Commissioner Booth offered that there was not enough city
staff to support two meetings and it would get complicated with the community council
meetings. Winder mentioned that the developments on Gunn Avenue had additional meetings
to get public input rather than getting the input at additional Commission meetings. Janson
asked the Commission about their preference for projects being a conditional use.
Commissioner LaMar said they should be conditional. Winder asked about a component of
projects being a minimum size. Janson said the City has to offer options for property owners
to develop their property or there could be a legal issue. Lilly said the building height map
highlights the minimum stories allowed in each area. The Commission briefly discussed
drive-throughs and where they could be located. Commissioner LaMar offered that on poor
air quality days, that drive-throughs be closed. Lilly said the minimum height requirements
would restrict most drive-throughs. Lilly said he would like to leave the commercial
businesses on the east side of Highland Drive alone and see what ripple effects the energy of
the City Center produced.

Chair Healey requested a second work meeting on the overlay zone. The Commission
decided on having a meeting Tuesday, September 10" at 5:15 p.m.
