Proposed Ogden Municipal Code Title 8 Amendments
          Community and Economic Development Executive Director Johnson reported that Ogden City Administration is
proposing to make significant changes to the City’s Title 8 Airport code and to the City’s administrative code related to airport
operations. Ogden City has been working for years to make improvements to the Ogden-Hinckley Airport, to bring it in line with
industry standards, and to create a thriving airport environment that would attract aeronautical industry. Recent efforts have
included efforts to attract commercial air service, updating Title 8 to address lease terms and fees, adopting an airport master plan,
updating the City’s general plan related to airport operations, and hiring professional, qualified airport management. Partly spurred
by an audit report requested by the Utah State Legislature, the Administration is proposing to take the next steps in improving
airport operations and regulations by rewriting the City code and administrative code to better reflect industry best practices and
legal standards. This effort is proposed to take place over several phases as the code updates and rewrites can be processed. The
City has been working with outside legal counsel and airport consultants to work through the proposed changes to the City’s
legislative and administrative codes. Changes to Title 8 of the City’s code of ordinances are subject to Council approval while
changes to the City’s administrative code are subject to mayoral approval. The consultants and Administration will be proposing
several significant changes to the code. These changes include relocating some elements of Title 8 to other areas of the legislative
code while removing other sections from the legislative code and placing them in the administrative code. As mentioned, these
changes will take place over several phases and will not be implemented in one single proposal. The initial phase of the proposal
is specific to Title 8, Chapters 1, 4, and 8 of the City’s legislative code. These chapters relate to definitions, aeronautical activities
including fixed base operators (FBO), and airpark minimum standards. The current chapters provide standards for specific uses
and operations; however, based on industry best practices and consultant input, these standards are inadequate. The proposal for
the changes to Title 8, Chapters 1, 4, and 8 includes removing the substantive regulations, rewriting them to a higher standard, and
moving them to the administrative code. It should be noted that the removal of the regulations from the legislative code to the
administrative code removes the Council’s ability to approve the regulations. Once in the administrative code, any changes would
be subject only to mayoral approval.
          Mr. Johnson, Airport Manager Condie, and Ryan Leick of Ascension Group Partners, used the aid of two PowerPoint
presentations, the intent of the first being to orient the City Council members to Ogden-Hinckley Airport (OGD) fundamentals and
their role in the establishment of airport policies. The second presentation expounded on the implications of the proposed Code
amendments aimed at providing minimum standards for OGD; it provided a clear explanation of the differences between an
ordinance and an administrative policy for the purposes of governing OGD. The conclusion was that an administrative policy will
facilitate the implementation of best practices at OGD, and the City Council will receive an annual report on operations and the
status of the Airport. It will also be within the Council’s purview to amend the administrative policy if deemed necessary. For a
copy of the presentations in their entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Discussion among the Council and the presenters centered on how OGD compares with the Provo Airport; the difference
between a commercial airport and an executive airport and the length of time it would take Ogden to get certified as an executive
airport; current service levels at OGD; private hangar usage at OGD; fuel storage facility requirements and fuel flowage fee
revenues; the history of updating design standards at the Airport; the benefits of streamlining OGD processes by adopting an
administrative policy rather than an ordinance; and success of this type of proposal at other airport facilities.
          Council members expressed concern about ceding control of the Airport entirely because they have been presented with
different proposals and scenarios in the past that were described as having a high likelihood of success for the Airport and that has
not been the case. Mr. Leick stated that based upon the proposed administrative policy, the Council will still have some level of
involvement and control in airport processes; certain actions will require a vote of the Council, and the goal of this proposal is to
accelerate response time to any changes or projects at the Airport. Mr. Johnson added that Administration is not asking to entirely
eliminate Title 8 of the Ogden Municipal Code; there are some components of that ordinance that will remain in place, and the
proposed administrative policy will ultimately augment and build upon the City ordinance. The policy will govern primary
management documents for the day-to-day operations at the facility.
          Council member Graf suggested it would have been helpful for a few Council members to be involved in the meetings
that have taken place with Ascension Group and Administration to develop this proposal. Vice Chair Hyer agreed; the Airport and
the laws that govern it are very technical and strict, and it is difficult for the Council to have a clear understanding of the
implications of these proposed adjustments. Vice Chair Hyer stated he would like to hear from end-users at the Airport to
determine their comfort level with the primary management documents that have been created by Ascension. Council member
Choberka and Chair White agreed.
          Council member Blair stated he needs to fully understand the apprehension from the rest of the Council members; he
feels completely comfortable moving forward with the vision Mr. Condie has identified for the facility. He wants to have the
opportunity to review changes that are proposed, but he feels that will still be possible under the administrative policy. He believes
Administration and Mr. Condie should have responsibility for the day-to-day operations of the Airport.
          The Council discussed the need to hear from stakeholders at the Airport, which led Garret Hain, VP of Avflight, the fixed
base operator (FBO) at OGD to offer his input about the investment his company has made at the Airport and the importance of
moving to an administrative policy and minimum standards for the Airport. Until that takes place, he cannot invest further into the
Airport.
          Closing discussion among the group centered on the Council’s appreciation for improved communication between City
Administration and the Council regarding the Airport and the joint goal of making the Airport profitable and offering a desired
service to the community. They also discussed the relationship between the proposed administrative policy and the City’s General
Plan and Transportation Master Plan (TMP) and whether it would be necessary to amend the General Plan and TMP whenever the
administrative policy is adjusted.
           Council staff indicated another discussion on this matter could be scheduled for August 19; the Council indicated they
were comfortable placing an action item on the August 19 meeting to consider the proposal but would also like a discussion item
on the work session agenda for that same night to allow for more discussion or any questions that arise between now and then.

         The meeting adjourned at 6:21 p.m.
