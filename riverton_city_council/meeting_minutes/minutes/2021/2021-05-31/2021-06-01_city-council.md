         American Rescue Plan (ARP) Discussion - Mayor Staggs and Kevin Hicks,
         Administrative Services Director

Mayor Staggs said that he had sent an email to all of the Councilmembers discussing the
American Rescue Plan funds. He said the City has not received the ARP funds and will not
receive them until after the new budget year. He presented a list of guiding principles.

Councilmember Buroker said she wanted to clarify that there are no issues with lead in the
pipes in the City. City Manager, David Brickey, clarified that there is no water infrastructure that
needs to be updated.

Mayor Staggs presented the State of Utah top priorities for the ARP funds. Mayor Staggs’
presentation is included with the packet. Mayor Staggs said that he believes the City will need
to match any funding that is received from the County or the State for specific projects. He said
that he believes the top priorities should be water infrastructure and networking and broadband.

Councilmember McDougal said that he doesn’t want to signal what Riverton City is doing right
now until the City knows what the state is doing and what the County is doing. He said that
emergency preparedness should be one of the top priorities considered by the Council. Mayor
Staggs said that he thinks Riverton should be in the driving position so that the City is the one
deciding how the funds will be used.

Councilmember McDougal said that water, broadband and a small percentage for emergency
preparedness should be the top priorities for Riverton City.

Councilmember Buroker asked if lining some of the canals could be considered water
conservation. Mayor Staggs said that lining the canals could be included but would need to be
verified. Staff suggested that expanding the ponds could be a project but not lining the canals
because the City does not own the canals.

Councilmember Buroker asked if the City needed a public defender and if the funds could be
used for that service. Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said the City already has a public defender
and this is already in the budget.

Councilmember Stewart said that projects that require grant money need to be in the budget in
order to receive the match from the grant funding. Trace Robinson, City Engineer agreed with
Councilmember Stewart and said that projects that receive grant funding are included in the
budget and then typically receive a 6.25% match.

Councilmember Wells said that he agrees with the guiding principles. He said he is confused
with how the money would be used in the Greenwell project and how the County would work
with the City to fund this project.

like to dedicate the use of the well to the golf course only. He said that Riverton City tried to
convey to the County that the Greenwell could be a win, win for both the County and Riverton

         Ordinance No. 21-13* - Amending and Updating Riverton City's Ordinance
         Regulating Development on Private Lanes, Proposed by Riverton City
Page | 6 Minutes – City Council Meeting
June 1, 2021



Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, said that this public hearing was for a text
amendment which was initiated by Riverton City. He said that the amended ordinance
addresses several key issues relating to development on private lanes. He said it clarifies that
all required setbacks shall apply, and shall be measured from the edge of the private lane,
rather than property lines. He said it also stipulates that the private lane area will not be
counted towards determining the total buildable area for the main dwelling and for accessory
structures. He said “flag” lots, or lots where a portion of the lot area is made up solely by the
private lane, may not count the narrow portion of the lot occupied only by the private lane
towards the minimum lot area.

Mr. Lethbridge said the amendment allows the maximum number of lots utilizing a private lane
to be determined during Conditional Use permit approval. He said the minimum width
standards are set, and the City has the discretion during approval to require additional width and
infrastructure, including curb, gutter, and sidewalk, based on the number of lots and the
circumstances of development. He said the ordinance also clarifies that required infrastructure
must be installed prior to issuance of a building permit.

Councilmember Stewart said that there may be easements that are undevelopable and he
doesn’t want to restrict areas that are not able to be developed. Mr. Lethbridge said that 1,500
feet is very lengthy and this would fall well under this 1500 feet. Mr. Lethbridge said he thought
the City would be protected even if the 1,500 feet language was removed.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Comment
period.

Councilmember Stewart MOVED that the City Council approve Ordinance No. 21-13* -
Amending Sections 18.(20,25,30,35,&40).070, Lots Located on Private Lanes, as
described in Exhibit “A” and remove the 1,500 feet maximum. Councilmember McDougal
SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for discussion on the motion; there being none,
he called for a roll call vote and the vote was as follows: Councilmembers Buroker-Yes, McCay-
Yes, McDougal-Yes, Stewart-Yes, and Wells-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

*Ordinance No. 21-13 had previously been used so after City Council Meeting, this
Ordinance was renumbered to Ordinance No. 21-16 to avoid a duplicate numbering.


         Ordinance No. 21-14 - General Plan Amendment & Rezone of 5.91 Acres Located
         At Approximately 12900 South 1830 West, Current Designation of Medium Density
         Residential and R-4 Zone, Proposed Designation of Medium High Density
         Residential and RM-6 Zone, Ryan Kent with Dragonfly Development, Applicant.


Mr. Lethbridge explained that this is a public hearing for an application to simultaneously amend
the Land-Use Element of the City’s General Plan and also rezone several properties that total
about 5.91-acres. He said the properties are located near 12900 South 1830 West rezone.
He said the application was made by Ryan Kent with Dragonfly Development. He said the
properties are mostly vacant and nearly surrounded by existing housing as can be seen on the
aerial image.

Mr. Lethbridge said the current General Plan is Medium Density Residential as are all the
adjoining properties and other properties in the area. He said the designation “Medium Density
Page | 7 Minutes – City Council Meeting
June 1, 2021



Residential” has a 10,000-sf minimum lot size and corresponds to the R-4 Zone (single-family
10,000-sf lot size).

Ryan Kent from Dragonfly Development said there has been a lot of discussion with neighbors,
staff, and Councilmembers. He said that the staff has been very good to work with him. He
said he wants a gated community and upscale neighborhood. He said he would commit to
single family homes and that seemed to resonate and hit home with residents. He said he
wanted to know what rambler style means and would rather have a height restriction instead of
just rambler style. He said he would like the buyers to be able to dictate whether to have a two
story or single level home.

Councilmember Wells asked about the height restrictions, two story versus bonus room. He said
that most of the emails that he received were residents concerned with high density, two story
homes and multi-family units. He said he believes the residents would be supportive of this
project if the units are all single family units.

Councilmember Buroker asked Mr. Kent why he was considering an HOA community. Mr. Kent
said that he believes people are interested in a gated community and people like that
maintenance and snow removal is handled privately.

Councilmember McCay asked Mr. Kent how much he plans to charge for the HOA. She said
that she does not like HOA communities and that the City can take care of maintenance and
snow removal. Mr. Kent said that he lives in an HOA and there are people who like that option.
He said that an HOA can control garbage cans, yard care, and bring fiber to these communities
for about half the price.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;

Cecile Page said her property is adjacent to this development. She said that residents like the
HOA option and the residents think that this will appeal to older people. She believes that older
people will create less traffic problems.

Bryson Brady said thank you to the Council and staff. He said he lives in the property that
borders this development. He said that he believes this development is very thoughtful and he
supports the development. He said he doesn’t understand the reasons for the rezone and
thinks this development could continue in the current zone.

Mr. Kent said that he believes that if the neighborhood looks nice and is maintained then he
doesn’t think neighbors will be worried about the density. He said he doesn’t think that people
will notice a big difference and it will save on water usage.

Mayor Staggs called for additional public comments; there being none, he closed the Public
Comment period.
