Proposed Ordinance 2020-39, amendments to the definitions of
warehousing and personal storage units and to allow them only in the M-1
and M-2 Zones
          A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding proposed zone text amendments came
before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Ogden City Planning is proposing several amendments meant to clarify.
and define commercial warehousing and personal storage. In addition, the proposed amendments would remove the uses from the
commercial zones limiting warehousing and personal storage uses to the M-1 and M-2 zones. Ogden City posted a notice of
pending ordinance on June 26, 2020. A notice of pending ordinance limits new land use applications related to the proposed
change for a period of 180 days.
          Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the
proposed ordinance amendments; he first clarified the differences between warehousing and personal storage uses; warehousing is
typically industrial or commercial in nature and it has a related use for a businesses storage of product intended for sale. The
personal storage/self-storage use is space for storage of personal belongings. Given recent development trends, staff is concerned
that personal storage can be lumped into the warehousing definition and there may be pressure to convert commercial buildings to
personal storage or warehousing uses when that building becomes vacant. The tax production (sales and property) would be
reduced by a conversion of a commercial building to storage units and the use could potentially create 'dead zones' in important
community building areas. He provided a chart communicating the zones in the City where storage and warehousing uses are
presently permitted and noted there are currently 7 storage unit businesses in the C-2 zone, 12 in the M-1/M-2 zone, and 3
nonconforming uses in other zones. Staff has recommended creating a definition for personal storage units and clarify the
warehousing definition by excluding it as an accessory to the main use. Relative to the pressure to convert vacant buildings to
storage units, it would be necessary to evaluate whether the use would still meet the definition of commercial, which is to provide
for the sale and supply of a complete range ofretail and wholesale goods, entertainment, and personal services for the area and
also a center for recreational, entertainment, and cultural activities. Businesses need interaction and activity between one another
and allowing a storage unit use in the middle of a retail or commercial center would create a 'dead zone'. The General Plan

    Proposed Resolution 2020-23, consideration to adopt a master plan, known
    as MAKE Ogden Downtown Master Plan for the downtown area of Ogden
    City
              A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider a resolution
    adopting the 2020 Downtown Ogden Master Plan. The memo stated Ogden City Administration is proposing the adoption of the
    2020 Downtown Ogden Master Plan, also known as the MAKE Ogden Plan. The plan is the product of a collaboration between
    the City and Denver based consultants Design Workshop to develop a comprehensive strategy for the continued development of
    Ogden's downtown. The plan establishes a series of tenets on which the plan is based and articulates a series of episodes through
    which the City will navigate as it develops the downtown area. The docmnentprovides the plan for both the physical development
    and the economic development of the City.
              Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide the
    Planning Commission's recommendation regarding the Plan. Work to develop the Plan began in the spring of 2019 and
    culminated with two Planning Work Sessions in June and July 2020. The five tenets of the Plan include real estate and land use;
    historic and cultural; transportation and mobility; parks and open space; and social equity and amenities. There are four
    development episodes contemplated in the plan:
                  •    Episode 1 - catalyze. Developer led/City supported.
                  • Episode 2 - support. City led/developer supported.
                  • Episode 3 - connect. Developer led/City supp01ted.
                  •    Episode 4 - expand. City led/Developer supp01ted.
              The Plan provides design standards for development to occur in the downtown area of the City. The Planning
    Commission reviewed the Plan document for General Plan consistency relative to community identity, economic development,
~   housing, land use, and neighborhood planning. The Planning C01mnission found consistency with the Plan and voted unanimously
)   to recommend approval.
              Todd Johnson and Robb Berg of Design Workshop then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the final
    draft of the MAKE Ogden Plan. Mr. Johnson summarized the history of the work done by Design Workshop in many different
    communities throughout the United States; the group that worked on this project identified the following dilemma: the City of

          Minutes of Regular Meeting of Council of Ogden City, Utah, September 1, 2020 Page 286


Ogden needs a comprehensive vision to guide growth and development in its Downtown and to maintain and grow its tax base -
which will improve the long tenn fiscal health of the community and ability to meet basic service needs. How can Ogden
strategically evolve its downtown core to take advantage of its historic and natural assets to become a nexus for business and
employment attraction, residential growth, recreational and cultural activity that increases the value of the land contributing to the
long-term health of the Ogden community? The comprehensive approach used to tackle this dilemma is known as' legacy design'
and the Make Ogden project will be a 'legacy project'. It will return the future of Ogden's downtown back to what made it great at
the turn of the century. Ogden is an ideal size for a legacy project because it has adequate space for a walkable and dense active
core that will flare out into the fringes of the downtown. He presented a diagram illustrating the private and public obligations and
investments needed to achieve the vision of the plan and create a sustaining tax base for the community. The vision of this plan
has been tested with the developers in and around the block of the City being referred to as the Wonder Block, which is
undergoing a massive redevelopment project. The next steps are for the Council to deliberate and take action on the proposal to
adopt the Plan and begin implementing it as other development and redevelopment opportunities come forward.
          Mr. Berg then described the essence of the Plan document; the decisions made today and over the next several months
will have a considerable impact on the future of downtown Ogden. He discussed the work done over the past several months to
create the Plan and identified several of the stakeholders who participated in the project. The six areas of emphasis for the project
were: land use, historic assets, mobility, open space, equity, and branding. An episodic approach will allow the City to capitalize
on catalytic public and private investments over the course of the next 25 years. The Plan identifies four districts that make up the
City's downtown, as well as the neighborhoods smrounding these districts. Efforts to engage the community in planning for these
districts was expanded beyond the core downtown and smveys were published in English and Spanish in order to increase
response. The intent of the survey was for participants to identify assets, challenges, and priorities in Ogden's downtown area.
Over 1,000 people responded to the online survey. Many of the challenges identified helped Design Workshop to focus planning
efforts; these challenges included vacancies/empty lots, 20 th Street; old buildings; services for the needy; Washington Boulevard;
and 24 th Street. Equally, there was a great number of assets identified in the survey, including 25 th Street; The Junction; Ogden
River; Union Station; Municipal Gardens; and the LDS Temple. The Plan focuses on emphasizing and protecting these assets. He
presented graphics that identified the public's desires for private and public investment in the downtown core; there are
opportunities to pursue or incentivize development of properties that are not currently contributing to the community's tax base in
a meaningful way. He concluded by discussing growth targets identified for the different episodes in the Plan and summarized the
economic impact that these growth targets will have on the City. Comprehensive design standards must be created and applied to
growth and development in the downtown core via the creation of an overlay zoning mechanism. He thanked everyone in the City
that was involved in this project.
