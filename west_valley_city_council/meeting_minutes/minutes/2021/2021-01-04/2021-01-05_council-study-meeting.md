     Mayor Bigelow informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular
     Council Meeting scheduled January 12, 2021, in order for the City Council to hear
     and consider public comments regarding Application ZT-9-2020, filed by Randy
     Moore, requesting a Zone Text Change to allow properties with access and frontage on
     a street with a planned Right-of-Way width of at least 80 feet where an exempt housing
     development is proposed to apply for the RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential)
     Zone.

       Proposed Ordinance 21-01 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
       Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

       ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 21-01, AMEND THE SECTION 7-5-101 OF
       THE WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND
       REGULATIONS GOVERNING APPLICATIONS FOR RM ZONING
       Steve Pastorik, CED, discussed proposed Ordinance 21-01 that would amend
       Section 7-5-101 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to amend regulations
       governing applications for RM Zoning.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:

               Randy Moore’s original application defined qualified 55 or older
               development and allowed properties with access and frontage on a street
               with a planned right-of-way width of at least 80 feet where a qualified 55
               or older development in which the density must not exceed 8 units per
MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 5, 2021
                       -3-


        acre is proposed to apply for the RM (Multiple Unit Dwelling Residential)
        zone. The Planning Commission recommended approval subject to the
        density being capped at 7 units per acre.

        After reviewing Mr. Moore’s proposal, the City’s Legal Department
        modified the language to not include the federally defined term “qualified
        55 or older development” which could change. Despite the revision, the
        end result is the same because qualified 55 or older developments are the
        only form of exempt housing.

        Included with this issue paper is a map that shows remaining vacant land
        within the City as well as roads designated on the Major Street Plan as an
        80’ right-of-way and roads designated as 100’ right-of-way or more
        excluding nonaccess highways and freeways like I-215 and Bangerter
        Highway. The map also highlights the one-half mile areas within eligible
        light rail stations. The purpose of this map is to illustrate where rezone
        requests to the RM zone are currently allowed and where they would be
        allowed if this application is approved.

        Also included in this report is a graph showing the population of the City
        by age. As of 2018, 17% of the City’s population was aged 55 or older.
        Over the next 10 years, just over 15,000 more residents will fall into the
        55 or older cohort.

 Councilmember Buhler verified that this would allow specific projects to be
 developed along roads on 80-foot-wide streets instead of 100-foot-wide streets.
 Steve replied yes. Councilmember Buhler asked why this requirement is in place.
 Steve replied that 100-foot-width roads tend to have transit, services, and higher
 traffic. The applicant, Mr. Moore, feels that this type of development (55 and up)
 wouldn’t create as great an impact in a neighborhood. Councilmember Buhler
 asked if the City should allow development of RM on 80 foot right of way and not
 limit to 55 and up. Councilmember Lang stated that she could see the
 consequence of having one or more of these projects in an area. She indicated she
 would prefer to see the ordinance remain as is. Steve noted that the development
 would need access and frontage on an 80 foot right of way. Mayor Bigelow stated
 that he doesn’t understand the justification for the change. He indicated that he
 understands the restrictions in place as they are. Steve stated that the applicant
 feels this type of housing is needed, is successful, and is beneficial to the City.
 Mayor Bigelow requested some examples of how this would impact the City and
 where it may or may not work. Councilmember Christensen stated that streets are
 widened often. He indicated senior developments generate less traffic. Mayor
     MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – JANUARY 5, 2021
                            -4-


      Bigelow asked how large these are. Steve replied they tend to have 2-3 bedrooms
      and more living space. Mayor Bigelow asked if they are typically single level.
      Steve replied that with the proposed density, these would likely be single level.
      Councilmember Lang asked what the setbacks would be. Steve replied that all of
      these projects in the City have been done as a PUD which allows for flexibility.
      He noted that the City generally tries not to do anything less than 20-foot setbacks
      in the front and rear. Steve stated the number of trips generated per unit in a
      senior housing development are usually less than a single-family homes.
      Councilmember Lang asked what percentage of people must be 55 and up. Steve
      replied federal standards require 80% of the units must be occupied with one
      member of the family being 55 or older. Councilmember Buhler stated that he
      doesn’t like to make changes to accommodate specific needs. Mayor Bigelow
      indicated that he likes the idea of more 55 and up communities but he doesn’t
      know if allowing more of them at 80-foot street widths is the right answer. Mayor
      Bigelow and the Council discussed how different types of development creep into
      different areas.

      The City Council will consider Ordinance 21-01 at the Regular Council Meeting
      scheduled January 12, 2021 at 6:30 P.M.

RESOLUTION 21-02: APPROVE A MASTER SERVICES AGREEMENT WITH
RINGCENTRAL, INC. FOR TELECOMMUNICATION SERVICES
Ken Cushing, IT, presented proposed Resolution 21-02 that would approve a Master
Services Agreement with RingCentral, Inc. for telecommunication services.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       We are beginning to see issues with our old phone system. As a replacement the
       city needs something that will work well with those working at home during any
       emergency.

       Formal bidding will not be required since RingCentral is in use by a part of the
       Police Department. We will be expanding that system. Upgrading our phone system
       will allow us to set up different calling trees for different types of emergencies that
       we can switch to on the fly as needed.

Mayor Bigelow asked if there will be a physical phone. Ken replied in some locations there
will be a physical phone (such as in shared areas like the front desk at the Fitness Center)
but others will just have a computer-based phone system. Ken noted that people could
install the app on their phone and answer that way without disclosing their cell number.
Mayor Bigelow discussed the system crashing in emergency events. Ken replied that this
could happen in any situation but explained how the system would continue to operate.
Councilmember Lang asked how much the phone system costs now. Ken replied that there
hasn’t been a contract which has caused great difficulty in repairs and the aging software.
Councilmember Lang asked if this cost includes all extensions in the City. Ken replied yes.

The City Council will consider Resolution 21-02 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
January 12, 2021 at 6:30 P.M.
