Mike       Winder       called    the meeting            to order.    He recognized          local legislators      present        who ran in the
recent      election.     Mayor       Jeff Silvestrini          said the council         was striving       to create a city that everyone
loved.      In January      2022, the council                met with      executive      staff in a strategic       planning        session.    Prior
to the meeting,          everyone         read the book, "For              the Love      of Cities."     The book      was about spaces and
things      people      loved     or did not about their              communities.          In every     comtnunity,       there are people
known       as the "doers,"          or co-creators,           and the people          invited    to the meeting       that evening
represented          some of them in Millcreek.                    The city wanted          to leann from        the creative       people    in the
community           and to know           where         to align its efforts.


1. Discussions           on the Promise                 Program,       Arts,     Sustainabihty,         Public     Safety,     Historic
Preservation,            Community              Life,     Budget,      Public      Works/Permitting,             Planning,         and Code
Mike       Winder       explained      the group           represented         the city council,       city staff, planning
commissioners,            historic     preservation            commission          members,       community        council      members,        arts
council      members,           Promise       committee         members,         business     council    members,         rotary    club
members,        and local        officials.      He explained           that there were nine tables which                  each had two hosts
to introduce         the following            topics:     Millcreek       Promise      Program,      arts, sustainability,         public     safety,
historic     preservation,          community             life, budget,     public     works/building         services,      and
planning/economic                development.             There would          be time for people         to discuss      the topic     and ask
questions,       and then people              would       rotate tables based on their preferences.                  There would            be six
rotations      of the nine tables.             The rotations          and discussions        then consumed          the rest of the meeting.
