Proposed Defense Depot Reuse {DDR) Zone Building Height Amendment
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding an amendment to building height
regulations in the Defense Depot Reuse (DDR) Zone came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated a petition has
been filed by Boyer Company to increase the maximum height limit for buildings north of 400 North and east of Depot Drive
from 50 feet to 90 feet. The area proposed for increased height is located with the DDR Zone, which is classified as one of the
City's manufacturing and industrial zones. The petitioner has indicated an increase in demand for taller buildings within BDO.
         Planning Manager Montgomery summarized the memo and made a presentation to expound on the implications of the
proposed ordinance amendments. He presented an aerial map to orient the Council to the area of BDO that is subject to the
proposed amendment which is in the northeast quadrant of the installation. At present, the CUTI'ent maximum building height in the
DDR Zone is 50 feet. The petitioner would like to increase the maximwn building height to 90 feet in the area north of 400 East
and east of Depot Drive. This area is bordered by agricultural/wetland property to the east and the Weber County Fairgrounds to
the north. Planning Commission considerations included the following:

        Mayor's Proposed Fiscal Year 2020-2021 Budget Presentation
                   The Mayor's Proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2020-2021 Budget came before the Council for consideration. A memo from
        Council staff explained each year during the first Council Meeting in May, the Mayor is required to present a proposed budget for
        the upcoming FY. The Council accepts the proposed budget for review and, through a series of work sessions, reviews the
        proposal over the next two months. A tentative budget is adopted after holding an initial public hearing. A second public hearing
        is held at the time of adoption of the final budget. During the budget hearings, the Council outlines their proposed changes to the
        budget and accepts public input. Once the final public hearing is closed, the Council makes its changes and adopts the final City
        budget. By State law, a budget must be adopted by June 30. An exception to the timing is allowed if the Council is considering a
        proposed property tax increase.

Board/Council/Trustee Business
          Council member Stephens stated he continues to hear feedback from constituents regarding the recent development
trends that have led to an increase in the number of apartment units that are being constructed in the community. He is concerned
that the City is being inundated with apartments. Planning Manager Montgomery referenced an informational item provided to the
Council several weeks ago regarding construction of apartment projects throughout the City; his concern relates to the fact that
most of the apartment development is occurring in commercial zones of the City. This is related to a lack of standards in
commercial zones that leads to easier development. He stated a temporary ordinance has been enacted that would halt construction
of apartments in commercial zones for a six-month period to give the City time to detennine the appropriate design standards for
that type of development in commercial zones. The Council will have the opportunity to consider the revisions to those standards
in the coming months. This led to a discussion regarding tax revenue implications of allowing residential development in
commercial areas; commercial is taxed at 100 percent of their property valuation, whereas residential uses are taxed at 55 percent
of their valuation. Additionally, the City loses the opportunity to generate sales tax revenue when commercial property is
consumed by residential uses.
