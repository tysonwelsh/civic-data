       PRESENTATION - Neighborhood Improvement Team Update

Ms. Mathews started off the presentation by introducing the new Neighborhood Improvement
Team and its members. Mr. Taylor then spoke on what kind of infractions the Neighborhood
Improvement Team enforces. Some of these include excessive weeds, junk, and homeless
encampments. He then showed a few example pictures of these infractions. Mr. LeDoux then
gave an overview of the reporting and enforcement process.

Officer Andreason went over the successes the team has had since the beginning of the fiscal
year. He then presented a slide show about a particularly large project they have been working
on.

Ms. Mathews reviewed other activities the code enforcement has been involved with. These
included Picnic with Police activities and back to school nights.

      PRESENTATION - Metropolitan Water District of Orem - Central Utah Water
      Conservancy District Agreement

Mr. Tschirki, began by going over a brief history of the Orem Metropolitan Water District. Mr.
Tschirki then spoke on past water agreements between Orem and Vineyard. Mr. Tschirki
updated the council on the status of a large water storage tank Vineyard City is building. He
stated that once Vineyard City has finished their water storage tank, they would investigate
halting Vineyard borrowing water from Orem. He then went over some of the conditions for the
proposed agreement. These include providing multiple emergency connections between
Vineyard and Orem’s water distribution systems and providing an option for Orem to purchase
back Vineyard’s portion of Orem’s shared water tank.

General discussion ensued regarding canal water rights and procedure, “grey water,” water usage
of Sleepy Ridge Golf Course, and the status of the Farley property tank.

Mrs. Schriever asked the council to allow this agreement to be on the consent agenda for the next
city council meeting, rather than having them under the scheduled agenda. The Council gave
permission to do so.

       UPDATE - Request for an Advisory Opinion from the State Property Rights Ombudsman

Mrs. Schriever updated the council on the current legal status of the moratorium passed during
the August 9th, 2021 council meeting. Mrs. Schriever stated that Wright Development had filed
for an advisory opinion on the completeness of their site plan application and on the application
of the moratorium to their development. Mrs. Schriever then stated they have retained outside
counsel to assist the city through the process. She expressed she does not know how much time
the whole process will take, and the only deadline they’ve had to meet so far is agreeing to use a
staff attorney in the office of the ombudsman as opposed to an independent third party. Mrs.
Lauret asked if the city has a timeline from the ombudsman. Mr. Earl responded that a timeline
will most likely be set once they have a staff member assigned to issue an opinion. Mr. Earl also
stated there is a couple of potential jurisdiction issues the city needs to get resolved before they
can get into the merits of the request. Mrs. Lauret then asked if the City’s hired legal counsel has
a background in this sort of issue. Mrs. Schriever stated he does, and the city has hired him
before to work on other land use cases.

Mr. Davidson stated the primary focus of the advisory opinion filed by Wright Development is to
review the adjacency issue that was a topic of conversation during the last council meeting. Mr.
Earl agreed and added Wright Development has requested an advisory opinion to review if their

development application was vested before the moratorium was put into place, which depends on

Mr. Sumner asked Mr. Davidson if he had heard from any legislators on this topic. Mr. Davidson

Spencer responded that he would like the legislator to come to a council meeting so they could
discuss things face to face. Mr. Davidson responded there is a commission on housing
affordability that was put together by the legislature, and they are tackling this very issue. He
then went on to state this is not an Orem specific issue and that he expects this discussion to be a

have local legislators involved as well.

Mr. Peterson stated a state senator reached out to him and expressed support for the moratorium.

Mr. Davidson expressed that “people are watching” and how the city handles this could persuade
the state legislature to pass a state-wide law or code amendment taking more power away from
cities. He stated that no matter how the City of Orem views the moratorium, it may be viewed in
a completely different light by other municipalities and legislations.

Mr. Sumner asked Mr. Davidson if there is anything the city could do to “plead their case” to the
Utah League of Cities and Towns at their upcoming meeting. This caused general discussion
regarding legislature, municipality, and developer opinions and influence as well as zoning, city
growth, and housing.


       DISCUSSION - State Street Districts

Mr. Bench gave summary of what happened at the last Planning Commission meeting. These
include a negative recommendation pertaining to overall height standards in the State Street
District Zone, A positive recommendation pertaining to setbacks from residential zones within
the State Street District Zones, and a negative recommendation pertaining to parking standards
within the State Street District Zones. He then went over the proposed amendments pertaining to
building height, residential building setbacks, and height standards in the State Street District
Zones. Mr. Bench then showed a summary of Limitation on Residential Uses. These include the
percentage of commercial vs residential use in development requirements. He then asked the
council if they would like to modify these requirements. Mr. Spencer stated this is something he
would like to go over, expressing the council should dissect everything possible and go into
detail to make sure they get State Street right. Mr. Peterson agreed with this statement and said

PERSONAL APPEARANCES

Time was allotted for the public to express their ideas, concerns, and comments on items not
scheduled as public hearings on the agenda. Those wishing to speak should have signed in prior
to the meeting, and comments were limited to three minutes or less.

Ethan Harris stated he lives in the Hillcrest historic neighborhood with his family. He stated that
since living in the area, the mall continues to buy up property and intrude into the neighborhood.
He expressed his concern with the proposed Wright Development property, saying the
neighboring street will become overflow parking for the apartments, and said this would bring
more crime and traffic to the area. He then stated the council has received a nine-page petition to
halt the development.

Jared Moore expressed how he feels the residents in the area would not be listened to, but
expressed thanks to certain members of the council for voting to pass the moratorium. He asked
the council to reconsider what could be built on the property, saying the attempted bank
development on the property a few years back would have been a great addition to the
community.

Stephanie Visnaw stated that giving unlimited height maximums and high density housing the
ability to be built next to residential zones is a bad idea. She expressed that high density housing
projects cause parking issues for surrounding neighborhoods. She then read a few Facebook
comments on the Orem Police Department’s page expressing concern with parking issues caused
by high density housing developments.

SCHEDULED ITEMS

        ORDINANCE - Amending the temporary land use regulation (moratorium) previously
adopted by the City Council on August 9, 2021, with respect to the City Center District zones,
the Hub District zones, the Canyon Crossing District zones, the Arts District zones and the North
Village District zones pursuant to Utah Code Section 10-9a-504, to exclude from the moratorium
the following types of construction: interior remodels; exterior remodels-exterior finish
materials, roofing, windows, doors, awnings, canopies, decks, patios, and architectural features
designed solely to enhance the aesthetic appeal of a building; signs; fences and retaining walls;
roof structures for parking and drive-thrus; utility improvement infrastructure (electric, natural
gas, telephone, fiber, water, sewer, storm water); parking lot and driveway improvements; curb,
gutter and sidewalk; lighting improvements; landscaping improvements; cellular infrastructure;
and other structures and improvements that do not create or increase habitable building space
(but in no case to exceed a height of 60 feet); and also to exempt from the moratorium all
construction that is purely commercial (no residential uses) in nature that does not exceed a
height of 60 feet and provides parking of at least one stall per 250 square feet of gross floor area.

Mr. Earl stated these amendments are being made to remedy some issues the passed moratorium
inadvertently created. Some of these changes include allowing commercial remodels, fencing
maintenance, sign maintenance, and developments that are purely commercial in nature.

Mr. Spencer asked Mr. Earl if the lower height standards in the North Village District would be
overruled by the listed 60-foot height maximum. Mr. Earl responded that 60 feet would be the
maximum height, but more restrictive requirements such as those in the NVD Zone would still
stand.

Mr. Peterson moved by Ordinance to amend the temporary land use regulation (moratorium)
previously adopted by the City Council on August 9, 2021, with respect to the City Center
District zones, the Hub District zones, the Canyon Crossing District zones, the Arts District
