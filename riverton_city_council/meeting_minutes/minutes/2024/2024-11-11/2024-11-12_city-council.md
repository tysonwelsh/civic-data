Discussion Items

       Animal Control Discussion

Kevin Hicks, City Manager, provided a presentation on proposed options for establishing a City-
operated animal shelter, highlighting the need for a Council decision to facilitate a budget
adjustment by December 3 and expedite the project. The presentation included a brief history of
the City's animal shelter services, noting a seven-year partnership with Stone Ridge Veterinary
Services that saved the City approximately $1.5 million compared to contracting with Salt Lake
County Animal Services.

The proposal outlined five potential locations for the shelter, all leveraging existing City-owned
land or rental options to minimize costs. These included:

      Station 120: Retrofitting part of an existing fire station while maintaining ambulance
       operations. However, it was noted this location may not serve as a long-term solution
       due to future projections for fire service expansions by 2030.

        Water Shop Land: Building a new facility on City-owned land near the Old Farm Park
         and fishing pond.

        Water Reception Area (Variant of Option 2): Incorporating the water reception area as
         the public-facing component of a new shelter on the same land.

        Garamandi Well Site: Repurposing an abandoned well site, which is already budgeted
         for decommissioning.

        Flex Space Facility: Renting and renovating a local flex space building, following a model
         currently used by Herriman.

Mr. Hicks emphasized the importance of deciding quickly, with temporary solutions such as
continued use of Stone Ridge or temporary support from Herriman available through June 2024.

Mayor Staggs favored the option to have the animal shelter at the Water Shop with the
reception area used as the public entrance. He said this modified approach would reduce costs
rather than building a separate structure for customer entry.

Councilmember McCay said she leaned toward Option B (Water Shop) as the most viable
choice. She cited its affordability compared to other permanent options and its use of an existing
City-owned site, which would maximize utility without additional land acquisition. She
expressed concerns about potential impacts on residents near Sanborn Drive if public access
were routed there and suggested positioning the entrance near the park and pond area.

Councilmember Pierucci raised a question regarding the potential of outsourcing animal shelter
operations to a nonprofit organization. In response, Mr. Hicks confirmed that staff had not
explored the option of a nonprofit partnership and highlighted key concerns with outsourcing
shelter management, including potential boarding costs and loss of direct control over the
shelter's operations and public interactions.

Councilmember Pierucci emphasized his discomfort with the high costs associated with any of
the proposed options relative to the small number of animals and residents served. He
expressed concerns about the cost-effectiveness of the investment, describing it as a point of
personal struggle.

Councilmember McDougal contributed insights on nonprofit animal rescue organizations,
drawing on a family member’s experience in the sector. He explained that these nonprofits
typically intervene when animals are at risk of euthanasia, rather than providing long-term
boarding or shelter services. Many operate with a "no-kill" policy, which could place Riverton in
a similar situation as its current model, with limited capacity for handling animals.

Councilmember McDougal emphasized the need for a permanent, long-term solution for
Riverton’s animal shelter needs, arguing that continued temporary fixes would only lead to
increased costs over time. He expressed support for the option utilizing the water shop with
some modifications, citing its affordability and strategic location.

Councilmember Buroker strongly supported the option involving using the water shop and its
existing reception area. She noted that this setup mirrors the current experience for residents at
Stone Ridge, where interactions are limited to a reception area rather than the animal boarding
space. She believed this continuity would maintain public satisfaction while utilizing a city-owned

         Fee Schedule Discussion

Mayor Staggs initiated the fee schedule discussion by highlighting ongoing efforts to streamline
the City's fee structure. He explained that after reviewing nearly 400 line items, approximately
85 have been eliminated, bringing the total down to around 300. This review excluded impact
fees, which are currently undergoing a separate analysis expected to be completed in the first
part of the year.

Councilmember Pierucci asked whether the proposed false alarm fees are sufficient to
discourage repeat false alarms and whether the fees are adequate to cover the costs of City
staff, including police, responding to these incidents. Mr. Hicks said the he believed the costs

         Youth Council Discussion

Mr. Hicks said the draft ordinance for the Youth Council is modeled after ordinances from other
cities with established youth councils. The primary goal is to formally create the Youth Council
as an official entity, as it has operated informally for over 20 years. The ordinance seeks to
ensure clarity, consistency, and alignment with City policies.

Mr. Hicks said that Youth Council advisors also submitted a scholarship recommendation, but it
requires further refinement.   He will present a proposal by December 3 for Council
consideration.

Councilmember McCay expressed concerns about the proposed 50-member cap for the Youth
Council, suggesting that a higher limit could better accommodate community interest and event
needs. She proposed raising the maximum number to 60 or 80, especially given the large
number of events where Youth Council members assist.

Councilmember McDougal expressed his support for the proposed Youth Council ordinance.
He noted that maintaining a cap on membership may help manage logistical and liability
concerns while preserving the program’s quality and effectiveness.

Councilmember McCay recused herself from this item and left the Council Chambers.

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, presented a development agreement for a
vacant property located at the northeast corner of 13400 South and 2700 West, a parcel
approximately 0.32 acres in size. This property is surrounded by developed properties, with
the large undeveloped tract located to the south across 13400 South. The agreement
proposes to modify the land use and zoning of the property.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that a development agreement combines zoning and specific
standards, including restrictions or allowances, tailored to a specific parcel. In this case, the
proposal is to change the zoning to RM-6, which typically requires a minimum of half an acre.
The application also includes requests for additional units, modifications to setbacks, and
adjustments to fencing requirements, specifically opting for existing fencing standards instead
of the typical solid masonry requirement. Development agreements are supported by Riverton
City and Utah State code but require public hearings, just like a rezone. The Planning

Commission held multiple hearings and ultimately recommended denial of the proposal.

Dan McCay, Applicant, presented the proposed development on a 0.32-acre lot at 2682 West,
13400 South and discussed several factors related to traffic and density, which were concerns
raised during the Planning Commission’s review process.

Regarding traffic, Mr. McCay acknowledged that traffic is a concern in the area, noting that the
Wasatch Front Regional Council’s traffic projections may not account for further development
in the area, such as the nearby Bangerter interchange. He shared data showing that cul-de-
sacs generally generate fewer daily trips compared to streets like 13400 South and 2700
West, which have much higher traffic volumes. A traffic engineer had advised that the
westward exit would be safer for ingress and egress due to better visibility and lower speeds,
while the eastward exit would pose more challenges due to traffic congestion and limited
visibility.

Mr. McCay also addressed concerns about density, noting that current zoning allows for
single-family homes and accessory dwelling units (ADUs), which could be built without
needing the development agreement. However, he proposed a layout that would allow for two
single-family homes with shared walls, increasing yard space while also ensuring safer traffic
flow on the lot. The design also took into account maintaining visibility at the corner, which had
been a concern for traffic safety.

Councilmember Pierucci asked the applicant why he was seeking a development agreement
as opposed to a rezone.

In response, Mr. McCay shared concerns about the potential issues that might arise from
modifying zoning regulations for a single lot. He expressed support for higher-density zoning
along high-traffic corridors like Redwood Road. He also highlighted the difficulty of adjusting
zoning codes for this one lot without unintentionally creating inconsistencies or opening the
door for broader, less controlled zoning changes throughout the City. He concluded that a
development agreement would be a practical tool to handle the specific issues related to this
lot, while still allowing for flexibility in addressing its unique circumstances.

Councilmember Buroker asked how much living space would be possible under the current
zoning. In response, Mr. McCay noted that with the inclusion of an accessory dwelling unit
(ADU), the total square footage could be around 1,400 square feet, which aligns with the lot's
space restrictions.

         Ordinance No. 24-22 – Amending Section 18.225.020(h), Accessory Structures
         General Standards, regulating use of storage/cargo containers as described,
         amendments proposed by Riverton City

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the current ordinance prohibits cargo containers for storage
purposes, except temporarily during a move. However, an interpretation had been made by
staff allowing cargo containers to serve as internal structures for accessory buildings, provided
their exteriors conformed to existing requirements for accessory structures.

After prior discussions with the City Council, staff prepared amendments to formalize this
interpretation. These amendments proposed that cargo containers could be used internally for
accessory structures if their exteriors were finished to meet city standards, allowing the roof
and doors to retain the original material.

The Planning Commission reviewed the proposed amendments over several meetings and
ultimately recommended a different approach. They suggested that cargo containers could be
allowed as accessory structures if they met specific conditions:

        The containers must comply with existing accessory structure regulations, such as
         placement, size, and square footage.
        They must be painted a single color consistent with the primary dwelling.
        Logos, alphanumeric characters, and other markings must be removed.
        The containers must be maintained free of rust or damage.
        Temporary storage containers used during moves were to remain restricted to seven
         days and required to be placed on private property.

Councilmember McDougal expressed support for the proposed amendments regarding cargo
containers as accessory structures but objected to the provision allowing storage containers to
be used with only a coat of paint. He argued that while allowances were already being made
for the roof and doors, the containers should also be clad with materials consistent with the
standards for sheds and garages outlined in the existing code.

Councilmember McCay agreed with the Planning Commission’s recommendation.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;

Jeremy Sorenson commented that he agreed with Councilmember McCay’s comments.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Mr. Lethbridge explained that the proposed update would clarify inconsistencies in the
Planning Commission code. The current code mandates bi-monthly meetings using the term
"shall," which implies meetings must occur, even during holidays. The proposed amendment
specifies that meetings are generally held on the second and fourth Thursdays but may be
rescheduled for reasons such as holiday conflicts, lack of agenda items, or joint discussions
with the Council, provided proper notice is given.

Additionally, the code includes language about Planning Commission members issuing
concurring or dissenting reports or recommendations, which has not been practiced in over 25
years. Instead, members express their agreement or dissent through votes and comments
without separate recommendations or motions.

The update, not classified as a land use item, did not require Planning Commission input and
was presented directly to the Council for approval.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Public Hearing.

Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 24-23 –
Amending Riverton City Code, Section 2.95.060 Organizations, Actions, and
Recommendations of the Planning Commission with the change that we include the
language that states that any member of the Commission may also make a concurring
or dissenting recommendation. Councilmember McCay SECONDED the motion. Mayor
Staggs called for discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote
was as follows: Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes and Pierucci-yes.
The motion passed unanimously.

ordinance should specify the roles of elected officers to ensure clarity and maintain Council
oversight. This would prevent the creation of programs, such as a Youth Court, without the
Council's knowledge or approval. He proposed simplifying the process by having officers
appointed by the advisors, or jointly by the advisors and a designee of the city manager, based

impacting the quality of the program.

80. She expressed support for allowing older kids to participate in training opportunities, while
younger participants could contribute to community events like Town Days and Halloween
activities. She supported the idea that advisors, who have direct knowledge of the youth, should
have input in the decision-making process regarding leadership positions and proposed a
combination of a vote and advisor input.

Mayor Staggs emphasized the importance of maintaining a manageable group size to preserve
the prestige of being a Youth Council member and to manage the available budget effectively.

process, where advisors would first develop a list of candidates, and then the Youth Council
