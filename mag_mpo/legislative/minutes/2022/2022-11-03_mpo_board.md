Details found at: https://arcg.is/1vOXzr MPO Regional Planning Committee November 3, 2022 Page 4 RTP Draft Projects List Approval – Kenndall Willardson, MAG (1:04:23) Every four years the Regional Transportation Plan (RTP) is updated to reflect the changing needs of Utah County and to be in compliance with federal regulations. The RTP will include Highway, Transit, and Active Transportation Projects. In its current form only highway and transit projects are listed while active transportation will be finished pending the completion of a study by the end of the year. Costs for highways are listed while the MPO is in the process of updating transit costs with UTA in the coming months. The transportation projects in the list are needs based. In the coming month as part of air quality conformance, the projects will also be fiscally constrained by comparing transportation costs against revenues and phasing projects where they are fiscally conceivable. It is anticipated that many projects needs will not be met due to updated revenue sources. Mayor Anderson moved to approve the RTP draft listing of projects. This approval allows MPO staff to present the needs-based list knowing that it is a draft and further refinement will occur. Mayor Fugal seconded the motion. Mayor Olson and Mayor Larsen objected. The motion passed unanimously. Mayor Kaufusi opened the motion for discussion allowing the Committee members to express their issues and concerns regarding the Utah Lake bridge. She reported that MAG has scheduled a meeting with Provo and Orem to discuss concerns about the Utah Lake Bridge landing on November 14 th. UPWP Annual Performance and Expenditure Report – Shawn Eliot, MAG (1:31:20) Shawn Eliot reported that the annual Unified Planning Work Program describes the transportation planning work within the Mountainland MPO. The program gives a general overview of the planning work to be accomplished over the planning year by describing the planned work, budget, and funding sources. The Annual Performance and Expenditure Report describes the planning activities conducted and the funding expended for FY 2022 (July 2021- June 2022). Overall, MAG budgeted $2,851,655 for the year and expended $2,483,967, leaving a balance of $367,689, which is reprogrammed in the following year's Work Program. MPO Regional Planning Committee November 3, 2022 Page 5 2023 Proposed TAC/RPC Meeting Dates and Bylaws Update Approval – Shawn Eliot, MAG (1:34:27) Mountainland MPO Bylaws state that MPO RPC and the MPO TAC meet monthly. In reviewing past meeting and cancelations, and reviewing workload, MAG staff recommends that both committees go to a bi-monthly schedule. On average, four of twelve meetings annually are canceled. Staff believes that going to a bi-monthly schedule and allowing the chair to call for non-scheduled meetings, committee members time can be better used. With this change, the 2023 meeting schedule is attached. In proposing this change, the MPO Bylaws have been reviewed. The changes/clarifications being proposed were presented MAG Staff recommends the changes proposed: • MPO committee meetings meet bi-monthly (try for a year and revisit to see if adjustments need to be made) • Make proposal to clarify that a local government alternate member to the MPO Board be either elected officials only or allow both elected officials and staff. • Make a proposal to keep MPO Board and MPO TAC leader’s terms as currently listed or change them each to one-year or two-year terms. MPO TAC recommended the proposed changes by staff except: • Keep meetings monthly, concerns with project cost overrun funding not being available in a timely manner • Let MPO Board alternate be a senior staff person • Let MPO Board decide how long the chair can serve on their committee (TAC wants to keep theirs every two years) Mayor Johnson moved that the Mountainland MPO Bylaws be changed as proposed: adopt the 2023 meeting calendar, that Section 2.3 (e) be changed to clarify that a local government alternate member be an administrative person or another elected official selected by the original appointee, and the MPO Board and TAC leader’s terms stay as is. Mayor Westmoreland seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously. Corridor Preservation Request 820 North Provo – Jim Price, MAG (1:56:15) Jim Price explained that the Corridor Preservation Fund Program is intended to protect future ROW, relief induced hardships and save taxpayer dollars. Funding comes from revenue from the vehicle registration, totaling approximately $4.5m annually. Provo City is requesting funds to purchase an existing residence located at 1280 West Camelot Dr. Corridor #38, 820 N Provo MPO Regional Planning Committee November 3, 2022 Page 6 Appraised Value: $665,000 Estimated Closing Costs: $20,000 Total Request: $685,000 This request is within the purpose and policies of the Corridor Preservation Fund Program; the fund has an adequate balance, and the property is apparently needed for future widening of 820 North. While this appraisal is revised from an earlier one and reflects an increase in valuation to close a gap between the original appraisal and one provided by the property owner, the adjustment looks reasonable on its face.
