         The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agenda for the City Council meeting scheduled to begin at 6:00
p.m.; discuss proposed amendments to the Diversity Commission ordinance; discuss a proposed zoning text amendment and
master plan update for Ogden Bend; discuss the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 budget specific to the budget message and goals and
guidelines; discuss Council/Board business; and hear Council/Board comments.

Agenda Review
        Chair Richey and members of Council staff reviewed the items listed on the agenda for the City Council meeting
scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m.

Proposed Amendments to Diversity Commission Ordinance
         This item was not discussed and will be included on the August 6 work session agenda.
         Council members Myers and Choberka communicated concerns that they have regarding amendments to the Diversity
Commission including changes to language included in the ordinance and the terms of members of the Commission. Council
Executive Director Eller-Smith stated that those concerns will be provided to Administration in advance of the discussion of the
Diversity Commission in the next work session meeting.

Proposed Ogden Ben Mixed-Use Zoning Text Amendment; and
Ogden Bend Master Plan updates
         Planning Manager Brierley explained the Community and Economic Development Department has proposed a zoning
ordinance text amendment to the mixed-use zoning standards. The proposal includes a request to make the following changes to
the code, to conform to the Ogden Bend Master Plan updates:
              1. Modify building types allowed;
              2. Modify maximum building lengths fronting the street; and
              3. Establish sign standards.
         Mr. Brierley used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to orient the Council to the area encompassed in the Ogden Bend
Master Plan area; summarize the mixed-use zoning procedures; review proposed amendments to the concept plan section of the
Master Plan; and to expound on the implications of the proposed code amendments. He concluded the Planning Commission
recommended the following change pertaining to mixed-residential areas: at least one building type comprising at least 25 percent
of the buildings or dwelling units shall be platted or condominiumized to allow ownership of individual dwelling units. Lotus
Development, a property owner in the Plan area, requested the following:
              • Allow surface parking;
                • Allow six-story building heights at 18th Street and Wall Avenue;
                • Allow 10-foot-high ground floor and nine-foot-high upper floor;
                • Less than 10-foot setbacks on Wall Avenue and 18th Street;
                • Allow buildings that ‘appear’ to be separated.
           The Planning Commission made the following recommendations responsive to Lotus’ requests:
                • Plan already allows surface parking;
                • Changed from 4 story maximum to 5 story maximum;
                • Changed first floor height from 14-feet to 10-feet;
                • Maintain 10-foot setbacks on Wall Avenue and 18th Street;
                • Buildings along Wall Avenue, Washington Boulevard, and 20th Street can “appear” to be separated.
           With the proposed changes, the Planning Commission has recommended the Redevelopment Agency (RDA) adopt the
project Master Plan amendment and that the Council adopt the proposed zoning text amendments. For a copy of the presentation
in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
           Mr. Brierley engaged in discussion with the Council regarding changes in ownership of properties located within the
Master Plan area; configuration of the open space/park areas in the Plan area; walkability of the plan area/pedestrian circulation;
and parking accommodations throughout the Plan area.
           Chair Richey concluded that the two applications will be presented for consideration and action on August 20, 2024, and
he invited the Council to direct any additional questions they may have to Planning staff.

Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 Budget
         Proposed Budget Message: Communication and Public Engagement Coordinator Garside facilitated the Council’s
review of the proposed budget message for the FY 2024-2025 budget, which includes a message from the Council Chair, a budget
‘snapshot’, information regarding the relationship between the five-year Strategic Plan, property tax increases and the property tax
disbursement, budget highlights, funding for Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) projects, and Council budget priorities. For a copy
of the budget message in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Proposed Goals and Guidelines: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
review changes to the proposed FY2024-2025 goals and guidelines when compared to the goals and guidelines for the last FY:
              • Comprehensive Financial Sustainability Model. Adds reference to the comprehensive financial sustainability
                  model (CFSM) and indicates that it is the Council’s intent to consolidate the CFSM with the newly updated
                  General Plan and other key policy documents for use as the updated Five-Year Strategic Plan.
              • Salaries and Benefits. Adds language stating that unexpended salary and benefit funds shall be returned to
                  fund balance to be reappropriated for salaries and benefits in the new fiscal year.
              • Certified Tax Rate Evaluation. Changes evaluation of the certified tax rate from a biannual review to an
                  annual review.
         Ms. Eller-Smith also summarized the goals pertaining to economic development, community safety, recreation, City
image and reputation, and fiscal sustainability. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the
meeting.
         General Budget Discussion: There was no additional discussion of specific budget items; however, for the benefit of the
newest members of the Council, the Council and Council staff explained the format of the meeting during with the truth in
taxation hearing will be held. The Council thanked Council staff for being a valuable resource throughout the entire budget
preparation process.

Council Business
         Council staff reviewed the Council calendar for the month of August.
         Chair Richey drew the Council’s attention to an email from Mayor Caldwell announcing Police Chief Young’s
retirement.


         The meeting adjourned at 5:09 p.m.
