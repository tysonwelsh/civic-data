     FILED BY WEST VALLEY CITY, REQUESTING AN AMENDMENT TO
     SECTION 7-2-115 OF THE ZONING ORDINANCE TO CLARIFY THE
     MEASUREMENT STANDARDS FOR A COMMERCIAL VEHICLE
     FROM THE "GROSS WEIGHT" TO THE "GROSS VEHICLE WEIGHT
     RATING" AND ESTABLISH THE ALLOWED VEHICLE CLASS SIZE
     FOR STORAGE OF VEHICLES IN AGRICULTURAL AND
     RESIDENTIAL ZONES
     Mayor Lang informed a public hearing had been advertised for the Regular Council
     Meeting scheduled April 4, 2023, in order for the City Council to hear and consider
     public comments regarding Application ZT-1-2023, Filed by West Valley City,
     Requesting an Amendment to Section 7-2-115 of the Zoning Ordinance to Clarify the
     Measurement Standards for a Commercial Vehicle from the "Gross Weight" to the
     "Gross Vehicle Weight Rating" and Establish the Allowed Vehicle Class Size for
     Storage of Vehicles in Agricultural and Residential Zones.

       Proposed Ordinance 23-09 related to the proposal to be considered by the City
       Council subsequent to the public hearing, was discussed as follows:

       ACTION: ORDINANCE NO. 23-09, AMEND SECTION 7-2-115 OF THE
       WEST VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND RESTRICTIONS ON
       PARKING COMMERCIAL VEHICLES IN RESIDENTIAL AND
       AGRICULTURAL ZONES
       Eric Bunderson, City Attorney, discussed proposed Ordinance 23-09 that would
       amend Section 7-2-115 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Amend Restrictions
       on Parking Commercial Vehicles in Residential and Agricultural Zones.

       Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included
       information as follows:
      MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – APRIL 4, 2023
                           -3-


              West Valley City code 7-2-115 currently prohibits the storage of
              commercial vehicles within any residential or agricultural zones. However,
              the current definition utilizes a measurement method that is difficult to
              ascertain and creates no exceptions for recreational vehicles, government
              vehicles, or emergency vehicles.

              Changes to 7-2-115(1) will change the measurement for a commercial
              vehicle from the “gross weight” to the “gross vehicle weight rating”. The
              gross vehicle weight rating is a more readily available number, and the
              change will allow code enforcement officers to be more consistent and
              accurate during enforcement. The new definition of a commercial vehicle
              will include any vehicle with a gross vehicle weight rating of over 14,000
              pounds with exceptions for recreational vehicles, emergency vehicles, and
              government vehicles.

       Councilmember Christensen asked if a tow truck would now be considered
       overweight. Eric replied that most tow trucks would be overweight under the
       proposed ordinance. The Mayor and Council discussed pick-up truck sizes and
       RV’s. Mayor Lang asked what a state or local government vehicle would apply to.
       Eric replied something like a bus. Mayor Lang expressed concern about furniture
       companies delivering equipment and not actively loading/unloading. She stated that
       she would like the ordinance to allow for a three-hour window. Councilmember
       Nordfelt stated this would be difficult to enforce. Mayor Lang stated that officers
       can dive their route and come back to check. She indicated that she doesn’t want
       furniture companies or other similar services to not deliver in West Valley City
       because they receive tickets. Councilmember Whetstone suggested a 30-minute
       timeframe. Councilmember Fitisemanu suggested eliminating the time-frame so
       that the Code simply states actively loading, unloading, or installing. The Mayor
       and Council agreed. Mayor Lang stated that she would like to allow F-550’s.
       Councilmember Nordfelt expressed concern that would then allow box trucks and
       tow trucks. After discussion, the Council agreed to allow pick-ups of any weight or
       size but keep all other weight requirements as proposed.

       The City Council will consider Ordinance 23-09 at the Regular Council Meeting
       scheduled April 4, 2023 at 6:30 P.M.

ORDINANCE 23-10: AMEND SECTIONS 22-2-116 AND 22-2-203 OF THE WEST
VALLEY CITY MUNICIPAL CODE TO AMEND CERTAIN PARKING
RESTRICTIONS AND CIVIL PENALTIES
Eric Bunderson, City Attorney, presented proposed Ordinance 23-10 that would amend
Sections 22-2-116 and 22-2-203 of the West Valley City Municipal Code to Amend Certain
Parking Restrictions and Civil Penalties.
       MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – APRIL 4, 2023
                            -4-



Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       If approved, this ordinance will amend Subsections 22-2-116 and 22-2-203.

       Changes to 22-2-116(2) alters the existing defense for parking recreational
       vehicles on the street for 24 hours by requiring it to be near the owner’s residence.
       It creates a new affirmative defense that will allow individuals to park recreational
       vehicles on the street next to their own residence for up to 7 days to allow for real
       property maintenance.

       Changes to 22-2-116(4) will change the measurement for a commercial vehicle
       from the “gross weight of a vehicle” to the “gross vehicle weight rating”. The
       gross vehicle weight rating is a more readily available number, and the change
       will allow the parking officers to be more consistent and accurate during
       enforcement. The new definition of a commercial vehicle will include any vehicle
       with a gross vehicle weight rating of over 14,000 pounds with exceptions for
       recreational vehicles, emergency vehicles, and government vehicles.

       Changes to 22-2-203 will increase the civil penalties for repetitive offenders by
       $50.00 each citation within a 12-month period.

The Mayor and Council agreed to allow pick-up trucks of any size on the street as well to
align with the previous discussion. After further discussion, the Council stated that they
would like to add that the trucks have factory beds and not extended cabs when parking on
the street.

The City Council will consider Ordinance 23-10 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
April 4, 2023 at 6:30 P.M

RESOLUTION 23-43: APPROVE MODIFICATION NO. 1 TO A FEDERAL AID
AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITY AND THE UTAH DEPARTMENT OF
TRANSPORTATION FOR THE CROSS TOWNE TRAIL
Coby Wilson, Public Works, presented proposed Resolution 23-43 that would approve
Modification No. 1 to a Federal Aid Agreement Between the City and the Utah Department of
Transportation for the Cross Towne Trail.

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:
      MINUTES OF COUNCIL STUDY MEETING – APRIL 4, 2023
                           -5-


       The West Valley City Engineering Division is in the process of designing a segment
       of the Cross Towne Trail along Parkway Boulevard. This project will close gaps in
       the trail between Bangerter Highway and 2700 West. Improvements include the
       addition of sidewalk where needed along with minor roadway widening to
       accommodate new bike lanes in Parkway Blvd. In addition, a pavement
       preservation treatment will be applied to the entire roadway within the project
       limits.

       The current project estimate is just under $2.4M. The City previously received
       $484,241 of federal Transportation Alternative Program (TAP) funds and
       $1,350,000 of County Transportation funds for use on this project. This
       modification is for an additional $321,889 of TAP funds that the city requested in
       2022. TAP funds require a local government match of 6.77% ($54,575) and the
       City is responsible for the difference between the available funding and the project
       total cost, currently estimated to be $282,645, which will be paid with local
       transportation funds.

       It is anticipated that the design and right-of-way acquisition will continue thru 2023
       and construction will begin in 2024.

The City Council will consider Resolution 23-43 at the Regular Council Meeting scheduled
April 11, 2023 at 6:30 P.M

about cars and tires being stored in someone’s backyard. She indicated that she
would consider this a health hazard that the City could enforce. Wayne replied that
it could be but noted that there are different definitions and variations of what a
health hazard could be. He indicated that the Health Department may have different

        Enforcement process: When 5 garage sales have been verified,
