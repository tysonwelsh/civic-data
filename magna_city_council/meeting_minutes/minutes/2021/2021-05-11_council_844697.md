Magna Chamber of Commerce

                Sara VanRoosendaal stated the Chamber reached out to Salt Lake County and
has gotten approval for the ICAP, whereby funds received will be used for diversity and inclusion,
and to get infrastructure in place. She also spoke about the chamber’s business sign advertising.

                                 −−−−−−−−−−−−−−
Magna United / Magna Community that Cares

                Kellen Schalter stated the coalition is working on its Community Action Plan and
will probably present that mid-July. It has decided on the following programs for support in the
community: Me Time,” a program for students experiencing mild depressive symptoms; Guiding
Good Choices; Too Good for Drugs and Too Good for Violence, which will be done in after school
programs; and Vocational and Lifeskills, which the State Legislature mandated, and for which the
collation will be trained to help with. The coalition is going to be writing an action plan on
implementing the programs and how to use funding to get them all in the community in the next
year. Then, vape detectors are getting installed in Matheson Junior High School on Monday, May
17, 2021.

Ordinance

              Paul Ashton reviewed the following application, which was heard during the April
27, 2021, Council meeting. The public hearing was closed; this is a continuation of the discussion.

Application #OAM2021 – Ordinance #21-04-01 to consider amendments to Chapter 15.14 of the
Magna Municipal Code governing special development standards in the Magna Overpressure
Zone. The amendments intend to clarify the boundaries of the area, outline conditions required
for building and developing within the Overpressure Zone and moves this section of code to
Chapter 16.02.

              Travis Hair, Planner, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, stated the
Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District has made the changes to the ordinance that the
Council requested. The changes were in 16-02-130 Zoning and Conditional Uses in the
Overpressure Areas, Sections B, C, and G.

               Jay Springer, Legal Counsel, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, stated
the three changes were to 1) allow industrial developments up to 60 feet, and the Greater Salt
Lake Municipal Services District (GSLMSD) did confer with Northrop Grumman on this change;
2) modify the per acre lot size to maintain the 6 units per acre for residential units within the zone,
and that was consistent with making it more applicable to a gross per acre unit, which would allow
for public developments, streets, sidewalks and those things to be accounted for in those
developments; and 3) require any future development of any kind that is affected by the
Overpressure Zone to get approval from the director as a condition of approval, in this case the

would be affected by this ordinance. Those changes have been incorporated into the final draft

               Council Member Peel asked if Fastenal had signed off on these changes.
