1. Public Hearings
1.1 Consideration of ZM-19-016, Rezone from R-1-8 to R-1-6 to Allow for Each
Residence to be Located on a Separate Parcel by Utilizing the Flag Lot Policy Location:
1643-1647 E. Woodland Avenue Applicant: Greg Sneyd Planner: Robert May
Robert May said the property currently had two homes on it, and in order to put each home
on a lot, it needed to be subdivided under a denser zone. He highlighted the property on a
map for the Commission. He said the northern lot currently had access on the Jean Massieu
School of the Deaf property. The subdivision would grant the northern lot an access to the
south instead through a flag lot. May said the flag lot policy requires 20-foot setbacks on all
sides. The current house on the northern lot does not meet that requirement, so it would be
demolished, and a new home would be built. The Canyon Rim Citizens Association and East
Mill Creek Community Council each recommended approval of the rezone at their meetings
in October. The planning staff found that with a development agreement limiting the
proposed rezone to a 2-lot flag lot subdivision as per plan and requiring the removal of the

existing home to the north before a subdivision plat is recorded, it would guarantee the intent
of the proposal and ensure zoning compliance. Commissioner Mumford asked about a plan
for the new home. May said there was a building footprint, but no floor plan yet. The

license that could be terminated at any time. Commissioner Mumford asked how the property
arrangements were made. Sneyd said his son knew the property owner. He said the real estate
purchase contract was in the works.

Commissioner Reid said the staff report referenced protection of the tree canopy. Sneyd said
most of the trees were along the perimeter of the property but one tree would be removed due
to the location of the new house. Sneyd said he could replace the tree elsewhere on the
property.

Vice Chair Stephens opened the public hearing.

Marv Poulson, 3631 Carolyn Street, thanked Commissioner Reid for referencing the trees
because the City needs as many trees as possible.

Vice Chair Stephens closed the public hearing.

Commissioner LaMar and Claerhout said they liked the proposal. Commissioner Mumford
said he would like a formal property agreement with the neighbors in place before moving
forward with the application. May said without the agreement, the application could not
come back for a subdivision. He added that the application should be subject to Millcreek
Code 19.77.040 for tree preservation.

Commissioner Booth, in consideration of ZM-19-016 rezone from R-1-8 to R-1-6, moved to

recommend acceptance. Commissioner Reid seconded. John Brems suggested adding a

zoning condition for only allowing two dwelling units. May said the density would not allow for

more than two residences. Francis Lilly said the northern lot would be noncompliant with the
existing home which is why the development agreement addresses that the noncomplying
structure be removed. Commissioner Booth moved to amend his motion to recommend
approval of ZM-19-016, a rezone from R-1-8 to R-1-6, and that it be subject to all
conditions and stipulations in the development agreement and in the staff report. The staff
report included the following two conditions: 1) limiting the proposed rezone to a 2-lot flag lot

subdivision as per plan; and 2) requiring the removal of the existing home to the north before a

subdivision plat is recorded

Commissioner Reid seconded the amendment. Vice Chair Stephens called for the vote. All

Commissioners voted yes. The motion passed unanimously.

1.2 Consideration of CU-19-004, Conditional Use Permit for a Mixed-Use Multifamily
Development Consisting of 48 Dwellings and 980 Square Feet of Retail Space Location:
949-959 E. 3300 S. Applicant: True Land Company Planner: Erin O’Kelley
Erin O’Kelley said the staff report reflected an application of 49 dwelling units and 1267
square feet of commercial space along 3300 S. The applicant first made application for the
project in May. The application went to the Millcreek Community Council and the applicant
was given feedback from concerned residents. Then the applicant commissioned a traffic and
parking study and worked on mitigations. She said staff was recommending continuance of
the item and then went on to explain the project. The subject property is two lots along 3300
S. One property is currently vacant, the other property is commercial with a small warehouse
space to the north. The property has sidewalk along 3300 S. but no curb or gutter
improvements. The property line running along Lincoln Street (945 E.) has no improvements
for curb, gutter or sidewalk. The surrounding property uses are commercial and residential.
She said residents previously expressed concern about the narrowness of Lincoln Street. The
proposed project includes full curb, gutter, and sidewalk added to public streets with
additional angled parking along Lincoln Street, ingress/egress access to the north of the
property, commercial space on the corner and along 3300 S., 1-bedroom and studio unit
types, public and private patios, and landscaping along public streets and pedestrian
pathways.
O’Kelley said the elevations presented showed notes that incorporated mitigations based on
the residents’ concerns. The applicant had previously held neighborhood meetings and the
main concerns raised were about traffic, access, parking, privacy, security, fencing, lighting,
and noise. The Millcreek Community Council had similar concerns at their meeting on
September 3, 2019 and recommended denial of the project because of the traffic/parking and
road development on Lincoln Street. She said the applicant wanted to use a shared parking
plan per Millcreek Code 19.80.080 and then a parking exception per 19.80.090. She
presented a table of shared parking requirements and said there would parking agreement
conditions. Commissioner LaMar asked about ordinance parking requirements. O’Kelley
said if the applicant did parking for uses on only the property 92 stalls would be required, but
by doing a shared agreement, 104 stalls would be needed in conjunction with the Maliheh
Clinic to the west. The applicant has proposed 72 stalls on the property and wanted to use 20
stalls from Maliheh Clinic. O’Kelley said the applicant was changing the parking proposal
and informed O’Kelley two hours before the start of the meeting. Vice Chair Stephens said
the Planning Commission makes decisions based on staff reports, so the applicant should not
make last minute changes. O’Kelley said the staff report referenced mitigations for privacy,
lighting, fencing, and traffic. Commissioner Reid asked about the property proximity to the
bus stop. O’Kelley said the bus stop was about 300 feet away. Commissioner LaMar asked
about the required open space. O’Kelley said private balconies could count towards the open
space in this zone. Francis Lilly said the developer would document the open space with the
application moving forward. Vice Chair Stephens asked if the City had a minimum duration
standard for shared parking agreements. Lilly said the standard was three years, but it should
be longer. Commissioner LaMar asked about the frontage being on 3300 S. instead of 945 E.
O’Kelley said it was based on the building floorplan configuration. Commissioner LaMar
said the clarification for which side to base the 50% commercial frontage requirement needed
to be made.

Applicant — Bryan Bayles said the area was conducive to his development by proximal
similarly scaled developments, and access to transit and services. He said 3300 S. did not
have a park strip but the proposed development would include one. He said the angled
parking on 945 E. would naturally slow traffic down. He said the 30-foot height in the rear of
the building would from be existing grade. The developers added architectural variation to
the proposed building to make it not look like one building. He said he had met with
neighbors and tried to address their concerns by adding a privacy fence, raising windowsills
to 42 inches, and moving the trash enclosure away from existing residences and limiting the
hours of pick up. The north fencing would be replaced with a 6-foot solid fence. He said
parking was a problem, so there will be almost 8 feet of road dedication to accommodate for
future redevelopment of the Maliheh Clinic building and adding angled street parking. He
said he was willing to make public improvements on 945 E. though Millcreek 9 Apartments
developers were not required to install them. He said he dropped the requested number of
units from 49 to 48 units and changed the number of studio and 1-bedroom apartment units.
The change would influence the required parking stall count which was due to Maliheh
Clinic being unwilling to share parking. He said he was proposing a reduction in parking.
The units would be 16 studio, 28 1-bedroom, and 4 2-bedroom which would reduce the
parking stall requirements to 81 stalls plus 9 stalls for the commercial. He asked for a
reduction from the Commission to 72 stalls. Vice Chair Stephens said he needed to see the
final parking stall numbers and details in the next staff report. Commissioner Reid brought
up different target demographics with the unit change. Bayles said the target residents would
be millennials and baby boomers. Commissioner Sieber asked about the bike area. Bayles
said it would be inside a covered garage. Commissioner Claerhout brought up the idea of
reducing the number of units to meet parking needs. Bayles said he needed a certain number
of units to have the property purchase make financial sense.

Wayne Johnson, Millcreek Community Council, said the Community Council could not
approve a project that was not done. He said the Council had concerns about traffic and
parking. The traffic study assumed all traffic would go onto 3300 S. and the angled parking
on 945 E. was not accounted for. He expressed concern about all traffic going to the north
and the size of the road.

Vice Chair Stephens opened the public hearing.

Diane Wenzl, 3240 S. 1000 E., asked if she could give comments again at another public
hearing since the application was being continued. Vice Chair Stephens said yes. Wenzl said
Maliheh Clinic was currently using the empty lot (subject property) as overflow parking for
their employees and patients. She then presented pictures of the problems to the Commission.
She presented a collision map of accidents in the area in a 4-year period which indicated
about 20 accidents per year. She said off street parking was insufficient with the reduced
amount of 72 parking stalls and 116 stalls was needed during peak times. She mentioned that
ADA compliant parking spaces must be added to the requirement, taking the count to 75
stalls. She said the parking study was not site specific and did not consider the Maliheh
Clinic. The recommendation in the traffic study was not valid for the Lincoln Street site
because it was conducted 6 months before the clinic was there (2018) and was based on
number of units, not bedrooms. She said the proposed project density would be greater than
anything else on the street. Commissioner Stephens asked that her comments be provided in
advance for inclusion in the next staff report.

Vice Chair Stephens closed the public hearing.
Commissioner LaMar said a reduction in parking by 20% would be too much (what was
proposed by the applicant), so a reduction in units might be needed. He said this residential
project with minimal commercial was not the intent of the commercial zone. He considered
the parking structure as part of the residential, so the building should have 50% commercial
north to south. Commissioner Booth said he liked the idea of the project but not the lack of
parking. Vice Chair Stephens said the angled parking points to the north, so traffic would
head north, and the clinic used a lot of parking space. Commissioner Mumford asked Lilly if
there was a scheduled improvement plan for 945 E. Lilly said Lincoln Street did not show up
as a dedicated right-of-way until around the year 2000. He said the developer would widen
and fix the bottleneck problem on 945 E. He said the road was substandard, but there are no
plans to improve it. He said parking and traffic studies look at general circumstances, but this
site was unique. He said the Planning Commission did not have to accept the findings of the
parking study. Vice Chair Stephens said the study did not consider the number of bedrooms
and occupancy rates for comparisons. Commissioner LaMar said the parking study
recommended more parking than what the ordinance required. Commissioner Reid said the
Maliheh Clinic did not have enough parking. Lilly said it would be the developer’s burden to
justify a reduction in parking. Commissioner LaMar asked why 3300 S. was used for
commercial frontage and not 945 E. when most of the building fronts on it. Lilly said he
would look into it. Vice Chair Stephens asked if part of the commercial space could be used
as a leasing office for the residential units. Lilly said he did not think leasing was a
commercial use unless it was for space off site.
Commissioner LaMar, regarding CU-19-004, moved to continue the application to a future
date so the applicant could work with staff on finalizing the application. Commissioner
Mumford seconded. Vice Chair Stephens called for the vote. All Commissioners voted yes.
The motion passed unanimously.
