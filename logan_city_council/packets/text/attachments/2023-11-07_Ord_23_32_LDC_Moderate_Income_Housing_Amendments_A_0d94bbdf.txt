 Dear Logan Municipal Council,



 Recently the Planning Commission passed a recommendation to modify the land
 development code from an occupancy limit of three individuals to four. This is very
 disappointing for several reasons which I will detail below. However, the purpose of this
 email is to state my conviction that your job as elected officials is to reject this proposed
 change.



 Point #1: The city staff does not understand the impact that this change will have.

 Details: At the planning commission meeting, it was stated repeatedly by city staff that
 the only reason that the over-occupancy was enforced was related to other
 neighborhood impacts, such as noise, parking, lack of upkeep, etc. In other words,
 according to city staff, residents complain about over-occupancy only when other factors
 such as noise, parking, etc. arise. This, of course, is completely false and demonstrates
 a complete lack of understanding of the issue. Occupancy enforcement has a direct
 correlation to property use. For example, let’s say the occupancy limit were a family or
 TWO individuals instead of the current three. If someone wanted to invest in a property
 to rent out, knowing that it could only be rented to two individuals would drastically
 change the profitability of the investment. Therefore, the investor wouldn’t be willing to
 pay as much for the dwelling, which then makes the dwelling more affordable for a family
 to buy.
Now consider the opposite that is being proposed: changing the occupancy limit from
three to four will increase the potential income from individuals. When a dwelling comes
up for sale, investors will be willing to pay more for the dwelling, making it LESS
affordable for a family wanting to buy that house.

In summary, changing from three to four will benefit investors and individuals while being
severely detrimental to single-family neighborhoods, and make it harder for and LESS
affordable for families. Yet this is being proposed as a measure to make housing more
affordable!



Point #2: This is in direct conflict with published plans by Logan City.

Details: Consider the Logan General Plan, found at the following link:

https://cms9files.revize.com/loganut/document_center/Planning%20Zoning/
LoganGenPlan%20v20%20low%20for%20web.pdf

Pages 10-1 and 10-2:

“Many neighborhoods have a mix of owner occupied and rental housing. While a mix of
rental/owner occupied housing helps ensure choice, when the proportion of rentals is
high enough that the neighborhood reaches a “tipping point”, that encourages the rest of
the ownership units to be converted to rentals. The “tipping point” ratio varies according
to maintenance levels and types of occupancy (students vs. families), but when reached
it causes homeowners to lose confidence that their property values will be maintained,
causing homeowners to leave. Neighborhood deterioration may result.”

“The following steps can bring significant, positive improvement to our neighborhoods:

      1. Increase number of owner occupied dwelling units.”

This change will NOT “increase the number of owner occupied dwelling units”. The ONLY
demographic that this change will benefit are individuals that buy and then rent to other
individuals while the owner lives in the house. While I acknowledge that this would
indeed happen, our experience over the last 20 years indicates that this wouldn’t happen
very often and the vast majority would be investor-owned dwelling units, NOT owner-
occupied.



Now consider the Adams Neighborhood Specific Plan available at the following link:

https://cms9files.revize.com/loganut/docs/Adams%20Neighborhood.pdf

From the Executive Summary:
 “Single family dwellings once oriented towards family ownership and stability has given
 way to multi-family dwellings catering to a younger generation of students. Home
 ownership has evolved into investment property ownership, both with competing goals of
 neighborhood stabilization and return on investment. The challenge of this planning effort
 is to harness the positive aspects of each seemingly competing need by defining and
 implementing land use and regulatory policies that protect the neighborhood character
 while encourage new investment in the Adams Neighborhood.”

 “The Adams Neighborhood Plan envisions a healthy, stable and vibrant neighborhood
 where a mixture of housing types and styles, families and students, old and new can
 blend and continue to lend to the unique character of the neighborhood. Key elements
 explored within the Adams Neighborhood Plan include:

        Apply zoning and land use stability so that future generations have the
        confidence to invest in residential properties and raise families in the Adams
        Neighborhood.
        Celebrate and preserve the unique housing characteristics of the Adams
        Neighborhood and increase owner occupancy.”

 Conclusion: It is absolutely mind-boggling that city staff and the planning commission
 are recommending the code change from three to four individuals. It is obviously contrary
 to the city plan and the neighborhood plan. It’s clearly in line with someone’s plan,
 though – so the question is, whose plan or agenda is pushing this? Who will it benefit?
 The answers to those questions are not needed to define what your obligation is: reject
 this proposal (and any other such preposterous proposals) that come from the city staff
 and the planning commission until they start recommending changes that are good for
 the city.

SUBJECT: Moderate Income Housing - Pending Land Development Code
Changes (Ord. 23-32)




This notice is being sent out to the Neighborhood Chairs, Neighborhood
Councils, and others who have subscribed to the Neighborhood Newsletter and
is regarding legislation currently pending before the Logan Municipal Council
involving Moderate Income Housing. We sent around a copy of the City’s
Moderate Income Housing Plan and proposed Land Development Code
amendments to all of the Neighborhood Councils a few months ago to ask that
you to share this information with your groups, friends, neighbors, and to
encourage input and feedback on proposed code changes that may affect your
neighborhoods. We are again encouraging your participation in this decision
making process.


The current population of Logan is approximately 54,148 and is growing at a
rate of approximately 1.2% per year. Since the year 2000, approximately 10,108
new residents have called Logan home and we are anticipating that by 2030,
another 5,000 new residents will be calling Logan home. It is projected that a
significant percentage of these new residents are not outsiders moving in, but
rather they are the children and grandchildren of current residents. Given that
the price of housing is so high and that there is a significant shortage of
affordable housing, the difficult question facing Logan residents and decision
makers is where these new residents, whether owners or renters, will live.


During the 2022 State of Utah legislative session, the State passed House Bill
(HB) 462 mandating that all local governments plan and provide for more
moderate income housing which is housing for residents who make less than
80% of the area’s median income. Logan’s Moderate Income Housing (MIH)
Plan was adopted in 2022 and identifies both short term and long term goals
and strategies that should be considered in helping to provide more moderate
income housing.
