       A.     RIVERTON ACRES PRIVATE LANE, PLZ-23-2033, A CONDITIONAL USE
              APPLICATION FOR A PRIVATE LANE LOCATED AT 3546 WEST 13400
              SOUTH. APPLICANT - BOB JONES.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich presented the Staff Report and stated that the above item is a
request to add one additional lot to an existing private lane. The subject property is located
at 3546 West 13400 South. Mr. Prestwich reported that the property is a combination of two
parcels totaling 1.03 acres in size. Staff recommended the matter be tabled until the
applicant has written permission from both property owners for the private lane.

The applicant, Bob Jones, reported that the Krafts are the adjacent neighbors and they have
been speaking with one of the partner properties who reported that they are ready to move
forward. He was surprised upon receiving a letter from the City stating otherwise. The
property can be accessed from the private lane or through an easement. He suggested the
Planning Commission approve the .50-acre subdivision with a contingency that they bring
in a compliant plan.

Chair Gilchrist asked Mr. Jones if he suggested an additional private lane along the west
side of the property. The request was for a private lane and any easement requests would
require going through proper channels with City Staff. Mr. Jones stated that there is space
on the property to provide an easement as opposed to accessing the private lane. He can
access the property either way.

Commissioner Rushton asked how long the applicant has owned the property. Mr. Jones
stated that he is not the owner and was acting as the owner’s representative. Their intent
was to purchase the property and create two lots. The home in the front has been
refurbished and the desire was to build a second home on the rear lot. Access to the

point without approval of access. Mr. Prestwich responded that there may be issues with

       E.     ORDINANCE AMENDMENTS TO TITLE 17 - SUBDIVISIONS, PLZ-23-5010,
              PROPOSED CHANGES TO PUBLIC IMPROVEMENT STANDARDS AND
              TO THE APPLICATION, REVIEW, AND APPROVALS PROCESSES TO
              CODIFY CURRENT PRACTICES, IMPROVE EFFICIENCY, AND BE IN
              COMPLIANCE WITH THE REQUIREMENTS OF UTAH SB 174 AND UTAH
              HB 406. APPLICANT - RIVERTON CITY

Mr. Prestwich reported that Staff found a solution that will bring the City Code into
compliance with State Code and mitigate potential conflicts. The Ordinance states, “This
supersedes anything that would conflict in the other ordinance” which would provide that
solution. The proposed amendments will create two separate tracks. One set of standards
will address commercial or condominium subdivisions. The second would apply to
townhomes, single-family homes, and duplexes. The second set is required by State Code.
Streamlining the process will continue. The proposed amendment will provide a seventh
section to Subdivision Ordinance Title 17 that will apply to subdivision applications for single-
family dwellings, two-family dwellings, and townhomes. Condominiums and commercial
subdivisions will follow a separate track for the time being. He noted that this section does
not apply to land use regulations approved or agreed upon by the City Council exercising its
land use authority. Also included is a section for geological hazard areas. The City has
chosen to hold a public hearing that would take place at a Planning Commission Meeting for
the Preliminary Plan. Staff would next approve the Final Plan at an administrative level. The
following procedures were defined:

Preliminary Subdivision Applications:

Riverton City designates the Planning Commission to review and approve Preliminary
Subdivision Applications. Mr. Prestwich explained the City Council was excluded by State
Code. Of the provided options, Staff felt Commission approval was the appropriate
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                               12
January 11, 2024


selection. Final Subdivisions are approved by the Development Services Director, Jason
Lethbridge, or designee to review and approve the Final Subdivision Applications.

Application process:

This is the pre-application for a DRC Meeting. Certain information must be provided if a
DRC Meeting or pre-application meeting is requested.

Section 050:

This Section establishes the process for the Preliminary Plan. A complete application has
15 days to review and return comments. Return comments may only be returned under
Section C. Each subsequent review cannot include new items to comment on. City Staff
will review the applicant’s resubmittal to determine their level of completion. Applications
and plans ready for presentation to the Administrative Land Use Authority will also be
reviewed by City Staff. Plans may only be reviewed four times. Should Staff determine the
fourth review is not ready to be presented, the application would be brought before the

The Administrative Land Use Authority:

Mr. Carter emphasized the importance of recognizing that the relationship of the
development community at large will be less cooperative and more adversarial. Often,
developers hire the cheapest engineers possible who produce the least amount of work
necessary to comply with the submittal. They then wait for Staff to design the project for
them through the redline process. If there is an incomplete submittal, going forward the City
will not redline it but reject it for non-compliance. The four-turn review process will begin
once they have something complete. What the legislature does not recognize is that they
are placing a higher standard on the development community to pay their engineers more
upfront to get the job done correctly the first time.

Chair Gilchrist asked if an application with material changes made to the Site Plan which are
not consistent with the red lines of the original submittal, will return to a resubmittal of the
initial application. Mr. Carter stated that with a developer-initiated change, they will interpret
the changes as a new application with the potential to waive a portion of the associated fees.
If the changes are developer-initiated and there is no direct relationship between the red
