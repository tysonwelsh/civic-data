52   2.2) Public hearing and consideration of Bernahl Development Services request for
53   approval of the Texas Roadhouse Building Addition located at 1402 East Main Street, with
54   an exception to the Architectural design standards.
55
56   Brittney Harris presented the item. They are requesting an exception to have only 30% glass
57   rather than the current 40% standard set in the code.
58
59   Commissioner Jackson asked why the building currently has 30% glass. Mike West explained
60   that there were no glass requirements at the time the building was originally built.
61
62   Emily Bernahl was present representing Bernahl Development Services. She emphasized that she
63   wants the addition to match the store stylistically, and that adding more glass would make it
64   difficult to fit new booth seating.
65
66   Commissioner Jackon opened and closed the public hearing.
67
68   Commissioner Kunze asked if the addition would cut into any setbacks. Harris explained that
69   there are no setbacks for commercial zones, but that there will still be space between the building
70   and the street.
71
72                   Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to approve the exception with the findings
73                   that the proposed exception still allows the site to meet the intent of the urban
74                   village design district, the applicant provided adequate justification for why they
75                   needed the exception, to match the character of the building with the standards
76                   that were in place when the building was built, and that the exception will not
77                   increase the impact to their existing building which currently has 30% glass. He
78                   included all DRC comments. Commissioner Kunze seconded.

85   2.3) Public hearing and consideration of Flagship Homes’s request for approval of a
86   preliminary subdivision for Pioneer Village, a 34-lot single family residential project
87   located at approximately 1300 West 1400 South.
88
89   Brittney Harris presented the item. Zoning would allow for 26 lots.
90
91   Commissioner Everett asked if calculations were done showing the value of the lots compared to
92   the value of the road. Gary Ellis said he wasn’t directly involved in the process, but those who
93   were thought it was a fair trade.
94
95   The road will not be fully covered by the development. The developer will need the city’s help to
96   build in extra fill and swing the road to the South. Alternatively, a bridge would need to built by
     Lehi City Planning Commission                     2                                       May 8, 2025
 97   the city for the road to go straight and cross the river. Commissioner Everett asked about the
 98   timing of the road project. Ellis said it would be wiser to continue the road through to the end.
 99   The applicant, Tony Trane, explained that the intent is to finish the project and work with the
100   parks and engineering departments to find adequate funding. Commissioner Eyre clarified that
101   the ideal scenario is to find funding for a bridge and to build the road straight.
102
103   Tony Trane explained that the developer would be building the bulk of the road but would need
104   assistance with placing fill in a few areas. He mentioned that Steve Marchbanks from the parks
105   department was eager to get the bridge in as it would benefit the park. The development would
106   benefit from a connection to 1700. If funding for the bridge isn’t found, the road will be moved
107   to the south. They want to have the road finished by the beginning of the next school year and
108   are planning on building it at the same time as the development. There are 34 lots being built, as
109   that was what was promised to residents, and the City Council preferred that the road be used to
110   justify the extra density. The developer has been making land trades with local landowners
111   which will improve the park and allow the road to be built.
112
113   Commissioner Jackson opened public comment.
114
115   Kenneth Wade thanked Trane for listening and responding to residents’ concerns. He appreciates
116   that current residents haven’t had to deal with too much exposure to the project. He said that
117   while the road will be a major benefit to the neighborhood, it is also a major benefit to the
118   developer, and that the Planning Commission should ensure that the increased density should be
119   in response to how much the developer is actually helping the city. He also stated that the road
120   will do no good to anyone if it isn’t finished.
121
122   Larry Hadfield also thanked Trane for listening to residents. He is concerned about the crowding
123   on 1100 West and worries about the safety of schoolchildren crossing there especially. He thinks
124   that adding and finishing the road will be a great help to the problem, but that there need to be
125   safety measures taken when building it as well.
126
127   Commissioner Jackson closed public comment.
128
129   Tony Trane stated that he agrees with the public that the road must be finished. He clarified that
130   the intent is to take the road south to form a connection if the city can’t find funds for a bridge. It
131   is his opinion that 34 lots is an appropriate request based on what has been presented.
132   Commissioner Everett asked if the developer needed the road as much as the city did. Trane
133   explained that it would be a huge benefit, but that it is being constructed under guidance from the
134   city council; it is not a requirement or a necessity. Commissioner Eyre asked how much it was
135   going to cost. Trane gave an estimate of $500,000 for one section, which does not include
136   subbase material or the bridge.
137
138   Trane cleared up some possible misconceptions about bonus lots, explaining that they are a
139   benefit, but that a bonus lot isn’t an additional lot that is tacked on to a project. Instead, higher
140   density means that everything has to be resized and redistributed.
141
142   Commissioner Everett asked staff if they feel the deal has been artificially inflated. He wants to
143   make sure the city is being compensated. Gary Ellis explained that staff felt good about it and
144   that there needs to be some way for the developer to profit or the project won’t get done.

216   2.5) Consideration of Clyde Capital Group’s request for revisions to the Vistas at the Point
217   Amenities removing the amphitheater from their required amenities.
218
219   Brittney Harris presented the item. Code that allows substitution of amenities was passed in
220   2021. The applicant is requesting removing the amphitheater, but will still build the clubhouse
221   and an overlook park. Staff has concerns over just removing the amphitheater as it gets rid of a
222   community gathering place and no substitutions were suggested.
223
224   Commissioner Everett asked if any other amenities has been put in yet. Harris explained that the
225   trail is in, but as the development is under construction, no other amenities are completed at this
226   time.
227
228   Ryan Christofferson was present representing Clyde Capital Group. They would like to put a
229   pocket park in the development rather than the amphitheater, as they are unsure the space would
230   be used.
231
232   Commissioner Everett clarified that the plan was to add an additional park. Christofferson
233   confirmed. A concept plan for the park has been submitted to the city. The park would be in a
234   different place than the planned spot for the amphitheater. Commissioner Everett asked if wind
235   studies had been taken into consideration with the new location. Christofferson clarified that the
236   main driver of getting rid of the amphitheater is cost, not the wind study from the flight park.
237
238   Commissioner Kunze asked if the park would be the same size as the amphitheater was planned
239   to be. Christofferson explained it would be a fit-in park that utilizes the natural environment.
240   They are planning on having grassless landscaping and including bench swings.
      Lehi City Planning Commission                     5                                       May 8, 2025
241
242   Commissioner Eyre explained that it felt as though the rug was being pulled out from under the
243   city. He asked Christofferson to explain how it would help the city. Christofferson explained that
244   the amphitheater may not be used by the neighborhood. Commissioner Everett asked what the
245   factors behind this assertion were. Christofferson explained that the company felt they could do
246   something more interesting once the wind studies came out from the flight park. Mike West
247   mentioned that the apartment builder had already made some changes to the initial plan, and
248   Harris explained that the wind study threw a few wrenches in the original plan. The preliminary
249   plan was approved, but the city encouraged working with the flight park to minimize impact.
250   Harris clarified for the Commission that they have discretion as to what qualifies as a community
251   area.
252
253   Commissioner Lockhart asked if it would be more restrictive for the applicant to table the issue
254   or deny it, if the Commission felt there needed to be more work done. Harris recommended
255   tabling it.
256
257   Commissioner Eyre didn’t like that a more accessible amenity was being replaced with a less
258   accessible amenity. Commissioner Lockhart agreed, mentioning that she didn’t like
259   compromising a TOD, as they are already controversial.
260
261   Commissioner Kunze shared a question from Kim Struthers and asked if the clubhouse also
262   served the townhomes. She explained that if it didn’t, it wouldn’t count as a townhome amenity.
263
264   Commissioner Jackson shared the feeling that it felt like the developer was trying to pull the rug
265   out from under the city. He shared that he thinks the plan needs more work.
266
267                   Motion: Commissioner Lockhart moved to table the item with the findings that
268                   the proposed amenities do not meet the intent and requirements of the TOD zone,
269                   and that the Commission would like to give the applicant an opportunity to revise
270                   the plan in a timely manner so that they have time to identify the pieces that make
271                   up a TOD and incorporate them into the amenities appropriately. She included all
272                   DRC comments. She left it open to the applicant to work with staff until they
273                   found something satisfactory. Commissioner Kunze seconded.
274
275                   Vote: Commissioner Everett, yes. Commissioner Lockhart, yes. Commissioner
276                   Eyre, yes. Commissioner Jackson, yes. Commissioner Kunze, yes.
