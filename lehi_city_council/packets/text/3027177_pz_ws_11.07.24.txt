28   2) Discussion of Draft Bike and Pedestrian Plan: Introduction, Goals, and Existing
29   Conditions
30
31   Kim Struthers presented the plan, with the mission statement of “Lehi will be safe and comfortable
32   for families and individuals of all ages and abilities to walk, bike, and roll to their desired
33   destinations.” The plan consists of eight short-term goals to be completed in the next 5-10 years.
34
35       1. Update City code and design standards to increase safety and comfort for pedestrians, and
36          non-vehicular travelers.
37       2. Add 20% of missing sidewalks along developed roads.
38       3. Complete lighting on 16 miles of all trails.
39       4. Focus expansion of the active transportation network on connections to key destinations,
40          including downtown Lehi, schools, transit, and neighboring communities.
41       5. Add an additional 11 miles of paved trails and 12 miles of on-street bike infrastructure to
42          the network.
43       6. Designate pedestrian districts at community gathering places.
44       7. Complete murals and public art at 3 trail under-crossings
45       8. Complete two or more segments in the existing recreational prioritized trail network.

55   3) Discussion of Amendments to the Buffering and Screening Requirements for
56   Commercial Projects next to a Residential Use or Zone.
57
58   Kim Struthers presented the amendments.
59
60   The draft presented would delete the compatibility assessment that prevents residential and
61   commercial zones from being next to each other. The new goal of the requirements would be to
62   minimize impact on residential areas, rather than seeking to eliminate them entirely. Fence
63   requirements would stay at 6 feet for commercial/residential boundaries and would increase to 8
64   feet for heavy industrial/residential boundaries. In addition, fences will now be required to be
65   concrete/masonry, to further help with noise impact. Landscape buffers will now increase with
66   the height of buildings, and with the impact of the zone’s use. There was further clarification that
67   landscaping buffer zones can include sidewalks and trails, but may not include vehicles,
68   dumpster enclosures, generators, or other equipment storage.
69
70   The Planning Commission had some discussion about a clause that would allow developers to
71   pitch lower minimum landscape buffers. There was worry that this would be abused as a
72   loophole, and that there weren’t enough specifics to provide reasonable guidelines for who could
73   apply for the lower minimum buffer. The clause was ultimately removed from the amendment.
