Proposed Ordinance 2023-41 amending the Ogden Municipal Code to
adopt a two-family and small lot residential zone (R-2S)

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed zone text amendment
relating to two-family and small lot residential zoning came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated that, as a
result of an Ogden City Council initiative, the Planning Commission was asked to make a recommendation on the property at |
3841 Jackson Avenue, and whether it should be rezoned from R-3 to R-2. After considering the housing needs of the community,
the Planning Commission recommended the creation of anew zone, R-2S, which would allow townhomes, duplexes, and small-
lot single-family homes. This proposal is only for the creation of a new zone, the potential zone change of the Jackson Avenue
property would be considered at a later date.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and stated the property is the former Grandview Elementary School
property, which was declared as surplus by the Ogden School District Board on September 15, 2022. The City received a great
deal of public input on the Grandview property. Nearly all sentiment was to keep the property as open space, although a potential
development on the property was subject to the R-3 zoning standards. Council members expressed concern with the R-3 zoning
and what would be allowed to be developed under those regulations, with a lack of clear height limits being a specific area of
concern. In response, Administration directed Planning staff to prepare arecommendation to the Planning Commission and City
Council to amend the zoning standards for the R-3 zone to provide a reasonable height limit, which ultimately was approved by
the Council on April 4, 2023. Despite the amendments to the R-3 zoning standards regarding height limits, Council member Hyer
has indicated he would still like to pursue a rezone of the Grandview property. In evaluating the housing needs of the community,
the Commission considered that, if the incomes of future households match current levels, about two thirds of those future
residents will be able to afford to live in midrise or high-rise apartments. Additionally, about 15 to 20 percent would be able to
afford to live in “missing middle” housing types such as townhomes, duplexes and multiplexes, small cottages, while less than a
quarter of future residents would be able to live in a detached single-family home. Because most of Ogden is built out, much of
the vacant single-family land is in sensitive, protected or hard-to-develop lands, and there are very little options for expansion.
Arguably, it will be very difficult for Ogden to accommodate even half of the need for single-family and ownership housing. The
surrounding area is mixed development with offices, multi-plexes, a park, a church, and single-family developments. The
Commission also considered the site’s proximity to Weber State University, McKay-Dee hospital, and the OGX line, and found
this location to be suitable for housing more dense than typical single-family development. Consequently, the Commission is
recommending a new zone, R-2S, which would allow townhomes (platted so units can be individually owned), duplexes, and
small-lot single-family homes. The zone would not allow mid-rise apartment buildings. This proposal is only for the creation ofa
new zone, the potential zone change of the Jackson Avenue property would be considered at a later date.

Council member Hyer inquired as to required setbacks for the lots in the R-2S zone, as well as access to legal parking
spaces behind the housing units. Mr. Brierley stated the proposal would require two parking spots and each unit willhavetwo |
parking spaces in the alley behind the home, or a two-car garage, or single-car garage with one exterior parking spot. Setbacks
will be a minimum of 10 feet in the front yard, three feet in the side yard, and 10 feet in the rear yard. Developers will need to be
intentional of their design of the types of subdivisions and homes that can be developed in this proposed zone, and he believes
there is a great demand for this type of product.

Consideration of the appointment of Mara A. Brown as Chief Administrative
Officer for the City of Ogden

A memo from Council staff explained Mayor Caldwell is requesting advice and consent on the appointment of Mara A.
Brown to the position of Chief Administrative Officer for Ogden City effective upon advice and consent of the Council. Ms.
Brown would succeed Mark Johnson who retired June 30, 2023.

Mayor Caldwell addressed the Council and noted that subject to Utah Code 10-3b-202, the Mayor in a municipality
operating under the Council-Mayor form of government ‘shall’ appoint with the Council’s advice and consent a Chief
Administrative Officer; each person appointed as Chief Administrative Officer... shall be appointed on the basis of the person’s
ability and prior experience in the field of public administration and any other qualifications prescribed by ordinance. The Mayor

. may not appoint a Chief Administrative Officer during an interim vacancy period. He stated that he can tell the Council, without

’ exception, that it is critical for the employees of the City that there be a full-time, fully empowered Chief Administrative Officer.
He summarized the responsibilities and duties of the Chief Administrative Officer, noting Ms. Brown has been employed with the
City for 16 years, is a municipal attorney, and is a great fit for this position. He also discussed the recruitment and interview
process for filling the position; there were 46 applications and a diverse interview panel. It is important to point out that Ms.
Brown was the preferred candidate for all members of the interview panel, which represents state-wide municipal leadership. Ms.
Brown is aware that, upon the election of a new Mayor, she could be terminated; however, he feels strongly that this appointment
is a signal of the City’s investment in its employees and a way of providing consistency and stability for them. He concluded it is
his honor to present Ms. Brown for advice and consent from the Council for the position of Chief Administrative Officer.
