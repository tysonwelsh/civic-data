1.    ADOPT A RESOLUTION UPDATING SECTION 6 AND SECTION 14 OF THE UTAH COUNTY
      HUMAN RESOURCES RULES AND REGULATIONS (Continued from the October 10, 2017
      Commission meeting)
(Track A, 05:40)
Utah County Human Resources Director Cammie Taylor summarized the Board of Commissioners have requested a
‘punch-in, punch-out’ time tracking system for county employees; these updates to the Rules and Regulations
coordinate with that system. Elected Officials and the Commission Office will be exempt from the new system.
Employees will primarily check in on desktop computers, except for circumstances in which they have received
approval from the elected Department Head, or the appointed Department Head and the Commission – this places
responsibility back onto Department Heads to know county needs and make appropriate decisions regarding employee
schedules. Ms. Taylor noted the goal is not to hinder regular business or create extra work for supervisors, so a mobile
check-in option is available for those who attend court or otherwise work outside of an office setting.

(Track A, 09:00)
Commissioner Graves asked about the correction course for supervisors who consistently correct missed ‘punches,’
and Cammie Taylor explained that decision will ultimately fall to the Department Head. Utah County Deputy
Attorney Robert J. Moore noted discipline is determined by the Department Head according to state statute. Mr.
Moore also mentioned a language correction is needed because the paragraph regarding mobile check-in approval
‘from the elected Department Head and the Commission’ is currently written to indicate the Board can make that
decision, which they cannot; the elected Department Head solely holds that responsibility. Commissioners Lee and
Graves expressed concern the Board would not be able to direct whether or not an employee may use the mobile
option, and asked Mr. Moore to research the statute relating to that issue.

(Track A, 17:40)
Commissioner Graves suggested the mobile check-in option be removed entirely from the resolution. Commissioner
Ivie stated the Board needs to trust the Department Heads and other Elected Officials to control the time management.
Commissioner Graves mentioned the Board knows of issues where hours worked are not accurately documented; there
could be a potential $1,000,000 annual savings through vacation, holiday pay, and time tracking. Cammie Taylor and
Robert Moore reiterated the need for a mobile option, and the responsibility being placed on Department Heads.
Commissioner Graves countered there are ten Elected Officials to oversee eighty percent of county staff, which
translates to approximately $40,000,000 of the budget. Commissioner Lee shared concern employees will
intentionally refuse to participate in the new system, and Ms. Taylor noted hourly employees would not be paid
without logging their worked hours. Commissioner Graves stated the current problem is a result of people fraudulently
claiming to work from 8:00 A.M. to 5:00 P.M. with a five- or two-minute leeway; seven minutes daily per employee
adds up to the million dollars in overpayment. When Ms. Taylor pointed out the Commission controls the budget and
can audit employees at any time, Commissioner Graves argued he consistently hears complaints that it is “so hard to
fire people in the county.” Commissioner Graves explained it would not be hard to fire somebody who is inadequately
performing their job duties if the county would manage and follow the rules. Ms. Taylor replied that is not her
experience, as the person who regularly deals with disciplinary action in the county; she finds the Department Heads
to be very responsible in dealing with their employees. Commissioner Graves said he strongly disagrees, and would
be more than willing to share times, places, and actions to support his statement.

5.    APPROVE AND AUTHORIZE THE COMMISSION CHAIR TO SIGN THE NOTICE OF
      TERMINATION OF THE INTERLOCAL COOPERATION AGREEMENT BETWEEN UTAH
      COUNTY AND PROVO CITY REGARDING THE USE OF THE JUSTWARE ATTORNEY CASE
      AND DOCUMENT MANAGEMENT SYSTEM
(Track A, 43:50)
Utah County Attorney Jeffrey R. Buhman summarized the county had been using a statewide software system
developed for prosecutors by prosecutors; that system eventually died and the county contracted with Journal
Technologies as the result of a Request for Proposals (RFP). Journal Technologies provides a case management
system called JustWare, but it is expensive and a financial hardship for smaller entities and cities. The County
negotiated with Journal Technologies to host the data at a reduced rate for cooperating cities.

Jeffrey Buhman and the commissioners discussed the amount of information available to other participating agencies,
with comments from Utah County Deputy Attorney Paul Jones. Commissioner Lee questioned if the notice of
termination is premature considering some inter-agency infighting, and Commissioner Ivie asked about the county
benefit in terminating the contract with Provo City. Mr. Buhman replied it would be less management needed from
his office. Commissioner Ivie suggested an adjusted county reimbursement from the cities may be more prudent than

1.     DISCUSSION ON WATER RIGHTS AND WATER ISSUES
(Track B, 00:01)
Water developer Evan Johnson presented his concerns about water rights and water issues in Utah County, using
online videos to help demonstrate his points; they can be found through the following links:
            Salt Lake Corporation – Utah’s Biggest Water Hoarder
                   – https://youtu.be/1AHru00veZU
            Utah’s State Engineer is Anti-Farmer and Pro-Federal Control of Utah’s Water:
                   – https://youtu.be/valWK_xXPI8
            “Do you want diarrhea today or cancer tomorrow?” is Utah’s Drinking Water Policy
                   – https://youtu.be/W25WGwF-Mbw
Mr. Johnson explained there is a water monopoly in the state of Utah. He grouped Salt Lake City, Sandy City, Deer
Creek, Provo River Water Users Association, Washington County Water Conservation District, Jordan Valley, and
the Weber Basin Water Conservation District as a water cartel – these organizations combine to magnify their power
and money-making opportunities. Mr. Johnson gave examples showing how regulations are artificially written to take
advantage of homeowners, businesses, and farmers. Mr. Johnson pointed out he has become a target of the cartel by
trying to expose this information; organizations have sued him and threatened police investigations for his independent
purchase and sales of water. The commissioners thanked him for the information.

                                          PUBLIC COMMENTS
(Track B, 37:45)
Provo resident Robert Stevens agreed with the statements made by Evan Johnson during the Work Session today. Mr.
Stevens explained how water rights can be taken away by bureaucrats. He spoke of his experiences dealing with water
conservation districts, and the amount of water wasted by government policies.

(Track B, 44:35)
Jack Robinson mentioned it would be interesting to find out how water quality has impacted affordable housing in the
county. Mr. Robinson spoke about Michael Martin, a 57-year-old male who has been without a place to live since
May of this year. Mr. Robinson has been trying to help Mr. Martin obtain proof of identification so that Mr. Martin
can receive his monthly federal Social Security checks, but has faced many complications because of an incorrect date
of birth and address listed on a Utah County Jail booking release document. Mr. Robinson provided the Board with
paperwork on Mr. Martin’s behalf (copies of which can be found attached to these minutes). Utah County Sheriff
James O. Tracy explained his office cannot change a record created by another entity. Mr. Robinson reiterated the
transient population cannot get assistance without identification, and Commissioner Lee asked Sheriff Tracy to work
with Mr. Robinson and find a resolution to Mr. Martin’s plight.
