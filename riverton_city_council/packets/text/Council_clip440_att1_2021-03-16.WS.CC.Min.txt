34          Salt Lake County Update on Election Process 2021 - Sherry Swensen, Salt Lake
35          County Clerk, and Lannie Chapman, Chief Deputy Clerk
36
37   Sherry Swensen, Salt Lake County Clerk, explained that they had received their equipment from
38   Dominion and the new equipment has the ability to do Ranked Choice Voting. She said that
39   Ranked Choice Voting was advantageous because it eliminates the need for a primary election.
40
41   Ms. Swensen said that the cost for a primary and general election in Riverton without ranked
42   choice voting would be $48,357. The cost for a general election only with ranked choice voting
43   would be $30,396. She explained that this cost included the additional ranked choice voting
44   software costs which will be split between all of the cities participating in ranked choice voting.
45
46   Mayor Staggs asked if Salt Lake County elections office had the software for ranked choice
47   voting and if they felt comfortable to move forward with this software.
48
49   Ms. Swensen said she felt confident that they will be ready to go with this new software.
50
51   Councilmember Buroker asked for clarification on how the ranked choice voting software costs

 1   would be split. She also expressed appreciate for how well the 2020 election was run by Salt
 2   Lake County.
 3
 4   Ms. Swensen explained that the ranked choice voting software was $25,000 and this cost would
 5   be split equally between all cities participating in ranked choice voting.
 6
 7   Councilmember Wells expressed concern over the cost to educate the public about ranked
 8   choice voting.
 9
10   Ms. Swensen said that the Lieutenant Governor’s office had $250,000 set aside for educating
11   the public about ranked choice voting. The Lieutenant Governor’s office created a video along
12   with other materials that can be utilized for any city participating in ranked choice voting.
13
14   Lannie Chapman, Chief Deputy Clerk, said that she could introduce Riverton City to the contact
15   person at the Lieutenant Governor’s office that could help with public education.
16
17   Councilmember Wells asked what other cities had sent a letter of interest for ranked choice
18   voting.
19
20   Ms. Swensen said that Salt Lake City and Riverton City were the only cities that had sent the
21   letter indicating that they wanted to participate in ranked choice voting. She said that a handful
22   of other cities had expressed interest but had not provided anything in writing.
23
24   Councilmember McDougal asked if the ranked choice software cost was a one time cost.
25
26   Ms. Swensen said that the $25,000 cost for ranked choice voting software would be a cost for
27   each election that the software was used and this cost would be split each voting year among
28   cities that participated in ranked choice voting.
29
30   Councilmember McCay asked if Salt Lake County charges the City per ballot mailed.
31
32   Ms. Swensen said that they do not bill per mailed ballot but instead run a prorated charge and
33   give each City they contract with a “not to exceed” amount.
34
35   Councilmember Stewart asked Ms. Swensen what her interpretation was of the letter that
36   Riverton City sent to her and the Lieutenant Governor’s office. He asked if she believed
37   Riverton City was participating in ranked choice voting or if she believed the letter just
38   expressed interest in ranked choice voting.
39
40   Ms. Swensen said she believed that Riverton City was committed to participating in ranked
41   choice voting.
42
43   Councilmember Buroker said that her understanding was that Riverton City had chosen to
44   participate in ranked choice voting but that the City could choose to withdraw by a certain date.

47   would like to provide information to the candidates that are planning to run for office as soon as
48   possible. He thanked Ms. Swensen and Ms. Chapman for their presentation.

 1            UTOPIA Presentation by Roger Timmerman
 2
 3   Roger Timmerman stated that Utopia is a political entity of the State of Utah and partners with
 4   different cities to provide fiber. He explained that Utopia at the beginning had a negative image
 5   because they were losing money. He said they now have a model that helps build fiber
 6   networks to connect cities and does not lose money.
 7
 8   Mr. Timmerman presented a proposal for upgrading the core city facilities (presentation is in the
 9   archived files for this meeting). He said the initial phase of the upgrade would cost the city
10   $7,160 per month with a 5 year commitment. He said this is probably not more than what
11   Riverton City is currently paying and the City would get a big upgrade to their fiber. He
12   explained that the fiber is an open access infrastructure and is available to anyone including
13   schools, businesses, and wireless providers.
14
15   Councilmember Stewart said that the parks in his area were not included in any of the phases.
16
17   Mr. Timmerman said that this was a mistake on his part and he would fix it and submit a new
18   proposal which included these parks.
19
20   Councilmember Buroker asked for clarification on copper versus fiber and also if fiber could be
21   used for 5G networks.
22
23   Mr. Timmerman explained that they would use all new fiber. He also said that 5G does not
24   compare with fiber and is for cellular devices.
25
26   Mayor Staggs said that in order for 5G to be effective it has to ride the fiber.
27
28   Councilmember Wells asked who owned the assets and how many of the cities that Utopia
29   works with have expanded the service to residents.
30
31   Mr. Timmerman said the assets are not owned by the cities. The assets are owned by Utopia
32   who has board members who make decisions. He said that most of the cities Utopia has worked
33   with have provided fiber to the residents.
34
35   Councilmember McDougal suggested that the Cares Act money could be used to pay for this
36   cost up front. He also asked if providing fiber to city parks was necessary to get fiber to the
37   residents.
38
39   Mr. Timmerman said that each phase was necessary to get the network in place to be able to
40   provide fiber to residents.
41
42   Councilmember Stewart explained what bilateral meant in the fiber context. He said the upload
43   speeds are very slow and that is why the fiber needs to be updated.
44
45   Mr. Timmerman said that with everyone sending video it will be necessary to upgrade the fiber
46   in all cities.
47
48   Mayor Staggs thanked Mr. Timmerman for coming to present.

 7   Mayor/Council/Manager Reports

 9   Mayor Staggs said that he had emailed the council regarding the $4.6 million dollars that the
10   City would receive in relief funds. He said that 50% will be distributed once the bill passed and
11   the other 50% in a year. The approved uses for these funds are any COVID related expense
12   including first responders, revenue shortfalls, and improvements to water, sewer and
13   broadband. In the past, he said the City has been hesitant about updating fiber because they
14   didn’t want to go into debt. He said he would like to gage the temperature of the council in
15   regards to fiber and said he could meet with each councilmember individually. He said they
16   could create a committee or have all of the council weigh in on the options and go over pros and
17   cons.
18
19   Mayor Staggs reported that he had a good meeting with Salt Lake County regarding the golf
20   course. He said they had discussed putting the golf course out for RFP to possibly renovate the
21   facilities and he said they discussed renewing the contract with the County. He said that Salt
22   Lake County was getting $225 million in relief money and the county is looking at using some of
23   the money to invest in a water source. The County has contemplated taking a pipe from the
24   green well and running it directly to the golf course. He said he would not like this idea because
25   it locks in a single user for all of the potential capacity of that well. He would like to improve the
26   well so that it could be used for the golf course and the residents.
27
28   Councilmember Stewart said during the Unified Fire Authority meeting they had considered
29   asking each city to contribute part of their relief money for the shortfall of UFA and he wanted
30   the council to be aware when looking at these funds.

33   and a future work session on water efficiency standards.

36   metering project.
