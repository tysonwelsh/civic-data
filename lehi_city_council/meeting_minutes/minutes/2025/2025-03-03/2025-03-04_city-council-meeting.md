   overview of UDOT's extensive program for the area over the next 8 years, including the
   Mountain View Corridor project and the 2100 North project. The 2100 North project plans to

   3.b) Presentation and Discussion of Lehi Block Mixed Use Concept - Gardner Group
   The Garner Group and Wasatch Group presented a new concept plan for a mixed-use
   development in Lehi, located near the Weave building. The key changes in the new plan include
   reducing the amount of office space and increasing the retail and residential components to
   create a more balanced, true mixed-use development. The developers believe this revised plan
   will better meet the market demand for mixed-use environments. The council discussion
   focused on several key points. They expressed concerns about the impact on city infrastructure,
   including sewer, water, transportation, and power capacity, and the need for a thorough analysis
   before approving any changes. There was also debate over the appropriate balance between
   residential and office/commercial uses. The council suggested including a portion of for-sale
   residential units to promote homeownership opportunities. Despite these concerns, the council
   acknowledged the benefits of mixed-use, walkable developments in this area of Lehi. Concerns
   were also raised about the close proximity to the TOD land use designation, and this new
   proposal would reduce the number of units allowed in the future TOD. However, the council
   indicated that further analysis would be needed before approving any changes to the existing
   development agreement.

   3.c) Presentation of Lehi City Department Goals- Cameron Boyle, Assistant City Administrator
   Cameron Boyle, Assistant City Administrator presented an overview of the city's goals and
   strategies based on feedback from the budget retreat. The vision for the city is to “foster a
   thriving, family-centered community while providing diverse opportunities and a high quality
   of life”. Boyle highlighted some of the key accomplishments from the previous year, such as
   building new parks, updating transportation plans, and improving emergency management
   plans. He then outlined the priorities and goals for each department. The highlights included
   focusing on regional transportation and transit solutions through collaboration. Updating the
   city website and improving customer service were included. Ensuring competitive staffing and
   wages, especially in parks, police, and fire. Promoting better regional collaboration on issues
   like transportation and recreation. Maintaining sound budget and financial planning. Improving
   code enforcement and zoning. Enhancing parks, recreation, and public works offerings. Lastly

   3.d) Discussion of Family Park Employees- Parks Department
   Steve Marchbanks from the Parks and Recreation department discussed the challenges of
   staffing Family Park, which opened last year. He explained that the park is currently being
   staffed for 98 hours per week, which equates to 2.5 full-time employees at a cost of around
   $110,000 annually. Marchbanks emphasized that having a consistent staff presence at the park
   is critical to prevent vandalism and damage to the facilities, especially the expensive playground
   equipment. He noted that the department is already stretched thin with 24 employees
   maintaining 900 acres of park space. The council discussed several options, including keeping
   the park open on Sundays without staffing the splash pad, or potentially sharing staff between
   Family Park and Ivory Ridge Splash Pad. However, there were concerns that this could lead to
   divided attention and inadequate oversight. Ultimately, the council acknowledged the need for
   additional staffing to properly maintain and secure Family Park. Suggestions were made to
   reallocate funds from other departments or use the city's fund balance to cover the costs
   temporarily. The mayor agreed to work with the parks department to identify funding solutions.
   The discussion highlighted the challenges of balancing resident demands for park amenities
   with the city's limited resources and staffing capacity.

   3.e) Presentation of Lehi City Grant Program Update- Matthew Lee, Analyst
   This item will be presented at the next Council meeting.

   3.f) Discussion on Attainable Housing Overlay Zone- Planning Department
   Brittany Harris from the city's Community Development department presented the proposed
   attainable housing overlay zone, which aims to facilitate the development of affordable, owner-
   occupied single-family homes. The key elements include limiting the sales price to under
   $400,000, restricting the overlay to detached single-family homes, requiring smaller lot sizes,
   and including design requirements such as no garages. The council discussion focused on
   several areas, including concerns about enforcing owner-occupancy requirements. Jason Harris,
   resident and home builder, cautioned the council on potentially over-regulating and limiting
   flexibility to meet the target price point. The council acknowledged the importance of balancing
   regulatory requirements with market feasibility to ensure the success of the attainable housing
   program. They discussed ways to provide more flexibility for developers while still maintaining
   the core goals of the overlay zone, such as affordability and owner-occupancy.
