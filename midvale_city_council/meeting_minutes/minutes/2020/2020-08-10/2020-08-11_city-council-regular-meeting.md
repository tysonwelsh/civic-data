Step 1: Adopt a resolution setting the tax rate for calendar year 2020 and levying
taxes on all real and personal property within the corporate boundaries of Midvale
City, Utah:
Prior to September 1, 2020, the City Council is required to set the rate of property tax for
all real and personal property within the corporate boundaries of Midvale City. The
Certified Tax Rate is the tax rate used against the assessed values of properties located
in Midvale. On June 18, 2020, the City Council passed resolution 2020-R-28, setting a
proposed tax rate in excess of the “Certified Tax Rate.”
Each year the County Auditor calculates a “Certified Tax Rate.” The tax rate formula is
designed to give the City the same amount of property tax revenue received in the prior
year, plus any new growth that happened in the City (such as new homes or businesses).
This rate is then applied to the assessed taxable value for every property within Midvale.
The formula does not include any automatic increases, outside of new growth within the
City (new growth revenue is used to provide services to the new residents). Property
taxes do not automatically go up when a property’s value increases (in fact, the Certified
Tax Rate goes down in that circumstance to ensure the City receives the same amount
of money as the prior year).
The Certified Tax Rate used to calculate a taxpayer's property tax liability is made up of
different categories. The City has both general and General Obligation debt service
categories. Both of these categories are added together to arrive at the Certified Tax
Rate. Twenty years ago, Midvale City residents approved a General Obligation bond to

| construct a police station (currently used by UPD).

Certified Tax Rate Calculation
County auditor calculates a “Certified Tax Rate” each year for each entity.
* Calculation ensures City receives same amount of property tax revenue received
in the prior year, plus any new growth that occurs in City.
* New homes, businesses.
* Rate is applied to assessed taxable value for every property in Midvale.
* Residential exemption — 45%.
* No automatic increases (such as inflation).
COMPARE
When taxable values go up, certified rate goes down.
¢ Current housing market.
¢ When taxable values go down, certified rate goes up.
* Housing crash of 2008/2009.
* If City wishes to receive more revenue than the Certified Tax Rate calculates, must
go through Truth in Taxation (TNT).
- Last time City went through Truth in Taxation — 2016 calendar year (fiscal year
2017).

Truth in Taxation Process |
On or before June 22nd, the City must accept Certified Tax Rate or declare intent to
exceed calculated Certified Tax Rate.
* City declared intent — Resolution 2020-R-29 (6/16/20).
* County Auditor notified.
* Public hearing must be held before tax increase may be voted on.
* County Auditor must approve public hearing date.
* Cannot conflict with another entity in County with same type of hearing.
* No other public hearing items can be on the agenda.
* Notice placed on www.utahlegals.com, Public Notice website
Truth in Taxation Noticing Process
Two weeks before public hearing, County publishes combined advertisement with other
entities proposing tax increases.
* Specific requirements for advertisement set out in state code.
* One week before public hearing, City publishes ad (in addition to County ad, which
is run again).
* City holds public hearing listed on Truth in Taxation advertisement.

RETIREMENT HEALTH SAVINGS PLAN (RHS):

L Contribution Sources and amounts: 1. Direct Employer Contributions: A discretionary
amount to be determined each Plan Year (none for FY 2020) 2. Mandatory Employee
Compensation Contributions: Decreased Merit or Pay Plan Adjustment per City Policy
(None for FY 2020) 3. Mandatory Employee Leave Contributions: Accrued Sick Leave
per City Policy (None for FY 2020) Only participants with accrued sick leave in excess of
480 hours at the end of the first full pay period in January are eligible for contribution to
the RHSP. Contribution is equal to sick leave earned during calendar year less sick leave
used during calendar year multiplied by 50%. Hours necessary to reach 480 hours are
considered “used.” AFTER the contribution to the RHS plan, participants may elect to
convert 25% of remaining sick leave in excess of 240 hours to vacation leave. City
contributes 10% of employee’s accrued sick leave to RHS at time of separation from
service.
