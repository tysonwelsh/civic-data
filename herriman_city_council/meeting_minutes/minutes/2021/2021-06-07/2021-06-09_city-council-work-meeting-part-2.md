   2.2       Discussion regarding a text amendment adopting language to enable large project
             master planned communities - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi explained the zoning text amendment was presented to the Planning Commission
and was intended to create an additional zoning tool for the City to use to address large developments within
the City, as well as potential annexation areas. He reviewed zoning, and explained the allowances this tool would
provide. He stated it was currently being referred to as the Large Project Master Plan Community Subzone
(LPMPC) and explained the reason for the name. The master plan community required a property owner to
apply for a sub-zone and coordinate with the City to execute a development agreement. He explained this tool
would create a zone that allowed the City and the developer to work with a zoning tool to develop a large project
area. He stated it was a 400 acre requirement. The Planning Commission made the recommendation to reduce
the threshold acreage from 400 acres to 200 acres.

                                 Councilmember Jared Henderson arrived at 5:58 p.m.

City Attorney Andrizzi recommended the Council consider adoption along with the recommendation of the
Planning Commission. Discussion ensued with regards to an appropriate threshold level, and there was
agreement that 200 acres was an appropriate number. Mayor Watts asked what they would do if someone had
June 9, 2021 – City Council Minutes                                                     Page 4 of 21
199 acres and wanted to develop. City Attorney Andrizzi answered that they could still develop under a
development agreement, but it would use the standard zoning. Mayor Watts asked if there was a potential middle
range which may make sense for large areas that are under 200 acres. There was further discussion about a
middle and an upper acreage range with different requirements. It was noted that there were not many large
pieces left in excess of 100 acres, but there were some larger than 200 acres. City Attorney Andrizzi relayed a
200 acre requirement was in the middle compared to other Cities, but he noted it depended on what the City
desired.

Councilmember Steve Shields asked what specific inadequacies there were in the existing zoning classifications
which required this tool. It was explained they wanted to try and achieve more of a product mix, and the basic
zoning classifications separated plan uses. Councilmember Shields discussed the flexibility and changes in plans,
but expressed concern with empowering something that circumvented the general plan and zoning which they
currently had in place. He acknowledged what the goal was, but felt that it could diminish the value of the
general plan and existing zoning.

City Attorney Andrizzi explained it did not create a zone itself, rather it was a tool to allow an owner to come in
with a proposed zone. He expressed there was still the hierarchy of the general plan and existing zoning that
would apply. The proposed zoning would have to fall within the general plan, and its approval would be at
Council discretion. There was further discussion about how it was used in tandem with the general plan and
would not circumvent. City Attorney Andrizzi explained any proposed zoning changes would go through a
required public hearing process at the Planning Commission and ultimately at the consideration of the City
Council.

City Attorney Andrizzi outlined the owner of the contiguous threshold acreage would come to the City and
apply for the use of the tool. The application by code would have to include a description of the subzone(s), a
map of the property, and would have to list the permitted, conditional, and accessory uses. There would also
have to be a proposed MDA. The Planning Commission would then review the application, and the City Council
would have the ultimate determinations with no obligations to approve a development agreement. The applicant
would have no vested rights until the City Council approved a development agreement and/or the subzone(s).

                                   Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn arrived at 6:12 p.m.

City Attorney Andrizzi listed the development agreement would at minimum have to include a master plan of
the project, development standards for all the uses, development design guidelines for all of the uses, procedures
to make changes to the process, the term of the agreement, an amendment process for that if necessary, and an
indemnification provision.

City Attorney Andrizzi stated another aspect was that a large portion of this became administrative. Once a
subzone was established and the permitted uses were authorized by the City Council, the implementation of the
project became administrative. City Attorney Andrizzi explained there were still requirements by State Statute
which would have to be approved by the Planning Commission, but it was still largely administrative. He gave
some examples about what an exception would look like, and he noted the zoning would never change
administratively. City Attorney Andrizzi reviewed the language of the tool within the existing City code.

Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn asked why they would not just use the plan development overlay zone. City
Attorney Andrizzi explained this required the codification of a development agreement, and that the planned

City Planner Michael Maloy gave the hypothetical where they gave a planned development for an annexation

City Attorney Andrizzi continued with his review of the proposed language changes and reiterated the key points

Councilmember Ohrn asked for clarification about the use of this tool for an annexation. City Attorney Andrizzi

identified during the annexation process. City Attorney Andrizzi clarified the subzone was like an MDA. He
said MDAs were, by statute, an amendment to the land use code. It would be an additional provision of the
particular development agreement. He communicated was why they ran it through the entire land use process
and public hearing procedure.

Councilmember Shields commented the issue previously seen without utilizing this type of tool was the
requirement for the MDA sometimes forced their hand to approve certain zone changes and density changes
which were less popular but were contractually obligated. City Attorney Andrizzi explained there would still be

Finance Director Alan Rae noted the State and County were supposed to release the new tax rates; however, it
had not been done. He informed the Council the rates in his calculations were his personally estimated tax rates.

Finance Director Rae reminded the Council the CDA and HCSEA were not technically part of the City budget,
and they were treated as a blended component. He communicated as they approved the budget for these entities,
it was typically an amendment of the current budget, so the 2021 budget was revised, and they would propose
the 2022 budget. He expressed that because they could not approve a revised budget after the end of the year,
they needed to move forward with the revised budget for 2021, and they would postpone the proposed budget
for 2022 until after the truth in taxation hearing which would be held August 4, 2021.

Finance Director Rae communicated they also needed to include fire tax in the City budget rather than in a
separate fire district. He explained this was due to the fact the district was not created until January 4th. He
outlined the changed numbers for the budget, and pointed out that almost $6M was for fire safety. He also
pointed out the property taxes that were originally proposed with the budget. He discussed the expenditure line,
and he explained it moved $5,947,148 to the fire district. He showed his estimate for what the maximum tax
collected would be for fire. He summarized it would come in on the property tax line, go to the fire district in
the expenditure line. He said they had originally budgeted to collect $5,947,000 in property taxes, and so this
would reduce to $0. The revenue to the fire district would come in as a transfer from the general funds. He
stated that the $17.15M collected last year by UFSA would now start to be collected by the City. The residents
would not pay any additional taxes, the City would simply move where the money was collected.

Councilmember Ohrn noted that they might see more taxes as far as dollar amount, but this would not be based
on the levy amount. It would be based on the evaluation of property. It was clarified for the public the budget
was not being increased by $6M. Finance Director Rae said, in general, they should never see the tax rate
increase. He explained as long as their house property value raised or fell at the same rate as everyone else’s,
they would not see any change in taxes. He further explained as the values went up the rates went down, and
vice versa. Utah State property taxes were structured so that a City or County received the same amount of tax
every year. He noted they would receive a little more that year because of new growth. Councilmember Ohrn
clarified Herriman City, the Fire Service District, and the HCSEA did not raise taxes, but there might be other
entities which have raised them. Finance Director Rae noted property values did not grow and shrink at the
same rate, so residents may see some differentiation from their neighbors. It was reiterated an additional tax
rate was not charged by the City. There was further explanation on the topic.

    3.2    Road Improvement Bond 2021 discussion - Alan Rae, Finance Director
Finance Director Rae explained they were granted $800K per year from the State legislature for the next 15
years. He stated in order to use that money, they were proposing a new bond of roughly $10M. He noted they
would normally go through an underwriter; however, they had been approved by the Bank of Utah they wanted
to purchase it at 1.7-percent. Finance Director Rae noted it might change over the next 60 to 90 days.

            Area - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Andrizzi stated this was the final legislative step to create the fire service district. He explained at

Mayor Watts explained the creation of this district would allow for the district to collect taxes as opposed to the
taxes being set into the general fund. This would ensure they were specifically designated to be utilized for fire
services. He further expressed while they would love to do it that year, it did not quite align and would instead
be set up for 2022.

City Attorney Andrizzi also pointed out this was a separate district that worked independent of the City. He
explained the residents paid for the fire services through their property taxes, but those property taxes could
only be levied to a certain amount. He stated the levy amount rate decreased as property taxes increased. He
June 9, 2021 – City Council Minutes                                                                 Page 20 of 21

topic.

Councilmember Shields commented the Council wanted to make sure residents knew what their taxes were
