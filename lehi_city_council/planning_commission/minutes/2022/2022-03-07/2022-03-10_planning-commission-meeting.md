2.4) Consideration of David Doty’s request for approval of the Lehi Bakery Rebuild Site
Plan located at 172 West Main Street.
Brittney presented information on the agenda item. Not a public hearing item with final approval.

The applicant, Gavin Thomas, was present. Gavin clarified the footprint and layout of the
building. He stated there will be more retail space for customers. He stated if Lehi City will
allow employees to park on city property, the rebuild will be within parking requirements. The
landscaping and signage requirements meet code. Gavin stated utilities were not buttoned down
and they would need to meet with an engineer to meet those requirements.

Ross Dinsdale stated the applicant had only had discussion with staff regarding the bakery use.
He stated there has not been any discussion with the office and retail use. He acknowledged there
is not public comment, but there is a cute mural nearby. Highest use is Saturday mornings, and
Legacy Center and Center Street parking usually fills up, but not during the week. The property
owners do not offer any parking but can be approved due to the mixed use zone.

        Motion: Commissioner Ellis moved to approve David Doty’s request for approval of
        the Lehi Bakery Rebuild Site Plan located at 172 West Main Street; with the findings
        that the proposed use, at the proposed location, will not be detrimental or injurious to
        property or improvements in the vicinity and will not be detrimental to the public health,

2.8) Public hearing and recommendation of Thanksgiving Station LLC’s request for review
of the Thanksgiving Station Concept Plan, a 77.8-acre mixed-use development, including

2,000 new residential units, within the Thanksgiving Point Area Plan located at Ashton
Boulevard and Executive Parkway.
Brittney Harris presented information on the agenda item.

Ross Dinsdale clarified questions for the traffic study. The 2000 unit concept limits it to one
major improvement of Clubhouse Drive. Connecting Clubhouse Drive will provide a four lane
road and will open more ability for traffic to flow on 2300 West. The Clubhouse Drive extension
has to be built before a certain number of developments can be built. That was put in place as a
trigger for the developer. The City is working on 2300 West and is working with a consultant to
widen the road to five lanes for more capacity where UDOT has agreed to help with
improvements that will begin early next year. There have been a few options provided for
Mountain View Corridor to help with traffic. Ross stated 2300 West has been designed to be a
five lane road.

The applicant, John Bankhead, with Gardner was present. John gave a presentation on the
Thanksgiving Station proposal. Mr. Bankhead stated they have worked extensively with staff
with the new development. The request offers financial support and stability to the Thanksgiving
Point Institute. The design principles have remained unchanged and are focusing on open space,
frontrunner access, connectivity, and pedestrian access. UTA is an applicant on the concept and
area plan. The cost of the infrastructure fit with minor modifications and Clubhouse Drive will
be improved. The build of Thanksgiving Point will remain unchanged and will not be developed.
Two portions of the AP, the TOD and the residential development. The applicant stated they are
still actively working with staff on ped access on connectivity to the East side of the corridor.
The TOD requirements will be met and adopted as part of the AP with open space. The applicant
provided a detailed use plan for the south parcel with retail and connectivity with part of the area
being discussed for buffering. The general plan would be to have a ten foot walk that would go
against the property line adjacent to the neighbors, with 25 feet of planting to provide buffering
and a visual relief, as well as a sound barrier. He also stated there will be no loading docks or
exiting in the rear of the buildings. They plan to restrict the heavy commercial tenants, so the
neighbors don't end up with nuisance issues. There are two parcels that are commercial use
where they are not sure what will be occupied. There are different types of uses that could be
considered for the last mile distribution facility, but they want to steer away from anything that
would create traffic problems, where they didn't want more office-use space, so in terms of uses
they looked for something that had an even traffic flow with sound and visual that can be
buffered. The applicant stated affordable housing crisis is increased demand and inflation, so the
price of housing is unaffordable. Providing higher density will be naturally lower and will be
asking Lehi City to apply for the HTRZ to help with unlock other government programs to help
with affordable housing criteria that is set by the government. They know they will have cars in
the development and will need to provide parking, so the gas station and car wash are something
they plan to build. Over time, they hope frontrunner is more usable and the parking ratio will
start dying down. Getting community to Frontrunner other than cars. They are working on a
stationary plan and will be looking at park and rides, bus rapid transit loops, shuttle systems, etc.
UTA will take the stationary plan and incorporate it into their master plan of transportation to
address the broader transportation network. The applicant understands that traffic is issue
number one. The traffic study became significantly better with the development of Clubhouse

Drive and when the project goes in the Clubhouse Drive in installed, there will be a traffic
reduction at Triumph and Ashton. The applicants traffic consultant is working closely with the
Lehi City Traffic Engineer for connectivity. Staff and the applicant have a list of more minor
improvements that they will be paying with impact fees to help improve the traffic issues. Feel
confident that without Clubhouse Drive, with or without their project, they will be pushing in
advance the traffic issue. He feels that the legislatures are trying to balance competing interest.

Commissioner Carlson was concerned with traffic and would like a solid commitment from
UPDT, UTA, and legislatures before anything begins. Commissioner Britton would love a visual
of traffic improvements. Commissioner Jackson expressed he is pro development and likes
TOD's, but he doesn't care if the answer is a no from other players. Commissioner Nielsen asked
about the development and being developed over time and what the triggers are. He asked about
burming in industrial use and why there was a sidewalk along the property line. He expressed
there could be a bigger buffer provided.

The applicant clarified that the density around the area is unchanged they need the density for the
TOD. The triggers on the units are traffic, which would be Clubhouse Drive. The other triggers
are sewer, water, and other infrastructure. They are not an issue now because they number of
units can be supported. The applicant stated it was a good place for pedestrians. The applicant
stated they are not opposed to burming.

Ross Dinsdale stated they have worked with Ivory Homes and have widened 3600 West more
than what was planned to accommodate for the development. Commissioner Everett asked about
how many years this has been planned. Ross stated 4 years ago the City applied for funding and
that is when the 3600 West project was widened and has been planned from the start of Holbrook
farm. Commissioner Newall would like to see heavy commercial be restricted to Neighborhood
Commercial and needs to be stated there is no manufacturing or industrial allowed from the
Heavy Commercial.

The applicant would like to preserve the option of the position of the building. He will give as
much room in the back and move the buildings to the north as possible.

Commissioner Carlson was concerned about noise from the Heavy Commercial or
Neighborhood Commercial vehicles.

The applicant stated the distribution would be on the front of the building, with parking in the
back for a light buffer.
Commissioner Britton asked about Mixed Use instead of Commercial.

The applicant responded that they are trying to keep the residential close to the TOD. They are
open to other options.

        Motion to extend by Commissioner Ellis until done with agenda and to take a five
        minute break. Bunker seconded.


Commissioner Nielsen opened the public hearing.

Dan Hamilition stated he is a resident of the point meadow dev. The traffic is a main concern,
adding that 2300 West is a mess. The additional 2000 units will only compound the problem.
The Heavy Commercial use will add to traffic, and if it becomes a last mile facility, the early
morning hours will be an increased traffic time with delivery/drop offs. He agrees with

Breanna Scowsan is concerned about the Heavy Commercial behind her house and does not want

She feels that Clubhouse will just shift traffic to a different location.

Rudabough Hercham stated there is no representation for a large portion of the west side of Lehi
with the Planning Commission. There isn't a lot of trust between the residents and the City. He
expressed concerns that there were a lot of condescending remarks with the Commission. He
stated those being told around Thanksgiving Point don't understand development and how it
works is insulting. When remarks are made, he can understand why the citizens think the
Commission takes bribes. He stated the reason he lost the appeal with the 7/11 was because he
didn't print off s study. Heavy Commercial has shown that if they have rights, they can do what

intentionally. Mr. Hercham expressed the biggest issue is traffic. His family loves Thanksgiving
Point, but they have not been able to go for years because traffic is so bad. There is a

the fact that Thanksgiving Point can run off of donations is commendable, but that Thanksgiving

and are not dealing with the core issues.

Commissioner Nielsen acknowledged the online comments.

Commissioner Nielsen closed the public hearing.

Ross Dinsdale addressed infrastructure. He stated in all the planning, surrounding properties are
being included, and how much traffic will be generated by all properties that are adjacent. Part of
the reason the City was opposed to the 5400 units was because of water and sewer, but the 2000
units are feasible. The City has uses for res and mixed uses is one of the only ways to reduce
traffic. The more uses we can get close to where people live is a way to reduce traffic. Another
point is that traffic will not get better, and traffic intersections will be busier, but the level of
service and acceptable amount of time is what we want to measure. We have the luxury of
having to wait time, and now that we are growing, we must wait. But what needs to be measured

is what is a reasonable amount of time to wait in places.

The applicant, John Bankhead, was appreciative of the public comment. He feels that making
Utah great is preserving open space and acknowledged that not everyone will want to live here
but hopes because they chose to be here that it will preserve what make Lehi a great place. He
hopes they can work through the challenges with the state, staff and community. He is grateful
for Thanksgiving Point and feels it will be vibrant.

Commissioner Nielsen asked about the amount of open space and if it will be a City Park or
privately maintained. It will be open space and privately maintained but has not been finalized.
Commissioner Ellis asked the applicant to walk through the concept plan and what comes next.
The applicant wanted to state the concept will help shape up in the way it has been presented and
hoped the number of units and the way the infrastructure is being looked at that the city is
comfortable with that. Commissioner Nielsen asked if the Heavy Commercial had to be zoned at
this time and if it had to be designated to something. Kim Struthers explained some sort of use
had to be identified and the closeted thing in the City standard would be Heavy Commercial and
could be called nonresidential until options are available that meet requirements. The plan will
govern what Heavy Commercial means and can be tailored and compatible with the use. The
applicant needs to know the general direction before they continue with the development/request.
Commissioner Nielsen is concerned about getting the buffer right. Commissioner Jackson would
like to see when they are getting loaded, and if it will/will not affect traffic. The applicant is
happy to work through the details with the proposed distribution use. Eyre is not opposed to
office warehouses but is very uncomfortable with Last Mile distribution use and is adamantly
against that.

Kim Struthers stated if they can prove a level service D or greater, they are meeting the code.
Ross Dinsdale stated his recommendation would be to have the commission have them meet
requirements within the traffic study. John stated the consulted traffic engineer is actively
pursuing the study by staff’s recommendation. Some of triggers are when they can demonstrate
infostructure support, the number of units are in alliance with the zone, and that zoning
requirements are being met.

        Motion: Commissioner Jackson moved to make a positive recommendation of
        Thanksgiving Station LLC’s request for review of the Thanksgiving Station

        Executive Parkway; Noting that moving forward a traffic study with a letter grade is
        completed that will be used in conjunction with Staff to establish significant and

        code. Also noting to increase the landscaping buffer between commercial and residential

        public hearing. With the findings that the proposed concept meets TOD requirements

        potential to provide regional benefits if carefully planned and phased, is consistent with

        the Lehi City Development Code, and conforms to the goals and policies of the General
        Plan. Including all DRC comments. Commissioner Bunker seconded.
