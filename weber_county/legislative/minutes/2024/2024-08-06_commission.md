D. THOUGHT OF THE DAY: Commissioner Bolos: The last three years I have ridden the Snake River on an
   inflatable kayak. I was nervous this year as we approached the biggest wave on the river, the Kahuna; I had not
   fallen out the previous three years but this year I feel out numerous times. The bulk of our family will ride in a
   raft while the rest ride in three to five kayaks. I noticed the kayakers make their own little clique. They talk
   about the big rapids before they go through them. If anyone falls out, they debrief after to find out what
   happened, what went right, how much fun it was, what is coming up next. It made me think how we all hit
   things in our lives like these rapids; we prepare for them the best we can; sometimes we fall out; sometimes it’s a
   blast. But we go down with those who have been with us all the way through and if something went wrong, we
   talk about it after and do better next time. It’s those people around you who help you not only get through
   challenges but also help have fun with each other.

F. RECOGNITION:
   1. Recognition of the Kyle Nordfors for Utah State Search and Rescue Volunteer of the Year.

            Sheriff Ryan Arbon (Weber County Sheriff): This is a once-a-year award; you send the nomination to staff
            that represents Utah Search and Rescue. We nominated Kyle Nordfors and Lt. Mark Horton (only Kyle
            could be considered as the award is for Volunteer of the Year). I am going to read the nomination we
            submitted:

                          Weber County Sheriff search and rescue began using drones on 2018 using a single low resolution
                          thermal camera drone. The drone was instrumental in saving lives of three men. Soon after formal
                          standard operating procedures were developed using aviation industry standards as the backbone and
                          merging with them with search and rescue practices. Many successful rescues followed gaining the
                          attention of drone blog websites across the nation. In March 22 Weber County Search and Rescue
                          logged the 600th life saved nationwide with a drone. Mark and Kyle have worked hard to show the
                          value of drones used in not only search and rescue operations but they have also helped in
                          homicides, worked on critical legislation, and trained hundreds of other drone operators how to use
                          of the drones. They have helped bring critical legislation for the benefit of all public safety users and
                          have been nationally recognized for their efforts. The dedication that Mark and Kyle exhibited in
                          this situation and their unwavering drive to see it through to the end serve to protect agencies
                          throughout Utah, as well as the county, and allow them to continue operations with drones that have
                          proven life-saving many times over and over in a variety of situations.

            These two men are literally the top 1% of the 1% when it comes to Search and Rescue drone technology and
            when it comes to just getting it done without the restrictions and red tape we face daily. These guys save
            lives. And so does our team; Debbie is wonderful; the Search and Rescue team is absolutely phenomenal.
            We receive requests from throughout the state, throughout the nation, and throughout the world from other
            countries wanting to know why we are so successful. It is my honor to recognize these two, especially Kyle
            for the award of Search and Rescue Volunteer of the Year.

            Lt. Mike Horton (Weber County Sheriff’s Department, Search and Rescue): In 2018, we wanted to start a
            drone program and felt behind the curve with Search and Rescue. With Kyle’s help, we quickly led to the
            front. We learned with the drones we could cut search times in half and cut rescue times in half. Kyle
            developed a program that keeps the drone with someone after they have been found which then helps guide
         the rescue teams in. Kyle then takes it a step further and plots the best ways out for people. Because of
         Kyle’s work and dedication, the program is just phenomenal and we are being recognized worldwide.

         Kyle Nordfors: All I did was bring my aviation and airline policies I learned in the airlines and tried to apply
         them to Search and Rescue. This award is more reflective of the team as a whole. I just fly the little drone.
         If it wasn’t for the team on the ground, climbing the mountains to extract people, my drone would be useless.
         And it’s a representation of good leadership stemming from the Commission to the Sheriff and Lt Horton
         who allowed me the leeway to do certain things that helped get to where we are today. I am travelling to
         China and London to spread what we have learned. A drone manufacturer reached to us and offered us to
         demo a new large drone that can carry a litter so our ground teams no longer have to carry someone out or
         carry supplies up a mountain. Thank for your ever growing support.
