Item 1: A discussion reviewing the progress of Fiscal Year 2025 Council Budget Priorities
(25-001) 00:09:20

Justin Harrison, Council Executive Director, presented the review of the Fiscal Year 2025
Council Budget Priorities. Councilor Rachel Whipple reminded the Council that increasing
homeowner occupancy is a key priority.

Priority 1: Increase Homeowner Occupancy The goal is to increase homeowner occupancy to
50% by 2029 through a balance of home types throughout the city. Harrison noted that a zoning
update is currently in progress, with Development Services being approximately halfway through
the process. Councilor Craig Christensen inquired about the timeline for completion, to which
Harrison confirmed the ongoing efforts.

Councilor Whipple clarified that the objectives include simplifying zoning regulations and
revising residential codes to align with the General Plan, allowing for smaller lot sizes. When
Development Services presents the simplified zoning updates, the Council will have
opportunities to gradually adjust parameters as needed.

Councilor Whipple confirmed with Harrison that no additional funding is required for this
priority in the current year as $200,000 has already been budgeted from the Legacy CIP fund.

Priority 2: Active Transportation and Street Safety Harrison reviewed the projects associated
with this priority, providing a status update on their funding. Some projects have continuous
funding, others were initially unfunded but received later funding, while some remain partially
funded or unfunded. He commended Public Works for their efforts in advancing these projects.

Councilor George Handley pointed out the partially funded and unfunded projects, questioning if
they would be prioritized next year. Councilor Katrice MacKay indicated that this topic would be
addressed in the next agenda item. Councilor Becky Bogdin sought clarification on a partially
funded project, and David Michaelsen, Engineer II, responded to her inquiries.

Councilor Whipple asked Michaelsen what projects had been sacrificed to complete the currently
finished projects. Michaelsen replied that there are always additional sidewalks that could be
built with more resources.

Priority 3: Explore Parking Plans for Permit Areas in Slate Canyon and Joaquin Policy
Analyst Melia Dayley provided an update on the progress of parking plans in these areas.
Councilor Handley inquired about the direction for permit plans, to which Dayley responded that
the Council could explore various approaches. Handley emphasized the need for public updates
on progress.

Councilor Whipple asked if fees generated from permit areas would be returned to the City.
Harrison confirmed they would. Whipple expressed a desire to avoid inequity in parking access.
Parking Program Manager Sandy Bussio provided an update on parking enforcement efforts in
Slate Canyon.

Priority 4: Employee Talent Retention The Council reviewed the effectiveness of the Cost of
Living Adjustment (COLA) increase as part of talent retention efforts.

Priority 5: Efficient Delivery of Provo City Services and Programs Harrison invited
questions from the Council, but no additional comments were made.

Item 2: A discussion regarding Council priorities for Fiscal Year 2026 (25-002) 00:40:40

Justin Harrison, Council Executive Director, presented expectations and guidelines for the
discussion, breaking the priorities into six categories and inviting input from the Council.

Priority 1: Homeowner Occupancy (HOO) Councilor Christensen emphasized HOO as the top
priority and expressed willingness to allocate funding as needed. Councilor Whipple raised
concerns about enforcing Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs). Councilor Handley advocated for
diverse housing price points, not just types. Christensen suggested enforcement and awareness be
the primary focus.

Councilor Hoban inquired about barriers the Council could remove to facilitate affordable
housing. Councilor Whipple cautioned against contradictory plans that might hinder efforts for
sufficient rental options . Councilor Handley proposed rewording the priority to emphasize a
balance of price points.

Priority 2: Increase Pedestrian Safety Councilor Bogdin highlighted areas in need of
sidewalks. Councilor Whipple commended Public Works for their efforts but noted that the
$500k annual budget for sidewalks would take 40 years to address all needs. She urged Public
Works to keep the Council informed of special projects related to pedestrian safety.

Priority 3: Capital Projects Planning Councilor Handley expressed support for pursuing
capital projects, especially when external funding is available. Councilor Bogdin voiced
concerns about rapid airport growth causing traffic congestion. Airport Director Brian Torgerson
confirmed additional county funding but noted a remaining shortfall of $25-$30 million for
completion.

Priority 4: Employee Talent Retention Councilor Handley supported COLA increases and
emphasized the need for police and fire personnel to focus on educational efforts alongside
regular duties. Councilor Bogdin and Fire Chief Headman discussed phased hiring to improve
fire department staffing levels.

Priority 5: Efficient Delivery of City Services Deputy Mayor Isaac Paxman clarified that this
priority ensures ongoing essential services while focusing on new initiatives.

Priority 6: Organizational Efficiencies and Taxation Councilor Whipple suggested improved
public communication on how taxes are used effectively. Councilor Handley requested feedback
on potential tax increases and their applications. Councilor Bogdin noted public concerns over
rising fees and utility costs.

Priority Voting Results: Policy Analyst Michael Sanders facilitated a quadratic vote among
Council members, resulting in the top priorities being reimagining enforcement for HOO,
employee retention for public safety, sidewalks for schools, and HOO land acquisition.
