 -ZONING CODE AMENDMENT CRITERIA-

 Midvale City Code 17-3-1(F) outlines the criteria necessary for amendments to the
 zoning code. A proposal may only be approved if it demonstrates one or more of the
 following:

       1. The proposed amendment promotes the objectives of the general plan and
       purposes of this title;
       2. The proposed amendment promotes the purposes outlined in Utah State Code
       10-9a-102;
       3. The proposed amendment more clearly explains the intent of the original
       language or has been amended to make interpretation more straightforward; or
       4. Existing zoning code was the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact.

Dear Chairperson and Members of the Planning Commission,

I, Haley Brenkmann, am a property owner residing within 500 feet of the proposed three-lot
subdivision at 120 E 7615 S, Midvale, Utah. Pursuant to my rights under the Midvale Municipal
Code and Utah Code § 10-9a-205, I submit this letter as a formal objection to the applicant’s
request for preliminary subdivision approval.

My opposition is based on legal, environmental, and public-welfare concerns that warrant denial
or significant modification of the proposal.

  I.​   Zoning Intent and Neighborhood Compatibility

Under Midvale Municipal Code §§ 17-7-1.1 and 17-7-1.3, the SF-1 Zone is designed to preserve
the low-density, single-family character of residential neighborhoods by maintaining consistent
lot sizes, setbacks, and open space. The proposed subdivision—dividing a single parcel into
three—directly conflicts with these standards and would disrupt the established lot pattern and
visual continuity of the surrounding area. Approval of this configuration would set an adverse
precedent, weakening the intent of the zoning code and undermining the goals of the City’s
adopted General Plan. Furthermore, the subdivision, as currently designed, does not conform to
the governing requirements of the Midvale Municipal Code (MCMC). Specifically, under Title 17,
Chapter 7, Section 1.3 (Single-Family Residential Zone – SF-1) and Title 16 (Subdivision
Ordinance), the City mandates strict compliance with standards governing minimum lot area,
frontage, access width, and roadway safety—requirements that this proposal fails to satisfy.

   ●​ Frontage Deficiency – Lot 101
         ○​ Provides only 20 feet of frontage along a private access easement.
         ○​ Falls significantly short of the 55-foot minimum frontage requirement under
            MCMC §17-7-1.3(C).
         ○​ The access easement does not qualify as legal frontage on a public street, as
            required by ordinance.​
   ●​ Frontage and Access Deficiency – Lot 103
         ○​ Fronts a 16.5-foot private right-of-way (ROW) labeled as “7615 South (Private
            ROW).
         ○​ This frontage is less than one-third of the required 55-foot minimum.
         ○​ The private ROW also fails to meet the minimum 20-foot access width required
            for shared residential drives.
         ○​ The ROW lacks a turnaround, in violation of Fire Code D103.4 and MCMC Title
            16.​

   ●​ Access-Width Deficiency – Private ROW
         ○​ The 16.5-foot ROW fails to meet the 20-foot minimum for two-way traffic, creating
            a hazard for emergency, service, and delivery vehicles.
         ○​ As a dead-end drive exceeding 150 feet, it must include an approved turnaround,
            which is absent.
         ○​ These deficiencies create direct public-safety and emergency-access violations
            under MCMC Title 16.​

   ●​ Public Safety and Infrastructure Impact
         ○​ The substandard access configuration poses an obstruction for fire apparatus,
             tow trucks, and public works vehicles.
         ○​ Such design conditions would render the subdivision unsafe for occupation and
             inconsistent with the City’s adopted roadway standards.​


The proposed subdivision plat fails to meet the mandatory frontage, width, and access
standards set forth under MCMC §17-7-1.3(B)–(C) and MCMC Title 16 (Subdivision Ordinance).
The current configuration violates multiple provisions governing minimum lot frontage, access
width, and emergency vehicle accommodation. Given these clear statutory and public-safety
deficiencies, the subdivision as proposed is noncompliant with Midvale City Code and must not
be approved unless it undergoes substantial redesign and reconfiguration to fully satisfy the
City’s frontage, access, and safety requirements.

 II.​   Public Safety and Unauthorized Excavation

The applicant, Leonard Sharp, has previously engaged in unauthorized cutting and excavation
into public roads and rights-of-way in the vicinity of 7660 South and 7615 South without required
city or utility authorization.

Such activity presents an immediate hazard to surrounding homeowners and public
infrastructure. Unauthorized disturbance of public roadways and underground utility systems
creates risk of rupture, fire, or explosion and endangers both property and life.
 III.​   Infrastructure, Drainage, and Flood Irrigation Concerns

Under Midvale Municipal Code Title 16, all preliminary subdivision approvals must demonstrate
that public facilities—including storm drainage, sewer, and water systems—have adequate
capacity to serve the proposed development (MCMC § 16.01 et seq.).

No certified engineering or drainage documentation has been provided to date. The subject
property is historically flood-irrigated land and has been inundated regularly for over 100 years.
The soil and grade are not engineered for dense residential development.

Constructing additional infrastructure—such as paved driveways, compacted building pads, and
underground utilities—will alter natural grading and drainage patterns, increasing runoff and
elevating the risk of flooding to adjacent parcels, including my own.

Building homes on or adjacent to flood-irrigated or former flood-plain land also presents serious
geotechnical and structural risks, including:

    ●​   Foundation instability and heaving from saturated soils;
    ●​   Sewer and storm-drain backflow during high water events;
    ●​   Long-term water intrusion and mold contamination; and
    ●​   Substantial increase in flood-insurance costs or ineligibility under the National Flood
         Insurance Program (NFIP).

Under FEMA flood-risk disclosure guidelines and Utah Code § 57-1-1 et seq. (Real Estate
Disclosures), any known material conditions that affect a property’s use or safety—including
historic floodplain or irrigation-based flooding—must be disclosed to future buyers. Failure to
disclose that this land has a history of flooding would expose both the developer and the City to
potential liability under Utah’s real-property disclosure laws and federal flood-insurance
regulations.

Given the property’s century-long history of flood irrigation and surface-water exposure, Midvale
City should require that this risk be explicitly recorded on the subdivision plat and disclosed to
all future owners, ensuring informed consent before construction or sale.

Additionally, the proposed development may impede existing water-access rights belonging to
neighboring properties. Under Utah Code Title 73 (Water and Irrigation), a property owner
cannot obstruct or modify irrigation flows or ditch access without proper authorization and
consent from the rights holder. The applicant has not demonstrated compliance with this
requirement.

 IV.​    Public Welfare and Municipal Obligations

Under MCMC § 17-1-1, Midvale is obligated to protect public health, safety, and general welfare
through orderly, lawful development. Granting approval for a subdivision tied to unpermitted
work and unresolved water-rights conflicts would be inconsistent with that obligation and could
expose the City to avoidable legal and financial liability.
Subdivision regulation in Utah is a matter of local control, governed by each municipality’s
ordinances and due-process procedures. Under Utah Code Title 10-9a, and as reinforced by
Midvale City policy:

   ●​ The regulation of land subdivision lies primarily with local government entities, which are
      responsible for ensuring compliance with zoning, access, and health-and-safety
      standards before development begins.
   ●​ Public hearings are required for subdivision applications that create new lots or alter
      existing zoning, and adjoining property owners—typically within a 500-foot notification
      zone—must be formally notified and given the opportunity to present opposition or
      concerns before any approval is granted.
   ●​ Valid objections must demonstrate that a proposal violates zoning, endangers health or
      safety, or interferes with the reasonable enjoyment of adjoining property.
   ●​ The Planning Commission and Zoning Board retain final discretion over approval or
      denial, based on staff recommendations, legal compliance, and public testimony.

In this case, the applicant disregarded the required public process and moved forward with
preliminary site work and development activity without confirmation of development plans or
authorization from the City of Midvale. Proceeding in advance of Planning Commission approval
violates the city’s land-use procedures and undermines public transparency, fairness, and due
process as outlined in MCMC Title 16 and Utah Code § 10-9a-509.

 V.​   Insufficient Road Width and Emergency Access Concerns

The existing public roadway along East 7615 South is not wide enough to accommodate more
than one car at a time, making it unsuitable for two-way traffic or multi-parcel development
According to Midvale City roadway and access standards, public and shared drives serving
multiple units must meet specific minimum widths to ensure public safety and emergency
response access:

   ●​ A shared drive serving three or more residential units must have a minimum width of 20
      feet for two-way traffic and 15 feet for one-way traffic.
   ●​ Dead-end roads exceeding 150 feet in length are required to include a designated
      turnaround area to allow fire engines, tow trucks, and emergency vehicles to safely
      reverse direction.

The roadway on East 7615 South fails to meet these minimum standards. Adding three new
dwellings will create access issues for residents, service vehicles, and first responders. A
one-lane road without adequate shoulder or turnaround space presents an unacceptable
public-safety hazard and violates the intent of MCMC Title 16, which requires adequate access
for emergency services as a condition of subdivision approval.

Without verified compliance with the city’s width and access requirements, the proposed
subdivision cannot be considered safe or legally approvable.
VI.​   Request for Action

Given the extensive evidence of procedural violations, safety hazards, and disregard for
Midvale’s zoning and public-welfare standards, I strongly urge the Planning Commission to take
the following actions without delay:

   ●​ Deny the Preliminary Subdivision Application in its entirety.​
       The applicant’s ongoing pattern of unauthorized excavation, disregard for public
      process, and failure to provide mandatory engineering, drainage, and access
      documentation render this proposal noncompliant with MCMC Titles 16 and 17, Utah
      Code Title 10-9a, and Utah Code Title 73. Approval under these conditions would be
      unlawful and contrary to the City’s duty to safeguard public health, safety, and welfare.
   ●​ Issue an immediate stop-work order on all site disturbance, grading, or construction
      activities occurring at or near 120 E 7615 S until full compliance with City requirements,
      permitting, and inspection processes is verified.
   ●​ Require a comprehensive independent engineering assessment addressing:​
       • Existing and future drainage impacts on adjacent flood-irrigated properties;​
       • Geotechnical stability of the soil for residential construction;​
       • Floodplain and stormwater management capacity; and​
       • Emergency-vehicle access and fire-code compliance under Midvale roadway width
      standards.
   ●​ Mandate formal disclosure on the recorded subdivision plat acknowledging that this
      property is historically flood-irrigated land and part of a former floodplain, consistent with
      Utah Code § 57-1-1 et seq. and FEMA flood-risk disclosure requirements. Future buyers
      and occupants must be fully informed of the long-term flooding and structural risks
      inherent to the site.
   ●​ Investigate potential violations of the Midvale Municipal Code related to unauthorized
      excavation into public rights-of-way, and pursue enforcement or penalties as warranted
      under MCMC Chapter 9.02 (Public Property and Utilities Protection) and Title 16
      (Subdivision Ordinances).
   ●​ Require verification of neighboring water-access rights prior to any plat approval. No
      subdivision should proceed that interferes with, diverts, or restricts existing irrigation or
      surface-water rights protected under Utah Code Title 73 (Water and Irrigation).

If the applicant cannot demonstrate full and lawful compliance with these standards, I
respectfully but firmly request that the City of Midvale deny this subdivision application outright
and remove it from further consideration until all violations and deficiencies have been resolved
to the satisfaction of both the Planning Commission and the public record.
