         1. 2    Mayor' s Report


Mayor Overson wished to share with the council some examples of the various relationships and
partnerships Taylorsville shared with other entities.


Along with representatives from several other communities, the Mayor had recently participated
in a press conference with the Utah Rivers Council announcing a rain barrel event. The Rainharvest
program involved cities and counties purchasing rain barrels at a reduced rate so that it was more
affordable for residents to retain rain water for irrigation purposes, thereby conserving water
during the extreme draught. Taylorsville had originally committed to purchasing 40 rain barrels
for citizens to acquire for$ 55 instead of the full price of$ 83. As it became clear that there was a
high demand for the rain barrels, the administration ended up purchasing 100 total rain barrels and
receiving 101 reservations for them in very quick order. She loved that the citizens had become
aware of the importance of conserving water.

The Mayor then explained that the Green Committee, Code Enforcement and Jay Ziolkowski in
Community Engagement were teaming up to provide residents with information and support
regarding landscaping and parking strip questions. The goal was to ensure that city ordinances
were aligned with water-wise landscaping.

Another partnership Taylorsville enjoyed was with the Unified Fire Authority.       Mayor Overson
sat on the UFA' s Board of Directors and was pleased to announce that the Board had selected
Assistant Chief Dominic Burchett to succeed retiring Chief Dan Petersen. She described the
selection process as very intense, and she looked forward to introducing Chief Burchett to the city
council at a future meeting.

The Mayor then mentioned the city' s partnership with the Taylorsville Exchange Club.         Earlier

that day, to show its appreciation to public safety personnel, club members had visited the police
department and UFA Stations 117, 118 and 109 with breakfast treats. She pointed out that the club
was often joined by the Youth Council on service projects around the community.

City administration and staff met the previous week with Granite School District' s Superintendent
Dr. Richard Nye and Board Members Winder and McDermott about the mutual needs of the school
district and the City. The meeting was a great opportunity to share ideas and exchange information

regarding each entity' s concerns and needs. Mayor Overson appreciated having the chance to
collaborate with the school district.


Another great partnership the city enjoyed was with Taylorsville- Bennion Improvement District.
She had met their newest board member earlier in the day and felt he was a great addition to the
Tay-Been team. The Mayor shared with them a copy of the recent city survey results and was

discussion.


Mayor Overson reminded the council that Salt Lake County was building a canoe boat launch at
Millrace Park. She had recently paid a visit to the area and reported that the project was moving
along nicely. She appreciated Taylorsville' s partnership with the County.

HB440 ( the Homeless Services Amendments Bill) affected Taylorsville as well as all other cities
in Salt Lake County. The bill presented challenges, but she made it clear it was important for each
municipality to do its part, including Taylorsville. Mayors across the valley were working together
to find a location in each city for an overflow shelter in the winter months. Internally, city
administration was reviewing the criteria created in HB440 and attempting to identify a location
that could potentially house such a shelter. Each city needed to have a plan in place by September
1st and she promised to update the city council as a plan developed.

During the previous month, there had been 125 business license applications submitted to the city
with 135 licenses issued. One hundred-three building permits were issued with 5 plan applications
completed. Fifty-three code enforcement cases were opened in February, while 69 such cases were
resolved.




Mayor Overson closed her report by expressing her great appreciation to John Taylor, Scott
Harrington, Tracy Cowdell, and Jay Ziolkowski for the hard work they had done on a very
challenging issue. They were an exceptional staff.

3.        REPORTS


          3. 1    Salt Lake County Public Works Report—Ben White


Mr. White explained that Salt Lake County' s Kevyn Smeltzer was unavailable that evening so he
would be presenting information to the council on his behalf. He explained that Salt Lake County
had three primary responsibilities in Taylorsville which were traffic signal maintenance, storm
drain system maintenance and streets.


He displayed a photograph of the previously mentioned boat ramp currently under construction at
Millrace Park. Councilmember Cochran confirmed that this would be a walk-up ramp as opposed
to the type that one would back a boat in via truck and trailer.


Mr. White explained that while he was addressing the council, he would also update them( and the
residents) on the status of various engineering projects in Taylorsville that did not necessarily
involve Salt Lake County.      One such project was the city-wide installation of Google Fiber
infrastructure.   He displayed a map showing in which areas Google had completed work, where
their crews were currently working and which areas they had yet to begin. As Google did their
digging, many challenges with the storm drain system had been discovered. He indicated there
were approximately 50 storm drain boxes that had been overlaid over the years. Public Works was
now attempting to raise the boxes to grade level so that they could be accessed if necessary in the
future.


Regarding street lights in Taylorsville, it had recently become apparent that a very large number
of street lights in the city were out. In total there were approximately 2700 street lights, about half
of which belonged to Rocky Mountain Power. RMP' s lights were typically older than those
belonging to the City and therefore were failing more frequently.

Mr. White explained that Rocky Mountain Power would be upgrading any older power meters to
digital versions that could be read remotely. Affected residents would receive a written notification
from Rocky Mountain prior to installation of the new meter.

Construction season would soon begin.        Mr. White mentioned several projects that would be
affecting the city soon. He displayed a map outlining twenty different projects planned for 2022
and 2023.
