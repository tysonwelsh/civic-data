          5. 1      Ordinance No. 25- 04—      An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville Approving a
                    Zoning Text Amendment to Sections 13. 02. 200 and 13. 26. 040 of the
                    Taylorsville Municipal Code, Amending Standards Related to Nonconforming
                    Signs— Terryne Bergeson


Terryne Bergeson presented the proposed ordinance to allow certain nonconforming signs to be
rebuilt if destroyed by an act of nature or another catastrophe. She explained the current code
required    signs   to be rebuilt to current standards   if more than 50% of a sign were to be destroyed.

The amendment would apply to signs at commercial centers larger than 7 acres in size.

Representing the property owner of Taylorsville Town Center, Katie McCullough spoke in support
of the amendment. She suggested that removing the nonconforming status would place an undue
hardship on property owners and tenants, particularly impacting smaller businesses' visibility.

Council members discussed the ordinance, with some expressing concern about going backwards
on sign standards. Others noted the potential impact on small businesses.

          5. 2       Discussion      on   the Updates of the General Plan and Station   Area   Plan— Mark
                     McGrath


Long- Range Planner Mark McGrath first updated the City Council on the status of the General
Plan revision. He explained that the planning commission had provided a great deal of helpful
input. Three major issues were driving this update which were the current housing crisis( regarding
both     supply     and      lack   of affordability),   the corresponding rapid population growth and
mobility/ transportation. He stressed the importance of getting people out of their cars and into
mass transit. He then explained there were nine different chapters within the document, with each
onehaving an introduction, an ` existing conditions' segment and a community vision. Mr.
McGrath then listed six guiding principles as well as four fundamental strategies that would be
woven throughout the entire document.


The updated plan proposed adding an additional 7, 200 residential units in the city within the next
35- 40 years, emphasizing the creation of balance, stewardship, resilience, civic beauty, and
health/wellness. He mentioned that because the retail landscape had been so significantly altered,
it was important to ensure that Taylorsville' s economic development activity was looking towards
the future and not the past.

Mr. McGrath expected the planning commission to make a formal recommendation regarding the
updated plan within the next month or two, after which it would be placed on a council agenda for
adoption/   ratification.




He then moved on to presenting information about the Station Area Master Plan which would have
a public hearing before the planning commission the following week. He anticipated that the city
council would vote on it within the next month or so. It was a state- mandated plan that required
all cities with a fixed guideway transit station to have a small master plan for each station, with
the primary objective of creating a better land use/ transportation connection and putting residential
density near them. Any property within 1/ 4 mile of the station fell within the plan area and
Taylorsville had three such areas, all along 4700 South and the new BRT/ MidValley Express route.
