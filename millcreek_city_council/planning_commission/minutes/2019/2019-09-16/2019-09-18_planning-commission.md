1.1 Consideration of GP-19-002, Amendment of the Future Land Use Map Changing
the Designation of Certain Property from Neighborhood 2 to Neighborhood 1 Location:
4000 S. 700 W. Planner: Blaine Gehring
Blaine Gehring said Millcreek staff had been requested by the Millcreek City Council to
process an amendment to the Millcreek Together General Plan in response to a neighborhood
petition. The request is to change the area in the vicinity of 4000 S. and 700 W. designated as
Neighborhood 2 in the General Plan to a Neighborhood 1 designation. The subject area is in

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 18 September 2019 Page 2 of 14
one of the few remaining historical agricultural areas left in Millcreek, where several
properties are slowly being sold off and rezoned for housing development. Millcreek has
three remaining agricultural zones which host a variety of agricultural uses that are no longer
compatible with urban living. The area being requested for the amendment is primarily zoned
A-1. The subject area is approximately one mile west from the Meadowbrook TRAX Station
and is situated among other medium to high density housing projects to the north and into
South Salt Lake. Due to the proximity of the TRAX station, the area has become a site for
medium to high density housing which also contains some low-income housing. Basic land-
use planning principles encourage a higher residential density when close to public transit,
parks and more intense uses like manufacturing and industrial. One of the goals of the
Millcreek Together General Plan is to increase the amount of affordable housing in the City.
Millcreek’s Future Land Use Map indicates this area to be designated as Neighborhood 2 to
accommodate a mix of different medium to higher density housing types. Gehring said the
Neighborhood 2 designation typically allows 6-18 dwelling units per acre with building
heights from 2-3 stories. The A-1 Zone allows for duplexes up to 8 units per acre in a
dwelling group by conditional use which is within the Neighborhood 2 density range.
Gehring said that two rezone applications have been proposed in this area by Rick Whiting.
The first was for 11 townhomes which received a positive recommendation for approval
from the Planning Commission. A final decision by the City Council on that application was
delayed because of the neighborhood concerns. After receiving input from the neighborhood
to consider some 55+ senior type housing, Whiting made a second proposal for 22 55+ senior
condominiums. However, the neighborhood subsequently opposed that application and the
Planning Commission recommended denial on the rezone. It has not been scheduled on a
City Council agenda yet. A neighborhood petition to change the General Plan came after the
second application was made and it states that their input was not included in the original
plan because they are in a Taylorsville zip code and therefore had not received notice.
Notices were never mailed to any property owners in the City concerning the General Plan
but were posted on the City’s website and in the City’s newsletters. At their meeting on
September 3, 2019, the Millcreek Community Council voted unanimously to recommend the
General Plan amendment from Neighborhood 2 to Neighborhood 1.

Chair Healey told the public the Commission would make a recommendation to the City
Council.

1.2 Consideration of ZT-19-003, Amendment of the Town Center Overlay Zone
Planner: Francis Lilly and John Janson

Millcreek Planning Commission Meeting Minutes 18 September 2019 Page 5 of 14
John Janson said he would do an overview of the zone proposal though staff recommended
continuance of the item. He said the structure of the ordinance covers 3 districts;
Neighborhood, Mill Center, and Marketplace. The ordinance is an overlay that uses
categories of uses with a list of prohibited uses. There are two proposed conditional uses
within that framework; multi-family and mixed use. This is a form-based code that creates a
place and components of place. The code encompasses building design, what streets ought to
look like, and a signature feature which would be a lineal park in the city center. There has
been a lot of public input so far, but there will still be more. The General Plan suggested the
City have a city center and four other centers. There have been open houses on the proposed
overlay zone which produced generally supportive comments and community gardens were
also suggested then. The proposed ordinance went to the community councils for review. Mt.
Olympus Community Council recommended that no more than 25% residential be in the
comer buildings on 1300 E. and Highland Dr. The other three community councils requested
more time to review the proposal. He said the Mountair neighborhood expressed concern
about building height. Janson said the ordinance requires building stepbacks all around the
city center, so the buildings are not too opposing from the street. The Planning Commission
has had two work sessions on the overlay zone. They previously discussed heights, uses,
stepbacks, affordable housing, park designs, commercial recreation, stronger building design
standards, and arcades versus awnings versus columns at those work sessions.

Janson said Millcreek has had a city center study done which was recommended by the
General Plan. He highlighted a map from the study of the recommended building heights in
three zones around the city center ranging from 1-6+ stories with the greatest height allowed
in the center of the city center. He then showed a proposed height allowance map that
showed four slightly different areas ranging from 12-74 feet. He showed a map that
highlighted existing parcels within the height allowance map. He said it was important to
note that park improvements are pledged with future tax revenue increment generated by new
development. He showed examples of projects in Sugar House that could be examples of
what to do or what not to do with building size, stepbacks, architectural variation, and use of
windows. The ordinance proposal would include components for windows, corner plazas,
streets specified by name, and design features to be upgraded. He said the commissioned
traffic study would be completed in the near future and it includes the recommendation to
turn Highland Drive from 4 lanes to 3 lanes.

Commissioner LaMar asked about the minimum requirement of 48 feet of building height.
Janson said the stepbacks would be included in the height. Commissioner LaMar asked about
story requirements. Janson said the allowed height would be based on feet, not stories. He
said there has been a lot of developer interest and the height in feet proposal allows them to
do more with the property. Janson said they would try to maintain the southeast view of the
mountains from the city center open space. He said the proposal was an evolving ordinance
and it would go back to the community councils for additional review. Chair Healey said the
height allowance map should be adjusted from 74 feet to 76 feet. He said the city center is
the City’s chance to make an impact for generations to come.

1.4 Consideration of SD-19-012/EX-19-002, Preliminary Plat Approval of a Flag Lot in
a R-1-8 Zone and Recommendation to the Millcreek Mayor Regarding an Exception to
the City’s Sidewalk Standards Location: 2525 E. Neff’s Lane Applicant: Ryan Heath
Planner: Robert May
Robert May said this was a two-part application; preliminary plat approval of a 2-lot
subdivision via the flag lot policy and a sidewalk exception request. He said the Planning
Commission is the land use authority for subdivisions. The scope for denying a subdivision is
very limited. The Millcreek subdivision code does offer an appeal process in 18.08.040
Appeals. May said the applicant owns a little over a half acre in the R-1-8 zone and the
property meets the area, frontage width, and setbacks of the base lot and flag lot requirements
in the flag lot policy. The lots are subject to the Residential Compatibility Overlay Zone
(RCOZ). Access to a flag lot can be through an easement or property owned to the street and
this one will have ownership to the street. All development in Millcreek is subject to
Millcreek’s Title 14 which says all new development must install public improvements; curb,
gutter, and sidewalk. The developer is asking for an exception to that requirement. May
reported that the Public Works Director could not justify recommending this subdivision for
a sidewalk exception because 1) the open irrigation ditch poses a risk with its proximity to
the travel way; and 2) although the new home would be the first home with sidewalk on the
north side of Neffs Lane, it is just a matter of time before other homes subdivide or rebuild,
completing the missing segments of sidewalk.
May said many residents that live in the immediate area have shared their concern for the
need for sidewalks and to deny the sidewalk exception. Many based their concerns on the
fact that children are forced to walk in the road to and from school. Some of those same
residents have voiced their concern regarding the flag lot subdivision because it does not fit
the character of the neighborhood. The East Mill Creek Community Council voted to
recommend denial of the proposed sidewalk exception based on their concern that Neffs
Lane should have all public improvements installed, including sidewalk. May went over the
staff analysis and findings in the staff report. The proposed flag lot subdivision can meet the
requirements of Title 18 of the Millcreek Subdivision Code in addition to the adopted Flag
Lot Policy. The proposed subdivision amendment is in harmony with the General Plan and
character of the immediate area. Many lots on private rights-of-way and flag lots coexist in
the area. The proposed subdivision does not create a zoning violation or conflict with the
Residential Compatibility Overlay Zone standards. Staff found that the proposed
development shall be subject to the Millcreek Landscaping Code Chapter 19.77, specifically
tree preservation under landscape design standards and guidelines (19.77.040). Staff found
that at a minimum, the applicant must identify all trees outside of any proposed buildable
area within the proposed flag lot in an attempt to preserve some of the ambiance and the
nature of the surrounding homes.
There were no comments from the East Mill Creek Community Council.
