                                            Workshop

Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District 2023 Budget and Proposed Fee Increase

              Pam Roberts, General Manager, Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District
(WFWRD) presented a PowerPoint presentation reviewing the 2023 budget priorities, that
includes a $2.50 per home per month fee increase, various services provided to residents,
continuous improvements on efficiencies using technologies on equipment. Alternate fuels will
continue to be used to keep costs low and it continues to find ways to expand the Seasonal
Container Reservation Program (SCRP) at the lowest cost possible. Hiring and retaining CDL
drivers remains a challenge and effects SCRP.

               Mayor Flint stated there was an issue with reserving dumpsters for SCRP. By the
time residents received the notice in the mail, reservations for dumpsters were already open and
all the dumpsters were reserved. Reservations need to be open after the notice is mailed and
received by residents. There also needs to be a system in place to disperse dumpsters more
evenly throughout the community. There will be areas with a lot of dumpsters and then areas with
none.

                 Council Member Price asked if there is a way to provide one dumpster per
neighborhood or have a way to make a dumpster available if a resident does not need it for the
full length of the reservation.

                Ms. Roberts stated it depends on staffing and right now there is a limited number
of drivers. Full-time drivers are being pulled from their routes and coming in on their days off to
help with SCRP. She is working with her team to address the timing of the postcards and when
reservations will open for dumpsters.

               Council Member Little stated he would like dumpsters to be placed back on the
streets.

                  Ms. Roberts stated when dumpsters are placed on the street, the public deems
that dumpster theirs and landscapers and construction companies use those dumpsters. There
are also issues with scavengers going through the dumpsters. There are staffing concerns at the
landfill as well.

              Council Member Little stated the increase is reasonable if the same service is
being provided to every resident.

                Ms. Roberts reviewed a PowerPoint presentation reviewing cost increases since
the last fee increase in 2018 and WFWRD’s cash projections for 2022-2026.

               Council Member Perry stated having the dumpsters in the street is more
convenient. He suggested putting a sign on the dumpsters stating it is a community dumpster and
to ask your neighbor if you can dump garbage in the container.

               The workshop meeting was closed.

Unified Police Department

in the Domestic Violence Investigation Unit. He introduced Detective Josh Smith who will be

enforcement for 11 years. He has served on the Community Intelligent Unit with Salt Lake City,
has knowledge in community policing, and has worked with councils and community members.

non-emergency phone number to VECC is 801-840-4000. The previous number will now be used

Council Reports

Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District (MSD)

               Mayor Flint stated the MSD is finalizing its 2023 budget. There will be a public
hearing in October. There is still an issue with the Justice Courts, and she is not sure where its
budget will end up. The MSD is joining Salt Lake County to complete an audit. Funds will not be
approved until the MSD knows where the funds have been spent. Salt Lake County has officially
transferred Big Bear Park to White City Metro Township. There is an agreement that the attorney’s
are reviewing. As soon as the agreement is approved, the White City Metro Township Council will
have the opportunity to review and approve it.
