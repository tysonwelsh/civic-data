Order of Business

Resiliency Amendment to General Plan

               Kayla Mauldin, Long Range Planner, Municipal Services District (MSD), stated
the Resiliency plan was always meant to be included in the General Plan, but it was not ready
when the General Plan was adopted. This amendment does not override anything in the General
Plan. It looks at ways to foster resiliency in the Kearns Metro Township by not only looking at
brick and mortar, but at the social infrastructure as well. There are over 40 action items that have
been identified that Kearns could take during the next five years to promote infrastructure and
community resiliency. No public comment was received. However, one partner, Salt Lake County
Library, requested some revisions to the plan.

               Jerry Walden, Kearns Metro Township Planning Commission, stated the Kearns
Metro Township Planning Commission recommended approval of the amendment including the
revisions from the library.

               Council Member Snow, seconded by Council Member Schaeffer, moved to adopt
the Resiliency Plan with the revisions recommended by the Planning Commission. The motion
passed unanimously.

UDOT – 5400 South Improvements

                 Tyler Schmidt, Traffic Engineer, Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT),
stated UDOT is in the process of putting together a project along 5400 South from 4220 West to
5340 West relating to safety improvements and the slowing down of traffic. Reduced speed limit
signs do not necessarily work, people go as fast as they feel comfortable. However, the
installation of safety barriers down the center of the road with planter boxes have been proven to
work. When drivers see these types of barriers they slow down. The barriers would be
strategically placed at location where the highest number of accidents occur. The barrier would
block some left-hand turns. Depending on which funding source UDOT uses the installation of
these barriers could take place as early as Fall, 2022 or 2024. It would cost approximately $1
million.

             Council Member Snow asked if these barriers are like what was installed on 8400
West in Magna.

             Mr. Schmidt stated yes. That would be an example of what could be built, the
design has not been determined yet. UDOT would be seeking help from the Kearns Metro
Township as well as Salt Lake County to do project.

               Mayor Bush asked how many medians would be installed.

                 Mr. Schmidt stated at least five. The cost/benefit ratio is extremely high for this
project. It will help to prevent accidents and save lives.

              Council Member Snow asked if UDOT would contract with the Kearns Metro
Township for maintenance of the area.

             Mr. Schmidt stated UDOT would put in the infrastructure and Kearns Metro
Township would put in the landscaping.

               Mayor Bush asked if access would be limited to any businesses in the area.

            Mr. Schmidt stated access to the Salt Lake County Library would be limited.
However, UDOT would put in a walkway to the library.

               Council Member Peterson asked if a lane would be eliminated when the barriers
went in.

               Mr. Schmidt stated the barriers would be in the middle of the road, using the two-
way turning lane.

Stakeholders

Unified Police Department – Crime Deterrence Equipment Request

                Chief Levi Hughes, Unified Police Department (UPD), stated the new active patrol
model is proving to be very successful as the stats show a decline in drive by shooting, burglary,
weapon offenses, and damage property calls. With the new model, officers are more visible and
interact with residents.

             Chief Hughes and Officer Peter Jin then requested additional crime deterrence
equipment that would help officers in Kearns better serve the community. The new requests
include:

   -   UTV – This is basically a side by side, which would allow officers to be more visible within
       the community during community events. This vehicle would also be used to help with the
       homeless problem in areas that are not accessible in the normal patrol manner.

   -   Oculus Training System – This system is basically a video game system where the trainee
       is virtually in a 3D realm. The training is state of the art. However, the cost would be
       ongoing for five years. One year would not be long enough to train all his officers. He is
       hoping the UPD would contribute to this cost as well.

              Mayor Bush stated this item should be put on hold until the UPD and other cities
within the UPD have had an opportunity to opine on this.

   -   Shotguns – This is not the lethal weapon but are shotguns that shot a 40-caliber sponge.
       This would stop a criminal without killing them, it would save lives.

   -   Ballistic Shields/Rams - The shields are approximately 3 feet tall with a window and lights
       on them. The precinct only has three or four of these shields and he would like to have at
       least 10.

   -   Drone – Kearns Precinct would like to have its own drone and drone pilot. This would allow
       officers to see what is happening in an area before going in. The drone would be used a
       lot.

   -   LPR Cameras for Intersections

   -   Stop Sticks – These are used to stop fleeing vehicles. The sticks are used once and then
       send back to the manufacture who would replace the ones that were ran over.

   -   Decimal Meters – In order to prosecute those cases a decimal device is needed.

   -   Bluetooth Mic – This would allow plainclothes officers to communicate without others
       knowing.

   -   Night vision – These would be purchased to be used in the warehouse district and other
       unlighted areas.

   -   Digital sign – This would be placed outside the precinct and would be used as a message
       board for the community. It would advertise upcoming events or be used to keep the
       residents of Kearns informed on traffic or other issues as needed.

The total cost of this request is $193,000. If the Council subtracts the cost of the Oculus Training
System, it will come to just under $130,000.

               Mayor Bush stated the funds to cover this request would come out of the $1.4
million the Council set aside for the shot spotter. She wanted to make it clear that these items
would be assets of the Kearns Precinct and would stay in Kearns.

Code Enforcement in Deterring Crime

               Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Kearns Metro Township, stated Kearns Metro
Township Council amended the business license revocation ordinance last year and was able to
use it to revoke a business license where numerous criminal activities were taking place. The
Council can also use the Uniform Dangerous Building and Abatement Code, which talks about a
building being considered a public nuisance if it is used for criminal activities. The Council would
have the authority to abate these activities. It could evict any renter. This has not been used in
Kearns yet, but the Code gives Kearns the authority to do so. He asked what other resources
Chief Hughes would need from the Council if it decided to pursue these types of abatement
activities.

             Chief Hughes stated the best way to figure that out would be to test the law. It
would be more of a trial and error process.

                 Mr. Bracken stated he would need evidence and documentation that he could use
to file a claim. This coming year, he will work on refining the Kearns Code to better align with the
State Code.

               Chief Hughes stated there are a couple of homes in the Kearns Township that he
could use the code on and will start in that direction.

Kearns Community Council

– Night Out Against Crime

               Mayor Bush stated every year the Council contributes to the Night Out Against
Crime activity. The focus this year will be on crime prevention.

                Roger Snow stated the Night Out Against Crime activity will be held on June 18,
2021, from 6 to 9 p.m. It will be held at the Unified Police Department (UPD) precinct. A dinner
of sandwiches, chips, and cookies will be served. The dinner will be premade and presealed.
Swag bags will also be handed out. The UPD will give individual tours of its new precinct as well
as the community center. In 400 of the swag bags there will be motion-sensitive, solar-power
lights so residence can put on their garages to light up the area. Lights are a good way to deter
crime. The Community Council will also hand out 100 Ring doorbells through a raffle. The only
requirement for this doorbell is that the residence has to have WIFI. Installation for this doorbell
will be provided. It is asking for $15,000 from the Council for this activity.

                Mayor Bush stated the only concern she had with the doorbell is she did not want
the doorbell to be sold or returned for money. She asked if the UPC code was blackout or removed
if that would prevent it.

              Chief Hughes stated if it is not disclosed where the doorbell was purchased it
would deter the returning of it.

              Council Member Snow stated we have to trust that the winner will keep it. We
cannot deter everyone.

                Council Member Peterson stated with the purchase of 100 doorbells, 20 could
be installed in each Council district. That way the doorbells would be evenly distributed.

                Mr. Snow stated the community council could make that work. People could sign
up for the raffle according to district. If 20 people in one district did not sign up, then those that
did would get the doorbell. If there were more than 20 people, than a raffle could be held.

                Council Member Butterfield suggested that a police officer deliver the doorbell
and possibly help with the installation. This would give the police an opportunity to get to know
the citizens of Kearns.

               Mr. Snow stated that was a possibility, he would investigate it.

               Council Member Butterfield, seconded by Council Member Schaeffer, moved to
allocate $20,000 to be used for the Night Out Against Crime event for the purchase of doorbells
(20 for each district) and security lights. The motion passed unanimously.
