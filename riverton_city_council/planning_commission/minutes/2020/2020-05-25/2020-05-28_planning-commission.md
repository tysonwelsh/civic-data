1.     SUBDIVISION, RUSSELL WILSON WITH SYMPHONY HOMES IS REQUESTING
       APPROVAL OF “RIVER RIDGE LANE SUBDIVISION” LOCATED AT
       APPROXIMATELY 13239 SOUTH LOVERS LANE.

Development Services Director, Jason Lethbridge, presented the staff report and stated that
the above item qualifies for a single-phase subdivision approval. The property was identified
on a map displayed. The above matter was included in a previous rezone application and
is currently zoned RR-22. The entire area consists of RR-22 and R-1 zoning with large
animal rights.

Mr. Lethbridge reviewed the roadways and access connections for the property. He reported
that there are two stormwater management areas on each end of the development running
east to west. These will consist of two landscaped stormwater ponds and a creek channel
with easements.

Fencing for the property will consist of a six-foot Trex-style fence, a six-foot pre-cast concrete
fence, and a wrought iron fence with columns. The proposed fencing was determined to
meet all ordinance requirements.

The Code requirements for road connectivity were reviewed. Mr. Lethbridge reported that
the detention area to the east is an option for the City to create a right of way for the south.

He explained that the conditions for approval are more extensive in the staff report than
usual and staff recommended approval.

Commissioner James reported that he read the public comments on the above item and
spent time at the property. Connectivity to the north and making the pond institutional were
of concern. He further asked if CC&Rs will be appropriate for the development and if the
City will be following the zoning for large animals. Mr. Lethbridge explained that the number
of animals allowed will be based on the City’s zoning requirements and that CC&Rs are
required only for the common open spaces. City Attorney, Craig Bott, further stated that the
City cannot consider CC&Rs in any decision and they cannot be enforced by the City. They
are a contract between individuals. Mr. Lethbridge explained that there is a current stub in
the plans of the above item to the north for the potential access. Commissioner James
asked if the stub on 13200 South is sufficient to provide access to the north if it is developed
residentially. Mr. Lethbridge confirmed that it is and that it meets all City Standards.

Commissioner Rushton asked about the sidewalk requirements for one-half acre or one-
acre lots and if there is an advantage to the Trex-style fencing over pre-cast cement.
Mr. Lethbridge was unaware of any advantage but stated that the Trex-style fencing was
chosen to meet the ordinance requirements. The Commission will ultimately determine the
fencing types in the development. Sidewalk requirements are not generally based on
zoning, the individual zones do not have requirements for sidewalks. Input from the Land
Use Authority and City Engineer will be considered in determining the sidewalk
requirements.

Chair Cannon proposed the importance of directing foot traffic off of the road with a sidewalk
to the existing business near the neighborhood. He asked what the depth of the sewer is
off of River Ridge Lane and if it will be problematic to run a stub line from River Ridge Lane
back to the south. Chair Cannon also asked if the potential connectivity to the south at the
detention pond should be resolved before the development of the property.

Commissioner Russell reported that there are five-foot-wide sidewalks along each way and
asked for the developer to address the 15-foot temporary gravel road that runs parallel to
each public road.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that there have been questions about the barn and staff is mindful
of it. The east detention pond was also an issue of concern and for the access to meet the
minimum requirement. Future frontage or access would be addressed by the Planning
Commission. City Engineer, Greg Wilson, reported that the sewer is 15 to 18 feet deep and
drains to the east through the proposed development.

Land Manager for Symphony Homes, Russ Wilson, presented a presentation on Symphony
Homes history and home designs. The site plan for the above item was reviewed. City code
permits horses in the RR-22 zone, however, most homeowners prefer not to have horses.
He concurred with information and suggestions from staff.

Russ Wilson explained that the temporary gravel roads are to provide access for the existing
residents to the north during construction and will be abandoned when public roads are built.

Commissioner James asked what the percentage of water is on the property being
discussed and where the water is currently coming from. Mr. Wilson reported that most of
the water is on the proposed property and comes from a culvert on the opposite side of
Lovers Lane. The pond area was dug out by the previous owner to provide easier access
to the water for their livestock. Symphony Homes obtained a Stream Alteration Permit from
the State and is currently realigning the pond to its original condition.

Commissioner Rushton referred to a letter submitted from Tamera Larson regarding the
agreement on the fencing between the proposed property and the property she retains.
Mr. Wilson reported that Ms. Larson agreed to the fence and to share in the cost.

Commissioner Lefgren asked if the garages will be recessed. Mr. Wilson stated that the
garages will be in front of the homes and that there will be a setback.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Barbara Dillman, a Lovers Lane resident, stated that Lovers Lane is not a wide road. She
was concerned with the small access on 13200 South and the new traffic generated from
the 16 homes in the potential development.

Brad Gilson, a resident and former Riverton City Engineer, was present representing Ken
Olson who is the property owner to the south. Mr. Gilson reviewed the water study and
stated that Mr. Olson suggested that the retention pond be moved to his property to prevent
the point discharge and potential impact on his property. In response to a question,
Mr. Wilson reported that the retention pond is to be located on the low end of the two
proposed developments. Commissioner Lefgren asked why the developer had not accepted
Mr. Olson’s offer.

Mr. Bott clarified that the land to the east, where the retention pond will go, is where the
connecting road is planned to go to the south. When the road is constructed, the City will
move the retention pond. Moving the retention pond to the location of the connecting road
will hamper development.

Chair Cannon asked if moving both retention ponds to the middle of the property had been
discussed and if doing so would decrease the density. Mr. Gilson responded that this has
been discussed with the developer and it did not decrease the number of lots.

Commissioner James asked where the connectivity would be if the retention pond was
located to the south. Mr. Gilson explained that if the pond were in the far northeast corner
of Mr. Olson’s property, on the south side of the proposed development, the connectivity
would be on the south side of Lot 112. A drainage and the easement would be placed
between Lot 112 and the lot next to it. Mr. Gilson confirmed that the creek has been piped.

Mr. Bott explained that it is the developer’s choice to determine where the contents of the
property are located. The Commission’s function is only to determine if the plan submitted
meets the City requirements.

Commissioner James was interested in good planning and was looking at connectivity for
the future to prevent potential issues.

Dorreen Waldorf, a Lovers Lane resident, expressed concern with additional traffic as a
result of the proposed development. The water concern begins on the west side of Lovers
Lane, crosses the road, and moves onto the subject property item and Mr. Olson’s property.
The water then runs south and is shared with multiple residents on Lovers Lane who use
the water for their horses. This water has been undisturbed and is safe for animals to drink.
She was concerned that the water will not be safe to drink if it is redirected.

Tom Scribner, an attorney from Utah County, was present on behalf of Mr. Olson and
addressed the drainage concern. Gilson Engineering completed a study for the drainage
and designed a plan that will result in 700 gallons of water per minute. Mr. Scribner reported
that the current Engineering Plan is 14 years old and does include the entire process. He
recommended that the issue be addressed immediately and that the project be postponed
until the engineers are on the same page.

Dale Bennett from Benchmark Engineering was representing the engineer and surveyor of
record for the developer. He reported that Symphony Homes had Bowen and Collins
conduct a study that showed that the discharge from future development meets City
requirements. During a 100-year storm, the drainage will be flooded and have a substantial
amount of water regardless of the property. Mr. Bennett reviewed the Drainage Plan in the
current site plan. He explained that the proposed system will reduce the flow of water. Every
property has been accounted for and the design meets City requirements.

City Engineer, Trace Robinson, stated that lag time and the drainage area needs to be
considered. He reviewed the history and the City’s plan to reduce the amount of water
draining to this area.

Mr. Olson asked that the comments submitted by Morgan Hamilton and Lori Olson be read
aloud. Commissioner Rushton reported they are in the record and have been read by the
Planning Commission Members. Mr. Olsen read from the City Code concerning connectivity
and expressed concern with being landlocked when stub roads should be established. He
felt that the City was ignoring the Code requirements. Mr. Olson reported that Mr. Lethbridge
approached a neighbor asking if there was interest in a stub road. Symphony Homes agreed
to give Mr. Olson a 30-foot access. He agreed to take the stormwater and pay for all of the
improvements to upgrade the system but Symphony Homes did not honor the agreement.
Water on the proposed development does not drain to Butterfield Creek as has been
reported. Mr. Olson disagreed with the current situation and stated that a stub road should
be required.

Mr. Lethbridge did not know the individual Mr. Olson was referring to and stated that he has
not had any communication with property owners to the south other than Mr. Olson. In
addition, he has not made any offers on behalf of the City.

Megan McDougal, a Lovers Lane resident, reported that her home has been flooded due to
rain. The water is important to the neighborhood and has a significant impact on the
community and animals in the area. There are no sidewalks and the residents know to be
careful when children are walking to school. Traffic will increase with 16 homes and a small
access into the neighborhood will be an issue.

John Wheatley, a Riverton resident and Vice President of Symphony Homes, stated that the
subdivision is designed with a natural high point in the land. A portion of the water naturally
drains to the east and naturally drains to the west hence the two retention ponds on the east
and west sides. An engineer designed them to meter out the water. Due to the size of
Mr. Olson’s property, he could build an access road to Lovers Lane to accommodate more
homes. Symphony Homes does not need to provide access and will charge Mr. Olson for
a sewer because it devalues the lots.

Scott McDougal, a Lovers Lane resident, reported that he shares an easement through
Mr. Olson’s property to access his home. The beauty of the area was described. The pond
attracted his family to the area when they were looking to buy a home. The pond is what
makes Lovers Lane unique. This development will detract from the beauty of the area.
Mr. McDougal asked the Commission to consider this. He pointed out that his basement
has been flooded on at least two occasions in the last three years due to water issues.

Kevin Anderson, a land use attorney representing Symphony Homes, pointed out the
following:

       1.     Staff has determined that the proposal satisfies the City standards.

       2.     Concerning connectivity, the City has proposed the south connectivity or stub
              road because the property to the south will provide the most efficient access
              to the proposed development, which is consistent with City requirements.

       3.     With respect to drainage, the Drainage Plan in the site plan is reduced by one-
              third of the current plan.

The above three issues demonstrate that the plan, as proposed, satisfies the City
requirements and should be approved.

Perry Newman, an abutting property owner, reported that his property line is to the west and
north. His grandfather previously owned the subject property. He asked who owns the
property and if the irrigation water that puddles to the south of the barn weekly has been
considered. He read a comment from another neighbor. Riverton City’s Connectivity Code
was reviewed and Mr. Newman stated that if the City does not require logical connectivity
with associated utilities, the City is ignoring the Code and eliminating the ability to follow the
Code for connections, public health, sewer, and water.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Mr. Wilson reported that Tamara Larson continues to irrigate her field and the remainder
property. She has an irrigation detention basin and irrigates her field with sprinklers and the
run-off water comes down a ditch. It was noted that the photograph displayed of Mr. Olson

Commissioner Rushton asked if the City will be required to use eminent domain to add the

Commissioner Lefgren asked if an easement in a setback affects the lot premium of a

for the sewer easement because people are not willing to pay the price for the lot with the

recommended that an arrangement be made between property owners to acquire the

Chair Cannon reviewed the history of the area and reported that there are two separate ditch
issues. The irrigation water on Ms. Larson’s property is a different canal than the one that
services Mr. Olson’s property.

Commissioner Lefgren asked what the long-term traffic plan is for more homes in the area
considering emergency access, secondary access, etc. Greg Wilson reported that a traffic
study is usually requested. In this situation, a traffic memo was requested and it was
determined that traffic is an inconvenience; however, it will not impact the drivability of
Lovers Lane. Commissioner Lefgren asked if the residents would use a dirt road if
emergency vehicles occupy the neighborhood road. Mr. Wilson reported that the road will
be paved when the land to the south is developed. In response to a question, Russ Wilson
reported that currently Symphony Homes maintains the dirt road and he assured the
Commission that it will meet Fire Code. Mr. Lethbridge reported that the City’s Fire Marshall
participates in the review of site plans.

In response to a question, Russ Wilson reported that Symphony Homes will ticket those who
encroach into the right-of-way, so emergency vehicles can access the area. Commissioner
Hansen stated that it is significant to address Lovers Lane with a City perspective.

Commissioner James stated that there is conflicting testimony regarding the above matter.
Technical answers are in conflict and connectivity has not been resolved with the intent of
the General Plan and ordinance. The proposal does not meet the full standard of the City’s
subdivision ordinance. Commissioner James suggested tabling the matter to resolve a
consensus of connectivity and determine the impact of the pond issues.

2.    AMENDED CONDITIONAL USE, KENNETH OLSON IS SEEKING AN
      AMENDMENT TO A PREVIOUSLY APPROVED CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT FOR
      A PRIVATE LANE LOCATED AT 13265 SOUTH LOVERS LANE.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the staff report and stated that the above item was approved by
the Planning Commission in November of 2017. The property was identified on a map
displayed. The applicant made application for an amendment to the original approval
including two conditions that address the width of the private lane. Mr. Lethbridge read
Conditions 4 and 5 and reported that Mr. Olson is bound by the written minutes of the
meeting. The updated plan was on a map displayed.

Chair Cannon opened the public hearing.

Mr. Olson referred to the audio recording from the November 2017 Planning Commission
Meeting and stated that the Commission approved the conditional use permit with a 25-foot
lane for an access easement and 20 feet for the second home. Mr. Olson claimed that the
minutes were written incorrectly and inconsistent with the motion made by the Commission.
Riverton City Planning Commission Meeting                                              9
May 28, 2020

Mr. Olson attempted to play a recording of the Planning Commission Meeting from that
evening but was not able to.

Mr. Scribner stated that this is an error and can be added to the original approval.

Commissioner Rushton asked if a wider road would assist in the applicant’s future goals.
Mr. Scribner stated that the applicant does not desire a wider road because the currently
approved lane will cost a significant amount of money.

Mr. Lethbridge stated that the minutes were approved by the Planning Commission and not
altered at any point before or after the approval. The Commission can amend the conditional
use permit. Eliminating Condition 4 will allow the lane width to be reduced.

Mr. Gilson stated that the measurements required in the current approval will not work from
a design perspective because of the curve in the lane.

Ms. McDougal stated that she listened to the recording and that the written minutes were
incorrect.

There were no further public comments. The public hearing was closed.

Commissioner Hansen asked why it has taken 2.5 years for the applicant to acknowledge
the error. The staff member responsible for the previous application documented in the
meeting minutes that the lane could be 25 feet on the access easement. When the original
request for the approval was made there were no edits to the eight Conditions provided.
This may have been suggested and missed by the Commission. In response to a question,
Mr. Lethbridge reported that the 20-foot lane will meet all requirements for emergency
vehicles. The Fire Department reviewed the matter and did not have any issues.

Commissioner Hansen moved to approve PLZ 20-2015, amending the Conditional Use
Permit for a Private Lane located at 13265 South Lovers Lane originally approved
November 9, 2017, as follows:

       1.     Remove Condition #4 from the original approval.

The motion was seconded by Commissioner Hartley. The motion passed with the
unanimous consent of the Commission.

DISCUSSION ITEMS

4.     RESIDENTIAL LOT STANDARDS, DISCUSSION OF CURRENT STANDARDS
       FOR RESIDENTIAL LOT SIZE AND SHAPE.

Mr. Lethbridge presented the staff report and stated that there has been discussion on
parcels of land with awkward configurations. The City does not have an ordinance that
specifies a minimum width. Prohibiting flag lots and setting a minimum width to a lot are
ideas to address the issue.

Commissioner Lefgren stated that private lanes that service one lot should be calculated
into the acreage of the land. Mr. Lethbridge stated the private lane ordinance will be
reviewed and setbacks and lot size calculations addressed. Commissioner Lefgren stated
that there is a need for a minimum on the width of those awkward parcels.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that another discussion item is what constitutes an irregular lot.
There is a need to define it and an example was given to illustrate the concern.

There were no objections or concerns from the Commission. Mr. Lethbridge offered to bring
the matter back in the next ordinance amendment package.
