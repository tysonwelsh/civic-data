 PC 22-046 Cytiva Admin Building [Design Review Permit] Wendy Zane/Hyclone Laboratories
 Inc Corp, authorized agent/owner are requesting a Design Review Permit to construct a new
 20,648 square foot addition to their campus that will house office space, conference room space,
 and a break room that will provide a central location for campus gathering. This project is
 located at 1725 Hyclone Rd in the Industrial Park (IP) zone; TIN 02-083-0018; -0016 (Woodruff
 Neighborhood).


STAFF: R. Holley reviewed the request for a Design Review Permit to construct a new 20,648
administrative building addition to the Hyclone campus located at 1725 Hyclone Road in the


Industrial Park (IP) zone. The administration building addition will be located southwest of their
current building and over a portion of the existing parking lot. The lost parking stalls have been
replaced in a new parking lot west of the manufacturing and storage buildings near 1000 West.

The Land Development Code requires new buildings to have varied facades as viewed from adjacent
streets. Blank walls exceeding 40 feet are prohibited. The Land Development Code states that 30%
transparency (windows, fenestration) is required on street-facing front facades. The proposed
addition shows a variety of materials and glass except for on one east-facing (inwards) facade. This
area is approximately 50 feet in width and is comprised of blank concrete block wall. The Planning
Commission will need to determine the code applicability of this wall section, as it is screened by
existing buildings to the southeast and not very visible from the adjacent streets.

R. Holley also let the commission know that condition number 11 regarding fencing was a typo and
should be removed.

Commissioner Croshaw asked about the proposed food truck parking. R. Holley said that all food
trucks are required to obtain a license through the city.

PROPONENT: Ryan Berry, the architect on the project said that the food truck parking will be for
special occasions when they bring food trucks in for their employees. He also mentioned that the
east wall was left blank for future growth, if they need more office space, they will fill in that area.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Peterson said that he doesn’t see where the blank wall would cause
any issues where it is not visible. Commissioner Guth said it would be nice for the blank wall to be
broken up a little bit or screened with some landscaping. R. Holley said that they could paint a stripe
on the blank wall and that would meet the code requirement. Commissioner Heare stated that he is
okay with the blank wall. Commissioner Doutre said that the wall will be screened by other buildings
and landscaping. Commissioner Guth is worried about setting precedence for future projects to
request the same allowance. Mohammed Abdullahi, Assistant City Attorney said if you can articulate
the reason for the allowance, it is fine. The commission discussed if the east-facing wall is street-
facing. R. Holley stated that from the highway, as you drive by, you may be able to see a glimpse
of the wall between the trees and the existing building. You will not be able to see the wall broadside
from the street. The Planning Commission determined that the blank wall was not visible from
adjoining public streets.

PC 22-038 Land Development Code Amendment-Mixed Use, Chapters 17.10 & 17.34 -
Continued from the July 28th meeting. [Code Amendment] Mike DeSimone/Logan City are
requesting a Code Amendment to the Land Development Code to Chapter 17.10.100 Mixed-Use
Development Standards and Chapter 17.34 Residential Density and Height Bonuses. The changes
modify specific density, design & development standards for projects in the mixed-use zones as
well as establish a density bonus program for mixed-use projects.


STAFF/PROPONENT: M. DeSimone review the request for an amendment to the Land
Development Code, Chapters 17.10 & 17.34, Mixed Use. This is a proposal to modify the City’s
approach to Mixed Use Development by clarifying the intent and expected outcomes in the Mixed
Use zone, modifying the base density, adding performance-based density bonuses, encouraging
additional open space, useable outdoor space, and public gathering areas, increasing the
proportionate amount of commercial building area, requiring commercial uses along major street


frontages, requiring a variety of residential structure types, modifying the allowable building height,
and allowing for a reduction in minimum parking requirements.

The General Plan and LDC both describe a Mixed-Use Center as a dense, compact, walkable
environment containing a mixture of uses, including a commercial base that serves the project’s
resident population. The inherent difficulty with any regulatory approach to Mixed Use is in trying to
prescribe a set of qualitative & design based standards to a unique project in a diverse neighborhood
with the recognition that one size does not fit all. These proposed changes to the Mixed Use
Development Standards reflect an evolution of thinking and are reasonable and appropriate.

In response to the July 14, 2022 Planning Commission hearing, the following changes were made
to Chapters 17.10 and 17.34 of the Land Development Code:

 •   Moved the Mixed Use Design Characteristics language to the Intent Statement.

 •   Clarified where Residential Uses are allowed in a vertical mixed use building.

 •   Clarified the language regarding Useable Outdoor Space, Public Gathering Space and linked
     to Chapter 17.28. Added a definition of public gathering areas include plazas, parks, public
     squares, sidewalks in front of small businesses, courtyards with seating, or any other area that
     provides outdoor seating (chairs or benches), are visually prominent, and are easily accessible.
     All of these items are qualitative items yet measurable.

 •   Added language giving the Planning Commission discretion when reviewing a phasing or
     timing plan for residential and commercial construction. The goal is to time commercial and
     residential construction so that they occur simultaneously; however, the City needs to be able
     to afford some flexibility to a project if warranted.

 •   Provided a reference in Residential Structure Type to Chapters 17.08 & 17.62 where the
     descriptions are located.

 •   In the Residential Density Bonus element, clarified the link back to Chapter 17.34 where the
     specific standards for residential density bonuses are located, and included the provision of
     “Complete Streets” as a project enhancement.

 •   Added a requirement that the minimum sidewalk width in front of a commercial area shall be
     at least 10’. The purpose of this is to create areas where patrons and pedestrians can
     congregate.

 •   Added a requirement that public gathering areas shall comprise at least 40% of the minimum
     Useable Outdoor Space requirement. The Mixed Use requirements are 10% of the total project
     area has to be set aside as open space and 10% has to be set aside as useable outdoor space.
     Chapter 17.28 defines both of these types of open areas.

 •   Included a definition of “Complete Streets” and “Public Gathering Areas.”

 •   Clarified the language around “Enhanced Commercial” versus “Additional Commercial” so that
     we are offering a density bonus for additional commercial square footage above and beyond
     the minimum required. Chapter 17.34 establishes the baseline of 15 additional residential units
     per acre if the minimum amount of required commercial space is doubled.


 •   Included language giving the PC flexibility in reviewing & applying a minimum residential and/or
     commercial requirement in the mixed use zone.

Commissioner Peterson asked how the 5% affordable housing bonus would be enforced or how the
city ensures that the apartments stay low-income for multiple years. M. DeSimone said that there
would be a binding contract signed and recorded that states the number of low-income units that will
be maintained. Commissioner Peterson asked how the parking reductions for vertical housing would
work. M. DeSimone said that depending on the number of units and the unit types, the parking stalls
per unit could be lowered through an alternative parking plan. Commissioner Doutre asked if the
density bonus for enhanced open space could be updated to state that increasing the outdoor space
could allow a decrease in commercial space or an increase in residential density instead of offering
both. And if there could also be a bonus or standard added that would allow for a decrease in
commercial space based on the proximity to an adjacent commercial or large employer.
Commissioner Hear talked about having a mixed-use zone that allows bonuses for the adjacent
developments and not having to create commercial space. Commissioner Peterson said that would
not be a mixed-use zone. One of the key elements of a mixed-use zone is retail or commercial and
residential together. If mixed-use developments have less commercial space required, the code
goes back to what it originally was and continues to allow projects similar to Homestead.
Commissioner Doutre said that the adjacent commercial space needed to qualify for a reduction in
commercial space would have to be a specific size that is proportionate to the size of the proposed
mixed-use development. The new development would also have to include a pedestrian connection
to the adjacent commercial areas.

There was also discussion about modifying the list of uses permitted in the MU zone.

PUBLIC: No public comments were made.

COMMISSION: There was discussion about including either an allowance for reducing the
commercial requirements for a mixed use project located close to existing commercial areas or
creating another density bonus type of approach. There was further discussion about open space
and useable outdoor space requirements, including connectivity between mixed use projects and
adjoining areas. The commission discussed what the minimum sidewalk width should be in front of
commercial spaces and directed staff to increase the minimum width. The commission also
discussed the range of uses appropriate for a mixed-use zone. M. DeSimone stated that any decision
on modifying the list of uses would be handled through a separate code amendment.
