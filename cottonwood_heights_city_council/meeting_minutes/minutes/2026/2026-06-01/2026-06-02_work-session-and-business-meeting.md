        4.3      Public Safety Range Discussion – Police Chief, Robby Russo.

Police Chief, Robby Russo, reported that the Public Safety Training Range is a strategic
investment in officer readiness, regional partnerships, and community safety. The City’s vision
was to partner with other agencies along the Wasatch Front, and they had obtained signed letters
of acknowledgment and appreciation from police chiefs in the Salt Lake Valley, Utah Highway
Patrol, the Canyons School District, and others.

The project will provide a secure, modern, indoor firearms range for the Cottonwood Heights
Police Department (“CHPD”), school guardians, and regional partners. Scenarios can be produced
on screen in a controlled environment so that, for example, an officer can be trained on how to
react to a car driving toward him while trying to flee, which will improve officer readiness and
response capabilities and may avoid a crisis in the future. The public safety asset was supported
through federal funding and designed with sound mitigation, security, and aesthetics in mind.

Chief Russo reviewed the financial aspects of the project as follows:

    •   A $2.1 million federal grant was received to purchase the range.
    •   The CHPD currently pays approximately $7,000 each year to rent outside ranges for
        training.
    •   The projected annual outside agency revenue was projected at a minimum of $62,000 based
        on rates for the Murray range. CHPD currently pays $500 per one-half day in rental fees,
        and the rate was increasing to $600.
    •   Long-term maintenance costs were estimated at $17,500 per year to maintain the filtration
        system. Lead removal cost was net zero as removal contractors credited the City for its
        value.

Independent sound testing for the Multiple interactive Learning Objectives (“MILO”) range that
was provided by the manufacturer indicates that sound levels drop from 102 to 103 dB inside the
range to approximately 70 dB at 100 feet, which is comparable to common urban background
noise. The closest home to the proposed location is 140 feet away. Hearing and eye protection
are required inside the range. Chief Russo indicated that the ranges visited by the City Council
were not insulated, but the City’s range will have modern sound mitigation systems.

In response to a question from Council Member Hyland, Chief Russo confirmed that there would
also be an eight-foot wall and landscaping surrounding the range, which will further reduce noise.

Chief Russo reported that a Certified Range Master is required during live-fire operations, but an
additional full-time equivalent (“FTE”) employee would not be required, as each agency will bring

        4.4      Flash Vote Discussion – Council Member, Suzanne Hyland.

Council Member Hyland stated that when the City contracted with FlashVote, the City Council
was assured that they would help develop a member panel that is representative of the community.
The goal in sampling is to have a sample that represents the population, and the gold standard for
statistical surveys is a random sample. Y2K Analytics is expensive because they keep reaching
out to people until they have enough respondents to be representative of the population, which
takes a lot of time. FlashVote offered an alternate opportunity to develop a member panel that is
representative of the population. The FlashVote margin of error is higher than other methods
because the member panel is substituted for a random sample.

The reason the Council decided to fund the surveys was because they want trusted results, and
FlashVote assured them that this could be accomplished through the member panel. The surveys
are conducted quickly rather than taking weeks like random sampling, but the tradeoff is accuracy.

Council Member Hyland discussed the member panel. Outreach had been conducted through the
community newsletter, Council efforts, and other methods. The current member panel was
approximately 50% male and female. Age ranges were also equally represented with the exception
of age 30 and under, which FlashVote had indicated was not uncommon. Cottonwood Heights is
predominately an older community, so the current age distribution was representative of the
population. However, there was a discrepancy in district participation, with District 1 only having
approximately 16% participation and District 4 having approximately 35%.

Council Member Hyland initially spoke with FlashVote about either adjusting the member panel
or selecting participants to even out the differences between District 4 and District 1. However,
FlashVote wants all the raw data they can get. Instead of randomizing the member panel, their
solution was to weight survey data to balance representation across districts, which they indicated
would ensure equal influence from all districts regardless of population size. They are developing
a dashboard that will enable the City to look at the results differently.
