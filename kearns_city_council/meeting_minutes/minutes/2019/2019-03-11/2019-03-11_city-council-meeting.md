Salt Lake County Planning and Development Services Division

              Application No. 30882 – Adoption of a new chapter to the Kearns Metro Zoning
Ordinance to create two “public facilities” zones, the PF and PI zones, as zoning in which public

uses like parks, schools, recreation centers, civic buildings, churches, utility buildings, and similar
uses are allowed.

                  Curtis Woodward, Salt Lake County Planning and Development Services
Division, stated the main purpose for having a public facility zone is for clarification. The current
zoning map does not show where the key public facilities are. The proposed chapter is patterned
after zones that were adopted by Draper City. There are two different types of zones – the PF
zone is for those buildings that are publicly owned such as the recreation center. The PI zone
are for facilities that are not owned by the government but used by the public such as churches.
There are designs standards that are built into the zones such as height, coverage, and set back
from property lines. The Kearns Metro Township Planning Commission at its meeting held tonight,
voted to continue this application for one month to allow time for the Salt Lake County Planning
and Development Services Division to look closer at the height and coverage requirements. There
are two ways to deal with the adoption of this type of ordinance. The Council can consider the
ordinance and the map together or it can adopt them one at a time. The Kearns Metro Township
Planning Commission is leaning towards adopting the two separately.

               The Council agreed that the adoption of the two items should be done separately.

               Mayor Bush stated the Council would table this item for one month.

            Pam Roberts, Executive Director, Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling District
(WFWRD), presented the 2018 yearend report updating the Council on the following items:

    Kearns diverted close to 20 percent of its waste away from the landfill by recycling, use of
     green trailer, and the green curb side pickup program.

    WFWRD is reaching out to elementary schools to encourage recycling.

    Green waste services will start in the Kearns area on March 18, 2019.

    WFWRD is focusing on developing leadership qualities within its organization.

    A new financial controller was hired.

    Approximately 258 Kearns residents responded to the customer satisfaction survey, which
     indicated that 94.6 percent were satisfied with services.

    Employee satisfaction is close to 83 percent. This is a 10 percent increase from 2016.

    Since 2012, WFWRD has had four winners and five runner ups for the national driver of
     the year contest sponsored by the National Recycling Association.

    Last year, with 46 side trucks and 2 front loader trucks, WFWRD saved over .5 million in
     fuel cost by switching to C&G fuel.

    The clean-up program was restructured with WFWRD collecting over 160 tons of metal
     resulting in over $21,000 in revenue.

    Mattresses were collected separately and taken to Spring Back Mattresses to be recycled.

               Council Member Snow stated she thought the clean-up program rotated when
pick up would be within the different neighborhoods. Pickup this year is scheduled for August,
which is the same time as last year.

                Ms. Roberts stated this year a new rotation was started and that would explain
why it is the same time as last year. The biggest complaint that she receives on this program is
that citizens would like it to happen twice a year or the dumpsters remain in the neighbors for two
days instead of the one.

               Mayor Bush asked if bigger dumpster could be used. The dumpsters fill up fast.

               Ms. Roberts stated the dumpster are 16 yards. It would cost more to go to a larger
dumpster.

               Council Member Snow asked if the number of dumpsters had been reduced.

Kearns Improvement District

                Pam Gill, Kearns Improvement District, stated recently Sandy City residents had
to deal with contaminated water and she wanted to update the Council on what would happen if
the water in Kearns was contaminated. She stated first the Kearns Improvement District would
contact the State Division of Drinking Water and follow whatever protocol they were instructed to
do. Board members, employees, the Kearns Metro Township Council, and the Kearns Community
Council would also be notified. Information would be posted on the various websites as well as
door to door flyers would go out. Each situation would be different, so it is hard to say what exactly
would happen. In some cases, residents would be told to boil their water and in other
circumstances a no drink order could go out. She then updated the Council on the fix a leak
program and the water conservation kits.

ORDINANCE NO. 19-03-02                                                         Date: March 11, 2019

       AN ORDINANCE OF THE LEGISLATIVE BODY OF THE KEARNS METRO
       TOWNSHIP, UTAH, AMENDING TITLE 14, CHAPTER 16, SECTION 010 OF ITS
       CODE OF ORDINANCES, TO PROHIBIT EXCAVATION OF TOWNSHIP RIGHT-
       OF-WAYS IN CERTAIN CIRCUMSTANCES AND ESTABLISHES MINIMUM
       STANDARDS FOR REPAIRS FOLLOWING EXCAVATIONS IN RIGHT-OF-
       WAYS

The Kearns Metro Township Council ordains as follows:

              SECTION I. The amendments made herein are designated by underlining the
new substituted words. Words being deleted are designated by brackets and interlineations.
Renumbering is designated by parenthesis indicating where the text has moved from or moved
to.

              SECTION II. Section 010, Chapter 16 of Title 14 of the Kearns Metro Township
Code of Ordinances is amended to read as follows:

               A.      No right-of-way of any Township road shall be dug up or excavated and no
approach, road, driveway, pole, pipeline, conduit, sewer, ditch, culvert, billboard, advertising sign,
or other structure or object of any kind or character shall be placed, constructed or maintained
within any such right-of-way except as permitted by and in accordance with the regulations of the
county engineering division acting for the Township.

               (B).   Cuts or open excavations on newly constructed, paved, or overlaid rights-
of-way are not allowed for three years. If an emergency cut or excavation occurs, the responsible
persons shall comply with any special conditions imposed by the Township regarding restoration
of the roadway as detailed in Section C.

               (C).     Any person making any excavation, bore, or boring in or under any
Township right-of-way shall, at their own expense restore the surface to its same type and depth
of pavement as that which is adjoining, including the gravel base material (at a minimum 3”
Bituminous surface and 8” untreated base course). All restoration shall conform to the Township
standards and all restorations shall be accomplished within seventy-two (72) hours from the time
of the excavation, unless additional time is granted in writing by the Township. If, the road has
been constructed, reconstructed, or overlaid within the preceding three (3) years, or the pavement
has been chip sealed or slurry sealed with in the preceding two (2) years the permittee shall be
responsible for the following:

              New Streets, Reconstructed Streets, or Overlaid Streets: The asphalt surface shall
be milled down a minimum depth of two inches from curb (or edge of asphalt) to center of road
and resurfaced with asphalt. If the cut extends through the centerline of the road the milling and

paving will be from curb to curb (or edge of asphalt to edge of asphalt). Milling and paving shall
extend to a length of 15’ in each direction from each side of the cut. The County Engineering or
designee reserves the right to require a length greater than fifteen feet in each direction and a
depth greater that two inches if there are special circumstances.

               Slurried Streets or Chipsealed Streets: The pavement shall be re-slurry sealed with
an approved type II slurry from curb (or edge of asphalt) to center of road and a minimum of 15’
in each direction from each side of the cut. If the cut extends through the centerline of the road
the application of slurry seal will be from curb to curb (or edge of asphalt to edge of asphalt)…

               Excavation of Township rights-of-way during the winter months (herein defined as
October 15 to April 15) will be allowed only if the work is a new service connection, required
maintenance, emergency or otherwise approved by the Township. Permanent patching of a
Township right-of-way excavated in winter shall be delayed until April 15 or later and the outdoor
ambient temperature is 50 degrees and rising. The permittee will maintain a temporary patch until
such time as the permanent surfacing is accomplished. The temporary patch will provide a smooth
driving surface and be restored within 72 hours of being notified that repairs are required.

               (B)(D). Any person who violates the provisions of this section is guilty of a Class C
misdemeanor.

               Section III. This ordinance shall take effect upon publication and posting as

              Section IV.     Immediately following this ordinance, the recorder shall make or
