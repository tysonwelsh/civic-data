ZT-3-2022
West Valley City
An ordinance change to prohibit private streets, allow parking spaces for townhomes and
condominiums attached to the side of each other to back onto a street, and create two new
street classifications for use in planned unit developments.

At the request of the City Council, City staff is proposing an ordinance amendment to:
    1. prohibit private streets,
   2. allow parking spaces for townhomes and condominiums attached to the side of each other
      to back onto a street, and
   3. create two new street classifications for use in planned unit developments.

For the first change, the City Council has expressed concerns about the financial burden private
streets place on the residents of planned unit developments (PUDs) with private streets. The
proposed ordinance would prohibit the creation of new private streets. Streets internal to a
development under one ownership, such as an apartment development where no subdivision is
needed, would still be allowed.

For the second change, the current ordinance includes requirements that prohibit the driveways
for attached housing like townhomes to access public streets. Section 7-9-106 prohibits vehicles
in parking spaces for all development except single and two unit dwellings from backing onto
streets. Section 7-9-107 establishes minimum spacing between driveways for all development
except single and two unit dwellings. The proposed ordinance adds townhomes and condos that
are attached to the side of each other to the list of uses that are excluded from these two
provisions.

For the third change, two new street classifications are proposed for use in planned unit
developments with attached housing. The current standard for residential streets is a 54’ wide
right-of-way composed of 29’ of pavement, 2.5’ of curb and gutter on both sides, 5’ parkstrip on
both sides, and 5’ sidewalk on both sides. The proposed new street classifications are:
     a PUD Primary Street that is a 44’ wide right-of-way composed of 29’ of pavement, 2.5’
        of curb and gutter on both sides, 5’ parkstrip on one side, and 5’ sidewalk on one side and
     a PUD Secondary Street that is a 34’ wide right-of-way composed of 29’ of pavement
        and 2.5’ of curb and gutter on both sides.

There are several reasons for creating two new street classifications for use in PUDs with
attached housing. These reasons are outlined below:
     By ordinance definition, PUDs are intended to allow flexibility and initiative in site and
       building design and location. Having only one residential street classification limits
       flexibility and initiative in design.
     Many PUDs have sidewalks that are internal to the development. For example, the fronts
       of rear-loaded townhomes often face each other with the front door in the front of the
       unit. Requiring sidewalks on all streets would result in sidewalks on the sides of
       buildings with no doors.
     With roughly 92% of the City developed, new development often occurs on small, infill
       parcels or developed parcels that are redeveloped. Such parcels are often narrow, have an
       awkward shape, require connecting to adjoining streets, or exhibit other characteristics
       that make development more challenging. Having only one residential street
       classification would make development on such parcels difficult if not impossible in
       some cases.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. The ordinance should be approved as proposed by staff.
   2. Continuance.

   3. Denial.

                would no longer allow private streets. He stated that an apartment complex with
                its own internal driveways or circulation under one owner would not be
                considered a private street. If there are two or more owners with properties

                talk to Mr. Dahle offline regarding his specific situation.

                Mr. Dahle asked what the reasoning was behind eliminating private streets. Steve
                said that the Council had received comments regarding residents paying both
                property tax and an association fee, and the City is not responsible for the private
                road. They have also been informed of underfunded associations that cannot
                provide proper maintenance or repairs of private streets and those associations are
                coming to the City for help. Mr. Dahle said that it sounds like this is a
                housecleaning issue for residential properties. Steve stated that the Council did
                not limit it to residential properties; it encompasses all properties. Mr. Dahle
                asked if it would be possible to make an exception for industrial properties.

                Chair Meaders asked Mr. Dahle what he believes is the benefit of a private road

                who has access to the property. Steve said if the Commission feels it would be

Z-6-2022
Wadsworth Lake Park, LLC
4625 West Lake Park Blvd.
Zone change from M to B/RP
5.78 acres

Wadsworth Lake Park, LLC has requested a zone change on a 5.78 parcel at 4625 West Lake
Park Blvd. from M (Manufacturing) to B/RP (Business/Research Park). About 30% of the parcel
is already zoned B/RP with the balance being M. Surrounding zones include M to the north, east,
and west and B/RP to the south. Neighboring uses include office buildings to the north, west, and
south and the Stonebridge Golf Course to the east. The subject property is designated as business
park in the West Valley City General Plan and is developed as an office building that, according
to the Salt Lake County Assessor, is 81,170 square feet in area.

The current M zoning on the property does not allow medical office; however, the B/RP zone
does allow medical office. The applicant is requesting this zone change to expand the leasing
options on this property to include medical office. For reference, staff has included the
commercial and manufacturing zone use table to compare the allowed uses in the M and B/RP
zones.

Staff is supportive of this request because:

        a portion of the property is already zone B/RP,
        part of the Lake Park Corporate Center is already zoned B/RP,
        the B/RP zone is more closely aligned with the General Plan designation of business park
         than is the M zone, and
        the M zone includes uses that would, in staff’s opinion, not be appropriate in Lake Park.

Staff Alternatives:

        Approval. The zone change should be approved as it complies with the General Plan.

        Continuance.

        Denial.

Discussion:        Steve Pastorik presented the application. There was no further discussion.

C-11-2022
Castlewood Development
2940 West 3650 South
Conditional use approval for a 330-unit apartment development
3.02 acres


Castlewood Development is requesting conditional use approval to redevelop 3.02 acres at 2940
West 3650 South into a 330 unit, 3 to 7 story, Class A apartment development. The proposed
unit mix for the project is 42 studio units, 193 one-bedroom units, and 95 two-bedroom units.
The majority of the property (2.94 acres) is zoned CC (City Center) and the balance (0.08 acres)
is zoned R-2-8 (Two Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet). The entire
building is within the CC zone. The property is designated as very high density residential (over
20 units/acre) or office for most of the property and promenade/plaza for the remaining north
portion in the Fairbourne Station Vision, which is part of the City’s General Plan. The property is
currently vacant as the office development previously on the property was demolished in 2014.

During the study meeting there were questions raised about parking and possible future
improvements along 3650 South. While there are some locations on 3650 South where on-street
parking can occur now, the frontage of the subject property is not one of those locations.
According to the Public Works Department, 3650 South will be improved between 3200 West
and 2700 West in 2024 or 2025. Planned improvements include consistent curb, gutter, and
sidewalk along both sides of the street as well as a left turn lane in the middle of the street. Once
these improvements are made, no on-street parking will be allowed.

The Commission also inquired about visitor parking since the parking structure is secured. The
applicant is working on solutions to this issue.

Project Review
The first section below outlines the purposes in the CC zone and how this development
accomplishes these purposes. The remaining sections address the main ordinance standards (it is
not a comprehensive list) applicable to this development. Staff provided the applicant with a list
of items where the development plans needed to either be corrected or clarified. At the time this
report was prepared, updated plans had not been received. For this reason, staff is recommending
that this application be continued.

City Center Zone Purposes
The following is a list of purposes from the CC Zone (underlined) as well as a brief description
of how the apartment development meets the stated purpose:
       Create a recognizable center or downtown for West Valley City. At 7 stories, this
        apartment development will add to the unique, urban environment in Fairbourne Station.
       Encourage and direct development that supports transit. High density residential
        development is one of the best land uses for supporting transit.
       Encourage infill and redevelopment near the future transit station by City Hall. The
        apartment building replaces an office development demolished in 2014 and is
        approximately 650’ from the West Valley Central TRAX station and 400’ from the bus
        hub.
       Reinforce the use of public transportation by locating higher-intensity development,
        including employment-oriented businesses and higher density residential uses, adjacent to
        transit stops. This project is creating higher intensity development by replacing a low
        intensity, 1 to 2 story office development with 330 residential units.
       Encourage mixed-use development to reduce automobile dependency and roadway
        congestion by combining trips and locating destinations within walking and biking
        distances – all interconnected with transit. This is the third residential building in
        Fairbourne Station that will complement the hotel, medical office building, police
        headquarters, and professional office building.
       Provide a mix of housing types, costs and densities. This project will provide a variety of
        studio, 1-bedroom, and 2-bedroom layouts with varying rents. At 109.3 units/acre, the
        proposed density of this project exceeds that of Phase 1 of the Residences at Fairbourne
        Station at 78.7 units/acre and of Phase 2 of the Residences at Fairbourne Station at 69.8
        units/acre.
       Promote architectural and site design treatments that enhance the visual appearance of
        development within the Zone. At the time this report was prepared, staff was waiting on
        updated plans to verify compliance with the architectural and site design treatments.

Parking
The parking ordinance allows the Planning Commission to reduce the parking standard for multi-
family residential from 2 spaces per unit to 1.3 spaces per unit for studio units, 1.5 spaces per
unit for one-bedroom units, and 1.75 spaces per unit for two-bedroom units if the Commission
finds that the reduction is not detrimental to the surrounding area and will enhance the proposed
development. The CC Zone also allows a 10% reduction to parking requirements. The applicant
has submitted the attached parking study in the form of a memo from Hales Engineering to
support the proposed amount of parking.

Amenities
For a project of this size, the ordinance requires a minimum of five amenities from the list in the
ordinance with at least three of the five being major amenities. This project has six major
amenities (outdoor swimming pool, courtyards, clubhouse/community room, rooftop patio,
fitness room, and community game room/recreation room) and seven minor amenities (outdoor
fire pit/fireplace, hot tub, building lobby, package locker system, indoor bicycle storage, electric
vehicle charging stations, and outdoor covered pavilion with tables and a grill).

Landscaping and Recreation Space
The ordinance requires a minimum of 10% of the site area as landscaping and a minimum of
40% of the site area as landscaping and recreation space. While staff is waiting on revised plans
to verify these numbers, it appears based on the plans submitted that the project will meet these
standards.

A 5’ landscaped area is needed along the west side of the property and additional detail is need
on the landscape plan.

Architecture
Staff needs updated plans to verify compliance with the architectural standards. There are several
inconsistencies between the floor plans and elevations that also need to be addressed.

Signage
Wall signs are not allowed for residential buildings with frontage on 3650 South. However, a
monument sign is allowed and such a sign could be considered as the required entry feature.

Pedestrian and Bicycle Accommodations
Outdoor bicycle parking is needed. Additional pedestrian connections from the building to the
sidewalk along Lancer Way and to the adjoining park are also needed.

 C-12-2022
 KeyBank West Valley
 2807 S 5600 W
 C-2 Zone (1.12 acres)


The applicant, Ben Gingrich, is requesting conditional use approval for a new bank, which
will be located at 2807 S 5600 W. The property is zoned C-2, General Commercial, and the
West Valley City General Plan designates this area as General Commercial. The subject
property is also located in the 5600 West Overlay Zone. The surrounding zoning is C-2.
Adjacent land uses include a credit union to the north, a restaurant to the east, and multi-
tenant commercial buildings to the south and west.

The applicant is proposing to construct a 2,442 sq. ft. KeyBank branch. Access to the site will
be from existing shared access points on Grantham Lane, which is a private street, Highbury
Parkway, and High Commons Way. There are two existing buildings on the adjacent parcels:
a restaurant to the east and a multi-tenant commercial building to the south. The subject parcel
has an existing pad in the north-west corner that was placed there for a future building. There
have been multiple proposals to develop the pad in the past, but no one has followed through.
The proposal for the bank does not utilize the existing pad. That area will be incorporated into
a portion of the new building footprint and into expanding the parking lot.

The bank will be oriented to the north and will have drive-thru lanes on the east side of the
building. Some of the existing parking lot and landscape islands will be reconfigured. The
applicant is working on providing additional information to staff on the existing uses in the
neighboring buildings so that parking can be determined for the site. The exact number of stalls
needed will be based off the shared parking calculations in 7-9 for all the uses on the site.

The existing 5600 West streetscape landscaping will remain west of the sidewalk. The rest of
the landscaping on the site needs to meet the other general site and parking lot landscaping
requirements of Title 7. The applicant is proposing to remove the lawn east of the sidewalk
and landscape with trees, shrubs, perennials and ornamental grasses. All landscaped areas
need at least 50% coverage of live plant material.

The building shall meet all the Commercial Design Standards outlined in 7-11-300P. Since
the building is also located in the 5600 West Overlay Zone, there are also additional building
requirements for the exterior like 100% masonry materials on the street-facing façade, which
is the west elevation, as well as a requirement that the design of all building elevations shall
be such that there is equal emphasis on all sides of the building.

There will be wall signs on the building. The applicant has not requested a new monument sign
at this time, but there is enough street frontage for one additional monument sign in the future.
All signage on the property shall comply with the regulations in Title 11.

Staff Alternatives:

the following:

           1. The site shall be constructed per the approved plan.

           3. Landscaping shall meet all applicable Title 7 requirements, which includes at
                    least 50% coverage of live plant material in all landscaped areas.

                    outlined in 7-11-300P, as well as the 5600 West Overlay requirements found in

              5.    Signage shall comply with Title 11 of the West Valley City Code. One
                    additional monument sign is allowed.
              6.    Shall meet the requirements of all affected departments and agencies.
              7.    Subject to review upon valid complaint.
