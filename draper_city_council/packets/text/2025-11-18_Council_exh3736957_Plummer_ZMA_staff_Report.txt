BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY

This application is a request for approval of a Zoning Map Amendment from RA1 to RA2 for
approximately 1.31 acres located on the south side of 13200 S. at approximately 1360 E.
13200 S. and 0.97 acres located on the east side of 1300 E. at approximately 13239 S 1300
E. (Exhibits C and D). The applicant is requesting that a Zoning Map Amendment be
approved so that the applicant can subdivide the properties.

The subject property that fronts onto 13200 S. has a single-family home that was
constructed on the property In 1969. Two barns have also been constructed on the subject
property – the first barn was built in 1970 and the second barn was built in 1987.

The subject property that fronts onto 1300 E. has a single-family home that was
constructed on the property in 1971. A barn was built on the property in 1973.

According to Draper City Municipal Code (DCMC) Section 9-8-020(B)(1) the purpose of both
the RA1 and RA2 zones is to “foster low density development with little impact on its
surroundings and municipal services; to generally preserve the character of the City’s semi-
rural areas; and to promote and preserve conditions favorable to large-lot family life,
including the keeping of limited numbers of animals and fowl. The predominant use in
these zones is intended to be detached single-family dwellings, protected from
encroachment by commercial and industrial uses.”

Criteria for Approval
A Map Amendment is a matter committed to the legislative discretion of the City Council
and is not controlled by any one standard. However, in making a recommendation to the
City Council, the Planning Commission should consider the following factors in Section 9-5-
060(E)(1) of the DCMC:

        1. Map Amendments:
            a. Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with goals, objectives and
               policies of the city's general plan;
            b. Whether the proposed amendment is harmonious with the overall character
               of existing development in the vicinity of the subject property;
            c. Whether the proposed amendment is consistent with the standards of any
               applicable overlay zone;
            d. The extent to which the proposed amendment may adversely affect adjacent
               property; and
            e. The adequacy of facilities and services intended to serve the subject
               property, including, but not limited to, roadways, parks and recreation
               facilities, police and fire protection, schools, stormwater drainage systems,
               water supplies, and wastewater and refuse collection.

Staff notes that the zoning designation of RA2 is compatible with the subject property’s
current mapped land use designation of Residential Low/Medium Density (Exhibit E). The
uses allowed in the RA1 and RA2 zones are substantially the same with the exception of the
Agricultural business and Animal specialties uses being permitted as conditional uses in the
RA1 zone and are not permitted in the RA2 zone. The use of animals and fowl for
recreation and family food production would become a conditional use under the RA2
zone. Staff also notes that multiple residential zoning designations ranging in intensities
from RA1 to R4 are located within 300 feet of the subject properties (Exhibit F).

Engineering and Public Works Divisions Review.
   1. The change in zone from Residential / Agricultural (RA1) to Residential Agricultural
      (RA2) is proposing to change from low density residential use to a slightly higher
      residential use. This change in zoning represents an increase demand on the streets
      and utilities by approximately doubling the density per acre. The proposed zone has
      an approximate increase in traffic from the current approximately eight peak hour
      trips and approximate 70 vehicle trips per day to the proposed density of
      approximately 16 peak hour trips and approximately 140 vehicle trips per day. The
      site has access to 13200 South, a minor residential collector street. Based on the
      capacity of the fronting public street, the change in zone has the potential increase
      traffic on 13200 South, but does not represent a significant increase where any
      mitigation would be required. There are some times when congestion exists on
      13200 South and on nearby 1300 East, especially during school beginning and
      ending times. Typically, a use where 100 peak hour trips are generated triggers the
      need to have a traffic impact study (TIS) completed to analyze mitigation
      requirements. The size of the subject parcel and the potential number of trips
      generated from a parcel of this size does not trigger a TIS. There are existing public
      frontage improvements on the fronting public street.

   2. Connectivity with the subject parcel to public right-of-way is to 13200 South, a minor
      residential collector. Accesses are required to meet Draper City standards as
      outlined in our Master Transportation Plan and city code. Any development on the
      site will need to be evaluated for access location to comply with the required
      spacing based on the city’s access spacing requirements. Access is evaluated at the
      time of a site plan or subdivision application.

   3. There are existing storm drainage facilities fronting the subject parcel. Any future
      change in site drainage will be required to be addressed with any subdivision or site
      plan application and shall comply with the provisions of the development
      requirements within the Draper City Municipal Code.
   4. Sanitary sewer facilities will be provided by Jordan Basin Improvement District. Any
      subdivision or site plan application will require a commitment to serve from the
      sewer district that facilities are adequate to provide service for the proposed uses.

   5. Drinking water is provided by WaterPro to the subject parcel. Any subdivision or site
      plan application will require a commitment to serve from WaterPro that facilities are
      adequate to provide service for the proposed uses. This commitment is made at the
      time of subdivision or site plan approval.

1. What is the requested zoning designation?
We are requesting a zoning change for both lots (lot on 1360 E. 1300 S. and 13239 S. 1300 E, Draper,
Utah) from RA1 to RA2, residential agricultural medium density zoning, see sketch above or Draper
zoning map.

2. Please explain the reason for the request and the justification for approval.
The actual zoning for the property lot 1360 E. 1300 S. and 13239 S. 1300 E, in Draper, Utah is
RA1, large lot residential development. The proposed subdivision, that will be development on
this property, will have two lots with footage less than 40,000 S.F., that is less than the required
per the actual zoning.

3. How is the requested zoning designation harmonious with the overall character of
existing development in the vicinity of the subject property? Explain.
The existing developments around the property range from R1A, R2A, R3, and R4 zones. Zone
RA2 is predominant in the area with the lots directly south and east zoned RA2, and it has the
footage that we will have on our properties, which makes harmonious with the overall existing
development.

4. Are facilities and services, such as roadways and utilities, sufficient to serve the property
under the proposed zoning? Explain.
Yes, there are existing utilities and services, such as existing roadways that have access to
property under the proposed zoning.

5. Is the proposed zoning designation consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of
the City’s General Plan? Explain.
Yes, the proposed zoning designation is consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies of the
City’s General Plan.
