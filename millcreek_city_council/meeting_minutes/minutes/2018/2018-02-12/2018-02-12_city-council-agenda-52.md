1. Discussion of Canyon Rim Park Irrigation System and Splash Pad; Wayne Johnson
Wayne Johnson said he put together some detailed information on how much it would cost to
redo the irrigation at Canyon Rim Park. He based the figures on a similar bid from last year, and
it would cost $1.35 a square foot; total of $981,974 to redo the irrigation. That cost would be
from start to finish replacement without the replacement of the meter. He said the system can be
patched for the next five years; it doesn’t need an immediate replacement. His recommendation
is to not replace it this or next year. He said by adding a recirculating water system for a splash
pad, it would produce 250 gallons of water a minute, which would water 4-5 acres of grass
during the seasonal operational period of the splash pad. He said reused water can either not
provide enough or more than what a park needs. The problem with that is the splash pad doesn’t
operate the whole time the grass would need to be irrigated. He strongly recommends having a
public hearing for support if the Council were to move forward and to get a study done for the

3. Legislative Update; Dave & Ashley Spatafore
The Spatafore’s, lobbyists for Millcreek, updated the Council on relevant legislation.

H.B. 38, Fireworks Restrictions, passed. The bill changed the number of days fireworks can
legally be discharged to the 2 days before and 1 day after July 4" and 24'", New Year’s Eve, and
Chinese New Year. Council Member Uipi asked about fireworks at high school games. Mayor
Silvestrini said those are by permit and regulated by the County. If the County issues a permit in
Millcreek, they should give the City notification. David Spatafore said the day limits do not
involve commercial displays. He said by May 1, the County must devise a map for where
fireworks can be shot off and it must be exhibited at any point of sale location for fireworks. A
city can tighten the regulations after May 1“. The bill increased criminal penalties to a $1,000
fine and increased civil penalties for violators to be responsible for cost and private property loss.
Parents are responsible for their kids. Spatafore stated that 90% of revenue for fireworks retailers
is in aerial fireworks.

H.B. 124, Water Holdings Accountability and Transparency Amendments, there has not been
any action on it in the Natural Resources Committee. The bill is to create more transparency with
Salt Lake City. Millcreek has about 65% of their water coming from surplus Salt Lake City
water and the rest is from Jordan Valley Water District.

H.B. 175, Oversight Committee Creation, passed out of the house committee. The bill gives
more powers for an auditing committee to have broad investigative power. There were a lot of
testimonies against the bill; ULCT, counties, and school boards are opposed to it. Council
Member Marchant said the cities should let their legislators know how they feel about it.

H.B. 242, Local Animal Control Regulation Amendments, states that cities and counties cannot
have an ordinance limiting the number of pets in a house. Mayor Silvestrini said the City and
County do not currently have that regulation.

H.B. 269, Identity Theft Paraphernalia Provisions, went through committee to the House floor.
This gives police officers a tool to arrest and prosecute for aspects relating to identity theft.

H.B .271, Government Enterprise Amendments, requires a city of the first and second class to
get a study before holding entertainment or recreation events to see if there would be a private
enterprise available to do the event. This would require the city to identify all competitors that
might be interested in providing the services. The studies are expensive, and it would require a
public hearing. Mayor Silvestrini said there is a civic benefit to having events and citizens need
to lobby against this bill.

S.B. 164, Transportation Funding Amendments, creates a transparent process to distribute the
County of the First Class Highway Projects funding. The County wants a committee to screen
projects of regional significance. The bill did not get out of committee today, but will be back.

H.B. 376, Interlocal Provision of Law Enforcement, amends state statute that allows a
municipality to have an interlocal agreement with the county for law enforcement services, for
