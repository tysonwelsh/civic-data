    3. DISCUSSION AND POSSIBLE ACTION: CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT
       APPLICATION FROM ALTA SKI AREA FOR THE INSTALLATION OF BW
       EXPLODER REMOTE AVALANCHE CONTROL DEVICE ON EAST BALDY:
       DAVE RICHARDS

Planning Commission Chair Jon Nepstad introduced Dave Richards, Alta Ski Area Avalanche
Office Director. Mr. Richards described that Alta Ski Area’s proposal to install an additional
remote avalanche control device (RACS) on East Baldy is intended to improve safety for
employees and customers. The terrain feature where the device would be installed is significant to
circulation between large segments of Alta Ski Area and is known to be hazardous for ski patrollers
conducting conventional mitigation in the area. Mr. Richards acknowledged that the specific
technology proposed to be installed in this location, a BW Exploder, is new to the market, but he
observed that Alta Ski Area has always been a leader in developing and improving new avalanche
mitigation technique, and stated that Alta Ski Area believes the technology may be superior to

similar existing devices. Mr. Richards described the materials he provided to the Commission,
which included design specifications, an analysis of the device’s explosive characteristics, and
materials related to an ATF regulation that applies to explosive devices such as the proposed BW
Exploder.

Mr. Richards acknowledged that the proposed device would include a “tank farm” structure that
must be located along a nearby ridgeline and described the reasons why the structure needs to be
located as proposed, including property lines, the boundary between Salt Lake and Utah Counties,
and the location of existing utilities. Mr. Richards described that the structure will be adjacent to
an existing building on Sugarloaf Pass. Mr. Richards stated that Alta Ski Area will camouflage the
structure as much as possible so that it creates minimal disruption of ridgeline views.

Mr. Richards described weaknesses associated with more common existing RACS devices,
including Gaz-Ex devices, which can be unreliable, and O’bell-X exploders, which must be
frequently reloaded with compressed gas by helicopter. Mr. Richards described that because the
BW Exploder uses methane, which is lighter than air, the compressed gas tanks can be located
more flexibly, whereas Gaz-Ex tanks must be situated above Gaz-Ex exploders. Mr. Richards
stated that BW-Exploders provide a faster detonation velocity than Gaz-Ex, which applies a bigger
and more effective load to a slope. Mr. Richards opined that because of these advantages, BW
Exploders may supplant Gaz-Ex exploders. Mr. Richards added that the Federal Bureau of
Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms, and Explosives does not regulate compressed gas devices, which
streamlines the planning, installation, and operation of BW Exploders.

Planning Commission Member Jeff Niermeyer asked where the tank farm would be located if it
were moved off the ridge. Mr. Richards stated that the only other feasible location for the tank
farm is near the top terminal of Snowbird’s Baldy Express lift, which is on Snowbird’s property,
and in Utah County, but that it would also require more extensive excavation than Alta Ski Area’s
preferred location. Mr. Niermeyer acknowledged the significance of those constraints.

Planning Commission Member Rob Voye asked whether the preferred location for the tank farm
will require excavation to bury gas lines, and Mr. Richards described that lines would be laid above
ground without excavation and anchored to exposed rock to prevent damage and displacement by
“snow creep.”

Planning Commission Member Maren Askins asked Mr. Richards to describe the timeline for
replacing existing Gaz-Ex and O’bell-X exploders. Mr. Richards described that Alta Ski Area
doesn’t own Gaz-Ex, but that Gaz-Ex in place throughout the canyon require constant maintenance
and sometimes fail to operate. Mr. Richards described that Alta Ski Area’s O’bell-X devices are
exhibiting significant wear and tear, and that if BW Exploders prove to be effective and reliable,
Alta Ski Area will replace its O’bell-X devices with BW Exploders within five years.

Mayor Roger Bourke asked Mr. Richards about plans to install additional RACS devices on Mt.
Baldy, and Mr. Richards confirmed that Alta Ski Area and Snowbird will begin a project to install
more RACS the final week of July 2023. Mr. Richards reported that the exploders planned for
installation in 2023 will eliminate the need for the use of artillery for avalanche mitigation at Alta
Ski Area.

Planning Commission Chair Jon Nepstad asked whether Alta Ski Area would be significantly
inconvenienced if it installed the smaller of two tank farm structures, and Mr. Richards described
that it would need to be reloaded once during each winter, whereas the larger structure holds
enough gas to last an entire season. Mr. Nepstad asked whether the ski area regretted choosing
O’bell-X devices in some locations, and Mr. Richards opined that each different RACS device is
relatively new and that users learn their strengths, weaknesses, and specific applications over time.

Mr. Nepstad asked Interim Town Manager Chris Cawley whether Alta Ski Area will need another
conditional use permit if they replace one type of RACS with another, and Mr. Cawley indicated
that because the land use itself would not change, Alta Ski Area would not need another conditional
use permit, although it may require a building permit.

Mr. Nepstad asked Mr. Richards to describe the plan to camouflage the BW Exploder and tank
farm structure, and Mr. Richards that Alta Ski Area intends to use a color that blends in with the
yellow and brown quartzite rock that is prevalent on Mt. Baldy. Planning Commission Member
Dave Abraham recommended Alta Ski Area consider using a weathered steel exterior or treatment
for the structure. Mr. Nepstad asked Mr. Richards to describe the installation timeline, and Mr.
Richards described that the exploder would ideally be installed by the end of September, pending
Town of Alta land use approvals. Mr. Nepstad recommended the Commission conduct a field trip
to view the exploders during the upcoming winter.

Mr. Niermeyer asked Mr. Richards whether using the smaller tank farm structure would impose a
hardship on Alta Ski Area, and Mr. Richards opined that it would not impose a significant hardship.

Mr. Nepstad asked for a motion to approve Alta Ski Area’s application for a conditional use
permit to install a BW Exploder RACS on East Baldy. Mr. Niermeyer introduced a motion
to approve the conditional use permit application with the condition that Alta Ski Area use
the smaller of the two tank farm structure options. Mr. Voye seconded the motion, and the
motion was carried with unanimous consent of the planning commission.

    4. DISCUSSION AND POSSIBLE ACTION: CONDITIONAL USE PERMIT
       APPLICATION FROM ALTA SKI AREA TO INSTALL TWO CONEX STORAGE
       CONTAINERS ON A SEMI-PERMANENT BASIS ON PRIVATE LAND
       ADJACENT TO THE WILDCAT PARKING LOT: DAVE RICHARDS

John Guldner described that Alta Ski Area had not anticipated the need to store the Wyssen Tower
exploders it installed in Summer 2022 and was initially storing the exploders in the Alta Ski Area
Wildcat Parking Lot in Spring 2023. Alta Ski Area needed to expedite relocation of the exploders
and it began excavating and grading a pad to locate two Conex shipping containers adjacent to the
Wildcat Parking Lot on private land. After beginning the project, Alta Ski Area realized the need
to obtain permits from the Town of Alta and contacted the Town to begin the process. Mr. Guldner
described that the Town decided to allow the project to continue since it was a small project with
significant environmental impact or impact to adjacent properties. Mr. Guldner described that Alta
Ski Area and the Town of Alta would continue to work together in the future to conduct Town of
Alta permitting processes as efficiently as possible.

Planning Commission Chair Jon Nepstad described to Dave Richards, representing Alta Ski Area
on the matter, that the Town of Alta may have placed itself at risk by allowing the project to
continue before permitting was complete, and opined that the Town and Alta Ski Area should
continue to communicate about upcoming projects and permitting processes. Planning
Commission Member Jeff Niermeyer asked Mr. Richards how much earthen material was used to
fill in around the containers. Mr. Richards described that half the total height of the containers was
buried, with fill sloping down toward the parking lot. Mr. Richards apologized on behalf of Alta
Ski Area for beginning the project without seeking permits beforehand.

Mr. Nepstad asked for a motion to approve the conditional use permit application from Alta
Ski Area to install two Conex storage containers on a semi-permanent basis on private land
adjacent to the Wildcat Parking Lot. Planning Commission Member Maren Askins
introduced a motion to approve the application, Mr. Niermeyer seconded the motion, and
the motion was carried with unanimous consent of the Commission.

    5. DISCUSSION OF CLIMATE CHANGE AND LONG-RANGE PLANNING:
       MAYOR ROGER BOURKE

Alta Mayor Roger Bourke opined that climate change will create unpredictable impacts on Alta
and recommended the Town of Alta develop a plan to adapt to the changing climate. Mayor Bourke
asked the planning commission to lead the Town’s approach to understanding and planning for
climate change. Planning Commission Member Dave Abraham suggested the Town should focus
on resilience, particularly regarding extreme weather events. Mayor Bourke described that Alta
would experience much more variable weather patterns in the future and stated a desire to plan to
maintain the character of Alta in the face of major changes. Mr. Abraham recalled that Unified
Fire Authority had presented to the Alta Planning Commission on wildland fire preparedness and
mitigation at residential properties and suggested that the Town pursue measures such as
defensible space and other disaster preparedness measures.

Planning Commission Member Jeff Niermeyer recommended the Town consider a “scenario
planning” approach to addressing a future with an unpredictable climate. Mr. Niermeyer opined
that although it would be impossible to predict the future of Alta’s weather patterns with certainty,
climate science has determined that a wide range of conditions could exist. Mr. Niermeyer asked
whether the Town should contemplate whether Alta Ski Area would someday be unable to operate
due to warm temperatures and high-elevation rain-snow lines and evaluate other strategies for
generating revenue to support municipal services.

Mayor Bourke asked the planning commission to conduct a meeting devoted to brainstorming
about climate change scenarios and adaptation strategies. Planning Commission Chair Jon Nepstad
suggested the commission engage Alta Ski Area, local hotels, and other ski industry
representatives to understand their outlook on climate change. The commission discussed
consulting a climate scientist such as Dr. Robert Gilles.

Interim Town Manager Chris Cawley asked Mr. Nepstad whether he had contacts in other
communities that have developed municipal climate change adaptation plans. Mr. Nepstad

    6. DISCUSSION AND POSSIBLE ACTION: APPOINTMENT OF A COMMISSION
       CHAIR AND VICE-CHAIR

Commission members and staff discussed the schedule by which the commission appoints its chair
and vice-chair. Town of Alta Attorney Polly McLean recited Town of Alta Code 10-4-5 which
provides that the planning commission chair is appointed to a 5-year term, and that the chair may
not serve for more than two consecutive terms. Planning Commission Member Jeff Niermeyer
introduced a motion to appoint Commission Chair Jon Nepstad as planning commission
chair for the remainder of Mr. Nepstad’s current term as a planning commission member.
Planning Commission member Dave Abraham seconded the motion, and the motion was
carried with unanimous consent of the Commission.

Chair Nepstad asked the planning commission to recommend a member to serve as vice-chair.
Planning Commission Member Rob Voye recommended the commission consider Jeff Niermeyer.
Chair Nepstad introduced a motion to appoint Mr. Niermeyer as commission vice-chair; Mr.
Voye seconded the motion, and the motion was carried with unanimous consent of the
commission.

    7. DATE OF NEXT MEETING

Planning Commission Chair Jon Nepstad recommended the commission continue to offer virtual

in person when meeting agendas include substantive items, looked forward to the commission
meeting with Alta Ski Area to discuss future progress in March 2023, and suggested the
commission convene for a retreat-style meeting to brainstorm future initiatives.
