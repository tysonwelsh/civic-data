The following are the minutes of the Planning Commission Meeting held on Thursday, September 17, 2020
at 5:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 W. Herriman Main Street (12090 South), Herriman,
Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in City Hall, on the City’s website, and
delivered to members of the Commission and media.

City Planner Michael Maloy explained the Council was considering efforts to restructure the financial situation
with Unified Fire Authority. Taxes paid to UFA had been pooled, but with proposed changes, the money
collected for Herriman would be spent in Herriman. Fire services would not change.


Assistant City Planner MacDonald stated that item 3.1 was due to a construction mistake, and the applicant
requested to change the side setback from five to three feet. She explained the property was vested under the
old code, was part of a PUD, and such a change would be forbidden in the current code. Commissioners
expressed anxiety with setting a precedent, by allowing such small setbacks and discussed options such as
fines.

The Valhalla Subdivision 35 lots would have two separate public hearings for the rezone and the subdivision.
The developer had multiple meetings with neighbors to resolve issues. Staff recommended continuing the
subdivision, as the rezone still needed to be approved by the City Council. The rezone was only for a small
portion of the property. Commissioners expressed concern with homes being built up the hillside, and
thought the area should have larger lots. City Planner Maloy and Assistant City Planner MacDonald explained
system improvements, detention basin and density credits. The existing home flag lot was a concern. City
Ordinance limited flag lots.


City Planner Maloy explained he wanted to present this item to the Commissioners, as it would be on a future
agenda. Wasatch is developing pods 35 and 39 and proposed signage similar to Mountain View Village, which
was not allowed in Herriman. The zoning overlay would allow taller signs. The signage site plans would be
back to the Commission as a conditional use item. Commissioner Jackson Ferguson liked the large sign on
MVC and the directional signs in the development. He thought the smaller monument signs would be difficult
to read. Scrolling signs (8-second duration) would be acceptable. City Planner Maloy explained the applicant
was working with a sign company. Commissioners liked the color scheme and stated the signs should be
uniform and all headers should face the same way.


FFKR Susie Petheram requested feedback on the draft plan that was sent to the Commissioners. She
reviewed the sections to identify those to circle back to for more in depth commentary.

Commissioners opined that better quality open spaces were needed. Developers have designated unbuildable
areas as open space, in that past, which needed to stop. Commissioners emphasized preparing for the future
by requiring properties, such as big box stores have a flexible plan for sectioning the buildings for multiple
users, if needed.

Commissioner Jacobson requested the average lot sizes in Herriman and stated more information was needed
before making a decision. He thought the community was getting too much higher density and more single
family housing was needed. Commissioners questioned the calculations to determine units per acre and
requested further clarification from staff. Density needed more precise calculations. Unbuildable areas, parks
and open space should not be included in the calculation. Zones should accurately reflect the average lot size
of the area. Commissioners were frustrated with final products not matching expectations. City Planner Maloy
explained gross density was currently calculated, but other cities calculate net density. He recommended
reviewing the options with an action plan to determine the best method moving forward. Commissioners
mentioned calculating residential units based on population and infrastructure capabilities. More studies
needed to be done to determine which types of housing were needed. More support services, recreational
facilities, and potential open space based on the current city size, were needed. Commissioners stated historic
buildings needed long term protections and would like to see more extensive maps for current and proposed
trails. More sit down restaurants with pickup accommodations, other than a drive thru were needed.
Innovative pickup and drop off solutions with better traffic flow would be beneficial at dance classes and
similar businesses. Commissioners debated the importance of preserving the past versus focusing
developments for future needs. The City should create community spaces and events such as farmer’s markets
and craft fairs. Transportation, infrastructure and trax continuation solutions were necessary. Commissioners
requested strategies for preserving open space and more information for future flex zones along MVC. They
requested future materials be available in a Word document that tracked changes and requested the
opportunity to review all materials prior to the next meeting. FFKR Susie Petheram said she would email the
map.

Assistant City Planner MacDonald identified the request to reduce the required side yard setback, reviewed the
location and displayed the vicinity map. The property line was supposed to be five feet, but due to an error
with the garage size, the property had a setback of only 2.9 feet, between the home and the property line. The
PUD setbacks were allowed flexibility, with Planning Commission approval. There was ten feet of space
between the houses and others on the street have similar setbacks. If approved, a waiver would be required
from utility companies.

Applicant representatives Attorney Dean Dansie, Brett Woodland and Clark Graff thought the staff report
was thorough and the recommendations were appropriate. Fieldstone Homes had reached out to utility
providers for waivers. All utility lines were at the front of the house. It was stated that ten feet total distance
between properties was consistent with the neighborhood.

Commissioner Lorin Palmer asked if the neighbor was okay with the close setback. He didn’t want to disurupt

the lives of the homeowners and mentioned a stipulation stating this was a one time allowance. The applicant
stated they were still in discussion with the neighbor and both properties were rentals.

Commissioners expressed concern that this would set a precedent. City Attorney Andrizzi stated it would not
be in the builders’ best interest to make mistakes in the future. He offered the proposal could be rejected and
a variance requested. The situation was difficult due to the mistake being caught so late, and the situation
would be very different if the certificate of occupancy had not been submitted. Commissioners asked how
much space was on the other side of the house and asked if the house was too big for the lot. It was offered
that the garage was built to be twelve feet instead of 10 feet. The applicant addressed the issue and said there
was a discrepancy on the building plan which showed two separate dimensions for the garage. The sizing and
placement of the window wells was discussed. A grate was installed on the window well, which touches the
fence. Opening the grate for emergency access would be problematic and evacuating during a fire would be
difficult. The Commissioners were uncomfortable with the safety element and discussed denying the proposal
in favor of a variance. Commissioner Palmer did not want to adversely affect the family that lived there. City
liability and details of the variance were outlined. Assistant City Planner MacDonald offered the variance
process would be decided by the appeals authority, which would require them to meet the state statute
variance requirement. City Attorney Andrizzi offered the conditions of approval from multiple departments
would be required to address the issues. The Commission debated the best course of action to take. The
adjacent home had a seven foot setback, but that property owner refused to complete a lot line adjustment.
City Planner Maloy stated windows were not allowed within three feet of the property line, which affected all
windows along that side of the house. The basement window would probably need to be removed and the
area retrofitted.
