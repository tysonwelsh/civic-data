3. Valley View Office; 7545 South Temple Drive; Preliminary Site Plan; BR-P Zone; East

Star Farm Land LLC/Doug Young (applicant) (#32107; parcel 21-26-306-001]

Bob Elder, Ensign Engineering, was representing Doug Young. The proposed 10,000 square foot

office is a single level building that will use one acre of the 6-acre site and is an extension of office

space for the South Valley Water Reclamation Facility. City code for parking and fire access has been

met, and staff supported the application.

Megan Jensen said the building will be located on the northeast corner of the property. Two access

points are provided from 7400 South, which is a private driveway to the treatment plant. The building

is 10,600 square feet with the tallest point at 20 feet. Renderings were shown. The site is required to

have 31 parking stalls, which are provided. A pedestrian path to Temple Drive is required to connect

to the main entrance. The residential homes to the north are separated from this parcel by a street and

an existing masonry wall.

Staff recommended approval of the Preliminary Site Plan for Valley View Office located at 7545

South Temple Drive with the Requirements of Approval listed.

mitigate each of the reasonably anticipated detrimental effects of the use, and which would comply

with existing law. Such information should be clearly included in any motion made by and subsequent

4. Victory Builders Rezone; 1390 West 9000 South; Rezone 2.54 acres from SC-2
(Community Shopping Center) Zone and SC-1 (Neighborhood Shopping Center) Zone to
C-G (General Commercial) Zone; Victory Builders/Tanner Hansen (applicant) [#32744;
parcels 27-03-278-024, 025, 027]
Tanner Hansen, applicant, said the request is to rezone the property from SC-1 and SC-2 to the General
Commission (C-G) zone. The main reason is that the C-G zone allows for more lot coverage. He spoke
to the reason the property is hard to develop due to the shape. However, the small business incubators
that they build will fit well. These retail flex buildings allow for small businesses to move from their
home into a commercial space. The long portion of the property has good visibility from 9000 South, and
they have made the design aesthetically pleasing. The new commercial growth in the area is exciting.

Tayler Jensen provided an overview of the three parcels that comprise 2.54 acres. The proposed uses are
allowed in the current zoning, and there will be additional uses allowed in the C-G zone. The reason the
applicant is requesting the rezoning is for greater maximum lot coverage. The C-G zone fits within the
current community commercial land use designation. The concept plan and elevations were shown,
which would be fleshed out through the DRC and Planning Commission process. A recommendation
will go to the City Council for final decision. If approved, a site plan and subdivision application would
be submitted and reviewed by the Planning Commission at a public hearing.

Rezone requests are legislative policy decisions. Review of the applicant's proposal shows both the
current designations and the request fall within the adopted West Jordan General Plan. Based on the
analysis contained in the report, staff recommended that the Planning Commission make a
recommendation for approval (Positive Recommendation) of the proposed rezone.

Ammon Allen was concerned with some issues regarding landscaping, flow of the site, and certain
uses that would be allowed with a change in zoning. He asked if there is process for a master
development agreement in commercial zones.

Tayler Jensen said that in the past there might have been an option for zoning conditions, but that is not
allowed anymore. The Commission needs to determine if it is appropriate or not, and then trust that the
city ordinances in place can address any concerns.

Ammon Allen asked if the applicant was waiting for the zone change before investing the money it
would take to get the site plan ready for approval at the same time. He would feel more comfortable
approving the site plan and subdivision at the same time as the rezone.

Tayler Jensen said the subdivision and site plan applications were being reviewed, but the applicant
needed more lot coverage and he put them on hold in order to ask for a rezoning. The entitlements
have to come before the site plan can be approved. The meeting today was already noticed for the
rezoning only. This process would be pushed back several months if they had to notice another hearing
and for review of the site plan and subdivision plat. Also, those submittals require engineering, which
is not usually required prior to entitlement due to the large expense.

Larry Gardner said that the subdivision and site plan processes do not require City Council approval,
but the rezoning does. It is not good policy to approve something that is dependent on a zone change or
to require that the applicant go to the extra expense of engineering when the rezoning could be denied.
Duncan Murray said this project would not require a development plan. Under current law they have to
be careful about requiring a development agreement when there is not a development plan, if it is not
part of a special zone, if something is not being specifically negotiated, or unless the applicant requests
one.

Trish Hatch appreciated the format and information of staff’s presentation.

George Winn said he was happy to see a use coming in that is more conducive to the shape of the
property, which has contributed to it staying undeveloped.

George Winn opened the public hearing.
