32   Associate Planner and Sustainability Analyst, Samantha DeSeelhorst reported that the request was
33   for a Conditional Use Permit. The subject property is located at 6826 South Highland Drive. A
34   professional office could operate within the existing building on the lot. The applicant was Range
35   Architecture on behalf of Block Builders, LLC. Staff recommended approval of the Conditional
36   Use Permit with some Conditions of Approval, which were included in the Staff Report. Ms.
37   DeSeelhorst shared a map of the area with the Commission. She explained that the office space
38   would be a professional office for Block Builders, LLC, which was a general contracting company
39   that specializes in out-of-state commercial and retail builds. The proposal was to utilize the existing
40   building on the site. The building has been vacant for a few years. Block Builders, LLC intended
41   to do some tenant improvements on the interior. There was also a proposal to do some cosmetic
42   exterior improvements, such as landscaping.
43
44   The office would function from 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. and be open from Monday through Friday.
45   Ms. DeSeelhorst reported that there would be six employees utilizing the office space for
46   accounting and estimating purposes. No clients would visit the site. The building will be used as
47   internal space for the Block Builders, LLC team. It was administrative space only, so there would

20          1.2     Project CUP-22-013 - A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Take
21                  Possible Action on a Request to Replace Three Existing Traffic Calming Gates
22                  with Crash Gates, Located at 9094 South Galette Lane, 9119 South Renoir
23                  Lane, and 3534 East Breton Lane.
24
25   Community and Economic Development Director, Michael Johnson, explained that the item was a
26   Conditional Use Permit application. It was an amendment of the previously approved Giverny
27   Planned Unit Development (“PUD”). This was a 162-lot PUD that was originally approved by the
28   Planning Commission in 2015. The application was to amend a small portion of that original
29   approval as it pertained to the Fencing Plan. The proposal was to replace three existing mechanized
30   traffic calming gates on the north boundary of the development property. 9094 South Galette Lane,
31   9119 South Renoir Lane, and 3534 East Breton Lane were the streets that connect to the existing
32   public streets directly to the north. Mr. Johnson shared images of the current traffic calming gates
33   on the connecting roads. The gates are mechanized and retracted vertically into the ground. They
34   were approved as a traffic calming measure after concerns were raised in 2014 regarding cut-
35   through traffic and traffic flow in the area.
36
37   The proposed replacement was for less technical for non-mechanized crash gates. They will be
38   locked and only be accessible to emergency vehicles in emergencies. Mr. Johnson shared some
39   background information about the PUD. The Giverny PUD was approved in 2015 and was
40   currently 90% complete. There was still active construction taking place but much of the
41   construction had been completed, especially in the northern area. The original PUD contained a
42   Fencing Plan. That was the reason the current proposal was considered an amendment. The
43   document showed traffic calming gates that allowed access. Shortly after Giverny was approved,
44   the project was appealed by a number of different groups. Ultimately, Regal Homes, who
45   developed the property, entered into a private Settlement Agreement with a group of residents. One
46   of the terms of that agreement related to the traffic calming gates. For instance, it needs to take a
47   certain amount of time before the traffic calming gates will open and allow access.
48

 1   Since the mechanized gates were installed in 2019, the operations had been unreliable. Whenever
 2   repairs took place, the gates defaulted to the down position, which allowed for free-flowing traffic.
 3   This resulted in calls to the City from Giverny residents and residents to the north. This had been a
 4   consistent issue since installation. As a result, the Giverny Master Association submitted the
 5   current proposal to resolve the ongoing operability issue.

 7   Mr. Johnson overviewed the various impacts of replacing traffic calming gates with locked crash
 8   gates. It was critical for emergency vehicle access to remain. The Fire Department reviewed the
 9   crash gate proposal and had no issues with it. There were Fire Code requirements for crash gates
10   and all of those requirements would need to be met. As for Public Works and snow removal, the
11   City did not do snow removal in Giverny because it was a private development. The road is
12   privately owned and privately maintained. It is not part of the City infrastructure but the stub streets
13   to the north are. There were no concerns about the crash gates from a Public Works perspective. It
14   would not impact snow removal or road maintenance. The ARC needed to review any Planned Unit
15   Development. The proposed switch to crash gates was reviewed and approved. The ARC had no
16   issue with the design.
17
18   Staff looked at the direction the gates will swing. Concern about the gate direction was included in
19   some of the public comments. There was a recommended condition that the gates swing inward
20   toward Giverny rather than into the public street. Mr. Johnson reported that pedestrian access was
21   proposed to continue unaffected. Navigation concerns were mentioned in some of the public
22   comments. There were concerns that the crash gates would result in a lot of turnarounds in
23   driveways. Staff recommended a condition to alleviate those concerns. Mr. Johnson reported that
24   Staff recommended approval of the amendment based on the Impact Analysis, with Conditions of
25   Approval, which were as follows:
26
27      •   Crash gates shall open into the private development area and not towards the public road;
28      •   Prior to installation, detailed plans shall be submitted and approved by the City and Unified
29          Fire Authority (“UFA”);
30      •   On-street parking restrictions shall be appropriately demarcated, through red curb, signage,
31          or a combination of the two, in the area affected by open gates;
32      •   Signage shall be installed at the applicant’s expense and to the satisfaction of City Staff on
33          Despain Way, Treasure Way, and Green Hills Drive to clarify that there is no regular vehicle
34          cross access between neighborhoods. Similar signage shall also be installed within the
35          Giverny Planned Unit Development; and
36      •   The applicant shall demonstrate that an amendment to the 2015 Settlement Agreement has
37          been signed by all applicable private parties agreeing to the gate replacement.

16          1.3     Project CUP-22-011 - A Procedural Determination Pertaining to a Request to
17                  Construct an Eight-Foot Fence on the Property Located at 7275 South Caladora
18                  Cove.
19
20   Mr. Johnson reported that the item was a matter of procedural clarification. The City received an
21   application several months earlier for a fence height extension. This was the construction of a fence
22   or wall on a property that was greater than six feet in height. Fences were allowed to be up to six
23   feet without a permit. The City also allowed taller fences for various reasons, up to 12 feet with a
24   Conditional Use Permit. In the City Ordinance, the Conditional Use authority was delegated to the
25   Administrative Hearing Officer. Normally, for these types of applications, an Administrative
26   Hearing is held. This was a public hearing that happened during the day. Other types of
27   applications that were approved this way included short-term rentals in permitted areas and small
28   home business applications with minimal impacts. These are normally minor Conditional Use
29   Permits with relatively little impact. In this case, the item was brought to the Planning Commission
30   for consideration as the situation was no longer a minor Conditional Use.
31
32   The subject property was located at 7275 South Caladora Cove. A Conditional Use Permit
33   application was submitted for the construction of an eight-foot fence on the side yard between
34   properties. There was an Administrative Hearing. At the hearing, the applicant was asked to
35   provide more clarification on the height, how the existing grade was measured, and so on. No
36   decision was made during the initial hearing. Subsequently, a letter from the applicant was
37   received. The applicant decided to withdraw their application and build a six-foot fence instead.
38   However, the applicant had built an eight-foot fence. The enforcement process had begun.
39
40   Mr. Johnson reported that the next door neighbor submitted an Administrative Appeal stating that
41   the fence should be considered part of the home and should be subject to the setbacks of the house
42   rather than being installed on the property line. In addition, the person constructing the fence
43   resubmitted a Conditional Use Permit application to build an eight-foot fence. The fence had
44   already been constructed. The City did not accept that application as there was pending
45   enforcement. The homeowner appealed the decision not to accept the application. Only one
46   Appeal Hearing had taken place so far that related to the City’s decision to not accept the
47   Conditional Use Permit application. The other appeal was put on hold by the appellant, pending the


     Cottonwood Heights Planning Commission Meeting – 09/07/2022                                        5
 1   outcome of that process. The Hearing Officer ruled in favor of the property owner and determined
 2   that the City did not have the authority to deny an application from being made.
 3
 4   The City had since accepted the application for an eight-foot fence. Mr. Johnson reminded the
 5   Commission that the eight-foot fence had already been constructed. That application had to go
 6   through the Conditional Use Permit process. Since multiple decisions had been appealed,
