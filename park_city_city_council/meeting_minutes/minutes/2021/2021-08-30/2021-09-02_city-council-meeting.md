Discuss the Downtown Trash and Diversion RFP Scope:
Luke Cartin, Environmental Sustainability Manager, and Jonathan Weidenhamer,
Economic Development Manager, presented this item. Cartin reviewed the current trash
contract had been extended a few times, but there were no waste diversion goals within
it. Some challenges were trash volume increases, the location, and amount of storage
facilities along Main Street. He explained the City's Environmental Leadership goals
included recycling and green waste and indicated the community's businesses had
voiced this was important to them as well. Cartin indicated the Historic Park City
Alliance (HPCA) agreed to increase year-round trash pickup to address the overflow
problem. He proposed a 10-year contract, with progressively green goals identified for
each year of the contract.

Weidenhamer stated the City was working on the contract in conjunction with the
Business District Trash Subcommittee. Also, Jennifer Wesselhoff, Chamber of
Commerce President, supported trash diversion programs and thought they aligned with

the sustainable tourism programs in the community. He indicated trash diversion didn't
appear to be a big cost. Council Member Gerber asked how common a 10-year contract
was, and what would happen if there was a problem mid-contract. Weidenhamer stated
he would recommend starting with a six-year contract to make sure both parties were in
sync. Mayor Beerman stated there was a lot of monitoring and refining in this plan and

responsible for clear billing that showed what was being done. Council Member Henney

participating in diversion and they would be interested in this contract. Council Member
Henney stated Waste Management had a company philosophy that garbage was a

and/or recycled. Cartin stated there was a challenge in Summit County, in that the
landfill had less than 30 years of life. But these were some solutions that could extend
that life.

Council Member Worel asked what the process was, to which Weidenhamer stated he
would release the Request for Qualifications (RFQ) in a few weeks, receive the
proposals by October, and set the award for October with the contract beginning
November 1st. Council Member Henney asked if the contract would start a broader
recycling effort throughout the community. Cartin stated the HPCA trash bid was the

additional services to this contract. Cartin stated there was an opportunity to add green
waste, glass, and more to the contract. The goal was to solidify the baseline and then
add the opt-in services. Council Member Henney asked Council to consider a service
contract with Recycle Utah for the additional service of clearly marking recycle and trash
dumpsters in order to eliminate confusion.

Wesselhoff stated she was pleased with the survey results from businesses and the
priority they gave to green waste and recycling. The Chamber supported the business
community's sustainable goals. She discussed the Chamber’s Sustainable Tourism
Pillars, along with their management plan and strategy, and believed the City and
Chamber were aligned.

Discuss a Master License Agreement for Small Wireless Facilities in City Right-of-
Way:
John Robertson, City Engineer, presented this item and presented slides on wireless
towers. He noted the macro towers were not being discussed for the City. He reviewed
the passage of Senate Bill 189 in 2018. As a result, the City received requests for small

wireless facilities in 2019. Staff began to draft documents and brought this issue to
Council in July, 2020. The community expressed concern about potential health effects
that could occur from having these towers. He stated the City could not deny a request
on the basis of health, but it could require regular reports on the radio frequencies that
emitted from the towers. He stated there were four documents staff prepared in the
materials packet: the ordinance, the master license agreement, the design standards,
and the permitting application.

Robertson noted the permitting process would ensure the applicant was in compliance
with federal and state requirements, with pole heights and with radio frequencies. He
stated the City would require poles that would be similar to the design of the current
light poles on the City rights-of-way, although they would be about five feet higher.

Council Member Worel asked what the required insurance would cover, to which Tom
Daley, Deputy Attorney, stated the liability insurance would cover the cost of
equipment/infrastructure if it was damaged in the right-of-way. Council Member Gerber
indicated there were already towers on top of buildings around town, and asked if the
City could require a fee since it was on public property. Robertson stated companies
that had towers on buildings would pay a fee for those locations. Daley stated the City
could not charge a fee that exceeded a reasonable cost since the cell companies
needed to afford it to provide service to the population. He noted the State Legislature
laid out the fee language, along with fee caps. Council Member Henney was
disappointed the City had no choice in the approval of these cell towers. He thanked
staff for their efforts of staying on top of the issue. He indicated the concern was health
and he asked if the Health Board had discussed radio frequencies. Council Member
Worel stated the board had not discussed this issue, but she would pass along the
suggestion to the board chair.

Mayor Beerman asked if the cell tower could be hidden in the street banners and not on
the top of the poles, to which Robertson stated the banners would block the
transmission. Council Member Gerber asked if technology would continue to advance
so the appearance would improve in the future. Robertson stated the City could not
delay approvals until a better design was available. Daley stated the Federal
Communications Commission (FCC) prohibited a moratorium on approvals. Mayor
Beerman asked if exceptions could be made for the Historic District. Daley indicated
there was latitude for better placement in certain districts. Council Member Doilney
thought the City could set a high bar for cell tower placement that was in line with the
high bar set for standards Citywide. Council Member Worel asked if the City had liability
if cell towers were proved to cause health issues. Daley stated there was no precedent,
so he couldn't say, but normally the City's liability was very limited. Mayor Beerman
thought the middle depiction of a cell tower on a light pole would be acceptable, but he
thought the depiction on the far right was awful.

           Small Wireless History
2018 State Senate passes - SB 189 Small Wireless Facilities Deployment Act
  SB 189 – “permits a wireless provider to deploy a small wireless facility and any associated utility pole
  within a right-of-way under certain conditions.”
  SB 189 – “permits an authority to establish a permitting process for the deployment of a small wireless
  facility and associated utility pole under certain conditions.”

2019 – PCMC Engineering receives requests from wireless providers to install
SWF’s in Park City, starting with placement in the Historic District.
  July 2020 – Engineering/Planning/Legal conduct a workshop with Council regarding a proposed
  permitting process with draft guiding documents and receive public input.
  August 2020 to September 2021 – Eng/Pln/Legal update the Ordinance, Master License Agreement,
  Design Standards, and Permit Application.
  Spring of 2021 these documents were presented to Historic Preservation Board for input and
  comment.
PCMC SWF Permitting Process
Ordinance – establishes the siting and use of the facility within the public right of way….delineates the
process to be followed to obtain regulatory approval from the City….confirms the required process to
follow when placement of a SWF within the Historic District.

Master License Agreement – per the Ordinance, requires all providers to enter an MLA prior to applying
for placement of SWF within the City. The MLA defines the structure of the relationship between the
provider and the City….confirms any fees, required insurance, and limitations of liability.

Design Standards – Provides direction and definition to the provider for the “where” and “how” their
facilities may be placed within the right of way.

Permit Application – Permit application ensures that the provider has submitted all required
information and taken the required regulatory steps necessary for the processing of the request.
PCMC SWF Permitting Process
Goals of the permitting process:
   • Ensure compliance with all federal and state requirements
