Call to Order

call. Councilmembers Buroker, Haymond, McCay, McDougal, and Pierucci were present.

Citizen Comment

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;

Clark Butler shared concerns about increased traffic and speeding on the residential streets
near his home. He mentioned that the installation of speed bumps in the past had been effective
in slowing down traffic but were removed during recent road repaving. Expressing worry about
the safety of the neighborhood, he suggested possible solutions, such as reinstalling speed
bumps or implementing a three-way stop at specific intersections.

Mayor Staggs responded to the resident's concerns about increased traffic and speeding in the
Foothills subdivision. He said that a previous Council decided not to install speed bumps during
road repaving to facilitate emergency response and snow plowing. However, he expressed a
willingness to revisit the policy and explore potential solutions to address the resident's safety
concerns. He mentioned the possibility of considering stop signs and assured the resident that

Presentations

         Unified Fire Authority

Chief Wade Watkins presented a comprehensive overview of emergency service statistics and
responses for the year 2023. This presentation is included in the packet.

Councilmember McCay asked for explanation about the costs associated with Unified Fire
Authority being called out to neighboring communities. Emphasizing the commitment to
dispatch the closest unit to priority one calls, Chief Watkins mentioned the continuous
adjustments and improvements made to enhance the system's performance. The commitment
to dispatch based on geolocation was underlined as a priority, and he acknowledged the
evolving nature of the system.

Councilmember Buroker emphasized the importance of addressing concerns about the level of
service provided to Bluffdale. She clarified that the tracking of numbers is ongoing, reassuring
that the issue is not overlooked.

Councilmember Pierucci asked about staffing levels and whether there is consistency or
turnover. Chief Watkins said there is satisfaction of the fire crews serving Riverton and staffing
levels are very stable.

Jahn Davis, Senior Paramedic of Heavy Rescue Station 121, emphasized the diverse
specialties covered by heavy rescue, including extrication during accidents, and highlighted the
team's dedication to both citizen and firefighter safety. The crew consists of individuals with
significant experience in the fire service, including military veterans. He discussed the team's
ongoing training in various technical aspects, ensuring readiness for diverse emergency
situations.

         Resolution No. 24-11 – Approving an Amendment to the Riverton City Personnel
         Policies and Procedures Manual

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, discussed the City's default six-month probationary period for all
newly hired employees. Recognizing that this timeframe may not be sufficient for certain roles,
such as police officers who require more time to demonstrate their abilities adequately, the City
would like to assess each job description individually. The goal is to determine whether a longer
probationary period, potentially up to a year, is necessary based on the specific job
requirements. This approach aims to be fair and tailored to the demands of each position,
ensuring that the probationary period aligns with what employees need to demonstrate before
transitioning to merit status. The decision on the probationary period length will be made at the
beginning of the employee's tenure, in consultation between the department head or city
manager.

Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the City Council approve Resolution No. 24-11 –
Approving an Amendment to the Riverton City Personnel Policies and Procedures
Manual. Councilmember McDougal SECONDED the motion. Mayor Staggs called for
discussion on the motion. Mayor Staggs called for a roll-call vote. The vote was as follows:
Buroker-yes, Haymond-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Pierucci-yes. The motion passed
unanimously.

Discussion/Action Items

         Building Use Fee Schedule

Sheril Garn, Events and Operations Director, discussed the need to revisit fees for the
community center. The existing fees date back to 2006, and she proposed updating them
before reopening the community center. She shared a spreadsheet comparing Riverton City's
fees with those of surrounding cities, highlighting that Riverton's fees were the most inclusive.

While not seeking a vote at the moment, Ms. Garn suggested taking a tour of the community
center during the next council meeting to provide a visual understanding of the available rooms.
Additionally, she sought input on rental fees for the pre-function area and whether it should be
considered a separate reservation space or included in the auditorium reservation.

Mayor Staggs asked if consideration for reserving the community center would be given to the
Arts Council or the Jazz Band. Ms. Garn explained that Riverton City's events take priority in
the schedule, followed by formally recognized organizations and then the general public. She
highlighted the current practice of not charging fees for certain community partners, such as the
South Valley Chamber, for using the council room. She also mentioned instances where state
legislators or groups conducting town hall meetings have not been charged a fee, considering it
a public service.

Councilmember Pierucci said he does not believe the City should aim to make a profit from
public facilities because these facilities are for public use. He voice support for maintaining a
low fee structure, even if it is dated. The majority of the Council agreed with Councilmember
Pierucci.

         Parade Route Discussion

Ms. Garn shared that despite initial considerations for changing the parade route to enhance the
community's enjoyment, further discussions led to the decision to maintain the existing route.
The decision was influenced by ongoing construction and utility work on 13400 South and
13800 South, as well as the need to ensure a smooth flow for parade participants and
attendees.

Councilmember Pierucci was supportive of the parade route staying the same but expressed a
desire to bring back the announcers for the parade.

Councilmember McCay would still like to consider a longer route in the future.
