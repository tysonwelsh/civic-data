#1 Nelson Resource Gravel Pit Conditional Use Permit 1st Amendment 
Zetterquist reviewed the staff report for the Nelson Resource Gravel Pit Conditional Use Permit (CUP) 
1st Amendment. 
Staff and Commission discussed ownership of the road, reclamation of the area that will no longer be 
actively worked 
Lee Nelson stated there was no gravel taken out on the north end and are just now making the gravel pit 
smaller. 
Harrild stated that full reclamation will need to happen on the south end that is no longer being worked. 
Mr. Nelson stated reclamation has been started and it is beneficial for them to reclaim those areas. 
Staff and Commission discussed access over the bridge. 
Mr. Nelson stated the bridge weight is 10 tons per axle.  
Harrild asked what the weight would be on the gravel trucks. 
Mr. Nelson stated 17 tons of gravel and should have 5 to 6 axles. The bridge will not be a problem. 
About 500 loads have been taken out already when Johnson owned the pit. The plan is to do 50 loads per 
year. 
Harrild stated condition 16 will be updated.

#2 Windmill Farms Golden Retrievers Conditional Use Permit 
Harrild informed the commission that changes to the kennel code are being suggested and staff doesn’t 
recommend approving or denying the current application until the code changes are decided on. 
Staff and Commission discussed how the application could be affected. The applicant would not have to 
pay the application fee again but would be asked to pay the noticing cost. 
Jed Packer commented he is in support of the changes to come and feel like the right steps are being 
taken to help them accomplish what they would like to do. Paying the noticing fee seems a reasonable 
compromise. 
Olsen motioned to extend the Windmill Farms Golden Retrievers Conditional Use Permit up to 90 days; 
Gunnell seconded; Passed 5, 0.

#4 Discussion: Amendment to Title 17.07 regarding Kennels 
Zetterquist reviewed the proposed amendments to Title 17.07 regarding Kennels. 
Staff and Commission discussed the addition of a rural kennel option to the code and the other proposed 
amendments. It was suggested to require a sign stating contact information that can be used 24 hours to 
reach owners if a problem were to arise. Accessory uses and what that means was discussed. 
07:02:00 
Spackman left. 
Staff and Commission discussed the size of acreage needed for a rural kennel. 
Mr. Packer asked what “excludes 2 incidental litters” means. 
Harrild responded that someone can have 12 dogs and 2 litters and not be in violation of the code. 
Mr. Packer informed the commission of the purpose of kennels and improving genetics and raising the 
incidental litter. 
Watterson asked how long a dog is trained. 
Mr. Packer stated he trains dogs in the AKC Hunter section and in that dogs can be trained from 
anywhere from 1 to 5 years. His dogs are not bred until 2 years old and they are not bred after 6-7 years 
old. 
Christensen asked Mr. Packer what he was asking for. 
Mr. Packer stated he would like the incidental litter to be raised to 6 and explained his reasoning. 
Staff and Commission discussed what staff meant by incidental litter because the current language is 
ambiguous. Commissioners discussed if limiting the number of litters is the Commission’s job if the 
number of adult dogs are limited. 
Staff and Commission agreed to have the revisions brought back next month as a public hearing to 
gather more input from the general public before recommending a final version to County Council.
