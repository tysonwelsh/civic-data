WORK SESSION
Chair Jan Bradshaw called the meeting to order at 5:31 p.m. The agenda items were reviewed and
discussed.

Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink, reported that the Zone Map Amendment to property located
at 4813 South Viewmont Street involves a reapplication from the same property owners who are
represented by Greg Fabiano. A similar application for a rezone to R-1-10 was presented last year
and ultimately not recommended by the Planning Commission and denied by the City Council.
That zone would have yielded three lots in the proposed location. The applicant is now proposing
to rezone the property to R-1-15. If applied to this lot, the zone would allow for a lot split with
average lot sizes of 8,000 square feet. The zone differs from the neighborhood but the lot sizes
are very similar to what exists. There is language in the LDR-S zone that can be applied.
Mr. Teerlink received written comments that were forwarded to the Commission Members.

Commissioner Ricks was conflicted with respect to the R-1-21 zone and the district designation,
which seemed to be at odds with one another. She was not opposed to what is requested but was
concerned about rezoning a small area in the middle of others. She questioned whether the zone
is suitable in this area or if they should consider rezoning a larger area. Ways to solve the problem
long term were discussed.

Mr. Teerlink explained that this version of the General Plan was adopted in 2016 and this area of
zoning was adopted in 1999. The General Plan was applied to a larger area and although it
encompasses this area, it is not helpful because the existing zone is much larger than the proposed
zones. It may warrant a broader discussion with the Council to determine whether the R-1-15 can
be extended to the corner. Doing so would not create further subdividing opportunities but more
conformity and continuance of the R-1-15 zone further up Murray Holladay Road.

Mr. Teerlink confirmed that the intent of the zoning was to preserve existing one-half acre lots.
He explained that the majority of the zoning district map has been created by finding an average

Mr. Teerlink next addressed the ordinance amendment for off-site improvement bonding and
requirements. He explained that the proposal is odd for the Planning Commission to be involved
in since property owners are required to install off-site improvements where required. The
enacting sections of the ordinance that provide details are in a chapter that is not one for which the
Planning Commission has purview. There are, however, portions in Chapter 13 that refer to the
types of positions that process and manage the funds associated with off-site improvements. It
also specifies who can serve as the appeal body and the approving authority. Those are the only
amendments being considered tonight. The section “Fee in Lieu” provides an opportunity for a
property owner to pay an amount determined by the City Engineer to the City who installs the
improvements at a later date. Originally, it was proposed that the Treasurer manage those funds.

Section 13.76 of the Supplementary Regulations was changed to specify that the Community and
Economic Development Director is required to make decisions on whether a Fee in Lieu is
practical. In addition, the City Manager is the appeal authority for granting exceptions to this
section. The Planning Commission was tasked with making a recommendation on the two sections
to the City Council because they are in Chapter 13. The remaining amendments for Title 13
address sidewalk use, modifying use standards for sidewalks, type of sidewalks, and maintenance
responsibilities required of property owners, and addresses who modifies the maintenance
agreement repair standards for off-site improvements. Staff suggested that the Planning
Commission make a recommendation to approve the amendment as drafted.

Commissioner Mackin moved to adjourn the Work Session and resume the regular meeting at

consent of the Commission.

1.      Zone Map Amendment, R-1-21 to R-1-15 – 4813 South Viewmont Street.
        Consideration and Recommendation of a Request by Greg Fabiano, Agent for
        Owner, Cheryl Cromar, to Amend the City of Holladay’s Zoning Map at this
        Location from the Currently Designated Zone, R-1-21 (one home per one-half acre),
        to R-1-15 (one home per 15,000 square feet). Consideration as Per Procedures Ref.
        Holladay Ordinance §13.07.030 – File Number 21-4-02.
Planning Manager, Jonathan Teerlink, presented the staff report and stated that the request is to
amend the zone map for property located at 4813 South Viewmont Street. The application is
legislative in nature and involves a zone map amendment from R-1-21 to R-1-15. The application
appeared before the Planning Commission and City Council by way of a similar type of application
but a different request. Previously the request was for R-1-10, which would have yielded
approximately three lots for 10,000 square-foot properties. Other properties on the street range
from 12,000 square feet in size to one-half acre.

The request for R-1-15 or 15,000 square-foot lots is comparable to what exists in the neighborhood.
15,000 square-foot lots would yield two lots in this location with each averaging 17,000 square
feet. The General Plan District for the area is Low-Density Residential-Stable (LDR-S) and the
recommended zones are R-1-8, R-1-10, and R-1-15.

Greg Fabiano, the agent for the property owner, stated that the request is within the boundaries of
the existing properties. The request would make the two lots within the prospective subdivision
approximately 19,000 square feet in size each, which is significantly larger than the majority of
properties on the street. The zone change was the first step in subdividing the property.

Commissioner Howard Layton noticed that the property will be an island surrounded by the
existing zone. He asked if there had been any effort to contact the two property owners to the east
to perhaps package it together and provide more continuity and fluidity. Mr. Fabiano stated that
they have not contacted the neighboring property owners and to this point, there has been a great
deal of opposition to the requested rezone. They were not opposed to working with the
neighboring property owners if they are willing to consider combining properties.

Two written comments were submitted and provided to the Commission Members in advance of
the meeting.

Chair Bradshaw opened the public hearing.

Ray Flack gave his address as 4871 Viewmont Street and reported that he moved to Holladay in
1955 when he was five years of age. He has known the Cromars throughout that time. The parents
wanted to have one acre of property and purchased a parcel formerly owned by the McClures.
Doug Cromar sold the McClure home out front on the .21-acre site and kept the rest. Mr. Flack

Commissioner Lloyd referenced the lot line demarcation and stated that the four properties directly

Commissioner Ricks commented that the difference between the district map and the current

Mr. Teerlink stated that one option involves making an amendment without input from the property
owners. The other would involve reaching out to the property owners and informing them that
there is interest from the Commission in rezoning this section to R-1-15 for the sake of congruency.
The neighboring property owners would have to be a co-applicant in that scenario. Commissioner
Lloyd questioned whether a consensus could be reached in the neighborhood. She did not consider
the proposal to be outrageous and thought it was efficient, well-conceived, and appropriate.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Mackin, Mr. Teerlink stated that the request will
be based on the threshold of 15,000 square feet in terms of acreage. Any lot that is greater than
.34-acre would still conform to the current zone. Anything below that threshold would be
considered non-conforming. Having a lot that is closer in conformity alleviates some concerns
from lending institutions and those who hold title. The drawback was the process and having a
one-half acre country estate zone being placed on the property where that use no longer exists.

Commissioner Lloyd moved to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council that the
Zone Map Amendment for 4813 South Viewmont Street be changed from R-1-21 to R-1-15 as
it is compatible with the findings contained in the General Plan with regard to continuity of the
neighborhood, compatible lot size, and traffic will be nominal and appropriate. Commissioner
Howard Layton seconded the motion. Vote on motion: Commissioner Ricks-Nay,
Commissioner Mackin-Aye, Commissioner Lloyd-Aye, Commissioner Howard Lloyd-Aye,

4.       Ordinance Amendment – Off-Site Improvement Bonding and Requirements
         (13.01.100, 13.76.210). Consideration and Recommendation of an Amendment
         Updating Approval Procedures as they Relate to Off-Site Improvement and Bonding
         Requirements. (Sidewalk, Park Strips, Street Trees, Curbs, and Gutters.) File #21-
         4-08.
Mr. Teerlink presented the staff report and stated that late last year, staff was interested in
correcting problems with procedures for off-site improvement requirements, specifically as they
relate to bonding, orders of operation, and areas of the City that get more focused attention on gaps
in sidewalks and curb and gutter. Most of the language requiring that type of procedure from the
City is in a chapter that the Planning Commission does not have purview over. Chapter 13 contains
specific sections that specify that when new development is proposed or homes are rebuilt, the
improvement of the right-of-way is required. He explained that the City does not necessarily install
gutters and sidewalk in large stretches. When new subdivisions are created, improvements are the
responsibility of the developer. When the County made the decision to not require it in this area
and allowed unimproved rights-of-way, it placed that burden on property owners seeking to
redevelop. The methods by which improvements occur were as follows:

     •   Complete the work;
     •   Allow a payment in lieu where the proposed cost of the improvements is assessed by the
         City. The property owner pays the City to install the improvements at a later date; or
     •   A waiver.

Chapter 13 addresses the standards and specifies the procedure when a fee in lieu is proposed
including who manages the funds and how the funds are collected and recorded. It was also
necessary to reclassify and retitle staff reports. A change was also made to specify that the
exceptions section is heard by the City Manager rather than the Community Development Director
with a recommendation from the Technical Review Committee (“TRC”). Mr. Teerlink explained
that text amendments are being made to Chapter 14, which the City Council has purview over.

Commissioner Howard Layton asked who determines whether the curb, gutter, or sidewalk needs
to be replaced. Mr. Teerlink stated that the City Engineer makes the determination with a
recommendation from the TRC to the City Manager or requires the improvement to be replaced.
Many of the City’s gutters are 80 to 90 years old and many need to be replaced. If development
is not proposed and the City Engineer discovers a drainage problem in front of a property owner’s
home, he can identify the issue and use the 50/50 cost-sharing situation to remedy that problem.
