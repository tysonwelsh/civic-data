PUBLIC HEARING:

PC 23-036 Lundstrom Landing Short Term Rental [Conditional Use Permit] Kevin & Baylee Bouck
are requesting a conditional use permit to operate a 4-bedroom short-term rental in a home located
at 1512 East 1260 North in the Traditional Neighborhood Residential (NR-6) zone; TIN 07-041-0015
(Hillcrest Neighborhood).

STAFF: Tanya Rice, City Planner reviewed the request for a Conditional Use Permit to operate a 4-
bedroom Short Term Rental of an entire home located at the corner of 1500 East and 1260 North in
the Hillcrest Neighborhood. The Short Term Rental will allow for a maximum occupancy of 10 people
and requires 4 parking stalls. The applicant currently has an active driveway and right-of-way permit
to add a second driveway to meet the parking requirement. As conditioned the proposed Short
Term Rental meets all requirements and staff is recommending approval.

Commissioner Guth asked about the distance requirement from other Short-Term Rentals. T. Rice
said there is no other Short Term Rental within 500 feet of this property.

PROPONENT: Theodore Mahan, representing the homeowners spoke about all of the renovations
that have been done on this home. The owner plans to use the home as a short-term rental until the
fall when their daughter moves in to attend school. The home will be well maintained by the owners
and will not negatively impact the neighborhood.

PUBLIC: Tom Lee, who lives near the proposed Short Term Rental, said he is concerned about the
strangers that a Short Term Rental will bring to the neighborhood, and said that Short Term Rentals
should be located in resort areas, not in a residential neighborhood. He also brought up a concern
with the approval for a second driveway.

Alan Stephens spoke about the problems related to Short Term Rentals, commercialization of

residential areas, and how the proposed rental allowing 10 people to stay in the home violates
occupancy for the zone.


Linda Hodges a neighbor to the east spoke about the young children that live in the neighborhood
and walk past this house to get on the school bus and how the strangers that stay in this home who
are not familiar with the area will not know where to watch for the children crossing the street. The
Short Term Rental is not a good fit for the neighborhood, and they do not want it there.

Melanie Pond Spoke about this home already being listed on Short Term Rental sites and being
rented out since April.

Liz Kefauver spoke about the neighborhood going downhill and working to get the zoning changed
to not allow Short-Term Rentals.

Brent Carruth Spoke about the residents not being notified of any changes proposed in their
neighborhood for the past 15 years and the Future Land Use Plan prohibiting commercial use in
neighborhoods.

Ted Mahan spoke about the university and how many people staying in Short Term Rentals are
parents visiting their children who are attending the university, or professionals visiting for work. He
has seen people banned from booking on the Short Term Rental sites for as little as a noise violation.
Mr. Mahan said that many long-term renters do not take care of the lawn or the outside of the home.
With Short Term Rentals, the owner is constantly taking care and maintaining of the home.

Peggy Cashell agreed with all of the other comments made and spoke about affordable homes in
the area being purchased to use as a Short Term Rentals, driving the homeowners who live in the
homes out of the neighborhood and causing the neighborhood to go downhill.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Heare asked Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director to
address the comment made regarding the Short Term Rental violating Occupancy for the zone. M.
DeSimone said that occupancy is one family or 3 individuals. Short Term Rentals are allowed to
rent to two people per bedroom plus two, a four-bedroom Short Term Rental is allowed to have 10
people.

Commissioner Heare said there are state notification requirements that the city follows on every
project. The noticing process was followed when the Land Development Code was amended to
allow Short-Term Rentals and there were not many comments received against it. If residents want
to change the Land Development Code, they need to contact their elected officials to address a
proposed change.

Commissioner Heare said that if there are illegal Short Term Rentals operating, neighbors need to
report it to the City so enforcement action can be taken. M. DeSimone added that the City is
prohibited by state law from seeking out illegal short-term rentals via their websites to start any
enforcement action, the City relies on complaints. Commissioner Heare said that the neighbors
should also report if any violations are happening at a permitted Short-Term Rental Property.

T. Rice reviewed the process to obtain a business license for a Short-Term Rental which includes
noticing all of the neighbors within 300 feet of the Short-Term Rental. T. Rice also mentioned that
this proposed Short Term Rental applied for a permit after a complaint was made and the City
contacted the owner to notify them that they needed a Conditional Use Permit and Business License
to operate.

23-036 Lundstrom Landing Short Term Rental with the conditions of approval and finding for

Commissioner Heare encouraged the public to contact their City Council Representative regarding

PC_ 23-033 Old Ephraim Apartments 3 [Design Review & Conditional Use Permit] Nathan
Whittaker/GBN Enterprises LLC, authorized agent/owner are requesting a design review and
conditional use permit to develop ten townhome units with a total of 60 bedrooms. There are 103
proposed parking stalls, 93 on-site, and 10 off-site. The project is located at 643, 645, 655, and 675
East 400 North in the Campus Residential (CR) zone; TIN 06-058-0005; -0006; -0007; (Adams
Neighborhood).

STAFF: Russ Holley, Planner reviewed the request for a Design Review and Conditional Use Permit.
This is the third review of this project, the first two proposals were completely different buildings, but
the second and third proposals are very similar with the same building and site layout, the only
differences are related to parking with an offsite location and a request for a reduction in the
previously approved campus academy parking stalls.

Considering all buildings and conditional uses on the 1.29-acre site, the total parking requirement
would be 109 parking stalls. The applicant is requesting that the Planning Commission consider a
parking reduction and allow 103 parking stalls.

On March 9", 2023, the Planning Commission conditionally approved Old Ephraim’s Apartments
(second submittal) with 109 parking stalls. The new (third) application shows an additional 10
parking stalls located off-site, within the allowed 600-foot radius of the site, which totals 103 parking
stalls with one parking stall in the front setback, which is not allowed in the NR-6 zone, and a deficient
drive aisle that is 18 feet wide, which does not meet the 24-foot minimum drive aisle width established
in the Land Development Code. The Planning Commission will need to determine the viability and
access to these proposed offsite stalls.

The applicant is requesting a reduction in Campus Academy parking from the 25 stalls originally
required down to 17, which would meet the residential parking demand for the site. However,
Campus Academy has 24 beds and if it ever converted back to student housing, the parking demand
would not be met.

This is atypical where there are two projects with two separate permits to approve. The properties
are all one owner and one developer, they share parking and open space. Where the properties are
all contiguous and adjacent both permits can be approved together.

PROPONENT: Lance Anderson spoke about the 25 parking stalls for Campus Academy coming
from the original Old Ephraim project that included a 150-stall parking structure. Since then they
have determined that their need is only 17 for Campus Academy. They do not intend for Campus
Academy to revert back to student housing, but if it did revert, the parking would only be at a 5%
reduction for the entire site.


Nathan Whittaker confirmed that he has no intention of Campus Academy reverting back to student
housing.

PUBLIC: Jeannie Simmonds asked if the smaller drive isles affect the number of parking stalls and
if the commission would rather have seven extra parking stalls for Campus Academy or be seven
under what is needed when 25 students are living there.

COMMISSION: Commissioner Doutre asked if one of the conditions allows the reduced drive isles.
R. Holley said that the site plan has already been approved by the Planning Commission for Campus
Academy. The Commission will need to determine if the 18-foot wide drive aisle is sufficient for the
10-stall off-site parking lot.

Commissioner Lucero asked if when Campus Academy was originally approved if 17 stalls would
have been permitted. R. Holley said that the parking approved for Campus Academy was done
under an alternative parking plan, and if the applicant could demonstrate that 17 stalls were adequate
to meet the anticipated parking demands, it would have been permitted.

Commissioner Lewis said that there have been several exceptions granted for this project and the
correct decision was made with the last approval requiring 109 parking stalls.

Commissioner Doutre brought up a concern with the parking if Old Ephraim is built and Campus
Academy is still being used as student housing. Nathan Whittaker said that he already has student
contracts in place for the next school year for the existing apartments on the site so the construction
of Old Ephraim will not start for another year.

Commissioner Guth asked the applicant to explain how the 17 requested stalls are being calculated.

Nathan Whittaker said that they are calculating for 30 clients (which is a much more reasonable
occupancy count than the 48 that the existing CUP allows for) at a 4 to 1 required staff ratio which
equates to 8 staff, plus 4 additional administrative staff for a total of 12 staff on site. In addition, there
will be 2 twelve-passenger vans, and 3 flex parking stalls for visitors for a total of 17 stalls. No
parking is needed for the clients as they are all underage and do not drive.

Commissioner Guth said if they go up to the maximum occupancy allowed which was 48 clients,
they would need more than 12 parking stalls for staff. Commissioner Doutre asked if the maximum
occupancy could be changed to 30 to ensure the 17 parking stalls are enough.

M. DeSimone said that the applicant would need to volunteer to reduce the maximum occupancy of
Campus Academy from 48 to 30, the commission cannot require the reduction.

Nathan Whittaker said that he is willing to reduce the maximum occupancy for Campus Academy
from 48 down to 30 if the Planning Commission will approve a parking reduction to 102 parking stalls
for the entire site.

Commissioner Heare stated that he is still concerned with a future parking issue should the
Conditional Use end.

MOTION: Commissioner Lucero made a motion to Conditionally Approve a Design Review and
Conditional Use Permit for PC_ 23-033 Old Ephraim Apartments 3 with the proposed amendment to
the existing CUP for Campus Academy (PC 22-069) to reduce the occupancy to 30 and the parking
to 17 stalls for a total parking requirement of 102 for the entire site as stated in the conditions of
approval and findings for approval as listed below. Commissioner Doutre seconded the motion.
