32   Mr. Johnson reported that with regard to the Short-Term Rental Ordinance, there has been a desire

31   The Commission’s primary area of focus was the definition of Short-Term Rentals to make a

11          3.3     (Project ZTA-21-001) A Public Hearing to Receive Comments and Make a

16   Mr. Johnson reported that Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADU”) are defined as long-term rentals of
17   greater than 30 days within a single-family home or on the same property as a single-family home.
18   Common terms for such units include “basement apartments” or “mother-in-law” units. During a
19   recent State legislative session, HB-82 was passed as part of the Building Code Regulations. Most
20   notably, the State legislation allowed internal ADUs within the footprint of a primary dwelling on
21   a property to be a permitted use in every single-family residential zone in the State of Utah. By
22   this action, the State effectively preempted this portion of the local authority in allowing internal
23   ADUs statewide, effective October 1, 2021. The State legislation also placed strict limitations on
24   what the City can regulate or require for internal ADUs. Updated State Building Code Standards
25   were previously adopted and updated referencing specific requirements for ADUs that differ from
26   other multi-family construction. The legislation also created a pilot program for funding and
27   financing of ADU-related loans.
28
29   Specifically, the State legislation provides that except as provided, municipalities may not
30   establish restrictions or requirements for the construction or use of one internal ADU within a
31   primary dwelling (thereby limiting local authority). A municipality cannot restrict the square
32   footage size of an ADU, the total lot size of where the ADU is located, or the street frontage. It
33   requires that ADUs comply with all applicable building, health, and fire codes. A new provision
34   allows for the requirement that any egress window located in what will be converted into an ADU
35   be brought up to current Building Code Standards. Other Building Codes already adopted at the
36   State level allow for exceptions in the construction of ADUs as they relate to issues like fire-wall
37   separation, return air, and climate control.
38
39   The State legislation additionally provided a list of what cities are allowed to regulate including
40   requiring permits or licenses, restricting ownership to owner-occupancy, prohibiting multiple
41   utility meters, and requiring ADU design in a manner consistent with single-family dwellings. As
42   identified at the work session, a separate bill that was passed no longer allows cities to impose
43   design standards on single-family dwellings, which made the point moot. A city can require one
44   additional off-street parking space. There had been much prior discussion about parking
45   requirements in the previous City’s effort to adopt an ADU ordinance. Now the City is limited to
46   requiring one additional spot.

 3   than 6,000 square feet in size. Cities can also prohibit an ADU in a zoning district covering 25%

18   Mr. Johnson then gave a summary of the proposed Ordinance. He noted that the process has been
19   ongoing for 1 ½ to 2 years to take public input and prepare a draft ordinance that was felt to meet
20   the City’s needs. Staff started with an ordinance that was recommended by the Planning
21   Commission but denied by the City Council. Modifications were made to bring the proposed

24   The Purpose Statement referenced the subject State Code, and the Definitions were updated to
25   comply with State Code. Additional language defined where ADUs are permitted (within every
26   single-family residential zone) and the allowed exceptions of lots less than 6,000 square feet in

29   Mr. Johnson reported that the proposed Ordinance allows for the possibility of detached ADUs (as
30   an accessory building on the property). As detached ADUs are not regulated or governed by State
31   Legislature, the City has more authority to regulate these detached units. The language for
32   detached ADUs in the proposed Ordinance is the same as it was presented previously. A much
33   more rigorous process is required including Planning Commission public hearings, extra parking,
34   extra property buffering, enhanced setbacks, and other requirements. No updating or change to
35   those rigorous standards had occurred since the last publication.
36
37   The approval process requires inspection, either of new work needed to bring ADU in compliance
38   with Code or walk-thrus to verify that an area proposed for an ADU already meets the Code
39   requirements. After that, yearly licensure keeps track of the inventory of the legal and licensed
40   ADUs in the City. Detached ADUs require the same process by way of a Conditional Use Permit
41   from the Planning Commission.
42
43   With regard to development standards, the proposed Ordinance sets forth what the City is allowed
44   to require for internal ADUs. The main difference from the previous ordinance was the
45   requirement of one parking stall versus the previously allowed two spaces. That one space is in
46   addition to the two spaces is already required for any single-family use. The proposed Ordinance
