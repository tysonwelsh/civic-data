CALL TO ORDER
Council Chair Jacob called the work session to order at 5:30 p.m., and noted the meeting was open
to the public in Council Chambers, available remotely via Zoom, and streamed live on YouTube.

A.     FY2022 Budget Progress Update
Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck updated the Council on the FY2022 Budget process,
and emphasized that the process was ongoing and numbers would continue to evolve. She showed
anticipated on-going General Fund revenues and expenses, and presented recommendations for
FY2022 Budget expansion (not including 15 full-time positions requested by City departments).
Council Chair Jacob suggested yearly compensation reviews. Council Members Pack, Whitelock, and
Worthen expressed agreement with the suggestion.

Ms. Steck provided an update on the Water Fund, including FY2021 and FY2022 cost estimates (acre
feet purchased from JVWCD). Council Member Whitelock commented on the high cost to the City of
exceeding a certain level of water use and requested assurance from administration regarding how
the City would handle water usage. City Administrator Korban Lee commented that renewing the
City’s contract with Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District (JVWCD) was contingent on the City
adopting standards that met JVWCD efficiency standards. Council Member Worthen shared
personal experience with making her yard more water-wise, and said she saved a significant
amount on her water bill. Ms. Steck stated no rate change was proposed water rates in FY2022. The
five-year plan for the Water Fund included a 2% rate increase per year in future years.

Ms. Steck presented an anticipated FY2022 Budget for the Sewer Fund, with no rate change
proposed for FY2022 (future year rate increases of 3% per year). She said she believed reserves in
the Sewer Fund and Water Fund were sufficient to support no rate change in FY2022. Council Chair
Jacob expressed support for consistent minor annual rate increases from a policy and planning
City Council Work Session Minutes – March 24, 2021                                              Page 2

standpoint. Council Member McConnehey expressed support for not increasing rates unless
absolutely necessary.

Ms. Steck showed anticipated Sold Waste Fund and Storm Water Fund budgets and proposed a 3%
rate increase for both funds in FY2022 to support needed construction projects. She stated the
overall impact to citizens for utility rate increases in FY2022 would be less than $10.00 per year. No
rate change was proposed for the Streetlight Fund in FY2022; however, future year rate increases
of 2.5% per year were recommended.

Responding to a question from Council Member Whitelock, Ms. Steck explained that developers
were financially responsible for basic infrastructure associated with new development and spoke
of growth and development impact fees.

Regarding possible restoration of City positions eliminated in the previous budget year, Council
Member Worthen spoke in favor of keeping institutional knowledge. She suggested the possibility
of using some of the funds set aside for an art center to reinstate some of the positions, and
suggested there were creative ways the City could help the art community in the short-term even if
an art center was not yet built. Vice Chair Green pointed out that one-time money could not be used
to fund ongoing expenses. Council Member Pack said he agreed it was better to retain good
employees than to obtain new employees, but said he agreed with not using one-time funds for
ongoing expenses and expressed a desire to safeguard funding for the art center.

Council Chair Jacob said Council discussion of the FY2022 Budget would continue at a future
meeting.

B.      Council Expectations and Procedures
Council Chair Jacob asked for feedback from the Council regarding work session and Council
meeting format. Council Member Whitelock said she appreciated open dialogue and discussion with
Staff during work sessions. Council Member Worthen said she would be fine with meeting on nights
other than scheduled Council meeting nights if needed. Council Member Worthen said she received
negative feedback from developers related to going through the process with Staff and the Planning
Commission only to be sent back at the City Council level. Council Chair Jacob responded that the
Land Use Committee was formed in response to that issue. Council Member Worthen said she
appreciated being able to review staff reports prior to public meetings.

Council Member McConnehey said he felt the Council meeting format had improved over the last
three years, and said he believed there was more that could be done. He suggested each
presentation to the Council begin with an explanation of what was needed from the Council. Council
Member McConnehey said he believed work sessions were valuable and should be an opportunity
to discuss principles rather than wordsmithing. He requested a way to know what pending items
were waiting on action from the Council, and emphasized that he believed things had improved and
were getting better.

Council Member Lamb said he would be fine with scheduling work sessions on nights other than
Council meeting nights. He said he had enjoyed having work sessions before Council meetings, but
commented that an hour and a half was not enough time to finish a lot of what needed to be
accomplished.

       b. Jones Ranch Sub-Area Plan 1
City Planner/Zoning Administrator Larry Gardner showed a land use diagram for Jones Ranch. He
said the term “fluctuating density residential” would be changed to “Jones Ranch high residential”
on the land use map. He showed a proposed area plan for Sub-Area Plan 1, including Village 1,
Village 4, clubhouse amenity/groomed open space, and passive open space. He emphasized that
overall density for Jones Ranch was set by the Master Development Plan (MDP) at 5 units per acre.

Bryon Prince with Ivory Development displayed the proposed placement of low, medium, and high-
density residential in the sub-area, a streets diagram, and explained plans for trail connectivity. He
emphasized that parking standards were outlined in the Master Development Plan. All proposed
products would have a two-car garage and full-length two-car driveway, with additional guest
parking provided. Mr. Prince noted that the amenity detail would be defined with a preliminary plat.
Responding to a question from Council Member Pack, Mr. Prince said the HOA would be responsible
for maintaining passive open space.

Council Member Lamb asked the anticipated price range for the first phase of housing. Mr. Prince
responded the cost of housing had risen dramatically in the last 12-18 months. He said the hope was
to deliver a product in the $300,000-$400,000 range in the higher density single-family lots. Mr.
Prince said part of the strategy for the sub-area was to show Ivory’s vision for the area. He said it
was possible some of the units would be removed from the plan if the market demanded.

Mr. Prince said the HOA would maintain all common landscaped areas. Landscaping would be
maintained by homeowners on single-family lots. He confirmed Ivory would encourage water-wise
planting. He provided more detail on the planned trail system and answered questions from the
Council.

 8.    BUSINESS ITEMS
a.     Discuss repealing the Mayor’s Emergency Order
Council Office Director Alan Anderson showed March and April 2020 calendars with declarations of
COVID-19 states of emergency highlighted. On March 16, 2020, the Mayor signed Emergency Order
2020-02, which was extended by the City Council with Resolution No. 20-22 on April 8, 2020. On
April 10, 2021, HB294 would go into effect, eliminating the state-wide mask mandate.

Mayor Burton stated the reasons for putting the emergency order in place still existed. He
emphasized he could not predict what would happen with new strains of the virus, and said he
wanted to be able to handle circumstances as they came. The Mayor said the County Health
Department recommended keeping the emergency order in place.

Responding to a question from Council Member Lamb, Mayor Burton said any future emergency
funding would come directly from the Federal Government, and he did not know if the City would
receive future funding without an emergency order in place. Council Member Lamb said he did not
want to continue a state of emergency just because everyone else was doing it. Mayor Burton said
it was his understanding that he would not be able to reinstate the emergency order once it was
ended.

Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck said she supported the Mayor’s stance on the issue.
In order to be eligible for funding through FEMA or ARCMA, the City would have to have a state of
emergency in place. Ms. Steck advised it was prudent to keep the state of emergency in place until
requirements were met for any funding available.

Vice Chair Green said it was his understanding that FEMA funding was reliant on a governor’s
declaration, not a city declaration. Ms. Steck responded that if the City still had a declaration in place
at the time the Governor’s Declaration ended, a City application for funding may still be eligible for
consideration. She emphasized there were many unknowns when dealing with the Federal
Government and said she would not want the City to miss out on an opportunity for assistance.

Council Member McConnehey left the meeting at 9:03 p.m., and returned at 9:08 p.m.

Community Engagement & Government Affairs Director Tauni Barker agreed and felt it would be
wise to hold off discontinuing the declaration and confirmed that SB195 limited the ability to
declare a state of emergency in response to the same disaster. She said the Utah League of Cities and
City Council Meeting Minutes – March 24, 2021                                                Page 7

Towns (ULCT) recommended the City continue the state of emergency until the end of the pandemic
and new normal conditions were established. Mr. Anderson advised it may be beneficial to wait
until rules of SB195 were outlined.

Council Member Whitelock said the Council had heard why the declaration should not be
discontinued, and asked what data was driving the decision to come before the Council at that time.
Council Chair Jacob responded it was more a question of philosophy than data. He explained that
declaring a state of emergency a year ago allowed the City to react to the pandemic and other
emergencies that occurred, but the situation was no longer an “emergency”. He said a new strain of
the virus or an earthquake would be a new emergency situation. Council Chair Jacob emphasized
that discontinuing the emergency declaration would not mean everything was repaired and back to
normal. The Legislation brought up the question, which prompted the current Council discussion.
Council Member Whitelock said she would prefer to make data-driven decisions rather than
philosophical. She pointed out that the virus had not stopped spreading or mutating.

Council Member Pack said he did not believe any abuse of power had occurred. He said he would
not want the decision to only be money-driven, but stated the City needed all the help it could get
in servicing programs and helping citizens.

Vice Chair Green said he agreed with Council Chair Jacob that a state of emergency was declared
because there was something that needed to be responded to quickly; however, after a year he
believed the City could respond to anything without having an emergency declaration. He said it
was clear with HB294 and SB195 the Utah Legislature was saying in the future cities did not have
authority to declare states of emergency on their own. He said he had a hard time believing the
Federal Government would check that each city had declared a state of emergency. He questioned
what emergency the City would be trying to respond to with a continued declaration, and said the
question was not “if” the declaration would be discontinued, but “when”. Vice Chair Green pointed
out the City had no authority over County Health Department mandates.

Council Member Worthen said she did not understand the rush. She said there was more reasons to
keep the declaration in place than to discontinue. She said she understood the declaration would be
discontinued in the next few months but said she would prefer to keep options open to get funds
and help the citizens.

Council Member McConnehey said he would like to see something from the Mayor’s Office with
milestones and a recommendation of when to rescind the emergency order. Council Chair Jacob
commented that the Mayor or the Council could end the state of emergency at any time without
approval of the other party.

Vice Chair Green said he wanted better clarity if the emergency order were only extended for money
and expressed the opinion that milestones should be tied to money if money were the reason for
extending. Council Chair Jacob asked if the City needed the money. He said the Council was told in
the budget presentation prior to the Council meeting that the City would be okay for the next 12
months. He asked if it was still needed and right to maintain a state of emergency and cede power
from the Legislative Branch to the Executive Branch. Council Chair Jacob expressed the opinion that
the City was no longer in an emergent situation.

Council Member Worthen responded the Council did not know what was around the corner, and
keeping the declaration in place would not hurt anything. Council Chair Jacob said he believed it
City Council Meeting Minutes – March 24, 2021                                                Page 8
