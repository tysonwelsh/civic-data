Proposal 
The applicant is requesting approval of a Conditional Use Permit to replace 5 1 existing 
distribution poles in Eastern Summit County that have been identified as structurally deficient 
through field inspections. The proposed work is necessary to ensure compliance with the 
National Electric Safety Code, Rocky Mountain Power standards, and other applicable state and 
federal regulations. Due to the age and condition of the existing poles, the replacement poles 
will be approximately 3.5 feet taller to increase ground clearance and support wildfire 
mitigation efforts. This project is intended to improve  Rocky Mountain Power’s electrical 
system, enhance public safety, and reduce wildfire risk in the area. 
 
Project Description 
Project Name: Rocky Mountain Distribution Pole Replacement   
Applicant(s): Andrew Badger, Rocky Mountain Power   
Property Owner(s): Rocky Mountain Power via Easements 
Location: Multiple Parcel in Eastern Summit County   
Type of Process: Administrative   
Final Land Use Authority: Eastern Summit County Planning Commission 
 
Background 
Rocky Mountain Power (RMP) is the primary electricity provider in Summit County. On August 
11, 2025, RMP submitted a Conditional Use Permit (CUP) application requesting approval to 
replace 5 1 existing electrical distribut ion poles located within Eastern Summit County. The 
poles proposed for replacement were identified during routine field inspections as A ‑condition 
poles, indicating structural deficiencies and a heightened risk of imminent failure. Replacement 
of these poles is necessary to maintain public safety, ensure system reliability, and comply with 
the National Electric Safety Code (NESC), Rocky Mountain Power standards, and applicable 
state and federal regulations. 
3
2 
 
The proposed project consists of routine distribution system maintenance within existing Rocky 
Mountain Power rights ‑of‑way and easements and does not involve the establishment of new 
utility corridors. The subject poles are located along several existing distribution circuits, 
including Oakley ‑11 (OKY‑ 11) and Kamas ‑12 (KAM ‑12). Of the 5 1 poles proposed for 
replacement, 16 poles are located within FEMA‑designated Special Flood Hazard Areas (SFHAs). 
The project proposes like‑for‑like pole replacements, with new poles installed in close proximity 
to the existing poles. Due to the age of the existing infrastructure, replace ment poles will be 
approximately 3.5 feet taller than the existing poles. This increase in height will improve ground 
clearance, enhance system safety, and assist in wildfire risk mitigation. 
Protecting public safety while providing reliable electrical service is a core objective of Rocky 
Mountain Power’s ongoing infrastructure maintenance program. The proposed pole 
replacements are intended to enhance the safety and resiliency of the local electrical system, 
reduce the potential for wildfire, and ensure continued, reliable service for residents and 
businesses throughout Summit County.

Findings of Fact: 
1. On August 11, 2025, Andrew Badger (Rocky Mountain Power)  submitted a Conditional 
Use Permit (CUP) application requesting approval to replace 46  existing electrical 
distribution poles located within Eastern Summit County.  
2. Of the 46 originally proposed poles, 3 are located within Utah Department of 
Transportation (UDOT) right -of-way and 1 is located within Park City municipal 
boundaries. These 4 poles are not subject to this CUP application. Therefore, the total 
number of poles within Eastern Summit County under this application is 42. 
3. Specifically, the pole located on Parcel PC -65-A-X is within Park City M unicipal 
boundaries. Poles located on Parcels PP -105-A-1-X, PP-98-1-X, and PP-98-1-X are within 
UDOT right-of-way and will require separate permitting from the appropriate agencies. 
As such, these poles are not included in this CUP application. 
4. On March 3, 2026, the applicant revised the proposal to include an additional 9 poles, 
bringing the total number of proposed pole replacements to 51. 
5. The proposed work is necessary to ensure compliance with the National Electric Safety 
Code, Rocky Mountain Power standards, and other applicable state and federal 
regulations.  
6. The proposal is located within two zoning districts: Agriculture -10 (AG -10) and 
Agriculture-80 (AG-80). 
7. In both zones, utility towers and associated transmission and distribution lines 
regardless of the height are permitted as Conditional Uses.   
8. The proposed project does not involve the establishment of new utility corridors.  
9. The subject poles are located along several existing distribution circuits, including 
Oakley‑11 (OKY‑11) and Kamas‑12 (KAM‑12).  
10. The project does not include residential, commercial, or industrial development and 
does not involve the construction of habitable structures or facilities requiring water 
service. 
11. The proposal includes increasing the height of the existing el ectrical distribution poles 
from approximately 35 feet to 38.5 feet  to increase ground clearance and support 
wildfire mitigation efforts.  
12. A total of 16 poles are located within FEMA- designated Special Flood Hazard Areas 
(SFHAs). 
13. The applicant has submitted the required documentation and permits to the Summit 
County Engineering Department in accordance with applicable floodplain regulations. 
14. The proposed project consists solely of the replacement of existing electrical 
distribution poles within established Rocky Mountain Power easements and 
rights‑of‑way. 
15. Because of the exclusion of poles from the definition of a Structure, power poles (Utility 
Towers) are exempt from the ridgeline development requirements, setbacks, and height 
requirements.  
16. Staff and relevant service providers have reviewed the proposal.

Chapter 8: Community Health and Recreation 
 
The Community Health and Recreation Element considers how the built environment 
affects physical, mental, emotional, and social health and strives to influence built 
environments to foster positive health outcomes.  
 
This Element is supplemented by the Summit County Rail Trail Corridor Plan, which 
specifically considers recreation along the Historic Union Pacific Rail Trail. The Rail Trail 
Corridor Plan is a separate external Appendix of this General Plan. Other relate d and 
supporting plans include: 
 
• Summit County Active Transportation Plan 
• Snyderville Basin Recreation 5-Year Trails and Open Space Plan 
• Summit County Health Department Community Health Assessment 
• Summit County Health Department Community Health Improvement Plan 
 
Context 
 
Community health in Eastern Summit County is shaped by the close -knit communities but 
limited convenient access to health infrastructure. Access to healthcare services, while 
improving, remains a challenge. The population is increasingly focusing on wellne ss and 
outdoor activities. However, mental health services, substance abuse treatment, and care 
for aging populations could be improved.   
 
Recreation has historically been directed toward the National Forests and State Parks in the 
area, but the community's growing desire for more comprehensive, local recreation 
opportunities is growing. Although North Summit Recreation was recently created, they are 
limited in capacity and are primarily focused on recreation programming and community 
sports. South Summit  residents have not voted in sufficient numbers for a recreation 
district. Instead, non -profits like the South Summit Trails Foundation have been slowly 
working to build and maintain trails in the area while the municipalities, Oakley, Kamas, and 
Francis, have some facilities like City Parks with various courts and playground equipment.  
 
Existing Conditions 
 
The 2024 Summit County Community Health Assessment (CHA), utilized both quantitative 
and qualitative data collection, including surveys, focus groups, and secondary data 
analysis, to present a holistic picture of the community's health landscape. Summit County 
residents identified key strengths and health priorities that were used in the development of 
the Community Health Improvement Plan (CHIP). The CHA data and the CHIP will guide the 
Health Department’s efforts over the next five years and work in tande m with the goals and 
objectives of this general plan for priorities that are outside the focus of public health.

The Summit County Health Department oversees efforts to sustain and improve factors that 
influence health outcomes in Summit County. Detailed plans for how the health department 
will partner with community organizations to prioritize health, please see the  2026-2030 
Community Health Improvement Plan (CHIP). The three priority areas for the 2026 -2030 
CHIP are: (1) Strengthen Partnerships to Impact Community Health Drivers; (2) Improve 
Food and Nutrition Access for an Evolving Community; and (3) Increase Immu nization 
Coverage to Reduce Preventable Disease. 
 
The following strategies in the remainder of this chapter act as guiding tools to provide 
recreation and improve health outcomes as they relate to land use.  
 
GOAL: Create land use policies and programs that promote positive health outcomes

income households, and households without transportation or internet resources, from

transportation infrastructure, commercial opportunities, and medical facilities, and

Strategy 8.1.3: Create smoke -free housing policies for all new affordable 
housing developments and multi-unit housing complexes to reduce exposure 
to secondhand smoke and promote healthy environments. 
 
Policy 8.2: Ensure Eastern Summit County adopts technologies that ensure rural 
communities can improve social, economic and educational opportunities.
