        An application for Preliminary Subdivision Plat for a one-lot plat, located at 3432 S State

        ACTION ITEM
        Applicant:    Fred Cox
        Address:      3432 South State Street

City Planner, Jed Shum, presented the Staff Report and indicated that the application was for
preliminary plat approval to plat three parcels into one at 3432 South State Street in the
Commercial Corridor (“CC”) Zone. A satellite image was reviewed, and Mr. Shum reported that
Ichiban Sushi and Grill and Chicken Express were currently operating on two of the parcels. The
purpose of the Subdivision Plat was to build a new structure at the location.

The proposed Preliminary Subdivision Plat, Site Plan, and elevations were reviewed. The Alta
Survey submitted by the applicant verified that there were no outstanding easements or issues with

Twelve additional parking stalls will be added for a total of 100 stalls to meet parking
requirements. The building will maintain the design style of the existing Ichiban Sushi and Grill
restaurant. All site landscaping will be brought up to current landscaping standards.

2.     PUBLIC HEARING
       An application for preliminary subdivision plat approval consolidating seven parcels into
       a one-lot subdivision, and vacating lots 11-23 of the Longview Park Subdivision.
       ACTION ITEM
       Applicant:     SLC OPC Land, LLC
       Address:       3711 South State Street

Senior City Planner, Spencer Cawley, presented the Staff Report and indicated that the subject
property is located at 3711 South State Street in the CC Zone. The site had primarily been utilized
for automotive uses since the late 1970s. A recreational vehicle dealership closed in 2024, and a
car rental business was currently operating on the northern portion of the property. The proposed
lot would have four points of access on State Street, Helm Avenue, 200 East, and Secord Street.

The proposal would combine seven existing parcels into one 8.144-acre lot. In order to do so, lots
from the 1910 Longview Park Addition must be vacated. The plat would also correct a Salt Lake
County recording discrepancy on the 200 East right-of-way. The applicant intended to construct a
113,000-square-foot United States Department of Veterans Affairs (“VA”) outpatient clinic.

Mr. Cawley reported that, as conditioned, the one-lot subdivision would comply with CC Zoning
District requirements outlined in § 17.03.050 of the Land Use and Development Ordinance. An
outpatient clinic or medical office is a permitted use in the CC Zone, and the proposal complied
with lot size and large-format commercial building form requirements. The proposed height was
34 feet with a parapet to screen rooftop mechanical equipment. Setbacks would comply as
conditioned.

The proposal complied with parking ratio requirements specified in § 17.06.160. A total of 451
off-street parking spaces was required, and 600 were proposed with 362 garage and 238 surface
spaces. Parking for 24 bicycles was required, and 26 were proposed.

The proposed Subdivision Plat, as conditioned, complied with the following requirements of §
17.10, Subdivision and Platting:

       1.      § 17.10.120 – Requirements for Improvements & Design.

       3.      § 17.10.160 – Drainage, Storm Sewers, and Stormwater Facilities.
               o      Approval of the Subdivision is contingent upon Compliance with the City
                      Engineer’s review for this standard.

       4.      § 17.10.170 – Water Facilities.

                      hydrants and all water and storage supply improvements.

The proposed Subdivision Plat was reviewed. An existing public utility easement near Secord
Street will remain, and a proposed new public utility easement would run along the south, west,
and north sides of the property.

Due to the subject property’s proximity to a residential district, the applicant was required to
provide a traffic study. Traffic was expected to increase by approximately 47% during peak
morning and evening hours. The study determined that several access improvements should be
made, and it was Staff’s recommendation that they be included as Conditions of Approval.

       1.      Construct right-in/right-out driveways and install appropriate stop signs along State
               Street.

       2.      Construct a full-access driveway with appropriate signage along Secord Street, to
               the satisfaction of the Fire Marshal.

       3.      Construct a full-access driveway with appropriate signage along 200 East.

As conditioned, the proposal complied with landscape standards outlined in § 17.06.300. A number
of trees will be removed from the site, and City Code requires that each non-invasive/non-noxious
tree be replaced with at least four other trees with a minimum caliper of four inches. The
applicant’s arborist inventoried 428 trees, of which 401 were invasive or noxious. Based on City
requirements, a total of 108 trees would need to be installed to replace the 27 non-invasive trees
being removed. The applicant proposed installation of 118 trees.

State Code requires good cause to vacate lots, and Staff determined that there was good cause to
vacate Lots 11 through 23 of the Longview Park Addition Subdivision due to the following:

   •   No public street or municipal utility or easement has been vacated or amended.

Public notice was published on the City website and Utah Public Notice website and posted at the

Matt Connolly of Molasky Development spoke on behalf of the applicant, SSLC OPC Venture
LLC, which was selected in 2025 to build a clinic and lease it back to the VA. The Salt Lake City
clinic will be part of the Sergeant First Class Heath Robinson Honoring our Promise to Address
Comprehensive Toxics (“PACT”) Act of 2022, which approved 30 clinics throughout the country
to bring community care to veterans.

Lead architect, Bruce Moore of Perkins Eastman reviewed the Site Plan. The VA South Salt Lake
Outpatient Clinic will have the following features:

   •   Two-stories and 109,795 gross square feet
   •   Approximately 90 total exam and consultation rooms
   •   Services will include:
          o Primary care
          o Dedicated women’s clinic
          o Residency clinic
          o Dedicated tele-health
          o Outpatient laboratory, pharmacy, and prosthetics services
          o Shared medical group and conference spaces to group therapy and classes

Mr. Moore reviewed a circulation plan. The main entrances will be on State Street and Helm
Avenue, with secondary access from 200 East and Secord Street. Most visitors will access the
clinic from State Street. A dedicated ambulance pickup is located on the 200 East side of the
building, and a loading dock will be accessed from Secord Street. Staff parking will be in the rear
lot and parking structure.

Concept renderings were reviewed. The façade will consist of masonry and metal panel
construction with large expanses of curtain wall that are reflective of the region. The main entrance
will include a drop-off area near the women’s clinic.

Lead designer, Michael Stevenson reported that the design placed a high priority on intuit
movement and safe, convenient drop-offs. The parking structure was needed to meet parking count
requirements, and it was important that the structure be visible from the main entrances. There will
be a clear line-of-sight and movement from the entry to the drop-off and parking areas. The
building’s architectural emphasis is on State Street, with a large graphic that clearly identifies the

Chair Carter asked if the property would be tax exempt. Mr. Conolly reported that the privately
developed and owned facility will be leased to the VA, and to his knowledge it was not tax exempt.

Commissioner Slifka asked if 600 parking spaces will be adequate for both patients and visitors.
Mr. Cawley reported that the applicant was providing more parking than required. Based on the
building’s square footage, only 451 spaces were required. Mr. Connolly stated that the facility
would be the 12th VA clinic they had developed across the country, and parking of six spaces per
1,000 square feet was a proven ratio. Recent procurements had reduced the count to five per 1,000,
so 600 spaces will be more than sufficient. In response to a follow-up question, he indicated that
there was little risk of needing overflowing parking, but if so they would need to utilize public
parking or public transportation.

Commissioner Slifka asked for more information on the projected 47% increase in traffic and how
it would impact residents on 200 East. Mr. Moore stated that the majority of traffic should use the
primary entrances on State Street and Helm Avenue. The clinic’s hours of operation will be 7:30
a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Monday through Saturday, so it will be closed on evenings and Sundays and
should not have significant negative impacts on the neighborhood on 200 East. Dedicated staff
parking will be in the Secord Street parking lot south of the loading dock.

structure is one story with an elevated second parking deck.

new facility will primarily serve the primary care and outpatient needs of veterans who previously
went to the hospital for that care.

There were no further comments. The public hearing was closed.

Chair Carter encouraged residents to contact Director of Neighborhoods, Sharen Hauri for more
information on the types of trees the City requires. He asked the applicant to respond to other

Mr. Moore stated that the ambulance area will be for pick-ups and staff entry only. Its primary
purpose is to be able to quickly move a patient to an emergency vehicle if necessary, and it will
not be used for drop-offs. Surgical or invasive procedures will not be offered at the clinic. Hours
of operation are 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. Monday through Saturday with no overnight stays. Mr.
Connolly added that janitorial staff may be onsite after hours, but the building will close at 5:30
p.m.

In response to a question from Chair Carter, Mr. Cawley reported that the alleyway near the stand
of trees was not being vacated, so those residents will still have access to their garages. No
improvements to the alleyway were required by code. In response to a follow-up question from
Commissioner Slifka, he reported that commercial uses are required to buffer along property lines,
and buffers touching residential properties must be 10 feet with an eight-foot-tall fence.

Mr. Graycheck reported that eight canopy trees were proposed along the east property line, and
the privacy fence is along the south side of the parking structure. No fence was proposed along the
alleyway. In response to a question from Commissioner Slifka, he confirmed that residents would
look at the buffer and parking lot.

Commissioner Slifka referred to the public comment about vehicles jumping the curb and asked if
signs or other mitigation measures could be implemented. Mr. Weidenhamer stated that the
concerns were realistic, and the Traffic Calming Committee can address the issue quickly. Staff
and the applicant had also discussed potential improvements in the area, and he did not believe
they would be opposed to adding signs.

Commissioner Slifka asked about the height of the parking structure. Mr. Stevenson confirmed
that the elevated deck is 11 feet from grade or approximately 15 feet with the railing.

Regarding the comment about fuel contamination, Mr. Connolly reported that an environmental
study identified where the RV business had hydraulic lifts, and the soil in the area would need to
be removed. Contamination leeched to the northwest of the building, and there were no issues on
the remainder of the site. A corrective action plan was in review with the State. The spring had
been mapped.

Commissioner Slifka asked if the City had any plans to increase lighting on 200 East.
Mr. Weidenhamer reported that the streets budget was recently increased by $4.3 million to

mouse problem for six to 12 months.

Commissioner Spencer asked about if the City could require that the Siberian Elms remain on the
property. Mr. Graycheck indicated that the February 2012 Utah Invasive Species List stated that
the Siberian Elm is considered a non-native, invasive plant. All trees would be from the South Salt
Lake City recommended water-efficient plant list. Commissioner Spencer stated that Mr. Wellard
is a restoration ecologist, and he indicated that Siberian Elm is not an invasive species. Chair Carter
stated that he has Siberian Elms in his yard that he must cut back every year because they grow
like weeds, and they are an invasive species in North America. Ms. Ungricht reported that Ms.
Hauri reviewed the plan and agreed that, while it is unfortunate to remove so many mature trees,
they are an invasive species and should be replaced. The proposal also met landscaping code.

Commissioner Spencer remarked that it can be difficult to turn left onto 3900 South from 200 East,
and the daycare is located at that intersection.
