Proposed Ordinance 2023-11 to change a street name from “1355 South” to
“1425 South” to identify more correctly a street within the Canyon Terrace
development

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding a proposed street renaming action
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Ogden City Engineering requests a street name change from “1355
South” to “1425 South” which is geographically more correct and identifies the street more with the existing Canyon Terrace
development, instead of the neighborhood street above, 1350 South.

Planning Manager Brierley summarized the memo and offered additional background and points of consideration:

e The Canyon Terrace Subdivision plat was recorded in 1985.

e In March of 2022 Phase 2 was split into Phase 3 and 4.

e A building permit was issued, and construction began in September 2022.

e The proposed street name changes from “1355 South” to “1425 South” will tie in better with the Canyon
Terrace development.

e The street and buildings are currently under construction and there are no residents living on the street.

e The Police and Fire departments do not have a conflict with the proposed street rename.

Mr. Brierley concluded the Planning Commission has reviewed the proposal and forwarded a positive recommendation
to the Council.

Proposed Ordinance 2023-10 amending the Fiscal Year 2022-2023 Budget
in the amount of $3,040,000 in order to fund future park property purchases
and development

Amemo from the City Comptroller regarding a proposed budget amendment came before the Council for consideration.
The memo stated the Council will consider an ordinance increasing the Fiscal Year (FY)2023 City budget by $3,040,000. The
proposed budget amendment transfers funds from General Fund fund balance ($3,040,000) to the General Fund for future park
property purchases ($3,000,000) and for consulting services to create a master plan for the Lynn Elementary property for
development of additional park and recreation space ($40,000).

Comptroller Stout summarized the memo and stated the City has a General Fund unassigned fund balance, in excess of
the minimum required by the State, of approximately $16.4M or approximately 22 percent of estimated annual revenue. The City
is going to appropriate $955,000 from this fund for the purchase of property from the Ogden City School District, generally
known as the former Lynn Elementary site. This property is adjacent to the City’s 4" Street Park. The City would like to complete
a study in order to develop a plan for the best use and configuration for the development of the former Lynn Elementary sitein |
coordination with the existing 4th Street facility. The estimate for this study is $40,000. The City Council has requested an
additional appropriation from the unassigned fund balance as a reserve fund for future land purchases that fit into the City’s park

‘the reserve will be $3,995,000 leaving approximately $12.4M in the general fund unassigned fund balance or approximately 17 :

Council member Blair inquired as to the manner in which the transferred funds would be earmarked. Ms. Stout stated it i
will be placed in a General Fund Land Purchase Account; eventually, after the land purchase of the Lynn Elementary site is ;
completed, she would propose placing the remaining funding in the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) budget so that it can be
carried forward year after year to provide annual funding for land acquisition.
Vice Chair Richey asked if the funding can still be considered as part of the City’s General Fund reserve if it is not spent

Proposed Resolution 2023-7 approving the acquisition of surplus property
from the Ogden School District for 605 North Jackson Avenue; AND
Proposed Resolution 2023-8 approving the acquisition of surplus property
from the Ogden School District for 685 North Jackson Avenue

A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department came before the Council to consider two
resolutions approving the acquisition of surplus property from the Ogden School District (OSD). The memo stated on December
13, 2022, the Ogden City Council passed a resolution declaring the City’s intent to purchase four properties deemed surplus by
Ogden School District. Per State law (§53G-4- 902), once the Council passes the resolution declaring intent to purchase, the City |
has 90 days to deliver to OSD an earnest money offer to purchase the surplus property at the purchase price. The City’s
Administration is proposing the use of Quality Neighborhoods funding for the purchase of two of the properties. The first is a
single-family home located at 685 North Jackson Avenue and the second property is a large undeveloped parcel located at 605
North Jackson Avenue. In both purchases, the guidelines for the City’s Infill Housing program require the Council approve the
final development concepts. The Council has directed that each property be acted upon separately.

Community Development Manager Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
discuss Administration’s presentation regarding the OSD properties. Mr. Smith reviewed the schedule of the City’s consideration
of the properties declared as surplus by the OSD and summarized the Administration’s evaluation of the three properties that
Administration proposes to purchase. He noted that the property at 605 North Jackson Avenue is 7.01 acres in size and has an
appraised value of $1,075,000. Administration’s recommendation is to purchase the property for a new single-family home
development with Quality Neighborhoods as the funding source. He added that 685 North Jackson is 0.5 acres in size and contains
an existing single-family home; the appraised value is $384,000 and Administration recommends purchasing the property and
renovating the home for resale as a single-family home. The funding source for this property is also Quality Neighborhoods.

Council member Blair referenced the single-family home at 685 North Jackson and inquired as to the process
Administration will employ to renovate it. Mr. Smith stated that the City will use a process similar to what has occurred in past
renovation projects; the City will act as the developer and will hire a contractor to renovate the home, which will eventually be
sold to anew owner for owner-occupancy.

Council member White clarified that the acquisition of the Lynn Elementary site is not part of the two actions listed
under this agenda item. Mr. Smith stated that is correct.

Council member Hyer referenced the canal that runs along the 605 North Jackson property and asked if the City has
considered piping it. Mr. Smith stated that is an option, but engineering work has not been performed to determine if piping is
necessary or will be beneficial. Council member Hyer asked if the canal is included in the property or is outside of the property
boundary. Mr. Smith stated he believes it is part of the property.

Chair Choberka noted the reason that the Council asked for these two properties to be separated from one another for the
purpose of taking action in their acquisition is that some Council members felt differently about acquisition of the single-family
home. She noted that she feels that the City’s involvement in purchasing and renovating the home will inflate its cost and she )
wants to avoid doing that.

Proposed Joint Resolution 2023-1 supporting the future enhancement and
development of and dedication to the Union Station and surrounding
properties, the museum artifacts and collections, including the historic
railcars and locomotives
A memo from Council staff came before the Council to consider a joint resolution relating to future enhancement of the
Union Station campus. The memo stated the proposed joint resolution underscores the Council’s and Mayor’s support for the
. future enhancement and development of and dedication to the Union Station and surrounding properties, including the museum
) artifacts and collections, and the historic railcars and locomotives. The joint resolution also identifies specific strategies for
: ensuring that the Union Station ultimately reflects the desires of Ogden residents. This includes creating an ad hoc Union Station
Advisory Committee and a museum planning group.
Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the recitals included in the joint resolution document, including:
e = History of the Station
e Ownership/Management History
e Union Station Foundation Relationship
e Collaboration with Utah Transit Authority
e City’s Purchase of the Property
e Recognizing Union Station’s Significance to Ogden
e Commitment to the Enhancement of Union Station Consistent with the Desires of the Greater Ogden
Community
The commitments identified in the joint resolution include creation of a thirteen-member committee to oversee
development of the Union Station Campus; two of the seats will be occupied by individuals from the Union Station Foundation
Board. A Museum Planning Group will also be created to focus on technical guidelines for artifact storage, maintenance, and
display. There is also a focus on implementing the MAKE Ogden Master Plan at the site while retaining ownership of the property
underlying the Union Station. The joint resolution also calls for adequate funding for ongoing maintenance and operations at the
Union Station and selection of a development partner that respects the historical significance of the facility. The development will
synergize downtown Ogden, increasing tourism, events, and opportunities for local businesses; it will also work to move the
FrontRunner stop to the Union Station.
Council member White stated this is just one more step the Council and Administration can take to communicate to the
public how important the Union Station is. She applauded those who wrote the joint resolution and looks forward to taking this
step. The Council and Administration are committed to preserving the Union Station and making it thrive. Vice Chair Richey .
agreed with and echoed Council member White’s comments. He encouraged residents to stay involved and participate in ongoing
_ meetings aimed at planning the future of the Union Station campus.
y Council member Hyer stated he is also pleased by the joint resolution and its communication of the value the Council and
- Administration place on the Union Station. There is still a great deal of work to be done and many unknowns, but he looks
forward to this project and further enhancing the facility.
Council member Nadolski agreed, but highlighted the angst that has been expressed by the public regarding the
unknowns relating to the future of the Union Station. The history of the building should be the top priority and it is necessary to
find a way to fund the preservation of the museum. He is happy that the Union Station Foundation will have some representation
on the committees that will be assembled to oversee ongoing projects and he hopes the City will listen to their feedback to avoid ;
any situation in the future where the public does not understand the City’s position on the facility.
