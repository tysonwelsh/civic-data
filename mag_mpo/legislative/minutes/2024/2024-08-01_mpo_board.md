Corridor Preservation Request Provo 800/820 North 790 West 800 North [00:17:22] Calvin Clark said the Utah County Corridor Preservation Fund is available for the purchase of property for right of way from willing sellers as opportunities arise. This proposal is for corridor 38 and would be the acquisition of the full residential property. Mayor Dan Olson moved to approve the Provo City request of $430,875 for 790 West 800 North from the Corridor Preservation Fund. Mayor Brent Frost seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. 848 North 900 West [00:20:09] Calvin Clark stated the second request is along corridor 38 as well. This is the purchase of a portion of a residential lot. The closing costs are slightly higher with the complication of separating the lot. As part of the 800/820 North capacity project, 900 West will be a cul-de-sac, and that is why there is so much property needed. Commissioner Jim Evans moved to approve the Provo City request of $169,800 for 848 North 900 West from the Corridor Preservation Fund. Safety Action Plan [00:22:01] Calvin Clark stated this is a project that has been discussed in the last few meetings. He turned the time over to the consultant, Charles Allen. Charles Allen gave the background of the process leading up to today's meeting. He summarized the deliverables from his firm including the project website, which allows a user to view information, recommendations, countermeasures, and overall safety patterns for the different projects. This particular deliverable is something MAG staff will be able to update over time to measure improvements. By the adoption of the plan tonight, it allows all the entities within the MPO to apply for implementation grants as well as apply for additional planning grant money. Mayor Carla Merrill moved to adopt the presented Safety Action Plan. Mayor Denise Andersen seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor. 2025 TIP Adoption [00:37:10] Bob Allen stated this is the adoption of the 2025 TIP plan. The TIP is a list of the regional transportation projects over the next two years. Any major regional project to be built in the area must be in the RTP Phase 1. There is an interactive map that shows the TIP projects on MAG’s website. There were 14 projects that have been programmed for funding during 2027 and 2028. There was discussion related to the Provo Airport score and the amount of money programmed for the project. Bob Allen responded the airport does not fit within the typical selection projects. The TIP process compares a road to a road and trail to a trail. There are no other airport projects to rank this against. The airport is a regional draw that reduces traﬃc to and from the Salt Lake City airport. This is a small portion of TIP funds contributing to a larger funded project. There were questions raised if safety was rated and weighted in this last TIP selection process. Discussion progressed explaining the reason for the score and clarifying the programmed funds are a portion of the total project improvements and additional entities have committed funds to the project as well. Mayor Jim Miller moved that the 2025 TIP, including the 2024 MAG Funded Projects List, be adopted as presented with the addition that MAG staff work with Utah County or other entities to find additional funding for the Provo Airport project, to allow for less money to be paid from the TIP funds. Mayor Denise Andersen seconded the motion, and the motion passed all in favor.
