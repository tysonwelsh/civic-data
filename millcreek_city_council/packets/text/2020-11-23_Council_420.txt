 3.        Proposed           Historic         Preservation             Ordinance;         Blaine         Gehring,         Department             of
 Legislative          Policy

  Blaine       Gehring,        Legislative          Policy     Analyst,          said as with        a lot of what Millcreek                 inherited         from
  Salt Lake County,                 this chapter       in the zoning              ordinance        falls short of what is needed to preserve
  the historic          features      of Millcreek.           Currently          the chapter's          purpose       is to "preserve          sites with
  special       historical,         architectural,       or aesthetic           value which          are unique         and irreplaceable             assets."
  It only      identifies         four structures        of historic        value         and discusses           a required        conditional       use
  pen'nit      process.       What       it fails to do is identi'fy              other historic          structures        and districts       and outline           a
  process       for identi'fying          and how to preserve                   them. Most          historic       preservation         ordinances           create
  a Historic          Preservation         Commission.            The current             ordinance        has no provision             for that.
  Researching             other cities'      historic        preservation           ordinances          revealed       common          elements       for an
  ordinance:           creation       of a historic      preservation             commission,             creation     of a list of historic              sites and
  neighborhoods,              designation          of those historic              sites and districts,            standards       for rehabilitation            and
  design       guidelines           for preserving        historic       structures         and sites. The proposed                  Historic
  Preservation            Ordinance         establishes         a Historic         Preservation           Commission             and defines        its
 membership,              powers,        and duties.         The coinmission                would       have 7 members,              l from       each council
  district      and 3 at large as chosen by the Mayor                              with     advice       and consent           of the City      Council.
  Each member               shall be a bona fide resident                   of Millcreek            and have professional                  background           and
 knowledge              related     to historic      preservation           (history,        architectural          history,     archaeology,             historic
  architecture,           planning).


 The ordinance              describes        the duties        and powers           of the cornrnission               to include        issuing     certificates
 of appropriateness,                 establish       a repository         for important            documents,          artifacts,      and items of
 historical           significance.       The Commission                 will     provide       a written         history      of Millcreek,        historical
 events to promote                  education,       designate          entries     to the Utah          State Register           of Historic       and
 Cultural        sites and to the National                   Register      of Historic          Places;      establishes         a Historic        Sites and
 Districts       list and the criteria            for including            a structure         or district        on the list and a designation
 procedure;            and designate         specific        historic      sites and districts.


 In addition           to the four homes             in the current             ordinance,       the Evergreen              Avenue       Historic         District
 was listed           on the National         Register         of Historic          places in 2007 but not recognized                          in the current
 ordinance            along with        two homes         on the Utah             Register:        Susanna         Bradford       mansion         (Oakwood),
 and the Nathaniel                Baldwin        home.       Four homes            listed     in current         ordinance        include,      Gardner
 Home,         Edward         Pugh Home,            Butler     Home,        and the Mill            Creek Farmhouse.                There      are other
 historic       sites and homes             in Millcreek         not on any register.                These include              900 E. & Scott
 Avenue,         2960 S. Cascade Way,                    1555     E. Millcreek              Way,     3426 S. Oakwood                 Street, Old Fari'n,
 3182 E. Millcreek                  Canyon       Road,       East Millcreek           School        (Reid        School),      Libby       Edwards         School
 (Utah       School       for Deaf and the Blind),                  Iceberg        Drive      In, 1810 E. Orchard                 Drive,     3325 S.
 Oakwood              Street, Baldwin         Radio       Factory,        Wasatch           Lawn        Entry,     and 1524        E. Millcreek            Way.


 The ordinance              would       establish      a historic        landmark           register.     This provides            further     recognition
 of significant           historic      resources        and provides             protection        for historic       resources.          The register
 shall be prepared                and maintained          by the Historic             Preservation               Coinmission.          It shall describe
 the contents,           criteria     for designation           on the register             and designation            procedure           and results         of
 the designation              such as eligible          grants      and tax credits            for rehabilitating              an owner's         properties.
 Also,       retain     the existing       conditional          use process          for any modifications                   to a historic        site or
 structure.

 The ordinance             would       establish       standards         for rehabilitation              and design         guidelines       to be used by
 the Historic           Preservation         Com+nission            in advising           the Planning            Coinmission          and City       Council
Mfflcreek          City     Council          Meeting          Minutes                                            23 November              2020          Page 4 of 10



  of the appropriate                 treatment           of historic      properties.            It would        establish       general         design     guidelines
  such as avoiding                demolition             of historic       buildings,         treatment           of vacant         historic      buildings,           rehab
  work      to preserve             existing       historic       features,      and avoiding              moving       buildings           whenever             possible
  to avoid         creating         artificial      groupings           of historic       buildings.           Establishes           criteria     for additions              and
  new construction                  within       a historic       district.     Retains          the list of uses for historical                    sites and
  buildings.         Establishes             an appeal process                for any person             adversely         affected        by decision            of the
  Historic         Preservation            Commission              of Zoning           Administrator.             The ordinance                 was patterned            after
  other ordinances              recognized               as good examples                by David           Amott     with       Preservation             Utah
  (formerly          Utah Heritage               Foundation).            Mr. Amott          reviewed             the proposed           ordinance          and made
  recommendations                    on the ordinance               in addition          to providing            photos      of historic          sites and
  buildings         in Millcreek.
