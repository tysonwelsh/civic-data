Item 1: Ordinance Amending the Zone Map Classification of Real Property at 1534 South
1100 West (PLRZ20230250) 00:06:06

Aaron Ardmore, Planning Supervisor, presented the proposed plans to amend the zone map
classification for the property located at 1534 South 1100 West.

Councilor Becky Bogdin expressed appreciation for the plan but noted concerns from her
constituents regarding sewer infrastructure. Mr. Ardmore clarified that the current sewer line
would be routed along Lakeview Parkway and assured that no plat would be recorded until the
sewer system was established.

Gordon Haight, Public Works Director, explained that property owners could negotiate
individually with the developer. If a property owner does not agree, the project cannot proceed,
and the city or property owners will have to manage the situation independently. He also confirmed
that eminent domain would only occur with Council approval, emphasizing that neither the city
nor property owners are obligated to act without agreement.

Councilor Bogdin inquired further to confirm the absence of eminent domain. Mr. Haight
reiterated that eminent domain could not proceed without explicit Council approval and addressed
her concerns directly.

Chair Katrice MacKay asked whether sewers are typically constructed through roadways. Mr.

noting that developers closely monitor Council decisions and adapt their plans accordingly to meet
the Council’s priorities.


 Item 2: Discussion Regarding Cat Animal Control and a Trap, Neuter, Release (TNR) Policy
(24-098) 00:18:33

Melia Dayley, Policy Analyst, presented the discussion regarding a Trap, Neuter, Release (TNR)
policy for cat population management. She acknowledged the efforts of David Pyle, an intern who
contributed significantly to the presentation but was unavailable due to class commitments. Ms.
Dayley provided an overview of prior meetings on this topic and introduced two options:

    1. TNR Program Proposal: A three-year pilot program to be evaluated before further
       decisions are made.
    2. Status Quo Option: Amending Provo City Code 8.02.200 as recommended by Police
       Animal Control.

Ms. Dayley explained that while the Council could amend the code directly, implementing the
pilot program would require passing a resolution recommending administrative implementation.

Presentation by Best Friends Animal Society

Scott Giacoppo, Director of National Shelter Support for Best Friends Animal Society, shared
his background in animal control and his evolving perspective on the TNR program. Initially

opposed, he came to support the program after investigating its effectiveness nationwide. He
emphasized TNR’s potential to address community concerns while reducing nuisance behaviors
like spraying and howling. He cited examples of Utah cities implementing TNR and outlined
strategies, such as deterrent equipment, to prevent property damage.
Holly Sizemore, Chief Mission Officer for Best Friends, elaborated on the benefits of the TNR
program, emphasizing its potential to reduce complaints and improve community perceptions. She
shared experiences from other cities and underscored the importance of consistent results to
convince stakeholders.

Opposition from Local Animal Control

Captain Brian Wilken presented arguments against the TNR program, citing cats’ invasive
nature, impact on wildlife, and limited long-term success. He raised concerns about logistical
issues, such as identifying neutered cats and the influence of external advocacy groups. He also
noted that rabies vaccinations provided under TNR are not permanent.

Jason Swenson, Provo Animal Control Officer with 17 years of experience, highlighted
operational challenges, including community resistance to feed stations and stress among officers
managing cat-related issues. He questioned the practicality of enforcing feed station policies and
the effectiveness of the program in the Provo context.

Council Discussion
    Chair Katrice MacKay expressed openness to trying the program, citing the low financial
      risk and potential community benefits. She suggested exploring a hybrid model, combining
      TNR with euthanasia for persistent nuisances.
    Councilor Gary Garrett questioned the data supporting TNR and emphasized the
      importance of detailed reports on its effectiveness before full implementation.
    Vice-Chair Rachel Whipple raised questions about comparative costs between the TNR
      program and current euthanasia practices, noting potential cost offsets.
    Councilor George Handley supported exploring alternatives, observing that the status quo
      has not proven effective.
    Councilor Becky Bogdin expressed reluctance to proceed, citing strong opposition from
      police officers, the animal shelter, and administrative uncertainty.


Motion
Councilor Handley proposed a motion to recommend that the administration explore a
Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) for the pilot program. Councilor Travis Hoban seconded.

Vote: 5-2 Garret and Bogdin opposed

Item 3: Discussion Regarding the Addition of Two Full-Time Airport Operations Specialists
(24-100) 01:29:26

Brian Torgersen, Public Works Division Director - Airport, presented the need for two additional
full-time Airport Operations Specialists.

Mr. Torgersen provided an update on the airport’s growth, noting the addition of new destinations
and significant increases in passenger enplanements. He highlighted a graph illustrating that
enplanements had doubled projections following the opening of the new terminal. From 2022 to
2023, Provo Airport was recognized as the fastest-growing airport in the nation. He also noted that
a two-week closure for construction in September 2023 accounted for an outlier in the growth data,
as pointed out by Mayor Michele Kaufusi.

Mr. Torgersen explained the importance of the Aircraft Rescue and Firefighting (ARFF) Index,
which determines staffing and equipment requirements for fire safety. He stated that the airport is
approaching the threshold for Index C, necessitating additional staffing. He presented the current
schedule, emphasizing the need to maintain at least three staff members onsite at all times. With
14 current full-time employees (FTEs), the airport is requesting two additional FTEs to address
the increasing operational demands.

Chair Katrice MacKay clarified that the funding for the additional FTEs would be covered by
airport revenue.

Vice-Chair Rachel Whipple observed discrepancies in the schedule numbers, suggesting that four
additional FTEs would be required to meet the airport's needs. Mr. Torgersen acknowledged the
concern but emphasized caution to avoid over-expanding too quickly. He reiterated the need to
balance staffing prudently with anticipated revenue.

Vice-Chair Whipple expressed concern about potential staff burnout and suggested that the airport
might need to request more FTEs in the future. Mr. Torgersen agreed, noting that the airport is
emerging from a slow season and is being conservative in its projections but acknowledged the
possibility of returning with additional staffing requests.

Councilor Becky Bogdin appreciated Mr. Torgersen’s prudence and confirmed that the airport’s
revenue would cover the cost of the additional FTEs. Mr. Torgersen affirmed this.

Item 4: Presentation Regarding Municipal Bonding (24-101) 01:51:38

Brian Baker, Municipal Advisor for Zions Bank Public Finance, presented on municipal bonding.
He provided an overview of financing options, including cash payments, loans, grants, and bonds.
He emphasized that while Utah tends to be fiscally conservative and bond-averse, bonds can often
be the most efficient option for funding large projects.

Chair Katrice MacKay inquired about the relationship between Intermountain Healthcare (IHC)
and county-issued bonds, which Mr. Baker confirmed. She also asked about the percentage of

revenue allocated for bond repayment. Public Works Business Manager Jimmy McKnight
responded that 9% of annual revenue would be allocated to bond payments.

Vice-Chair Rachel Whipple asked about the percentage savings initially anticipated. Mr.
McKnight explained that while a 2% savings was currently planned, they had previously projected
up to 18% savings; however, achieving this would have required rate increases.

Councilor Craig Christensen sought clarification on total obligations and inquired about alternative
approaches, such as combining “pay-as-you-go” strategies with bonding. Administrative Services
Director John Borget noted the challenges of construction inflation, explaining that delaying
projects can lead to significantly higher costs.

Councilor George Handley asked whether cities commonly use general fund reserves to offset
bond amounts, reducing the need for larger loans. Mr. Baker confirmed that this approach is
sometimes used but cautioned that it can affect credit ratings.

Councilor Becky Bogdin asked whether the city would need to raise rates in two years when bond
payments begin. Mr. McKnight shared that while raising rates is a possibility, other options could
also be considered to manage the financial impact.

The presentation concluded with a discussion of Provo’s bond ratings and examples of past bond-
funded projects. Vice-Chair Whipple expressed appreciation for the detailed presentation, and Mr.
McKnight noted that the resolution under consideration was focused on funding a portion of the
city’s expenses through bonding.
