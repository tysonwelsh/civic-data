     1.3. Presentation of a proposed Rosecrest Master Development Agreement 4th Amendment –
          Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Maloy presented the fourth amended Rosecrest Master Development Agreement proposal. He
reviewed the original agreement and the previous amendments. Key reason for this amendment was
realignment of proposed collector roads, Juniper Crest and Mountain View Corridor (MVC). It also added 169
acres of property to the MDA and 219 dwelling units. The amendment would also reduce the multi-family
dwelling units by 22.9 acres and 373 units and increase the quantity and location of medium density
developments. The amendment would increase open space by 14 acres. Future annexations would be included
at the option of the master developer. City Planner Maloy explained a coordinated development approach
would be best for contiguous development and the addition of utilities. He presented a side-by-side comparison
with the 2008 General Plan and identified changes and park locations. An amendment to the CDA was needed
to support the rezone. Open space, trails, right of way sections and park concepts were provided. He reviewed
the amendment process, procedural guidelines, and legal limitations. Commissioners requested clarification for
the number of units allowed per the general plan, compared to the development agreement, and asked which
trumped the other. City Planner Maloy responded ideally the developer and City to work together to determine
the number of units. Commissioners agreed with placing higher density near Mountain View Corridor (MVC).
Chair Berbert offered the concern parks and recreation were lacking in the area west of MVC and more athletic
field space was needed. Commissioners Berbert and Palmer would prefer reducing density and adding more
soccer and baseball fields. City Attorney Andrizzi recommended discussing changes with the applicant.
Developer Mike Bradshaw offered further background on the area. He stated the area was very steep,
topographically challenged, and a difficult area to build. They chose the largest spot available for the middle
park. Adding another large, flat space would be cost prohibitive. Their intention was to focus on trails and
connectivity with the proposed Laguna and Malibu subdivisions in the steeper areas. They have tried to
incorporate more single-family homes and more product type variety. He stated they proposed 650 units lower
than they could have requested. Technical and design guidelines were utilized to ensure compliance with the
general plan. Commissioner Ferguson asked if the homes would be part of a Homeowners Association (HOA).
Developer Bradshaw responded the area would be a combination. Commissioner Ferguson identified small
lots with less than 3,200 feet and small setbacks and requested their type. Developer Bradshaw responded that
area would be detached single family homes with small setbacks. Commissioner Jacobson asked what type of
development was planned at the southeastern end. Developer Bradshaw responded the area was mixed use
with 70% residential and 30% commercial and would not contain mixed-use units. Developer Bradshaw added
the interchange was shifted to the south by UDOT, which moved the commercial up a hill with sixty-foot
grade elevation, which prevented connectivity with Juniper Crest Road. Commissioners wanted quality
commercial areas that made sense.

         Applicant: Seth Vance, Focus Engineering, on behalf of Herriman City
         Address: 6822 W Herriman Highway
         Zone: R-1-15 (Residential)
April 1, 2021–Planning Commission Minutes                                            Page 12 of 13

Planning Manager Clint Spencer reviewed the conditional use request for Herriman Historic Park, located at
6822 W Herriman Highway. The applicant was Herriman City, in cooperation with Seth Vance at Focus
Engineering. Proposed amenities included a pavilion, bike rack, restroom, amphitheater, historic buildings,
wagon feature, an amphitheater, a garden demonstration area, and more. The Hidden Oaks MDA specified this
area for parks and recreation. The area directly north was planned residential and a future church site, which
were compatible uses. The Engineering study recommended forty stalls in the parking lot off Herriman Highway.
This application was received before the water efficiency standards were adopted in March 2021. However, the
City was working with Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District (JVWCD) to make the park as water efficient
as possible. The park tree requirement was 310 trees; however, one tree may be replaced with five shrubs.
Architect Heidi Shegrud stated the Kidd, Eastman and Dansie cabins would all be moved to the park. All of
them were in a state of disrepair and would require lots of restoration for people to visit inside. After historical
designation, grant funding could be requested to restore them so people could go in them. Commissioner
Heather Garcia expressed worry that children may fall off the fence bordering the drop-off. Landscape Architect
Shegrud said they were planning a concrete three rail fence for more structure and stability at the drop-off, with
a two-rail fence further west where the landscape changed. The fence would look wooden to fit in with the
historic feel of the park. There will be centrally located restrooms and a plaza with a historical monument. A
play structure was not planned but pocket parks were nearby. The Commissioners liked the design and park
concept. They had no concerns regarding the number of trees. Landscape Architect Shegrud stated she had put
in as many trees as possible.

Recommendation:
1.   The applicant be granted a waiver of strict compliance for the total number of trees to be planted on
     the site from 310 to 105 (138 with shrub substitution provision) as proposed.
