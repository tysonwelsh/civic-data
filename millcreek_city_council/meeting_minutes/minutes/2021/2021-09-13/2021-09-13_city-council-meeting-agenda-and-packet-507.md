  Mayor       Silvestrini       said the Council                started    a first reading         at their last meeting               but there was not
  enough       time,    so it was moved              to another           meeting.       Francis       Lilly     said there were three types of
  ADUs       that the ordinance             regulated;           internal,     attached,       and detached.             An internal            ADU,
  permitted       by state law, would                be a mother-in-law                apartment          which        fits within         the footprint        of
  the home       and has owner-occupancy                         in the primary            dwelling.       The minimum               lot size for internal
  ADU        would      be 6,000 square feet and having                         an internal        ADU         would     require       1 additional          off-
  street parking         stall. A new attached                  ADU        must be on a lot at least 6,000                      square feet and must
  meet all Residential             Compatibility                Overlay       Zone (RCOZ)              and setback            requirements,          just like
  any other home              addition.      There must be owner-occupancy                             in the primary             dwelling        and 1
  parking      stall per bedroom             plus the required                off-street     parking           for the primary         residence.
  Mayor       Silvestrini       asked if the Planning                   Commission           was okay with              the minimum              lot size
  because      some of the community                      councils         were not. Lilly          said yes. He said detached                      ADUs
  must be eight feet from                 side and rear property                 lines     and six feet from             the primary            residence,        be
  no more than 10% taller                   than the primary               residence       up to 24 feet tall,            and have 1 parking                 stall
  per bedroom           plus required         off-street          parking      for primary         residence.           There      cannot       be any
  trespassing        light     onto and no visible                stairways       or entries       visible       from    adjoining          properties.
  Windows         within       15 feet of a neighbor's                  property      line must be translucent                    or not visible           from
  the neighbors'           property.       ADUs         are prohibited           on slopes of 30% or greater.                       These different
  types of ADU           applications          would       be approved             by staff.


  Lilly     said another        type of ADU             would       be the conversion              of an existing             structure.        If the existing
  ADU       meets all the requirements                    in code, staff approves                it, if it does not comply                   with      code then
  it can be legalized            through      a special          exception       by the Planning                Commission            without        special
  height     or setback         requirements.           Lilly     said it would          be harder        to convert           a garage       or accessory
  building      into a dwelling            than it would            be to convert          an old accessory              dwelling          that was never
  legal in the first place.              If the building           meets all requirements                  for height,         setbacks,        lot coverage,
  design      standards,        and lot size, then staff approves                      it as though            it were a detached               ADU.       If the

 Lilly        said how the questions              were answered            would      determine       how      easy it would       be to convert
  an existing        accessory        building       into an ADU.          Planning        staff recommended             that if the City
 wanted          to permit     detached       ADUs,        there needed to be an option                  in place to convert            existing
 structures.         State law does not require                 cities to create a process             for detached         ADUs,       only      for
 internal        ADUs        on lots greater         than 6,000 square feet provided                   that off-street        parking      is
 available.         Staff recommended                an option       to the Planning           Cornrnission       where     accessory           dwelling
 units would             have the same setbacks               as accessory          structures.      Given     the additional        design
 standards         for ADUs,         the detrimental           impacts      of an accessory           dwelling     could be resolved               to
 make it similar             in impact      to an accessory            structure.      In order to promote           equity     between          the
 different        types of applicants,             staff expressed         a desire to apply          the same standards             to a new
 detached          ADU       that the City        would      apply     to a conversion           of an existing      structure.


 Lilly        explained      that the Plaru'iing          Cornrnission's           rationale      regarding      detached      ADU       setbacks
 were that accessory                structures       not intended         for human        occupancy          are fundamentally           different
 than accessory             dwellings.       The time        spent in an art studio             in an accessory       structure         is typically
 shorter        than the time spent sleeping,                  bathing,     and cooking           in an accessory        dwelling        unit.
 Conversions              of such structures          should     consider      not just the design             of the structure       itself,     but the
 fact that human             occupancy        is inherently          more impactful            than a hobbyist       in a garage. The
 existing        R-1 zone setbacks            were designed             to ensure an appropriate               space between         dwellings,
 and setbacks             as narrow       as 30 inches        for an intervening           dwelling       can change        the character          of    an

 established         single-family          neighborhood.
