   2.1    Discussion of proposed amendments to Title 10 of City Code for home occupation
          regulations (City File No. Z2021-121) - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy offered an overview of the proposed amendments for home occupation
regulations, specifically in regard to daycares run out of someone’s home. He explained the Planning
Commission had drafted a list of amendments for the Council to review, and read through the list of
proposed changes.

                      Councilmember Jared Henderson was excused at 5:39 p.m.

City Planner Maloy stated the amendments considered addressed the following regulations:

   ● Amend the number of additional employees that may be allowed for a home occupation
   ● Clarify the amount of parking needed for additional employees
   ● Establish the maximum number of customers allowed at a given time
   ● Establish the maximum number of children or students allowed in both daycares and group
     instruction
   ● Extend the hours of home daycares
   ● Clarify the definition of group instruction
   ● Allow storage of home occupation materials in accessory structures

City Planner Maloy explained staff had reviewed the daycares which operated in the City, and felt
overall, ensuring there was an appropriate ratio of adult caretakers would increase the safety of the
daycares. Currently, Herriman did not allow for additional employees from outside the home to work
for a home occupation business. He compared this to other Cities, and noted many cities allowed for
one or two employees who did not live in the home to work for a home occupation business. He asked
the Council for direction if they wanted to allow for additional employees to work at the home
daycares, and if so, how many employees should be allowed. He also asked the Council if the size of
the home or square acreage of the property be taken into account when determining the suitability of
a home for a daycare. He noted a rule which mandated only 20-percent of the home could be used for
April 27, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                                  Page 3 of 11
a home occupation business, and asked the Council if they wanted to increase the limit.
Councilmember Ohrn asked who would be responsible for the enforcement of the regulation, and City
Planner Maloy acknowledged this would be nearly impossible to enforce. He said it could be clarified
in the ordinance, if the Council decided to increase the limit.

City Planner Maloy said generally, the City did request the daycares report what their pick-up and
drop-off schedules were, and said right now it was not a requirement, but could add it to the ordinance
as a conditional use.

Councilmember Ohrn felt one additional employee would be sufficient, and felt more than one extra
employee would make it a commercial business. She felt one more employee did increase the safety
for the children, but felt more than one employee would cause a disturbance to the neighbors.
Councilmember Shields perceived daycares differently than other businesses in town, and felt the
existence of daycares allowed for other businesses in Herriman to function. He was in favor of
allowing two additional employees in the case of daycare only, although he acknowledged parking
could pose an issue. He thought the ordinance should offer a distinction between daycares and other
home occupied businesses. He commented if the maximum number of children allowed in a daycare
was twelve, it seemed to make sense to allow for more employees. Mayor Palmer was also in favor of
two additional employees for daycares. Councilmember Ohrn reiterated she felt it would impact the
neighbors of the daycare negatively if more employees were allowed, and further commented this
would have to be monitored as complaints were received once the change to the Code went into effect.

Councilmember Shields thought the constraining factor on employees should be based on if the
daycare could provide designated parking for the employees. He thought as long as the daycare could
provide parking, additional employees should be allowed, but if there was only street parking, the
number of employees should be limited in order to mitigate any potential burden on neighbors.

   2.2     Discussion of the draft General Plan update - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Maloy discussed the draft of the General Plan, and looked for direction moving forward.
He said one of the main topics he wanted to go over that evening were agricultural land uses, and
identifying where the City wanted to have agricultural zones in the future. He observed the need to
determine where developers should be allowed to put retail versus wholesale spaces.

Ms. Susie Pethoram presented the updated General Plan which included the Council district
boundaries, as well as the zoning map and current boundaries. She noted currently, there was no
agricultural future land use category, and said one of the suggestions had been to create an agricultural
overlay zone, and determine what neighborhoods in the community might be suitable for the overlay
zone. She said they had received positive feedback to this idea at their outreach events, as many
people with smaller lots wanted to have livestock. She commented a retail and office employment
center had come up in conversation, and explained there was a matrix which identified several areas
throughout the City which might be a good fit for that space.

Councilmember Ohrn thought the current designations for land use seemed messy and arbitrary, and
indicated several areas on the map which were zoned in a manner that did not make sense. Consultant
April 27, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                                   Page 4 of 11
Pethoram explained the desire to allow flexibility with retail spaces, which was the reason some of the
retail and flex use spaces were zoned for the specific use. She elaborated on the specificity of the
zoning of the retail spaces, so there was clarity about what kind of businesses the City wanted to
attract. Councilmember Ohrn replied she thought having a lot of small, specific zones was too
disorganized, and did not want applicants to have to request an amendment if they wanted to put a
new type of business in a certain zone. Councilmember Ohrn also stated this was intended to be a
plan for the future, and did not have to depict the way that businesses were laid out in the City. She
pointed out it would be difficult if a business ever needed to move locations within the City, and if they
needed to move into a zone which was currently zoned as residential, they would have to request a
General Plan amendment, which was a lengthy process.

Councilmember Shields observed the need to delineate between residential zones and agricultural
residential zones, and did not want to create issues with non-compliance, or make the plan so specific
different sides of a road were in different zones. He agreed with Councilmember Ohrn this plan was
indicative of their vision for the City, and highlighted the need to balance the wishes of current
landowners in the City with the vision of the Council. Councilmember Ohrn agreed and noted she did
not want to limit any agricultural property owners in the City. She reiterated the zoning map for the
City seemed to be chopped up, and restated landowners would have to request a General Plan
amendment, which was a long and expensive process to go through.

Councilmember Shields thought agricultural rights should be preserved in areas of the City which had
large swaths of undeveloped, natural land. He specifically mentioned the area north of Butterfield
Park, and thought this would preserve Herriman’s cultural and natural history. City Planner Maloy
agreed the City should maintain agricultural rights in some sections of the City, and identified areas
which could be designated as agricultural. Councilmember Shields referenced his district, and thought
it would make sense to designate the area as residential as it primarily contained homes. He added
while most of the area was neighborhood residential, they might want to carve out a couple parcels in
the foothills to be open space.

Councilmember Shields also commented he thought 140000 South and Mountain View Corridor should
be designated as retail, and the Council agreed. He added designating it as commercial would not
change the rights as listed in the MDA. City Planner Maloy confirmed this was correct.
Councilmember Shields also indicated a small area was currently zoned as commercial to the south of
Butterfield Park, and Consultant Pethoram clarified the structures in the zone could be for things such
as stables, which would support the amenities offered at the park. Councilmember Shields agreed with
Councilmember Ohrn’s earlier point the General Plan should not have so many little carve-outs.
Councilmember Shields continued to point out areas on the map he thought should be preserved as an
agricultural zone.    The Council briefly noted some other areas on the map, and debated if the zone
should be changed.

        8.1 Discussion and consideration of a request by Palmer Helsten to amend Title 10 of City
            Code to permit flag lot development on a minimum of two-acres on minor collector
            streets in Agricultural Zones. (City File No. Z2022-026) - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Maloy explained this was a minor amendment to their current flag lot regulations. He
said there were large properties which were difficult to develop in any fashion other than the ability
to do a flag lot, and explained the applicant’s property was on a minor collector street, which restricted
the ability to develop. He said if the amendment was approved, then the property would be eligible for
a flag lot. He explained staff had done a study to determine how many other properties in the City
would be impacted by the change, and found only two dozen other properties met the minimum lot
size and was located on a minor collector. He said the Planning Commission had reviewed the item
and recommended approval, and suggested to keep the two acre minimum for flag lots. He said this
would promote a low density housing pattern, which was something the City desired. He added flag
lots were always entered and exited in a forward fashion, so there would be no threat to public safety.

Councilmember Ohrn expressed her support, and recommended to include the A-.25 zone. She did
not think it would cause any kind of an issue, and asked if language could be put in the Code to state if
the flag lot was in an A-25 zone, it could not be divided, in order to keep larger lot sizes as intended.
She did not think lot sizes should be less than a half-acre, but she thought people should be allowed to
do additional things with their acreage. She also pointed out one of the lots was City-owned, so there
were actually only 23 lots which would be impacted by the change. She agreed with the Planning
Commission’s recommendation, and her only additional suggestion was to include language which
would not allow anyone to divide their property into anything smaller than a half-acre.

Councilmember Shields discussed flag lots had become a frequent discussion, and commented there
were many reasons that made sense, but also many reasons to not allow them. He did not understand
where the public safety risk came from with flag lots, and thought it seemed out of harmony with good
neighborhood planning to restrict large properties to only two parcels. Councilmember Ohrn replied
some of the safety risk had to do with access, and said in some cases, people had to back out of the
driveway onto the road, if the driveway was too narrow for a car to turn around. Councilmember
Shields reiterated his disagreement with the idea of limiting the division of larger lot sizes, and pointed
out it did not make sense if someone accumulated adjacent lots, then they would be allowed to
subdivide, but if someone had multiple acres to begin with, they would not be permitted to subdivide
within the confines of the current Code. He thought the current proposed modification was fine, but
thought there was a larger discussion to be had about flag lots. He thought it should wait for requests
and applications to come in, rather than trying to solve problems ahead of time. Councilmember Ohrn
agreed things could be addressed on a case by case basis.

       8.3 Discussion and consideration of the creation of the Diversity Inclusion Advisory Board
           - Lorin Palmer, Mayor & Tami Moody, Assistant City Manager
Assistant City Manager Tami Moody gave a brief history of the development of the Diversity Inclusion
Advisory Board, which had been suggested by Mayor Palmer. She explained if the Council approved
the creation of the Board, a set of bylaws and framework of the Board would have to be drafted, and
observed the need to amend Title II to recognize the Board as one of the Herriman City Boards and
Commissions. She reported Mayor Palmer had been actively engaged in the creation of the Board, and
said he had spoken with other communities which had created diversity committees or boards in order
to gather information on what format they used, and what had worked well for them.
April 27, 2022 – City Council Minutes                                                 Page 9 of 11
Mayor Palmer explained his desire to follow Draper City’s plan, which had been successful. He stated
it would initially be a multicultural event during Herriman Towne days, with possibly a few other
smaller events. He clarified this was not advocacy or police oversight, just multicultural events which
would raise awareness of the different ethnic groups which lived in the City and get involved. He
further clarified they were not targeting any specific group of people. Assistant City Manager Moody
asked the Council if they wanted to approve or deny the creation of the Diversity Inclusion Advisory
Board.

Councilmember Shields commented in the last Council Meeting in which this topic had come up, there
had been a dogmatic discussion regarding definitions which had gotten sidetracked from the main
intent of the conversation. He thought the Diversity Inclusion Advisory Board would be a wonderful
addition to Herriman City, and he was excited about the intent of the board and the benefits it would
bring to the community.

Councilmember Ohrn agreed the previous conversation might have gotten pedantic. She said she
wanted to ensure no one felt excluded, and was wary of the government taking over a social issue.
She said they would need to hand this over to the community, and make sure the City was not always
the entity spearheading events. Mayor Palmer agreed this was the intent of the Board, and said the
City would only be a facilitator of the events, and the goal was for members of the community to take
action.

Councilmember Hodges thanked Assistant City Manager Moody and Mayor Palmer for their dedication
to get the committee to come to fruition. He commented the City was very diverse, and he looked
forward to celebrating. He thought Draper’s model would be a good one to follow.

Mayor Palmer expressed his appreciation for the Council’s support, and for Assistant City Manager
Moody’s work in creating the committee.

Councilmember Shields moved to approve Ordinance No. 2022-19 authorizing an amendment to Title
2 of the Herriman City Code creating the Diversity Inclusion Advisory Board. Councilmember Ohrn
seconded the motion.

   8.4      Approval of a Memorandum of Understanding with Edge Homes to extend the mutual
            development of the Mt. Ridge 5-acre park - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi explained there was a provision in the Mountain Ridge Development
Ridge MDA which required the City and the master developer to work together on the creation of a
five acre park within the development. He said the mutual use provision was for 18 months after the
signing of the MDA, and the period had actually expired in March of that year. He reported he had
been in contact with the developer, who wanted to extend the period by twelve months. He clarified
this would be an extension from the date of expiration. He said if the Council chose not to agree to

long as it remained open space.

Councilmember Ohrn expressed her support to extend the agreement, and said they needed to get this
item back on their agenda, and make sure there was follow up.

Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas clarified the developer would be reimbursed for the park by
the City, and it would not be developer funded. Mayor Palmer thanked her for the comment.
