2.       A discussion regarding the Legacy CIP Fund. (21-021) (0:30:00)

Wayne Parker, CAO, shared background information regarding the City’s use of CARES Act
funding and how budget savings will be programed in the future. One eligible use of CARES Act
funding was for public safety expenses, including payroll, which resulted in a remaining balance
in the city budget. They have proposed putting the budget surplus into a Legacy CIP fund which
would be used to fund important needs in the city. Mr. Parker outlined some of those needs,
including the following:
     Replacement and possible relocation of Fire Station 1
     Interim expansion of Airport Terminal to accommodate more space at existing terminal
     West Side Grocery Store (funds for data analysis, incentives, or public improvements)
     Canyon Road Park
     Set aside for other future projects (about $2 million)

Mr. Parker responded to questions from Councilors. Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked how
these projects had been identified or prioritized versus other needs in the City. Mr. Parker
explained that most of these items were existing unfunded needs in the CIP primarily from the
general fund. Other items, like the airport expansion, were based on a present opportunity. There
was some discussion of whether the Edgemont area, with two private neighborhood parks, really
needed a new park versus other areas of the city which seemed to have a more obvious dearth of
park space. Mr. Parker explained that they had examined legacy projects which would have a
far-reaching benefit. This was also a means of following through on a commitment made to the
neighborhood at the time that the Timp-Kiwanis Bounous park was sold to the Provo School
District, as well as a decision based on level of service calculations for parks facilities. Councilor
David Harding requested data on the level of service calculations for parks and recreation
facilities. He suggested a heat map or map with a color scale would be helpful to illustrate.
Councilor George Handley was still unclear on some of the details with the grocery store
incentive funds. With regards to the data analysis portion, Mr. Parker explained that this was
meant to fill in gaps in the kinds of data grocers obtain on their own, in order to make a
compelling case that they should invest in a location in Provo. Presentation only.

network utilizes categories in a hierarchy of road types: major arterial, minor arterial, collector,
and local. He noted that collector roads were critical in planning; if not designed in a system,

6.       A discussion on Provo City Code section 9.80 parking permit program. (21-011)
         (2:56:32)

John Magness, Policy Analyst, presented. He gave an overview of how the parking permit
program process works, as outlined in Provo City Code, Chapter 9.80. He reviewed the
application process and highlighted the requirements for a resident’s application, including the
petition/signature component. Once an application is received, if there is sufficient interest in the
proposal, the Council can request that a parking study is completed by the City’s parking
coordinator. Once the report is received, the Council holds a public hearing and can either adopt
or reject a resolution of intent. Following the resolution of intent, residents impacted by the
proposal are notified of the proposal. A formal ordinance is required to create the actual permit
parking area. Mr. Magness displayed a flowchart illustrating the sequence of steps.

Councilor Shannon Ellsworth asked whether the parking permit program was contemplated just
for areas where commuters utilized street parking to the exclusion of resident parking. Mr.
Magness said that was one purpose, but there could be other reasons for implementing a
program; he pointed to Section 9.80.020 which outlined the legislative findings supporting
various parking programs. Ms. Ellsworth noted that the context for the proposed Slate Canyon
Drive and Carterville area parking programs seemed to be based on a different premise. She also
wondered if the ordinance specified a certain threshold or percentage of available on-street
parking. Mr. Magness noted that the ordinance did not specify a particular level, but this was one
of the factors identified and examined as part of the parking study to be considered with the rest
of the data and observations about the prospective parking permit area. Presentation only.

10.      A presentation from the Sign Committee regarding the number of digital signs per
         business and hold times for freeway-adjacent businesses in SC3 zones. (19-102) (3:43:46)

Aaron Ardmore, Planner, presented. Mr. Ardmore reviewed the types, sizes, and styles of signs
permitted in commercial zones, noting that the downtown area has a very specialized sign code
that depends on the street location and building type. Hannah Salzl, Policy Analyst, highlighted
the changes proposed for affected properties in the SC3 zone. The hold time standard has been
adjusted for the SC3 zone where it is adjacent to highways, to 8 seconds, consistent with the
federal highway signage standards. It would only be applicable to the Provo Towne Centre Mall,
and Councilor David Sewell clarified that the affected sign itself must also be highway-adjacent.

the community. Councilor Bill Fillmore indicated that he was ready to move the proposal
forward. Mr. Harding was concerned about enacting piecemeal amendments, lest a property
owner invest in a sign and then face more restrictions down the road. Presentation only.
