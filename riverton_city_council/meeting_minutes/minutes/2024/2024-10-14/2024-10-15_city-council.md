Discussion Items

       Trails Discussion

Cary Necaise, Public Works Director, presented on City trail development and improvement
initiatives. He provided an overview of a City map that highlighted current and potential future
trails, distinguishing between types such as existing, planned, sidewalk, and paved trails
through color coding. He noted that Midas Creek and Rose Creek trails had been identified as
priority areas, with estimated costs of $150,000 and $250,000, respectively, for full completion.

Mr. Necaise proposed securing approximately $100,000 annually for trail maintenance and
development, suggesting that consistent funding could enable trail connections and
improvements in phases rather than scattered across various sites. He recommended creating
a rating system, similar to street evaluations, to prioritize future trail maintenance and
development needs.

Mayor Staggs highlighted the importance of integrating an annual budget for trail development
into the City’s five-year plan, suggesting a yearly allocation of $150,000 to $200,000 for
consistent improvement.

Councilmember McDougal expressed strong support for prioritizing trail development and
maintenance. He noted that a recent survey indicated residents' top request was for more trails.
He suggested treating trail maintenance with similar importance to sidewalk upkeep, proposing
that as county funding for trails becomes available, the City should utilize these funds to connect
trails.

Councilmember McCay expressed enthusiasm for trail development, suggesting that if the City
contributes $150,000, matching funds from TRCC and state programs could potentially multiply
this investment by three or four times.

Councilmember Haymond inquired about future plans for canal-side trails, questioning if these
would ultimately be paved or left as dirt trails. In response, Mr. Necaise clarified that, currently,
there is an agreement with Welby Jacob to pave their trail, which will serve as their new
maintenance road.

Mayor Staggs discussed the Welby Jacob Trail project, noting that the primary expense was the
planned bridge rather than the linear asphalt strip. He highlighted recent conversations with
UDOT, which now manages the project, expressing concern that UDOT’s initial timeline placed
bidding for construction in the fall, despite funding being available for over a year. He advocated
for advancing the timeline, aiming to complete construction before the school year to avoid
continued transportation needs.

Councilmember Buroker expressed support for the trail development plan, suggesting that a
larger map showcasing connections with nearby cities like South Jordan, Herriman, and
Bluffdale would help guide goals for regional connectivity.


         Architectural Code Changes

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, presented updates on proposed revisions to
architectural standards and underlying codes to provide clearer direction while allowing more
flexibility in design. He noted that current standards are rigid, requiring specific elements, which
can limit creativity and flexibility. Proposed changes would enforce "four-sided architecture,"
where all sides of buildings are equally treated, rather than focusing solely on the street-facing
sides. Additionally, he suggested setting limits on materials per building—no more than four
types and no less than two—aiming to avoid excessive variation.

Mr. Lethbridge referenced past Council feedback, proposing a restriction of 75% for any single
material on a building’s façade, with potential exceptions in the downtown area where brick is a
dominant theme. He proposed reducing the specific requirements in the downtown ordinance
from five specific design elements to a more manageable number and replacing rigid elements
(such as clock towers) with more adaptable features like towers or vertical accents. For older
buildings undergoing renovation, new code requirements would ensure cohesion with
surrounding structures by identifying two primary architectural elements that align with existing
themes. This would allow Planning Commission staff to refer to original approvals to maintain
consistency while offering flexibility to adapt to market trends.

Mayor Staggs expressed frustration regarding the allowance of infill developments to have
finished grades significantly higher than the surrounding built environment. In response, Mr.
Lethbridge explained that in some cases, developers choose to leave the dirt on-site rather than
remove it for cost reasons. He emphasized the need to balance various site requirements
during the review process, including engineering, storm drainage, and access.

Mr. Lethbridge discussed ongoing work regarding residential conversions to commercial
buildings, noting the challenges these cases present. Currently, each conversion has been
evaluated on an individual basis, but staff anticipates an increase in proposals and aims to
establish minimum standards to ensure a proportionate change in the building's structure.
Additionally, he addressed the need for clarity regarding renovation triggers. He emphasized the
importance of distinguishing between minor changes, such as repainting, and more significant
renovations that might require adherence to updated standards.

Councilmember McCay proposed adding stone as an acceptable primary material alongside
brick for commercial buildings in the downtown zoning area, emphasizing that modern designs
can include various materials. Mr. Lethbridge clarified that while the current proposal includes a
minimum 30% brick requirement for downtown buildings, there is room for adjustment. He also
acknowledged that while the downtown zone may have more specific material requirements,
other zones would have different criteria focusing on the variety of materials.

Councilmember McCay also inquired about the current minimum size requirement for
commercial lots, currently set at half an acre. Mr. Lethbridge explained that while this size is
necessary to accommodate various commercial elements, there is interest in exploring potential
adjustments to better facilitate smaller businesses, like Swig.

Councilmember McDougal expressed concern that the proposed flexibility in architectural
standards for the downtown area could undermine the thematic consistency the City has been
working to maintain. He emphasized the importance of adhering to established styles, such as
the use of brick and arches, to preserve the character of the downtown district.

Addressing the Mayor's comments, Councilmember McDougal highlighted ongoing frustrations
with how developments impact residents, particularly in relation to height variances in fencing
and structures. He cited a specific instance involving a development on Water Street, where the
anticipated outcome differed significantly from what was delivered. This discrepancy led to
residents feeling dissatisfied, especially when they were faced with towering walls that blocked
views or created privacy issues.

Mayor Staggs and Councilmember McDougal suggested that the Council should have more
authority to influence final site plans, particularly in commercial developments, to prevent
discrepancies between community expectations and what ultimately gets built. They
emphasized the importance of ensuring that developments align with residents' visions,
particularly regarding height and design features.

Mr. Lethbridge acknowledged that previous practices had not adequately clarified how
developments would affect the surrounding areas, especially concerning grade differentials. He
noted that often, engineering adjustments made during the review process were not effectively
communicated to the public or the Planning Commission. He recommended implementing a
conditional use permit for projects with a significant grade differential (over 18 inches) between
properties. This approach would provide an additional layer of review and ensure that residents
were informed about potential impacts on their views and privacy.

Councilmember Buroker expressed skepticism about the Council approving site plans, arguing
that it wouldn't effectively address ongoing issues due to potential changes in Council
composition. She emphasized that the real solution lies in improving how site plans are
presented, advocating for clearer descriptions and visual representations. By implementing a
code that specifies allowable grade differences, she believes that many of the problems
encountered with developments could be resolved.

Councilmember Haymond expressed concern that the City's requirements for aesthetics might
hinder the development of functional buildings that don't need to be prominently featured or
"fancy." In response, Mr. Lethbridge clarified that it's common for cities to implement varying
standards based on the type of building. While public-facing structures often adhere to higher
aesthetic standards, more industrial buildings, like workshops or maintenance facilities, can
have more lenient requirements.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments; there being none, he closed the Citizen Comment

Mayor/Council Reports

Councilmember Buroker reported on the latest mosquito abatement updates, announcing that
the season is officially over. Throughout the season, 614 acres were treated, with 3,482 traps
inspected and 58 service requests completed. Additionally, she shared insights from the recent
Unified Fire meeting, highlighting a presentation from the Utah Task Force regarding their
deployment to assist with Hurricane Helene.

Councilmember McCay reported on a recent TRCC Board meeting where they discussed grants
aimed at funding cities and nonprofits. Preliminary approvals have shown positive results for
Riverton and neighboring cities, with the total available funding increased from $5 million to $6
million.

Councilmember McDougal recognized the efforts of Councilmembers to attend the Wasatch
Front Regional Competition held at Riverton High School for bands across the state.

Councilmember Haymond thanked City staff for the Halloween Bash which was a big success.

Mayor Staggs expressed appreciation for the Council's work, noting that Councilmembers not
only serve in their primary roles but also represent the City on various boards, including fire,
mosquito abatement, county-wide boards, the sewer district, and sanitation district.

Mayor Staggs reported a groundbreaking ceremony had taken place for a new four-acre
development site across the street from City Hall. This project was made possible through a
collaboration with a developer after the City acquired additional property from UDOT’s
expansion of Redwood. He also addressed an animal control matter, explaining that an animal
that had been put up for adoption was discovered to already have an owner. After the adoptive
resident chose to return the pet, it was reunited with its original owner.
