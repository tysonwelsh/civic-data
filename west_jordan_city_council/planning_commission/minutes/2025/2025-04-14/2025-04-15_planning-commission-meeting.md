2. Kinder Construction; 5782 West Feulner Park Circle; Conditional Use Permit for

Outdoor Storage and Operations in a Drinking Water Source Protection Zone; M-1
Zone; Kinder Construction/Jim Kinder (applicant) [#34333; parcel 26-11-226-007]

Jim Kinder, applicant, said a concern regarding sand was mentioned in the report. He said that he only

uses 8 mm to 4 mm sand for pipe bedding. He stated that he would probably start construction on a

building within a year. He has paperwork from 2000 that showed he did not need a building on this lot,

and he made application for the conditional use permit at that time. He wants the building to be able to

pay for itself with rental units, etc.

Questions were raised regarding current vehicle storage, which the applicant stated he had been doing

for 25 years. The applicant will be allowed to pave the surface for vehicle parking while a site plan and

building permit are being processed.

Megan Jensen said the site has been used for storage and has become a code enforcement issue. The

parcel is within the Drinking Water Source Protection zone so extra attention is given to protecting the

ground water. The property will have a future building and will be reviewed through the site plan

approval process. An existing fence meets the screening requirements for storage that is in that area.

Most of the storage area for materials is gravel, and a paved area will be for equipment and vehicle

parking. She reviewed the recommended conditions of approval.

6. Bingham Heights Phase 5 Rezone; 5891 West 7000 South; Rezone 20.96 acres from LSFR
(Low Density Single-family Residential (WSPA) Zone to R-1-9 (Single-family Residential
9,000 square foot lot minimum) Zone; Perry Homes Utah/Lynsi Neve (applicant) [#34366;
parcel 20-26-200-006]
The applicant was not in attendance at this time but arrived later.
Mark Forsythe gave an overview of the project requesting a rezoning from a West Side Planning Area
zone to R-1-9. The proposal meets the current future land use designation of very low density
residential with a density of 3 units per. The concept plan shows 63 lots, which meets the number of
ERCs for water that are allowed for this property. Zones to the north, east, and west are similar to the
proposal. The MFR property to the south is undeveloped and will allow approximately 57 units and
amenities in a planned development. The concept shows roads connecting to Bingham Heights to the
east, Copper Rim to the west, and the Jordan School District property to the north.
Clarification was given on the road widths as they connect to planned roads in Copper Rim. Nathan
Nelson stated that there are open space parcels in Copper Rim next to the road in that location, but the
driving surface would be the same in both developments.
An explanation was given of why the rezoning is required even though density is not changing. The
LSFR zone was part of the West Side Planning Area zoning, but that designation had been relegated to
only the Highlands development. Years ago, the city council determined that properties outside of the
Highlands could not be developed with WSPA zoning. The ordinance states that properties outside of
the Highlands would have to be rezoned to a zone that would support a similar density, which is what
the applicant is doing with this request.
Tom Romney, Focus Engineering and Surveying, stated that the R-1-9 zone is very compatible with
the surrounding zoning and is a great fit for the community. Even though zoning is for 9,000 square

8. Text Amendment — Conditional Use Permits; Continued from March 18, 2025; Amend

the West Jordan Code Section 13-7E-10 Status of Conditional Use Permit, adding
revocation language for non-use of conditional use permit; City-wide applicability; City
of West Jordan (applicant) [#34450]

Duncan Murray reviewed changes to the proposed amendment that were suggested by the planning

commission regarding noticing and due process.

Ammon Allen asked for an explanation of why both 24 and 12 months are used.

Duncan Murray explained that a new conditional use permit is allowed 24 months, as recommended by

the planning commission, to get a building permit or business license and to get the business up and

operating. The other situation is for an existing conditional use permit that has been abandoned with 12

months of inactivity, which is a reasonable time to give before termination.

Jay Thomas opened the public hearing.

Further public comment was closed at this point for this item.
