MINUTES FOLLOW-UP: Council member Worthen asked for an update on the Mendon Road
repairs. Director Josh Runhaar said he will have a more accurate timeline by next week. There
are no temporary options. If the repair is a bridge, it will be 8 to 12 months. If the repair is a
culvert, it will take 2 to 3 months. It will probably be a culvert. The costs, etc. will be discussed
at the June 27, 2017 budget opening.

Executive Buttars remarked that there have been numerous erroneous rumors about the
Mendon Road — for example, several people were arrested for riding bikes there. This is not
true and he urged people to not spread or believe rumor mill comments. Runhaar reiterated the
county has pursued every option for funding assistance as well as various “fixes” for the road.
Cache County is too small for FEMA and too big for Federal Highways assistance. The county
is working diligently to find the proper solution at a cost that is best for all concerned. Buttars
has set a goal to have the work done by the time school begins in the fall.

e Discussion-County Option ‘4 Cent Sales Tax for Transportation Maintenance
Funding — Executive Buttars stated HB362 provided a funding alternative for local

jurisdictions for roadway maintenance. Buttars recommends moving forward to place
the question on the ballot for the 2018 countywide election so more voters will have a
voice in the decision. He has presented the idea to the Mayors Association and they will
take it back to their communities for input. In answer to Council questions, Director
Runhaar said the sales tax money is distributed by the state directly to the receiving
entities based on 50% point of sale and 50% population. All the collected funds come
back to the county with no encumbrances by the state, etc. Buttars’ proposal is for a
distribution of 35% to the County, 55% to the cities and 10% to mass transit. The
Council is supportive and Runhaar asked that the issue be kept in the forefront for 2018
implementation and commented this is the best written tax one can find because it has
multiple use applications and is cheap and clean funding.

Re: 2014 RAPZ Grant Allocation for Tennis Courts
Dear Craig,

The purpose of this letter is to request Cache County's approval to use RAPZ funds awarded to
Nibley City by Cache County in 2014 at a different location than originally proposed.

In 2014, the Cache County Council allocated $40,000 in RAPZ funds to Nibley City to construct
tennis courts, as there are currently no tennis courts in Nibley. Nibley will be contributing
approximately $100,000 in additional matching funds for this project.

As you are also aware, in 2014, Nibley City and Cache County entered into talks related to
recreational property and funding swaps related to the development of Green Canyon High
School in North Logan on land owned by Cache County. As a result of those discussions,
Nibley and Cache County entered into an agreement in which Nibley purchased 20 acres of
sensitive land adjacent to Heritage Park, which is now known as Firefly Park, and which will be
developed as a nature park.

Due to this significant change in park development, the Nibley City Council decided to

update Nibley’s Parks Master Plan prior to completing either of these projects. This caused a
delay in expending the RAPZ funds, for which the Cache County Council recently granted an
extension. The master plan is almost complete and one of the proposals in the plan is to move
the tennis courts from Heritage Park to Nibley’s Anhder Park on 3200 South. Some of the
reasons for this move are related to the addition of the 20-acre Firefly Park. There is now a
need to expand the parking lot at Heritage Park in order to accommodate the new nature park
next to Heritage Park. The area where the parking lot would be expanded would be the area
where Nibley was previously planning to put the tennis courts. Also, after studying the fireflies
present in the wetlands in the nature park, Nibley has learned that putting up lights for the tennis
courts could be detrimental to the fireflies. For these reasons, Nibley City would like to request
Cache County's approval to use these RAPZ funds to construct the tennis courts at Anhder
Park instead.

Thank you for your consideration.

A request has been made to extend the effective period of approval of the Nautica Subdivision for
~four months (Exhibit A).

Current expiration: June 14, 2017

The Cache County Land Use Ordinance §17.02.070 Effective Period for Land Use Authority
Approval, section B specifies that,

“At the discretion of the Land Use Authority, the effective period of approval may be extended
for up to two (2) years beyond the one year period of the original approval. Within that
extension no operation is allowed until the permit has been recorded and all conditions of the
permit have been met. To request an extension, an applicant must submit nonfinancial
justification to the Land Use Authority prior to the expiration of the original one year period of
approval.”

The provided justification for a request for extension (Exhibit A) identifies that the delay in
recording the approved subdivision plat within a year of approval was due to:

1. Change in property ownership;

2. Water shares were seasoned, and;

3. Plat revisions as requested by the County.

The Development Services staff has completed a review of the justifications for the extension of the
effective period of approval and has identified the following:

1. Eight months prior to the expiration date on October 18, 2016, there was a change in property
ownership from Lewiston State Bank to LSH Development, LLC.

2. Staff has been unable to determine the meaning of “seasoned water shares”. The State
Division of Water Rights was also unable to determine what that phrase may mean.

3. 13 months prior to the expiration date in May of 2016, a full plat review had been completed
by staff and the county surveyor, and all revisions had been completed by the initial
surveyor, with the exception of a single plat note.

a. For reasons unknown to staff, the new property owner then contracted with a different
surveyor to have a new survey and survey plat completed that would replace the
previously reviewed and approved plat.

b. On February 28, 2017, the applicant submitted a request to amend the unrecorded plat of
the Nautica Subdivision with the new survey and was notified at that time that additional
fees for plat review would be required prior to the review of staff and the county
surveyor.
