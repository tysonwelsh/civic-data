Planner Whetstone said the intent of the agricultural protection program is four-fold:

was established in the Utah State Code in order to maintain agricultural 
activities, which is important to both the State and Summit County.

and unreasonably restrictive State and local actions. 
4. To educate and raise the awareness of the County’s values and to ensure the 
right to farm.

Staff recommends the Planning Commission conduct a public hearing and consider 
forwarding a positive recommendation to the County Council.

FINDINGS OF FACT 
1. The applicant requests the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission review 
and forward a positive recommendation to the Summit County Council to 
approve the designation of five parcels, namely: CD-487 (142.78 acres), CD-498 
(47.12 acres), CD-395 (78.29 acres), CD-408 (40 acres), and CD-487(142.78 
acres), and Parcels CD-487-C (20.18 acres), consisting of a total of 328.37 
acres, as an Agricultural Protection Area.  
2. The application indicates that the property has been and is currently used for 
raising hay and farm animals, as pastureland, and as range land for grazing 
cattle and horses.  The property owner states that he intends to continue 
agricultural use of the property in similar.   
3. The Eastern Summit County General Plan seeks to ensure that agricultural land 
uses and operations in Eastern Summit County are protected from any adverse 
effects of the development.   
4. It is the intent of the agricultural protection program to:

a. Provide incentives to farmers and ranchers that encourage them to stay 
on the land. 
b. Augment and carry out the State’s agricultural protection area provision, 
established in Utah Code Annotated, Section 17-41-101, to maintain 
agricultural activities that are valuable to the State and the County.   
c. Protect farmers and ranchers from nuisance complaints, undesirable 
rezoning, and unreasonable restrictive State and local actions. 
d. To educate and raise the awareness of developers and new residents that 
Eastern Summit County values, which form the foundation of the General 
Plan, will be protected from any adverse effect of development and help 
ensure the right to farm in Eastern Summit County.    
5. Section 11-1-3: AGRICULTURAL PROTECTION AREA, of the Eastern Summit 
County Development Code required the agricultural Protection Advisory Board 
(APAB) and the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission to review 
applications for recommendation to the Summit County Council.   
6. The land subject to this application is well suited for agricultural protection, in 
terms of size (328 acres), viability for agricultural production and location 
remote from residential and commercial development.   
7. On December 11, 2019, the Agricultural Protection Advisory Board (APAB) 
conducted a public hearing on this application and forwarded a positive 
recommendation to the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission and 
Summit County Council, with no modifications to the application.  
8. No public comment was received at the December 11, 2019 hearing.   
9. The APAB recommended no specific limitations on the types of agricultural 
production in the proposed agricultural protection area.   
10. The APAB recommended that the viability of the land for long-term agricultural 
use depends on the ability of local jurisdictions to carry out and implement 
agricultural preservation practices.

11. Due to the ability to apply for agricultural protection, Summit County is 
recognizing the importance of agricultural and agricultural protection 
designation as one factor in long term viability.    
12. The property is currently used for raising hay and farm animals, pastureland, 
and range land grazing for cattle and horses.  Parcel CD-487-C contains a 
residential house, in addition to agricultural uses and structures.   
13. Zoning of the parcels is Agricultural and Grazing 40 (AG-40) and Agricultural 
and Grazing 90 (AG-80). 
14. The parcels are zoned for agricultural use. 
15. Agricultural buildings and uses are allowed uses in the AG-40 and AG-80 
Districts.   
16. Section 11-1-3 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code allows a 
property owner to request an Agricultural Protection Area designation if there 
are at least five (5) acres of active agricultural production within any area to be 
designated.   
17. Within each of the five (5) parcels, there are a minimum of five (5) acres of 
agricultural production and use.   
18. On November 20, 2019, and February 7, 2020, five (5) notices of the public 
hearing was published in the Summit County News.  
19. On November 22, 2019, and February 7, 2020, five (5) notices of the public 
hearing were posted near and on the property per requirements of the Eastern 
Summit County Development Code.   
20. No written objections to the creation of an agricultural protection area have 
been received. 
21. No requests for modification to the application have been received. 
22. Any person affected by the establishment of an agricultural protection area may 
file a written request for modification of the proposal or written objections to 
the proposal within fifteen (15) days from the date of the required public 
hearing notice.

CONCLUSIONS OF LAW 
1. Designation of the identified parcels for Agricultural Protection Area is 
consistent with Goal 2.2 of the General Plan, which states, “Acknowledge the 
historic rural and agricultural character of Eastern Summit County.”  
2. The application for Agricultural Protection Area complies with Section 11-1-3 of 
the Eastern Summit County Development Code for approval of an Agricultural 
Protection Area. 
3. The combined acreage of the parcels proposed for agricultural protection 
designation is 328.27 acres.  
4. No written objections to the creation of an agricultural protection area have 
been received. 
5. The parcels are zoned for agricultural use.

COMMISSION COMMENTS AND QUESTIONS          
Chair Clyde said the setbacks are a concern to him.  He doesn’t think they are adequate 
for a commercial use.  The 12’ setbacks should be at least doubled.  A 50’ setback would 
not be unreasonable.  The noises generated by this use could be disturbing to a 
neighbor.  There is plenty of room for greater setbacks because this is a 10-acre site.   
 
Commissioner Sargent said he would like the developments clustered within the 
interior, depending on the use.  Is that part of the MPD?  A discussion ensued.  Planner 
Milliner summarized the discussion by saying there could be a perimeter setback and 
the interior setback can be addressed by the building code.   
 
Commissioner Rydalch said her concern is about the height restriction.  Is 45’ 
adequate for hotels?  It wouldn’t allow many stories.  Planner Milliner said this would 
allow a commercial use on the ground floor and maybe 2 to 3 floors above.  
Commissioner Rydalch said if they limit the height, they would be building 
horizontally.  They need to decide what they want to see happen in the County.     
 
Planner Milliner said a possibility is to establish 45’ height with exceptions up to a 
certain height.  More height could be given in exchange for more affordable housing, 
underground parking, or something like that.  He can talk with the fire district and see 
what their capabilities are.  Several Commissioners said that would be their preference.   
They need to look at the future of Eastern Summit County and what they want it to look 
like.  If they start building up, they will change the look and feel of Eastern Summit 
County.  Commissioner Rydalch said some of the uses they are permitting will need to 
be tall.  Commissioner Sargent agreed, but said not every building will be a 45’ 
structure.
