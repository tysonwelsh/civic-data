Proposed Resolution 2025–3 approving the Ogden City Path to Citizenship
Program
         A memo from the Administration regarding a proposal to create a Participate, Apply, Test, Help (PATH) to Citizenship
Program came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the goal of the PATH program is to remove obstacles to
obtaining citizenship and to educate new citizens on their civic responsibilities. A pilot program was initiated in 2023 supported
by grant funding from Salt Lake County allocated as part of a statewide effort to expand naturalization for legal immigrants.
Several community partners pooled resources and in 2024, 17 Weber County residents became naturalized citizens of the United
States. The Council will consider proposed program guidelines and a preliminary budget of $21,000. Funding will come from
grants, private donations, and Ogden City.
         Community Engagement Administrator Lartigue summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
summarize the eligibility requirements for naturalization, provide naturalization statistics for 2019-2023, and to discuss the
resources needed to implement the program. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the
meeting.
         Chair Richey introduced in writing proposed Resolution 2025-3, entitled:
                  “A resolution of the City Council of Ogden City, Utah, approving the PATH to Citizenship Program.”
         ON A MOTION BY COUNCIL MEMBER GRAF AND SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER BLAIR,
RESOLUTION 2025-3 WAS ADOPTED UPON THE FOLLOWING ROLL CALL VOTE: VOTING AYE – COUNCIL
MEMBERS BLAIR, GRAF, HYER, MYERS, VICE CHAIR WHITE, AND CHAIR RICHEY. VOTING NO – NONE.
Council member Choberka was not present when this vote was taken.

Proposed Resolution 2024-14 approving the Weber Housing Authority to
exercise its powers for a project within the boundaries of the City of Ogden
          A memo from Council staff regarding a proposed resolution allowing the Weber Housing Authority (WHA) to exercise
its powers for a project within Ogden City boundaries came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated Weber
Housing Authority is requesting a resolution finding a need for its permanent supportive housing project at 2325 Madison Avenue.
Utah Code Ann. §35A-8-401states that a county housing authority may not undertake a project within a city’s boundaries unless
“the governing body of the city [adopts a resolution] . . . declaring that there is need for the county authority to exercise its powers
within that city.” The project has been reviewed by the Planning Commission and found to be consistent with the General Plan.
          Chair Richey discussed the timeline pertaining to this matter; the Council office received the proposed resolution from
Weber Housing on November 4, 2024, which started discussion of the proposal. The Council is very aware of the need for these
types of housing projects in the community but has received a great deal of feedback and concerns regarding the proposal. The
Council met with the WHA Executive Director, Andi Beadles, in a work session meeting on January 7, 2025, to discuss the
proposal and get answers to the questions that have been asked regarding the matter. He concluded that the Council would accept
additional public input tonight regarding the proposal.
          Mayor Nadolski thanked Chair Richey for his introduction of this item; he is willing to work with WHA to help find a
solution to the need for this type of permanent housing and he is willing to dedicate staff resources to further analysis of the
proposed location, or identification of a more suitable location for the project.
          Chair Richey invited public input and set guidelines for the public comment period.
          Jeremy Peterson expressed opposition to the proposed housing project, citing concerns about safety and the proximity to
a nearby elementary school.
          Emily Taylor shared her concerns about increased crime and poverty in her neighborhood and the impact this project may
potentially have on her young daughter.
          Ian Hale raised concerns about the facility's ability to handle the needs of high-risk individuals and the potential for noise
and light pollution.
          Jessie Lundell shared a personal story about the importance of citizenship and community support, emphasizing the need
for inclusive housing in the community.
          Stacy Frampton and Kent Frampton expressed concerns about the safety of their neighborhood and the impact the
proposed project will have on their children.
          Amy Wicks voiced support for the project, noting she listened to the recording of the January 7 work session meeting and
was disappointed by some of the comments she heard from Council members that display a very clear and alarming lack of
understanding of the issues the unsheltered population of the community face. Supportive housing cannot be placed in an outlying
area of the community; rather, it needs to be centrally located.
          Taylor Knuth voiced opposition to limiting the public to one-minute to provide input this evening as this limitation
directly violates the Council’s Rules and Norms. He then expressed support for the project, highlighting the importance of
supportive housing and the positive impact on the community.
          Ila Kent stated she supports the project; she has people in her life that would never be able to live on their own without
support and it is important for these people to have access to supportive housing in a central location, rather than on the outskirts.
          Jackie Kent stated she also supports the project and feels Ogden City can be a leader in providing this type of housing for
those in need of supportive housing.
          Carrie Harbath shared a personal story about the challenges of caring for a disabled child and the importance of building
supportive housing for vulnerable individuals in the community.
          Shardae Jones emphasized the positive impact of supportive services on the community and the need for the proposed
facility.
          Teresa Bramwell raised concerns about the burden the proposed housing project will have on Ogden City and the need
for other cities in Weber County to share the responsibility to provide this type of housing. She has been a victim of crime over
500 times in Ogden and she is tired of carrying the burden of supporting the homeless population in her neighborhood.
          Lucia Madrigal cited studies from across the country that indicate that providing this type of housing does not create
negative impacts for surrounding neighborhoods or affect home values. Studies have found that this type of housing actually has a
positive impact on property impacts and community stability. The housing also does not result in a significant increase in crime.
          Nick Milford stated many of the arguments for and against the project are emotional, but he prefers data driven decision
making. Some have indicated there are lost opportunity costs associated with approving this type of housing for the subject
property because it could be used for a different type of project that may be more profitable; however, he believes the cost of the
project is being exaggerated and the value of the property is being understated. The project is not a homeless shelter and should
not be considered as such when determining the value and the impact on the neighborhood. The plan is to give just enough support
to the people that need it in order to be productive citizens. This will have a positive impact on the entire community. The building
is currently vacant and filling it in its current form is most responsible.
           Brooke Barragan, Lucy Barragan, and George Barragan shared their support for the project, emphasizing the importance
of inclusion and compassion of the types of people that will ultimately occupy the units in the project. Choosing to reject the
project is inaction on the part of the City and will only perpetuate the problem that has led to homelessness. People with physical
and mental disabilities need love, support, and a safe place to live.
           Thomas Harrington shared personal stories and experiences that highlight the need for supportive housing in the
community.
           Abby (no last name stated) stated her mother, father, and little brother are homeless and it is very important that they are
close to resources that can help them to get out of homelessness. She hopes the Council is doing appropriate research into this type
of project and listening to people who have personal experiences with homeless rather than just those who are responding to the
proposal out of their own fear. She understands their fear, but many of the things they are scared of are not cause for fear.
           Joanna Droubay stated she is a developer who has invested heavily in Ogden, and she wished to echo all the support the
Council has heard for the project. She added that it is urgent that the Council move as quickly as possible to address the housing
crisis in any way possible; she suggested they approve the resolution to allow WHA to move forward with this project, which is
only 25 units that will not have as dire an impact on the neighborhood as some have said.
           Heath Satow stated he is very upset by the comments that have been made about the people that will be supported by this
housing project. If these types of comments were made about any other demographic, they would not be accepted. Many of the
people that will be housed in this place are currently being housed temporarily elsewhere and are taking the place of people that
are currently sleeping outside in the cold. He is also troubled that the amount of time dedicated to this topic tonight was limited.
           Angel Castillo raised concerns about the definition of disability and the need for data-driven decisions. She drew the
Council’s attention to the need for independent living opportunities for those who have been discriminated against based upon the
Fair Housing Act.
           Antonio Cavallo stated he and his wife chose to live in the neighborhood surrounding the subject property because of the
services that were available there; the people who will live in this proposed facility will also benefit from the presence of those
services. He encouraged the Council to consider the kind decision to make; approving this facility would be kind and that is the
most important thing to strive for.
           Adriana Van Lee stated she lives two blocks away from the proposed facility and attended an open house meeting there
last night. She supports the proposal and hopes that the Council is basing their decision upon data rather than emotion and the
perceptions of those that are not familiar with the project.
           Jordan Scott stated he has the experience of living in a housing facility like the one that has been proposed by WHA; he
was homeless for a period of time and eventually secured temporary housing. This exposed him to people who were dealing with
mental health issues and drug use, but he felt safe there. This type of housing is not a reward for people who are not trying to
make their life better; rather, housing is given to certain types of people who are working in a positive direction. He would not be
in the position he is in now if it were not for his access to the housing option he was given.
           Linda Sanchez relayed a personal story of the only time she has ever felt unsafe in Ogden City, and it had nothing to do
with a homeless individual; in fact, a homeless person stood up for and protected her and it is ironic that these are the people that
other residents live in fear of. She feels the location that has been selected by WHA is appropriate because it is close to many
services that the residents need. These people just need one person to stand up for them, tell them they matter, and believe in them
and she is willing to be that person. She encouraged the Council members to be that person as well.
           Travis Pate stated his mother lived in this facility when it was a care center; he does not dispute the need for care and
compassion for the people in the community that would benefit from this project, but he does not believe this is the appropriate
location. He invited everyone who has spoken to listen to the past discussions of the Council about their support for the project,
but a desire to examine other suitable locations.
           Kevin Lundell stated he lives in the neighborhood where the subject property is located; the narrative that came from the
December 3 meeting when this project was first discussed was that their support was ‘paid for’ and he is here to say that is not true
and that is evidenced by the turnout at tonight’s meeting and the amount of support that has been expressed. The community
overwhelmingly supports this project in this location. Other approvals have already been secured, and WHA has been working
with other community partners and stakeholders regardless of the statements that have been made to the contrary. The
neighborhood is made up of people who want to help lift everyone, and this includes the unhoused. He encouraged the Council to
be different.
           Andrea (no last name given) encouraged the Council to think about those with mental health issues or other disabilities
that can contribute to homelessness; the people that will live in this facility are just trying to get by and she asked that the Council
approve the resolution.
           Jennifer (no last name given) voiced her support for the project.
           There were no additional persons appearing to be heard.
           Chair Richey thanked everyone who participated tonight; there has been a lot of discussion of this project and the
Council and Administration are simply weighing all of the options available to them and carefully considering how the final
decision will impact the broader community. The intent tonight is to table action on the resolution to provide additional time to
consider the project and continue the dialogue with the WHA.
          Council discussion with Mayor Nadolski centered on efforts of City Administration and WHA to evaluate other optional
sites for the project; the Council also discussed the different options available to them this evening in terms of action on the
proposed resolution. They concluded to entertain a motion to extend discussion of the proposal to provide additional time for the
Council and Administration to confer with WHA and other community partners. Council member Hyer stated he will support the
motion to extend discussion if the Council is willing to make the statement that the property at 2325 Madison Avenue is not the
optimal location for the permanent housing project. Chair Richey stated the motion to extend will give the Council additional time
to analyze the property. Council member Hyer stated he feels the Council has already adequately analyzed the property and he
does not feel it is the right location. He would like for WHA to pursue other locations.

                    “A resolution of the Ogden City Council approving the Weber Housing Authority to exercise its powers for a

Public Comments
           Laura Lewis referred to the discussion during tonight’s work session meeting regarding the creation of two new positions
in the Mayor’s Office; she is concerned about increasing the budget by a half million dollars for the new positions and wondered
if the new positions are actually necessary. She suggested looking outside the City for executive positions, rather than hiring
internally. She also proposed using bonuses instead of creating new positions to reward employees. She highlighted the financial
burden these types of decisions place on taxpayers; she mentioned 16 new positions were created in the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-
2025 budget and she questioned the City’s ability to sustain those ongoing expenses.
           Taylor Knuth addressed the Council regarding their rules and norms as well as their meeting procedures. The published
rules document indicates that members of the public addressing the Council during public comment will have up to three minutes
to provide comment. He criticized the Council for not following their own rules and procedures. He then asked Mayor Nadolski to
clarify the statements that include the word ‘we’; he asked Mayor Nadolski to defined who ‘we’ is. He also referenced past actions
and discussions in regard to housing in Ogden; in 2019 the Council was presented with a housing needs assessment for central
Ogden. This assessment was written by a renowned economist and expert in housing and the assessment has been ignored by the
Council. He stressed the importance of trusting subject matter experts on topics such as homelessness and housing needs; there is
a local Homeless Coordinating Council, which holds public meetings, and no member of the Council nor the Mayor has engaged
with that Council in the past 15 years. He concluded the Ogden Housing Authority (OHA) has already approved the WHA
housing project.
           Linda Sanchez announced an upcoming Ogden City Town Hall meeting with Mayor Nadolski and Chair Richey on
January 20, 2025, at 6:00 p.m. at the main library on Jefferson Avenue. This will be an open discussion format, and the public will
have the ability to ask questions of the participants.
           Heath Satow reiterated his support for the Weber Housing Authority (WHA) permanent housing project and asked that
the Mayor carefully consider his position on the matter. He stated that he feels the project is great and the way that the people who
will live in the housing units have been spoken about is unacceptable. He emphasized the need for immediate solutions in terms of
housing for homeless individuals.
           Chris Barragan thanked the Council for their consideration of the WHA project this evening, but echoed Mr. Satow’s
comments about the need to make a quick decision. He suggested that everyone consider whether they would be willing to sleep
outdoors until a decision is made on the housing project so that they understand the true impact their inaction is having on people
in the community. He stated that the WHA project is well thought out and the Council needs to move in the direction of uplifting
and caring for the people in the community. He asked that the Council communicate the date that they plan to make a decision on
the proposal.
           Adrienne Zubler shared her personal experience with homelessness and noted she is dismayed by the opinion that people
have about disabled and vulnerable individuals. She learned the hard way that anyone can experience homelessness; there are not
enough homes for the population in the community, and not enough shelter space for homeless individuals.
           Sean Bishop echoed Mr. Barragan’s suggestion that the Council advertise when they will be discussing the WHA matter
again and when they plan to make a decision. He reminded everyone that the people that will live in the housing project are
already Ogden residents, but this project will give them a place to stay off the streets. People want to live in the housing project
and will not abuse that privilege. This is a unique opportunity to Ogden City, and he asked the Council to make the humane
decision. He concluded by reading a text from someone he loves that is unhoused. He stated he lives in the neighborhood where
the subject property is located, and he supports the housing project in his backyard.
           Jordan Scott raised concerns regarding access to resources for Ogden residents, including transportation and medical
services. It is important to locate a permanent housing project in an area where residents can easily access the services they need.
           Teresa Bramwell shared her experiences with crime and the need for solutions to prevent individuals from repeatedly
being a crime victim; she urged the Council to focus on practical solutions for the entire community.
           Shelby Hornbeck expressed disappointment in the Council's antagonism towards community members supporting the
housing project and called for education on the matters of homelessness and the lack of housing, as well as community
engagement. She stated that the desire to put the City’s homelessness issue out of sight and mind is not a valid reason to reject the
WHA project.
          Jackie Kent questioned the Council's familiarity with the WHA housing program and called for clear communication and
informed discussions on those programs as well as the current proposal. She stated that she found documents regarding the WHA
programs that answered many of the questions that the Council was asking during the last work session meeting and that left her
wondering whether they had read the meeting materials prior to attending the work session.
          Lucy Barragan emphasized the importance of addressing the fears some in the community have regarding homelessness;
she also emphasized the need for the City to provide support to those in need.
          Angel Castillo shared her personal story of temporary disability and the importance of supporting individuals with
disabilities. She also urged the Council to consider the long-term benefits of the WHA permanent housing project. The proposed
project is not a detox facility but is a housing project for people with many different types of disabilities that limits them from
fully functioning them on their own and need some level of support. There are 25 people in need of this housing, and they may
ultimately end up homeless, which costs the City much more money than this project would.
          Stacy Bernal shared her positive experiences with affordable housing and the impact access to a two-bed room unit in
Ogden had on her quality of life. She urged the Council to approve the project and support vulnerable residents in the community.
          George Barragan stated that one of the previous speakers discussed her encounters with crime but noted that there are
many individuals in the community that are suffering much more than her. Additionally, that individual has indicated she lives in
Salt Lake City but also owns a home in Ogden, but there are many residents who do not have one place to stay. He called for
consideration of the needs of those without homes, emphasizing the importance of providing resources and support.
          Robert Hunter stated he does not question the compassion of the City Council and Mayor, but he wished to remind them
that there are hundreds of thousands of dollars on the line and there is a very important timeline that must be followed for this
project. He feels the Council has heard a great deal of misinformation and hopes that will be resolved to give the Council the
ability to make a quick decision on this proposal.
          Travis Pate stated that he does not feel the Council has ‘missed the mark’ on the issue of the WHA project; he discussed
past missed opportunities in the community and encouraged the Council and Administration to continue to work as a team and
involve the public as much as possible in their efforts.
          Debra Barrington stated that she has lived in the neighborhood surrounding the proposed site of the WHA project for
over a decade and she does have some concerns about some conditions in the neighborhood that may make it difficult for those
struggling with drug and alcohol abuse issues to stay away from their vices. She has personally been approached by people
attempting to sell her drugs. She knows there are many people struggling with disabilities and other vulnerabilities, and she wants
everyone to have access to housing and other opportunities, but if the housing project is located in the wrong place where there is
already high crime and drug use, it may make impact the success of the individuals who would be living in the project. She
appreciates the Council’s time and willingness to take a step back and evaluate the project from a very high level.

Council member Comments
          Chair Richey and other Council members attempted to respond to some of the comments and questions raised during the
public comment period; they emphasized the amount of time they spend reading through the materials that are presented to them,
as well as all other information submitted by applicants and the public. They also expressed empathy for the position of many in
the community that are facing difficult circumstances and emphasized their commitment to working to address those issues and
take action on the Weber Housing Authority (WHA) proposal as quickly as possible.

Mayor comments
         Mayor Nadolski agreed with Mr. Hunter that the Council is very compassionate, and they are committed to finding a
solution and work with himself and other community partners on the Weber Housing Authority (WHA) project.

member Choberka was not present when this vote was taken.
