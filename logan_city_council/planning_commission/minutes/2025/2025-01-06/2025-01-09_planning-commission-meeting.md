PC 24-045 Latitude 41-Continued from the December 12 meeting [Design Review &
Subdivision Permit] Nathan Leishman/Latitude 41 Land LLC, authorized agent/owner is requesting
a Design Review & Subdivision Permit to subdivide two properties into three lots and build one
6,000-square-foot retail building and two four-story, multi-family residential buildings with 109 total
residential units and one 750 square-foot commercial unit located at 774 & 776 South Main Street
in the Mixed Use (MU) zone; TIN 02-065-0043; -0015 (Woodruff Neighborhood).

Staff: Aimee Egbert, Logan City Planner, reviewed the request for a design review and subdivision
permit. The Planning Commission continued this project at the December 12, 2024 meeting so the
applicant could submit revised plans for the commercial building and the gazebo that included more
massing and a better selection of materials in order to meet the frontage requirement. The applicant
was also required to submit a revised site plan showing enhanced landscaping along Main Street,
landscaped end caps at the ends of the parking aisles, and the removal of two parallel parking stalls
located near the northwest entrance to the project.

A. Egbert reviewed the updated retail building plans that include new porticos on the north and south
ends of the retail building covering the drive-through lane and creating the required building mass
along the frontage that was needed to meet minimum LDC 60% frontage requirement. A. Egbert
also reviewed the updated parking plan submitted by the applicant, showing that the two parallel


parking stalls at the northwest entrance have been removed and one landscaped island added to
the retail parking aisle.

A. Egbert reviewed the updated conditions of approval requested by the Commission at the
December 12, 2024 meeting: remove condition number 10 requiring the pavilion to be constructed
as proposed on the southwest corner of lot #1 to count towards the 60% frontage requirement;
amend condition number 12 to permit a reduction in the ground floor height of the commercial
space located on Lot #2 from 15 feet to 10 feet; and amend condition number 14 removing the
“clubhouse” as outdoor useable space.

Commissioner Guth asked about the proposed landscape plan not providing end caps on both
ends of the retail parking aisle.

Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director said it is a requirement in the Land Development
Code for all parking aisles to have landscaped caps on both ends. The applicant will have to have
the landscaped caps on all parking aisles for their landscaping/parking plan to be approved with the
building permit.

Commission: The Commission discussed the possibility of a parking reduction being needed to
allow room for the missing landscaped end cap and determined that they would not allow a reduction
in parking for this area. The Commission also determined that condition number 10 requiring 60%
frontage along Main Street needs to be added back into the conditions.

PC 25-002 The Townhomes at River Crossing [Design Review & Subdivision Permit] Russ Holley,
/Logan City, authorized agent/owner is requesting to subdivide .67 acres into 10 lots to build two
four-unit, two story townhome buildings located at approximately 100 West 600 South in the Mixed
Residential Low (MR-12) zone; TIN 02-061-0021 (Woodruff Neighborhood).

Staff: Aimee Egbert, Logan City Planner reviewed the request for two, four-unit, two-story,
townhome buildings located at approximately 100 West and 600 South, with one building on the
west side of 100 West and one building on the east side of 100 West. The proposal includes a ten-
lot subdivision, with eight building lots and two common areas. The townhomes are being proposed
as an affordable housing project by the City of Logan in conjunction with Visionary Homes for
essential workers and income-qualified buyers. As proposed this project meets ail Land
Development Code requirements for density, lot coverage, frontage, setbacks, design standards,
height, landscaping, parking, and outdoor space.

Commissioner Peterson asked where the dumpsters would be located. A. Egbert said that each
unit will have a black garbage can.

A. Egbert also mentioned that the new 105 West Street (old portion of 100 West) will have to be
posted with no parking signs.

Proponent: Russ Holley, Logan City, spoke about the 100 West Street alignment project that
demolished 3 homes in this area to connect to the new bridge and how this project is a good addition
to the City because it is replacing those three homes plus adding five additional homes. R. Holley
stated that Logan City has donated this property and partnered with Visionary Homes to build the
proposed townhomes. Logan City will also be allowing a significant reduction in impact fees for the
building of the townhomes to help offer these townhomes at affordable pricing. The townhomes are
planned to be income-restricted and available to essential workers, such as teachers, police,
firefighters, etc. A deed restriction will also be required to avoid any of the homes being sold shortly
after purchased to make a profit. The approximate discount on the townhomes will be $55,000 to
$75,000 less than current market pricing.

Commissioner Guth asked about crosswalks at the 100 West 600 South intersection. R. Holley said
that the 4 existing crosswalks at that intersection will remain and spoke about the 600 South Trail
that will run along the south side of 600 South to the east to Main Street where it will connect to the
future pedestrian underpass, and along the west side of 100 West to the south to connect to the
River Walk Trail and the Golf Course Trail.

Public: Doyle Zollinger who owns Valley Storage at 521 South Main, said he is not opposed to the
project. He also asked about the gravel that still needs to be replaced in his access to 100 West
since the 100 West alignment project was complete.

Angie Conger, who lives on Hollyhock Lane said that the neighborhood was not happy to learn that
this area would not be green space but are in favor of affordable housing. A. Conger spoke about
the lack of visibility at the 100 West 600 South intersection from 600 South and the speed limit being
raised at the bridge causing people to speed through the intersection causing many accidents along
100 West. A. Conger spoke about the vehicles that currently park on 105 West and said that the
“no parking” signs are only on the east side of the street and are too small to read and too tall to see


from a vehicle. A. Conger spoke about the damaged curb and gutter and driveway approaches
along the west side of 105 West.

Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director said the public comments made regarding
repairs required for the street, curb and gutter, and driveway approaches can be brought up to the
Logan City Engineer, Darren Farar.

Commission: Commissioner Doutre asked about the street trees blocking visibility from the
intersection. R. Holley said there are enhanced setbacks on conners for traffic visibility, and the
Logan City Forrester picks tree species that limb higher for the park strips so they do not block traffic
visibility. R. Holley also stated this will be evaluated during the landscape design phase as well.

STORMWATER / IRRIGATION

Storm water detention/retention shall be designed and constructed according to Logan City
standards. This includes the retention of stormwater from any adjacent street that currently
discharges to the property. Retention of the 90% storm event is required and shall be
provided using Low Impact design methods.

Property is less than 1 acre, so compliance with the State Storm Water Permit is not required.
A sediment and erosion control plan will be required for all construction activities.

Above ground and below ground stormwater facilities shall be designed to be a minimum 1
foot above the historic high groundwater elevation as determined by the geotechnical
engineer. Regardless of the design, stormwater facilities that are found to be constantly wet
by groundwater will be required to complete a design revision that revises the facility to meet
Logan City’s standard.
