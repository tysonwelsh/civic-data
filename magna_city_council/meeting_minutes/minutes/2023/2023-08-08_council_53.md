Presentation of 2022 Audited Financial Statements

                Stewart Okobia, Director of Finance, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District
(MSD), reviewed the Annual Financial Report for the year ending December 31, 2022, presented
by Squire in component units, which segregates out revenues, assets, etc., rather than having
everything together in blended unit. He highlighted the MSD’s Statement of Net Position, showing
total assets of $56.1 million, and total liabilities of $25.4 million. After subtracting the liabilities
from the assets, the result is $31.7 million in assets, which is basically the net cumulative fund
balance. That net position decreased by $5 million from last year. In 2022, revenues were $34.7
million, an increase of $1.7 million from last year; and expenditures were $42.7, an increase of
$14.2 million from last year. A lot of that expense increase was because the MSD closed on a
$20 million bond in 2022, and with the way government financials are presented, a bond is
basically an expense. Magna Metro Townships revenues for 2022, were $11,029,074, and
expenses were $11,016,004, which is an increase in the fund balance of $13,000 from last year.
Last year, the fund balance was $747,278; this year it is $760,348. That is basically, the amount
the Council can spend on any Magna project, without having to get it approved by the MSD Board
of Trustees. Then, the total of Magna’s assets was $6,176,079, and the total liabilities amounted
to $5,415,731.

He also reviewed Squire’s findings of deficiencies in internal control, which it considered to be
material weaknesses, which the MSD will need to clean up going forward: Restatement of
Financial Information and Audit Adjustments. The restatement of financial information involved
some capital assets that were not reconciled, some prior year adjustments that involved the
different metro townships, and balance sheet accounts that were not reconciled timely. The audit
adjustments had to do with expenditures exceeding the final budget in the General Fund because
the MSD categorized some of its contract and maintenance costs by putting them in the Capital
Fund, but it spent the money from the General Fund.

                 Council Member Barney asked why the unincorporated County was missing in
this financial statement.

             Mr. Okobia stated the unincorporated county is accounted for in Salt Lake
County’s budget, so as not to double account for things.

Update on Magna Water District

            Mr. Clint Dilley, General Manager, Magna Water District, updated the Council on
the Magna Water District projects:

• As part of the Environmental Protection Agency’s (EPA) drive regarding lead and copper, the
Magna Water District is working to get into businesses and homes to find out what services they
have, and to get information about what the costs will be to eventually replace all of the lead
services. The district has received a $100,000 grant from the Utah Division of Drinking Water
Board to assist with this effort. The district sent information to property owners about that, but did
                                                   2

not get a great response, so it will have to try a more targeted approach. The project needs to be
done by 2025, but the district is in good shape, and there is not a lot of lead out there – probably
less than 5 percent.

• The Secondary Water Reuse project is under construction and on schedule for water delivery
in 2025. There have been some challenges with secondary water quality due to suspended solids
increasing and plugging up the filters, which the district has had to make some adjustments for.
The district would like to see secondary water usage go up, as that lowers drinking water usage,
which is more expensive to deliver.

• The Haynes Well Field well is being replaced. The district has applied for a $1.9 million grant
through Congressman Chris Stewart’s office, and that was approved by an appropriation
subcommittee and is in the final budget to be approved by Congress in the next budget cycle.

• The district has started its employees training on the Arbinger Outward Mindset program, which
will create a more positive, proactive team environment.

              Mayor Peay stated people do not want to go to the expense of secondary water
by disconnecting their sprinkling system from their drinking water and hooking it up to the
secondary water system.

                Mr. Dilley stated the cost of the meter is also a challenge. The Magna Water
District received $375,000 through the Utah Division of Water Resources to go toward secondary
meters. The Magna Water District will also help people pay for their meter through the savings
they get using secondary water.

               Council Member Prokopis asked what percentage of people in the Magna Water
District had secondary water as an option.

               Mr. Dilley stated the Magna Water District sees about 20 percent of usage, but
that number is skewed because of the bigger users. Nearly all parks, schools, and churches have
secondary water, and secondary water is going into all the new developments. The district is
waiting on Zone 3 Secondary Water Booster Pump Station to deliver water to areas within that
zone. It wants to put one more reservoir in Zone 3, but that would be a closed loop system and
harder to manage. It is in real estate negotiations with a property owner to get land to build that
on.

              Council Member Barney asked what Dominion Energy’s interest was in water
replacement insurance.

               Mr. Dilley stated Dominion Energy partnered with HomeCerts to provide targeted
insurance for water replacement, and with Rural Water Association of Utah. The district’s concern
is some people might think HomeCerts represents the district, and it does not. He also advised
a company not affiliated with the Magna Water District is sending out a flyer to targeted
homeowners telling them they have bad water in an effort to sell them a water filter. The district
has sent out a news blast from its email and put a message on Facebook to get the message out
about that.

American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) Fund Potential Allocation for Magna Metro Township Capital
Improvement Projects

                 Steven Kuhlmeier, Salt Lake County Public Works Engineering Project Manager,
delivered a PowerPoint presentation on the allocation of the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA)
funds, reviewing the ARPA funds through May 24, 2023, for the Magna Metro Township, with a
total distribution of $3,189,431, with a remaining balance of $2,639,431, after funding the 8000
West/2700 South Realignment Project, the 2820 South Sidewalk Project at 7440 West-7736
West, and the 2700 South Environmental Study; the 2023/2024 carryover projects, including an
additional $1,850,000 for 2024, with a total of $2,000,000 million for Magna Main; the Greater Salt
Lake Municipal Services District (MSD) staff’s recommendations for 2024 new projects; the
Magna 2024 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) priority list, including projects the MSD staff is not
recommending be included in the 2024 budget; and the ARPA fund allocation projects to be
discussed, which included the 8000 West 2700 South Realignment, Breeze Drive Sidewalk, 8000
West Widening, 8400 West Rail Crossing, Magna Main Reconstruct, 8950 West Sidewalk, and
3500 South & Wing Point Storm Drain Upgrade.

               Council Member Hull stated she would like to see a breakdown of the Magna
Traffic Calming project recommended by the MSD staff for 2024.

               Mr. Kuhlmeier stated those are for small traffic calming projects in four or five
locations throughout the community. He would get the requested breakdown to the Council.

              Council Member Prokopis asked why the 8000 West SR 201 Turn Lane Project
was the Magna Metro Township’s responsibility. He thought the Utah Department of
Transportation (UDOT) should fix that.

                 Mr. Kuhlmeier stated UDOT is required to provide a study when there is a certain
traffic impact. UDOT did the study for that area, and the study showed no improvement was
needed. However, people are now driving through a pothole or on the shoulder there, which is
dangerous. Half of that is in UDOT’s right of way, so Magna could see if UDOT would partner
with it to do that turn lane; however, the element is time.

               Council Member Hull stated she wanted to pull the 8400 West Bridge Review
from the CIP project list because that the Council no longer wants to do a bridge review, but rather
wants to look at doing a four-way stop there. That is critical with the high school buildout being
finished September 2025.

                Council Member Barney stated that is a UDOT road, and he did not want to be
in the same position Magna was in now with the right-hand turn lane. UDOT should bear some
responsibility for that.

               Mayor Peay stated Ivory Homes should provide money for that project too.

              Mr. Kuhlmeier stated two large developments are going in there, and they will be
required to make some traffic improvements.

               Council Member Hull stated the Council is working with UDOT on that, but the
project needed to be bumped high on the list, as there is a safety aspect to that. The school will
be finished September 2025, and there will be two exits out of there. If the Magna Metro Township
provided money for it, it would probably be done sooner.

             Council Member Prokopis stated the burden for that should not fall on the Magna
Metro Township. That is a State Highway and a UDOT responsibility.

                Council Member Barney stated he agreed it would not be prudent to allocate
funding for that knowing there is a potential for a large future project.

               Mayor Peay stated he wanted to see the Breeze Drive Sidewalk project done.
That street has needed a sidewalk for 70 years.

                Council Member Hull asked if the 8000 West Widening project was to do sidewalk
and curb.

               Mr. Kuhlmeier stated that is a minor improvement project. It is to put in sidewalk,
curb, and gutter, and then the road will be restriped to have a narrow turn lane. The right of way
will be narrowed down to 66 feet in areas. There is not enough roadway width there to do buffered
bike lanes. Doing that would require acquiring more property.

              Council Member Hull asked if the Federal Government had committed any funds
to the 8000 West 2700 South Realignment project.

              Mr. Kuhlmeier stated there is an agreement with the Federal government, which
includes matching Federal grant funds allocated to this project, but the program rules require the
project be administered by the metropolitan regional organization (MPO), and that an
environmental study be done before it can start using those funds. He did not expect the Magna
Metro Township would be able to use those funds until at least the fall of 2024. The Magna Metro
Township has $250,000 for the Environmental Study.

                Council Member Pierce stated she thought the Council talked about doing a
signal there.

                Mr. Kuhlmeier stated the intersection warrants a signal right now, based on the
number of accidents there. However, the recommendation and what the Magna Metro Township
has funding for is to realign that area rather than putting in a traffic signal because a traffic signal
would not fix the geometric problem. If it did not realign the road, there would still be an oblique
angle coming up to the intersection, and there would not be optimal placement for pedestrians.
As the traffic continues to grow, it might warrant a signal.

               Council Member Pierce stated she felt the Magna Metro Township would be
doing a disservice to the community if it did not do the signal when realigning the road.

               Mr. Khulmeier stated Magna could put more money into that project if it felt that
was the ultimate solution beyond what the County and MSD have recommended here. The grant
money would not go toward that.

               Council Member Hull asked how much it would cost to include a light in the study.

               Council Member Pierce stated the Environmental Study would not affect the light.

Rail Crossing project.

               Mr. Kuhlmeier stated the County could seek out school grants. The intention of
doing this project is to put in safe sidewalks, which are specific to UDOT roads. It would take
awhile to seek out school grants, and it could take several years for those to be awarded and
funded.

               Council Member Hull asked if the Council could loop some of these projects into
the corridor assessment project with UDOT. It seemed silly to have a bunch of separate projects
on the same thing.

                Mr. Kuhlmeier stated the County’s plan is to seek out funding, and UDOT’s
participation is to backfill. The County is looking to get a consultant on board now. It will be
bringing proposals back here in about two to three weeks.
