2.    WORK SESSION

      a.     COMMERCIAL SITE PLAN: “PREMIER MEDICAL” TO BE LOCATED AT
             3433 WEST WATER STREET.

City Planner, Tim Prestwich, presented the staff report described the surrounding properties
and reported that the subject property is located in an M-1 zone. He commended the
applicant on their well-researched first submittal and believed they understood the City’s
primary concerns with traffic on Water Street and the impact on Castlewood. When
Castlewood was originally approved, the developer was required to install a six-foot white
vinyl fence. A property rendering was displayed. The applicant has designed their loading
docks for easy access with space to turnaround. A backing area is proposed to limit truck
traffic in front of residential homes. A flex building design was envisioned. Mr. Prestwich
remarked on the excellent building design and stated that it is superior to what is typically
seen in commercial areas and matches many of the requirements for Riverton’s downtown.

Mr. Prestwich stated that in the Regional Commercial Zone setbacks, building heights, block
coverages, turn of the Century features, lower one-third of wall/brick/stone, and horizontal
wall variations are all reviewed. The M-1 zone requirements differ and do not include a
horizontal wall variation requirement or the lower one-third wall to be brick. He noted that
the height and setback requirements have been met. The exterior details were presented.
Staff met with the architect and provided input on additional elements to be considered. The
M-1 zone Landscape Plan requires 20 trees per acre plus additional trees along the sidewalk
in front of commercial buildings, all of which the applicant has greatly surpassed. The 10%
landscaping requirement had been met in both phases.

Mr. Prestwich expressed concern with entry signage and truck traffic routes. The need for
signage directing the trucks to the appropriate entrance was emphasized. He remarked that
the site plan includes screening around the dumpster but lacks screening around the oxygen
tank. Fencing options were identified. The zoning ordinance default includes eight-foot
masonry between residential and commercial and allows the Planning Commission to make
exceptions. He described the history of the City approving commercial site plans without
fencing and stated that and a residential neighborhood with six-foot fencing against the
property provides a rational basis to determine that a six-foot fence may be adequate. The
Castlewood subdivision plans were presented.

A question was raised regarding the north face façade and continuity and if the truck
entrance could be removed from Water Street and added to the entrance that leads to
Stampin’ Up.

Commissioner Brown commented that the truck entrance appears to be narrow.
Commissioner Russell confirmed that the entrance measures 40 feet, which is 10 feet wider
than the standard to the east. Two-foot berms with heavy landscaping are planned along
Water Street. He asked for a roofline elevation.

Commissioner Rushton recognized that the M-1 zone has a requirement regarding noise
within 250 feet of residential. Mr. Prestwich reported that every use is conditional as would
the proposed flex building.

The applicant’s representative, Mike Haslam with Tom Stuart Construction stated that the
entrance off of Water Street could be reconfigured to run adjacent to the private drive. They
were working on an easement with Stampin’ Up for utilities and are striving to be a good
neighbor. With respect to the tenant occupying the majority of the building, Premier Medical
will be focused on the distribution of PPE, masks, and hand sanitizer. The second smaller
building does not yet have a tenant. Exterior materials were presented. Mr. Haslam was
agreeable to addressing fencing issues should they arise.

Commissioner Lefgren expressed concern with lighting. Mr. Haslam explained that is
designed so that pole lighting will not be necessary as the building illumination will
adequately light the parking lot.

In response to a question regarding the oxygen tank, Mr. Haslam reported that he spoke to
the owner who confirmed that it is approximately 22 feet tall. Screening issues were
discussed.

Commissioner Cluff asked if the proposed two-story building would include offices and
workers on the second story and if the neighbors’ backyards would be visible from that
location. Mr. Haslam confirmed they will initially have a view into the neighbors’ yards but it
will become shielded once the landscaping matures. The proposed floor plan was displayed.

4.    DECISION ITEMS

      a.     AMENDED COMMERCIAL BUILDING: “WALMART” LOCATED AT 13502
             SOUTH HAMILTON VIEW ROAD.

Mr. Prestwich presented the commercial building amendment for Walmart located at 13502
South Hamilton View Road. He explained that when a commercial business changes the
façade of their building, it becomes an amended site plan and requires Planning Commission
review. The building was originally built in 2007 and this was the fourth time it had come
before the Commission. A property rendering was presented. Mr. Prestwich stated that
they have proposed a color change to the exterior and the removal of signage to provide
relief to the roofline. The proposed signage meets the sign ordinance and staff
recommended approval.

Commissioner Lefgren opposed the bright color change and believed it conflicts with the
City’s architectural feel that the residents were in favor of when the request was originally
approved. A representative from BR Architecture stated that Walmart would be open to the
suggested changes as requested leaving the lower area blue and a grey above to better
blend in with the building.

Commissioner Russell agreed with Commissioner Legren’s concerns and was in favor of a
more neutral color.

Commissioner Russell moved to APPROVE Application #PLZ 21-8006 Walmart
Amended Site Plan with the following revision:

      1.     The arched areas be a neutral color that matches the building.

Commissioner Lefgren seconded the motion. The motion passed with the unanimous
consent of the Commission.

5.    DISCUSSION ITEMS

Mr. Prestwich reported that the General Plan was discussed at the previous meeting where
staff emphasized that the new process would allow the Planning Commission to first review
the General Plan followed by the City Council. The same procedure would be followed for
zone amendments. He indicated that after that meeting, several Commissioners recognized
that was problematic. The City Council instructed staff that while that decision looked good
on paper, it did not function well when met with the public and creates confusion. Therefore,
the City Council asked staff to bring the rezone back to the Commission and alert the
neighbors that the plan will be combined and presented to the City Council two weeks after

Mr. Prestwich explained that with a rezone and General Plan amendment, notices are sent

Commissioner Rushton asked if the McDougal request that was previously denied could be
