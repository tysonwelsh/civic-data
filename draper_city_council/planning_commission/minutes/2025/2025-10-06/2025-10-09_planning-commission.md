       A.      Study Session: General Plan Water Conservation Element - Presentation by
               Todd Taylor.

City Planner, Todd Taylor, reported that in 2022, the State Legislature passed SB-110, requiring cities
to adopt a water use and preservation element into their general plans by December 31, 2025. The
first administrative draft was received from the consultant and staff wanted to bring the proposed
goals and implementation measures to the Planning Commission for review. After reviewing, the
element is scheduled to return for adoption by the Commission on November 13, 2025, and by the
City Council on December 2, 2025. Mr. Taylor then reviewed the proposed Water Use and
Preservation Element goals and implementation measures.

Goal 1: Strengthen the City’s water conservation efforts through dedicated leadership,
support, and collaboration.

       Implementation 1.1: Explore the formation of a Water Conservation Team to support the
       Conservation Coordinator in addressing existing and future water use and preservation goals.

       Implementation 1.2: Consider developing a Water Conservation Intern or similar position for
       the development and support of additional programming, outreach, and education.

Goal 2: Develop additional citywide policies and ordinances that enhance water conservation
and efficiency through irrigation restrictions for existing developments.

       Implementation 2.1: Develop and implement a Time-of-Day Watering Ordinance to restrict
       outdoor watering between the hours of 10:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m.

       Implementation 2.2: Develop a Water Shortage Plan to help protect public health, safety, and
       welfare during periods of drought, temporary water shortage, and supply interruption.

Goal 3: Increase public awareness of water conservation through educational programming.

      it-yourself workshops such as Fix a Leak and Fix Your Sprinklers.

      Implementation 3.2: Provide residents with the resources to conserve water at home by
      providing free water-conserving tools such as smart leak monitors, leak detection tablets, and

        B.     Annual Planning Commissioner Training by City Staff.

Planning Manager Todd Draper conducted training on subdivisions, which are governed by Utah
Municipal Code Title 10, Chapter 9a, Municipal Land Use, Development, and Management Act
(“LUDMA”), Part 6: Subdivisions.

Per Utah Municipal Code §10-9a-103-76a, “subdivision” means any land that is divided, re-
subdivided, or proposed to be divided into two or more lots or other division of land for the purpose,
whether immediate or future, for offer, sale, lease, or development either on the installment plan or
upon any and all other plans, terms, and conditions. This can be accomplished via plat, metes and
bounds description, devise and testacy, etc., and includes divisions of land for both residential and
nonresidential uses.

Per §10-9a-103-76c, a subdivision does not include:

   i.   The division of agricultural land;
  ii.   A recorded conveyance document that either consolidates multiple lots or parcels into one
        legal description or joins a lot to a parcel, such as boundary line adjustments;
 iii.   A bona fide division of land by deed or other instrument if it states in writing that the division:
        A.     Is in anticipation of a future land use approval;
        B.     Does not confer any land use approval; and
        C.     Has not been approved by the Land Use Authority.
  iv.   A boundary adjustment;
   v.   A boundary establishment;
  vi.   A road, street, or highway dedication plat;
 vii.   A deed or easement for a road, street, or highway purpose; or
viii.   Any other division of land authorized by law.

Per DCMC § 17-1-020, the purpose of the subdivision code is to:

        A.     Promote and protect the public health.
        B.     Regulate future growth and development activity within the City in a manner that
               promotes the physical integration of diverse housing forms, preservation of Draper
               City community values, social integration of residents, and the efficient and orderly
               growth of the City.

       C.      Provide standards for the physical development of land, including roads, parks, water,
               and sewer systems, etc.
       D.      Provide adequate light, air, and privacy to secure safety from fire, flood, and other
               dangers, and prevent overcrowding and undue congestion of the population.
       E.      Assure the site is suitable for building and human habitation.

Subdivision types include:

   •   Regular subdivisions (more than 10 lots)
   •   Minor subdivisions (10 or fewer lots)
   •   Condominium plats
   •   Amended subdivisions
   •   Residential subdivision (dictated by State code)
   •   Commercial subdivisions

Mr. Draper then reviewed the basic subdivision process.

The Development Review Committee reviews the Conceptual Plan before beginning the official
process outlined below.

Preliminary Plat

   •   Application shows the location of existing buildings, grading, special features, etc.
   •   Staff review for compliance with DCMC
   •   Planning Commission or Zoning Administrator review
   •   If approved, the applicant has one year to move to the next step in the process or request a six-
       month extension.
   •   State law now allows applications for Preliminary and Final Plats to be submitted
       concurrently, which can be confusing but also speeds up the process, as most review and
       approval occur during the Preliminary Plat phase.

Final Plat

   •   Single-page document that is submitted for final approval.
   •   Applicant prints the Final Plat on mylar, and it is then submitted for signatures, reviewed by
       the City Attorney, and sent to the County Recorder for recordation.

In response to a question from Commissioner Nixon, Mr. Draper reported that the County Recorder
typically reviews the Final Plat before the mylar is printed, but sometimes reviews the Preliminary
Plat as well. That review is required before scheduling an appointment for the recording of the mylar.

The Planning Commission reviews Preliminary Plat applications for regular subdivisions at a public
meeting and must approve the Plat if it complies with the ordinance. For amended subdivisions, the
Commission reviews the Final Plat and may approve it if good cause is found to do so.

Mr. Draper reviewed example regular subdivision Final Plats, including example final signatures. He
then reviewed subdivision amendments.

Per Utah Municipal Code 10-9a-103(77):

       a)      “Subdivision amendment” means an amendment to a recorded subdivision in
               accordance with Section 10-9a-608 that:
                i.   vacates all or a portion of the subdivision;
               ii.   increases the number of lots within the subdivision;
              iii.   alters a public right-of-way, a public easement, or public infrastructure within
                     the subdivision; or
              iv.    alters a common area or other common amenity within the subdivision.
       b)      “Subdivision amendment” does not include a simple boundary adjustment.

Mr. Draper then reviewed definitions of various items that could be included on a Plat.

Protection Strip: A strip of land running parallel and adjacent to a public street and the abutting
                  private property, created for the purpose of controlling the access of property
                  owners abutting the street until the adjacent owners share in the cost of
                  infrastructure improvements.

In response to a question, Mr. Draper confirmed that the City allows protection strips. Ms. Jastremsky
added that standards for protection strips were included in a text amendment several years previously
and require an agreement outlining the adjacent property owner’s responsibilities and financial
obligations. There are several protection strips in the City, but they are uncommon.

Commissioner Nixon asked if the cost was determined based on fair market value. Ms. Jastremsky
reported that a pioneering/reimbursement agreement is required, and the subdivider shall contract to
deed the strip to the existing or future owners of the contiguous property for consideration named in
the agreement. Such consideration shall not be more than the fair cost of the land in the protection
strip, the street improvements properly chargeable to the adjacent property, plus the value of one-half
the land in the street at the time of the agreement, together with interest at a fair rate from the time of
the agreement until the subdivision of the adjacent property.

Easement:         A present or future right of use under, on, or above the surface of property by a
                  person or agency other than the legal owner of the property.

In response to a question from Commissioner Ogden, Mr. Draper clarified that public utility
easements are not always explicit. Still, the City Code identifies the entities to which the easement
would apply. Easements are not clear on all Plats, and in some cases staff requests that they be
clarified in an amended Plat. Easement owners have some control over what the property owner can
do within the easement area.

Trail:            A path, hard or soft surfaced, intended for public use for recreation or alternative
                  transportation methods, and which may provide access to City, State, or Federal
                  open lands or recreation areas.

Alley:            A public thoroughfare less than 26 feet wide for the use of pedestrians and vehicles,
                  providing a secondary means of access to the rear of abutting properties.
