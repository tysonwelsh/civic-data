Citizen Public Input

               Mark Elieson stated he has a feral cat problem at his home and cannot get a
response from the Animal Services Department. Neither the animal control officers nor the Trap,
Neuter, and Release program will pick them up. In 2011, the state of Utah passed a law that
declared all feral cats in the state as community animals. According to Animal Services’
interpretation of that law, it cannot put the cats down. However, the law says the cats are
supposed to be cared for by community cat takers, and that is the Animal Services Department.
He wanted the cats off his property.

              Council Member Hull stated she has had some problems with feral cats in her
yard too. She suggested looking into whether the Magna Metro Township could put something
in an ordinance that differed from the County’s rules regarding feral cats.

               Council Member Peel stated he had another citizen from a different part of the
city come to him with the same problem. This needed to be addressed through the Greater Salt
Lake Municipal Services District level or the Animal Services Department.

             Council Member Ferguson stated he had an Animal Services’ meeting on
Thursday and would bring it up there.

            THIS BEING THE TIME heretofore set for a public hearing to receive public
comment regarding the following requests:

Ordinance #2018-09-01 – Adam R. Loser, sponsor for the property owner, requesting annexation
of approximately 210 acres of real property, located at approximately 4000 South and 8400 West,
AKA Little Valley Gateway.

Ordinance #2018-09-02 – Adam R. Loser requesting approval of the establishment of the initial
zoning of approximately 210.371 acres of real property to be as a Planned Community (P-C)
Zone, pursuant to Utah Code § 10-9a-507, for property located at approximately 4000 South and
8400 West.

Adam R. Loser requesting approval of an initial Master Development Agreement for the
development of certain real property, pursuant to the requirements of the Planned Community (P-
C) Zone, for property located at approximately 4000 South and 8400 West.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Ferguson, moved to open
the public hearing for Ordinances #2018-09-01 and -09-02, and the initial approval of the Master
Development Agreement. The motion passed unanimously.

                Charlie Precourt, Vice President and General Manager, Propulsion Systems
Division, stated Propulsion Systems Division produces rocket motors for national defense and
NASA. Due to the operations and materials used in its plant, zoning around the plant has been
done according to the Hercules Master Plan for the protection of the community in the event of
an accident. An accident would cause an overpressure that could take out windows, structures,
and buildings. The area that is being considered for a rezone is in a .3 pounds per square inch
(psi) overpressure zone, which means that a typical glass window measuring 30 inches by 30
inches would be putting up with 300 pounds of pressure instantaneously in an accident and would
shatter into fragments. The Council should consider the implications of that when rezoning to a
planned community environment, which would expose a lot more people to the risks. He had a
presentation he would like the opportunity to present to the Council showing Propulsion Systems
Division’s operations relative to the particular land being considered for a rezone, and which gives
some details of the concerns.

               Council Member Hull asked if the developer was aware of the overpressure zone.

Division, stated Planning and Development is aware of the different overpressure zones. There

the potential negative impacts.

      WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) is a municipality pursuant to Utah Code
Annotated (“UCA”) § 10-2a-401 et seq; and

       WHEREAS, the Magna Metro Township Council (the “Council”) is the municipal legislative

        WHEREAS, Magna received a Notice of Intent to Annex approximately 210 acres of
certain real property located at 4000 South and 8400 West, hereinafter known as Little Valley
Gateway; and

       WHEREAS, the surrounding property owners and affected entities received notice of the
property owners’ desire to become part of Magna pursuant to state law; and

       WHEREAS, Magna received and accepted a Petition requesting annexation into Magna
on April 10, 2018, and reaffirmed the petitions to the Clerk on July 31, 2018; and

Application #30732 – Ron Hall requesting approval of a zone change from R-1-6 to R-1-21 on
property located at 8175 West 3100 South.

               Ron Hall stated he purchased the vacant lot to build a primary residence with a
basement to allow for parents to move in too. However, he could not put in a basement due to
the lot being offset from the road by 300 feet, whereby there was not enough allowance for the
drop for the sewer. So, he would like to build a guest house for parents, but in order to do that,
he needed the property rezoned.

               Todd Draper stated this request went before the Planning Commission and it
favorably recommended approval. The neighbors present at the meeting spoke in favor of it as
well. Additionally, the staff recommends approval.

               Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Hull, moved to open the
public hearing. The motion passed unanimously.

                 Council Member Peel asked if once it was rezoned, the property owner could put
in apartments.

                Mr. Draper stated no. The guest home would essentially count as a second
dwelling unit, and could not be leased, rented, or sold separately from the main home. A guest
home is allowed in an R-1-21 or R-1-43 zone.

              Council Member Prokopis asked if the access would be from 3100 South or if
there was a separate access.

                 Mr. Hall stated the access would be a dedicated right of way from 3100 South.

               Council Member Ferguson, seconded by Council Member Prokopis, moved to
close the public hearing. The motion massed unanimously.

              Council Member Peel, seconded by Council Member Ferguson, moved to approve
Application #30732. The motion passed unanimously.

             Council Member Hull introduced Ryan Lusty, Manager, Magna Mosquito
Abatement District, who has an item of business he would like to discuss.

                Ryan Lusty, Manager, Magna Mosquito Abatement District, stated the Magna
Mosquito Abatement District is proposing a tax increase this year from 4041 to 4075, an 83
percent tax increase. It would increase property taxes on a $202,100 home that has a taxable
value of $111,000 from $4.55 a year to $8.33 per year. It will increase revenue to the district from
the $274,182 that was generated in 2018, to $501,552 in 2019. The Magna Mosquito Abatement
District has not raised taxes in over 30 years, but this year, the Salt Lake City Mosquito Abatement
District annexed part of it into its district, taking a third of the Magna Mosquito Abatement District’s
budget. Additionally, operational costs have increased. For comparison, the Salt Lake City
Mosquito Abatement District has a budget of $3 million, and property owners pay approximately
$20 per household. In Tooele County, property owners pay approximately $45 per household for
mosquito control.

               Council Member Ferguson asked if this would give the district a cushion.

               Mr. Lusty stated it will give the district a cushion, but he does not expect another
tax increase for 20-30 years.

               Paul Ashton stated state statute just requires that the district inform the Council;
it does not need Council approval.
