                                          Staff Meeting

Electronic Board Meeting Determination

                 Greg Schulz read the following statement regarding the electronic meeting
determination:

               Consistent with provisions of the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act, specifically
Utah Code Ann. § 52-4-207(4), and acting in my capacity as the Mayor of the Magna Metro
Township (“Magna”), I Dan Peay hereby make the following written determinations in support of
my decision to hold and convene electronic meetings of the Magna Metro Township Council
without a physical anchor location:

1.     Conducting Council Meetings with an anchor location that is physically accessible for
members of the public to attend in person presents a substantial risk to the health and safety of
those who may be present at the anchor location.

2.     This determination is based upon the following:

       a.      Pursuant to the Public Health Orders issued by the State of Utah and Salt Lake
       County, the Magna Metro Township has been determined to be classified as being in the
       “Moderate” transmission category meaning significant person-to-person transmission of
       the virus continues to occur in Magna; and

       b.     As low vaccination rates continue to exist in Magna, public attendance at a meeting
       with an anchor location poses high risks of person to person transmission of the virus.

                These written determinations shall be read into the minutes at the beginning of the
Council meeting held in Magna on April 13, 2021, and future Council meetings as appropriate.
Council staff are instructed to include in the public notice of each Council meeting the information

meetings.

               This written determination is effective until midnight on May 15, 2021, and may be
re-issued by future written determinations of the Mayor at that or any other appropriate time.

Proclamation

             Greg Schulz reviewed the following proclamation declaring April 2021 as “Child
Abuse Prevention Month:”

                                  MAGNA PROCLAMATION
                   Proclaiming April 2021 as “Child Abuse Prevention Month”

WHEREAS, the Children of Magna Metro Township (“Magna”) are the future of our state’s
success and investing in their general welfare, safety and livelihood are of an utmost priority; and

WHEREAS, all children deserve to grow up in a safe and nurturing environment to assure they
reach their full potential as they grow and develop; and

WHEREAS, the protection of children and strengthening of families is of concern and
responsibility of all Magna residents because the wellness of children affects our lives now and
will continue to affect us in the future; and

WHEREAS, child abuse is a serious problem with 116 confirmed child victims in Magna last fiscal
year with each of those cases impacting an entire family and affecting the community as a whole;
and

WHEREAS, child abuse respects no racial, religious, socio-economic or geographic boundaries;
and

WHEREAS, all residents of Magna need to become more aware of child abuse and its prevention
within Magna and actively encourage and support parents to raise their children in a safe and
nurturing environment, and

WHEREAS, preventing child abuse requires each Magna resident to be attentive to the problems
of families around them and commit to do everything they can to help.

NOW THEREFORE, the Mayor and Council of Magna proclaim April 2021 as CHILD ABUSE
PREVENTION MONTH and we call upon all our residents to commit to be educated on the impact
of child abuse and join in working for its prevention within our municipality.

ADOPTED on this the 13th day of April, 2021, in Magna, Salt Lake County, State of Utah.

Reports from Visiting Elected Officials

               Dave Alvord, Salt Lake County Council Member, announced that Senator Lincoln
Filmore ran a bill to get more proportionate Zoo, Arts and Parks funding to the districts. District 1
has been getting a huge and disproportionate amount of the money. Since the legislative session,
the County Mayor has agreed to double the funding to organizations in County Council District 2,
which includes the metro townships of Magna and Kearns, and the cities of South Jordan and
Herriman. Two organizations in Magna applied for funding – the Magna Area Arts Council, which
was awarded $10,009 (73 percent of its request), and the Oquirrh Hills Performing Arts Alliance
which only received $20,000 (27 percent of its request). The County would like to work with those
organizations on criteria to see if they can get a better percentage moving forward. He also asked
the Council to encourage other groups that qualify for funding to apply.

Council Reports

Unified Police Department (UPD)/Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area

                Council Member Prokopis stated the exit of Taylorsville is coming up. To date,
Taylorsville has only committed to hiring up to 10 officers. The UPD serviced Taylorsville with
around 60 officers. It has some current vacancies, so can absorb some of those officers back
into the organization. He has committed to the union and Chief Del Craig to do everything
possible not to have to lay off any of them. That might mean absorbing them through attrition or
creating a buyout. There are currently 18 people the UPD needs to find a place for. The board
is going to reach out to Taylorsville’s new police chief to see if he can help. Taylorsville has until
the end of this month to make job offers.

Resolution No. 21-04-01

                Paul Ashton reviewed the resolution authorizing execution of an agreement with
Salt Lake County for election services for 2021 vote by mail municipal elections and appointing
Greg Schulz as the Election Liaison. He stated this resolution assumes the contract with Salt
Lake County will be the same as it has been in the past. If the Council wants to go with ranked
choice voting, the language in the resolution will have to be modified, and the Council would want
to defer a decision on it. The deadline to inform the Lieutenant Governor that the Council wants

                                                  6
DATE            TUESDAY                                  APRIL                              13, 2021

to do ranked choice voting is May 10, 2021. He asked what the costs were for traditional voting
versus ranked choice voting.

                Greg Schulz stated traditional voting includes a primary and general election and
the costs could be as high as $8,647. If using the ranked choice voting and just having a general
election, the contract with Salt Lake County indicates that would be $5,234.

              Council Member Prokopis stated he would not mind being a guinea pig for the
ranked choice voting. If the Council does that, it could approve a resolution with Salt Lake County
at the next meeting.

               Mayor Peay stated he agreed with that.

              Council Member Peel stated he was intrigued by ranked choice voting. However,
he did not know whether he was in a position to make that decision, since he was one of the
candidates running this year.

               Council Member Pierce stated she did not have a strong feeling one way or the
other about making the change now or in the future. If other jurisdictions were trying it, the Council
could wait to see how that went.

               Council Member Hull stated she would like to hear if there were negatives. If
there is no negative side, then, she did not think it mattered.

              Mr. Ashton stated the negative side for using ranked choice voting is the
candidates do not have to declare candidacy until August, whereas with the traditional election,
candidacy declaration is in June.

               Council Member Prokopis stated he thought it was the way of the future and
would like to get in early on it. He suggested Mr. Schulz draft a new resolution for the next
meeting, with the language in there that the Council might entertain ranked choice voting. Then,
it would at least have the option of doing that. That gives the Council two weeks to do its
homework and be ready to make a final decision.

              Council Member Hull, seconded by Council Member Prokopis, moved to defer a
decision on Resolution 21-04-01 to the next meeting. The motion passed unanimously.

Resolution No. 21-04-02

               Paul Ashton reviewed the following resolution authorizing the Mayor to sign a
Land Purchase and Settlement Agreement with Salt Lake County and Darrell Sego for a flood
detention basin located at 7373 West 3500 South. The Council had asked that this matter be
deferred to Nathan Bracken, Legal Counsel, Greater Salt Lake Municipal Services District, to help
negotiate this with Mr. Sego.

DATE           TUESDAY                                  APRIL                            13, 2021

In order for the Magna Metro Township to buy this stormwater pond, it had to do a subdivision
and variance request. The various agencies were on board with it, although the Salt Lake County
Health Department wanted a note included in the subdivision plat that said water and sewer
services had not been verified for Lot 2, which is what the Magna Metro Township is buying So,
if there is a change in use requiring water and sewer services, a new plat map will have to be
submitted to the Health Department for approval. He reviewed some minor changes that the Salt
Lake County District Attorney’s Office had. The District Attorney’s Office will be taking it to the
County Council for approval on April 27, 2021. Mr. Sego has also agreed to this agreement and
the changes. He recommended the Council approve the resolution with the amendments to
reflect the recommendations of legal counsel.

RESOLUTION NO. 21-04-02                                                     Date: APRIL 13, 2021

    BASIN LOCATED AT 7373 W 3500 SOUTH

§§ 10-2a-401 et seq; and

easement to the municipality; and

therefore be it

attached agreement between Magna, Salt Lake County and Darrell Sego, Council Exhibit A
attached hereto, for the purchase of the property of the flood detention basin located at 7373 W
