 2. Discussion Topics
       a. Discussion on Proposed Amendments to the Landscape Ordinance Title 13 Chapter 13
          Council Member Whitelock said she had been working with the Mayor on proposed
          amendments to the Landscape Ordinance for some time, and noted that the most recent
          language had not been included in the agenda packet. Administrative Services Director Danyce
          Steck distributed physical copies of the most recent language. Council Member Whitelock said
          proposed amendments were drafted to increase enforceability.

          The Council discussed proposed amendments. Council Member Whitelock said she and the
          Mayor disagreed about trees in park strips. She said she believed trees in park strips were a
          problem. Mayor Burton expressed the opinion that trees in park strips made neighborhoods
          look better and feel better, and helped with traffic calming. He commented that if a driver
          went off the road and hit a tree, the tree prevented the vehicle from continuing further onto
          a property. He expressed support for providing the option, and said he and Council Member
          Whitelock had compromised with the following language: “one tree per twenty-five feet of
          frontage is required either in the park strip or within five feet of the sidewalk.”

          Council Member Jacob said he did not want to require or prohibit a tree in any given spot, and
          leaned toward encouraging specific types of trees that would not break up sidewalk or cause
          problems. City Planner Larry Gardner pointed out that the Water Efficiency Standards adopted
          by the Council did not allow sod in park strips, and expressed the opinion that some type of
          planting in park strips was needed.

       b. Property Tax Impact Schedule for FY2027
          Chair Bedore explained that due to new requirements under House Bill 236, taxing entities
          considering a property tax increase were encouraged to include a Property Tax Impact
          Schedule when discussing the budget. Although not required, the City was following a best
          practice from the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) by including the schedule whenever
          the budget was discussed. He noted that the Property Tax Impact Schedule was accepted on
          May 5, 2026, and was available to the public.

       c. Discussion of the Fiscal Year 2027 Tentative Budget
          Administrative Services Director Danyce Steck referenced guiding priorities for the West
          Jordan budget. She showed annual ending General Fund reserves since FY2020, with an
          anticipated FY2027 ending reserve of $22,663,959. She presented anticipated FY2027 General
          Fund revenue of $81,944,122, and presented anticipated property tax revenue and sales tax
          revenue.

          Ms. Steck said a population study released on May 8, 2026 showed West Jordan had grown by
          70 residents, and had not made the top 10 growth communities list. She said the Governor’s
          Office estimated a 3.9% sales tax revenue increase for the State, but emphasized that the City
          would not necessarily see the same level of sales tax growth. City Administrator Korban Lee
          said staff were not sure the growth number was accurate, and were working with ULCT to

          return on investment with the incentives, and Ms. Steck shared success stories.

          so many services that it cost residents a lot of money. Ms. Steck said current assistant staff in
          the Public Works building were very busy on a daily basis. She said they had tried utilizing a

          answered questions from the Council about City committees. He presented Non-
