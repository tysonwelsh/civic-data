     DISCUSSION – Storm Water Code Amendment

Mr. Tschirki introduced proposed changes to Orem City Code Chapter 23 pertaining to the City’s
Storm Water utility. He reviewed concepts and definitions pertinent to his presentation, and
provided a general explanation of the ways Orem managed storm water. Mr. Tschirki said HB226
from the 2020 Utah Legislative Session enacted a provision related to storm water permits and
certain appeals related to post-construction retention requirements. The legislation was also the
impetus for the reduction from the 90th to the 80th percentile storm event. He shared data related
to Orem’s rainfall events and exceedance intervals.

Mr. Tschirki said a five-year Storm Water Master Plan had been approved in 2016 which provided
guidance and examples to developers on managing and mitigating storm water quality and

        ANNUAL REPORT – Gang Loitering Areas

Chief Giles provided a brief report on the City’s Gang Loitering Areas. He said the Orem City
Council adopted specified gang loitering areas throughout the City in 2009; the areas defined
included city parks, public schools, and church buildings among others. He said they had not had
major gang issues for many years. Occasionally graffiti was found that “marked” territories, but
Chief Giles said it was not on such a scale as to worry. The Orem Police Department had tools in
place if necessary to subdue gang activity within the City.


        DISCUSSION – Law Enforcement Accountability Update

Chief Giles said recent national events let to questions and discussions regarding policing and law
enforcement as well as the Black Lives Matter protests in response to the killing of George Floyd
in Minneapolis, MN. He said many in the community had reached out to him regarding these
issues, and he wanted to share some of his thoughts and information from the Orem Police
Department (OPD). One idea that had come from the protests was to “Defund the Police”; Chief
Giles said this did not mean eliminating police departments entirely, but rather redistributing funds
to go to other non-police resources, like social workers and mental health advocates. He said Orem
was already doing some of these things, including employing two full-time mental health officers
and a part-time social worker, and felt the OPD was on the right path.

Chief Giles said he had received letters citing the “8 Can’t Wait” Campaign to bring change to
police departments. The eight points of the campaign are:
    1. Ban chokeholds and strangleholds
    2. Require de-escalation
    3. Require warning before shooting
    4. Require to exhaust all alternatives before shooting
    5. Duty to intervene
    6. Ban shooting at moving vehicles
    7. Require use of force continuum
    8. Require comprehensive reporting

Related to point two, Chief Giles said he had a goal for 100% of his officers to be Crisis
Intervention Team (CIT) certified. They were currently at about 80% certified, and the remaining

     ORDINANCE – Amending Sections 22-24-3(F)(1)(a) and 22-24-3(F)(2)(d) “Design
     Standards” of the Orem City Code, pertaining to accessory buildings in the Canyon Crossing
     District (CXD) zone

Mr. Bench presented the Planning Commission’s recommendation that the City Council, by
ordinance, amend Sections 22-24-3(F)(1)(a) and 22-24-3(F(2)(d) “Design Standards” of the Orem
City Code, pertaining to accessory buildings in the Canyon Crossing District (CXD) zone.

The Canyon Crossing District zone (encompassing the area around the intersection of 800 North
and State Street), like the other State Street district zones, contained design standards that were
intended to create an urban design feel and to maximize the development of properties in that zone.
Two of the main development requirements were that buildings must be brought up close to the
street with a maximum setback of ten feet and must also have a minimum of two stories.

As an exception to the above requirements, the current ordinance provided that one existing
building on a parcel may be expanded by up to 15% of the floor area of the building without
meeting the minimum height and maximum setback requirements. However, all other building
additions as well as new buildings must comply with the minimum height and maximum setback
requirements.

A property owner in the CXD zone recently expressed a desire to construct a large accessory
structure in the back of his lot and behind the existing buildings on the parcel. Upon consideration
of the request, staff realized that the current CXD zone did not allow the construction of an
accessory building for things like storage unless it complied with the minimum height and
maximum setback requirements. Since it would generally be impracticable to construct an
accessory building that met those requirements, staff believed that it was reasonable to allow an
accessory building in the CXD zone to be built in the rear of a lot even though it did not meet the
height and setback requirements. However, staff believed that the size of such an accessory
building should be limited in order to avoid encouraging the construction of new buildings in the
rear of a lot that ultimately may be devoted to primary uses since the intent of the ordinance is to
bring the primary uses out to the front of the lot.

As staff considered what size of accessory building would be reasonable to allow as an exception
to the height and setback requirements, staff looked to the exception that allowed a building to be
expanded by 15%, as referenced above, as an example. Staff therefore proposed to amend the CXD
zone to allow one accessory building that did not meet the minimum height and maximum setback
requirements to be constructed on a lot provided that the accessory building was no larger than
15% of the floor area of one primary building on the parcel. Staff believed that this would allow
property owners to meet their legitimate need for storage while still preventing primary buildings
or uses from being constructed in the rear of a lot.

Under the proposed amendment, one accessory structure would be allowed on a parcel in the CXD
zone provided it met the following:

   1.        A maximum area of 15% of the gross leasable floor area (GLFA) of the primary
             structure on the property.
   2.        A maximum height of one story.

          PUBLIC HEARING – Enterprise Fund Transfers

Mr. Nelson presented a recommendation that the City Council hold a public hearing to consider
input from Orem residents regarding proposed transfers from Enterprise Funds to the General Fund
in the Fiscal Year 2020-2021 Budget.

On May 12, 2020, the City Council received a draft of the Tentative Budget for the Fiscal Year
2020-2021. The budget contained a proposed transfer of funds from the utilities to cover non-
administrative or overhead costs to cover the City’s own use of City owned utilities. The State
Auditor required that cities pay the retail rate for their own use of city owned utilities. The City of
Orem used water, sewer, storm water and street lighting to supply its own properties. These
properties were primarily parks but also include the Cemetery, City Hall, Police Station, Fire
Stations, and other city properties. The City had long built the cost of supplying these properties
into the utility rates. The reasoning behind this decision was that the taxpayers and the utility rate
payers were almost always the same people. Therefore, the simplest way to cover these costs was
to include them in the respective utility rates. Now that the City was required to pay the utilities
for these services, the options available to the City were: 1) Increase property taxes to cover the

the funds back to the General Fund. The first two options involved either paying more for the same
services or paying the same for lower service. By making the transfer the City could provide the
same level of service for the same fees.

        RESOLUTION – Authorizing the Pick-Up of Specified Employee Contributions to the
        New Public Safety and Firefighter Tier II Contributory Retirement System

Mr. Davidson presented a recommendation that the City Council, by resolution, authorize the pick-
up of specified employee contributions to the New Public Safety and Firefighter Tier II
Contributory Retirement System.

Due to the cost of enhancements to the New Public Safety and Firefighter Tier II Contributory
Retirement System, participating City employees would now be required to contribute 2.27% of
their salary or wages to the retirement plan. An employer may “pick-up” the employee's portion
of the retirement contribution and treat it as an employer contribution under the Internal Revenue
Code. In the proposed resolution, the City agreed to pick-up the entire amount of the FY 2020/2021
employee contribution (2.27% of salary or wages) and committed to continue picking-up the
2.27% contribution in future years.

Mr. Macdonald received confirmation that this proposal was part of the budget that was approved
moments ago. Mr. Davidson said this was a change that came from legislation that enhanced the
retirement benefits extended to Tier II employees, an amount of approximately $150,000 for the
City of Orem. Mr. Peterson asked if other cities were covering this cost for their employees; Mr.
Davidson said there were some that were covering the cost and others that required employees to
cover the cost. Mr. Peterson said it was important to make the public safety and fire employees
aware of this, which Mr. Davidson said was the goal. Mayor Brunst said it was a competitive world
for public safety employees and he was glad they were offering something other cities may not.

Mayor Brunst moved, by resolution, to authorize the pick-op of specified employee contributions
to the new Public Safety and Firefighter Tier II Contributory Retirement System. Mr. Peterson
seconded the motion. Those voting aye: Richard F. Brunst, Jeff Lambson, Debby Lauret, Tom
Macdonald, Terry Peterson, David Spencer, and Brent Sumner. The motion passed.
