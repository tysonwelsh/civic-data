WORK SESSION

Discuss PCMC's Needs-Based Childcare Scholarship Program:
Michelle Downard, Strategic Initiatives Advisor, and Hans Jasperson, Nonprofit Services
Advisory Committee Staff Liaison, presented this item. Downard indicated there was a
request for childcare funding in the Emergent Needs Grant Program item that would be
voted on later today. She reviewed the history of the City subsidizing childcare to make
it more affordable for families in the workforce and for residents in the community. The
Council originally allocated $1 million which lasted two years and she noted those funds
were exhausted in December 2025. She felt to continue the program, the City would
need to allocate $655,000 annually. Downard indicated that Summit County contributed
almost $900,000 to help with childcare scholarships as well. There were also some bills
at the Legislature this session addressing childcare. She recommended that this come
back in the budget session so the Council could consider this funding with the City’s
other budgetary needs.

Downard broke down the funding and noted 10% of it went for provider incentives. She
wanted the Council to consider expanding that to 20%. She met with the school district
to learn how they expanded preschool in the district and she was impressed with all
they did in this area. The proposed 20% provider incentives would not include the
school district, but they would include for-profit and non-profit providers. She proposed
giving each provider $100 per month for each Park City enrolled child, and this would
replace the provider incentives for children receiving DWS childcare assistance. The
estimated total for the proposed incentives was $70,900 annually. Downard indicated
the Council could decide to implement the provider incentives retroactively or from
February 2026 going forward.

Jasperson reviewed the support the City provided to PC Tots, including free rent at the
library facility and public service contracts, which provided operational support to
nonprofits. In early 2025, PC Tots applied for a public service contract to support
teachers’ salaries, curriculum and class materials. The Nonprofit Services Advisory
Committee recommended a public service contract of $27,250 for FY25, and $50,000
for FY26 and FY27. The Council approved the FY25 amount but redirected the
remaining funds to the Emergent Needs Fund. The majority of Council wanted to
support PC Tots through the Childcare Scholarship program and noted if additional
support for PC Tots did not materialize, they could apply to the Emergent Needs Grant
Program for additional funding. At the time of this discussion, the eligibility requirements
and payment thresholds for Emergent Needs grants had not been established. In
December 2025, PC Tots applied for an Emergent Needs grant, and their request was
based on an unanticipated 26% healthcare insurance premium increase. The committee
separated PC Tots’ request from the other requests because of the unique background.

Jasperson stated the Nonprofit Services Advisory Committee was seeking clarification
on this issue. They offered two options: Option A – if childcare provider incentives are
approved, they recommend awarding $15,000 in a one-time emergent needs grant.
Option B- if Council does not approve childcare provider incentives, they recommend
awarding PC Tots a public service contract of $50,000 for FY26 and FY27. He noted the
provider incentive would help in the long-term, but PC Tots had this additional need. He
stated both options could be funded with the funds in this year’s budget and asked
whether the committee could approve one of the options for PC Tots or if the Council
had another option.

Downard indicated the City had the budget to fund childcare through the end of the
fiscal year if the Council desired. She also asked for Council direction on whether to
come back to discuss sunsetting or continuing the childcare program.

Council Member Parigian asked how the funding for providers equaled $70,900, to
which Downard stated the incentive would only be for providers located in the City
limits. Council Member Parigian asked how much a backflow preventer cost, which
some providers’ buildings needed, to which Downard stated $11,000 per commercial
building.

Council Member Toly asked to know more about the bill at the Legislature regarding
childcare. Downard stated the current one was mirroring the Big Beautiful Bill, (HB190)
which would expand support and give tax incentives to employers who supported their
employees with some type of childcare benefit. Generally, it was intended for large
employers that could build facilities onsite. It would build on top of the incentives found

high since 3-4 year olds were now attending preschool at the school district’s program.

made their fundraising lift higher at that time. Council Member Toly asked if PC Tots
could apply for another public service contract in the next go-around if the City awarded
the provider incentives and a one-time grant now. Jasperson stated that was what he
wanted the Council to weigh-in on.

1. Clark Ranch Discussion with the Planning Commission:
Christin Van Dine, Rick Shand, Grant Tilson, John Frontero, and Seth Beal, Planning
Commissioners, Jarrett Moe and Chris Day, Alexander Company, Sara Wineman,
Housing Project Manager, and Meredith Covey, Planner, were present for this item.
Mayor Dickey stated no vote would be taken tonight, but they would discuss where the
best location for the 10-acre development would be. Wineman reviewed the preferred
location would be chosen before they reinitiated the rezoning process with the Planning
Commission. Covey explained how the development would need to comply with the
code.

Day indicated the Alexander Company did a lot of work since last year on this project.
He displayed two 10-acre options within the 15 acres for the location of the
development. He noted they received good feedback at the Planning Commission
meeting in October, 2025, including the density and mass of the project needed to be
reduced significantly, the stormwater retention needed to be within the 10-acre
boundary, and the development should be compatible with PC Heights next door. There
weren’t many parameters for building length and number of units as well as building
height, so they set a baseline of metrics to show tonight. They reduced the project to lie
fully within 10-acres, including stormwater retention, and reduced massing and density.
The two options were the Triangle option and the Buffer option. With Triangle, the
terrain was marginally flatter and there was reduced visibility from the commuter
vantage. The constraints to this site included a higher cost per unit, more design
challenges, reduced unit count, limited views/marketability, more road and infrastructure
needs, increased risk of flooding, and more site disturbance.

The Buffer option would have a lower cost per unit, greater flexibility in site design and
programming, more efficient road layout, more efficient stormwater management,
improved use in relation to protected recreational open space, and less site disturbance.
The constraints to this option included slightly steeper slopes and more visibility from a
commuter vantage.

Day displayed building footprints for the sites. For building heights, they modeled 28
feet and 35 feet, which was comparable to PC Heights. Commissioner Shand asked
why Triangle was a higher unit cost if it was flatter. Day stated there was additional road
infrastructure and not a lot of flexibility for stormwater storage.
