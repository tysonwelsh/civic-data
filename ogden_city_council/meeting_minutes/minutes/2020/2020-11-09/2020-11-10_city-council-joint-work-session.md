Fiscal Year 2020-2021 Budget Amendment - Utility Bond Proceeds
          Comptroller Stout explained the City finalized refunding and issuing new utility bonds for 2020. This action included
refunding various bonds, in the amount of$21,63 8,309, for water and storm sewer utility projects. Refunding bonds is paying off
higher-cost bonds through the issuance of new bonds that have lower net-costs to the city. A bond refund is similar to refinancing
a home for a lower interest rate. This also included the issuance of $44,000,000 in new bonds for construction and system
improvements in Water, Sanitary Sewer and Storm Sewer, as outlined in the Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) and utility master
plans. This proposal recognizes revenues from refunding and issuing water, sanitary and stonn sewer utility bonds, and allows the
City to record bond-related activities to the City's general ledger.
          Council Policy Analyst Mabey reviewed the proposed timeline for action on the proposed budget amendment; a public
hearing can be scheduled for December 1, 2020, after which the Council can vote on whether to approve the amendment.

Proposed Amendment to the Definition of Linear Block
         A petition has been filed by Thaine Fischer to amend the definition of a linear block. The amendment would change the
definition of a linear block to include only one side of a street rather than both sides. The intent of the amendment is to allow
additional drinking establishments within the downtown area, particularly on 25 th Street. The Planning Commission has

recommended denial of the petition. The Council reviewed the petition at its April 7, 2020 work session. The Council requested
additional information at that meeting. The additional infonnation will be presented and discussed at this work session.
           Planning Manager Montgomery used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the implications of the
proposed amendments; this matter has been discussed by the Planning Commission and City Council several times over the past
five to 10 years. Most recently, the Planning Commission recommended denial of the petition and the Council discussed that
recommendation during an April 2020 work session meeting. The current petition is to remove the linear block definition
pertaining to alcoholic beverage regulations. The linear block defmition was a business license provision adopted in 1963 and later
incorporated into zoning regulations in 1981. Section 15-2-13 of the City Code includes the defmition; a linear block is defined as
both sides of a block and is used as a requirement for nonowner occupied vacation rentals, food trucks in the right-of-way, duplex
lots in the R-2 zone, and drinking establishments. Linear blocks are used for drinking establishments as a conditional use in the C-
3, CBD, MRD, M-1, M-2, and BER zones. No more than two drinking establishments are allowed per linear block. During review
of a conditional use permit the Planning Commission considers location of parking, adjacent uses and parking conflicts, and hours
of operation for compatibility with other uses. Concerns about impacts of concentration of uses include noise, disorderly conduct,
spill over effect of high concentrations, other activities caiTied outside, and hours of operation. 25 th Street is the only area with
nonconforming conditions; in 1992 there were 10 taverns or clubs on 25 th Street, but today there are only five. However, there are
two taverns/clubs that front onto Lincoln A venue and are not addressed on 25 th Street; therefore, they were not considered in the
evaluation of this petition. During the April 2020 discussion of this issue the Council requested current information regarding
taverns, bars, and drinking establishments; input from the Convention and Visitors Bureau; input from the Police Depaitment and
calls for service on 25 th Street; and possible options that would apply to the Central Business District (CBD). Since that time, the
Council has approved an ordinance impacting drinking establishments, which are defmed as an establishment where the sale of
alcohol exceeds the sale of food. The State will license a use as a bar if their food sales are less than 70 percent, but for City
zoning purposes, only drinking establishments are subject to the conditional use regulations and the limitation of two per linear
block provision. Currently there are 19 full-service restaurants in Ogden, 14 of those in the CBD; there are 20 bars, 11 in of which
are in the CBD. Mr. Montgomery discussed the differences between a restaurant and bar; a tavern is a beer only bar and a bar
must have food available, but patrons ai·e not required to order food in order to be able to purchase alcohol. Restaurants must earn
at least 70 percent of their sales from food. The CVB has indicated they feel Ogden's downtown does not have a vibrant nightlife
and this is because of the lack of establishments or higher-end bars and restaurants where a drink can be obtained without
purchasing food. However, the CVB wants to preserve a feeling of safety for patrons and visitors in all areas of the City. The
Police Depaitment has indicated that their highest call volume from bars on 25 th Street are from Friday to Sunday from 10:00 p.m.
to 1:00 a.m.; the nature of the calls relate to assaults, disturbances, and fights. He presented a chart illustrating trends in calls for
service comparing date ranges 2012-2015 with 2016-2020. The City's Police Chief has indicated that bars cause issues for the
City; the Police cannot stop the problems before they happen, but they will clean up afterwards. Mr. Montgomery stated that
Planning staff has developed multiple options for the Council to consider as they move towards taking action on the petition. The
first option would be to revise provisions to allow two - or another number deemed appropriate - drinking establishments per
each side of the street on 25 th Street, but corner lots would count towards the total number for both 25 th Street and the street they
face, i.e. Lincoln or Grant Avenues. The second option would allow for two or more drinking establishments on each size of the
street for properties carrying the CBDI zoning designation. This option would not eradicate the requirement for drinking
establishments to be a certain distance from community locations (churches or schools). The third option would be to add a
provision that bars that close at 11 :00 p.m. would not be considered in the two per linear block limitation. This would be added to
the present regulation that if more than 50 percent ofan establishment's revenue come from food, they ware not included in the
count of two per block. The final option is to change the number of drinking establishments allowed per each side of the street on
the 25u, Street block or in the Central Business District Intensive (CBDI) zone and also note that a bar closing at 11 :00 p.m. is not
considered in the tally of two per linear block.
           Chair Choberka inquired as to the options available to the Council that would not require the matter be referred back to

CARES Act Grant Funding
         Council member Hyer indicated he will recuse himself from participating in discussion of this issue as he is a small
business owner in the community who has applied for Coronavirus Aid, Relief and Economic Security (CARES) Act funding.
         Fiscal Year (FY) 2020-2021 Budget Amendment and Addendum to Interlocal Agreement with Weber County: The
City Council will be considering a proposed budget amendment to recognize and appropriate $6,612,850 in additional CARES
Act funding.
         Comptroller Stout used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the proposed budget amendment; revenue
sources include a CARES Act grant from the State of Utah in the amount of $2,312,850 and a CARES Act grant from Weber
County in the amount of $4,300,000. Acceptance and pass through of the County funds requires an addendum to the Interlocal
Agreement that was initially approved in September 2020. She briefly reported on the total amounts of CARES Act funds the City
has received thus far from the State ($7.5 million) and County ($7.3 million) and indicated that the total of$14.8 million has been
divided to cover City purposes and business/nonprofit grants. The City has awarded $3.3 million to nonprofit entities and $6.3
million to small businesses, with remaining funds of $1.6 million to award. The Community and Economic Development
Department has dedicated a great deal of time and effort to work with applicants for the funds and, to date, has awarded money to
44 nonprofits and 190 small businesses. The average award is $76,000 for nonprofits and $33,000 for small businesses. She then
provided a breakdown of how the money set aside for City purposes will be allocated; $1.4 million will be spent on public safety
hazard pay and $2.8 million will cover projects and equipment purchases that have been deemed necessary to respond to the
COVID-19 pandemic.
         Council member Stephens asked if the City was required to match any of the CARS Act grant funds it received. Ms.
Stout answered no; all monies were federal dollars with no match required.
