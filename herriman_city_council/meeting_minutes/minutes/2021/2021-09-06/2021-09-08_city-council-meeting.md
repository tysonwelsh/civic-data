    2.1. Discussion regarding the use of form pioneering agreements in Herriman City and a specific
          request for a pioneering agreement between Herriman City and Riverside Development, LLC -
          Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi reported the pioneering agreement was a tool used in many cities to reimburse
developers for costs of installing improvements such as water, storm drain, roads, etc. beyond their lawful
obligation to construct. Herriman used this tool in the past; however, there are no formal procedures in place
for addressing new requests for pioneering agreements. Adoption of a form pioneering agreement would allow
staff to equitably and consistently administer reimbursement to developers within the City. It would allow the
City to request improvements in deficient areas.This type of agreement could allow for improvements for future
development in accordance with the capital facilities plan, impact fee facilities plan or other land use element of
the General Plan.

Attorney Andrizzi explained challenges associated with a pioneering agreement. He offered an alternative
approach to consider the reimbursement agreement on a case-by-case basis. He reported the city recently
received a request from Riverside Development to enter into a Pioneering Agreement for certain improvements
constructed in connection with the Valhalla Subdivision. While the scope of the pioneering agreement for this
development had not been finalized, it would include improvements to abutting properties not owned by the
developer. The property owner would be required to install the improvements once receiving development
approval from the City.

Councilmember Ohrn relayed her support to move forward with the form of having a pioneering agreement.
Community Development Director Blake Thomas recognized how often these agreements are utilized for the
installation of improvements. Attorney Andrizzi indicated he would prepare a form back to a future work
meeting.

    2.3. Discussion regarding a proposed Public Infrastructure District Governing Document for the
          Olympia Project - Chase Andrizzi, City Attorney
City Attorney Chase Andrizzi said the current draft development agreement for the Olympia project anticipated
the developer would pay for all on-site improvements, including public infrastructure. Additionally, the
developer would also pay its proportionate share of off-site impacts to public infrastructure. As consideration
for this commitment, the developer requested the City Council consider adopting a public infrastructure district
(PID) as established in Utah Code. The Olympia finance working group met with the developer and
September 8, 2021 – City Council Minutes                                                                   Page 3 of 12
representatives of D.A. Davidson to discuss how a PID might work in the Olympia project that was not based
upon a recurring property tax levy. D.A. Davidson confirmed the PID could secure bonds using a one-time fee
paid at the time the building permit is issued for each unit within the project. This one-time fee would be paid
by the builder directly to the PID. The PID would then certify to the City the fee had been paid, whereupon the
City could issue the appropriate permit. The PID governing document is the instrument which created the PID
and directed operations. The City Council has absolute discretion on whether to approve a governing document.
There were certain statutory requirements about what must be in a governing document. Attorney Andrizzi
presented a map of the proposed PID boundaries and annexation area. The proposed governing document
would oversee the development of seven PIDs. They would be used to align financing with the phasing of the
development for greater efficiency as the development unfolded. He explained if development occurred faster
than anticipated, the unused districts could be dissolved as outlined in the governing documents.

Attorney Andrizzi continued with the information no impact fee would be collected as it would be a part of the
contract fee and compared it to the requirements of having an assessment. Councilmember Henderson asked
what constituted an assessment. Attorney Andrizzi explained this was the maximum amount to be considered
and matched current estimated impact fees. He noted the developer may only take out a fraction of the bonding
capacity; however, acknowledged there could be unforeseen challenges.

It was noted there would be a public hearing to receive public input at the September 22, 2021 meeting.

    9.1. Discussion and consideration of an ordinance amending flag lot regulations within Title 10 of the
          Herriman City Code (File No. Z2021-053) - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy offered a timeline of the flag-lot amendment. He offered an overview of the current
ordinance which, when adopted in 2017, intended to increase restriction and reduce flag-lot development and
added additional standards. He provided arguments for and against altering the regulations to allow additional
flag-lot development.

Planner Maloy reviewed the proposed title changes for flag-lot development. Minimum Requirements: A flag
lot shall meet the following minimum requirements:
     1. The staff portion of a flag lot shall front on and be contiguous to a dedicated local public street. Flag
         lots shall not front on a collector or arterial street.
     2. The minimum width of the staff portion of a flag lot shall be twenty-five feet (2025') to accommodate
         the required drivable surface, drive approach, utilities, fencing, and landscaping as needed. On properties
         where the length of the staff is greater than one hundred fifty feet (150'), the width of the staff shall be
         no less than twenty-five feet (25') of drivable surface unless otherwise may be increased if recommended
September 8, 2021 – City Council Minutes                                                             Page 9 of 12
       by the City Engineer and Fire Marshal authority having jurisdiction for fire services and approved by the
       Planning Commission.
   3. The maximum length of the staff portion of a flag lot shall be two hundred twenty feet (220') unless
       otherwise approved by the Planning Commission upon recommendation of the unified fire authority
       authority having jurisdiction for fire services.
   4. The flag lot shall have a hard-surfaced driveway from a public street to the required parking area for the
       flag lot. Adequate provisions shall be made for driveway drainage of the driveway, such as curb and
       gutter, landscaped berms bermings, or swales, and fencing.
   5. A flag lot shall not be created as part of a subdivision where there are more than two (2) lots in the
       subdivision or from an illegally divided lot or parcel.
   6. A flag lot shall not gain access via an easement on an adjacent property.
   7. No building or construction, except for driveways, shall be allowed on the staff portion of a flag lot
       unless the minimum width thereof is the same or greater than the minimum width for a lot as allowed
       by the zone in which the flag lot is located (excluding entrance features and streetlights).
   8. The front side of the flag portion yard of a flag lot shall be deemed to be that side nearest to the dedicated
       public street upon which the staff portion fronts, unless otherwise determined by staff on a case-by-case
       basis the Planning Commission.
   9. The staff portion of a flag lot shall be deemed to end, and the flag portion of the lot shall be deemed to
       commence, at the extension of the front lot line.
   10. The square footage of a flag lot shall be at least one-half (1/2) acre. The square footage located in the
       flag portion of the lot, which shall be exclusive of the square footage located in the staff portion of the
       lot, shall be at least one-third (1/3) acre.
   11. The front, side, and rear yard requirements of the flag portion of a flag lot shall be the same as required
       by the zone in which the lot is located.
   12. Only one single family dwelling shall be allowed on a flag lot.
   13. The approved building envelope of a flag lot shall be illustrated upon the final subdivision plat.
   14. A six-foot (6') solid vinyl privacy fence shall be installed on all sides of a flag lot, including along both
       sides of the staff portion of the lot, subject only to the requirements of section 10-21-3 of this title
       regarding front yard fencing. Planning Commission may remove or modify this standard to avoid double
       fencing, preserve mature vegetation, or maintain clear views for public safety.
   15. All applicable street improvements, including curb, gutter, and sidewalk, shall be installed along the front
       of both the flag lot and the lot or parcel from which it is being subdivided.

The purpose of the amendments would clarify the intent and language of existing City Code. Allow a flag-lot on

    9.2. Discussion and consideration of an ordinance removing group dwellings as a conditional use
         from the Forestry Recreation Zone as regulated in the Land Use Table of Herriman City Code Title
         10 (File No. Z2021-056) - Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Maloy spoke on the modification of the FR zone and pointed out an inconsistency of land use
within the Forestry Recreation (FR) zone. He relayed the Planning Commission recommendation to approve
the change.

City Planner Maloy reviewed the purpose statement for the forestry recreation zone permitting development of
canyon and hillside areas for forestry, recreation, and other uses to the extent such development is compatible
with protection of the natural and scenic resources of these areas for the continued benefit of future generations.
Based on the purpose statement and maximum development density, development intensity in the FR Zone is
relatively low. Furthermore, City Code 10-11-7 does not allow planned developments in the FR Zone, which
prevented clustering residential density. He recommended removing the group dwelling use from the land use
table as it was incompatible with the applicable development regulations within the FR zone.

groups as a conditional use from the FR (Forestry Recreation) Zone. Councilmember Smith seconded the motion.
