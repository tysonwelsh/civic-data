referencing Paragraph 7d, which is addressed in Paragraph 8. He expressed issues with language
pertaining to going out to the open market and seeking protection, which would allow the City to
repurchase the property under the terms anticipated. It was not that an appraised value is less than

        a.     Discussion on Golf Carts.

involved the legality of golf carts on City streets. Ms. Chamness explained that golf cart use on
streets is permitted and requires a driver’s license. During the last Legislative Session it was
clarified that a person may not operate a golf cart on the highway unless authorized by the
municipality in which the highway is located. She emphasized that if the City does not specifically
permit golf carts, they may not legally operate on the streets. Mr. Godfrey recalled that both Title
41(a) and the State Transportation Act define a highway as any street. Assuming that is true, Ms.

in looking at a modification to public ordinances that prohibit golf carts within the City.

Council Member Fotheringham expressed concern with underage drivers operating golf carts but
was not overly anxious to create an ordinance. Council Member Petersen agreed with the concern
regarding unsafe practices and underage drivers. She did not have an issue with adults or licensed
drivers but believed that the youth with no supervision are causing the safety issues. Mr. Godfrey

        b.      Holladay @20 Review.
Mayor Dahle reported that the Citizen Advisory Group made a presentation to the Council in May.
He believed the Council was wise to delay the matter to next year with the commitment that the
findings of the Citizen Advisory Group were valid. He explained that the conversation turned to
timing as they look for guidance from the Council on whether they wish to bifurcate the storm drain
issue and the tax increase or do them together.

Assistant to the City Manager, Holly Smith presented the following options for the Council’s
consideration:

   •   Educate and revisit work done with the Citizens Advisory Group this fall. Staff will prepare
       messaging materials, revisit the financial model, and use the time between now and the first
       of the year to review, reeducate, and prepare. Another mailer would be sent out with a
       survey opportunity and information on Town Hall Meetings. Citizen input could also be
       collected to allow for discussion and a potential decision by April 2021. The City would
       then be ready for the FY 2021-2022 budget leading into the Truth in Taxation process.

   •   In the event of a resurgence of COVID-19 cases, engagement in the fall may be difficult. As
       a result, some messaging may not be in place and timing may not be beneficial. Town Hall
       Meetings could be held in the fall with an Open House in February or March allowing in-
       person meetings before the Council makes a final decision.

   •   Should the Council wish to pursue the Storm Water Fee this fall, staff would have it in place
       by January 2021 and deal with the property tax increase in the Spring of 2021. An Open
       House was recommended in October, however, it could be challenging with the current

       COVID-19 situation. The benefit was that collection of the fee could take place sooner and
       still do Truth in Taxation in August 2021 with collection in November 2021.

Council Member Gibbons was in favor of the first option. He believed there were budget issues
that are equally pressing that will be a factor in the ultimate decision and include sales tax issues as
a result of COVID-19 that could potentially be impacted by the Unified Police Department (“UPD”)
situation. He stated that there may be even more compelling reasons to consider a tax increase
aside from the capital improvements. Ms. Smith explained that the financial model includes all of
the components and is a comprehensive evaluation of the City’s resources and expenditures. If it is
the desire of the Council, they could create education pieces or provide input if there are changes or
other items related to public safety or sales tax revenue.

Council Member Fotheringham questioned whether it makes sense to separate the two pieces. He
explained that the storm water issue could be dealt with more independently and the property tax
piece could be addressed in the spring. He had not formed an opinion but recognized it as a
potential opportunity. Mayor Dahle stated that they budgeted for a 10% decrease in sales tax and
there will be other issues associated with Utah Retirement Systems (“URS”). He understood that
the storm drain piece is not time restrictive like the property tax portion and could be managed in a
COVID-19 environment. He wondered if they should start the storm drain piece and then roll out
the educational piece in January and February.

Ms. Smith explained that the Storm Water Fee is straight forward. Property tax would require an
explanation of how residents will be impacted by the increase.

Ms. Chamness recalled that the previous Council struggled with this issue when it was discussed
two years earlier. There was concern regarding the implementation of a fee to be followed within
six months by a property tax increase and the appearance of not being transparent. She believed
there were good arguments for both options and the decision depends on the Council. Mayor Dahle
suggested that if they decide to proceed with the Storm Water Fee, that it be moved fairly through
the public process. He understood that the Storm Water Fee is easier to implement than a property
tax increase but emphasized the importance of taking it through in the current virtual format.
Ms. Chamness reported that Millcreek City has gone through a similar process and adopted a fee of
$9.81 per Equivalent Residential Unit (“ERU”). She confirmed that the Millcreek City Council
voted unanimously to support the imposition of a fee likely to start in January 2020.

Council Member Fotheringham explained that the fact that the monies cannot be used other than for
storm water is a huge factor. He believed there was a demonstrable need for storm water monies.
With the City currently using savings, he was in favor of the storm water piece moving forward.
Mayor Dahle stated that the consensus was to begin the Storm Water Fee process and consider the
property tax piece after the first of the year. Ms. Chamness wondered if it would be helpful to have
Fred Philpot provide the Storm Water Fee model. Mayor Dahle agreed with having Mr. Philpot
give a more comprehensive overview at the August 20 meeting.

        c.      Discussion of CARES Act Budget.
Ms. Chamness reported that the City received $906,000 from Salt Lake County as part of their
direct allocation “CARES” Act funding to address the impacts of COVID-19. She noted that the
allocations must be spent prior to the end of September and there are certain guidelines that the City
must operate within.

Ms. Smith stated that a survey was issued where the City utilized approximately 600 emails from
the business licensing database of which 97 responses were collected. Responses by industry were
listed with dental, hair salons, and mental health professionals being listed under the “Other”
category. Questions and responses included the following:

   •   The number of employees and customers served on a weekly basis. It was determined that
       approximately 4,500 were served between the 92 businesses or 50 people per week.

   •   The type of changes or closures that were experienced. Many reported reduced staff and
       operating hours.

   •   Upticks in expenses or changes made to do business in the COVID-19 environment. Many
       experienced an increase in Personal Protection Equipment (“PPE”) and cleaning supplies.
       70% did not report a change.

   •   Grants or loans received. Approximately 50% received some form of funding.

   •   The amount received. The amount awarded varied between $10,000 and $250,000.

   •   Where businesses need the most help. The majority reported that rent payments are the area
       where they are struggling the most.

   •   Those that answered ‘no’ to receiving funds were asked to respond to the type of help
       needed. Rent, payroll, PPE, and hand sanitizer were all needed.

Ms. Chamness reported that because it is a grant, the funding was paid directly to the County. The
County then needs to ensure compliance with all CARES Act regulations. The City of Holladay
signed an agreement specifying that they are responsible for how the money is spent. Council
Member Fotheringham expressed concern with the City being held responsible should funds be
misspent by those receiving the grant. Ms. Chamness confirmed that if money was spent
improperly and there was an audit, the City would need to reimburse the County for any funds that
are spent improperly. Ms. Chamness recommended that any grant process be rigorous and ensure
compliance. She would explore whether the County is willing to act as an agent on the City’s
behalf.

Ms. Chamness next reviewed the proposed $906,000 budget split between this fiscal year and last
fiscal year. Guidance was provided by the Treasury on July 8 that will change the staff time figures
from last year. She confirmed that staggered shift times will not count toward. She assumed that
$87,000 in staff time could be used, $14,000 in physical improvements made to City Hall,
plexiglass in the Court Room and the Planning Department, and new tables in the Mount Olympus
Room allowing them to social distance during public meetings. Technology expenditures were

        e.     Discussion on Holladay Boulevard Speed.
Mayor Dahle reported that this is a continuation of the previous discussion regarding lowering the
speed limit along Holladay Blvd between the Village and Cottonwood Elementary. A Traffic Study
was conducted and there was not a consensus from the Council for reducing the speed limit to 35
MPH.

Council Member Gibbons stated that the Traffic Study conducted by Dr. Joe Perrin in April
included taking data from three locations. The study utilized the average speed and the 85th
percentile to determine a number. He described a sight distance and grade issue near the elementary
school caused by the blind corner. Roadside development, culture, and roadside friction was the
second condition and included parents picking up children and students walking to and from school.
A third factor involved safe speeds for curves, hazardous conditions, pedestrian activity, and
parking practices.

Council Member Fotheringham believed there was cause for reducing the speed limit to 35 MPH
from the Village Center to the end of the school zone on the south side of Cottonwood Elementary.
He strongly recommended that the speed limit be reduced to 35 MPH from the Village Center to the
southernmost school zone.

Council Member Petersen reported that she had not received Dr. Perrin’s Traffic Study Report and
was not prepared to discuss or comment on it. She believed the decision should be based on facts.

Council Member Quinn was concerned as she has grandchildren who attend Cottonwood
Elementary. She supported the speed limit reduction.

Council Member Durham was in favor of a speed limit reduction. Ms. Chamness confirmed that
the consensus of the Council was to pursue the speed limit reduction and stated that signage could
be posted in the next few weeks.

Mayor Dahle recommended that Council Member Petersen be given an opportunity to review the
report and suggested that the matter be tabled to the August 6 Work Session.
