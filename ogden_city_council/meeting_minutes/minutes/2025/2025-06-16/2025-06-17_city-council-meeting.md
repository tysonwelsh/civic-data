Proposed Ordinance 2025–16 approving the Capital Improvement Plan for
Ogden City for the Fiscal Years 2026–2030
         A memo from the Management Services Department regarding the proposed Fiscal Year 2026-2030 Capital
Improvement Plan (CIP) came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the CIP is reviewed and adopted each year
as a planning tool for major capital investments in the city’s physical plant over a rolling five-year period. Each year’s plan is
updated based on the changing needs of the city, funding opportunities, projects completed and other similar factors. During this
year’s budget process, proposed capital projects will be reviewed alongside each department’s proposed budget and in context
with the overall needs and resources available to the city through the budget process.
         Council Senior Policy Analyst Burton summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide
Council staff’s analysis if the proposed CIP; he defined a capital improvement project as follows:
              • Projects exceeding $50,000 that add to the city’s physical plant:
                       o Includes design and/or construction.
                       o Includes public buildings, streets, utility lines, parks, and other public facilities.
              • Projects exceeding $50,000 for major rehabilitation or reconstruction of the physical plant
              • Projects resulting in the acquisition or long-term lease of land
              • Projects which require debt financing or borrowing
              • Public facilities that may be financed in whole or in part by the imposition of impact fees
         Funding sources for CIP projects include the General Fund, Federal and State grants, B&C Road funds/Active
Transportation funds, Enterprise Funds, Bonds, and other miscellaneous grant programs, partnerships, fundraisers, and
Redevelopment Agency funds. Mr. Burton presented the proposed CIP review schedule, with adoption of the CIP possibly
occurring on June 17, 2025 following a public hearing. The Planning Commission has reviewed the proposed CIP and has
forwarded a determination of compliance; projects that were included in the proposed CIP that were determined not to be
compliant were:
              •     Project EN017 – 1100 North and Monroe Boulevard Traffic Signal. Mt. Lewis Plan calls for a roundabout at
                    this intersection. The Commission is recommending an evaluation and if a traffic signal is preferred that the
                    plans be updated.
               • Project RG010 – Community Field House. No location has been determined so General Plan consistency
                    cannot be determined at this time.
          Mr. Burton noted there are 92 projects included in the five-year CIP, 27 of which are new, for a total funding proposal of
$249,362,741. He noted 42 of the projects are perpetual projects, 45 are one-time projects, and five are Recreation, Arts, Museum,
Parks (RAMP) grant projects. The CIP includes a funding proposal of $13,620,300 for the General Fund in the FY26 budget.
Funding proposals for all other funds for FY26 total $29,722,450. He presented a spreadsheet that provided a more detailed
review of the projects included in the proposed CIP; the spreadsheet included:
               • Total cost by Department

               • Total Project Cost

               • Future Funding Planned

Public Hearing regarding transfers from the Water, Sanitary Sewer, Storm
Sewer, and Refuse Funds to the City’s General Fund
          A memo from Council staff regarding proposed Enterprise Fund transfers came before the Council for consideration.
The memo stated the Council will accept public input on the proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026 transfers from the Water, Sanitary
Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse Utility Funds to the General Fund. The proposed budget includes transfers of $3,475,925 from
Water, $1,835,650 from Sanitary Sewer, $799,425 from Storm Sewer, and $935,700 from Refuse. The transfers represent the
equivalent of franchise and property taxes paid by non-governmental utilities.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound
on the proposed Enterprise Fund transfers; Utah Code Annotated §10-6-135.5 requires that notice be sent to all users when a City
is proposing to transfer funds from an enterprise fund to another fund for purposes “not directly related to the goods or services
provided by the enterprise for which the enterprise fund was created.” Ogden City’s notice is posted on the state’s Public Notice
Website and included with the city’s utility bills. The city’s utility billing system works on a four-week rotation with one quarter
of the users receiving their bill each week. Noticing began in early May and will conclude in early June. This ensures that all
utility users received notice at least one week prior to the hearing scheduled for June 17, 2025. For a copy of the presentation in
its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Chair White acknowledged some concerns the Council has heard regarding the proposed Enterprise Fund transfers and
reported that Council staff will be conducting some research into how other cities are handling their Enterprise Fund transfers to
determine if Ogden’s practices are in line with other communities. Ms. Eller-Smith stated she has already reviewed data for other
cities and found that Provo City’s transfer equates to 20 percent of the total enterprise fund expenditures, while Ogden’s equates to
12 percent.
            Council member Blair asked Ms. Eller-Smith to expound on the slide in her presentation that indicates the savings on the
utility rates (if the transfers were eliminated) would be less than the additional property taxes required to replace the funds
involved in the transfer. Ms. Eller-Smith stated that according to the LRB study, if the City were to eliminate the transfer and
reduce utility rates, but increase property tax to replace the funds involved in the transfer, taxpayers would actually be paying
more out of pocket. This is because not all property owners in Ogden City pay property taxes, but all property owners do pay
utility rates. Non-profit entities that own properties in Ogden are exempt from paying property tax, but they do pay utility rates.
            Chair White then opened the public hearing.
            Travis Pate stated he has questions about the administrative fee that is included in the Enterprise Fund transfers; he asked
if the administrative fee could be used to perform an audit of infrastructure in the community and determine any opportunities for
using any other entity’s infrastructure in the event of a natural disaster or emergency. He added he has put in a work order to ask
the City to reinstate a non-emergency dispatch line, which was eliminated in recent years.
            Joseph DeGraffenried stated that he received a letter regarding the proposed Enterprise Fund transfers; he pays
approximately $123 per month for water, but his electricity bill is usually around $20 or $30 per month. He was surprised by how
high his water bill was and that caused him to question the Enterprise Fund transfers. He feels property taxes are actually
reasonable, but his utility bill is not. He has a better understanding of the reason for the transfers after listening to Ms. Eller-
Smith’s presentation, but he still has concerns about the cost he is paying for water he does not use.
            Kerry Wangsgard stated he cannot recall when the City started making transfers from the Enterprise Fund to the General
Fund, but he does not believe it has been longer than 10 years. When he prepares his personal budget each year, he determines
how much money will be used for certain costs, such as his mortgage or other housing costs. If he believes he has a large amount
of money set aside for those costs but decides to use some of that money for a trip to Hawaii, this could result in insufficient funds
in the future to cover unexpected costs. He feels this is what the City is doing with the Enterprise Fund transfers and he does not
feel it is appropriate money management; money designated for a certain purpose should be used for that purpose. He understands
there are administrative fees for managing the utility departments, but those should be included in the budget for those
departments and fees should be set to cover those expenses. He feels continuing with the Enterprise Fund transfers is ‘creative
financing’ and it denies the City the ability to make an argument for a property tax increase if one is truly needed.
            Heath Satow stated he visited with Ms. Eller-Smith and representatives of City Administration; the transfer actually
makes some sense to him, especially given that replacing the money with property tax would require a significant increase.
However, he feels the City should explore opportunities for charging non-profit entities similar to all other property owners in the
community.
            Debra Darrington stated she is irritated each month when she receives her utility bill; she owns a property that has a
commercial line running into it, but the property is not being used for commercial purposes. The water rate she pays for the line is
$228 per month regardless of usage. Depending on usage, her bill can be anywhere from $600 to $800 during the summer months.
Past owners of the property have eventually declared bankruptcy as a result. She stated she has another residential property that
has recently been xeriscaped and requires minimal water, but weeds have begun growing in the xeriscape. She has noticed many
others are in a similar situation or they have not watered their lawns because they cannot afford their bills in the summer. She is
not sure why water rates in Ogden City are so high, but she implored the Council to explore opportunities for lowering rates.
            There being no further persons appearing to be heard, VICE CHAIR HYER MOVED THE PUBLIC HEARING BE
CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS, ALL VOTING AYE.
            Chair White noted that the Council is not taking an action on this matter tonight, but she did want to try to answer some
of the questions asked during the public hearing. She asked how long the City has been conducting the Enterprise Fund transfers.
Ms. Eller-Smith stated the State Law governing the transfers was changed in 2017 and that is when the City was required to notify
residents of the transfers; however, the Enterprise Fund transfers have been occurring for at least 25 years and probably more. The
City has always used the same percentages – six percent property tax, six percent franchise tax, and five percent administrative fee
– to calculate the transfer amounts. Chair White stated she would like for Council staff and Administrative staff to take the input
they have received tonight under advisement when considering future water rate adjustments and base rates depending on water
meter sizes. Vice Chair Hyer echoed Chair White’s suggestion.
            Council member Choberka added she supports Mr. Pate’s suggestion for an audit of City infrastructure.

Fiscal Year 2025–2026 Compensation Increases for Executive Municipal
Officers. Public Hearing on proposed increases for all city executive officers
          A memo from Council staff regarding Executive Municipal Officer compensation came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated during the 2024 legislative session, the Utah State Legislature adopted S.B.91 requiring that a
municipal governing body proposing compensation increases for executive municipal officers provide notice of and hold a public
hearing on the proposed increase. The proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026 Budget includes a proposed Cost of Living Adjustment
(COLA) of 2.97 percent salary increase for all public safety and general city employees, which includes executive municipal
officers. General employees and executive officers are eligible for a merit increase of 1.5 percent.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and stated the term “Municipal Executive Officers”
includes the following positions in Ogden City:
              • Chief Administrative Officer and Assistant Chief Administrative Officers
              • City Attorney and Deputy City Attorney(s)
              • Department Head and Deputy Department Head(s)
              • Division Manager and Deputy Division Manager
               • Chief and Deputy/Assistant Chief(s)
               • Public Safety Leadership
         Ms. Eller Smith then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to summarize the findings of a compensation study
conducted by McGrath, which has informed the proposed salary adjustments included in the proposed Fiscal Year 2025-2026
budget. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
         Council member Choberka stated she believed the merit increase had been increased to two percent rather than 1.5
percent. Ms. Eller-Smith stated that is correct and noted that all documents needed to facilitate the increase include a reference to
two percent rather than 1.5 percent; the fiscal impact of the 0.5 percent increase was approximately $200,000 and that dollar
amount is reflected in the proposed budget.
         Chair White then opened the public hearing.
         Kerry Wangsgard stated that in his private employment, he would often receive COLAs, but they were very small.
Giving managers authority to grant a smaller increase to employees may help to encourage them to seek employment elsewhere. If
all employees get the same compensation adjustment, some authority is taken away from managers to incentivize and reward
higher performing employees.
         Heath Satow stated that he would like the Council to more carefully examine the compensation for the City’s top paid
employees to understand how much more they are being paid than Ogden residents who fall into the median income category. The
difference is staggering. He is not saying that City employees do not earn their pay, but it is clear that some are paid a very high
wage.
         There being no further persons appearing to be heard, COUNCIL MEMBER RICHEY MOVED THE PUBLIC
HEARING BE CLOSED. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA, ALL VOTING AYE.
         Chair White stated no action will be taken on this matter, but she appreciates the public input; there has been extensive
discussion around the McGrath compensation study, and the study data will be refreshed in the next year or two.

          A memo from Council staff regarding the proposed Council Goals and Guidelines came before the Council for
consideration. The memo stated that each year, the Council adopts goals and guidelines with the budget to communicate the
council’s expectations for implementation of the city’s budget, the primary policy document of the City. In approving the budget,
the council strives to ensure that the policies supported by the budget reflect both the operating needs of the city and the desires of
its residents as outlined in the Five-Year Strategic Plan. The budget guidelines generally remain consistent from year to year
unless policy changes are implemented. The budget goals change as projects are completed, and focus moves to other projects or
priorities.
          Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and stated proposed amendments to the Goals and
Guidelines include a statement indicating the City is in the process of updating its Strategic Plan, but the 2019 version of the Plan
will be used until the update is complete; technical changes to update the budget year; a statement that audited information will be
reported with the Annual Comprehensive Financial Report for the Redevelopment Agency budget; and shifts in budget priorities
identified by the Council for each Department of the City.

consideration. The memo stated the salary schedules will set the salary ranges for elected officials, non-merit special employees,
merit employees, sworn fire employees, and sworn police employees.
