 Roll Call

     Councilor Shannon Ellsworth                Councilor Bill Fillmore

Prayer – Chair Katrice MacKay

Pledge of Allegiance – Councilor Rachel Whipple

Public Comment

Chair MacKay read the public comment preamble. Afterwards, she announced the mayor hosting a town
hall event on Monday, September 11 for the road on Grandview. She said the event will be held at
Westridge Elementary School and will begin at 5:30 PM.

Councilor Hoban explained that the developer and city staff came to a great resolution. The road will be
a turnaround that will allow a connection for pedestrian and bike traffic, but no vehicle traffic.

Bradley Day, of Provo, expressed concern for sanitation services provided in the community at hours
before what is allowable in code.

Chair MacKay requested that Mr. Day provide the name of the company to staff.

With no other public comments, Chair MacKay closed the public comment period.

Presentations, Proclamations, and Awards

1.           Provo Police New Officer Badge Pinning (23-011) 13:40

Chief Beebe presented. He discussed the significance of a police badge and what it represents. He
proceeded to read a short biography for each officer while a loved one pinned the badge on the officer’s
uniform. The officers recognized were Sean Dicristofano, Salmon Gee, Garrett Hood, Brock Hunger,
Gordon McKay, Branden Neville, Hunter Sivley, and Courtney Stelmack.

Heidi Allman, City Recorder, gave the oath of office to the group.

2.         A presentation congratulating Chief Administrative Officer Wayne Parker on his retirement
           (23-011) 32:30

Chair MacKay said “Today, we bid farewell to our esteemed Chief Administrative Officer, Wayne Parker.
With deep gratitude, we acknowledge his decision to retire, effective September 16, 2023. His legacy of
dedication and service spans nearly two decades. A true Provo native, his commitment to our city has
guided us through transformative times. From a revitalized downtown to technological milestones, from
improved transportation to thriving community centers, his ability to lead has helped shape our
progress as a city. His achievements extend beyond infrastructure and reach into the very heart of our
community. Today, we don't simply bid farewell to a Chief Administrative Officer but to a friend. Join me
in expressing gratitude for his service and wishing him a retirement as vibrant as the impact he's had on
our city.”

Mr. Parker was presented with a gift from the council office.

Roger Minert asked if he would be allowed to make a public comment. He mentioned he did not
understand that the public comment at the beginning of the meeting was for any item, and not
specifically for the road construction.

Chair MacKay confirmed with Brian Jones, City Attorney, that a comment could be allowed. With the
recording still going, Chair MacKay invited Mr. Minert to speak.

Mr. Minert said he lives precisely 267 feet from the drilling operation that has been going on for the last
three years on Grandview Hill. He expressed his frustration at having to endure the vibrations, the
endless penetration of the noise, and the dust. He said he has not been able to find anybody in the city
administration or the contractor who can indicate whether that well is producing acceptable water. He
said he has been told there is arsenic in the water, and the well will be discontinued, but the grinding
regenerator continues.

Chair MacKay requested Mr. Minert to provide his information to staff for Dave Decker, Public Works
Director, to contact him directly.

Adjournment

The meeting was adjourned by unanimous consent at approximately 6:10 p.m.
