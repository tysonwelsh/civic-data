    6. Public Hearing: City Initiated 2020 Q1 Text Amendment (Legislative Action)
On the request of Draper City for approval of a Text Amendment to various sections of Draper City
Municipal Code Titles 9 and 17 in order to update code, make clarifications, and to propose common sense
standards. Application TEXTMAP-975-2020. Staff contact is Jennifer Jastremsky, 801-576-6328,
jennifer.jastremsky@draper.ut.us
Ms. Jastremsky explained the purpose of the text amendments was to update codes, make clarifications,
and propose common sense changes. They would be doing quarterly text amendments. She reviewed the
following proposed code changes:

    •   Creating a new use: Assembly And Repair, Limited. This would be a home occupation-based use
        where someone could obtain a conditional use permit. The intent of this issue is to allow for limited
        repair or assembly within a home. Auto repair is prohibited. She overviewed other restrictions to
        the use and indicated this use is for small operations.
    •   Zone Establishment Table: The Council had recently approved the Major Freeway Arterial
        Frontage (MARF) district to the table of districts. The purpose statement will be added to the zone
        purpose section of chapter 8.
    •   Allowed Driveways: The city is requiring driveways to be at least 20 feet in depth. This is being
        done to help with parking within neighborhoods. Neighborhoods with parking issues typically do
        not have driveways.
    •   Development Standards for the South Fork Estates RSD Zone: requires all public street lighting to
        meet Draper City streetlight standards and comply with Rocky Mountain Power requirements.
Chair Adams asked where South Fork Estates is located.
Ms. Jastremsky indicated that it is located west of Bangerter Parkway and south of South Fork Drive. She
continued with overviewing the text amendments.

    •   Number and Type of Parking Spaces: Code does not allow both spaces of a tandem space to be
        counted except with multifamily. This change clarifies that tandem spaces cannot count towards
        guest parking but can count towards the residential requirement.
    •   Development Standards: requires setbacks for multi-family structure within five feet to 20 feet for
        living spaces and 20 feet for garages. Alleyways require a width of 26 feet and must meet fire code.
Christina Oliver mentioned they are aiming to require 20 feet of driveway for properties with garages as
well.
Ms. Jastremsky indicated that developments with carports don’t allow carports to be used for anything but
parking. Garages often are used for storage. Requiring a driveway will ensure some parking is provided.
Commissioner Hawker mentioned he supports the parking changes.
    •   Temporary Signage: There was some confusion on where a sign can be posted for special events.
        This clears up posting periods and where signs can be located. It limits the number of signs to 35
        per event. It provides allowance for the city to designate areas in the public right of way where
        signs may be posted. These would most likely go through the Parks and Rec Department. Spacing
        requirements are listed and time restrictions are included.
    •   Areas of Special Designation: Provides additional sign standards for properties along freeways. A
        code reference is provided regarding tower sign standards. The amendment will clarify that the
        MARF zone is included as a property that would qualify for a freeway frontage sign.
    •   Conditional Use Home Occupations: Adds the Assembly and repair, limited as a use for home
        occupations.
    •   Lots: Removes a requirement from the subdivision code on building envelopes. People already
        need to meet minimum setbacks in the zoning district. There have been instances where the code
        changes and conflicts with the subdivision plat.
Chair Adams opened the public hearing. There were no speakers. He closed the public hearing.
Motion: Commissioner Gundersen motioned to forward a positive recommendation to the City
Council of the proposed city initiated 2020 Q1 text amendments, Application Text Map-975-2020,
based on the findings and subject to the conditions listed in the staff report, dated April 14, 2020.

    7. Public Hearing: City Initiated Use Tables Text Amendment (Legislative Action)
A request by the City of Draper for approval of a Text Amendment for the purpose of amending the use
tables in Draper City Municipal Code Title 9. The amendment will update the permitted, not permitted and
conditional uses within the use tables for all agricultural, residential, commercial, public facilities,
manufacturing, and transit station district zones with the intent of reducing the number of listed conditional
uses within the city. The changes would apply city wide, excepting Commercial Special Districts, those
properties with Development Agreements and Master Planned Communities. Application TEXTMAP-976-
2020. Staff contact is Jennifer Jastremsky, 801-576-6328, jennifer.jastremsky@draper.ut.us
Ms. Jastremsky explained they were trying to look at uses in the code and which should be permitted,
conditional and not permitted. The purpose of looking at these uses is to see if the site plan process is
sufficient to accommodate most uses and to determine if other uses needed additional permits and process.
In 2019, the state legislature passed HB315 which sets a high bar for denying conditional uses. She indicated
that Mike, City Attorney, typically will make a statement in regards to when the Planning Commission can
deny a conditional use permit. The only time is when detrimental effects of the use cannot be mitigated.
She stated that staff has gone through all city zones and is proposing changes. These changes include:

    •   Agricultural Zones: Several conditional uses are proposed to be made permitted uses, like churches
        or places of worship. She mentioned that if a use only affects one zone, the zone is listed, otherwise
        it affects multiple zones. She commented that public higher education facilities are being moved
        from not permitted uses to permitted uses. Private higher education facilities, trade/ vocational
        schools and guest house will be removed from the use table. She clarified that guest house is an
        antiquated term as the city now has accessory dwelling units.
    •   Residential Zones: Agriculture, churches, municipal uses, single family dwellings, government
        service, cemetery and parks are uses to be changed from conditional to permitted. Limited day care
        was changed from not permitted to a conditional use. Convalescent care facility, trade/vocational
        school, general day care, veterinary service, private higher educational facility, agricultural sales
        and service, kennel, private school, cattery and general preschools were removed from the table.
    •   Commercial Zones: She listed several uses that were being moved from conditional to permitted
        uses. Many of these uses were found to not be high impact uses with detrimental effects. She
        provided a list of uses moving from not permitted to permitted uses. She indicated that bar
        establishments are already regulated by the state and limited under the business license code.
        Commercial vehicle and equipment repair were found to be a use that needs to move from permitted
        to conditional use. Assisted living facilities and commercial vehicle and equipment repair were
        listed as not permitted under the Town Center District. Residential facilities for elderly persons,
        nurseries and repairs services were changed from a conditional use to a not permitted use under
        several districts. Some uses were removed from the table as they were not permitted in any of the
        zones.
Chair Adams asked what business service under the Town Center is.
Ms. Jastremsky read the definition into the record.

    •   Office warehouse was added to the code under the MARF Zone. The text amendment would list
        the allowance of this use in other districts.
    •   Public Facility Zones: Several uses are proposed to be changed from conditional to permitted
        including hospitals in the public facilities zone. Charter schools, military facilities, post offices, and
        public facilities were among uses be recommended to change from a not permitted to permitted
        use. Many of these uses have protections through state code. Private schools and trade/vocational
        schools were moved from not permitted to conditional uses under the PI district. Wireless
        telecommunication facilities will be changed from not permitted to a reference to chapter 41 which
        regulates theses facilities.
Mike Barker asked if telecommunication facilities included macro-towers and small cells.
Ms. Jastremsky replied that the small cell facilities are located in title 8.
Commissioner Player asked it this would cover HAM radios.
Ms. Jastremsky indicated this would not cover HAM radios but covers the commercial applications like
Verizon Wireless.

    •   She continued with the list and mentioned that general preschools and correctional facilities are
        removed from the public facility zones.
    •   Manufacturing zones: She listed several uses like convenience stores, major utilities to be moved
        from conditional to permitted. She noted that staff is proposing personal instruction service within
        the M1 district. Uses being changed from not permitted to permitted included things like
        government services, vehicle repair and restaurants. Private higher education facility, private
        schools and trade/vocational schools were uses that saw changes from not permitted to conditional.
        Personal instruction service was changed to not permitted under the M2 district. Some uses were
        removed from the table.
    •   Transit Station Zones: She overviewed the difference between the three transit station districts and
        listed those used being moved from conditional to permitted uses. Bus terminals and other uses
        were moved from not permitted to a permitted use and those changing from permitted to conditional
        status. Repair service and limited vehicle repair were changed from conditional to not permitted.
Commissioner Player indicated that several conditional uses were removed.
Christina Oliver indicated that this was intentional and that several of these conditional use permits were
difficult to enforce.
