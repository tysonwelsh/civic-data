       Discuss COVID-19 Employee Related Programs – Councilmember McCay

Councilmember McCay said that many women have been affected by the COVID-19 pandemic
and especially in their employment. She said that she would like to know what Riverton City is
doing to support women in the workplace. She said she had talked to Trish Dixon, Human

Resource Director, and wanted to find ways to allow more flexibility for women and possibly
allow paid maternity leave.

Councilmember Stewart said that short term disability covers maternity leave in his workplace.
Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, said that the City currently has short term and
long term disability. He said that short term disability starts after 1 week of being gone from
work and pays at 60% and the other 40% can be supplemented by the employee. He said the
disability paychecks are tax free.

Mayor Staggs said that the Councilmembers could propose a change to these policies. Mr.
Hicks suggested that the Councilmembers implement maternity or paternity leave. Mr. Hicks
said that estimated cost for these benefits is difficult to estimate.

Councilmember Buroker stated that with continued growth and a strong job market, the City
should examine the current policies and decide how the City will be able to compete to have the
best employees. Councilmember McDougal said the City needs to consider that employees are
wearing multiple hats and doing multiple jobs.

Mr. Hicks said that from a financial standpoint it is easy to say that we should offer these
benefits. Mayor Staggs said that it is hard to compare the benefits of working in the private
sector versus working in the public sector and agreed with Councilmember Buroker’s statement.

Mayor Staggs said that he believes the Councilmembers are directing staff to look at benefits
and make recommendations on changes that might make the City more attractive and then
bring it back to the Council for consideration. Councilmember McCay said that she would like
them to present a couple different options. She said that she wanted to add in a job sharing
option where two part time employees could be hired to perform one job.

Mr. Hicks asked the Councilmembers when they would like to see the options. Councilmember
McCay said she would like it sooner than later. Councilmember McDougal said that he would
like to have a bigger, broader discussion in January. Councilmember Stewart said he believes
this is a strategic discussion and it should be discussed in January. Mayor Staggs said that the
majority would like it brought back in January.

         Park and Trails Committee Presentation (16 acre park and Edge Development
         park) – Jason Judd, Chair

Jason Judd, Chair of the Parks and Trails Committee, said that the committee has been
meeting regularly over Zoom and in person. He said that when the committee first met, the
Tracy Aviary was a big project but that project has been put on hold. He said the committee has
discussed different projects including enhancing the fishing pond, the 10 acre Edge Homes
piece of land and the 16 acre future park northeast of the Costco.

Mayor Staggs said that there will be four more pickle ball courts installed at the main City park
and the sand volleyball court will be relocated.

Councilmember Buroker asked if there is broad representation from all facets of the community
on the Parks and Trails Committee and if the committee feels like they have value. Mr. Judd
said there was a very diverse group and he does believe the committee has value.

         Signage Discussion (12600 S. Redwood Road) – Councilmember McCay

Councilmember McCay said that she would like to see the Silverwolf sign on Spirit Corner stay.
She said that she has enjoyed that Riverton City supports Riverton High School and she
believes there will be push back from residents if the sign is removed.

Councilmember Wells said that when he originally heard that the City wanted to move the
Silverwolf sign, he was against it but that was from a logistic standpoint. He said that he walked
the area with Councilmember Buroker and said that this was not maintained as well as it
should’ve been. He asked why the high school has not been maintaining this corner.

Councilmember McDougal explained the history of Spirit Corner. He said that this corner was
owned by UDOT but not maintained. He said that Mayor Applegarth decided that this should be
a Spirit Corner and the senior class at Riverton High School donated the sign. He said that
there is no parking by Spirit Corner and because it is not located on school grounds, it is hard
for the students or parents to maintain it. He said that he is supportive of it being moved to
school grounds where it can be utilized more.

Councilmember Stewart said this sign is on private property and he is concerned that this is not
part of the school. He said that the City needs to support all of the high schools in the City. He
said the Hope Walk should include all high schools and bring the schools together on a common
theme.

Mayor Staggs discussed the history of Spirit Corner. He said that he would like this corner to be
another way to communicate with the residents through a digital sign. He asked if there would
be an opportunity to get a grant to improve the corner. He said he does not believe this corner
needs to be part of the first phase of the signage installations.

Councilmember McDougal said he was concerned about spending money on this corner when
the City does not own this land. Ryan Carter, City Attorney, said he does not believe UDOT will
ever sell this land to the City because they know that someday the road will need to be
expanded and this is part of their long term traffic plan.

Mayor Staggs said that he asked Casey Saxton, Communications Director, to remove the
placement of a major monument on this corner from the plans for now. Councilmember McCay
asked what the cost would be to move the sign to the high school. David Brickey, City
Manager, said he did not have a dollar amount for moving the sign. Mayor Staggs said that he
has asked Mr. Saxton to come back with another plan for the corner that will be minimal.

Presentations/Recognitions

         Certificate of Achievement for Excellence in Financial Reporting and Award for
         Outstanding Achievement in Popular Annual Financial Reporting - Jim Clayton
         State Representative, Utah Government Finance Officers Association

Jim Clayton, State Representative from the Utah Government Finance Officers Association,
presented these certificates to Riverton City. He said that Riverton City has received the
comprehensive award for 12 years and the popular award for 10 years.

         Riverton Police Department Presentation – Chief Don Hutson

Chief Don Hutson said that Councilmember McCay delivered cards and notes to the police
department and he thanked her for coordinating this.       He said that the Emergency
Preparedness Coordinator, Jason Jones, has been presenting neighborhood watch and
emergency preparedness to various neighborhoods around the City.

Chief Hutson said that the South Jordan, Riverton and Herriman SWAT team competed in a
SWAT competition with SWAT teams from all around the western United States and were able
to place 3rd out of 16 other teams. He said that Riverton SWAT team has been called out to
other jurisdictions and been able to help with various incidents.

Chief Hutson said that he created a heat map which shows the location of all the vehicle
burglaries that occurred in Riverton City during the month of August. He said there were a
significant number of burglaries and the most that Riverton City has seen in one month. He said
they are analyzing evidence in these cases and looking for ways to stop the burglaries from
happening.

Chief Hutson said that VECC has redone their phone system and is constantly in a battle to
keep good employees. He said that VECC has improved on emergency calls but non-
emergency calls have been neglected. Councilmember Stewart agreed with Chief Hutson’s
analysis of the data from VECC. He said he would like to have a work session focused on
VECC. Mayor Staggs said that VECC should be full service and believes the City is paying a lot
of money and not receiving quality service.

Mayor/Council/Manager Reports

Councilmember McDougal said he had a conversation with Trace Robinson about the benefits

Councilmember McCay said that she loved the National Day of Service. She said that the
committee for the National Day of Service wanted to continue this project and has scheduled

Councilmember McCay said that she went to the TRCC meeting and said she was frustrated by
their budget. She said that multiple cities are receiving high dollar amounts for projects. She
does not believe that Riverton is receiving their share of the money and would like the City to
identify projects that can be presented before the TRCC board to receive funding. She said
that all-inclusive parks are a high priority for the board.

Mr. Brickey said that because of the pandemic, the TRCC board had pulled all of the grants

Councilmember Stewart said that Unified Fire Authority had budgeted each city to share the

phragmites. She said that these grants have been very beneficial for the Jordan River Parkway

Constitution on Constitution Day.

Councilmember Wells said that he was able to serve lunch at the Senior Center and it was a
