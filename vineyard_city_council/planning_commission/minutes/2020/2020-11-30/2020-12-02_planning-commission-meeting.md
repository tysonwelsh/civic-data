1. CALL TO ORDER AND WRITTEN DETERMINATION
Chair Anthony Jenkins called the meeting to order at 6:01 p.m. then read the following statement:

I, Anthony Jenkins, as the Chair of the Vineyard Planning Commission, do hereby find and declare as
follows:
     1. Due to the Emergency conditions which currently exist in the State of Utah, and specifically in
         Utah County and Vineyard City as a result of the COVID-19 Pandemic and the recent surge in
         COVID-19 infections across the state and in Utah County, the holding of public meetings with an
         anchor location as defined in the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act, presents a substantial risk
         to the health and safety of those who may be present at the anchor location; and

    2. The risk to those who may be present at an anchor location can be substantially mitigated by
       holding public meetings of the Planning Commission pursuant to electronic means that allow for
       public participation via virtual means; and

    3. The City has the means and ability to allow virtual participation in the public meetings in
       accordance with the Utah Open and Public Meetings Act;

NOW THEREFORE, BASED UPON THE FOREGOING,
       For thirty days from the date of this Order, meetings of the Vineyard Planning Commission shall
be conducted by virtual means without an anchor location.

3. OPEN SESSION
      At 6:04 p.m. Chair Jenkins opened the session to the public.

      Community Development Director Morgan Brim read the following statement from Vineyard City
resident, Kathy Spencer:

“Dear Planning Committee,
I am a citizen of Vineyard and live in the Water’s Edge development. I am not able to attend the
planning meeting this week but wanted to voice a few suggestions and concerns for our developing
area.
I think Vineyard has great potential to become an upscale, clean, prime living place in Utah County but I
see the need for a few improvements. There are so many homes and so many people moving in but I
fear most of the development is houses without a lot of infrastructure. We need grocery stores, gas
stations, and pharmacies in Vineyard before we need more retail. We could have more neighborhood
market style if that would fit in with the retail/housing plans going in at Town Center, but some form of
grocery stores is needed.
When it comes to retail, I feel we can do so much better than lower-level chain restaurants.
We should pursue getting places like Gourmandise, Blue Lemon, or even a Cheesecake Factory before
we acquire places like Little Caesar's. I strongly encourage you to look at the benefit of the citizens' and
our need for more infrastructure first before adding more retail or housing. I would also encourage
looking at the long-term value of putting higher quality establishments, restaurants, and retail into your
city, while you plan and develop the rest of Vineyard. I am hopeful to make this city a great place to live
now and in the future.
Thanks for taking the time to consider my thoughts,
Kathy Spencer”

Mr. Brim responded to the letter with a list of infrastructure projects and shared that the city has similar
goals. He sent Ms. Spencer the city engineers contact information as well as some economic
development projects that she could review.

Chair Jenkins stated that it was always a good thing when residents share their thoughts, positive or
negative about the future developments of the city. He then clarified that it is important to be clear
what the objective of the planning commission is, which is to encourage certain types of businesses by
zoning regulations. He stated that there are currently zoning options for businesses such as the mixed-
use area, which is being under-utilized. The other clarification that Chair Jenkins pointed out was that
the city does not have an active role in selecting certain businesses. Mr. Brim can reach out to types of
businesses and encourage specific types of businesses but he does not pick and choose what is allowed.
Similar to how you don’t pick your neighbors, anyone that wants to buy a home that can find a seller is
able to do so. The planning commission will ensure that the right buildings are built in the right places.
He then opened the meeting to other commissioners for their input

Commissioner Shan Sullivan stated that this is a semantics issue. She believed that the resident was
meaning to say economic development instead of infrastructure. She said that the Commission was
doing a lot of good things with the general plan and seconded the comments from Chair Jenkins.

                                                                                          December 2, 2020

Commissioner Jeff Knighton agreed with Commissioner Sullivan and Chair Jenkins. He clarified that
commercial establishments look at rooftops before any commitment to development in certain cities.
With more residential being built, it is enticing for grocery stores and other retail. He believed that Ms.

right store.

6. WORKSESSION
   6.1 The Planning Commission will discuss potential 2021 updates to the General Plan.
Mr. Brim introduced the topic and then turned the time to Planner II, Briam Amaya Perez.

Mr. Amaya began his presentation stating his intention to update specific elements of the general plan
in January of 2021. He then provided a brief overview of the general plan.

Mr. Amaya then asked the Commission for their thoughts on what comes to mind when thinking about a
general plan.

Chair Jenkins replied a general plan is overarching for all aspects of the city. It is not playbook for every
little thing but includes standards what people would like to see. Commissioner Blackburn answered
that the word vision comes to mind when thinking about a general plan. He also mentioned principles
and values. Commissioner Sullivan said that she believes the general plan is a guide for decisions that
the Planning Commission make. Alternate Gudmundson said that the general plan is the skeletal
structure of a city

Mr. Brim pointed out that the general plan is not law, but does provide the city a legal basis for zoning
changes and institute law. It’s like the Constitution in that laws need to be in line with the general plan.

                                                                                            December 2, 2020

Mr. Amaya than asked the Commission: “Why do we plan?”

Commission Brady mentioned that it is not fun to live in cities where no one planned. No planning can
cause people to live next to things they should. A good plan will promote healthy residential and
commercial growth and zones. Commissioner Blackburn stated that plans allow for more control over
your destiny in that the City is being proactive with issues and not responding or reacting to them.
Commissioner Sullivan stated that planning is not thinking about the future, but the predecessors to the
future.

Mr. Amaya said that plans are meant to be living documents. They are not stagnant but should
encourage growth. Successful plans prevent and solve problems.

Briam read the General Plan Vision: “Vineyard is a vibrant well-balanced community providing residents
and visitors a connection to our heritage and a path towards an enduring future. We promote a healthy
community through thoughtful public investments, innovation, strategic partnerships, an activated
lakefront and thoughtful shared open spaces”

He continued by presenting the hierarchy of a plan. He stated that a vision helps identify elements that
need to be focused on, such as transportation services, parks and open space, heritage and culture, etc.
Based off of the identified elements of importance, the City would create goals that help create an
implementation plan. The reason to update the General Plan is that it helps us to know how we are
doing, if we are making progress, if there are delays, and reassess public input. Including the public is an
important element of this update.

Mr. Amaya then shared a spreadsheet that presented each topic of the General Plan. He then asked the
Commission members to read it and send him their input. Mr. Brim clarified the assignment for the
commission and asked that they send it back in two weeks.

Chair Jenkins mentioned that he liked this assignment and doubted there would be many
disagreements. He asked Mr. Amaya if there was a section to indicate what to prioritize. Mr. Amaya
replied by saying to use comment section to identify priorities.

Commission Sullivan asked for a new space added to the assignment for proposed action. Mr. Amaya
noted.

Mr. Amaya continued his presentation by stating that there are two elements that the staff is focusing
on. The first one is open space, parks, trails, and the lakefront. There has been great momentum in
making progress with Sunset Beach, Vineyard Beach, Walkara Way, the downtown promenades, and
others. With the development going on, we want to capture open spaces before it is too late. He then
showed some examples of open spaces currently in the city such as the open space corridor and the lake
shore.

Mr. Brim stated that the plan is to make many small changes throughout the plan. There will be
specifically two chapters updated; the parks chapter and the moderate-income housing chapter. He
clarified that there needs to be a clear plan so that the City can meet the three areas of moderate-
income housing requirements from the state.
