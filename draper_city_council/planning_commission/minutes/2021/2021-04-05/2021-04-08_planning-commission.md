    2. WRITTEN DETERMINATION 2021-17 Pursuant to Utah Code Ann. 52-4-207(4)
I, Andrew Adams, do hereby determine conducting an electronic meeting of the Draper City
Planning Commission with an anchor location presents a substantial risk to the health and safety
of those who may be present at the anchor location and hereby authorize the Draper City
Planning Commission to conduct electronic meetings without an anchor location.

The foregoing determination is based on the following facts:

   •   Federal, state and local leaders, including the Draper City Mayor and City Council, have
       all recognized a global pandemic caused by the spread of the COVID-19 virus.
   •   While COVID-19 cases and hospitalizations in Draper show an encouraging downward
       trend and vaccines are becoming available to a larger segment of the Draper population,
       vigilance is required so that there isn’t a rebound in cases and hospitalizations.
   •   It is difficult, if not impossible, to predict the number of attendees at any meeting and to
       manage issues regarding social distancing in order to comply with state and local health
       departments health orders.
   •   COVID-19 poses a continuing and immediate threat to the health, safety, and welfare of
       Draper City residents.

    5. Public Hearing: Steele Body Works Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit
       (Administrative Action)
On the request by Robert Steele for approval of a Home Occupation Conditional Use Permit in
the C-3 zone regarding a Home Occupation Business as a Massage Therapist. Application:
USE-0028-2021. Staff Contact is Travis Van Ekelenburg, 801-576-6522,
Travis.VanEkelenburg@draperutah.gov

Mr. Van Ekelenburg presented. He included details such as where the home was located and
when it was built. He stated that the applicant will be the only employee of the business and will
attend to one customer at a time for a maximum of two customers per day. Business hours will
be from 9AM to 9PM during the course of a seven-day week. Only one room in the home will be
utilized for the business. The home meets the size requirements. The applicant plans to use the
driveway for parking, which will not impede the use of the home as a single-family home. This
is the only place where parking is permitted. There will be no signage on the property. He asked
for any questions.

There were none.
Approved minutes May 13, 2021


Applicant Robert Steele stated that he agreed with everything Mr. Van Ekelenburg presented.

Chairperson Adams opened the public hearing.

Lorraine Dieterle stated that, as a neighbor in the neighborhood where the proposed home
business is located. She stated that she has concerns about the parking and would like to add to
the stipulation that the owner cannot park in front of other homes or have customers park in front
of other homes. She noted that, historically, cars have parked in front of other homes.

Chairperson Adams closed the public hearing.

Mr. Steele addressed the parking concern, stating that clients would be instructed to park in the
driveway and not on the street.

Chairperson Adams reopened the public hearing

Charlotte Hatch stated that she is a next-door neighbor to Mr. Steele. She stated that traffic has
increased on the street due to Mr. Steele’s friends’ large vehicles parking in front of other
houses. She expressed concern over the traffic doubling once Mr. Steele starts having costumers.
Additionally, she stated that children, many times small children, cross the street from the bus
stop across the street. She stated that it would be difficult for children to see past large vehicles
on the street.

Chairperson Adams closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Fowler stated that the only visitors to the home that they have the authority to
control with this application are the customers who will be instructed to park in the driveway.
She suggested the neighbors work together to sort out the street parking issue.

Commissioner Squire agreed with Commissioner Fowler. She stated that, since the street is a
public road, they don’t have the authority to regulate who parks there.

Motion re: Commissioner Fowler made a motion to approve the Home Occupation Conditional
Use Permit in the C-3 zone regarding a Home Occupation Business as a Massage Therapist and
Commissioner Bingham seconded the motion.

Vote: A roll call vote was taken with Commissioners voting unanimously in favor of the motion.
This item passed with a 3 to 0 vote.

    8. Public Hearing: Big Willow Creek 3rd Amendment Development Agreement
       Request (Legislative Action)
On the request of Bryon Prince, representing Ivory Development LLC for a 3rd amendment to
the Big Willow Creek Development. Agreement for 41.33 acres of property located at
approximately 491 West 11400 South. Application DA-20-2021. Staff contact is Jennifer
Jastremsky, 801-576-6328, jennifer.jastremsky@draperutah.gov.
Approved minutes May 13, 2021
Ms. Jastremsky presented. She included details about the original development agreement. The
proposal today covers the phase 6, 2, and 3 portions of the development. She summarized the
history of the property. She stated that the amendment would add phase 3 in with the same
stipulations that the city has agreed to for phase 2. She went over the changes that the City had
approved to the code in 2020 concerning driveways and private roadway width standards.

Applicant Bryon Prince presented. He summarized the history of Big Willow Creek. He clarified
that they are wanting to proceed with the plan that was presented with 2020 codes as opposed to
the 2021 codes that were put in place after the plans were established. He stated that there will be
141 units regardless of whether or not private lanes are widened. They currently want to include
more single-family homes, but if the private lanes were widened, it would result in more
townhomes.

Commissioner Fowler asked about the access points.

Mr. Prince responded that the access point is in a safer area, which would reduce traffic in
private lanes.

Chairperson Adams opened the public comment.

Jason Staples stated that he lives at 11663 June Grass Drive. He stated that the private roads
would impact the public roads. Narrow private road parking would spill out into public roads. He
would propose that they meet the current, 2021, code.

Mark Palmer stated that he lives at 11649 S. June Grass Drive. He stated that all of the
homeowners have been deceived. They were told last night about new information regarding
their neighborhood that they have already bought homes in. Road designs have changed. It’s
dangerous driving and very congested. He asked if there would be a third access point in the
future. He stated that, if a natural disaster were to occur, they would not be able to get out of the
community.

Steve Affleck stated that he lives on 11608 Wildrye Drive. He stated that the reason codes
evolve, and change are for safety and the developers should be required to work under the
current codes whether the code changes a day or a week after the plans are approved. He stated
that the number of cars per house would increase traffic. He asked that Ivory Homes be held to
the current code.

Kelly Frazier stated that she lives in the Jensen Farm homes. She reiterated that none of the
homeowners were ever made aware about the multi-family homes that are being built. She stated
that there are many young children and many young families with small children in the
neighborhood that would be put in harm’s way due to the increased traffic.

Chairperson Adams closed the public comment.

Chairperson Adams clarified that the City Council will have the final say on the decision, but it’s
their job currently to pass on a positive or negative recommendation to the City Council.
Approved minutes May 13, 2021


Mr. Prince stated that nothing fundamentally is changing. The traffic impact for the 141 units
was analyzed and taken into account. The concerns about traffic will not be affected by what’s
been presented tonight. The private lane standard does not change the parking standards or the
traffic standards.

Chairperson Adams thanked Mr. Prince for his time and asked the commissioners to deliberate.

Ms. Jastremsky presented additional pictures and plans for the phases affected for Big Willow
Creek. She clarified what is required in the 2021 code.

Commissioner Squire asked if there was any parking allowed on the private roads, and what the
width is of the private roads currently.

Mr. Maxfield stated that on street parking is dependent upon the width of the street. That is
regulated by the fire department.

Mr. Prince answered that the private lane is 30 feet. The alleyway is 20 feet. Every single-family
home in this phase has a two-car garage and a full-length driveway. Nearby is located a lot for
parking stalls for visitors.

Commissioner Bingham stated that proper driveways and road width is necessary for safety.
Wider streets make for a better, safer community.

Commissioner Squire asked what the difference is in feet between the old and new code.

Ms. Jastremsky stated that the private streets go from 36 feet to 37 feet, and alleyways go from
24 feet to 26 feet.

Commissioner Squire asked what would happen if they were unable to reach a unanimous
decision.

Ms. Jastremsky stated that they can also pass a modified recommendation.

Ms. Jastremsky presented additional pictures and plans.

Commissioner Bingham stated that the code is well-based. If Mr. Prince is going to have the
same number of units regardless, we might as well make sure they are up to code. A foot or two
makes a big difference on the streets.

Motion re: Commissioner Bingham made a motion for a modified positive recommendation
allowing for phase 3 approval but that they comply with existing driveway and street standards
and don’t allow variances that don’t comply with code. Commissioner Fowler seconded the
motion.
