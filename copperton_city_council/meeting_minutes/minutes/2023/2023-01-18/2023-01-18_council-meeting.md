Copperton Elections Responsibilities

               Mayor Clayton stated there had been some questions relating to what constitutes
electioneering. The Council wants to understand its responsibilities on Election Day, in general,
and what the responsibilities are of poll workers.

                 Lannie Chapman, Salt Lake County Clerk, stated the Clerk’s office would be
administering the election for Copperton. Anybody noticing anything inappropriate taking place
less than fifty feet from the entrance to the vote center can call the main office at 385-GOT VOTE.
They will make sure the trained poll workers properly resolve the situation. Typically, someone
from the Clerk’s office will place the signs on the ground face down and then instruct the candidate
or campaign to remove the signs. This should not be something a member of the Council or
public needs to deal with. If necessary, the Clerk’s office will reach out to UPD for assistance.

               Council Member Bailey asked if political discourse was allowed inside the vote
centers.

                 Ms. Chapman stated poll workers they should try to get people who are engaged
in political discussion through the vote center as soon as possible so they can continue their
conversation outside. If that is not possible, the Clerk’s office should be called so they can have
the lead poll worker address the situation.

               Council Member Stitzer asked if Ms. Chapman could compile a list of the open
seats available in preparation for candidate filing week coming up in June.

                 Ms. Chapman stated she is currently working on such a list and once complete,
she will bring it to the Council to be proofed for errors.

Utah League of Cities of Towns

                Nathan Bracken stated as a paying member of the Utah League of Cities and
Townships (ULCT), Copperton will have all the same rights as other municipalities. ULCT meets
every Monday during the legislative session at the Capitol. They have a bill tracker and will be
doing daily briefing calls. Things move quickly during the legislative session, and it is important
to be plugged in. Copperton needs a representative and an alternate.

              Council Member Bailey, seconded by Council Member Severson, nominated Sean
Clayton as representative for Copperton in the ULCT and Kathleen Bailey as alternate. The
motion passed unanimously.

Copperton Ordinance Updates

               Council Member Severson stated the next planning commission meeting will be
on February 14. They are still working on getting revisions written and updated. Once finished,
those revisions will be sent to legal counsel for review. Then there will be a public hearing on
February 28th.

              Nathan Bracken stated before anything can be adopted, there needs to be a
public hearing before the planning commission and then the commission can make a
recommendation to the Council. There is pending legislation on subdivisions, and he is
concerned that if Copperton adopts anything now, it may have to do revisions and adopt again
later.

Announcements

                 Ryan Perry, Rio Tinto announced the early implementation of renewable diesel
fuel. This will reduce emissions. Rio Tinto submitted an annexation request to the City of South
Jordan for a large piece of property. That process will go through Salt Lake County Mayor’s office
and there will be some public hearings involved. All of Rio Tinto’s land is in unincorporated Salt
Lake County, so instead of the County being the land use authority, it will be South Jordan City.
Rio Tinto does not have immediate plans to develop. It recognizes it needs to be part of a
municipality to develop the land. This annexation could be a three to four year process.

Rio Tinto is also concerned about trespassing issues. People have been scavenging powerlines
below the Lion’s Club, and this is dangerous because it is not easy to tell the difference between
an active and a decommissioned line. UPD is working on curtailing this through heightened
security in specific areas. If anybody in the community sees something, they should let UPD
know immediately.

               Mayor Clayton suggested putting a press release on Facebook.

              Mr. Perry stated it is a delicate situation because Rio Tinto would rather not call
too much attention to the issue. Releasing very general information would be best.

                 Detective Harry Holt, UPD stated it would be best simply to mention suspicious
vehicles loitering in parking lots and not get too specific. He has alerted the officers who work the
graveyard shift and asked them to keep watch.
