                                 RESOLUTION 25-76

                   A RESOLUTION OF THE DRAPER CITY COUNCIL
                        ADOPTING A DATA PRIVACY POLICY


       WHEREAS, Draper City collects, maintains, and processes personal data in the
course of providing municipal services to residents, businesses, and the general
public; and

      WHEREAS, the State of Utah has established a statewide data privacy
framework recognizing an individual’s inherent expectation of privacy in personal
data provided to governmental entities; and

      WHEREAS, Draper City seeks to ensure that its collection, use, storage, sharing,
and disposal of personal data are conducted responsibly, lawfully, and in a manner
consistent with state policy and best practices; and

       WHEREAS, adopting a formal data privacy policy supports Draper City’s
obligations to Utah Code § 63A-19-401.

      NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED BY THE CITY COUNCIL OF DRAPER CITY,
STATE OF UTAH, AS FOLLOWS:

       Section 1. Adoption. The Draper City data privacy policy, attached hereto as
Exhibit A and incorporated by reference, is hereby adopted as the official policy
governing the city’s collection, use, retention, safeguarding, sharing, and disposal of
personal data.

      Section 2. Applicability. The data privacy policy shall apply to all city
departments, programs, officials, employees, and contractors who collect, maintain,
process, access, or store personal data on behalf of Draper City.

     Section 3. Effective Date. This resolution shall become effective
immediately upon passage.

1.       Purpose
This policy establishes Draper City’s privacy program, including its policies, practices, and
procedures for processing personal data in accordance with the Utah Government Privacy
Act, Utah Code § 63A-19-401(2)(a), and the annual privacy reporting obligation under Utah
Code § 63A-19-401.3. It aligns with records management and data governance requirements
under the Government Records Access Management Act (GRAMA, Utah Code § 63G-2) and
the Division of Archives and Records Services (DARS, Utah Code § 63A-12). Where applicable,
this policy references more specific policies, procedures, or guidance developed by the City.


2.       Guiding Principles
This policy consolidates privacy practices, defines governance roles and responsibilities, and
ensures compliance with generally applicable records management, data protection, and
data privacy obligations. It safeguards individual privacy rights, promotes transparency,
maintains the integrity and security of personal data, and ensures accountability across the
City. This policy guides the City’s alignment with the State Data Privacy Policy under Utah
Code § 63A-19-102.

3.       Scope
This policy applies to all City employees who manage, create, or maintain records, or who
access personal data in the course of their job duties. This policy also applies to all City
contractors who process or access personal data under an agreement with the City, pursuant
to Utah Code § 63A-19-401. 4.

4.   Definitions:
"Classification," "classify," and derivative forms mean determining whether a record series,
record, or information within a record is public, private, controlled, protected, or exempt
from disclosure under Subsection § 63G-2-201(3)(b). 2

“Cookie” means “Technology that records a user’s information and activity when the user
accesses websites. Cookies are used by website owners, third parties, and sometimes threat
actors to gather user data.”3

“Data breach" means— the unauthorized access, acquisition, disclosure, loss of access, or
destruction of personal data held by a governmental entity, unless the governmental entity
concludes, according to standards established by the Cyber Center, that there is a low
probability that personal data has been compromised.”4

"Designation," "designate," and their derivative forms mean indicating, based on a
governmental entity's familiarity with a record series or based on a governmental entity's
review of a reasonable sample of a record series, the primary classification that a majority
of records in a record series would be given if classified and the classification that other
records typically present in the record series would be given if classified. 5

“Device fingerprinting” means collecting attributes of a user's device configurations to create
a trackable profile for the device.

"Individual" means a human being. 6

5.      Governance

      5.1.   Chief Administrative Officer (CAO)

      A. The City Manager is hereby designated as the chief administrative officer (CAO) of the
          City, responsible for overseeing and fulfilling the duties outlined in Utah Code § 63A-
          12-103.

      B. The City Manager is authorized to designate and may assign these duties outlined in
          Utah Code § 63A-12-103 to one or more additional individuals to serve as
          supplemental or department-specific CAOs.


      C. All CAO designations shall be reported to the Utah Division of Archives and Records
          Services (Archives) within 30 days of the designation.

      D. If duties are divided among multiple CAOs, such specification should be reported to
          Archives along with the designation.

      E. CAO designations and assigned responsibilities shall be reviewed and, if necessary,
          updated annually by the City Manager.

      5.2.   Appointed Records Officers (AROs)

      A. Each designated CAO shall appoint one or more individuals to serve as records
          officers to serve as AROs and work with Archives and the Office of Data Privacy in the
          care, maintenance, scheduling, disposal, classification, designation, access, privacy,
          and preservation of records. 14

      B. A CAO may assign these duties to one officer or distribute them among several, as
         the CAO deems appropriate.

      C. If duties are divided among multiple officers, such specification should be reported
          to Archives along with the appointment.

7.    Awareness & Training

     7.1.   Departmental Data Privacy Training

     A. The responsible CAO shall ensure that all employees with access to personal data
        complete a data privacy training program within 30 days of starting employment and
        at least annually thereafter.

     B. The Draper City IT Department is responsible for monitoring employee completion of
        this training.

     7.2.   Department-Specific Training

     A. City Departments may develop and require additional Department-specific privacy
        training tailored to their unique needs, practices, and requirements.

     7.3.   Appointed Records Officer Training and Certification

     A. CAO(s) shall ensure that all appointed records officers (AROs) annually complete
        online GRAMA training and obtain certification from Archives in accordance with Utah
        Code § 63A-12-110.

     B. GRAMA Access AROs: AROs handling GRAMA transparency responsibilities must
        complete GRAMA transparency training and obtain certification from Archives in
        accordance with Utah Code § 63A-12-110.

     C. Records Management and Privacy AROs: AROs specializing in records management
        or privacy must complete both records management and GRAMA transparency
        training and obtain the corresponding certifications.

11. Processing

     11.1.   Minimum Data Necessary

      A. CAO(s) shall ensure that all programs obtain and process only the minimum personal
         data reasonably necessary to achieve a specified purpose. 20

      B. CAO(s) shall ensure regular review of data collection practices to confirm compliance
         with data minimization.


     11.2.   Record and Data Sharing or Selling Policy

      A. The City shall share or disclose personal data only when there authorized by law. The
         sale of personal data is prohibited unless required by law.

      B. Data sharing must comply with GRAMA or other applicable law and may include
         sharing with governmental entities, contractors, private providers, or researchers.
         Compliance with GRAMA or other governing law is contingent upon the purpose of
         the sharing, the parties involved, and the nature of the records.

      C. Each CAO shall report annually to the Chief Privacy Officer on personal data sharing
         and selling activities, including data types, legal basis, and recipient entities.

      D. All contracts involving personal data must include appropriate privacy protections.
         Written data-sharing agreements are recommended to ensure compliance.


     11.3.   Retention and Disposition of Records Containing Personal Data

      A. Employees shall maintain, archive, and dispose of records—including all personal
         data—in accordance with an approved retention schedule. 21

      B. Employees shall comply with all other applicable laws or regulations governing
         retention or disposition of specific personal data held by the City or its operating units
         or programs.

12. Information Security

     12.1.    Incident Response

      A. The City adopts the DTS Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan to manage security
          incidents, data breaches, and privacy violations.

      B. Employees shall report all suspected security incidents, including non-IT incidents
          such as unauthorized access to physical records, to the Enterprise Information
          Security Office (EISO). Each CAO is responsible for developing and implementing any
          additional Department-specific response measures for non-IT incidents .

      C. The CAO of each Department shall ensure compliance with all other applicable laws
          or regulations related to incident response and breach notification for specific
          personal data held by the City.


     12.2.    Breach Notification

      A. The City shall notify affected individuals or their legal guardians of a data breach in
          accordance with Utah Code § 63A-19-406. 22

      B. The City shall notify the Cyber Center and the state attorney general of a data breach
          affecting 500 or more individuals, pursuant to Utah Code § 63A-19-405. Departments
          that experience a data breach affecting fewer than 500 individuals must create and
          file an internal incident report in accordance with Utah Code § 63A-19-405(5). These
          requirements are in addition to any other reporting requirement that the Department
          may be subject to.

      C. The CAO of each Department that is subject to other breach notification
          requirements, such as those required for compliance with federal regulations, laws
          or other governing requirements (e.g., HIPAA or 42 CFR Part 2) are currently required
          to create and maintain their own Department specific breach notification policies and
          procedures that meet the requirements of the applicable governing laws and
          regulations.

     13.2.    Cookies, Fingerprinting, Key Loggers, and Tracking Technologies

The City is committed to transparency and privacy protection regarding tracking
technologies on its website and digital services, including cookies, device fingerprinting, key
loggers, and other similar methods.

      A. Cookies
          The use of cookies on City websites and digital services must comply with applicable
          privacy and security policies. Cookies must be limited to essential operational
          purposes. Any use of tracking or third-party cookies for analytics or similar functions
          must be clearly disclosed, with an option to consent where required by law.

      B. Device Fingerprinting
          Device fingerprinting is prohibited unless explicitly authorized by the CAO, with legal
          basis and justification documented in a PIA. Purpose and extent must be clearly
          defined, documented, and disclosed in a compliant privacy notice or statement.

      C. Key Loggers
          Key loggers are prohibited without specific CAO authorization and documented
          justification in the activity's PIA. Key loggers may be used only for a clearly defined
          operational need that meets security standards and legal requirements, including
          user notice where required.

   D. Other Tracking Technologies
       Use of web beacons, pixel tags, or similar tools is prohibited unless explicitly
       authorized by the CAO, with legal basis documented in a PIA. Disclosure must be
       included in user-facing privacy statements, with consent obtained when required by
       law.

   E. User Notification and Consent
       Users must be informed about tracking technologies via a clear website privacy
       statement explaining data collected, purpose, and preferential management options.
       Updates to tracking practices must be promptly reflected in the privacy statement.


   F. Data Security and Retention
       Data collected through authorized tracking technologies must be securely stored with
       access limited to authorized personnel. Retention must align with approved
       schedules and be limited to the defined operational purpose.
