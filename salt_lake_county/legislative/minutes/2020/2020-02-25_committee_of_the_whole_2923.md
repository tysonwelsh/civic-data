3.1 Legislative Update 20-0205
Attachments: Staff Report
Presenter: Kara Trevino (Approx. 1 hr.)
Discussion/Direction
Ms. Kara Trevino , Intergovernmental Relations Manager, Council Office, 
reviewed the following legislative bills:
- HB 347 Inland Port Modifications
Ms. Trevino stated this bill does two positive things for the County.  It changes the tax 
differential retained by the authority from 100 percent to 75 percent, with 25 percent 
flowing to taxing entities.  It also modifies the Inland Port Authority Board, wherein the 
Salt Lake City Mayor, Magna Metro Township Mayor, and Salt Lake County Mayor or 
designee will have a seat.    
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member Granato, to 
support HB 347.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- SB 150 Transportation Governance and Funding Amendments
Mr. Andrew Gruber , Executive Director, Wasatch Front Regional Council, stated this 
bill is part of a large effort to enhance the coordination of transportation, housing, and 
land use at Transit Oriented Development (TOD) sites.  It eliminates the Utah Transit 
Page 1 of 15
Committee of the Whole Meeting Minutes February 25, 2020
Authority’s statutory cap of eight TODs, requires local governments to submit a station 
area plan and comply with other statutory requirements; requires compliance with SB 34 
for moderate income housing, and allows the State Transportation Commission to 
prioritize projects.  
It also modifies provisions related to the Transportation Reinvestment Zone (TRZ) by 
creating a mechanism to authorize the State ’s participation in a TRZ and creating a 
Transportation Reinvestment Zone Fund that may be funded by legislative appropriations 
and revenue generated by a portion of state sales and use tax within a TRC.
It modifies the Road Usage Charge program wherein the Utah Department of 
Transportation will provide a plan to enroll all vehicles registered in the state by December 
31, 2021.
It modifies the Car Rental Tax by increasing the sales tax on short -term leases and rentals 
from 2.5 percent to 4 percent, with 2.5 percent of funds to continue to be used for 
corridor preservation, and 1.5 percent to go the Transit Transportation Investment Fund, 
with $4.5 million estimated additional annual revenue.
Then, it reduces the percentage of state gas tax the County gets from 14 percent to 7 
percent, effective July 1, 2021.  The gas tax allocated to cities and counties is based half 
on population and half on lane miles, and the County ’s population is less than 7 percent 
of the total County population, due to the incorporation of Millcreek incorporating and the 
creation of the metro townships.  
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member Granato, 
to support SB 150.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- SB 163 Community Reinvestment Agency Amendments
Mr. Blake Thomas , Director, Economic Development Division, stated this bill allows a 
Community Reinvestment Agency (CRA) to become a taxing entity and transfer new 
growth through an interlocal agreement at the end of a project.  
Council Member Bradshaw  stated this bill defeats the purpose of redevelopment 
agencies.  Once a redevelopment area is done, it should not be treated any different than 
any other property in terms of tax.  
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member Jensen, 
to oppose SB 158.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.

- SB 158 Urban Renewal Project Area Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill was written for Geneva Steel in Utah County.  It allows an 
Urban Renewal Project Area (URA) to extend funds for a project area without the 
approval of the taxing entity committee.  
Mr. Thomas stated this bill is tied specifically to a project area that meets the definition 
of an inactive industrial site.  It does not immediately affect the County, but it sets a 
precedent for other URAs to do this in the future.  He received some language for a 
substitute bill, which is still problematic.  It would allow a URA to hold out specific 
parcels and trigger those later compared to triggering them all at one time.  
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member Jensen, 
to oppose SB 158, and to work with the sponsor.  The motion passed unanimously.
- HB 299 Opportunity Zone Enhancements
Mr. Thomas stated this bill taps into the Department of Air Quality ’s (DEQ) incentives 
for installing electric vehicle supply equipment and has bonus points for being located in 
an opportunity zone.  It gives preference to Olene Walker Housing Loan Fund and 
Economic Revitalization and Investment Fund applications in an opportunity zone, as well 
as Utah Housing Corporation qualified development located in an opportunity zone.  It 
also creates a tax credit for construction costs for a parking structure in an opportunity 
zone.  Right now, the bill has a $ 16.5 million fiscal note.   His office is working on a 
substitute, mirroring language from South Carolina that also provides an incentive for 
grocers or farmer’s markets located in a food desert.  
Council Member Snelgrove  suggested tax incentives also be applied to projects that 
provide affordable housing.  
Mr. Thomas stated he could talk to the sponsor about that.  
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member Bradshaw, 
to support HB 299, and see if Mr. Thomas can get the substitute language put in.  Council 
Member Snelgrove amended the motion to include that Mr. Thomas work with the sponsor 
to include an affordable housing tax incentive.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- 1st Sub SB 108 Parking Structure Revolving Loan Program
Ms. Trevino stated this bill would allow Salt Lake County to create a Parking Structure 
Revolving Loan Fund and give it an annual funding source, which would be a portion of 
the County of the First Class Highway Projects Fund.

Committee of the Whole Meeting Minutes February 25, 2020
Ms. Dina Blaes , Associate Director, Regional Transportation & Planning Division, 
stated this came out as a way to accommodate many requests for parking structures .  
Basically, funds would flow to the County from the County of the First Class Highway 
Projects Fund.  Then, the County would put together a framework of policy guidelines 
and how those funds could be used.  Part of the goal would be to make sure the funds 
were used appropriately in terms of the parking requirements for mixed and shared use 
rather than just putting in a large parking structure, and finding a way to help meet some 
greater policy objectives with regard to vehicle miles traveled and how intensely people 
were parking in mixed use areas.  
Mayor Wilson  stated this would create seed money to start building a fund and would 
give the County a  broader toolbox, so that when a developer came to it asking for tax 
increment financing, it could offer another option.  If the County can get it up and running, 
it would take away some need to use tax-increment financing.   
Council Member Snelgrove  stated the number one priority should be to make sure this 
was for the betterment of the community and the number two priority should be to make 
sure it was not just for the enrichment of the developer.
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member Bradshaw, 
moved to support 1st Sub SB 108.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- SB 134 Property Tax Exemption for Wildfire Prevention
- SJR 10 Proposal to Amend Utah Constitution
Ms. Trevino stated these are joint bills that would require a constitutional amendment .  
SB 134 provides a property owner an abatement if they make improvements to their 
property to help with wildfire prevention.  It has a negative impact on the County ’s 
budget.  That was not the sponsor ’s intention, so the Utah Association of Counties is 
working with him.    
Council Member DeBry  stated he wanted to make sure the Unified Fire Service Area 
(UFSA) and Salt Lake Valley Law Enforcement Service Area (SLVLESA) were not 
impacted negatively.  
Ms. Catherine Kanter , Deputy Mayor of Regional Operations, stated a substitute bill 
carved those entities out.
Council Member Jensen  asked if this would require the County doing a judgment levy, 
for the rest of the taxing entities to pick up the abatement.  
Page 4 of 15
Committee of the Whole Meeting Minutes February 25, 2020
Mr. Darrin Casper , Deputy Mayor of Finance & Administration, stated the bill does not 
currently do that and it negatively impacts the County.  If the bill did that, it would not 
have an impact on any particular entity.
Council Member Burdick asked if there was a cap per year.
Ms. Kanter stated there was not an overall cap,  and there would be no way to estimate 
what the impact would be to the County because the property owner would seek the 
abatement after it spent the money.
A motion was made by Council Member DeBry, seconded by Council Member Jensen, to 
remain neutral on SB 134 and SJR 10, but work with the sponsor to ensure UFSA, 
SLVLESA, and the County are not impacted negatively.  The motion carried by a 
unanimous vote.
- HB 268 Property Tax Notice Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill has been amended.  The Council took a position to work 
with the sponsor on the bill because it was going to make the County reprogram its tax 
program to send out multiple notices.  
Mr. Stuart Tsai,   Auditor's Office, stated a property owner can now pick one person to 
have the notice sent to, rather than the Treasurer ’s Office being required to send out 
multiple paper notices.  
 
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member Snelgrove, 
to support 4th Sub. HB 164.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- HB 283 Outdoor Adventure Commission Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill creates an outdoor adventure commission to look at outdoor 
adventure from the standpoint of the residents, rather than tourism.  She brought this up 
to ensure the County got a representative on the commission.
Ms. Erin Litvack , Deputy Mayor of County Services, stated the committee will work 
and reach out to local organizations in every county to participate.  
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member Bradshaw, 
to support HB 283.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- HB 374 Local Government Building Regulation

Ms. Trevino stated this bill would allow a building permit applicant to be exempted from 
getting a local building inspection if their application was stamped by an engineer or an 
architect.
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member Jensen, 
to oppose HB 374.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- HB 324 Conviction Integrity Units
Mr. Sim  Gill , District Attorney, stated this bill provides for a prosecution agency to be 
able to create a conviction integrity unit to review convictions and make recommendations 
for changes in convictions and sentences obtained by the prosecution agency, and gives 
the prosecution agency discretion regarding a conviction integrity unit’ s 
recommendations.  It also requires notice to the victim if a petition is filed by the 
prosecution agency and gives district court the discretion to provide any relief.  
The committee would serve as independent advisory to the District Attorney ’s Office.  It 
would review cases where new evidence or errors were discovered.  It is a way to correct 
a wrongful conviction or sentence without undue delay.  It is really codification of what is 
already inherent power within the District Attorney’s Office.  
Mr. Rich Mauro , Executive Director, Salt Lake Legal Defender Association, stated the 
Salt Lake Legal Defender Association supports this bill.  Right now, there are a number of 
procedural roadblocks that prevent new evidence from being considered.  
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member Bradshaw, 
to support HB 324.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- HB 202 Local Government Nuisance Ordinance Reform
Ms. Trevino stated on February 11, 2020, the Council took a position to oppose this bill 
as written, but to allow the lobbying team to work with the sponsor on language.  The 
sponsor has made the changes the County requested of it.  
A motion was made by Council Member Jensen, seconded by Council Member Bradshaw, 
to support HB 202.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.

3.13 Unified Police Department Special Victims Unit Update 20-0078
Attachments: Staff Report
Special victims unit presentation
Presenters: Chief Kris Ownby and Sgt. Dan Moriarty (Approx. 20 min.)
Informational
Sheriff Rosie Rivera  stated Salt Lake County contracts with the Unified 
Police Department (UPD) for law enforcement services.  She wanted Council 
Members to know what units the County was paying for.  Today, they will 
be hearing about the Special Victims Unit.  
Chief Kris Ownby , Commander, Investigation Division, UPD, reviewed the 
Special Victims Unit (SVU), which investigates adult sex crimes, crimes 
involving children, and abuse on aging and vulnerable adults, as well as 
runaway kids and missing persons.  It gets approximately 1,200 cases a year, 
and it is seeing an increase in the number of cases being reported.  
Sgt  Dan Moriarty , Special Victims Unit, stated he supervises ten 
detectives and two civilians in the Special Victims Unit.  Two areas are no 
longer in the Special Victims Unit, yet the amount of cases it has is still equal 
Page 10 of 15
Committee of the Whole Meeting Minutes February 25, 2020
to what it was then, so sex crimes and child abuse cases are increasing.  Two 
detectives were added to the Special Victims Unit to help solve the buildup 
of cases, and they  have made a difference.  Before, detectives had 15 to 20 
cases a month; now, they have 10 to 15 cases a month.  There is a lot of 
stress on detectives in the Special Victims Unit.  The work they do impacts 
people for life, so they have a lot of responsibility to get things right.  
The Special Victims Unit has also been successful with getting the sex 
assault kits tested.  At one point, there were 435-437 untested sex kits sitting 
on the shelf, the backlog due to the amount of time and money they took to 
test.  Now, they are sent to the state crime lab automatically, and have all 
been tested.  The only untested cases are restricted cases, which are cases 
where the victim does not want police to take action.  
Mr. Sim Gill , District Attorney, stated every Special Victims Unit case has 
to be investigated with care and concern.  With regard to the rape kits, all are 
in the process of being tested and are yielding results in terms of people 
finally being prosecuted.  His office has filed 29 old cases, some for victims 
who were raped a decade ago.
