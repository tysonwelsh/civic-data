5.     ADOPT A RESOLUTION ESTABLISHING THE UTAH COUNTY PROSECUTORIAL REVIEW
       BOARD (Continued from the February 13, 2018 Commission meeting)
(Track A, 02:45)
When Commissioner Lee requested this item be continued without date, Commissioner Graves agreed so long as
public comment could be accepted today since they were allowed at the previous week’s meeting.

(Track A, 04:25)
Commissioner Lee mentioned he would like the opportunity to continue conversations about the proposed
Prosecutorial Review Board with several parties, including senators and the Utah Supreme Court Justice; hence the
request to table discussion. The three commissioners discussed the intention and possible complications of the
resolution with Utah County Attorney Jeffrey R. Buhman, with suggestions for updated language from Mr. Buhman.
Mr. Buhman presented statistics and information to explain why a Prosecutorial Review Board is unnecessary, and
Commissioner Graves expressed concern over potential misuse of taxpayer dollars.

(Track A, 48:40)
Commissioner Ivie invited public comment and Randy Kennard of the Utah County Attorney’s Office was the first to
step forward. Mr. Kennard described himself as a ‘typical prosecutor’ in the office and provided his background,
noting only eleven cases have been appealed in his 17 years of experience handling thousands of cases – none of these
were due to unethical conduct by Mr. Kennard. He talked about the judicial use of resources when each county
attorney has approximately 240 business days in one year to address upwards of 360 cases. Mr. Kennard equated this
proposed resolution to ‘friendly fire’ in a war, stating “It is bad enough to have to deal with the enemy without having
to look over your back to be able to see that you’re getting attacked somewhere else.” He summarized this Board is
looking for a solution to a problem that does not exist, and asked the commissioners to consider the consequences of
overburdening prosecutors and negatively affecting their ability to protect the community.

Utah County Sheriff James O. Tracy spoke as an elected official and also as a member of the Utah County Enforcement
Executives Association. Sheriff Tracy submitted a letter for the record (a copy of which can be found attached to
these minutes) and reiterated the many entities with oversight for prosecutors. He advised against the creation of this
review board. Salem City Police Chief Brad James read a letter (a copy of which can be found attached to these
minutes) supporting Sheriff Tracy’s comments and opposing this review board. Commissioner Lee mentioned he had
not been approached by any of the county prosecutors regarding this resolution and he would happily re-arrange his
schedule for further conversation. Utah County Deputy Attorney Christine Scott thanked him for offering to open his
doors and noted she would be setting a meeting with him. Ms. Scott also read prepared comments (a copy of which
can be found attached to these minutes) opposing the creation of this review board.

(Track A, 1:19:35)
Commissioner Graves mentioned he was not involved in drafting the proposed resolution and Commissioner Lee
questioned that statement. Commissioner Graves firmly noted his opposition, stating he supports Utah County
Attorney Jeffrey Buhman’s policy. Commissioner Lee pointed out the commissioners cannot legally collaborate on
creating resolutions, so Commissioner Graves’ note about his involvement with the draft may be accurate but still
misleading. Jason Groth of the American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) of Utah read prepared comments (a copy of
which can be found attached to these minutes) supporting the creation of this review board. Utah County Children’s
Justice Center Director Rebecca Martell added policies made with the best of intentions can have the worst
consequences; revisiting criminal cases for victims of domestic violence or sexual crimes against children can cause
irreversible damage. She strongly recommended the commissioners invite input from the Council for Victims of
Crime, a statewide panel for these victims, before moving forward and volunteered to provide contact information to
the Board.

(Track A, 1:29:35)
Sergeant Kerry Cole of the Saratoga Springs Police Department talked about his interactions with the Utah County
Attorney’s Office throughout the last three decades and commended them for operating with integrity and ‘setting a
very high bar.’ Sergeant Cole believes changing how things are done within that office will be detrimental to their
operations. Lieutenant Dennis Harris of the Utah County Sheriff’s Office has been in law enforcement for almost
forty years and does not view the creation of a Prosecutorial Review Board positively. He commented on the extensive
career of Utah County Attorney Jeffrey Buhman, reminding the commissioners that position is also elected and Mr.
Buhman must answer to the citizens who put him in office. Former Provo City Councilmember Steve Turley shared
his negative experiences with the Utah County Attorney’s Office which, he explained, have ‘wrecked his life and took
away reputation that no amount of money can give back.’ Mr. Turley talked about being falsely accused and expressed
support for the creation of this Prosecutorial Review Board.

(Track A, 1:47:20)
Utah County Deputy Attorney Carl Hollan gave examples of defendants seeking to excuse their behavior by shifting
blame onto others, a common defense in criminal cases. Mr. Hollan noted the creation of this review board would
validate attempts to hold criminals blameless. He outlined many questions regarding consequences for those accused
of misconduct, including the open-to-interpretation use of the word itself. Mr. Hollan stated, “Prosecutors accused by
those attempting to shift blame and focus away from their own bad conduct would thus enjoy none of the protections
that the criminal accusers enjoyed at their own trial. This feels less like a committee […] and more like an inquisition
board.” Mr. Hollan pointed out this Commission is failing to address several other problems – homelessness,
environmental health, employee wages, trampoline parks, the suicide epidemic, the opioid crisis, and transportation –
while pushing forward this Prosecutorial Review Board. He questioned their mode of prioritization in not taking on
those issues where they can impact meaningful change and instead pursuing a process to undermine the system of
government and is likely unconstitutional. Commissioner Lee countered it is inaccurate to state the Board has not
made progress on the homelessness issue with the Sheriff’s Office as they are actively working on a proposal.
Commissioner Graves voiced shame in being part of this process, stating he respects both the offices of the Utah
County Sheriff and the Utah County Attorney and expressed thanks to all of them.

11:00 A.M. – Commissioner Graves excused himself from the meeting at this time.

(Track A, 1:58:20)
Justin Gordon, President of the Utah County Deputies Association and member of the Fraternal Order of Police
supports the Utah County Attorney’s Office and prosecutors. Mr. Gordon discourages the idea of increasing their
workload and ‘tying their hands’ with additional oversight. He also questions the intentions of potential review board
members. Donna Whiting has worked as a Victim Advocate in American Fork for nine years and sees Utah County
prosecutors as being extremely conscientious. She does not think this committee would help victims of crime in any
way. Robyn Krushinsky recently joined the County Attorney’s Office after working with courts for twelve years; the
local prosecutors have a great reputation for being thorough in their investigations and she wonders why the county
would ‘start and feed a fire that just isn’t there’ through the creation of this review board.

Jason Randall of the Utah County Sheriff’s Office supervises the Special Victims Unit and also works with the Special
Victims Task Force. He is concerned that additional constraint on the prosecutors will ultimately result in fewer
convictions against criminals. Attorney and Pleasant Grove resident Ben Stanley commended the courage of law
enforcement champions advocating for this proposal. Mr. Stanley explained the need for oversight and review to
prevent morally reprehensible outcomes such as in the case of Roger and Pam Mortenson where there were tremendous
miscarriages of justice. Lieutenant Erik Knutzen of the Utah County Sheriff’s Office supervises the Investigations
Division and is involved in the Special Victims Task Force. He repeated earlier comments about the checks and
balances already in place for prosecutors. Utah County Deputy Attorney Jared Perkins also outlined the many
oversights, including the election of the Utah County Attorney and the personal conscience of the individual
prosecutors.

(Track A, 2:22:55)
Utah County Criminal Division Chief Deputy Attorney Tim Taylor asked Commissioner Lee who drafted the
resolution, and Commissioner Lee explained multiple individuals and groups from law enforcement to the American
Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) and defense attorneys were involved. Mr. Taylor noted the county attorneys were not
included despite extending many invitations in the past to work with the commissioners. Utah County Deputy
Attorney Pona Sitake expressed concern that non-county personnel are advising commissioners on county documents,
pointing out this review board would be a public body subject to the Open Public Meetings Act if created and subject
to frivolous lawsuits against itself. Utah County Deputy Attorney Adam Pomeroy commented on civil rights and
suggested having outside experts review internal policies before making rash decisions.

Utah County Deputy Attorney Lance Bastian echoed statements praising the ethics of Utah County prosecutors. Mr.
Bastian acknowledged the system may have flaws but there is genuine desire to find truth and justice on all sides. He
gave practical examples of the burden of proof for prosecutors in criminal cases. Mr. Bastian also pointed out to
Commissioner Lee that while Commissioner Lee has just stated his ‘door is open,’ the commissioner has not made
any attempt to knock on the doors of the prosecutors across the hallway from him. Mr. Bastian suggested that if
something is brought to their attention that causes concern, they could try speaking to the prosecutor rather than quietly
approach the ACLU and hastily comprise an ill-defined committee.

Dan Thomas strongly opposes this resolution, simply stating “the scope of county government is to deal with county
problems.” Utah County Deputy Attorney Craig Johnson provided an example of how the Prosecutorial Review
Board would prevent him from doing his job. Commissioner Ivie thanked everyone for their comments but noted that
just because the problem is not happening here does not mean it is not happening. He spoke of his belief in the
American governing system and summarized something can be done to make sure a problem that exists in the world
does not become a problem in Utah County. Commissioner Ivie reiterated this proposed resolution is not meant to be
an attack on the County Attorney’s Office, and Commissioner Lee stated he looks forward to continuing the
conversation and working through this issue.
