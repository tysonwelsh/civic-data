2. Discussion Items

   2.1. Transportation Master Plan Discussion – Bryce Terry, Assistant City Engineer

   Assistant City Engineer Bryce Terry Introduced the U-111 alternatives evaluation. He briefed over
   the realignment study done by Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT). The City conducted
   an additional study for the transportation master plan to determine the best alignment. A traffic
   consultant was hired to look at three different options to determine impacts relating to the
   potential U-111 alignments. Option 1 would be a direct tie to Herriman Blvd. Option 2 would be a
   direct connection down to 7300 West and Herriman Main Street. Option 3 was a connection to
   6400 West. He detailed how the traffic scenarios would impact city streets as well as land use,
   which would include development. Staff recommended option 3 as it would balance the traffic
   flow east to west. It would also create a central location to allow for commercial development
   and commercial access from north to south. He stated staff was looking for an official position of
   a preferred alignment to pass along to UDOT.

   Assistant City Engineer Terry asked if there were any questions. Commissioner Darryl Fenn
   questioned how far along was the UDOT study. Engineer Terry responded UDOT was conducting
   the environmental study and are starting to look at the alignments. Commissioner Fenn inquired
   if the alignments for the environmental work was available. Engineer Terry explained a previous
   feasibility study initially eliminated four of the seven potential alignments. Two out of the three
   options discussed were included in the original study, the third alternative alignment was created
   by Herriman City staff. Alignments always change. Councilmember Teddy Hodges inquired if the
   alignment being proposed was similar to the other vested parties’ alignment, such as the
   developer. Engineer Terry responded it was pointing out the intersection as the main discussion
   point between all parties. Councilmember Hodges questioned if the road would tie off to the
   intersection at 12600 South and require a left-hand turn. Engineer Terry confirmed it would
   eventually be an intersection.

Councilmember Ohrn inquired how much extra traffic would flow to Main Street. Engineer Terry
explained there would be low volume of traffic with the third option and anticipated a lot of
traffic utilizing 6400 West to access 13400 South. Councilmember Ohrn expressed concern with
the roundabout currently in South Jordan. Engineer Terry responded there are many
roundabouts which work well and acknowledged there are some needing to be redesigned. He
highlighted the multilane roundabouts along 11400 South which seem to cause confusion until
people become accustomed to them, after which, become more efficient and safer than a traffic
signal. He agreed the intersections need to be designed correctly in the beginning. Commissioner
Forrest Sickles inquired how option three compared to the original site plan. Engineer Terry
responded Olympia Main was being upsized based on this plan. Commissioner Sickles asked
about the right-of-way width through the area. Engineer Terry highlighted where the road would
be expanded to five lanes. Councilmember Ohrn confirmed there would be five lanes
transitioning into three lanes on 13400 South.

Councilmember Ohrn inquired the cost of the road and maintenance. Engineer Terry responded
the cost of the road would be on the developer, but the cost of maintenance would come from
the City. Commissioner Fenn asked for a cost comparison for each option presented. Engineer
Terry explained there would be three lanes in each scenario so the maintenance should be
similar. Commissioner Fenn asked if there would be any free-flowing east-west lanes. Engineer
Terry explained UDOT was currently studying the future 12600 South. Mayor Palmer interjected
the desire outlined in the Olympia Master Development Agreement was to push for the
intersection to be located in Olympia boundaries. Commissioner Rypien asked if 12600 South
would be the main road, or were others considered. Councilmember Shields commented having
an intersection would be more conducive for commercial retail rather than being free-flowing
road. Commissioner Rypien questioned why the intersections would not be incorporated.
Engineer Terry reviewed the scenarios with the benefit and downfall for commercial use and
impacts. Commissioner Rypien commented he was stuck on the contour of the road, stating
curvature is usually done because of a need. Engineer Terry explained curves could help slow
traffic and don’t hurt the flow. Mayor Palmer commented on the worry of bringing people out of
high-density areas.

Commissioner Fenn inquired about plans for the old U-111. Engineer Terry was unaware of what
the plan would entail; however, stated it was not removed from unincorporated Salt Lake
County. Commissioner Rypien asked which road was going through Olympia to 6000 West.
Engineer Terry responded it was Silver Sky Drive.

Councilmember Hodges inquired where the location of the new trail head access would be
located. Engineer Terry oriented the group as to the location of the trail head access.
Commissioner Rypien inquired about the plan to extend Herriman Highway. Engineer Terry
replied staff had been working with the county to extend the road out to the trailhead.

   2.2. Auto Mall Update – Nathan Cherpeski, City Manager

   City Manager Nathan Cherpeski referred to the Council’s last meeting in which three participation
   agreements were approved. He briefed over master development agreement adoption history
   and the step-by-step process which would be followed, then reviewed some changes being
   considered within those areas. He indicated an area on the map, which was being examined by
   the developer, for not only the auto mall, but for commercial uses as well. The developer was
   also considering expansion areas for commercial development. He pointed out the Garden plots
   explaining there had been discussions surrounding residential development; however,
   acknowledged the commercial zoning designation. He noted the developer was working on a
   public infrastructure district (PID) governing documents for consideration by the City Council.

   Manager Cherpeski asked if there were any questions and informed the group, they did have
   representatives from the developer present. Commissioner Rypien question a particular area on
   the map and asked if it was being considered for mixed use development. Manager Cherpeski
   explained the area was still owned by the City and outlined the current uses. Commissioner
   Rypien asked about the acreage being in mixed-use. Manager Cherpeski confirmed the mixed-use
   portion of the development would be between thirteen to fourteen acres. Mayor Palmer added
   there was still a lot of interest from dealerships. Manager Cherpeski detailed what auto
   dealerships entail. Councilmember Hodges commented there was a lot of different architecture
   coming through the area. Commissioner Garcia questioned what additional uses the developer’s
   requested. Manager Cherpeski answered the crescent piece would be some of the auto mall
   uses. Mayor Palmer interjected low impact commercial was under consideration due to the
   proximity to residential development. Commissioner Sickles questioned if it would be permitted
   or conditional use. Mayor Palmer brought up the point it would be incorporated into the MDA.
   Commissioner Bradford asked what the time frame would be for development. Some discussion
   ensued about possible start dates.

   Manager Cherpeski reiterated this was an update on what could be coming and asked for any
   further questions. Mayor Palmer commented a good plan was in place. Commissioner Garcia
   inquired about the elevation height. The developer responded it was unknown at this time.
   Mayor Palmer thanked everyone for input.
