Proposed amendments to Utility Billing Fees
           Treasurer Johnson explained that in conjunction with the City's continued implementation of the Tyler Enterprise
Resource Planning (ERP) Solutions system, the City's administration is proposing changes to billing and operational fees for the
water, sewer and refuse utilities. These changes are not related to utility fees such as water or sewer rates; rather, the changes are
generally related to updating fees and standards for associated services and account maintenance issues. Ms. Johnson then
introduced Gabe Johns, Senior Accountant, who has been the lead employee on the implementation ofthe recommendations of the
utility rate study.
           Mr. Johns used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on proposed amendments to the City's Utility Billing
Ordinance; he reported utility billing adjustments is the fmal phase ofthe ERP implementation and a marketing plan will be put in
place to alert customers of coming changes. Tyler ERP has reviewed the City's existing practices from three viewpoints: can
practices be clarified; do they need to be updated; and is the City using the best software and industry practices. Changes are
limited to ancillary fees and practices; utility rates will be discussed as part of the utility rate study; and proposed changes will not
be retroactive. He then provided a comparison of current and proposed fees and policies for individual fees within the utility rate
structure:

           Throughout the presentation there was discussion among the Council and Accounting staff regarding operational policies,
such as grace periods that would apply to payment timelines/late fee imposition; the responsible party for bills and late fees for
rental properties; benchmarking data provided by other cities and special service districts that was used to inform this proposal;
and opportunities for communicating information regarding a hardship assistance program for residents struggling to pay their
utility bills. Chair Blair stated it would be helpful for the Council to understand how much revenue the City stands to collect if the
fee adjustments are approved. Chief Deputy Attorney Stratford then noted the current proposal is for the Council to consider an
ordinance that would adjust the fees as specified by Mr. Johns; Administration can provide additional information as requested
and the Council could make further adjustments at a later date, but Administration would still like for the Council to consider
acting on the current proposal.

Council Business
         2021 Utah Legislative Session: Vice Chair White reported on the bills that City Administration is tracking in the 2021
Legislative Session; some of the bills would impact land use regulations and billboard laws in the City; if some of those bills are
approved, they could negatively impact the City. She thanked Management Services Director Brown for actively working to
address legislation and to keep the Council informed of the status of vital bills.


         The meeting adjourned at 5:25p.m.
