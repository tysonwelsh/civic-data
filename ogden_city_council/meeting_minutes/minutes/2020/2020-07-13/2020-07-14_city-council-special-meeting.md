Proposed Ordinance 2020-30, amending the Ogden City General Plan by
approving and adopting the Southeast Ogden Community Plan
         A memo from the Community and Economic Development Department regarding the Southeast Ogden Community Plan
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated City Administration has proposed an update to the Southeast Ogden
Community Plan. The community plan covers the area generally south of 36th Street and east of Gramercy Avenue. The
community plan sets forth vision statements for land use, housing, parks and open space, and transportation. The community plan
provides vision strategies for topics and issues within each of the vision statement areas. The Southeast Ogden Community Plan
update will be the first major rewrite of the community plan for the area since 1987. The Council reviewed the proposed plan at
the February 11, 2020 work session. At that meeting, questions arose about accessory dwelling units, code enforcement and
student housing. During this meeting the Council requested additional work session time be allocated for further discussion and
directed that members of the Planning Commission be invited to share their perspective regarding the plan. The Council held a

work session on March 10, 2020 to discuss the plan further. Members of the Planning Commission were invited to attend and
representatives from the City's Code Services Division were present to discuss code enforcement issues.
          Deputy Planning Manager Spencer summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to orient the
Council to the content of the Plan document. He presented an aerial image of Ogden City and highlighted the area identified as the
Southeast Ogden Community; it contains 2,157 acres and is approximately 12 percent of the City's total area. It has a population
of 9,674, or 11 percent of the City's total population. He discussed the history of the Plan dating back to 1987 when it was
adopted; it has been adjusted over the years as properties have been developed and zoning designations have changed. A 2017
petition to amend the Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) regulations in the Southeast Ogden Cmmnunity Plan area spurred this Plan
update. A cmmnunity plan is essentially a vision statement for future development of the area to occur; it is tailored to meet the
needs of a specific area and is a unique plan for the area that is also incorporated in the City's General Plan. Issues identified
throughout the Plan review process relate to AD Us, student housing, traffic, open space, and future land use considerations. Mr.
Spencer briefly reviewed the accomplishments of the existing Plan, including:
               • Design and construction of Skyline Parkway connection;
               • Browning Park and Shadow Valley Elementary School;
               • Welcome sign installation on Harrison Boulevard;
               •    Glasmann Pond construction;
               • Bonneville Shoreline, Birch Creek and Beus Canyon Trails developed;
               • New McKay Dee Hospital campus construction; and
               •    Several zoning changes.
          Since the Plan's creation in 1987, there are now 11.24 new miles ofroad, 119 new subdivisions, 941 new buildings with
893 of those being homes, and a population increase of 3,182. The community involvement process for this Plan updated included
meetings with Weber State University (WSU) students in March 2018 to discuss student housing, launching the Southeast Ogden
Community Plan website in 2018 (with 2,455 total views to date), and the first open house meeting held in January of 2019. This
meeting was advertised via 2,500 mailed notices and on social media; there were over 100 people in attendance. This meeting was
followed by steering committee meetings to discuss land use, transpmtation, housing, and open space. An additional meeting was
held with WSU to discuss the future of student housing to accommodate student population growth for the next five to 10 years.
Finally, the second open house meeting was held September of 2019 and over 60 residents were in attendance.

use, transportation, and open space. This led to a discussion about the input provided by the steering committees, specifically the
committee assigned to address housing and the topic of ADUs. Mr. Spencer then discussed the recommendations of the steering
committees that have been incorporated into the Plan docwnent. The docwnent includes land use objectives and strategies as well
as the following vision statement: "For Southeast Ogden to be a community that strengthens single-family neighborhoods, protects

          Mr. Spencer then stated the vision statement for the housing section of the proposed Plan is, "preserving existing single-
family neighborhoods, while providing additional housing options for the future growth anticipated in the Southeast Ogden
Community over the next 10-15 years." The Plan contemplates the difference between traditional rental prope1ties and ADUs. In a
traditional rental or duplex scenario, the first unit can contain a family unit and one related adult and the second unit can contain
the same or up to three unrelated adults. For an owner-occupied ADU, the first unit can contain a family unit with one related
adult, but the ADU can only have the family unit. Educating the residents of the Southeast Ogden C01mnunity Plan area about the
difference between rentals and AD Us was necessary in order to proceed with identifying the neighborhoods in the Plan area that
could accommodate AD Us. He presented a map to identify the areas that have been deemed appropriate for ADUs. This included

              Mr. Spencer discussed the final strategy of the Plan dealing with transportation; the vision statement for this component
    is, "Southeast Ogden is a community where all people can enjoy transportation choices that fit their needs and an infrastructure
    that supports safety, accessibility, and connected neighborhoods." The Plan calls for the integration of Bicycle Master Plan
    objectives in the Southeast Ogden community by improving prevalence of bicycle parking infrastructure at commercial locations
    and creating physically projected bike lanes along Harrison Boulevard. The City's Bicycle Master Plan could also be amended
    responsive to additions that are needed based upon physical conditions in the Southeast Ogden community. Considerations may
    include ways to implement bike lanes on downhill slopes of Shadow Valley Drive or other means to protect downhill slopes,
    facilitating connectivity of a bike lane from Shadow Valley Elementary School to 4600 South, and pursuing creative means of
    dealing with physical limitations on Country Hills Drive from Beus Pond north to provide bike protection on downhill slope. The
    Plan also calls for improved traffic flow through the community and specifically in and out of the WSU campus. Objectives
    related to this matter include encouraging WSU to create a connective road system to Dee Events connecting 4600 South with
    Country Hills Drive as the BRT develops and exploring safe and legal routes as BRT develops to create a designated route from
    Country Hills to Village Drive on WSU campus behind commercial businesses in that area. Additionally, it is necessary to address
    missing connections within neighborhoods to disperse traffic in the following areas:
                    • Navajo Drive to 5025 South;
                    • Ridgedale Drive with Bonneville Terrace Drive;
                    •    Glasmann Way from south to 4200 South; and
                    •    Detennine appropriate connection for Eccles Avenue near 3900 South to replace unofficial pattern connecting
                         Eccles through the Intennountain Health Care (IHC) property.
               Mr. Spencer then cited a list oflocations throughout the Southeast Ogden community to address issues such as: speeding,
    on street parking, traffic congestion, and pedestrian access and safety. Safe routes to school are a high priority in relation to
    pedestrian safety as well as upholding ADA standards throughout the community. Transportation infrastructure must be
    maintained in good repair and one of the topics that was raised throughout discussions of the transportation component of the Plan
    was the ability for residents to communicate transportation deficiencies to the City. The suggestion was made to create a user-
    friendly reporting mechanism that could help the City to prioritize and address maintenance issues. Finally, in relation to
    transportation, the Plan calls for integration of the BRT project in the community. This includes implementation of Transit
    Oriented Development (TOD) zoning around BRT stations, coordination with the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) to notify
    residents about bus changes, exploring the installation of protected bike lanes during the BRT construction phase, creating a park
    and ride lot at the Dee Events Center, and creating first and last mile solutions.
               Mr. Spencer concluded by summarizing the remaining steps in the process for consideration of the Plan document. The
    Planning Commission has provided a unanimous recommendation to approve the Plan. The next action lies with the City Council
    and once the Plan is adopted, staff can begin work to implement plan goals this year. Initial steps in the Plan implementation
    phase include zoning amendments for ADUs to set exact ADU thresholds for specific areas in the Plan area and making
    amendments to the ADU ordinance to allow enforcement. Additionally, zoning amendments would be recommended for the TOD
    overlay/rezone areas and for student housing overlay areas.
