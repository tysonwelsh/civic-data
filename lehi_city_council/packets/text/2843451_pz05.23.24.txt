70   3.2) Public hearing and consideration of Boyd Brown’s request for approval of the
71   Exchange Business Park Concept Plan, an 8-lot commercial development located at
72   approximately 1500 North 3600 West, with an exception to have parking between the street
73   and building and an exception to the building footprint.
74
75   After presenting on the item City Planner Brittney Harris went into the background on what
76   neighborhood commercial is because there have been a lot of questions.
77
78   Commissioners asked clarifying questions for staff. They inquired about the height limitations of
79   the footprints, specifically whether buildings could be three stories tall, noting the need for
80   compatibility with neighboring residential areas, which are primarily two-story condos. The staff
81   responded by confirming the height restriction to two stories to ensure compatibility with the
82   surrounding residential buildings.
83
84   Applicant Boyd Brown presented the project to the Commissioners, addressing their questions
85   about site challenges, including limited access and power easements. He explained the need for a
86   footprint size exception to accommodate users like a daycare and confirmed that the buildings,
87   though designed separately, might appear larger due to their layout. Mr. Brown assured the
88   Commissioners that the design complied with zoning requirements and highlighted the inclusion
89   of pedestrian-friendly amenities, such as a bike path and gathering spaces, to create a
90   community-focused area.
91
     Lehi City Planning Commission                   2                                     May 23, 2024
 92   Chairman Jackson opened public hearing.
 93
 94   Scott Larsen: expressed concerns about parking and traffic near his home at 1500 North 3661
 95   West. He explained that parking had become a significant issue after a bike lane was added, and
 96   residents were now asked to park behind their garages. He was also surprised to learn that a
 97   small alley behind his house would be used for commercial access. Additionally, Mr. Larsen
 98   highlighted the severe traffic congestion at the 1500 and 3600 intersection and wondered if a
 99   traffic light would be installed. While not opposed to the commercial development, he was
100   concerned about the impact on parking and traffic.
101
102   Cheryl Levinson: stated concerns about the already overloaded road systems in her area and the
103   potential for further congestion with new commercial and residential developments. She also
104   questioned the clarity of explanations regarding the Commission's decisions on footprint sizes
105   and was worried about additional noise and parking issues from small-scale businesses.
106
107   Britt Nyborg: proposed converting Building C into a community plaza with seating and greenery
108   to address traffic issues and improve neighborhood safety. She also suggested repurposing
109   parking areas for a UTA bus stop and advocated for more green space, appreciating the board's
110   efforts.
111
112   Michael Brown: understands the importance of development but was worried about the loss of
113   green space due to new development. He questioned why a two-story building was being placed
114   on the corner, noting that it obstructed the mountain view he initially enjoyed when he bought
115   his condo, and wondered why this particular location was chosen for the new development.
116
117   Cheryl Levinson again: she's wondering about negotiating with the development putting in the
118   infrastructure. She's curious if that has ever been on the radar.
119
120   Scott Larsen again: he is concerned about the garbage cans, and it would be hard to even get one
121   car through.
122
123   Ms. Harris summarized the online comments on Engage Lehi and email sent. Concerns included
124   the use of native plants and open space, access for emergency vehicles, traffic impacts and
125   signals, separation of residential and business traffic, and commercial parking use outside
126   business hours. There was also a question about water table considerations, though clarification
127   was needed on whether it related to soil or water availability.
128
129   Chairman Jackson closed public hearing.
130
131   Mr. Brown addressing concerns about the project, emphasizing its design benefits like reducing
132   neighborhood traffic and aligning with the original master plan. Staff discussed planned traffic
133   improvements, potential changes to parking and bike lanes, and water table management.
134   Commissioners debated exceptions to code requirements, particularly regarding building
135   placement and parking, suggesting adjustments to better fit the neighborhood while maintaining
136   necessary access. The discussion aimed to balance code adherence with community needs and
137   project benefits.

140   Mr. Chambers outlined three key aspects the commissioners need to focus on: 1) Whether the
141   project meets the existing code requirements, 2) Evaluating the access options and exceptions
142   presented by the applicant, and 3) If additional changes or exceptions are desired, those should
143   be addressed through tabling the application for further review rather than altering the current
144   submission. He emphasized that feedback can be provided, but the final approval should be
145   based on the applicant’s submitted plan and code compliance.
146
147           Motion: Commissioner Eyre moved to approve the exception from where the parking
148           between the street and the building, but does not approve the building size footprint
149           stating that they did not meet their burden of proof for Boyd Brown’s request for
150           approval of the Exchange Business Park Concept Plan, an 8-lot commercial development
151           located at approximately 1500 North 3600 West, with an exception to have parking
152           between the street and building and an exception to the building footprint; including all
153           findings stated in the report and all DRC comments. Commissioner Eyre seconded.
154
155           Vote: Commissioner Jackson, yes; Commissioner Everett, nay; Commissioner Eyre, yes;
156           Commissioner Lockhart, yes; Commissioner Kunze, yes. The motion passed.
