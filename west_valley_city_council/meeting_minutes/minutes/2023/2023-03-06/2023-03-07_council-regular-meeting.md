RESOLUTION 23-17: AUTHORIZE WEST VALLEY CITY TO ENTER INTO A
MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING WITH THE UNITED STATES
MARSHALS SERVICE FUGITIVE TASK FORCE
Nicole Cottle, Acting City Manager, discussed proposed Resolution 23-17 that would
authorize West Valley City to Enter into a Memorandum of Understanding with the United
States Marshals Service Fugitive Task Force

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       In an effort to coordinate local law enforcement resources in the apprehension of
       persons who have active state and federal warrants for their arrest, law enforcement
       agencies of the greater Salt Lake Valley, State and Federal agencies have united
       efforts and resources to form the United States Marshals Service Fugitive Task
       Force. Members of the police department are deputized through the Marshals
       Service and generally participate in a part-time role. The police department has
       worked in partnership with the US Marshals Service for many years and this
       partnership continuously aids in the apprehension of some of the most violent
       offenders in our community. This agreement clarifies the responsibilities of
       participating agencies to establish the groundwork for the administration and
       operation of the taskforce.
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING MARCH 7, 2023
                          -3-

       The primary mission of the task force is to investigate and arrest, as part of joint
       law enforcement operations, persons who have active state and federal warrants for
       their arrest. The intent of the joint effort is to investigate and apprehend local, state
       and federal fugitives, thereby improving public safety and reducing violent crime.
       Targeted crimes will primarily include violent crimes against persons, weapons
       offenses, felony drug offenses, failure to register as a sex offender, and crimes
       committed by subjects who have a criminal history involving violent crimes, felony
       drug offenses, and/or weapons offense. Task force personnel will be assigned
       federal, state, and local fugitive cases for investigation.

Upon inquiry by Mayor Lang there were no further questions from members of the City

RESOLUTION 23-19: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO PROVIDE A GUARANTEE
LETTER TO THE INTERNATIONAL OLYMPIC COMMITTEE
Nicole Cottle, Acting City Manager, discussed proposed Resolution 23-19 that would
authorize the City to Provide a Guarantee Letter to the International Olympic Committee

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       West Valley City and the Salt Lake City-Utah Committee for the Games (SLC-
       UTCG) have entered into an Olympic Venue Use Agreement that will allow the
       Committee exclusive use of the Maverik Center to host Olympic Men’s and
       Women’s Ice Hockey during the Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games of 2030
       or the Olympic & Paralympic Winter Games of 2034.

       SLC-UTCG is the organization working jointly with the United States Olympic and
       Paralympic Committee (USOPC) to bring the Olympic Winter Games to Utah in
       2030 or 2034. The final bid and subsequent decision from the International
       Olympic Committee (IOC) are set for 2023 or later.
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING MARCH 7, 2023
                          -5-

       It is anticipated that the IOC will invite SLC-UTCG into a Targeted Dialogue
       regarding hosting a future Olympic and Paralympic Winter Games sometime in
       2023. As part of that process, SLC-UTCG must obtain initial guarantees that
       confirm a range of information and obligations from public authorities and other
       organizations. In order to stay ahead of this process, SLC-UTCG hopes to complete
       the guarantees by March 31, 2023.

       West Valley City government demonstrated unwavering support that underpinned
       the success of the Salt Lake 2002 Olympic Winter and Paralympic Winter Games.
       SLC-UTCG now looks to build on that foundation.

RESOLUTION 23-28: AWARD A CONTRACT TO CODY EKKER
CONSTRUCTION INC. FOR A STORM DRAIN INSTALLATION ON
TOULOUSE STREET
Nicole Cottle, Acting City Manager, discussed proposed Resolution 23-28 that would
award a Contract to Cody Ekker Construction Inc. for a Storm Drain Installation on Toulouse
Street

Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as
follows:

       In the fall of 2022, the Public Works Department became aware of basement
       flooding on Toulouse Street, during a period of no rain, thus basement flooding
       from storm drainage seemed unlikely. The flooded basement is immediately
       adjacent to a corrugated metal pipe, installed in the early 1970s, draining the City’s
       street water into the South Jordan Canal. The pipe sits below the water level of the
       canal keeping the pipe full of water all the time, it is suspected that canal water
       saturates the ground around the pipe near the home and seeps into the basement
       through the compromised aging corrugated metal pipe. In late 2022 and early 2023,
       the Public Works Department planned for the Operations Division to replace the
       pipe in place using in-house forces. As the design progressed, it became clear that
       the best long-term solution did not include replacement in-place. UDOT recently
       acquired property at 3766 West Toulouse Street to accommodate work on the 4700
       South Bangerter Highway interchange. There is an existing storm drain installed
       in Toulouse Street, that dead-ends at Bangerter Highway that could be used to drain
       the neighborhood. The Public Works Department determined that the best long-
       term solution for the City would include a connection to the existing dead-end storm
       drain, running a new pipe through the recently purchased UDOT property, and from
       there connecting into the South Jordan Canal. This new storm drain location would
       enable the City to connect to a future Bangerter Highway storm drainage system,
    MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING MARCH 7, 2023
                          -12-

      ultimately removing the City’s water from the Canal, and improving the area
      drainage capacity. Additionally, the new location will take the storm drain away
      from the flooding basement, minimizing the possibility of future flooding if the
      canal water seeping through the pipe is the flood water source.

      The storm drain work needs to be completed before the South Jordan Canal begins
      running water in early April. As design and permitting for the new storm drain
      design progressed, it became clear that traditional contractor procurement methods
      would not allow the work to be completed before the canal water returns. For these
      reasons, the Public Works Department is requesting authorization to award a
      contract under Section 5-3-112 (Emergencies, Public Threats and Unforeseen
      Conditions) of the City’s procurement code.

      The Engineering Division reached out to several contractors seeking interest and
      capacity to do the work within the necessary timeframe, which did not yield many
      interested companies. Cody Ekker Construction expressed interest in the work and
      has the expertise and ability to fit the work into their schedule to accommodate the
      canal deadline.
