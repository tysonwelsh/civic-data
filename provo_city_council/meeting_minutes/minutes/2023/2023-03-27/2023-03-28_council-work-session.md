4.        A resolution approving a substantial amendment to the program year 2021 Annual
          Action Plan incorporating funds for HOME-ARP and approve the HOME-ARP
          allocation. (23-027) (01:04:27)

Melissa McNalley, Community Grant Administrator, presented. In September of 2021, Provo
was awarded $5,144,957 in home ARP funding on behalf of the Utah Valley HOME Consortium.
These funds are intended to be used to serve people who are experiencing homelessness or
near homelessness and fleeing or attempting to flee domestic violence, sexual abuse, human
trafficking, and stalking. In the fall of 2022, Provo staff started working with local organizations
to determine gaps in service and decide where the money was needed most. After
consultations and feedback, the staff is recommending funds be used for Supportive Services
and Tenant Based Rental Assistance – both to be operated by a non-profit organization.
Councilor Whipple discussed the need for a Non-Congregate Shelter. Councilor Fillmore asked
about the long-term plan for when the money runs out. Keith Morey, Director of Economic
Development, added that after the legislation passed, the intention is to create committees to
develop long-term plans. Councilor Handley echoed Councilor Whipple’s statements. Councilor
Shipley asked if the funds can be reallocated if the needs change in a few years. He also asked
Ms. McNalley to share how the ten percent allocated for administration was determined and
how that amount will support what is being budgeted in this proposal. Ms. McNalley shared
that changes can be made, and funds can be reallocated if needed later. She also spoke about
the administration costs. Presentation only. This item was already scheduled for the Council
Meeting on March 28, 2023.

7.        An ordinance amending Provo City code regarding micro mobility devices. Citywide
          application. (23-022) (01:35:42)

Mary Ditto, City Attorney, presented. She shared the code would include six sections if the
amendments were approved. The first section includes the definition. She shared the difficulty
of trying to add broad language to this section to include all types of devices, even new devices
to come. Section two prohibits the use of the devices on the sidewalks. Section three deals with
safety. She stated that the helmet requirement was removed, and the section mostly discusses
visibility. Section four prohibits the devices from obstructing sidewalks, so they do not inhibit
others where they are parked. Section five deals with personal property owners. Section six
states vendors renting the devices would only be allowed if they have a contract with the city.
Councilor Handley suggested if there is not a requirement for helmets, a public education
campaign is needed to discuss the importance of wearing helmets. Brian Jones discussed the
speed capability requirements in the state code. The council discussed the need for a speed
limit requirement on sidewalks, how it would be monitored per device, and how it would be
enforced.

11.       A presentation regarding efforts addressing homelessness in Provo City- Wasatch
          Behavioral Health. (23-018) (04:10:45)

Monte Memmott, of Wasatch Behavioral Health, presented. He shared that the statistics show
improvement during and after mental health court. He talked about the Wasatch Assistance
Team Counseling the Homeless (WATCH) program, a program that has been around for 20
years that was designed to help the homeless. He shared one of the biggest obstacles the
program has overcome is getting individuals signed up for Medicaid to help get treatment. He
discussed the Jail Transition program, a program that assists newly released inmates get back
on their feet. Both programs are run by the same staff and their focus is to provide resources to
individuals. Mr. Memmott said the priority of the programs is to get individuals the resources
they need. Those resources include housing, employment, and food stamps. After those basic
needs are met, they focus on secondary needs such as mental health treatment, therapy, and
medication management. He shared that they see roughly 200 individuals per month who are
experiencing some form of homelessness. He said of those 200 individuals, 20 of them are
people they have not seen before. Councilor Shipley asked about the capacity of people they
can help and if there are limitations to certain resources available. Mr. Memmott said one of
the biggest limitations is the number of housing vouchers that are available. He also said that
there are staffing limitations. He shared that on the days they welcome new people, there are
typically ten individuals who show up, but only two or three can be seen per day. Councilor
Fillmore asked if vouchers are being wasted and the reasons for it. Mr. Memmott explained
that not all landlords are willing to take the risk of renting to someone who qualified for a

12.       A presentation regarding the FY2024 Budget- Mayor's Office (23-008) (04:41:50)

Mayor Kaufusi reminded the council that Media Services falls under the Mayor’s Office. She
turned the time over to Dixon Holmes, Assistant CAO, to present the budget. He highlighted the
accomplishments of FY 2023 which included the grand opening of City Hall and the new Airport

said he hopes the collaborative effort between the council and the mayor’s office will continue.
He continued by going through the components of the mayor’s office and explained how the
budget is divided between them. Presentation only.
