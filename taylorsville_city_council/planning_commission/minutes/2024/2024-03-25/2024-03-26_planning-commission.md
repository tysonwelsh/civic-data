3. Public Hearing and Recommendation to the City Council for a Zoning Text
Amendment to Taylorsville Municipal Code Section 13.20.020. Amending
Minimum Front Setbacks for Corner Lots and Lots in the Single-Family R-
1-7 Zone (1224 / Terryne Bergeson / Planner)
hel Planner Terryne Bergeson explained that the application was initiated by Daniel
Balsam of Alpine Homes because current setbacks for corner lots restricted the building
envelope and compromised their ability to build the home they wished to. The amendment
included changes to R-1-7 front setbacks as well.
Side A front setbacks for corner lots in R-1-5 and R-1-6 zones were 5-10 feet greater than
those for interior lots, reducing the buildable area. Front facades might differ from corner
lots to interior lots of’a neighborhood. Clear view section prohibited structures which
exceeded 3'.in height within visibility triangle. The front setback increased in 2012. The
scale of development on R-1-7 lots was more like R-1-5 and R-1-6 than 8,000 square foot
lots. Homes built to current standards were legal non-conforming. The proposed changes
would not permit encroachment or exception to the sight visibility triangle. They would
increase the buildable area and allow uniform build-to lines. The changes would also
grant legal status to corner lots and R-1-7 lots built to pre-2012 standards.
Ms. Bergeson explained that the proposed text amendment would (a) amend the front
side A setbacks for corner lots in single-family R-1 zones from 30 feet to the same
minimum front setback as interior lots with the same zoning designation; and (b) reduce
the minimum front setback for lots in the single-family R-1-7 zone. Adoption of the

4. Planning Commission Review of the Taylorsville Land Development Code
Update Project, Chapter 13.04: Development Standards. (Jim Spung,
Senior Planner)

Senior Planner Jim Spung . explained that he wished to preview the proposed

development standards for the commissioners that evening. He hoped to have the entire

updated Land Development Code (LDC) put together by the end of May for the Planning

Commission to review. Following that, it was his intent to get it to the City Council in July

or August. He explained there would be approximately 4-6 months of lag time between

the date of adoption and the effective date.

Mr. Spung indicated that there hadn’t previously been a “purpose” statement in the

development standards but that was about to change. In short, the purpose was to ensure

that the quality and form of development within Taylorsville was what the City sought and
what was most helpful to residents.

The Applicability section became complicated at times, depending on the type of

application submitted. The section was intended to clarify when the LDC applied to new

and redevelopment projects.

A tiered approach was being introduced, with Tier 1 being for minor redevelopment
projects. The LDC included specific language to show what types of projects would fall
under this category.
Tier 2 was for “major” redevelopment and again, there was language to specify what
constituted a major development project.
In response to a question from Chair Wilkey, Mr. Spung explained this would:apply to
multi-family residential, mixed-use, and commercial development, but not single-family
residential.
The Commissioners and Mr. Spung discussed the important balance between ensuring
that redevelopment projects were thorough and trying not to overwhelm applicants with a
multitude of requirements. . 7 :
Commissioner Wright liked the idea of encouraging potential property developers to be
extra creative. , APO
Commissioner Wendel was intrigued by having Taylorsville be able to choose from
among a menu of ideas designed to encourage moderate-income housing.
Commissioner Young asked that Mr. Spung add the current code to presentations like

Mr. Spung continued by addressing grading and drainage, explaining that there were not
