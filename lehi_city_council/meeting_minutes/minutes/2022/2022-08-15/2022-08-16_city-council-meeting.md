Minutes of the Truth in Taxation Hearing of the Lehi City Council held Tuesday, August 16,

                        Katie Koivisto, Council Member

Others Present: Cameron Boyle, Assistant City Administrator; Beau Thomas, Deputy City
Administrator; Ryan Wood, City Attorney; Lorin Powell, City Engineer; Kim Struthers, Planning

Regular Session, 7:00 p.m.
1. Welcome, Roll Call, Pledge of Allegiance
Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present. Councilors
Condie and Koivisto joined virtually. Councilor Albrecht led the Pledge of Allegiance.

2. Truth in Taxation Hearing

    a) Public Hearing

    Dean Lundell, Finance Director, presented on the proposed tax rate. He explained how
    property tax rates are calculated and collected by each entity.

    Mayor Johnson opened the public hearing.

    Nick Franklin asked the Council not to raise taxes. He said that utility rates have already been
    increased and this would only add to the burden, especially for senior citizens on a fixed
    income. He suggested offering a rebate to seniors.

    Gavin Howard agreed with the previous comments. He was also concerned with the utility
    increases. He said that the new population should pay for additional police officers.

    Michelle Stallings did not agree with the tax increase. She said that sales tax revenue has
    increased and that should pay for additional costs. She said that the City already collects more
    revenue then other cities and suggested that the city exercise more fiscal restraint.

    Sandra Vanbloom disagrees with raising property taxes. She suggested that the city increase
    fees with an end date after projects are completed.

    Keith Campbell stated that he feels like he is getting priced out of living in the City. He also
    expressed concerns with those on fixed incomes. He said that employee raises are not
    necessary.

    Beverly Johnson thanked the city for their service. She asked that the city look at other
    alternatives besides raising the property tax and asked them to be creative.

    Councilor Koivisto stated that she is concerned with those on a fixed income, but the City has
    needed this for a while. They have also tried to be creative. She said that this hasn’t been an
    easy decision and she encouraged residents to watch the meetings where this has been
    discussed.

    Councilor Hancock stated that it’s difficult to raise taxes, but believes it’s fiscally responsible
    otherwise the city will get behind because of inflation.

    Councilor Southwick stated that they do have a budget that they have to work within every
    year and they have to turn down many requests, and wait for projects. He said that this is only
    a small increase that will help fund Police Officers which are needed.

    Councilor Albrecht stated that there is a balance between providing a level of service to the
    community and tightening belts. The decision wasn’t taken lightly and is concerned with those
    on a fixed income. This tax would fund essential services for residents.

    Councilor Condie stated that he would like to look at ways to minimize the impact to seniors.
    He said that if they don’t increase this tax then they are creating a bigger problem 5-10 years
    from now.

    Mayor Johnson explained that this tax would help maintain the current level of services. He
    said that they are dealing with a lot of difficult issues with cost increases and inflation.

    b) Consideration of Resolution #2022-72, adopting a final tax rate for the City of Lehi.


    Motion:     Councilor Hancock moved to approve Resolution #2022-72, adopting a final tax
                rate for the City of Lehi. Councilor Southwick seconded the motion.

    Roll Call Vote: Councilor Albrecht, Yes; Councilor Condie, Yes; Councilor Hancock, Yes;
    Councilor Koivisto, Yes; and Councilor Southwick, Yes. The motion passed unanimously.


3.. Adjournment
    With no further business to come before the City Council at this time, Councilor Koivisto
    moved to adjourn the meeting. Councilor Southwick seconded the motion. The motion
    passed unanimously. The meeting adjourned at approximately 8:05 p.m.
