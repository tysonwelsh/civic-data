         2. 1     Presentation —Scott Harrington


Chair Cochran turned the time over to Assistant City Administrator and Chief Financial Officer
Scott Harrington. Mr. Harrington began his presentation by displaying a copy of the notice that
was published by the City in anticipation ofthe hearing. He also displayed the notice that Salt Lake
County had published on behalf of a plethora of public entities that were considering similar
increases in property tax.

Mr. Harrington then explained that if the City Council were to approve the proposed tax increase,
a Taylorsville home valued at $ 681, 000 would see an annual increase of$ 63. 70. He pointed out
that the value of the average home in the City was lower than that— about$ 472, 000.



A historical depiction of Taylorsville' s property tax rate was then displayed. The table showed that
since 2015, inflation had increased 31%, although the amount of revenue to city coffers had not

changed. The reason for this was that as house values increased, the tax rate was reduced.


A sample Notice ofProperty Valuation showed that the owner of a home valued at$ 434,000 would
pay an additional $40.64 per year. He pointed out that although the entire bill was increasing by
 2, 500/ year, only about 6% of the bill was for Taylorsville.


The proposed tax increase would generate an additional $ 928, 881. Mr. Harrington explained that
Taylorsville continued to be a" pay as we go" City and therefore had very low debt for a city of its
size. This meant, however, that it was important to have periodic incremental increases to fund
capital projects and other needs.



As for business owners, one valued at $ 472, 000 would see an annual increase of$ 80. 24 ( or$ 6. 69
per month).     He   explained that   businesses did not receive the 45% residential exemption that
homeowners saw. Both business and homeowners would see a$ 25. 05 increase in taxes over what
they had paid in 2023.

Mr. Harrington then explained what the proposed tax increase would fund.
         Park projects ( Phase 2 of Taylorsville Park and Summit Park improvements): $ 6, 000,000

         Ongoing street light replacement program: $ 500,000
         Canal Safety& Trails: $ 1, 000,000
         2200 West Bridge Replacement: $ 200,000
         Increased Road Maintenance: $ 500, 000


He pointed out that these figures were based on " today' s dollars."   Delaying such projects meant
spending    even more for the same     things in the future.

          2. 2    Public Hearing to Receive Public Comment Regarding Resolution No. 24- 20,
                  a Resolution of the City of Taylorsville Approving an Increased Property Tax
                  Rate for Real Property—Scott Harrington


Chair Cochran opened the public hearing by asking those who wished to address the City Council
do so.


City resident Cheneil Hill expressed her deep concern about the proposed property tax increase
and the impact it would have on low-impact families. She explained that her mortgage bill
increased every year due to increased taxes. And despite paying 50%- 75% of her taxes to Granite
School District, she still had to pay for a variety of educational expenses for her children. As a
widow with five children, she said that she could not keep her head above water financially. She
mentioned rising costs of food, housing, and basic utilities. She asked why more taxes were needed
and wondered if there was another way to increase revenues.

Nicole McDermott, city resident, wished to thank the City Council. She said she and her family
made a conscious decision to move to Taylorsville because it was very well managed. She was
aware that citizens wanted plowed roads, repaired pot holes, nice parks, pickleball courts, street
lights, sidewalks, and bridges. But no one ever wanted taxes to be raised to pay for any of it. She
said she had attended many meetings where the Council and Administration had thoughtful
conversations to ensure they made fiscally responsible decisions. She wished to recognize how
challenging this decision was.

City resident Danielle Houston expressed appreciation for the work the City had put in to reducing
costs but indicated that she had questions/ concerns.
     1)    If" we can wait at least one year" as stated in a recent news article, why don' t we? Let' s
           allow inflation to adjust.
    2)     Employee salaries and benefits had increased each year for two years, including for the
           Mayor and City Council.
    3)     Has the City tried all other options before raising taxes?
    4)     She suggested that tax revenue had increased as property values had gone up.
     5)    There had been an insufficient amount of transparency.
     6)    Delay a decision so that an open house could be held which would allow the public a
           thorough review of the budget

Marc McElreath, city resident, appreciated that Taylorsville was a " pay as you go city," and he
agreed that all the previously mentioned city services were necessary. He spoke in support of the
proposed increase. However, he called for increased transparency in the overall budget process.
He appreciated that more detail was provided earlier in the meeting regarding what specific
services residents would receive for their additional tax dollars. But Mr. McElreath felt that
information should have been provided much earlier, and he asked for that to happen with future
budgets. He also mentioned that he felt Phase 2 of the parks improvements could wait— that it was
less important than public works services, employee wages, etc.


Chair Cochran closed the public hearing.

                   2. 21   Resolution No. 24-20 A Resolution of the City of Taylorsville Approving
                   an Increased Property Tax Rate for Real Property

Council Member Harker mentioned how much she loved it when citizens came to meetings to
learn and be informed. She said it was a big misconception that the City got more money when
property values increased. As Mr. Harrington explained earlier, the City would receive the same
amount of tax revenue on those homes unless this Truth in Taxation process took place. If the
Council did not vote to increase taxes, the City would no longer be able to provide the services
residents were accustomed to. She did not want to see that happen. No one wanted to pay taxes,
but it was important for everyone to contribute for the greater good so that Taylorsville remained
a nice place for all of us, as well as for our children and grandchildren in the future. Therefore, she
appreciated the education that had been provided to the City Council over the past several months.
Together they were taking into account all the things that residents had reported they wished to
see in the City.

Council Member Barbieri appreciated those members of the public that were in attendance for the
hearing, and especially those who shared their comments. She pointed out that the City Council
and Administration had been discussing the budget for 5- 6 months, and the public had been
welcome to attend these meetings, either in person or online. She was grateful to live in a" pay as
you go"   city that had a committee of citizen volunteers who thoroughly reviewed the budget.
Neither of those aspects of Taylorsville were typical of other cities in the area. One of her priorities
was to ensure that Taylorsville was able to attract commercial businesses that would continue to
add   tax dollars to       our " bottom      line."   But if we fail to keep the City looking nice with well-
maintained roads,          parks,   safe neighborhoods,       etc.,   those businesses would go elsewhere, and
Taylorsville would lose that revenue.


Chair Cochran said that over the years since he had been elected, he had asked his constituents if
they would prefer smaller increases each year or less frequent, larger increases. He said the
resounding    answer        was     always   the   smaller   increases. One resident had   suggested   waiting to
