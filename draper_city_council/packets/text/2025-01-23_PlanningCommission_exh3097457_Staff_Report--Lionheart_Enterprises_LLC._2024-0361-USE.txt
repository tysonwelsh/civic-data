BACKGROUND AND SUMMARY
This application is a request for approval of a Conditional Use Permit for approximately
6.80 acres located on the east side of 265 W. at approximately 12411 S. 265 W. Suite C
(Exhibits B & C). The property is currently zoned CBP. The applicant is requesting that a
Conditional Use Permit be approved to allow for a limited manufacturing use producing
oral dissolving strips.

The property was developed with two primary buildings in 1997 and is part of a platted
development known as the Draper Industrial Park. The buildings are divided into multiple
business suites and Lionheart Enterprises, LLC. is located in the northern building in suite
C. Lionheart Enterprises, LLC does business as NutraStrips.

Per DCMC 9-3-040, Limited Manufacturing is defined as:

         An establishment engaged in the limited processing, fabrication, assembly and/or
         packaging of products utilizing processes that:
                 A. Have no noise, odor, vibration, or other impacts discernible outside a
                building; and
                B. Do not violate any applicable noise ordinance.

The applicant’s submitted description of business operations can be found in Exhibit H
attached to this report. The business manufactures, packages, and ships dietary
supplements in the form of oral dissolving strips sold under the NutraStrips brand name.
There are approximately thirty (30) employees who work various shifts during the hours of
operation which are Monday through Friday from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The applicant has made
the following assertions regarding compliance with the standards of Section 9-5-080(E) of
the DCMC (Exhibit H):

    •    That the CBP zone allows for light manufacturing, office, and warehouse uses.

    •    That the facility was specifically built for light manufacturing, office and warehouse
         uses.

    •    That the proposed use is not injurious to existing or potential development as the
         existing surrounding businesses are all light industrial, office, or warehousing.

    •    That the proposed use would have a positive impact on the surrounding area.

Staff has reviewed the applicant’s initially submitted statements and documentation and
concurs generally with most of their assertions regarding the proposed operations
compliance with the criteria found in Section 9-5-080(E) of the DCMC, with the exception of
criterion (g) regarding safeguards regarding odor control.

During the preparation of this report a complaint was received by the City that the
NutraStrips business was in operation and was creating odors which were impacting a
neighboring unit in the building. Staff addressed this issue with the applicant, reminding
them that if noise, odor, vibration, or other impacts are discernible outside of the building
that the proposed operation would not meet the definition of a limited manufacturing use
and could not be approved under the current zoning classification. The applicant
submitted additional statements and documentation in response indicating that odors
from peppermint and other flavorings which are used in the manufacture of their product,
however, their operational protocols coupled with the filters in the HVAC system mitigate
the odor so that it is not discernible outside of the building (Exhibits G & H). The applicant’s
additional documentation also detailed additional work done inside the building to seal off
gaps in the common wall shared with the neighboring suite to eliminate odors from leaving
the NutraStrips suite and entering others nearby. Staff is satisfied that with the applicant’s
additional responses that the proposed use does indeed fall within the definition of Limited
Manufacturing as contained in DCMC Section 9-3-040.

Staff believes that the potential negative impacts of a limited manufacturing use at the
subject property would be the potential for noise, odor and vibration being discernable
outside of the building. There are no known noise or vibration impacts from the proposed
use that are discernable outside of the building, and the machinery and process used
create minimum noise and vibration inside the building.

The potential for negative impacts caused by odor created during the manufacturing
process and its potential of being discernable outside of the building is a chief concern to

                    Description of Proposed Use for Conditional Use Permit Application

NutraStrips is seeking a conditional use permit for its limited manufacturing operations within 12411 South
265 West, Unit C, Draper, Utah 84020, which is situated in a CBP zone of Draper City, Utah. The applicant’s
primary business focus is the manufacturing of oral dissolving strips containing dietary supplements. The
applicant additionally operates an administrative office and warehouse facility (one dock door and a
surface-level dock at the rear of the premises), which are both permitted uses in a CBP zone under Draper
City Municipal Code § 9-11-110. The specific proposed uses for this facility are the following:

    1. Limited Manufacturing: The limited manufacturing function of the facility is the production of
       oral dissolving strips. This includes the formulation, assembly, and packaging of dietary
       supplements into strip form. The applicant’s manufacturing processes will adhere to all relevant
       health, safety, and quality standards, ensuring that operations contribute positively to the local
       industrial landscape. The applicant has a current “good manufacturing practices” (“cGMP”)
       certificate and adheres to those practices. Because the applicant adheres to cGMP standards within
       a clean room environment (high-caliber filtration and ventilation systems and controls, lab coats,
       hair nets, and beard nets, etc.), it is not anticipated that there will be any detrimental effects to the
       applicant’s neighbors at the facility.

    2. Warehouse: The facility will include warehousing space for the storage of raw materials and
       finished products. This area will be used for inventory management and the efficient handling of
       incoming and outgoing shipments, which aligns with the logistical framework typical of industrial
       zones. The loading dock at the back of the facility is used on a nearly daily basis for shipping and
       receiving.

    3. Office Space: The proposed use also includes designated office space to accommodate
       administrative, managerial, and operational staff. These offices will support the business’s strategic
       and day-to-day operations, facilitating coordination among departments and ensuring streamlined
       production and supply chain processes.

The integration of these uses within a single facility is designed to promote operational efficiency while
maintaining compatibility with the existing character and intended use of the surrounding industrial area.
As discussed in more detail above, the proposed activities are expected to have no detrimental effects on
nearby businesses or traffic inasmuch as the facility was constructed in an area specifically designed to
accommodate the applicant’s type of business. Moreover, the applicant’s use of the facility is otherwise
permitted inasmuch as the applicant has certificates of registration from the federal FDA and the Utah
Department of Agriculture and Food.

The applicant’s administrative office hours are generally 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday,
while the limited manufacturing and warehouse portion of the facility runs three shifts per day, five days
per week. The applicant is closed for business on Saturdays and Sundays. The applicant has approximately
30 employees. Parking at the facility is ample; the applicant requires approximately 20 parking spots at any
given time. The facility, between its 12411 and 12453 buildings, has over 130 parking spots, with substantial
overflow parking at the rear of the 12453 building. The applicant has never known and has never been
advised of a parking shortage of any kind at the facility.
