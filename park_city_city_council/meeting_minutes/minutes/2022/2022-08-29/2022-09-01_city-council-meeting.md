Discuss Winter Transportation Operations - Parking Plan:
Matt Neeley, Transportation Director, and Johnny Wasden, Parking Manager, presented
this item. Wasden stated the Parking Department had been working to expand tools for
enforcement for the past four years. He noted his team was involved with peak transit
days and helping with traffic flow. One initiative was offering permits for residents to
park in their neighborhoods since they didn't have on-site parking. He discussed virtual
permits, "No Street Parking" signage and additional signage for peak traffic days.

Council Member Toly expressed concern that A-frame signage would fall over and
wouldn't be seen. Wasden explained signage would be put on both sides of the street
and notices would be on the Transit apps. Council Member Doilney asked what
solutions were in place to deter parking in Prospector, since there were already parking
issues there. Wasden stated the goal was to anticipate problem areas and then have a
clear communication channel to let visitors know where to park. The auxiliary plan for
parking in Prospector or other areas was timed parking signage that could be set out in
the hot spots.

about fines. Wasden stated the initial fee was $50 and that could escalate to $200.
Council Member Rubell noted this was an expansion of the parking program. The
Streets Department had been adding signage so people should know and make parking
adjustments.

1. Consideration to Adopt the Rail Trail Master Plan:
Heinrich Deters, Trails and Open Space Manager, Maria Vyas, Fehrs & Peers Active
Transportation Consultant, and Charlie Sturgis presented this item. Deters stated the
master plan was a community document. The Rail Trail was a great community asset.
He reviewed the history of the Rail Trail. Over the years, there were many comments on
the need for maintenance for the trail. This plan was conceptual and was phased. Each
step would come back to Council for approval, and he noted this was a multi-year
project.

Vyas reviewed the public outreach efforts for the master plan and stated some of the
feedback included the need for improved wayfinding, separating the modes, having
more dog waste stations and trash cans, the need to widen the trail, and adding
restrooms. The stakeholder group reduced the proposed width of the trail, minimized
lighting, and limited improvements to specific locations. Some concerns expressed
regarding dog safety, the potential gondola or tram, and lighting.

Deters proposed to widen the trail by adding eight feet of road base. Council Member
Rubell asked if the road base was eight feet throughout the entire trail. Deters stated the
plan still needed to be engineered and would come back to Council for approval. If the
plans interfered with other priorities, it could be reconsidered. Council Member Toly
asked if it could be four feet on each side of the pavement.

Deters discussed the Wag'On Trail, which was an area that could be an off leash
alternative to the trail. Council Member Dickey asked what the cost of a new trail would
be. Vyas explained where different amenities would be located along the trail.

to donate a bridge and so it would only be the cost of labor to install it. He stated there

                                                    PETITION

To: Park City Council

From: Homeowners living in proximity to the Rail Trail in Prospector

Re: Proposed Rail Trail improvements

Date: August 25, 2022

        On September 1, 2022 the Park City Council will vote on a proposal to “enhance” the Union
Pacific Rail Trail between Bonanza Drive, Wyatt Earp Way, and State route 248 east of US-40. The
“enhancements” include widening the trail by 8 feet to a total of 18.5 feet. The current trail averages a
width of 9.5 feet of pavement. According to Summit County regulations “Access roads shall be limited
to ten feet (10’) in width” (11-6-8: INFRASTRUCTURE STANDARDS;
https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/summitcountyut/latest/summitcounty_ut/0-0-0-18970).
Widening the trail will therefore turn it into a road.

        We the undersigned appreciate the efforts to improve the trail, however, there are significant
reasons against widening the trail.

        1. The major rationale for widening the trail is to slow down bikers who bike at high speeds and
prevent them from entering areas of the trail where people are walking. Instead of slowing down
bikers, widening the trail will allow bikers to go faster. Established traffic calming guidelines, which have
been implemented on some streets in Park City to slow down traffic, involve narrowing, not widening
roads. A widened trail will encourage bikers to ride abreast of each other and therefore larger groups of
bikers will be moving significantly faster.

        The proposed widening was initiated during the height of the COVID-19 pandemic. Bike and
foot traffic along the trail has decreased significantly with the waning of the pandemic. Recent addition
of a center yellow line on the trail has facilitated the flow of traffic since bikers are now much more
atune to staying on their side of the trail when passing walkers. So the initial rationale for widening the
trail was faulty and current conditions no longer warrant this change. Furthermore, as shown in the
survey only approximately 29.5% of respondents are interested in a wider rail trail, while 71.5% of
respondents did not express interest.
       2. There is a vibrant eco system of ducks, geese, herons, resident and migrating birds, beavers,
muskrats, ground squirrels, and flora in the wetlands on the north side of the trail between Wyatt Earp
Way and State route 248. Moose and deer come down from the south side hills to drink at the wetlands
and sandhill cranes travel along the rail trail in spring with their young. There has been no consideration
of how widening the trail (including widening the existing bridges over areas of the wetlands) will impact
the flora and fauna. An environmental impact study of the wetlands is essential before any changes are
made to the trail.

        3. There are many mature trees within 10-12 feet of the trail on the south side of the rail trail
east of Wyatt Earp Way. These trees sit on a berm. Widening the trail by any amount (proposal is for 8
feet) on the south side of the existing trail will require cutting down part of the berm and is likely to
impact (kill?) these mature trees by eliminating and/or cutting their roots. These trees provide an
important barrier between the trail and houses along the south side of the trail.

        4. Off-leash dogs climb the berm and defecate in the backyards of the houses on the south side
of the trail. Dog owners are usually unaware of this activity so the clean-up is left to the homeowners.
Widening the trail on the south side will make it easier for off-leash dogs to find a location to defecate.

        Given these concerns and the changes that have occurred since the waning of the pandemic, we
respectfully request that the Council does not approve the current proposal and suggest that potential
changes to the Rail Trail be re-evaluated.
