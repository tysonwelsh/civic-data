2. PUBLIC COMMENT
Mr. Steve Winters  read the following statement regarding studies of the harmful effects 
on developing fetuses from pregnant women ingesting fluoride:
Recently the world ’s most respected children ’s medical journal “The Journal of the 
American Medical Association - Pediatrics” (or JAMA Pediatrics for short) reported on 
a study which ran from 2008 to 2019.  In this study the fluoride levels in 601 pregnant 
Canadian mothers were found to correspond to measureable significant IQ drops in 
their children when they were tested later in their childhood.  When asked what 
pregnant mothers should be told by their pediatricians if they ask about drinking 
fluoridated water during their pregnancies the Jama Pediatrics chief editor Dr. Dimitri 
Christakis said, “I would advise them to drink bottled water or filtered water.
Fluoride in very small doses aggressively hardens even the already hardest surface in 
the body - tooth enamel.  So why would anyone not also realize that even in small doses 
fluoride must be indiscriminately hardening many of the softest tissues in the body - 
most significantly, developing brain cells in unborn and young children?
Fortunately, informed mothers in Salt Lake County are now avoiding fluoridated water 
during pregnancy.  Many are also providing filtered or fluoride free bottle water to 
their young children even though this is an unwanted cost for many financially 
struggling families.  Worst than this added expense, is all the uninformed mothers who

do not understand the permanent injuries caused to their unborn children by their

3. REPORT OF ELECTED OFFICIALS:
3.1. County Council Members
Council Member Winder Newton  stated she attended the National Association 
of Counties (NACo), annual conference, along with Council Members Bradshaw, 
Harrison, Stringham, and Mayor Jennifer Wilson and Katherine Fife, Associate 
Deputy Mayor of County Services, where they toured the Esperanza Community 
shelter and the Community First shelter.  The Esperanza Community is made up of 
tiny homes, with air conditioning, heating, and electricity, but no plumbing, and with 
community bathrooms.  The organizer said sanctioned camping had been tried first, 
but that it had been a disaster.  The Community First shelter is a supportive housing 
shelter, housing people who had been chronically homeless, most for about nine 
years, with an average age of 57.  The rent costs for that shelter ranged from $ 350 
to $550.
Council Member Harrison  thanked Wayne Niederhauser, Utah State 
Homelessness Coordinator, for setting up the connections for providing these 
tours.

Discussion/Direction
Judge Shawna Graves Robertson  asked the Council to reconsider two of 
the five positions that were cut during the mid -year budget.  The two 
positions are entry level office specialist positions and are desperately 
needed.  Several employees of the Justice Court have left, but the work still 
needs to be done as the Justice Court winds down.  A legal assistant position 
will be vacant soon too, but an employee will be promoted to fill that 
position.
Council Member Winder Newton  asked how Millcreek leaving the 
County’s Justice Court services, which was 40 percent of the caseload, 
would affect the positions in the Justice Court’s office.
Judge Graves -Robertson stated Millcreek is about 30-33 percent of the 
Justice Court ’s caseload, but its cases are increasing in the jurisdictions that 
are left and there is a backlog of cases.  The Justice Court also needs to get

about six months.

compared to other justice courts, with regard to staffing and caseloads.

4.3 Informational Presentation on Homelessness in Salt Lake 
County
23-0556
Attachments: Staff Report

Presenter: Wayne Niederhauser, Utah Homeless Coordinator  (Approx. 3:15 
PM - 1 hour)
Informational
Mr. Wayne Niederhauser , Utah State Homelessness Coordinator, 
delivered a PowerPoint presentation entitled “Office of Homeless Services, ” 
reviewing Local Homeless Councils in Utah and the continuum of care; 
homelessness being a community condition; the two distinct homeless 
populations - acute and persistent, and the data points; Utah ’s plan to 
address homelessness; the 2023 Annual Data Report on Homelessness; 
making homelessness rare; making homelessness brief; making homelessness 
non-recurring; an annual point -in-time count; the 2023 point-in-time count of 
subpopulations; chronic homelessness and permanent supportive housing; 
Utah’s Office of Homeless Services (OHS) for services and housing; Fiscal 
Year (FY) 2024 homeless shelter cities mitigation funding; rates of 
homelessness; deeply affordable housing need; supportive housing and how 
to fund it; FY 2023 deeply affordable housing funding; OHS Deeply 
Affordable Housing Fund; recommended funding for counties, separated by 
counties of the first class, counties of the second and third class, and 
counties of the fourth, fifth, and sixth class; FY deeply affordable housing 
funding; FY 24 attainable housing grants; gaps and needs; dedicated funding 
stream to address goals and strategies in the Strategic Plan; Utah Impact 
Partnership and Fred Story websites; and Hope Springs, including a 
rendition.
Ms. Erin Litvack , Deputy Mayor of County Services, stated the Mayor ’s 
Office has been working with Mr. Niederhauser toward the same goal, while 
learning at the same time.  Collaboration is critically important to the success 
of these efforts.  She noted the County is already engaged in these efforts, as 
it is the continuum of care.  She reviewed some of its accomplishments and 
efforts, including funding for opportunities for individuals experiencing 
homelessness, and it is contemplating some non -congregant models, 
including a shelter for families.
