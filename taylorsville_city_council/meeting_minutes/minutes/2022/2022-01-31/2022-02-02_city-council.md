3.      REPORTS

        3.1      Legislative Updates – John Hiskey

      John Hiskey explained for the benefit of the residents why it was important for the city to be
involved in what was happening during the 45-day state legislative session. Everything that took
place at the local level was directly affected by laws that were set by the legislature. He explained
where the council members and any interested citizen could go online to visit Utah’s nationally
acclaimed website. He provided a brief tutorial on navigating the website and how one could track
the various pending bills. He also described which legislators represented different parts of
Taylorsville and pointed out that redistricting would make some changes.

One bill Mr. Hiskey was watching closely was HB12--the Public Safety Retirement bill designed
to correct a change that was made during the 2008 recession. Rep. Gwynn happened to be a police
chief in Roy and he had discovered that the financial cost to implement this particular bill was
simply too high. He then directed that a substitute bill be drafted which if passed, would reduce
the current one-year requirement for a retired public safety employee to spend not engaged in
public employment before returning to it to only sixty days. For recruiting purposes, this could
make a significant difference.

Mr. Hiskey indicated that a poll showed that better wages appeared to be a higher priority than
were retirement benefits to first responders. Only 26% said retirement benefits were more
important than pay, 10% reported that health benefits were, and another 10% said their top priority
was increased training.

The mental health needs of first responders were being addressed in HB23 which was scheduled
to be heard on the floor the following afternoon. The bill had tremendous support and as currently
written, would assist not just current first responders but also their family members as well as
retired public safety personnel.

Mr. Hiskey explained to the city council some issues raised in other jurisdictions where statements
given by law enforcement officers following critical incidents were no longer being considered
“protected” in nature. The result was that attorneys were recommending their law enforcement

clients not provide the statements which made it more challenging to ascertain the facts
surrounding an incident. City attorney Tracy Cowdell weighed in on the issue, explaining the
challenge of ascertaining facts when officers could be criminally charged based on statements
made in a previously protected “Garrity” interview. There was a conflict with the fifth amendment
right against self-incrimination and Mr. Hiskey pointed out that this type of interview was separate
from one involving disciplinary action. It was an interview conducted in an information-gathering
capacity.

It was well-known that catalytic converter thefts had become a huge problem— in fact there had
been an increase of 600% according to the Utah Attorney General’s office. HB38 was drafted to
address the issue by requiring some purchasers to meet certain requirements, and also created a
presumption that a catalytic converter was stolen property in certain situations. Chief Cottam
stated that law enforcement was in support of the bill.

     Senator Thatcher was sponsoring SB46 which would prohibit public employers from taking
adverse action against an employee simply for having a medical cannabis card. The bill would
require such cards to be treated as any other prescription. Mr. Hiskey indicated that thus far there
appeared to be very little objection to the bill which did not involve the issue of potential
impairment—only action taken for simply having a card.

     Citation fine amendments were being discussed which would eliminate local revenue from
certain traffic citations issued by Utah Highway Patrol within Salt Lake County. Instead, the funds
would go to the state’s general fund. There was also discussion about encouraging cities to provide
affordable housing opportunities for law enforcement officers in the community.

        Councilmember Harker inquired about HB96 which addressed “vexatious” GRAMA
requests. Mr. Hiskey replied that if under this bill, if someone made more than one request within
a ten-day period, he/she would have to pay for staff time to complete the request, including the
first fifteen minutes of work which currently was statutorily required to be done free of charge.

Mr. Hiskey wrapped up his comments by mentioning that there were conversations taking place
regarding what local governments had done to promote affordable housing in their communities.

       3.2       Unified Fire Authority Report – Captain Richard Rich

      Unified Fire Authority Captain Richard Rich explained that there had been a significant
residential fire the previous day which had resulted in serious burns to a victim. The cause of the
fire was still under investigation.

There had been 1300 calls for service between October 1, 2021 and December 31, 2021. There
were some problems with the CAD system regarding the actual percentage of emergent calls. The
13% listed in his PowerPoint was not accurate. He indicated that the correct number should be
much higher, and they were working on the system to get a more accurate figure. He presented a
four-year monthly comparison which appeared to be trending upwards across all UFA
jurisdictions.

Captain Rich reviewed fire call types as well as EMS responses. He also presented data for each
individual fire station that typically responded to calls in Taylorsville.

The Captain then presented information regarding UFA’s strategic plan which would help direct
their budget for the coming year. Chief Petersen would be retiring at the end of March. Three
candidates had applied to fill the position. A “meet and greet” would take place on March 9th,
after which an ad hoc committee (including Mayor Overson) would choose the new chief. There
would be a change of command ceremony on April 2nd.

Next was a construction update for UFA stations located throughout the Salt Lake Valley. These
updates included seismic work on stations #110 and #116.

Unified Fire was experiencing recruitment problems as were most industries throughout the nation.
27 new recruits started recruitment school the previous day and would be there for the next 16
weeks. Their progress could be followed on YouTube. Additionally, there was a lateral hire of
four existing paramedics that came from other agencies. Those paramedics went through a four-
week orientation which included spot-checks to confirm that they met standards in various
paramedic skills. The UFA would use the same hiring process the following spring when they
hoped to get 15-20 quality candidates.

Several retirements had taken place at the end of 2021.
    Captain James Degering had retired after 36 years of service
    Captain Doug Morgan had retired after 23 years of service
    Paramedic Cathy Allen had retired after 26 years of service
    Controlled Substance Program Manager Karla Rickards retired after 28 years of service
    Ginger Watts, UFA’s Technology and Intelligence Business Manager was retiring after 21
       years of government service
