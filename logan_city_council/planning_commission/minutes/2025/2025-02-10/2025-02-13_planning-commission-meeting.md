PUBLIC HEARING:

PC 25-007 Logan City 10MG First Dam Tank & Booster Pump Station [Conditional Use Permit]
Darren Farar/Logan City is requesting a Conditional Use Permit for a new 10 million galion, drinking
water storage tank and booster pump station that will be located at 1580 Canyon Road in the
Recreation (REC) zone; TIN 06-093-0034 (Wilson Neighborhood).

Staff: Russ Holley reviewed the request for a Conditional Use Permit for a new 10 million gallon
drinking water tank that is approximately 30 feet tall and will be located on a 2.11-acre property at
approximately 1580 Canyon Road, near the Utah State University ropes course. The proposal
includes a separate booster pump building to the southwest of the tank and a new 25 foot wide
paved access road connecting to Canyon Road. The road is being proposed through a steep area
of land posing potential land disturbance and re-vegetation impacts.

R. Holley explained the proposed layout of the tank being positioned approximately 600 feet north
of Canyon Road and at an elevation 50 feet higher than Canyon Road on a shelf-like land feature.
The plans show half of the tank exposed, with the other half buried into the slope. Because of this,
the tank will not be visible from Canyon Road. There are a few areas where the tank will be visible
including sections of 400 North and the Cliffside Neighborhood.

The Commission will need to consider the color of the tank to best blend in with the area and also
ensure the area is well revegetated to lessen the visual impacts.

R. Holley reviewed the comments received on this project mentioning the trees along Canyon Road,
sidewalks, parking, pipeline location, and engineering. R. Holley also stated that this pending
decision is only regarding the proposed water tank and does not include any pipe infrastructure in
Canyon Road.


Commissioner Lewis asked if the top of the tank would be an earth top. R Holley said it will be a
partial earth top where it is buried into the slope. The other portion of the top could become a future
USU solar farm with solar panels on the top.

Commissioner Peterson asked about the Canyon Road trail that the access road crosses and if the
trail will continue onto this property. R. Holley said there is a condition requiring a safe crossing for
the trail and that USU owns the property surrounding the tank and there have not been any
recreation or trail easements established.

Proponent: Darren Farar, Logan City Engineer and project manager, spoke about the proposed
location being ideal for the water tank because it is at a higher elevation in the City which allows the
water to flow downhill naturally using gravity. D. Farar said that the portion of the water tank roof
that is not covered in the slope will be covered with gravel and that USU will have the option to add
solar panels to the roof as that was their original plan for this area. D. Farar also spoke about the
State of Utah’s water requirement and how half of the tank, 5 million gallons, will meet minimum
capacity requirements, and the other half will be for future capacity or future demand.

Commissioner Peterson asked if any of the water would be sold to neighboring cities. D. Farar said
the water is for Logan City use. There are agreements with neighboring cities to distribute water in
emergency situations only.

Public: Kristi Fatuesi asked about the notices being mailed to only property owners within 300 feet.
Asked to see the written reports from Engineering, Light & Power, and the Geotechnical report
before a building permit is issued. Asked what area of the City will be using this water and where
the water will be treated. Asked if the soil being dug out for the tank will be used to build up the
access road. Mike DeSimone, Community Development Director, said that she can contact Darren
Farrar, Logan City Engineer for access to the reports when they are available.

Wes Mangum also asked about residents being able to see all of the reports before a building permit
is issued.

Mike DeSimone clarified that building permits are issued by the Community Development
Department if the conditions of approval are met, and the proposed plans meet all building codes.
M. DeSimone also stated that public review is not a part of the building permit approval process.

Commission: Commissioner Peterson asked Darren Farar, Logan City Engineer to address the
questions brought up during public comment. D. Farar said that a portion of the water will be pumped
to the Cliffside area. The main water line will be piped to the west side of 200 East. The drinking
water will be treated at Dewit Springs where the water comes from. The soil taken from the hillside
will be used to fill in the front of the tank and to help build the access road. The Geotechnical study
is still in the process of being completed and will be posted to the Logan City Website under the
Capital Improvements page once complete, along with all of the other supporting documents and
reports. D. Farar also spoke about safety plans and said there will be a 42-inch discharge line that
would allow the tank to be emptied into the river if needed.

M. DeSimone added that the public notices were sent out to a much larger area than the required
300 feet in an effort to get the notice out.

R. Holley clarified for the public, that if the Geotechnical report came back stating this site is not

suitable, there would not be a building permit issued and the applicant would have to come back to
the Planning Commission with an alternate site.


Commissioner Lucero asked when the next public meeting will take place for the overall project and
if there will be any more opportunities for public input.

M. DeSimone said that the Mayor will make a statement regarding the findings of the final
engineering report at the February 18" City Council meeting, but other than that the only other
discussion will be regarding the budget.

Motion: Commissioner Heare made a motion to conditionally approve a Conditional Use Permit
for PC 25-007 10 MG Water Tank with the conditions of approval and findings for approval as listed
below. Commissioner McNamara seconded the motion.
