Pursuant to Utah Code 52-4-202 the undersigned, as Mayor of the Emigration
Canyon Metro Township Council, hereby declares that providing an anchor location for the
electronic meetings of the Council will present a substantial risk to the health and safety of those
who may be present at the anchor location for the following reasons:
The Township is located in Salt Lake County and is still under a “High Level of
Transmission” alert stage for the COVID 19 pandemic; and
The regular meeting place for the Council (the Fire Station in the Canyon) does
not have sufficient space in the meeting room to provide for the recommended physical distancing
to keep people safe from infection.

Reports from other Municipal Providers
Emigration Canyon Community Council (ECCC)
Bill Tobey stated annual brush chipping services will continue through the week.
The Unified Fire Authority (UFA) fuels crew is close to being fully staffed and expects to make it
through the entire township. There are several complaints from residents that have submitted
requests for home wildfire risk assessments and have not received a response. UFA
acknowledges that there is an issue with the system that accepts requests but has not found a
solution. Due to a late start in coordination with Salt Lake City (SLC), a planned large-scale fuel
treatment project is at risk. The area is on watershed property owned by Salt Lake City and
managed by the Department of Public Utilities. It was believed that SLC was budgeting and
preparing to pursue this type of work; however, the city utilities department does not have a
management plan in place to give permission to do fuels work and permission will not be
forthcoming. The ECCC is amending its bylaws to allow payments to be made with electronic
payment platforms like PayPal and Venmo.

Council Business
Emigration Canyon Creek Letter of Map Revision
Crystal Hulbert, Salt Lake County Public Works Operations, stated the cost of
updating the floodplain maps will be approximately $91,000. The current maps were created in
the 1980’s and have multiple errors. there have been a lot of development in the last 40 years.
There are houses in a floodplain area where there is not one and other houses that are not insured
that actually need flood insurance. If the Council decides to proceed with updating the maps, it
will have control of the process. It can pay for the study, review it, and then decide if it wants to
submit it to FEMA. It will be beneficial for the community to have an updated map.
Mayor Smolka stated there a few ways to fund the study; ARPA funds, capital
improvement funds, or the fund balance. If the fund balance is used, money will need to be
allocated to it.
Ms. Hulbert stated as soon as the funds are available in the engineering budget,
it will begin the process. It can take up to six months to complete.
