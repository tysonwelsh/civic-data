   Director
   Mayor        Silvestrini         said the council              is looking            at the prospect            of a telecommunications                           license
   tax which           is a franchise          fee on utility            cell phones          and telephones.                Millcreek            did not increase
   property       taxes         this fiscal      year,       but it is likely           the city     will     need to increase                  them      next       year to
   cover      the police          contract        fee. There           would      be public          engagement              before         anything           was
   implemented.             He asked           if the public            would       rather       see a property             tax increase               or a 3.5%
   increase       on their         cellphone         bill.     This     presentation             was meant           to educate            the council.


   Lisa     Dudley        looked         at other      revenue          sources         available       to the city          that    Millcreek               has not tapped
   into.     She said municipalities                     may     adopt        a telecom          license      tax on a telecommunication
   provider's          gross      receipts       from        certain     telecoini'nunication                 services.        TeIecommunication
   providers        may         either    pay the tax themselves                     or collect         the tax from              their     customers.           The
   telecom       license         tax is collected             and distributed             like     sales tax; it is paid                  to the Utah           State Tax
   Commission             then distributed               back.        It is general         revenue          that goes into           the General               Fund.      Utah
   cities     and towns           may      impose        a tax of up to 3.5%                 on the value               of telecom           service         provided
   within      their     boundaries.             If the telecom           service        provider           passes       the telecom             tax on to its
   customers,           the amount            is part of the service                sales price           for sales tax purposes.                      She showed              a
   table     of included           and excluded               taxable      services.         She explained               a table      showing            neighboring
   cities     and 3-year           average        taxable       revenue         per capita.          The      estimated           Millcreek            3-year        telecom
   tax revenue           was $362,751.               Dudley           explained         that with         the telecom             tax, the point-of-sale                   tax,
   and the portion of the sharing pool by If)opulation from the state, Millcreek would receive an
   estimated           $492,750          which      would        be equivalent              to a 4.16%            property         tax increase.


   To effect       the telecom             tax, Millcreek              would       need to approve                 an agreement                 with    the Tax
   Commission,             adopt         an ordinance,           and provide             notice      to the Tax            Commission.                 Utah      Code      10-
   1-4 is the Municipal                   Telecommunications                      License         Tax     Act.     Utah      State        Tax     Comn'iission             must
   receive      notice      from         the city     by October              Ist for    a January           Ist tax effective              date, the quickest
   option.      Dudley          reviewed          a potential          timeline         of council          document          approvals.               In Salt Lake
   County,        83% of the cities               and towns             have      assessed        a telecom          tax, 94%             have     assessed          an
   energy       tax,    and 89% have                assessed          a cable     tax.      Winder        said the tax base                 should        be broad         with

3. Pavement          Preservation            Initiatives;           John     Miller,       City     Engineer
   John Miller           gave an overview             on pavement             preservation,          maintenance              and cost, the current
   plans,     and pavement              funding      options.        The pavement            lifespan        cycle     started with              materials
   production,          pavement         design,     construction,            use, maintenance               and preservation,                   and then end
   of life.    Pavement         preservation             includes     preventative          maintenance,             minor          rehabilitation,             and
   some routine            maintenance,           not new pavement                 construction,           reconstruction,              major
   rehabilitation,           or corrective         maintenance.            The types of maintenance                    include           crack seal, slurry,
   chip seal and overlay,                micro      pave, mill        and overlay,          and full reconstruction.                     Water          and
   oxidation       kills     the road. He showed                the council          images       illustrating        these concepts.                   He gave a
   visual     cost breakdown              of how      far $500,000            could      go with      the various           maintenance                 options.
   He then illustrated             how long it would                 take to do the maintenance                    options          on Millcreek's                 179
   miles      of road. To reconstruct               all of them,           it would      take 303 years. He showed                            a graph with
   pavement        conditions           compared          to pavement           age. He said roads are designed                              for 20-30        years.
   Limitations          include      labor cost with           Salt Lake County,                 scheduling          issues, and 50+ years to
   address       all the roads.


   Additional        pavement           funding      options         are bonds        and a transportation                utility      fee (TUF).             Bonds
   fall on residents           and for-profit         businesses           with     property       taxes. The issue with                      bonds      is that it
  primarily        impacts        residents,       interest     rates, and exhausts                after 3-5 years. The TUF                        effects
  residential,          corninercial,      and public.          The General            Fund taxes help pay for pavement
  preservation           maintenance           and capital          projects,       and other city needs. With                      a TUF,         it pays for
  the pavement             preservation.          Perks with         the TUF         are that users contribute                  and share
  responsibility,            there are no Salt Lake              County           limitations,       and there would                be more consistent
  maintenance            projects.       He showed          an example             of Provo       City utilizing           a TUF            and the revenue
  produced.        Per month,           Provo      collects     $3.68 per single family                    dwelling,          $2.21 per multi-family,
  $9.98 for commercial                  A, $26.36 for commercial                       B, $79.28       for cornrnercial                C, $236.78             for
  commercial            D, $21.53        for public        use A, and $96.08               for public        use B. Mayor                    Silvestrini       noted
  Millcreek        did not bill         its own utilities.


  Miller      said the next step for obtaining                       a TUF,       is to do a transportation                 utility          fee study,
  introduce       the study to tlie council,                  then do coinmunication                  outreach.           He said 11 cities                 in Utah
  utilized      one. Mayor           Silvestrini         said a rational          basis was needed             for imposing                  the fee. Council

            lives     of others.      The single        event that stimulated             Mr.    Carnegie       to organize            the Fund was
