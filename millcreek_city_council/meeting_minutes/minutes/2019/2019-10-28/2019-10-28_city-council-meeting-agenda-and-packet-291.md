1. “Millcreek Common” City Center Open Space Discussion with EPG Design
Francis Lilly introduced Dave Harris and John Kelsh, EPG Design team, to the Council. He
said they would assist the City in designing the City Center open space. He said staff would
take a tour with them the next day to local sites.

Rita Lund introduced recent staff hire Kalie Boska, Information Center & IT Technician, to
the Council. Lund said Andrew Clark, the new Emergency Manager, started work that day.

Mike Winder said John Kelsh was meeting with city committees over the next few days. John
Kelsh went over his background experience with developing city downtowns (specifically
Rapids City, South Dakota) by creating audiences, utilizing evenings, and keeping the people
flow constant through the use of plazas with programming. He said the public and private
sector work together to create and maintain the space. He said there are many ways to make a
downtown work, but the critical point is getting the mass of people to the space. Mayor
Silvestrini said Millcreek was not looking for a cookie cutter shopping mall but something
that would be unique to Millcreek. Kelsh said it was better to invite people into the area than
an open free-for-all to fill the space. He said the consultants were in the final drafts of an
online poll to allow the public to prioritize amenities and provide their own ideas. He went
over the components of active parks, which included: a hard-surfaced plaza area, programmed
activities 250-360 days a year, a daily crowd of pedestrians, adjacent restaurants and
consumer retail, businesses open until 9-10 p.m., great restrooms, landscaping and
beautification, parking within 3 blocks, and adjacent upper floor residential. He went over
other park elements to consider like shade trees, shrubs and flowers, expansive lawn,
children’s play equipment, permanent seating, large art structures, ambient and safety lighting,
and water features. He mentioned that there are multiple park architectural styles to choose
from including mid-century modern, modern curvilinear, modern angular, modern with clean
angular lines, formal, traditional, pastoral, and organic. Kelsh said different amenity options
could be ice skating, a musical fountain, a splash pad, a stage, and summer/winter convertible
spaces. There should be very few amenities that people have to pay for to use since it is a
public space. He showed a sample activity calendar and site plan for a plaza.

Mayor Silvestrini asked if Kelsh had mapped out a schedule for public engagement. Kelsh
said he was meeting with city stakeholders for the next three days and would be conducting
the online public poll. Dave Harris said there would be a public engagement event series.
Mayor Silvestrini said he would like to engage with all parts of the community, even in the
high schools. He suggested having a Millcreek event to draw people to participate that would
not come to a meeting otherwise. Mike Winder said the event series dates would be
November 19, January 22, and March 17. Council Member Catten suggested meeting with the
Millcreek Business Council. Mayor Silvestrini said the Millcreek Business Council would
meet with the consultants as part of the stakeholder group. Kelsh said he could do some
engagement in the schools. Harris asked for the Council’s feedback with design and style for
the park. Mayor Silvestrini said themes in the Millcreek Together General Plan should be used
and said some features that had been previously discussed were a water-themed amenity, a
place for a farmer’s market, and a place for a movie night. He said Millcreek was connected
by nature, so the park should have a lot of trees. He said he would like variation in the plaza.
Council Member Catten recommended blending nature into a functional space. She brought
up the old seven canyons splash pad at Liberty Park in Salt Lake City.

3.1 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 19-45, Amending the Together
General Plan by Changing the Future Land Use Map in the Area of 4000 South and
700 West from Neighborhood 2 to Neighborhood 1; Blaine Gehring, Planner
Blaine Gehring said planning staff processed an amendment to the Millcreek Together
General plan in response to a neighborhood petition. The request was to change the area
in the vicinity of 4000 S. and 700 W. designated as Neighborhood 2 on the Future Land
Use Map to a Neighborhood 1 designation. Millcreek has two agricultural zones
remaining in the area which host a variety of agricultural uses that are no longer
compatible with urban living. The subject area is approximately one mile west from the
Meadowbrook TRAX Station and is situated among other medium to high density
housing just to the north and into South Salt Lake. Gehring said basic planning concepts
encourage a higher density of residential when in close proximity to public transit and
parks. One of the goals of the Millcreek Together General Plan is to increase the amount
of affordable housing in the City. Millcreek’s Future Land Use Map indicates the area to
be designated as “Neighborhood 2” to accommodate a mix of different higher density
housing types. During the overall General Plan process, it was felt that this was a good
area where medium density housing could be developed at a more affordable price range.
The area was designated Neighborhood 2 because of its adjacency to existing multiple
family housing, a large public park, and industrial zoning. At their meeting on September
3, 2019, the Millcreek Community Council sent a favorable recommendation to the
Planning Commission. At their meeting on September 18, 2019, the Millcreek Planning
Commission voted 4 in favor and 3 against a motion to send a favorable recommendation
to the City Council to change the area near 4000 S. 700 W. from Neighborhood 2 to
Neighborhood 1 designation. Mayor Silvestrini asked for public comment.

Bill Hodson, 3020 Metropolitan Way, asked what the application entailed. Council
Member Marchant said it was a change to the General Plan map, not the current zoning.
Mayor Silvestrini said it would not change the use of the property, but the General Plan
which provides guidance to what zoning should be adopted in the future. By changing the
designation, the long-term plan would be preserved as less dense residential. He said the
area is currently rural with open space but was adjacent to high density and TRAX. The
petition was started by the residents living there because they did not engage in the
General Plan process. Mayor Silvestrini said the Council had been open to listening to
suggestions on changing the Future Land Use map to accommodate what people wanted
to see for future planning and this application was an extension of that. The property was
currently zoned agricultural.

Marv Poulson, 3631 Carolyn Street, said he was in favor of the citizen petition.

Brandi Southard, 4088 S. 700 W., said the neighborhood residents were not given
General Plan notice because they have a Taylorsville zip code. She said property in their

meant that none of it was purchased with the belief that higher density should be allowed.
