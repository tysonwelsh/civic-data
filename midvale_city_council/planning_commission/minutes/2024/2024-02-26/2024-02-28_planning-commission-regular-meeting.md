2.   Carl Greene requests Rezone approval for two properties located at approximately
     8253 S Main St. It is proposed that the properties currently zoned Regional
     Commercial (RC) be rezoned to Multifamily Residential – Medium to High Density
     (RM-25).

     The rezone proposal is to change two parcels from RC to RM-25. Please see the
     Rezone Exhibit in the attachments.

     -REZONE PROCEDURE-

     Midvale City Code 17-3-1(E.) outlines the criteria necessary for granting a rezone as
     follows:
     17-3-1 Criteria/Required Findings. …rezoning of land is to be discouraged and allowed
     only under the limited circumstances herein described. Therefore, the planning
     commission may recommend, and the city council may grant, a rezoning application
     only if it determines, in written findings, that the proposed rezoning is consistent with the
     policies and goals of the general plan and that the applicant has demonstrated that the:

        1. Proposed rezoning is necessary either to comply with the general plan proposed
           land use map or to provide land for a community need that was not anticipated at
           the time of adoption of the general plan;
        2. Existing zoning was either the result of a clerical error or a mistake of fact, or that
           it failed to take into account the constraints on development created by the natural
           characteristics of the land, including but not limited to, steep slopes, flood plain,
           unstable soils, and inadequate drainage; or
        3. Land or its surrounding environment has changed or is changing to such a degree
           that it is in the public interest to encourage redevelopment of the area or to
           recognize the changed character of the area.

     The proposed rezone satisfies Goal #1 and #3 listed above. Please see the Reason for
     Rezone provided by the applicant in the attachments.

     Public notice has been sent to property owners within 500 feet of the subject parcel. No
     written objections have been received as of the writing of this report.

     This proposal has been reviewed by Planning Staff, the City Engineer, the Unified Fire
     Authority, and Public Works for compliance with the respective guidelines, policies,
     standards, and codes. Staff finds the proposed rezone complies with requirements outlined
     in Midvale City Code.

3.     Midvale City initiated request to amend Section 17-7-4.10(B)(1)(d)(iii) of the
       Multifamily Residential – Medium to High Density (RM-25) zone allowing a
       maximum of 5,000 square feet of neighborhood commercial in the Midvale City
       Municipal Code.

       Midvale City requests an amendment to the above-listed chapter of the Midvale City
       Municipal Code. This section regulates Neighborhood Commercial in the RM-25 zone.
       The proposed amendment increases the allowable commercial square footage from
       1,500 to 5,000 square feet. No other criteria will be changed. Staff feels that due to the
       higher density of the zone, a larger commercial area would be feasible.

       Public notice has been sent to affected entities as required in 17-3-9.B of the Municipal
       Code. No comments have been received as of the writing of this report.

       Staff recommends approval.


                                             ***
APPLICANT:

Wendelin Knobloch, Planning Director, explains the historical reasoning for the current zoning.
While it may have worked then, it is not allowing for enough space for today’s residential
expectations. The Planning Department intends to amend the code to allow for more higher
density housing and addresses the need for walkable neighborhoods.

DISCUSSION:

Chair Liedtke questions the possibility of the conditions where a homeowner would be able to
build out the residential area to the maximum 5,000 square feet.

Mr. Knobloch explains that there are strict conditions that must be met:
        1. The area must be pedestrian oriented
        2. Intersection of two surface streets that are at a minimum 50 feet wide (rare in Midvale)
These conditions would restrict how frequently these instances could occur with the smaller roads
that are all throughout Midvale City.

Commissioner Snow asks if there is a minimum square footage.

Mr. Knobloch clarifies that the text amendment would not include a minimum square footage,
only a maximum.

Commissioner Edwards would like clarification on the term “consolidation” used in the same
section of the code. In the previous case discussed in this meeting, there were two lots combined
to make the project possible.

Wendelin Knobloch, Planning Director, explains the historical reasoning for the current zoning.
While the neighborhood commercial may have worked in the past, it is not allowing for enough
space for neighborhood commercial expectations today. The City proposes to amend the code to
allow for more neighborhood commercial square footage and with that address one component
of the need for walkable neighborhoods.

Mr. Knobloch explains that the term “consolidation” is stretchable. Technically, several lots are
consolidated into one in a subdivision plat. In the previously mentioned development, the
consolidation does not have to occur in such a way, other instruments can be used to achieve a
similar outcome.

The Commissioners explain initial concerns about the massive change in the square footage
allowance. They then discuss the restrictions and how strict they are. The restrictions bring them

comfort knowing this amendment will not allow a massive change in residential structures
throughout the city.

Commissioner Edwards and Commissioner Anderson express concern that this amendment may
fit the previously presented development but consider the concerns of houses in other
neighborhoods.

Chair Liedtke reminds the Commissioners that the major streets intersection points strongly
mitigate the impact this amendment would have on the community. There are no homes that hit

Jonathan Anderson, Planner II, clarifies that this amendment only affects the RM-25, which is one
of the highest densities in the city. No neighborhoods zoned for SF-1, SF-2, or RM-12 will be
included in this change.
