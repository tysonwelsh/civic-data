while they intend to leave the starting and ending locations, they plan to move the route of the canal to
accommodate their site plans.

Jason White of 50s Capital provided some information regarding the current zoning. He addressed some
concerns from last meeting regarding the river trail and open space plans.

Ryan Smith shared his experience with being a business owner in Provo and discussed his vision of the
project.

Councilor Ellsworth asked about a parking structure project adjacent to the river.

Keith Morey, Assistant Director of Development Services, answered that the project had been approved
but had not been started.

Councilor Fillmore asked Mr. Smith to share his vision of the incubator.

Mr. Smith responded that he intends this space to be used for startup companies. He said his hope is to
allow a space for new business owners that allows them to get help from other surrounding companies
in a city and location that promotes success.

Councilor Fillmore also asked if there was a plan to help with retention of the companies who start in
Provo at this site.

Mr. Smith said that there is space for these businesses to stay in Provo after they expand. He also shared
that the way office space is being used has evolved.

Chair Handley opened this item for public comment.

Aaron Ardmore provided comments he received from Greg Bird, Tammy Sue Bird, and Trevor Anderson

Councilor Ellsworth shared her excitement and support for this project. She thanked the developers for
working toward a development agreement.

Councilor Hoban shared his excitement for the development. He encouraged the developers to be
cognizant of the surrounding businesses during construction, specifically the Heritage School. He asked
about the environmental concerns that were brought up last meeting.

Mr. Ivory shared that an environmental study will be conducted as part of the project approval process.

Mr. Smith added that they purchased some property near this site that had a substantial number of
studies done.

Councilor Whipple said she is excited for the project. She shared that this would be the perfect place to
live due to it being near so much. She said she appreciates the preservation of public access and the
creative office space to be a gateway to the city. She complimented the landscaping that will allow some
privacy from the surrounding schools and businesses.

Vice-Chair MacKay shared her support for the project. She asked about the barrier between the
businesses on the South side and the residences.

Mr. Ivory responded that there is not currently a transition and there is not a plan to create a gated
community.

Councilor Ellsworth asked for clarification on the excess capacity on the westside in terms of the sewer.

Mr. Ardmore answered and shared the Public Works staff recommendation.

Councilor Fillmore asked why the Planning Commission denied this proposal.

project is approved, future projects with higher densities will be requested.

Councilor Hoban asked if the developers were to propose something closer to the neighborhood to the

Vice-Chair MacKay explained the infrastructure issues that this project poses in comparison to the
project that was approved earlier.

Councilor Whipple said she likes the project plan and does not see why the areas that are not going to
be developed could not be used towards this project as far as capacity goes. She shared her concern

recommended that council not only consider projects that have four or five units per acre. He said that
restriction does not help with affordable housing.

Chair Handley explained that the council would like to see more diversity in housing types per area to
mitigate all the density being in one place.

Councilor Hoban said it is unfortunate that the developers financially cannot limit the number of units
they are proposing because he likes the idea of more affordable housing.

Chair Handley said his preference is to vote on the item tonight and shared why he would be voting
against the motion.
