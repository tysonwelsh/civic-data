       A.     "TIMBERLINE DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT," PLZ-25-4009, A
              DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT, GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT, AND
              REZONE OF APPROXIMATELY 4.24 ACRES LOCATED AT 13518 SOUTH
              REDWOOD ROAD. APPLICANT – RANDY SMITH, FIELDSTONE HOMES.

Development Services Director, Jason Lethbridge, reported that significant changes had
been made to the Development Agreement since it was last presented to the Planning
Commission. A Development Agreement functions differently from other land use changes
as it provides for standards other than what currently exists in the property’s zone.

The 4.24-acre subject property was identified on the Vicinity Map, and Mr. Lethbridge
reported that the existing zoning was Commercial Neighborhood (“CN”) and Rural
Residential (“RR-22”). The City Council previously considered an application for a large-
animal veterinary clinic on one parcel and approved its rezoning to CN, but that project did
not move forward. CN is the City’s most restrictive commercial zone; most uses are
conditional and limited to those that are more compatible with residential areas. The
proposed zone was RM-8.

The General Plan designates the property Medium-Density Residential (0.25-acre minimum
lot size) and Estate-Density Residential (0.50-acre minimum lot size). Mr. Lethbridge
reported that the General Plan is an advisory guide for land use decisions but is not a binding
document. The application included a request to designate the subject property Medium-
High Density Residential.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that comments had been received about an existing house in the
project area. Based on the submitted Site Plan, the house would be demolished and
replaced with an access road. It was brought to the City’s attention that the home was
placed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1992. However, the registry is a
tracking device and does not restrict property owners’ rights. If federal funding was received
for the property, there may be additional requirements, but there were no specific restrictions
against demolishing the structure. The Riverton City Historic Preservation Commission
requires a 10-day grace period to document the location prior to demolition. An advantage
of being on the National Register is that a great deal of information about the property had
been preserved.

The original Concept Plan was reviewed indicating two stub roads into the property, one
each from the west and north. The original concept showed 1775 West as a public right-of-
way connecting to Redwood Road. An existing cul-de-sac would not connect to the project,
but space would be left available to connect to the property to the south. The Planning
Commission previously heard and tabled the item pending further discussion by the City
Council. The applicant subsequently modified the site layout based on direction from the
Council and consultation with Staff. The roadway to the west will remain unchanged, and
1775 West will be extended to provide a compliant turnaround for emergency vehicles and
become part of the public right-of-way. Walkways that connect to other neighborhoods were
also included in the Concept Plan. Mr. Lethbridge displayed photographs of a neighborhood
with a similar configuration as that planned for 1775 West.

A total of 30 units on approximately 3,000-square-foot lots was proposed. The internal
alleyway will be 20-feet wide on its northern and southern ends and expand to 28 feet
adjacent to the fire hydrant. Current City Code only allows narrow widths on private lanes
serving two lots or less. Because the proposal was outside of City standard, the applicant
provided renderings showing full driveway widths off of the 20-foot alleyway. Mr. Lethbridge
also presented aerial photographs of the 20-foot private lanes in Daybreak.

The Development Agreement proposed the following minimums:

   •   Lot size: 3,000 square feet
   •   Front setback: 20 feet (home and garage)
   •   Side setback: 5 feet
   •   Rear setback: 8 feet
   •   Private road width: 20 feet

The RR-4 Zone specifies the following minimums:

   •   Lot size: 10,000 square feet
   •   Front setback: 15 feet home, 25 feet garage
   •   Side setback: 8 feet (10 feet on garage side)
   •   Rear setback: 25 feet
   •   Private road width: 25 feet

The RM-6 Zone is limited to single-family detached homes. RM-8 allows attached, multi-
family homes. The minimums for those zones are:

   •   Lot size: 5,000 square feet
   •   Front setback: 15 feet home, 20 feet garage
   •   Side setback: 5 feet
   •   Rear setback: 15 feet
   •   Private road width: 26 to 28 feet

Masonry fencing was proposed along Redwood Road, with six-foot vinyl fencing for the
remainder of the project perimeter. Mr. Lethbridge reported that whether the turnaround
area is fenced off from the rest of the development or left open was not addressed in the
Development Agreement.

Mr. Lethbridge reported that Riverton Cove, located west of City Hall, was developed as
RM-6 with lot sizes between 5,000 and 6,000 square feet. It meets all setback requirements
of the zone. The private lane is 28 feet of asphalt width with sidewalk on one side. Another
project near 3600 West and Water Street was also developed under RM-6 and has road
widths between 25 and 32 feet.

The Planning Commission had the option to recommend approval of the application with the
proposed conditions outlined in the Staff Report, recommend denial, or recommend
approval with modifications. The previous public hearing was closed, but City Attorney,
Ryan Carter recommended that it be opened for additional comment.

Chair Park referred to Section 4.1.3 of the Development Agreement, which specifies an
eight-foot front setback. Mr. Lethbridge reported that there was a discrepancy between the
written agreement and the Site Plan, which indicates a 20-foot setback. He spoke with the
applicant, and it was confirmed that the intended front setback is 20 feet. That was corrected
in the file copy of the Development Agreement.

Commissioner Cannon expressed concern about the narrow private lane and the lack of
sidewalks in the interior of the project. He liked that the access to 1775 West had been
changed, but believed the roads were too narrow. He was on the Planning Commission at
the time the 28-foot road was approved, and the approval was conditioned on gates being
installed. The applicant then wanted to eliminate the gate on the grounds that the roads
were too narrow. The subject property is one of the last parcels on Redwood Road that
could support larger lots, and Mr. Nokes had indicated that he would entertain an offer to
extend the road onto his property. That road could then be stubbed to the south and back
to Redwood Road, which could potentially create one to two more lots in the subdivision.

Commissioner Matheson asked if any development was projected to the south.
Mr. Lethbridge stated that equipment has been on the property for some time, but he was
not aware of any active construction or development plans.

possible to add sidewalks if the front setbacks were reduced. It will be a small community,

applications for the site several times. The applicant was ready to move forward with the
project, but wanted to add a second story to a previously approved one-story building.
Notices were sent to property owners within 300 feet of the subject property, and no public
comments were received.

Mr. Prestwich reviewed the aerial map and Site Plans and noted that a lot of work was done
to ensure that fire trucks can access the property and that there is appropriate screening for
the neighboring homes to the south and park to the east. After the initial Site Plan was
approved, the applicant returned with a request to remove the second story from Building
200 in the center of the property. That amendment was also approved. However, when the
developer applied for a building permit, the Site Plan had been modified to add a second
story to Building 300.

The new proposed Site Plan included the following changes:

   •   A maximum building height of 32 feet, 11 inches for Building 300.
   •   A small sidewalk was added from the public right-of-way to meet ADA compliance
       requirements.
   •   The driveway was shortened slightly.

Mr. Prestwich reviewed the approved and proposed elevations and reported that the
proposed front elevation for Building 300 is approximately 22 feet from the grade on 5600
West to the peak of the roof and 19 feet to the top of the wall. The rear of the building is
