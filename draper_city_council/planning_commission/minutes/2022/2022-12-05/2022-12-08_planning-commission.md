5.     Public Hearing: Southfork Lot 2 Zoning Map Amendment (Legislative Action)
On the request of Brad and Catherin Findlay, property owners, for a zoning map amendment to
change the zoning from RA1 (Residential Agricultural, 40,000 square foot lot minimum) to RA2
(Residential Agricultural, 20,000 square foot minimum) for approximately 1.61 acres located at
approximately 1094 E 13800 S. Application: TEXTMAP- 134-2021. Staff contact: Maryann
Pickering at 801-576-6391 or maryann.pickering@draperutah.gov
Commissioner Hawker asked to recuse himself from the hearing and was excused from this item.
Maryann Pickering presented the project. The applicant Brad and Catherin Findlay, property
owners, were requesting the change in zones from RA1 to RA2. The applicants would like to come
 Draper City Planning Commission

back and apply for a subdivision to sell the property behind their home, if it is approved through
the city council with a positive recommendation. A private road would have to be installed, and
possible demolition of a structure would need to take place if the zoning is approved. There were
public comments made over the phone and email in opposition of this item.
Applicant Brad Findlay addressed the commissioners. Mr. Findlay stated that his original plan was
to raise his kids with horses and that was why they bought the property years ago. His children had
allergies and having horses was not an option for them. Now the children have all grown up and
moved away and the property was hard to keep up on maintenance. The structure to be removed
was a barn. If the structure is removed they could build a home that would be the exact size of the
existing barn.
Chairman Adams opened the public hearing.
Public Comment:
Dave Oeser (resident in opposition). Mr. Oeser stated this change could impact his barn, horses,
fences and neighboring property. Mr. Oeser stated he thinks it should stay horse property. It is the
way it was designed for the location.
Ed Peterson (resident in opposition) Mr. Peterson stated large lots are nice. The distance between
houses should be bigger because it provides more privacy. Mr. Peterson stated the setbacks were
off and do not meet code. He expressed his concern that a ten-foot setback on a busy street was
not ideal. Mr. Peterson stated that landscaping should not be an issue. He expressed concern and
said if this were to be approved he would file a class action lawsuit against the city and Findlay’s
for the change in home values and lifestyles.
Catherine Findlay (applicant) explained she was unaware of the opposition. Mrs. Catherine Findlay
stated that they love their neighbors and want to keep the peace.
Tracie Petersen (resident in opposition). Ms. Petersen stated she was not notified of the meeting.
She stated that none of the neighbors in the area were notified of this or of the meeting. Change
does happen, but the neighbors don't want townhomes to go up near them. It is not a big enough
space for this to take place.
Chairman Adams closed the public hearing.
Commissioner Nixon asked what the radius of notifications went out to the neighbors. Jennifer
Jastremsky stated it was a 400-foot radius and approximately 21-22 notices were sent out in the
mail.
The commissioners discussed this item, noting the concerns of the residents. The commissioners
agreed that this would not be the best interest for the property, they have seen it happen in other
locations. Chairman Adams asked about the topography of the land. A map was shown that the
back property had a slight incline and hill. Chairman Adams stated it is a case-by-case scenario, it
would potentially be doable, the question was should it be done?

6.     Public Hearing: Kuwahara’s Pumpkin Patch & Thriller Park Conditional Use Permit
       (Administrative Action)
On the request of Alex Kuwahara for a Conditional Use Permit for outdoor storage on 4.19 acres
of land located at 12153 S 700 W. Application USE-180-2022. Staff contact is Jennifer Jastremsky,
801-576-6328, jennifer.jastremsky@draperutah.gov
Jennifer Jastremsky presented the project. The request is for Kuwahara’s pumpkin patch and
thriller park to use the outdoor storage during their off-season. This property has always been
agriculture and has gotten bigger over the years. Rather than storing things off-site they have
requested to store things onsite. Each year the business applies for a special event application for
their business.

The question arose about fencing for the areas where the storage would take place and what type
of fencing would be used?

Commissioner Squire asked if the fencing will be permanent or temporary. Ms. Jastremsky stated
that is a question for the applicant.

Applicant: Alex Kuwahara addressed the commissioners stating that offsite storage costs have
gone up and they would like to store all things on site to cut down the costs and keep the budget
down for their business to flourish. His family has been farming on that location for fifty years.
This business has gotten bigger each year.

Commissioner Nixon asked about the fence, what type would be used, and if storage sheds would
be brought in. Mr. Kuwahara stated all things stored on site would be in existing sheds,
greenhouses, and buildings. The type of fencing that would be used was the walls of the castle on
the inside. They would be like a partition closing off to the public and if anyone broke into the
property, they would not be able to access. The entire property is surrounded with a fence, would
not be in heaps, but would be securely and neatly stored in the buildings.

Commissioner Adams opened the public hearing

Public Comment:

JoDee Esplin (resident) stated she was concerned that this would be a rubber stamp approval. And
if so, it would make it so the business would not have to come in and reapply for future
applications. Ms. Esplin expressed concern about the parking issue and stated that during the
events cars are parked along both sides of busy streets, making it dangerous for all involved.

Chairman Adams closed the public hearing

Chairman Adams asked the applicant Alex Kuwahara about the parking, and how it could
potentially fix the parking issue.

Mr. Kuwahara stated with the business getting bigger and bigger the parking and traffic were
definitely impacted. Mr. Alex Kuwahara reported to the commissioners that he plans to post no
parking signs in areas. He also stated they hope to create a shuttle, from a local park to the location
to help with this in the coming years.

       Motion: Commissioner Nixon made the motion to approve the Conditional Use
       Permit as requested by Alex Kuwahara for Kuwahara’s Pumpkin Patch & Thriller
       Park. Application USE-180-2022. Based on the findings and subject to the conditions
       listed in the staff report dated November 29, 2022. Emphasizing this approval is for
       the conditional use permit and not on the special event application.

       Second: Commissioner Squire

       Vote: A roll call was taken with the Commissioners voting 5-0 in favor of the motion.
       Commissioners Fowler, Hawker, Squire, Nixon, and Bingham voted, “Aye”
