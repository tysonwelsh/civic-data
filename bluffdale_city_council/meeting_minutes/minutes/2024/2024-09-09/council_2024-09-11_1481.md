In 2022,there were 196 total cases, primarily for encroachment,vehicles, weeds,and building issues.
In 2023, there were 229 total cases, primarily parking issues in the Independence area. It was noted

Mr. Cramer presented a flowchart outlining the ACE Program. After a complaint is received, an
investigation is opened. If the issue is resolved quickly, the case is closed. If not, a Notice of
Violation is issued that indicates a timeframe for correction. Depending on the violation, they are
usually given seven to 10 days to correct the issue. If an extension is requested, it is usually granted
because, in his experience, people are more willing to come into compliance if you speak with them
and help them resolve the issue. If the issue is not corrected, a $100 per day fine begins to accrue.
Once the violation is corrected, he reinspects the property. If fines are not paid, a lien can be placed
on the property. If they are paid, the case is closed out. If a hearing is requested, they will appear
before an Administrative Law Judge.


Mr. Crowell stated that the City has contracted with an Administrative Law Judge. If needed, the
first hearing will be held in November 2024. New Notices of Violation are currently being issued
and it will likely be October before fines begin to accrue, so there may not be a need for a hearing in
November. They anticipate holding administrative hearings quarterly. An increase in the number of
cases that go to the administrative court is also anticipated, but most cases will be resolved prior to
that stage.

       7.1     Resolution 2024-58- Rescinding Resolution 2020-52 Naming Fire Station 92
               after Retired Fire Chief John C.Roberts. (StaffPresenter, Fred Donaldson)

City Attorney, Fred Donaldson presented an overview of the resolution. The fire station was
previously named the John C. Roberts Fire Station, and the resolution was to rescind the previous
resolution and remove his name fi"om the station. John Roberts is Bluffdale's former Fire Chief. In

Case# 221-908-416, State vs. John Calvin Roberts,Mr.Roberts was charged with one count ofMisuse
of Public Money, a second-degree felony, and five counts of Falsifying or Altering Government
Records, a Class B Misdemeanor. Based on payroll documents for the time period between March 1,
2018,and March 31,2020, Mr. Roberts was found to have made handwritten modifications to reports
that added employees to logs or altered employee hours. In comparing these records to the hours
actually worked, it was determined that they had been altered by Mr. Roberts to indicate that
firefighters were working for the City when they were also reportedly working for other fire
departments.


According to Todd Sheeran's investigation, over the two-year period, 367 daily reports were altered
by Mr. Roberts, resulting in short staffing of Bluffdale fire stations. Over a two-year period, one fire
station only reported being short-staffed nine days but was in fact short-staffed 231 days. This
resulted in the City not being able to identify the need for more firefighters. That need has only
recently been addressed via the Public Safety Fee. The misfeasance resulted in firefighters being
overpaid an estimated $128,609.51.


Mr. Roberts admitted in an interview that he had intentionally altered the time logs to generate
additional pay for affected employees and that he knew his actions were wrong. He pled guilty in a
Plea in Abeyance Agreement to one count of Attempted Misuse of Public Money, a third-degree
felony, on June 18, 2024. The elements of the plea, as articulated in his form, state, "As a public
servant, I appropriated public money to the benefit ofanother without authority oflaw." The facts he
admitted to committing were,"On or about March 1, 2018, while employed by Bluffdale City, I
diverted public funds to my employees without permission or authority to do so."

       8.2       Ordinance 2024-18 - Proposed Text Amendment Updating Section 11.30.060 of
                 the Bluffdale City Code to Establish Class A and Class B Notices. Notice to
                 Military Installations, and the Utah Department of Transportation, along with
                 related housekeeping items. (StaffPresenter, Caitlvn Tubbs.

Ms. Tubbs presented the Staff Report and stated that the Text Amendment was to bring City Code
into compliance with new state laws. SB-43 created two classes of notices, Class A and Class B,for
the purpose ofstreamlining the system for providing public notices. Similarly, HB-256 requires cities
and counties to provide notice to military lands or installations when a Land Use application is
received for property within 5,000 feet ofthe military boundary.

Class A Notices are for administrative items such as Site Plans. Those notices are required to be
posted on the Utah Public Notice website, Bluffdale's official website, and at City Hall. If the item
pertains to alteration or vacation of a street, right-of-way, or easement, a physical sign must also be
posted at the location.


Class B Notices are for legislative items and include all Class A noticing requirements plus a
geographical mailing. Depending on the type of application, notices will be mailed to affected
property owners within 300 or 1,000 feet ofthe subject property. Additionally,notices must be mailed
to affected entities. For example, YESCO if they are looking at site-specific signage standards, the
Canal Company, or anyone else who has formally requested that the City send them an Affected
Entity Notice.

Class A Notices will be sent regarding Administrative actions that the City is required to approve if
the application meets all City standards. Class B Notices will be sent regarding Legislative actions,
which allow for more discretion and for which the City encourages more public input.

With regard to Conditional Use Permits, Utah Code 10-9a-507(3) states,"A Land Use Authority's
decision to approve or deny conditional use is an administrative Land Use action." In the past,
Bluffdale removed the public hearing requirement from administrative permit actions, and Staff
proposed that the same change be made for Conditional Use permits.

Ms. Tubbs noted that the Council recently adopted the West Traverse Moxintain Compatibility Area
Study as Bluffdale's Compatibility Plan for all Land Uses within 5,000 feet of Camp Williams. As
part ofthe recent legislation, all properties within 5,000 feet of Camp Williams are required to notify
the Utah Department of Veterans and Military Affairs("UDVMA")ofall Land Use applications with
the exception ofbuilding permits. By statute, the UDVMA is then granted a 90-day review period to
determine ifthe use is compatible with the mission ofCamp Williams.

The Planning Commission reviewed the proposed Text Amendment on August 21, 2024, and
unanimously forwarded a positive recommendation to the City Council. On August 28, 2024, a
formal request was received from the Utah Department ofTransportation("UDOT")to provide notice
ofany Land Use application that could potentially impact High Priority Transportation Corridors,and
that was included for the Council's consideration. Staff did not receive any public input on the item.

In summary,the Text Amendment includes:

   • A revised noticing table to include Class A and Class B designations.
   • Removed the public hearing requirement from the Conditional Use Permit process.
   • Added UDVMA and UDOT notices to the noticing table.
   • Minor housekeeping amendments.
