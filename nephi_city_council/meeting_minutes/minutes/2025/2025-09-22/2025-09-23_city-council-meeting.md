SUBJECT:        DATA CENTER/AGRI-PARK ANNEXATION LEVEL 2 ANALYSIS


Nephi City has been approached by a number of parties to potentially annex land in order to
provide city services to data centers, their accompanying power generation facilities, and to
the R6 Agri-Park project. These projects have previously requested that the areas be added
into the city’s annexation area map as a Level 3. The Planning Commission and City Council
have done so. The areas in purple below have been specifically requested to be moved to a
Level 2 in the annexation area map so that the Level 2 analysis can be completed. The City
Council allowed these areas to be moved to a Level 2 in the annexation area map. The Level
2 analysis is found below. City properties have also been included as part of the Level 2
analysis even though they have not been officially moved to a Level 2 through Council
action. The City Council can determine whether or not they would like to include these city-
owned properties as part of a future annexation request.




The block of properties that is being requested for annexation is currently made up of mostly
agricultural dry farming land. There are 31 full parcels included in the Level 2 areas along
with 3 more partial parcels near the Agri-Park area. There are approximately 2,903 acres
included, with 775 of those acres being city-owned property. That leaves 2,128 acres
associated with areas for data centers and the Agri-Park. The city-owned properties contain
the city’s sewer ponds, city-owned land, and the J. Randy McKnight Nephi Municipal Airport. If
utilities were extended to serve the data center/Agri-Park area, they could also be used to
serve city-owned facilities.

The city’s annexation policy plan identifies the policies and criteria that should govern
annexations. This plan is linked below under Attachments. The policies from the plan are as
follows:

1. Sound development within towns and cities is essential to the continued economic
development of the State of Utah;

2. Municipalities are primarily created to provide urban-type governmental services essential
for development and for the protection of public health, safety and welfare in residential,
commercial, and industrial areas, and in areas under-going development;

3. Municipal boundaries should be extended, in accordance with specific standards, to include
areas where urban-type governmental services are needed and can be provided for the
protection of public health, safety and welfare, and to avoid the inequities of double-taxation
and the proliferation of special service districts;

4. Areas annexed to municipalities in accordance with appropriate standards should receive
the services provided by the annexing municipality consistent with a planned extension
policy;

5. Areas annexed to municipalities should include all of the urbanized unincorporated areas
contiguous to municipalities, securing the residents within these areas a voice in the selection
of their government and the services they shall receive;

6. Decisions with respect to municipal boundaries and urban development need to be made
with adequate consideration of the effect of the proposed actions on adjacent areas and on
the interests of other government units, on the cost of needed local government services, the
ability to deliver the services under the proposed actions, and on factors related to population
growth and density of the geographic area; and

7. The economic and financial impacts of annexation to existing towns and cities must be
evaluated to ensure that annexation is financially desirable.

The policy plan also has criteria for annexation that will be the basis for the analysis in this
report.
        The need for municipal services - Both the data centers and the Agri-Park have
         specifically requested to receive city-provided utilities, particularly electric power.
         Power:
Receiving power through other utility providers has proved to be very challenging. Nephi
City’s unique membership in UMPA as well as the interconnect that Nephi Power already has
with Pacificorp is attractive to these businesses seeking electric power. By providing power
Nephi City stands to gain additional infrastructure that could serve city-owned facilities. In
addition, the power needs of data centers and the Agri-Park provide the opportunity to provide
lucrative power sales that will enhance the ability for Nephi Power to pay off existing
infrastructure as well as improve all aspects of this community-owned power company.
Water:
Nephi City has water infrastructure near the area that could be used to provide industrial and
domestic water needs for data centers and the Agri-Park. This is particularly true if the city
requires data centers to use air-cooled mechanics to lower the temperature of their server
infrastructure. When it comes to power generation, the water needs greatly increase. It is
estimated that the water needs of a data center’s domestic use would be approximately 40
acre feet per year, roughly the equivalent of a subdivision of 45 homes. The power
generation facilities have a much larger usage. They can be anywhere from 220 to 436 acre
feet per year. This would be the equivalent of 245 to 487 homes. The Council has already
been clear that the water for power generation would need to be supplied by the
developer. The developer could purchase water rights and deed them to the community or
participate in the Central Utah Water Conservancy pipeline project to bring water to Juab
County.
Sewer:
The city’s sewer ponds are in the area but topography would necessitate that any connection
to the sewer ponds be engineered and would most likely need a lift station. It is most likely
feasible but would need to be examined more thoroughly by an engineer.
Natural Gas:
The proposed developments in this area would need to bring additional natural gas facilities to
the area for power generation purposes. Typical natural gas needs could be served with the
infrastructure that is in the area. High pressure infrastructure would be required to be owned
by the city’s utility once it was in the city limits. Power generation facilities would use a
significant amount of natural gas and sales of this would be lucrative for the natural gas
utility.

The Agri-Park is anticipated to have similar industrial utility needs to other industries in the
city. There are approximately 921 acres devoted to the Agri-Park. Some of that acreage will
be devoted to infrastructure and roads but the rest will be available for industries that are yet
to be determined. The industries that are expected to locate to the Agri-Park would most
likely be able to be served with the city’s typical utility extension. However, there may be
businesses who have high capacity needs for electric, gas, or water services. In this case,
the infrastructure investments made by the data centers would most likely enable the city to
have the capacity to serve them.
      The character of the community - Nephi City prides itself on being a rural
       community. While growth has occurred, it has been managed through annexation and
       zoning. This annexation would be, by far, the largest annexation the city has ever
       seen and may ever see. While the acreage proposed for annexation is significant, the
       industry of a data center would not have a large impact on the community. Data
       centers typically employ few people and therefore, the impact to general city services
       and other taxing entities is anticipated to be small. The Agri-Park may have a more
       significant impact as far as employment and indirect effects. However, the mission of
       the Agri-Park is to improve the value of agricultural products and land. This mission
       seems to fit the rural atmosphere that is desired by Nephi City residents and the city’s
       General Plan.
      Plans for extension of municipal services - Water and natural gas utilities are
       currently located at the airport to serve the National Guard Readiness Center. These
       utilities could be extended for typical uses at the developer’s cost. Higher capacity
       utility needs would require the developer to invest in an upgrade of infrastructure. An
       electrical connection along Meadow Lane could be installed to receive higher capacity
       service. This extension would be installed at the cost of the developer. Sewer
       services would need to be evaluated and engineered. Any sewer extensions would be
       at the cost of the developer.
      How the services shall be financed - Any development on the property would be
       required to extend the utilities as well as pay appropriate impact fees. Ongoing
       maintenance would be the responsibility of the city but developments would be paying
       ongoing utility fees to fund the ongoing maintenance. Some or all of the proposed
       developments in the area may utilize the state’s Inland Port Authority to finance their
       developments. This would essentially be a Redevelopment Area or Tax Increment
       financing. If this financing method is used, taxing entities would see a reduction in
       property taxes received for the length of the tax increment financing. This time frame
       is typically 20 years.
      An estimate of the tax consequences - Data Centers are significant investments
       due to the information technology infrastructure. Taxing entities could potentially
       receive a significant amount of property taxes from these investments. It is unknown
       what businesses will locate to the Agri-Park but the property taxes would be similar to
       any large industry currently in the city limits. None of the expected businesses are
       anticipated to contribute to the sales taxes of the city.

It is estimated that one data center development is approximately $7,000,000,000 in value. If
the development were assessed at this value, the property taxes to the city would be
approximately $3,528,000 annually assuming that the market to assessed value ratio is
100%. If the data center development used the Inland Port Authority to finance their
development, the city would receive 25% of the assessed value for the length of the tax
increment plan, typically 20 years. The calculation if the Inland Port Authority funding
mechanism is used would be approximately $882,000 annually. Currently the city receives
roughly $500,000 annually in property taxes. Utility revenues would be tougher to calculate
because the city does not currently have a rate for electric or natural gas usage of this
magnitude. Dividend transfers are currently calculated at 8% of the utilities gross
revenues. However, for major rate payers, only a portion of the revenue is used for this
calculation. This is to avoid dependence on a single ratepayer, which could significantly affect
operations.
     The interests of all affected entities - All public tax entities will be notified of any
        annexation proposal and will have an opportunity to voice their opposition, neutrality,
        or support of the proposal.
     20-Year Population Projection - The Data Center projects are not anticipated to add
        significant population numbers to the city. There may be a temporary influx during the
        construction of these facilities.
Depending on the number of jobs from industries in the Agri-Park, there could be an impact
on the population of Nephi City. Nephi City is currently growing at approximately 2% per
year. This is roughly 145 people or 50 households each year on average. An Agri-Park
business with 500 new jobs would add approximately 120 new households to the city
assuming that 70% of the workforce chose to live in the city limits. This could enlarge the
annual average growth rate of the city by a significant margin.
     Full Development Plan - A concept plan has not yet been shown. The city is

        anticipated that both developments would have their own private roads and campus

There are many pros and cons associated with this proposed annexation. The amount of
proposed acres for annexation is staggering. One can make the argument that these
developments put the land in this area to a higher and better use than its current use. The
counterargument to this, however, is that the land in its current state promotes a rural
atmosphere which is consistent with the city’s general plan. The Council will need to consider
which may be more beneficial to the city. The following are the key policy questions for the
Council to consider:
    Will the Agri-Park promote more agricultural use of land in the area? Would this
       preserve the agricultural properties that are valued in the west fields?
    Will the tax and utility revenue benefits that will accrue to the city and other taxing
       entities be worth the development impacts in this area?
    Is the job creation and tax base increases anticipated by the developments worth the
       extensions of city utility infrastructure?
    Will these developments alter the rural character of the region?

After taking these considerations into account the staff recommends allowing these areas to
move to a Level 1 in the annexation area map. Although there are potential negative impacts,
the city stands to gain a significant source of tax and utility revenue. The data center
developments, while impacting a large portion of land to the west, would have a minor impact
in government services and population while providing an inordinate tax and utility revenue
impact. In addition, the Agri-Park concept offers one of the first, market-based solutions for
increasing the value of agricultural land in the area. This could work toward preserving the
agricultural west fields without government investment. The Agri-Park has the potential to
impact the region’s taxing entities by bringing many jobs to the area. Some of these jobs may
be taken by individuals who are currently commuting out of the area for their
employment. Others will be filled with individuals outside of the area who may decide to move
here locally.

On balance, staff has determined that the benefits have the potential to outweigh the
costs. For this reason, staff is recommending that the proposed annexations be allowed to
proceed through the annexation area process.
