PUBLIC COMMENT PERIOD
David Pugh stated that Salt Lake City uses a three-can system that includes a brown bin for yard
waste and organic material. David expressed interest in West Valley City considering a similar
program. He stated he would prefer to see leaves and other green waste composted rather than sent
to the landfill and encouraged the City to explore options that would support composting and
environmental sustainability.

Rich Beaudoin expressed concern about pedestrian safety along 6400 West, citing open irrigation
ditches between approximately 3100 South and 4100 South. He noted that children, including
students walking to and from Hunter Junior High School, often walk close to the roadway due to
the lack of sidewalks and the presence of open ditches. He suggested the City consider replacing the
open ditches with piped drainage and adding sidewalks to improve safety and bring the corridor in
line with other improved roadways in the City. He also asked that Newton Farm Park remain a
priority project, noting the community has been waiting several years for its development.
Additionally, Beaudoin shared his views on immigration enforcement and recommended the City
reevaluate the use of local resources in that context.

Tim Flink expressed appreciation for the Council’s work and the complexity of issues addressed
during study meetings. He commented on the proposed golf course fee schedule, stating that while
most rate increases appear reasonable and in line with other courses statewide, he is concerned about
the removal of walking-rate options for seniors, military members, and player pass holders. He noted
that without a walking option, some users would face significantly higher costs, citing an example
of a substantial increase for weekday, non-peak 18-hole play. Tim requested that the City consider
reinstating a discounted walking rate or otherwise adjusting the pricing structure so these groups are
not required to pay cart-inclusive rates. He emphasized the importance of maintaining affordable
walking options, particularly for older golfers.

Geovanni Salazar spoke on behalf of Latino community members and others who feel vulnerable.

He emphasized that they are residents, neighbors, and members of the community. He urged the
Council to reject any future agreements with ICE or Border Patrol and to prioritize the well-being
of local residents. He also encouraged elected officials to clearly communicate their positions on
these issues, stating that the community will remember their actions and leadership.

Ryan Mahoney urged the Council to begin selling City-owned commercial properties back to the
private sector. He stated that while the original intent may have been positive, continued public
ownership acts as a bottleneck to economic activity and reduces potential tax revenue. He cited
restoring the tax base, improving efficiency and maintenance, and encouraging marketing and
growth as key reasons for divestment, and emphasized that the City’s role should be governance
rather than property management.
Ryan also spoke in opposition to application GPZ-9-2025, expressing concern that rezoning the 5.7-
acre site to General Commercial would eliminate potential future single-family housing and
agricultural use. He stated the site could instead accommodate approximately 15–25 homes and
urged the Council to deny the rezone request.

Cody Boden stated he had listened to the earlier study meeting discussion regarding private roads
and neighborhood conditions. He expressed concern about a neighboring property that he said has
accumulated large amounts of debris and materials, making the yard resemble a junkyard. He noted
that the condition of the property has limited parking and obstructed access to the home’s sidewalk
and entry. Cody referenced the City’s efforts to maintain welcoming and well-kept community areas
and asked for assistance through City code enforcement to help address the situation and improve
neighborhood conditions.

Mike spoke about the importance of improving the City’s image, stating it was identified as a top
community priority. He encouraged continued focus on that goal. He also referenced a recent study
meeting discussion about potential commercial development and comments questioning the need
for additional coffee shops. Mike expressed his personal support for coffee businesses and suggested
that consumer demand should guide what types of establishments open in the community. He
contrasted coffee shops with sugary beverage businesses, noting personal health preferences, and
emphasized that residents should have choices that reflect their interests.

A. ORDINANCE 26-01: ADOPT AN AMENDMENT TO THE FAIRBOURNE
   COMMUNITY REINVESTMENT AREA PROJECT AREA PLAN TO MAKE AN
   ADJUSTMENT REQUESTED BY SALT LAKE COUNTY
   Mayor Lang presented proposed ordinance 26-01 that would adopt an Amendment to the
   Fairbourne Community Reinvestment Area Project Area Plan to Make an Adjustment Requested
   by Salt Lake County
      MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING – JANUARY 27, 2026
                              -4-



   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as follows:

       This ordinance allows for the addition of certain property that is going to be vacated by the
       Wasatch West Valley Retail Subdivision into the Community Reinvestment Area. Salt Lake
       County has requested that said vacated property be added to the Project Area to avoid
       inconsistent property boundaries.

       The Wasatch West Valley Retail Subdivision was approved by the City Council on October
       28, 2025. As part of the subdivision plat, a small portion of 2700 West will be vacated.
       While preparing the plat for recording, the SL County Assessor’s Office acknowledged that
       the vacated portion would become part of lot 2 in said Subdivision. The County then advised
       staff that this small vacated right-of-way would need to become part of the Fairbourne
       Community Reinvestment Area. After reviewing the recommendation, the RDA Board has
       determined that the amendment of the Fairbourne Community Reinvestment Area promotes
       the public peace, health, safety, and welfare of West Valley City and the community
       surrounding the Project Area. Utah law permits the addition of the property to the project
       area without a public hearing as a minor property adjustment requested by the County
       Assessor.

E. RESOLUTION 26-03: AUTHORIZE THE EXECUTION OF AN INTERLOCAL
   COOPERATION AGREEMENT WITH SALT LAKE COUNTY FOR ACCESS TO
   AERIAL IMAGERY
   Mayor Lang presented proposed resolution 26-03 that would authorize the Execution of an
   Interlocal Cooperation Agreement with Salt Lake County for Access to Aerial Imagery

   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as follows:

      This resolution authorizes the City to enter into an agreement for the purchase and use of
      digital orthorectified aerial photography of West Valley City at 7.5 cm (3 in.) and 22.5 cm
      (9 in.) resolution for all areas within its geographical boundaries. The County grants the City
      a limited license for a web service of the imagery as well as electronic files of orthorectified
      imagery. The cost is $130 per square mile for the orthorectified imagery obtained from the
      County through the Surveyor. The City’s geographical boundary consists of approximately
      35.85 square miles. The total payment for orthorectified imagery is $4,660.50.
      MINUTES OF COUNCIL REGULAR MEETING – JANUARY 27, 2026

       Current, accurate digital orthorectified photography has many uses within the City. The
       photos are used within the majority of City Departments to visualize and identify growth;
       compare urban changes with aerial photography from previous years; as a reference for new
       spatial information; as a background for online map viewers and printed maps; for
       measurements of impervious surfaces; and more. The format is compatible with existing
       GIS applications that are used and developed by City Staff. By partnering with other
       agencies, costs for aerial photography are significantly reduced.


F. RESOLUTION 26-04: AUTHORIZE THE CITY TO ENTER INTO A MEMORANDUM
   OF AGREEMENT WITH THE STATE OF UTAH TO PROVIDE LAW
   ENFORCEMENT SERVICES
   Mayor Lang presented proposed resolution 26-04 that would authorize the City to Enter Into a
   Memorandum of Agreement with the State of Utah to Provide Law Enforcement Services.

   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as follows:

       The Utah Division of Forestry, Fire and State Lands oversees the management and protection
       of state sovereign lands throughout Utah. Due to legislative changes in 2024 (H.B. 469) that
       created the DNR Division of Law Enforcement, FFSL now seeks to contract with local law
       enforcement agencies to provide supplemental patrol services on sovereign lands. These
       services are designed to reduce resource degradation, prevent illegal activities, and ensure
       public safety on state sovereign lands through the deployment of law enforcement officers.

       Resolution NO. 25-109 was approved back in August of this year. Upon further review, the
       Division of Forestry, Fire and State Lands and the Division of Law Enforcement of the
       Department of Natural Resources found a clerical error. The original MOA was approved
       for a 5 year term. FFSL would like to amend the terms from 5 years to 1 year to allow the
       agreement to be revisited annually based on the funds they may or may not receive.

   Mayor Lang discussed proposed Resolution 26-06 that would authorize the City to Enter Into a
   Right of Way Purchase Agreement and Accept a Warranty Deed with and from JSB Apex
   Properties, LLC for Property Located at 4085 South 2200 West

   Written documentation previously provided to the City Council included information as follows:

       The JSB Apex Properties, LLC parcels located at 4085 South 2200 West is one of the
       properties affected by the required removal and replacement of ADA ramps for the 2026
       Asphalt Overlay Improvements Project. As part of this project several residential streets
       will be roto-milled and overlaid. Any existing ADA ramps which do not comply with the
       current ADA standards and regulations must be removed and replaced.

       The Warranty Deed will allow for construction over portions of the properties currently
       owned by the property owner. Compensation for the Warranty Deed in the amount of
       $4,000.00 was based upon an appraisal report prepared by the Fortis Group, LLC.

and she called for a motion.

Councilmember Wood asked for clarification on golf fees and walking rates. John Flores, Assistant
City Manager, explained that the current proposal keeps the standard golf rate the same regardless
of whether a player walks or uses a cart. He indicated that staff could bring back a revised fee
schedule that includes a potential walking-rate adjustment if the Council wishes. This could be
incorporated into the current approval or reviewed and confirmed with Golf and Parks & Recreation
staff and presented as a follow-up revision at a future meeting. After brief discussion, the Council
expressed interest in exploring a discounted walking rate but were comfortable moving forward with
the current fees so passes could go on sale, with the understanding that a possible adjustment could
be considered later.
