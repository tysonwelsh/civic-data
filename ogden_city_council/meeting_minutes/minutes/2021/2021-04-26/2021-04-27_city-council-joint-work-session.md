         The purpose of the joint work session was to review the agendas for the City Council and Redevelopment Agency (RDA)
meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00p.m.; receive information about the Weber State University Net-Zero Home Program; discuss
a proposed honorary street name designation for 2nd Street from Century Drive to Wall A venue; discuss Council/Board business,
including an update on the Community Renewal Energy Act (CREA); and hear Council/Board comments.

Agenda Review
          Chair Blair and members of Council staff reviewed the agendas for the City Council and special Redevelopment Agency
(RDA) meetings scheduled to begin at 6:00 p.m. There was a focus on the agenda item regarding proposed amendments to the
Business Depot Ogden (BDO) and the implications of each of the voting options available to the Council. Community and
Economic Development Deputy Director Cooper also provided data illustrating the success of the Quality Neighborhoods
initiative in its target area and reiterated Administration's request that a portion ofBDO lease revenue funding be allocated to the
renewal of the Initiative; this included testimonials provided by individuals who have received help or support through the Quality
Neighborhoods Initiative. There was then a discussion about the agenda items for the City Council and RDA meetings dealing
with the City's acquisition of the 4-C property; Council Executive Director Eller-Smith reported Mr. Cooper has received
additional environmental study fmdings for the property and she invited him to review those fmdings with the group. Mr. Cooper
presented the fmdings provided by the consultant, who has determined there is an 80- to 100-foot-wide plume of contaminated
ground and remediation can be completed through excavation of the soils, ongoing monitoring, utilization of vapor barriers in any
structures, and controls over access of the groundwater on the property. The City is protected from liability through written
assurance from the Utah Department of Environmental Quality, which essentially indicates the RDA has satisfied all State and
Federal Codes as a bona fide prospective purchaser and would qualify for liability indemnification. Ongoing liability would be
settled among the City and the previous owners of the property.

Weber State University Net-Zero Home Program- Information Item
         In October 2019, the City entered into a memorandum of understanding with Weber State University (WSU) to transfer
property at 2807 Quincy Avenue for the construction and sale of an energy efficient home. The project has been completed and
WSU plans to continue with the program, known as the Net-Zero Home Program. WSU is planning on partnering with Weber

             Community and Economic Development Director Christopulos reported he feels the program was very successful and the

    Proposed Honorary Street Name Designation -2nd Street from Century
    Drive to Wall Avenue
              Council Deputy Director Symes explained Anna Keogh has submitted a petition to assign an honorary street name for a
    portion of 2nd Street between the Union Pacific railroad tracks and Washington Boulevard. The initially proposed honorary street
    name was "Bingham Fort Lane" in honor of Bingham Fort, which was located in that area. The proposal has since been amended
    to include the name of Chief Little Soldier. The new proposal would assign the honorary name as "Bingham Fort-Chief Little
    Soldier Ln." The proposal was reviewed by the Planning Commission and a recommendation of approval for a shorter distance
    was forwarded to the Council with a vote of7-0. As recommended by the Planning Commission, the honorary street name would
    apply to the portion of 2nd Street between Century Drive and Wall A venue, which the Planning Commission found to align with
    the Fort's historic location more closely. Upon initial review by the City Council, the Council requested that the proposal be
    reviewed by the City's Diversity Commission. This review was completed, and the Diversity Commission forwarded a
    recommendation of denial to the Council with a vote of 9-1. The Diversity Commission's recommendation was based on the
    belief that the proposal did not accurately represent and honor the perspectives of all the peoples of the area at the time. At the
    Council's February 2 work session, Council members discussed the proposal and the recommendation from the Diversity
    Commission. Council members expressed opposition to the proposal as presented. The petitioner requested additional time to
    develop an alternative proposal that would help address concerns expressed by the Diversity Commission.
              Ms. Keogh stated she has appreciated being part of this process and she feels the adjusted proposed honorary street
    designation adds dimension to the name; she has examined records detailing the relationship between Indians and early settlers to
    the area and feels that the Bingham F ort-ChiefLittle Soldier Ln street name will highlight that history and relationship. She asked
    for Council support of the designation.
              Council members commented on their support for the honorary street name and commended Ms. Keogh and the
    Diversity Commission for paying such close attention to the history of the area and providing a recommendation that takes that
    history into consideration.

    Council/Board Business
              Community Renewal Energy Act (HB411) Update: Council Executive Director Eller-Smith used the aid of a
    PowerPoint presentation to provide the Council with an update regarding the CREA that was created by House Bill411 (HB411 ).
    A website has been created for the program- www. utah 1OOcommunities.org- and it contains information regarding the efforts of
    the communities that have signed on to participate in the CREA. She provided background information regarding the program and
    the actions the Council has taken to date, after which she identified future decision points for the Council to contemplate,
    including executing an inter local governance agreement as a stakeholder in the program and adopting a City ordinance defming
    the process for customers' formal entrance into the cmmnunity renewable energy program. There are 23 communities that have
    opted into the CREA; these communities have committed to pursue 100 percent renewable energy by the year 203 0. Those that
    sign the governance agreement and make their payments will move forward in the participation process. Each participating
    community will name one primary and one alternate board member. The main board member must be an elected official, the
    alternate can be a staff member. The agreement will become effective once five cmmnunities sign it and an agency will be fanned.
    The agency, in partnership with Rocky Mountain Power (RMP), consultants and regulatory experts, will develop and submit a
    program application to the Public Services Commission (PSC), including draft request for proposals (RFP) language for
    eventually procuring renewable energy. PSC approval will mean the Cmmnunity Renewable Energy Program can launch and the
    agency can procure resources. The estimated start-up costs are $700,000 and these costs carmot be shifted to non-participating
    customers. The costs, which do not include noticing, will be split among the participating communities in two payments, over two
)   years. Each cmmnunity's share is based on their original "participation percentage" weighted 50 percent based on population and
    50 percent based on electric load in 2019. The non-noticing costs estimate for Ogden City is $71,474.53. "Anchor communities"
    commit to paying more to raise the $700,000 and will be credited back as more cmmnunities join, which is considered to be a
    "backstop" mechanism. She presented a possible payment timeline for 2021 and 2022-2023, noting that Council staff feels it is
