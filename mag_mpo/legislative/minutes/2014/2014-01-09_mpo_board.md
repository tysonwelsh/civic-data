Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO) Orientation - Shawn Seager Mountainland Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO) does transportation planning for Utah County. There are four MPOs in Utah, Wasatch Front Regional Council, Cache MPO, Dixie MPO and Mountainland MPO. Regional Planning: is the board for the MPO and consists of one elected official (mayor or council member) from all the municipalities, the three county commissioners and representatives from the Transportation Commission, UTA Board and UDAQ. Regional Planning brings together the Utah County mayors and commissioners, UDOT, UTA, and UDAQ to collectively made responsible transportation decisions. The Regional Planning Committee discusses and supervises all transportation planning and air quality matters for Utah County. The Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) (city planners and engineers), MPO Finance Committee, and Active Transportation Committee (ATC) offers advice and recommendations on transportation issues to Regional Planning. Wasatch Choices 2040: a vision plan for the Wasatch Front. It recommends growth principles, land use and funding information for communities to use in community future planning efforts. Regional Transportation Plan (RTP): the RTP is a guide to maintain and enhance the regional transportation system for urbanized Utah County and is updated every four years. It is a coordinated system of capital-intensive roadway projects, transit improvements, and pedestrian/bicycle facilities needed over the next thirty years. The plan attempts to minimize impacts to society and environment while providing for enough capacity and transportation choices to ensure the region’s economy continues to grow. All regional transportation facilities and improvements needed through 2040 are Mountainland MPO Regional Planning January 9, 2014 Page 4 listed in the Regional Transportation Plan (RTP). The RTP must be fiscally constrained and meet air quality requirements. Transportation Improvement Program (TIP): the TIP lists federally funded transportation projects and programs for the fiscal years 2014-2017, and is updated every four years, projects can so be amended into the TIP. Other regionally significant projects funded with non-federal sources are also listed in the TIP. All TIP projects must be listed in the current Regional Transportation Plan to qualify for funding. The TIP projects are sponsored by municipalities, the county, UDOT, UTA, and others utilizing various federal, state and local funding sources. Air Quality Conformity: MAG is responsible to ensure that the 2040 RTP and TIP for Utah Valley urbanized area conforms to the air quality requirements of the State Implementation Plan (SIP) and the Environmental Protection Agency interim conformity guidelines for all pollutants in non-attainment or maintenance areas Public Participation: seeks and involves the citizens in the Mountainland area with the transportation planning efforts. Executive Council: the Executive Council is the governing board and final policy-making body of the Association. This body approves working budgets and staff policies. It is comprised of mayors, commissioners, and county council members of member jurisdictions in the Association’s boundaries of Summit, Utah, and Wasatch Counties. Local Corridor Preservation Fund Requests: Eagle Mountain and Spanish Fork - Jim Price Corridor preservation funds are available to protect potential highway corridors development and to secure land at significantly reduced costs before development occurs. Staff maintains an inventory of corridor preservation needs and the list is submitted yearly to the county commission for approval. Communities can submit a request for funds and staff then reviews the request to see if property qualifies and if funds are available. This program is ongoing, so when property becomes available and with the willingness of the owner to sell, the city can then apply for funds. Due to limited available funds it is anticipated the majority of corridor preservation efforts will be accomplished through local planning and zoning rather than outright purchase. The application lists the approximate amount funds which will be needed, then an appraisal, the actual market value, will be done after the funding request is approved. Funds not needed for purchase will be returned back to the Corridor Preservation Fund. Any unused property not used for construction will be Mountainland MPO Regional Planning January 9, 2014 Page 5 sold at market value as soon as possible. Whatever is received for the property when sold well be placed into the Corridor Preservation funds. Please refer to the Transportation Corridor Preservation Fund Policies for additional information or contact Jim Price.
