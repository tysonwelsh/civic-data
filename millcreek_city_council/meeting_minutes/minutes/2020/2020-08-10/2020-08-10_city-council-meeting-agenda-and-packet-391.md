   Blaine       Gehring        said Millcreek             had not had a fencing                 ordinance           since its incorporation               and
   residents       were seeking           to install           fences and with          no ordinance            in place,        it had been very
   confusing        for staff in offering                guidelines.         Discussions          on a new fencing                 ordinance       began in
   September            of 2017 with           further         discussions      in 2018 and 2019.                The proposed               ordinance       would
   apply      to all new construction                 of fencing            and reconstruction              of existing          fencing.      Existing      non-
   conforming             fences would           only be subject             to the provisions             of the code if their              location       or

   described        the fence definition                 and showed           examples          of violations           throughout          the City.     He said
   that generally           fences do not require                  a building      permit,        however,            a building      permit       was
   required       by the building              code for any fence over 7 feet high                           or any retaining             wall    over 4 feet
   high.      The Plaru'iing         Commission                recommended             that any fence over 6 feet high                        should      require
   a building       permit.        For single-family,                two-family,          and agricultural             properties,          chain link      or
   open style fencing              no higher         than 6 feet high would                    be allowed           if 50o/o was open or metal
   picket      and 4 feet if chain link                  for front     and corner         side yards.         All      other fencing          installed
   within       the minimum            front     or corner         side yard setbacks              could not exceed                6 feet in height.             The
   height       for interior       side and rear yards may not exceed                             8 feet in height.             Fencing       for multi-
   family,       commercial,           industrial,         and institutional            development            would       not be allowed            in any
   front      yard area except           as provided             in the Manufacturing               Zone        and fencing          may not exceed 8
   feet in height.          On corner          lots, the ordinance              would         require     that no view-obstructing                   fence that
   exceeds        3 feet in height          shall be placed             in what is called               the clear view           area on any corner
   defined       as the triangular             area 40 feet in each direction                     from      the intersecting           curb lines.         Like
   necessary        vision      at a corner,         drivers       need to be able to see or be seen by traffic,                               pedestrians,            or
   bicyclists       approaching           on the street while                backing       out of a driveway.                The ordinance              requires
   that no view-obstructing                    fence over 3 feet high                 shall be placed            at any driveway,                park strip, or
   automobile           accessway         in a defined            clear-view          area.


   Gebring        said fences shall be made of high-quality                               materials          that require          minimal        maintenance.
   Materials        such as brick          or stone, decorative                 precast       concrete,       heavy       gauge vinyl,            composite
   materials,       wood,       stucco,        chain link         up to 4 feet high,            split rails would           be permitted.           Prohibited
   materials       would       include      used metal             roofing      panels,       pallets,     tarps, chip-board              or plywood,
   scrap lumber,           metal     or glass, security              barbed      or razor wire,             and electrified          fencing       unless part
   of a legally         established       use in the A-1 or A-2                  Zones.         Use of retaining            walls      would       be
   encouraged           to reduce       the steepness              of man-made           slopes and provide                landscaping            pockets.        A
   building       permit       along with         drawings           stamped       and signed by a professional                       engineer        would        be
   required       for retaining         walls      over 4 feet in height.                No fences          shall be installed              on a front      yard
   retaining       wall     or within      4 feet parallel            to the top of a retaining                 wall.     The proposed             fencing

Mayor        Silvestrini        said Millcreek            enacted       a property           tax in 2018 when               it left the Salt Lake Valley
Law      Enforcement            Service       Area (SLVLESA)                 taxing          district.    The City          left to have more         control
over the level             of police     services.       That tax was a close as possible                          to the 2017 tax levied             by
 SLVLESA.             He said the County                prepares        the tax rate based on the needed revenue.                             There      was no
tax increase          then, who was collecting                    that tax changed.               He pointed          out that SLVLESA              had raised
taxes every year, and this year was considering                                    a higher        rate than Millcreek.              The Millcreek
property          tax increase        was raising         more money              to pay the Unified               Police     Department          (UPD)
contract      and the City            had only used property                 tax revenue            to pay for police            services.     The UPD
contract      bill had gone up by about $700,000                             for Fiscal         Year 2020-2021,               so to make payment                 the
tax needed to go up accordingly.                          He said Millcreek                had not raised           taxes since incorporation.                  The
proposed          increased      was 2.78%              which     would      be less than 1% per year since incorporation.                                 He
said the UPD            budget        was posted         on the UPD          website,          but he was willing              to talk to anyone            about
that budget.          UPD      provides        police     services       to about 250,000                 residents      of Salt Lake County               in
Millcreek,          Taylorsville,        Midvale,         Holladay,         Magna,           Keams,       White       City,    Immigration         Canyon,
Copperton,           Brighton,         and unincorporated               Salt Lake County                  though      Taylorsville       would      be
leaving      at the end of the fiscal               year. He showed                an organization             chart of the UPD              Millcreek
Precinct      which         included      about 46 officers             that are based in and work                      in Millcreek.         Millcreek          has
available         to it other services            and resources           shared with            other jurisdictions            that are not needed
every day. If Millcreek                  had its own department,                   residents         would     pay for everything              or forego
certain      services       based on affordability.                Some of the pooled                    services     in UPD       include       dispatch,
training,         SWAT,       accident        investigation         unit,    evidence          room,       special     victims     unit, mental          health
