5. Public Hearing, Petition #2020-5, to add definition of licensed family child care and to allow as a permitted use
   in R-2 zones.

   Ms. Jenny Rogers stated she desires to amend the Ordinance to benefit both home day cares and preschools.
   She stated the State identifies residential day care, family child care and preschools, each having specific
   regulations relating to the number of children, size of facilities, outdoor play areas and outside employees.
   This amendment would give the same options within Ogden City, which limits both home occupation child
   care and preschools to eight children. Her desire is to add a definition of family child care and preschool
   which will allow up to 16 children to match the State’s regulation. The State qualifies the number of children
   based on the size of the home, the availability of adult-to-child ratio and outdoor play areas. Ogden City allows
   a home occupation day care or nursery in both the R-1 and R-2 zones, but limits the number of children to
   eight. This Ordinance would add a definition of family child care and allow the use in the R-2 zone only, while
   allowing more children based on the State’s qualifications. She presented additional detail relating to the
   State’s regulations relating to number of care-givers to children ratios as well as training required fr care-
   givers. She stated a qualified care-giver would provide a better service to children than is currently allowed, as
   the Ogden regulations limit care-givers to members of the family living in the home, prohibiting outside
   employees. She felt the amendment would benefit both children and presents, and create additional jobs in
   neighborhoods. She suggested the State regulations could be implemented into the City ordinances in terms
   of number of children, care-givers, number of children based on the size of the child-care area and outdoor
   play space.

   Commissioner Stoker commended Ms. Rogers on the detail presented in her application and presentation.
   Commissioner Sandau suggested the hours of operation might be modified to allow children to be cared for
   earlier in the mornings.

   Ms. Corbridge stated this is a request to amend the R-2 Ordinance to allow family day care to allow more than
   eight children as well as adding a definition for family day care. She reviewed the State standards for both
   residential child care certificates and the licensed family child care, stating the differences include more
   children, require outdoor play areas, and require additional inspections per year. Ogden’s code restricts child
   care to R-1 and R-2 zones as a home occupation with up to eight children, while allowing a day care center in
   R-3, R-4. The applicant is requesting the R-2 zone be modified to allow Licensed Family Child Care and that a



                                                                                                                        7
  definition of Licensed Family Child Care be added to the Ordinance. The applicant claims there is as high
  demand for quality in-hoe day care and preschool.

  She stated in determining whether an ordinance amendment is appropriate, the Commission is to consider
  whether it is consistent with the General Plan and its effect on neighborhoods. She stated the Ordinance for
  day care centers requires one parking space per employee and one space per five children. She suggested
  this is excessive in the R-2 zone and felt in addition to the two side-by-side parking spaces required for a
  single-family home, only one additional space per employee should be required. She commented the General
  Plan encourages a variety of land uses, and residents benefit by being close to public uses such as daycare
  centers. She felt the definition proposed by the applicant is appropriate and suggested it tie to standards
  elsewhere in the Code. Staff recommendation is for approval of ordinance amendments which would add a
  definition of licensed family child care, modifying parking standards to require one space per employee in
  addition to the two on-site spaces required for the dwelling unit, add the use as permitted in the R-2 zone, and
  adding regulations for licensed family child care which would limit hours of operation, require staggered drop-
  off and pick-up times, allow outside care providers and other licensing regulations as also required by the
  State relating to play areas, fencing and size requirements. She presented proposed language which would
  limit hours from 7am until 8pm and recommended approval.

  Commissioner Safsten asked about the practicability of providing additional parking for employees due to
  increased pavement which would be in addition to the driveway and parking for the resident. Ms. Corbridge
  responded all parking would be required to be legally located, and not in the front setback. Commissioner
  Stoker suggested the hours of operation might begin earlier in the day as some seem to want or need child
  care as early as 5:30am in order to get to work on time. Mrs. Rogers stated there appears to be more of a
  nuisance to neighbors during evening hours, but agreed there is often a need for earlier hours. Commissioner
  Safsten stated early morning seems more important as some families have older children who are home after
  school and can help care for younger children. Commissioner Boykin expressed concern that the proposed
  language would be effective in all R-2 zones throughout the City and may or may not be appropriate City-wide.
  Chair Graf expressed he felt the definition is a good fit for the R-2 zone, stating there are limited R-2 zones
  throughout the City. Discussion continued relating to whether it might be better regulated as a conditional use
  in the R-2 zone. Mr. Stratford stated the conditional use standards would not eliminate the use in particular
  areas, but would only allow the Commission to define impacts which might be created by the use and mitigate
  those particular impacts. He stated if earlier times were allowed, standards by which that could be
  determined must be defined in the ordinance. Commissioner Stoker asked if Staff had contacted neighboring
  cities to determine what their regulations are relating to home childcare. Ms. Corbridge responded that she
  had not made such a contact. Commissioner Southwick stated the number of children is limited to 16, and is
  based on the size of the home, and that many Ogden homes in the R-2 would not be able to accommodate
  that many children. He felt the State limitations appear adequate.

  Mr. Montgomery discussed the history of childcare stating standards were established for home occupation
  day care based on the State laws at that time. Since then, many State regulations have changed, while Ogden
  has had only a few changes, typically based on a petition from a provider. The State now recognizes a
  preschool as a home business while the City identifies only home occupations including childcare and day
  care centers.

As there were no additional comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Southwick to close the public
   hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously.

8. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to 15-2-16 to amend definition of open space and changes
   to other chapters where open space wording is used.

  Mr. Montgomery explained this ordinance amendment is a follow-up to the recently adopted apartment
  regulations. He stated the proposed amendment would better define open space for all zones and clarify the
  use of open space throughout the Ordinance It also distinguishes required landscaped setback from required
  open space and better defines yard space, and requires all areas not used for building, parking or access to be
  landscaped in all zones. He stated some sections of the Code identify green space requirements rather than
  open space and these are now to be defined as open space. The regulations would be applicable to PRUD,
  group dwellings mixed use zones and neighborhood commercial zones. He reviewed the details of the
  proposed amendment and recommended approval.
