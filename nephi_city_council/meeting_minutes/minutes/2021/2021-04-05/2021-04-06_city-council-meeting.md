PUBLIC HEARINGS:

HEARING 1 - T-SHEPHERD ANNEXATION:

   Mayor Glade R. Nielson called the hearing to order and stated that the purpose of the hearing was to
receive public comment on the proposed T-Shepherd Annexation of approximately 28.97 acres. The
proposed annexation area is located ½ mile south of Four Mile Rd/State Route 28 intersection. The
property is owned by Nephi Yard/Jason Carlton and Will Jones.
   Steven Jones asked if the property was contiguous to the city boundary. Mayor Nielson answered
that it was an island annexation. Mr. Jones stated that he hoped it was not for the purpose of a housing
development.
   Hearing adjourned at 7:05 p.m.

PUBLIC HEARING 2 – FOUR MILE ANNEXATION:

  Mayor Nielson called the hearing to order and stated that the purpose of the hearing was to receive
public comment on the proposed Four Mile Annexation of approximately 156 acres. The proposed
annexation area is located 1 mile south of Four Mile Rd/State Route 28 Intersection. The property is
owned by Craig Dansie and James D. Phillips.
   Steven Jones commented that this annexation did not seem to be contiguous with the city
boundaries and that this annexation had not been made public. Myrna Trauntvein stated that she had
written several articles about the annexation. Councilor Memmott stated that this annexation was for
industrial purposes.
  Hearing adjourned at 7:08 p.m.

PUBLIC HEARING 3 – RED CLIFFS COMMERCIAL ANNEXATION

   Mayor Nielson called the hearing to order and stated that the purpose of this public hearing was to
receive public comment on the proposed Red Cliffs Commercial Annexation of approximately 57 acres.
The proposed annexation area is located approximately 1300 South Main. The property is owned by
Nephi City.
   Steve Hart questioned whether this annexation area was going to be for residential use.
   Hearing adjourned at 7:10 p.m.

PUBLIC COMMENT:

    Ron Segura stated that he is against the slaughterhouse development being proposed by Corey
Wright. He said he hoped that the city would have enough forethought to look at Provo City when
Kuhnis was located within their city. He stated that there are other locations that could be considered
such as by the sewer ponds or by the dairy. He said that he would be neighbor to the slaughterhouse
and this would impact his quality of living and his wife said that she would move if the slaughterhouse
was located at that spot.
    Brad Vickers stated that he lives in the west fields in Juab County and that he chose to live out of the
city and now the city is moving into his space. He said that he has lived out of the state for the last ten
years and has just recently moved back and he has done a lot of work on his house and he does not
approve of the slaughterhouse being built.
    Merrill Reed stated that he feels the same as the others who have spoken. He said that the west
bench is the logical place to locate the slaughterhouse. He said that he has become a hermit and likes
living out where he does. He has a screened in porch because of the flies and he feels like that the
slaughterhouse development would increase the number of flies around his home.
    Steven Jones stated that the wind blows to the west and that Nephi has promoted itself for years as a
family town with a balance of urban and agriculture west of the city. He said that he sincerely laments
any further annexation west of the railroad tracks. It would behoove the council to preserve the rural
part of our city. His thought was that from the freeway to the railroad tracks should be the residential
area growth. He is concerned about the water supply and that the city will never get water from Central
Utah Water and that our area is currently in a drought. He stated that we do not have enough water to
keep both streams going down Salt Creek. We need to be concerned about having sufficient water. The
aquifer can only handle so much draw.
   Ron Segura stated that he was told by someone from the city that we would need another sewer
lagoon to handle the wastewater from the slaughterhouse.
    Steve Hart stated that he has always been concerned about water. He said he moved here in 1983
and Utah is the second driest state other than Nevada. He knows that Utah County is pushing down to
this area and he has always been concerned about the water supply.
  Councilor Jones stated that he appreciated the input.

AIRPORT LEASES APPROVED:

   Mr. Atkinson reviewed with the council three airport lease agreements with Leon Roullard owner of
Skydive the Wasatch. At a recent work session, Leon Roullard from Skydive the Wasatch presented a
long-term vision for business he would like to conduct at the airport. Skydive the Wasatch has been a
successful operation at the airport for a number of years. As part of the vision shared with the City
Council, new lease agreements needed to be drafted for his existing hangar space, a new hangar space,
and the site of the home site on the south end of the airport property.

  The two hangar leases are based on the city’s sample airport lease agreement. The lease agreement
for the new hangar space will contain language to lease the space as if an 80 X 80 square foot hangar
were in existence. This is due to current interest in that space for a hangar of that size. This will ensure
the airport receives the revenues it needs to meet FAA requirements for leasing at market value. Once a
physical hangar is constructed, the agreement will be adjusted to reflect the actual hangar size. Each
agreement was for a 5-year term.
