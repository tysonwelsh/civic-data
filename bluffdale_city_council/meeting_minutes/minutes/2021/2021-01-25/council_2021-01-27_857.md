7.     Presentation and Discussion on Ranked Choice Voting, Presenter, Stan Lockhart, Utah
       Ranked Choice Voting.

Stan Lockhmt introduced Taylor Morgan as a member of their Utah Ranked Choice Voting team. He
acknowledged Nancy Lord as the Republican Conservative grassroots activist who first brought
Ranked Choice Voting to the State of Utah. Mr. Lockha1t reported that Ranked Choice Voting is
being used regularly in party conventions. The pilot began two years ago with two cities. They expect
several more this year.

Mr. Lockhart provided an overview of how Ranked Choice Voting works. He explained that voters
rank their cho ices similar to how plurality voting is done. Rather than marking a ballot with one dot,
they rank their choices based on their preferences.

In a multi-winner race, wh ich most cities have for Ci ty Council, they would go tlu·ough it once for
one candidate. A voter ranks his choices. The votes are tallied and each candidate gets their number
one votes. If someone rece ives more than 50% of the vote in the first round, they wi n. If they receive
less than 50%, the bottom person is taken off the table, their number two votes are redistributed, and
the process is repeated . It involves a series of instant runoffs until one candidate gets 50% of the
votes and is declared the winner. The process then begins again minus the wi1mer. This process
ensures that the winners are those with the broadest and deepest voter support.

After the election, Co unty C lerk, Amelia Powers in Utah County po lled those who voted in Payson

The advantages of ranked choice voting were identified as fo llows:

    •   It saves money. There is only one election and no primary. The amount charged for the
        primary is equivalent to the general election.
    •   It involves shorter and less expensive city campaigns. Candidates file in late August rather
        than June.
    •   It is better for both candidates and voters. There is less voter fat igue.
    •   It is ready for cities to implement. E&S voting equipment was awarded the State of Utah
        contract. It was noted that Salt Lake County uses Dominion.
    •   Voters are able to express their will with regard to the type of e lected representatives they
        want.
    •   The winner is by a majority of votes, with over 50%.
    •   It eliminates the spoiler effect. Long-shot candidates do not draw votes away from the most
        preferred candidate.

Mr. Lockhart stated that the C ity Council can send a letter to the State E lections Office by April 15 if
they intend to use Ranked Choice Voting.

Jeff Gaston asked if Ranked Choice Voting would apply w hen only two candidates are running. Mr.
Lockhart stated that it would not. It only applies if there are more than two.

Dave Kallas asked if the cities of Payson and Vineyard used it only for the general election and
bypassed the primary. Mr. Lockhart stated that they did. He explained that if there are a lot of
cand idates, the law allows Ranked Choice Voting to be run in the primary and the general election.

Mr. Lockhart reiterated that if the City Counci l chose to do Ranked Choice Voting, they must provide
notice to the Lieutenant Governor' s Office by April 15. If they chose plurality voting, the election
involves a primary in August and a general election in November. With Ranked Choice Voting, there
is one election in November and the filing periods are d ifferent.
