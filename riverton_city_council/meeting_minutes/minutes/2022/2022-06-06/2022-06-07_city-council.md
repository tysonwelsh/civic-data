         Pressurized Irrigation (Secondary Water) Fee and Connection Discussion

Mayor Pro Tem McCay asked Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, to start the
discussion on the pressurized irrigation fee and connection.

Mr. Hicks explained that there had been some misunderstanding about the pressurized
irrigation fee. If residents have access to canal shares and are current, the City will give them a
reduced rate regardless of lot size.

Mr. Hicks said that the City was made aware of this issue when a resident on Lovers Lane
wanted to connect to the secondary water system. Staff looked at this resident’s situation and
determined what it would take to allow them to connect and also examined the cost for this
connection. After determining these things, staff looked to the fee schedule to determine what
the monthly rate would be and that’s when they realized there was a problem with the fee
schedule. The fee schedule provides a fee for properties that are 0.75 to 1.0 acres but does not
specify the fee for properties over 1 acre.

Mr. Hicks said there were 112 properties that fit into the category of lots larger than 1-acre. He
said that 40 of these properties would not see a fee increase due to various assessments and
72 properties could see a monthly fee increase if the fee schedule was adjusted. Mr. Hicks
asked the Council to decide what they would like to do with properties that are greater than one
acre so that they can add an additional line to the fee schedule. Staff has recommended that
residents pay a “per acre” fee by multiplying $40.87 by the number of acres they own. Mr. Hicks
explained the increase in fees for each property if this recommendation was followed.

Mr. Brickey said he was hoping to provide transparency to the community and he wants to be
fair and transparent through the process. He said this fee adjustment could be delayed until
December but he doesn’t think this is neighborly.

Councilmember Wells said that many of his neighbors were not notified and believed their rate
would go up double. He would like notices that give more answers than questions and asked if
there is a legal reason that a small amount of information is included on the notice.

Mr. Hicks said it depends on the type of notice and he thought this notice provided enough
information. Another issue is residents saying they did not get the notice. He knows that
notices were taken to the post office but whether they are delivered is out of his control.

Mr. Brickey said that notice was not required. However, the Mayor said to send notice to any
residents who owned property over one acre.

Councilmember Wells asked additional clarifying questions of staff.

Councilmember Buroker said there were two separate items, the connection fee and the usage
fee. Mr. Hicks said that was correct and if the Council agreed with his interpretation of the
connection fees, then that is already in the fee schedule. However, he didn’t know if there was
a prior agreement that the City committed to connect this resident.

Councilmember Buroker asked if residents that haven’t connected are included in the numbers
presented by Mr. Hicks. Mr. Hicks responded that the numbers he presented are residents that
are already connected to the system.

Councilmember Buroker said that secondary water was initially connected at no cost to the

Public Hearings

         Resolution No. 22-39 – Adopting the 2022-2023 Fiscal Year Municipal Fee
         Schedule

Mr. Hicks explained that 72 notices were sent out last week. He said that residents that are
current with their canal shares need to call utility billing and show proof of these canal shares.
He said this issue arose when a resident came to staff and asked about the connection to the
secondary water system. Staff recognized there was a meter set fee in the fee schedule,
however, there was not a line on the fee schedule for how much to charge a resident who owns
more than one acre.

Mr. Hicks found there were 112 residential properties with more than one acre and were paying
$40.87 per month. 40 of these properties, if the City proposed a fee increase, would see no
increase because they are already assessed the one-half acre rate for residents with canal
shares. 20 are not assessed the $40.87 because they are non-users and are not connected to
the secondary water system. 2 of these properties are for elderly who are under a certain yearly
income figure. 72 properties were mailed a letter and would be affected by a fee increase.

Mayor Pro Tem McCay asked Mr. Hicks to show the spreadsheet of how each of the 72
properties would be affected if the fee was increased. Mr. Hicks shared the spreadsheet.

Mayor Pro Tem McCay called for public comments;

Richard Miller questioned why the City is not informing all residents over an acre even if they
have water shares. He never received a letter and heard about this fee increase from a
neighbor. He asked if this if for raising money or for conservation. He also asked why the City
is not using the meters to charge for water usage.

Ginny Burbank apologized to Claude Wells and said it was not her intention to cause people to
call him. She said the letter was not clear. She understood that the letter went out Thursday
late and most people received it on Saturday. She doesn’t normally check her mail but did
receive it yesterday and went to the neighbors to see if they saw the letter. She said she is on
fixed income and everything is going up in price. She would like an explanation on how 72
homes will make that much of a difference monetarily. She talked to neighbors who had to give
up 6 water shares to get building permits so this increase may affect people in the future.

Gary Curtis asked if they pay a 12-month secondary water fee, why they only get to use the
secondary water for 6 months. He believes the City should only charge for the months they use
the water.

Trina said this would affect her. She was not notified and she did have canal shares but had to

and only 1/5 of her property is watered. She doesn’t run her water feature in front of her yard
because she is trying to conserve water. She doesn’t believe this fee increase is right.

Christy Butterfield commented that she was not notified and has an acre and a half of land. She
does not use secondary water to water her pastures. She feels this is discriminatory against the

Mayor Pro Tem McCay explained that the public hearing had been closed for the municipal fee
schedule and she would open this up for discussion among the Councilmembers.

Councilmember McDougal said there was a concern about the mailers getting to residents. He
said they will be looking at this issue to see what happened. The City can only take
responsibility for the notice getting to the post office.

Councilmember McDougal said that one question residents had asked was why they are paying
every month for secondary water when it is only used for 6 months out of the year. He explained
that the secondary water cost is spread out over 12 months. The City is only charging residents
what it costs to run the secondary water system. He said that residents could have six months
of no charge and then in May they would get hit for $150 per month. The decision was made by
Councils past that they would equalize this payment.

Councilmember McDougal said the Council agrees that the most equitable way to charge for
secondary water is by metering which is why they are installing meters throughout the City. In
the past, councils have decided that fees would be assessed based on lot size. It was not
feasibly financially to go around annually to each property and assess how much water they are
using. Until the City can get the meters up and running, this system works the best right now.
He believes that the Council has to be fair and equitable across the City and doesn’t believe that
one group should benefit over another group. For the last 10-15 years, residents over one acre
have benefitted because they have not had to pay more for their lot size. When everyone pays
Page | 9 Minutes – City Council Meeting

their secondary water bill, this goes to maintenance and buying water shares. This mistake
slipped through the cracks years ago and now the result is a fee increase.

Councilmember Wells said there are 32 pump houses in the City and there are valves and other
parts that have not been fixed because there is not money budgeted for this. The maintenance
never stops for secondary water and especially in the winter months. One of his biggest
concerns is that residents were not notified about the fee increase. He does not want to do
anything until the City addresses this issue and he thinks there are other issues to address
before there is a vote.

Councilmember Buroker said raising this fee was not to raise money or conserve water. This is
really about solving a hole in the fee schedule. She believes the tough question is deciding
whether to make it more equitable now, knowing that they will look at this again down the line.

Mayor Pro Tem McCay said the City can only charge for water by lot size because they don’t
have metering throughout the City. She agrees with Councilmember McDougal that increasing
the fees is about fairness throughout the City.

Mr. Hicks asked the Council to let staff know what to charge properties that are over one acre.
In response, Councilmember Wells said that the City’s ultimate goal is to get the money to
install secondary meters so that the City can determine actual usage. He did not want to
change the fee schedule at this time.

Councilmember McDougal asked when secondary meter installation would be complete. Mr.
Brickey said it would be two years until all parts of the City have secondary meters.
Councilmember McDougal said that he would like to adjust the fee schedule now so that it is fair
to all residents.

Councilmember Buroker asked if there could be compromise and residents could be charged
$40.87 for the first acre and then a lesser amount for the remaining acreage. In response,
Mayor Pro Tem McCay said she agrees with this idea and would like to make a smaller
adjustment now. She suggested $40.87 for the first acre and then $20 per acre for the
remaining acreage. Mr. Hicks clarified with the Council that someone with 1.01 acres would
have the base rate of $40.87 and then multiply 0.01 by $20 for a total of $43.57.
Councilmember McDougal, Buroker and McCay all agreed with this idea.

Councilmember Buroker MOVED that the Riverton City Council ADOPT Resolution No. 22-
39 - Adopting the 2022-2023 Fiscal Year Municipal Fee Schedule and add line item 373 to
the fee schedule to divide residents who have a lot size over 1 acre into increments
allowing for an additional $20 increase for each additional acre greater than one acre
proportionate to the acre. Councilmember McDougal SECONDED the motion. Mayor Pro
Tem McCay called for discussion on the motion. There being none, she called for a roll-call
vote. The vote was as follows: Buroker-yes, McCay-yes, McDougal-yes, and Wells-no. The
