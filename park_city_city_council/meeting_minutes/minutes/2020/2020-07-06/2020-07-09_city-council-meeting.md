WORK SESSION

Park City Arts and Culture District Project Update:
David Everitt, Deputy City Manager, and Steve Swisher, GTS, the owner's
representative for the Arts and Culture District project, presented this item. Everitt
displayed the proposed design of the buildings. He reviewed the history leading up to
this project. Upon project approval, master planning began and continued throughout
2018-2019. Many things were desired for this district, including opportunities for cultural
activities, a vibrant pedestrian experience, a nice entry, a community living room,
natural beauty of the surrounding mountains, and a place to live, work, create and play.
The design would connect the district to other districts, be sustainable, and would
accommodate public programs. He explained the different buildings and indicated there
would be a food hall, a media arts building, a transit center, entrepreneurial spaces, and
affordable housing.

Council Member Worel asked what role the design committee had in the creative
process. Everitt indicated the committee was made up of experts in restaurant, children,

music, etc., and they played a part in determining what was going to happen on the site,
how it would all fit together, and who would use it. Swisher stated a youth center was
added to the plans, which created quite a bit of enthusiasm in the group.

Council Member Henney asked about the part the Arts and Culture District would play in
connectivity and why the bridge over Bonanza Drive was eliminated from the plan.
Everitt stated there were several commercial areas around town and the committee
tried to think of how the Arts and Culture District could connect to the other areas. He
noted the Park Avenue/Homestake Road transit stop was the most utilitized stop in the
region. Pedestrian paths from Park Avenue through Homestake to the District had been
planned. UDOT would be making improvements to SR 248 and the intersection at
Bonanza would be restriped and improved. The bridge concept was still being
considered. Council Member Henney stated another busy transit stop was in
Prospector, and asked if that stop could be evaluated as well. Everitt stated the process
would evolve, but there was thought to putting a connector that would include the two
major stops.

Everitt discussed funding for the project. The primary funding would be the 1%
Transient Room Tax (TRT), and other funding included the future proceeds from the
sale of property as well as funding from Transit, Affordable Housing, Walkability, a new
Housing Authority rent revenue bond, and Capital Improvement funds. The City
program space cost estimate was $38.1 million, Affordable Housing $18.4 million, and
Transit $8.7 million. He indicated most of the project cost would be in 2022 and 2023.

Everitt discussed the opportunities that the Arts and Culture District would bring to the
community, including regional transit connections, active transportation between Iron
Horse, Bonanza Park and Prospector area, the potential to install underground power
lines, etc. He noted in the next year, staff would submit the Master Planned
Development (MPD) application, award the contract for design services and demolition
services, submit an RFP for a construction management firm, and conduct outreach. He
stated it was a good time to develop with low finance rates and potentially lower
construction costs.

Council Member Joyce stated there were many things in the project design that Council
hadn't discussed, such as affordable housing and constructing a new transit center. He
hoped to have a discussion with Council to firm up plans and funding before the
demolition began. He suggested a phased building approach while TRT funds built up.

Council Member Henney reiterated there were many pedestrians in these areas today.
The idea was to attract them to the Arts and Culture District, but the issue was having a
connection over or under Bonanza. He wanted a way for pedestrians to connect to the
district. Everitt agreed and noted this was a challenge with the project. Mayor Beerman
also felt the walkability was very important. Council Member Worel asked about a

Council Questions and Comments:

aware of community values. She also appreciated the feedback from the Main Street

     Good Cause
•   Staff finds Good Cause for this Subdivision as it is consistent with the density,
    location and uses identified in the Deer Valley Master Planned Development where
    it is assigned 15 units of density.
•   The project is in keeping with the goals of the General Plan for the Upper Deer Valley
    neighborhood to create a limited number of single family homes supporting this
    resort neighborhood. Development clusters exist within pods and each pod is
    surrounded by recreation open space.
•   An extensive trail system provides for hiking and biking in the summer and connects
    Deer Valley to the Main Street Area. This project will contribute to the trail system
    and further connect the neighborhood to the Main Street core.
•   This project will create an on call van service program that will support carpooling to
    the ski slopes and the Main Street area, particularly during peak periods.
