Recognizing Jannette Borklund for more than forty years of dedicated
service in the Ogden City Planning Division
          A memo from Council staff regarding recognition of Jannette Borklund for more than forty years of dedicated service in
the Ogden City Planning Division came before the Council for consideration. Council member Hyer read the certificate of
recognition issued by the Ogden City Council and Mayor Nadolski and presented Ms. Borklund with the certificate.
          Council members shared their own personal experiences with Ms. Borklund, thanked her, and congratulated her on her
retirement.
          Ms. Borklund thanked the Mayor and Council for recognizing her; she has made many great friends and memories during
her time with Ogden City and is grateful for the opportunities that were afforded to her. The City has changed a great deal during
her career, and she has served with many different employees and elected officials; it has been a great experience, but she is glad
to be retired.
          Mayor Nadolski stated Ms. Borklund has actually worked with seven different Mayors and this reinforced for him that
the people who are doing the actual day to day work of the City are the employees that have dedicated years of their careers to the
betterment of Ogden City. Policies and politics change, but City employees do not, and he thanked Ms. Borklund for her service.

Public Comments
          Heath Satow referred to the work session this evening; he was interested by the discussion of the review of the
Prosecutor’s Office, which was requested last April. The review was valuable, and he suggested that a similar process be
employed in other departments of the City.
          Teresa Bramwell wished everyone a happy Independece Day, which is about taxation with out representation. She
acknowledged that Ogden City elected officials are representing her and she is grateful for their willingness to do so. She listened
to tonight’s work session as well and she heard of a large increase in pay for public safety employees and most general employees
that work for Ogden City. This causes her to believe there is a very big disconnect in Ogden City in terms of the services that are
being provided and the people that are receiving those services. These are the taxpayers. The same people who were criminals ten
years ago are still criminals today. The same people who were taxpayers ten years ago are still taxpayers today. Those groups of
people have not changed. The Police Department and Prosecutor’s Office are charged with dealing with criminals, but there is talk
of making these areas of the City self-sufficient. However, it is important to understand that the taxpayers will pay the taxes that
will fund those areas of the City. The City is not learning from its past and functioning any better. Women are still victims in
Ogden City and the ‘needle needs to move’.
          Tara Oakey stated she lives on the portion of Taylor Avenue that connects Weber State University (WSU) to the Dee
Events Center and her neighborhood has proposed a date for shutting down that section of the road to prevent traffic using that
section of the road to get to WSU. They love and support WSU, but the traffic is causing safety concerns for the people who live
there. She cited things that have been done on the street in an attempt to slow or reduce traffic, but none have worked. The
neighborhood suggests installing a gate at the end of the street that can be opened when needed; something similar has been done
on Birch Avenue and the same options should be made available to her neighborhood.
          Jake Oakey stated that over the past several months there has been construction on Country Hills Drive, and it has created
a significant inconvenience for his neighborhood. He and many others have had to find other routes to get in and out of the area
and he has noticed police vehicles parked at the old Jakes Over The Top restaurant in an effort to catch people that are driving past
the ‘road closed’ sign and cite them. He stated he appreciates their efforts to enforce the law, but he suggested that they park in
another area to remind people not to go through the construction area or even to help guide traffic. There is plenty of space for two
lanes of traffic.
          Acting Chair White referenced Mr. Satow’s comments and stated it is her understanding that there will be a large-scale
study similar to what was performed in the Prosecutor’s Office. She also referenced Ms. Bramwell’s comments and noted that the
City has attempted to determine whether women are treated fairly and equitably in terms of compensation as a City employee.

Mayor Comments
          Mayor Nadolski stated that he supports the study suggested by Mr. Satow as well. He stated that it is hard to be proactive
when he is always being reactive, but he is anxious to proceed with those important studies. He stated that Ms. Bramwell cited a
large increase in pay for public safety employees, but noted it is important to understand that the reason there is such a large
increase is because there has been a significant disparity in the wages paid to Ogden City employees when compared to other
entities. He is happy that the Council is supportive of increasing pay to the average compensation paid. He also thanked Mr. and
Mrs. Oakey for their comments and noted that Administration is working to address the matter that has been brought to his
attention. He cannot speak to what has happened in the past relative to the neighborhood they live in, but he is trying to be helpful
to address concerns that are raised.

Council member Comments
          Council member Blair thanked the Oakeys for the information they provided tonight, which is in addition to comments
that have been made by himself and other residents of that neighborhood over the past several weeks. He thanked Mayor Nadolski
for his willingness to examine that issue and try to find solutions.
          Acting Chair White stated City budgets are very complicated and City Administration has been a great partner to the
Council this year as they have worked together to address budget issues. Council member Blair echoed Acting Chair White’s
gratitude and asked the Mayor to thank department directors for their willingness to answer tough questions asked by the Council.
Council member Myers agreed; he has learned a great deal during the months-long budget process, and he has been impressed by
the civility of City employees as they have met with the Council to discuss compensation needs for various departments. This is
an exceptional time in Ogden City, and he is grateful for the effort that has gone into the budget process for Fiscal Year 2024-
2025.
