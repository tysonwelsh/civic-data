         The Point Update

Alan Matheson, Executive Director of the Point provided an update on the development project.
This presentation is included in the packet.

Mayor Staggs asked what the cost was to relocate the prison. In response, Mr. Matheson said
the cost of relocating the prison was estimated to be around $800-900 million, and the funding
for the relocation is expected to be covered by revenues generated at the Point of the Mountain
site. The model for the disposition of property at the site involves long-term ground leases,
typically lasting 99 years. Developers will lease parcels, build on them, and generate revenue
from the leases. This approach allows the state to maintain control over the development,
ensures a long-term revenue stream, and provides the opportunity for taxpayers to share in the
increased value of the property over time.


         Annual Financial Report

Kevin Hicks, Administrative Services Director, expressed gratitude to Meghin Costa and Jinny
Proctor, along with other department heads, for their dedicated efforts in completing the audit.
Despite challenges with new software, the team successfully compiled the audit report.

Jeff Miles, HBME, presented the audit report to the Council. The firm issued a clean opinion on
Riverton City's financial statements, confirming their accuracy and compliance with accounting
principles. The audit committee met a week prior to discuss details. Notably, the City maintains
a strong financial position, with the general fund's unassigned fund balance at the permitted
35% threshold. No state compliance issues were identified, and the federal single audit is
underway, with no anticipated findings.

Discussion/Action Items

         Utah Legislative Auditor General’s Report No. 2023-16: “A Performance Audit of
         Utah Housing Policy”

Mayor Staggs criticized the Legislative Auditor's report for undermining local land-use authority
and asked the Council to discuss. He raised concerns over the legislature's inclination to exert
control over municipalities' land-use decisions. He emphasized the significant progress made by
local governments, citing 24 legislative efforts in the past years impacting land-use authority. He
expressed the intent to draft a resolution outlining concerns and potential rebuttals.

Councilmember McCay expressed support for drafting a resolution in response to the
Legislative Auditor's report. She suggested sending the resolution to legislative members,
including senators and representatives in Salt Lake County and northern Utah County. She
emphasized the positive role of municipalities in providing zoning for housing but noted
challenges such as interest rates, land prices, and construction material costs beyond their
control.

Councilmember McDougal also expressed support for drafting a resolution that emphasizes the
negative impact of increased government involvement when decisions are distant from citizens.
The example of the city's transportation system was cited as an illustration. He suggested that a
collaborative effort involving other cities would be most effective.

         Resident Survey Discussion

Josh Lee, Communications Director, said the City is preparing for another citizen survey, similar
to the one conducted in December 2021, to gather feedback to inform the Council during the
upcoming strategic planning retreat in January. The survey will be distributed through various
channels, including social media, email, and postcards to ensure broad participation. The
questions in the survey cover aspects such as general satisfaction with the quality of life in
Riverton, perceptions of government services, safety in neighborhoods, and preferences for
various commercial businesses or uses. Mr. Lee asked the Council to provide input on the
survey questions.

Councilmember McDougal raised concerns about the timing of the upcoming citizen survey,
scheduled to start next Monday. He inquired if the last survey had also been conducted in
December. Mr. Lee confirmed that the previous survey had indeed taken place in December.

Councilmember McDougal highlighted the importance of communicating the associated cost
when asking residents about their preferences for new services. He emphasized the potential
discrepancy between a positive response to a service and a different response when a
monetary value is assigned to it.

Councilmember Buroker suggested adding adjectives to clarify the meaning of "quality of life"
that residents could use to express their perceptions, such as safety, quietness, and various
recreational opportunities. She recommended incorporating an educational component to the
question about utility billing. This would involve providing residents with comparative data on
utility fees in Riverton and other cities, fostering a better understanding of the reasonableness of
these fees. Lastly, she proposed that the question on revitalization be framed as a multiple-

and having walkers create a barrier during parades. She expressed support for implementing
these safety measures in Riverton to prevent potential accidents.

Public Hearings

         Ordinance No. 23-16 – Adopting Section 6.05.425, Feeding Wild Birds/Fowl,
         regulating the feeding of wild birds/fowl within Riverton City, Ordinance proposed
         by Riverton City

Jason Lethbridge, Development Services Director, explained that the ordinance aims to address
concerns about birds congregating and creating nuisances. The proposed regulation limits the
feeding of wild birds to containers on private property. In the front yard, only suspended
containers are allowed, placed at least five feet off the ground. In the rear yard, ground-level
containers are permitted, but they must be at least 10 feet away from the property line. The
ordinance does not affect feeding ducks or birds along waterways or in parks. Residential
chickens and other farm animals allowed under existing ordinances are also unaffected.

Councilmember McDougal expressed concern about allowing the feeding of ducks or birds in
public parks or along waterways. His argument was that altering the feeding patterns of wild
animals in these public spaces could lead to waste issues, negatively impacting the enjoyment
of these areas for all residents.

Councilmember McDougal asked about the enforcement aspect of the proposed ordinance,
expressing concerns about how it would be implemented. In response, Mr. Lethbridge said the
code enforcement officer or animal control would investigate reported violations and issue
warnings to educate residents about the ordinance. If voluntary compliance was not achieved,
citations could be issued, and legal action might be pursued.

Mayor Staggs asked if wild fowl was included in any previous ordinances. Mr. Lethbridge said it
was not and this would be a new section in the code.

Councilmember Buroker expressed concerns about the proposed ordinance restricting feeding
of ducks and other fowl in public areas, particularly near the river. She suggested eliminating or
rephrasing the last sentence of the ordinance to focus on private areas and exclude public
spaces while maintaining the provision for walkways near the river.

Mayor Staggs called for public comments;

Sharla Lenare expressed frustration over the delay in addressing the issue of feeding ducks and
wild birds in the area, noting that the Salt Lake County Health Department regulations already
covered many aspects of the problem. She emphasized the harmful effects of feeding wild
animals, including diseases, overcrowding, and aggressive behavior. She questioned why the
City was still working on enforcement measures three years later when the health department
regulations were already in place. She suggested comparing the City's existing regulations with
the county ordinance to ensure comprehensive coverage.

Richard Ludlow expressed concerns about the issues caused by ducks in the neighborhood,
emphasizing the safety risks associated with ducks running into the road and the impact on
wildlife behavior due to feeding. He highlighted instances of ducks being hit by cars, creating a
safety hazard. He explained the natural migratory patterns of wild ducks and the negative
consequences of feeding them, leading to the birds becoming domesticated and staying in the
area. He appreciated the Council's attention to the matter and Councilmember McDougal's
efforts in addressing the issue.

Kurk Weidauer provided additional insights into the challenges posed by the ducks in the
neighborhood. Describing a three-year-long issue, he emphasized the continuous pattern of
ducks flying over their houses, causing damage to properties, including homes, cars, and
outdoor furniture. He expressed gratitude for the Council's attention to the matter and hoped for
enforcement measures to alleviate the ongoing problems caused by the ducks.

Mayor Staggs called for additional public comment, there being none, he closed the Public
Hearing.

Mayor Staggs invited staff to provide comments, especially regarding concerns raised by the
public about Salt Lake County Health Department's regulations.

Ryan Carter, City Attorney, clarified that the motivation behind creating the ordinance was the
county's perceived failure to follow through with the enforcement of their own ordinance. He
emphasized that, until this point, the City lacked an ordinance that would empower Riverton City
to take enforcement actions. This gap in enforcement authority prompted the City to establish its
own ordinance to address the issue effectively.

Councilmember McDougal MOVED that the City Council adopt Ordinance No. 23-16 –
Adopting Section 6.05.425, Feeding Wild Birds/Fowl, regulating the feeding of wild
birds/fowl within Riverton City and removing the wording referring to waterways or
public parks. He said the rationale behind this suggestion is to prevent public spaces from
being affected by individuals feeding wildlife, ensuring that all residents can enjoy these areas
without interference.

Councilmember Buroker was hesitant to support the motion because families go down to the
river to feed the ducks and she did not feel comfortable making this an illegal behavior.
