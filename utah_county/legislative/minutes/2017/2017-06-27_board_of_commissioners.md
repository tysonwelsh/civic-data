3.     APPROVE THE RECOMMENDED LIST OF PROJECTS PROPOSED BY THE MOUNTAINLAND
       ASSOCIATION OF GOVERNMENTS REGIONAL PLANNING COMMITTEE TO USE THE
       COUNTY TRANSPORTATION SALES TAX FUNDS, PROJECTED FOR THE 2019 & 2020 YEARS
(Track A, 06:20)
Utah County Public Works Director Richard Nielson reviewed the list for projects funded by county sales tax
dollars. Each project is submitted to the Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG) Technical Committee,
then to Regional Planning as recommended. Regional Planning makes decisions for MAG-funded and state-funded
projects, and also recommendations to the Utah County Commission for county-funded projects; the Commission
determines the outcome on those projects. Mr. Nielson explained additional funding became available in February
2017, and the Board is being asked to consider two new projects added to the list: Elk Ridge Drive widening, and
Lakeview Parkway Phase 3.

Commissioner Graves talked about an anticipated budget shortfall in 2021 and 2022, and asked for the reasoning of
listing Elk Ridge Drive as a priority over Provo Lakeview Parkway. Utah County Public Works Director Richard
Nielson explained the ranking is determined by the Technical Committee based on technical merit and the need for
individual roadways. Commissioner Graves stated he has a problem with that process, mentioning he knows what
Provo City considers priorities because his father is the Provo City Engineer, and the committee’s choices do not
always reflect the requests from the cities. Shawn Eliot of the Mountainland Association of Governments clarified
the list presented today is the same list which was discussed by the Board two years ago. Mr. Eliot and
Commissioner Graves discussed an incoming high school and associated traffic risks, and costs versus needs.

(Track A, 15:40)
Commissioner Lee commented on the Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) modification revenue update.
There is a sentence which refers to the possibility of using a fund balance for the BRT (Bus Rapid Transit) ‘No-
Fare’ program. Commissioner Lee noted his adamant objection at prior meetings regarding a free-fare zone for
BRT – the model has been advertised as a guaranteed success, yet there are proposals to use taxpayer dollars to
supplement operation costs to “entice” riders to use the system. Commissioner Lee stated it is ridiculous and
unbelievable that an extra four- to five-million-dollar request above the budget is being considered for the project,
and he does not want the impression given that Utah County Commission approves of $1,100,000 of their funds
being used for that purpose. Commissioner Graves added he is offended to see a list which allows the Utah
Department of Transportation (UDOT) and the Utah Transit Authority (UTA) to keep asking for funding from local
budgets while mayors repeatedly try to address their shortages. UDOT already gets the majority of state road
dollars, while UTA does not provide adequate services to the cities. Commissioner Lee and Shawn Eliot discussed
the allocation of funds.

1.     DISCUSSION REGARDING BOOKMOBILE SERVICES
(Track A, 1:19:10)
Britton Lund, Bookmobile Program Manager for the Utah State Library, provided the commissioners with
documents detailing circulation and services provided in Utah County (copies of which can be found attached to
these minutes). Ms. Lund noted some stops have been replaced or entirely discontinued since the last report
provided in May 2016, and reviewed current inventory, circulation, and updates to the Integrated Library System
(ILS). She talked about benefits to the communities, budgets, route changes, and the rapid increase in popularity for
audio and electronic books.
UTAH COUNTY BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS
MINUTES – June 27, 2017
Page 10

(Track A, 1:28:40)
Britton Lund noted Vineyard has requested a stop in their town, and received their first visit from the Bookmobile
this last week. When Ms. Lund mentioned a second stop has been added to Lindon City, Commissioner Graves
asked why the commissioners do not make the decisions on where the Bookmobile goes in the county. Ms. Lund
replied the Board can call or email her at any time to request changes to the schedule, and Commissioner Graves
stated he does not want the Bookmobile to stop in Lindon City because they do not pay for those services.
Commissioner Lee added the commissioners are the government entity for residents in the unincorporated county;
based on services to that population, the county should only be paying $15,000 of the $100,000 contract with the
state. Ms. Lund explained the state will only contract with counties, and the counties choose how to budget for that
expense. Utah County has chosen to bill the individual cities and towns, whereas most counties pay the full amount,
and one or two counties share responsibilities with the school districts. Ms. Lund and the commissioners discussed
‘in-kind’ contributions and other methods to offset costs.

(Track A, 1:36:20)
Commissioner Graves asked what would happen if the Board cut their payment to the state in half, providing only
$50,000 for next year. Britton Lund responded services would decrease dramatically, and Commissioner Graves
noted the county can then request the Bookmobile only stop at locations that have paid for service. Ms. Lund
