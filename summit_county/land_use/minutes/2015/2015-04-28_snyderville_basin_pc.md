3. Public hearing and possible action regarding a Low Impact Permit for a Trailhead, 
Minor (10 parking spaces), an information kiosk, and two restrooms located east of 
the LDS Church off of Silver Summit Parkway – Amir Caus, County Planner 
 
County Planner Amir Caus presented the staff report and explained that the applicant has 
requested that this .3-acre site be approved as a trailhead for 10 parking spaces, which is a 
minor trailhead requiring a Low Impact Permit (LIP).  The applicants wished to have this 
item brought to the Planning Commission and to issue a 1000-foot public notice for the 
sake of transparency.  He explained that the applicant originally applied for a Conditional 
Use Permit (CUP) with about 40 parking spaces, but after some redesign and discussion 
with Utah Open Lands, the project was scaled down to 10 parking spaces to see how 
popular the trailhead becomes.  They could come back later to enlarge it, if necessary, 
which would require a CUP approval.  He explained that the 400-foot elk corridor will 
not be touched, and an additional berm will be placed.  The barren area around the site 
will be reseeded and vegetated with an appropriate seed mix.  In addition to the parking, 
there will be an information kiosk, two restrooms, one of which will be ADA compliant, 
an ADA-compliant parking space, and improvement of a connection trail between the 
LDS Church and the proposed trailhead.  This trailhead would serve as overflow for the 
existing trailhead down the road and could potentially become a primary trailhead if it is 
shown to be popular. 
 
Commissioner Peck asked who would be in charge of the restrooms.  Planner Caus 
replied that would be done on an as-needed basis by the Snyderville Basin Special 
Recreation District.  Heinrich Deters, representing Park City Municipal Corporation, the 
applicant, reported that the Recreation District provides maintenance once a week at the 
Highland Trailhead, and the City will take control of that once they get the approvals and 
set up a weekly maintenance schedule or as needed.  Commissioner Peck asked where 
the equipment would be stored.  Mr. Deters replied that it would be housed at the 
Trailside maintenance facility.

Public hearing and possible action regarding a Conditional Use Permit for an 
addition to an existing school; 3120 Pinebrook Road; Parcel PP-43-A-4-B-X – Ray

Principal Planner Ray Milliner presented the staff report for a 5,500-square-foot addition 
to an existing school on the east side of the building.  The addition would add six new 
classrooms, six new teachers, and about 20 new students.  He stated that Staff has found 
that this application meets the minimum requirements for approval of a conditional use, 
and the biggest issue relates to traffic.  During the peak drop-off time, cars back up into 
Pinebrook Road.  The Engineering Department went to the school and observed the 
traffic and found there was an issue.  The County Engineer’s Office has recommended 
that the applicant work with them to create a traffic mitigation plan, and the applicant 
would like to do that as part of the permitting process.  The primary mitigation discussed 
was a double-wide queuing lane to take a lot of traffic off of the road, which would 
probably be a better situation than what exists today.  Staff recommended that the 
Planning Commission conduct a public hearing, review the project, and approve it with 
the findings of fact, conclusions of law, and conditions of approval in the staff report.  He 
provided the architectural elevations and noted that they match the existing materials and 
design of the existing structure.
