41   4) AHOZ Update
42
43   Brittney Harris presented the item. She began by letting the Commission know that the Mayor is
44   very interested in the overlay zone. He would like to get it in place quickly, so that the city can
45   have its say on how housing policies are implemented, rather than having to defer to state
46   initiatives. It may go through faster than previously planned. Harris went on to provide some
47   rough data on pricing, as well as some visuals of what the neighborhoods may look like when
48   built. Because of how the market skews, the monthly payments aren’t as low as the planning

     Lehi City Planning Commission                    1                                   February 6, 2025
49   division would like, but they would be low enough to make a difference. Commissioner Everett
50   raised concerns about the mountable curb and accessibility for wheelchair users.
51
52   The current state legislation was briefly researched. If it passes, the state will allow 6,000 square
53   foot lots in any residential zone.
54
55   The AHOZ plan currently includes a 50% density increase, but lot size is still undecided (which
56   is something that will need to be discussed with engineering). The planning department has
57   determined that driveways will have 60 feet of frontage for driveways to align with both the
58   proposed garage and carport designs. House size will be limited to 1,200 square feet, with
59   additions allowed after two years. Several elements from earlier versions of the plan have been
60   maintained, including required owner-occupation, and developers submitting pro-forma for
61   reviews. They are also proposing a 20% limitation on their profit. They could potentially propose
62   higher profits if they include incentives for future owners.
63
64   Commissioner Everett espoused support of owner-occupation and asked if the plan was still to
65   offer the homes primarily to essential workers. Harris explained that the council was
66   uncomfortable with that aspect of the plan, but that income level could be a qualifier.
67   Commissioner Jones asked if there were any other qualifiers that would allow certain people to
68   purchase first. Harris said that there currently aren’t, but that any imposed restrictions would
69   likely have to be limited to 30 days. She stated that she was uncertain if it was legal to restrict the
70   sales based on demographics. Commissioner Jackson mentioned that he thought it was necessary
71   that the houses be owner-occupied, but worried that using income as a restriction wouldn’t be
72   effective, especially in the case of owner-operated businesses or parents putting children’s names
73   on leases. He expressed the view that there wouldn’t be any way to close every possible
74   loophole, and that trying would be more trouble than it was worth. Harris emphasized that the
75   plan isn’t a cure-all, but a baby step in the right direction.
76
77   Commissioner Lockhart expressed concerns over potential conflict with developers. She wants to
78   avoid the houses being reduced to concrete boxes for cost reasons. Harris mentioned that the city
79   has been considering classes for citizens on how to elevate property on their own (such as
80   bricklaying for facades, etc.) Mike West offered that they could require multiple house designs
81   as part of the contract process. He emphasized that the goal is to create houses that people want
82   to live in. Commissioner Lockhart recommended looking at Sugarhouse in Salt Lake City for
83   potential design standards. The Commission discussed several methods of encouraging people to
84   maintain and improve their property to keep neighborhoods nice.
85
86   The Commission was pleased with the progress that has been made on the project.
