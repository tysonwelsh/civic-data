                                         BRIEFING SESSION – 6:30 P.M.

       1.     Briefing Session – Conducted by Mr. Jim Spung.

Mr. Spung advised that the Utah Legislature earlier this year passed a bill saying cities must allow Accessory Dwelling
Units (ADU’s). The biggest change is ADU’s no longer require Conditional Use Permits and are now Permitted Uses.
In lieu of the Conditional Use Permit, there will be clear standards in place for the ADU which will be discussed in the
regular session tonight, including that the owners must still get a rental license and a building permit for structural
changes. Also, the State of Utah says that cities can prohibit ADU’s on lots smaller than 6,000 square feet, they
cannot be used for short term rentals, the owners must reside in the main house and a mechanism must be in place
to insure each property when sold has a record of it being an ADU. The Planning Commissioners had several
questions including parking requirements, allowable sizes of these units, why the need for business license and
building permit which Mr. Spung answered saying all these issues will be addressed this evening in the regular
session and review of the proposed draft zoning amendment.

                                              ZONING TEXT AMENDMENT



 3.          File 12Z21. City of Taylorsville – Recommendation to the City Council for a Zoning Text Amendment to
            Sections 13.070.020; 13.08.020; 13.09.030; 13.10.020; and 13.11.010, Related to Accessory Dwelling
            Units (ADUs), Extended Living Areas, Guesthouses, and Corresponding Definitions. (Presenter - Jim
            Spung/AICP/Senior Planner)

      3.1 Mr. Spung presented this item. The City of Taylorsville is requesting a text amendment to the Taylorsville
          Land Development Code to align with recent legislation passed by the Utah Legislature, Utah Code
          Surrounding the ADU changes mostly. He gave the definition for Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) as: A self-
          contained dwelling unit that is incorporated within the single-family dwelling that maintains complete
          independent living facilities for one family, including permanent provisions for living, sleeping, eating,
          cooking, and sanitation (i.e., separate kitchen). An ADU is part of the single-family dwelling and is not a
          duplex or multi-family unit. These are referred to in current code as accessory apartments, which needs to
          be changed to ADU. He presented a Summary of Current Code – pt. 1 and 2 as follows:
          •        Allowed with administrative conditional use permit (ACUP) in all residential zoning districts
          (except RM and MH).
          •        ACUP approval in the Mixed-Use (MU) zoning district.
          •        Requires owner occupancy.
          •        Only allowed in detached single-family dwellings (not mobile homes).
          •        Must retain single-family dwelling appearance.
          •        Separate utility meters/addressing prohibited.
          •        Requires residential rental license (business license).
          •        Must comply with building code.
          •        Must provide one off-street parking space (in addition to required parking for main dwelling).
          •        Entrance located on the side or rear of dwelling.
          •        Maximum size of 30 percent of dwelling floor area.
          •        Limited to two bedrooms.
          •        ACUP non-transferrable.
          •        Review after two years.
          •        Other requirements or conditions as approved.


3.2       He reviewed House Bill 82:
          •       New standards are in Utah Code 10-9a-530.
          •       Legislation requires all municipalities to allow internal ADU’s in all single-family residential zoning
                  districts.
          •       Limits local authority to regulate internal ADU’s.
          •       Goes into effect October 1, 2021.
          •       Under House Bill 82, Local government can still regulate the following:
                  1.         Prohibit short-term rentals in ADU’s;
                  2.         Require owner-occupancy in ADU’s;
                  3.         Prohibit detached ADU’s;
                  4.         Prohibit ADUs on lots smaller than 6,000 square feet;
                  5.         Prohibit separate utility meters;
                  6.         Require licenses/permits; and
                  7.         Prohibit the creation of ADU’s within a residential zoning district covering an area
                             equivalent to 67% or less of the total area in the city if the main campus of a state or
                             private university with 10,000+ students is located within the city.

3.3      Mr. Spung reviewed the proposed standards as:
         •       “Accessory apartment” will now be “accessory dwelling unit (ADU).”
         •       ACUP approval no longer required – permitted in all residential zones.
         •       Only allowed in detached single-family dwellings (not duplex, triplex, apartment, mobile homes,
                 etc.).
         •       Requires evidence of owner occupancy.
         •       Only allowed on a lot containing 6,000 square feet or more.
         •       Must retain single-family dwelling appearance.
         •       Separate utility meters/addressing prohibited.
         •       Must provide one off-street parking space (in addition to required parking for main dwelling).
         •       Requires residential rental license (business license).
         •       Requires building permit.
         •       Clarified business licensing requirements.
         •       ADUs shall not be used for short-term rentals (i.e., rental less than 30 consecutive days)
         •       Identified specific building code requirements.
         •       Requires a notarized affidavit recorded with Salt Lake County.
         •       Updated definition for “dwelling;” “dwelling, duplex;” and “dwelling, single-family.”

3.4      Mr. Spung listed and discussed requirements the Building Department needs at a minimum for an ADU as
         follows:

              1.   Living facilities including permanent provisions for living, sleeping, eating, cooking, and bathing
                   (IRC 202-definitions).
              2.   A 32” wide side hinged door leading to the public way without requiring travel through a garage.
                   (IRC 311. 1 & 2)
              3.   Wall and floor/ceiling fire separation between units with at least ½” gypsum or equivalent. (IRC
                   302.3 amended exception #3).
              4.   Access to the plumbing, HVAC and electrical controls for the ADU (IRC 1305.1 & 2005.2.1)
              5.   A written statement from an HVAC professional stating the HVAC system is sufficient for the ADU
                   and the primary dwelling (IRC 1103.8 and 1105.4)
              6.   Separation of electrical systems. The branch circuits supplying the ADU must be accessible to its
                   occupants (IRC 3705.8)
              7.   Sleeping rooms with emergency openings. (IRC 310.1)
              8.   Interconnected smoke and carbon monoxide detectors (IRC 314.2.2 & 315.2.2 and state
                   amendments).
              9.   A building permit for the installation of any of these features and any other construction related
                   to the ADU. All construction must meet current building codes.

3.5      Mr. Spung discussed proposals for extended living areas and guesthouses:
             1. “Extended living areas” = Additional and Accessory Facilities within its’ own structure with kitchen,
                 bathroom and sleeping areas, designed for temporary use by an extended family members for
                 medical and economic reasons on a non-rental basis, and in compliance with the standards as set
                 forth in this title. That definition also includes kitchen and living quarters for domestic staff or
                 other personnel typically employed in household maintenance, i.e., maid, butler, gardeners, etc.
                 That sounds like an internal ADU to him, just that the occupancy is different there because it is an
                 employment type rather than a third party person paying rent. It is also an antiquated type of use.
                 Staff is proposing eliminating “Extended living areas” entirely and if someone wants an
                 independent living area in the home they would need to get a permit for an ADU and could then
                   be legally rented or not rented out.

              2.   Reorganized and reworded standards related to “guesthouses.” Right now there is a lot of

         3.7.1   Commissioner Willardson asked if there was a limit on the number of people allowed therein.

                 the number). That was a concern for Commissioner Willardson with regard to migrant families

         3.7.2       Commissioner Wendel said she believed that it would help the City not only in tracking the ADU’s
                     for the purpose of wanting to know what is going on in the City but be in compliance with the
                     Moderate-Income Housing Plan that must be done every year. She felt a true ADU number would
                     also help. It would be showing additional funding for transportation and other issues that are
                     necessary for the City by showing that there are ADU’s available. She had a question of when
                     there are other exterior and separate structures what was the largest footprint a shed or a work
                     shop could have on properties right now? When talking about the 75 percent and coming down
                     to 50 percent, she had already seen some properties with very large workshops and sheds
                     thereon. Mr. Spung answered there are two different standards, one under the Accessory
                     Building Standards for the City. There is a standard for lots that are less than 15,000 square feet
                     and there is a standard for lots larger than 15,000 square feet. For lots that are less than 15,000
                     square feet the maximum area is 576 square feet or a conditional use permit may be obtained
                     which would allow up to 25 percent of the rear yard area. That is basically everything from the
                     back of the primary dwelling to the rear property line and on both side yards. That could turn out
                     to be a pretty large structure, depending on how large the yard is. Lots that are larger than 15,000
                     square feet would allow up to 854 square feet or with a conditional use permit, 25 percent of the
                     rear yard area. He mentioned that if a conditional use permit is chosen, if they opt to go bigger
                     or taller, there are additional architectural standards that apply to where they cannot just have a
                     huge metal shed in the back yard. The architecture has to resemble the home and be of different
                     materials other than metal. He has received many phone calls from individuals wanting to put a
                     huge steel shed in their back yard with a conditional use permit and he has to tell them no because
                     it must be architecturally compatible. That would make it considerably more expensive to do and
                     does discourage people from going that route.
