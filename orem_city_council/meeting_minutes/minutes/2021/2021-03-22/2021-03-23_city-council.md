         PUBLIC HEARING / RESOLUTION
         The City will receive Public Comment on the and consider approval of a resolution for a
         substantial amendment to the 2019-2020 Annual Action Plan.


Mr. Ransom stated that due to the Coronavirus Pandemic (COVID-19), the U.S. Department of
Housing and Urban Development Community Development Block Grant provided the City of
Orem with a second CDBG allocation of $287,940 to be used to prevent, prepare for, and
respond to COVID-19. Based on the recommendations from the CDBG Advisory Commission,
the City of Orem would like to allocate funds from the previously allocated $258,049.16 in
CDBG-CV funding for public service projects to the following projects: $24,000 to City of
Orem Victim Services to pay a portion of the salary of one advocate to better help address
increasing domestic violence rates, $5,000 to City of Orem Police Department’s Mental Health
Response Team to help provide small resources to homeless and low to moderate mental health
residents to better address growing homeless and mental health issues in the community, $1,650
to Sharon Park Elementary to pay for a bi-level water bottle filling station to help them prevent
COVID and mitigate its impacts, $3,300 to Orem Junior High School to pay for two bi-level
water bottle filling stations to help them prevent COVID and mitigate its impacts, and $30,000
($27,000 in direct assistance, $3,000 for program operation costs) to Community Action
Services and Food Bank for mortgage assistance for low to moderate homeowners living in the
City of Orem struggling to help make mortgage payments due to the direct impact of COVID-19.

Mayor Brunst opened the public hearing at 6:28 p.m. Hearing not comments he closed the public
hearing and brought the item back to the Council for further discussion.

     RESOLUTION/FENCE MODIFICATION
     Applicant requests approval for a six foot SimTek fence between their commercial property
     and two residential properties to the east of Sun River Gardens located at 312 West 1200
     North in the C2 zone

Mr. Bench stated that the Sun River Gardens recently received site plan approval to build a new
storage building on the property at 312 West 1200 North. One of the requirements of this
approval was that a seven foot high masonry wall be built to separate their commercial property
from the residential properties that are adjacent to their property. The resident at 298 West 1200
North, which is directly to the east of Sun River Gardens, recently installed a SimTek fence
between their property and Sun River Gardens. This SimTek fence is six feet high. It matches the
fence on the other side of their property and they do not want to have it replaced. The other
property owner who borders Sun River Gardens on the northeast part of the lot, currently has a
chain link fence which separates their property from Sun River Gardens. They are in favor of
Sun River Gardens matching the six foot high SimTek fencing that currently exists along the
southern property line. Both of the neighbors who border Sun River Gardens prefer the current
six foot SimTek fence to be the divider between the commercial property and their residential
properties. They do not want a seven foot masonry fence. The applicant requests the fence
requirement be modified to leave the six foot SimTek fence in place and extend it to the north.
Pursuant to Section 22-14-19(F) of the City Code, the City Council may modify the fence
requirement if it finds that:
1. The proposed fence provides an adequate buffer for the adjoining residential zone.
2. The appearance of the fence will not detract from uses in the residential zone.
3. The proposed fence will shield the residential use from noise, storage, traffic or any other
    characteristic of commercial or professional office uses that are incompatible with residential
    uses.
The adjoining residential property owners have indicated in writing that they support the
proposed modification and their letters are included with this agenda summary. He conclude
that staff has reviewed the proposed fence modification and believes the request meets all of the
requirements listed above.

Discussion ensued regarding a concern of setting a precedence in future requests, and how it is
built into the ordinance that allows an application to be made and the need to appear before the
council for each approval.
Mayor Brunst moved, to approve by Resolution for a six foot SimTek fence between their
commercial property and two residential properties to the east of Sun River Gardens located at
312 West 1200 North in the C2 zone. Mr. Macdonald seconded the motion. Those voting aye:
Richard F. Brunst, Jeff Lambson, Debby Lauret, Tom Macdonald, Terry Peterson, David
Spencer and Brent Sumner. The motion passed.

7.        PERSONAL APPEARANCES

Lachelle Thompson
I have some concerns about the large developments proposed on 1600 W. and 400 N. 1. 1600 N
is already very narrow and crowded as you go East to access 1-15. I'm concerned that we are
adding more residents to an area that isn't equipped to handle the current traffic needs. I request
that the 1600 N access to I-15 be widen with more lanes before we add more residents. 2. I'm
concerned about the increase of traffic in residential neighborhoods. I request parking lot access
to these developments be off State Street or where 1600 N is business zoned, not in residential
neighborhoods like 400 N or west 1600 N. 3. I'm concerned about the proposed 6 story height of
these developments so close to residential neighborhoods and by businesses that are single or
two story structures. I request setbacks of 75 feet from residential homes and structure height
lower as it approaches existing homes.

Barbara Pulley
We moved to Orem in 1969. To put that into perspective we moved here before they had a traffic
signal light at the intersection of 1600 North and State Street. We have lived on 1600 North this
entire time. We have watched a quiet country road turn into an extremely busy city street. We
built our home on property that my Grandfather owned and farmed until his death, so it is
meaningful to me, and is the main reason why we have stayed in Orem. When we wanted to
build our home we were only able to do so, if we put in the sidewalk, curb and gutter along our
property, and it had to be done at our expense. Later on the City put in the sidewalk, curb and
gutter all along 1600 North at the Cities expense (our taxes) for all those who didn't have it. Then
the street became so busy that the City had to come in on our frontage to widen the road, and
they tore up my sidewalk without any consideration. That's not the end of the story! A second
time they widened the road and again came in on our frontage. This time we were left without a
pull over in front of our house. Our newspaper delivery person told us it was to dangerous for
him to deliver the paper to us , and we would need to move our paper box. So after 50 years we
stopped taking the newspaper. And now we have the City figuring to allow a development that
will further frustrate the people who live in our area with even busier streets! There are certain
times of the day when we can't get out of our driveway as it is! Sadly this is only one of the
problematic issues we will be dealing with. Orem City, we have put up with a lot over the years
since we have been here, but this one is just asking to much!



Jim Condie
