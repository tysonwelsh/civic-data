Public hearing to allow input regarding transfers from the Water, Sanitary
Sewer, Storm Sewer, and Refuse Funds to the City’s General Fund
          A memo from Council staff regarding proposed Enterprise Fund transfers came before the Council for consideration.
The memo stated Utah Code Ann. §10-6-135.5 requires a notice to all users and hold a public hearing when a city is proposing to
transfer funds from an enterprise fund to another fund for purposes not directly related to services provided by that enterprise
fund. Ogden has transferred funds from the city’s water, sanitary sewer, storm sewer, and refuse utility enterprise funds to the
general fund for decades. The requirement to provide notice of the transfers and to hold a separate public hearing is the result of a
change to state law that occurred in 2016.
           Council Executive Director Eller-Smith summarized the memo and stated the Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 Budget
includes a proposal to transfer funds from the water ($3,224,250), sanitary sewer ($1,784,575), storm sewer ($768,000) and refuse
($912,000) Utility Enterprise Funds to the city’s General Fund in lieu of the collection of franchise and property taxes paid by
non-governmental utilities. She used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to expound on the history of the annual Enterprise Fund
transfers. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
           Council member Choberka thanked Council staff for their detailed explanation of the Enterprise Fund transfers, as well
as the history surrounding the transfers and the comparison of the transfers made by other government entities.
           Council member Myers stated that there has been a lot of work done on this issue, but this is the first meeting in which
this matter has been publicly discussed. He noted that the transfer is a good way for the City to pass some responsibility for City
services on to the many non-profit entities that use City utilities, but do not pay property taxes.

Proposed Ordinance 2024-17 approving the Capital Improvement Plan for
Ogden City for the Fiscal Years 2025 to 2029
          A memo from the City Comptroller regarding the proposed Fiscal Year (FY) 2025-2029 Capital Improvement Plan (CIP)
came before the Council for consideration. The memo stated the proposed CIP is reviewed and adopted each year as a planning
tool for major capital investments in the City’s physical plant over a rolling five-year period. Each year’s plan is updated based on
the changing needs of the City, funding opportunities, projects completed and other similar factors. During this year’s budget
process, proposed capital projects have been reviewed alongside each Department’s proposed budget and in context with the
overall needs and resources available to the City through the budget process.
          Council Deputy Director Symes summarized the memo and used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to provide an
overview of the total number of projects – including new projects – included in the CIP and to discuss the total funding proposal
of $262,795,234. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Council member Hyer referenced a CIP project to install a traffic signal at North Street and Monroe Boulevard, but the
Mount Lewis Community Plan called for a roundabout in that area and the Plan has not been adjusted. Mr. Symes stated that
should be addressed to provide consistency between the CIP and the Community Plan. He asked Public Services Director
Anderson to address that CIP project. Mr. Anderson stated that a few years ago the City received Weber Area Council of
Governments (WACOG) funding for improvements on North Street between Washington and Monroe Boulevards. He agreed that
the Community Plan identified a roundabout at the intersection of North Street and Monroe Boulevard, but upon evaluation of the
project area it was determined there was insufficient space to accommodate a roundabout. Administration proposed a traffic signal
rather than a roundabout to reduce the impact of private property owners and the cemetery that is located on one of the corners. If
the Community Plan is not changed and the roundabout is preferred, the City would need to attempt to buy property from the
private owners in that area; if those individuals refuse to sell, the City would need to pursue a taking action. He then noted that the
project is not planned for this year and there is time to change the Community Plan before the project commences.

          Memos from City Administration regarding proposed fee amendments pertaining to fire system inspections and water
rate and discount fees, as well as the tentative budget for Ogden City for Fiscal Year (FY) 2024-2025 came before the Council for
consideration. The memos stated the following:
               • The Fire Department has submitted a proposed ordinance amending the fee for inspections of private fire
                    protection systems. The ordinance, if approved, will change the fee from $12 to $15 per inspection and will add
                    language to the city code that will allow the fee to be increased annually based on the consumer price index.
               • Proposed amendments to water rates and discount fees would increase water rates for all users by four percent
                    per year for the next five years. This would be a set increase over and above the blended inflation adjustment.
                    The Council has received presentations on the proposed funding for the Canyon Waterline and the Council
                    adopted a parameters resolution for approximately $77 million in federal and state loans. The rate increases are
                    needed to pay the debt service on the loans. Additionally, the Council received a presentation on proposed
                    changes to the water and refuse discount program. Currently, Weber County administers a property tax relief
                    program and provides the list of qualified participants to the city. The City applies a 50 percent water base rate
                    discount and a $2.50 refuse discount to each utility bill. The proposal is to expand the program to individuals
                    who are blind, to disabled veterans, and to deployed members of the military and would apply a set monthly
                    discount rate of $15.50 to the billing account. The discount would increase with the blended inflation
                    adjustment; and
               • The public hearing regarding the FY2024-2024 budget was initially opened on June 4 and was continued after
                    initial public comment to June 18 to allow the Council to discuss any additional changes to the Fiscal Year
                    2024-2025 Tentative Budget. Additional changes may be made to the Final Fiscal Year 2024- 2025 Budget
                    which will be presented for consideration on August 6, 2024.
          Comptroller Sorensen first addressed the proposed fire system inspection fee amendment; the City requires a permit for
private fire protection systems and currently uses a third-party to track private fire protection systems and to perform inspections.
The proposed amendment will increase the inspection fee to not more than $15 per inspection; these fees are paid directly to the
inspectors through the third-party system and are not collected by the City.
          Public Services Director then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the need for the Ogden Canyon
Waterline project; the waterline was originally constructed in 1935 and plays a critical role in supplying water to Ogden City. The
presentation also included historical photos of the waterline to illustrate the manner in which the infrastructure is aging and the
risk of failure. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Management Services Director Stout then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation prepared by Lewis, Robertson &
Burningham (LRB), the City’s Financial Advisor, to provide an analysis of the City’s water utility fund, which will be one of the
funding sources for repayment of the bond that will be issued for the project. The City’s rate structure model includes the
provision of funding for major capital infrastructure; the model includes programmed general rate increases of four percent over
the next five years, as well as a blended inflation adjustment increase (BIA). The BIA is calculated yearly based upon a formula
that weighs the western region consumer price index (CPI) and the American City and County Commodity Channel Index (CCI).
The current recommendation is to maintain these inflationary increases as well as consider a rate increase to maintain fund
balances. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.
          Treasurer Johnson then used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the utility discount fee amendments;
successful applicants to Weber County’s property tax relief program for financial hardship are also eligible to receive the utility
fee discount. The current discount is 50 percent of the water base rate and $2.50 off of garbage service fees. Approximately 400
utility accounts quality for the discount and applying the discount in the City’s utility billing system is a time intensive manual
process. The current proposal is to expand the discount program to include the blind, disabled veterans, and deployed military
through the County’s property tax relief program; the new discount would be a flat amount of $15.50 per month adjusted annually
using BIA. By moving to a flat discount amount, modifying how the discount is associated with the customer and utilizing a data
import, the administrative burden on utility billing staff will be significantly reduced. The anticipated number of qualifying
applicants is 1,000.

meetings. For a copy of the presentation in its entirety, see the information packet for the meeting.

NO – NONE.

Public Comments
           Wayne Richey stated that he has circulated a petition and gathered 61 signatures in his neighborhood; he read the long
title of the petition, which related to requested road closure of Taylor Avenue via a gate on the south side of the Weber State
University (WSU) campus. This is due to increased traffic in the area that has impacted the safety and quality of life on the street.
He also noted that he sent a letter regarding the same subject matter to the Mayor’s Office a year ago and he read from that letter
as well. He cited another street on the north side of the WSU campus that has already been closed using a gate and this has set a
reasonable precedent for other residential streets surrounding the campus.
           Teresa Bramwell stated that she would love for the City Council to help her and other property owners in the vicinity of
25th Street and Monroe Boulevard. She wants to make back some of the money she has lost due to her ownership of her property.

Mayor Comments
         Mayor Nadolski was excused from the meeting at this point. Chief Administrative Officer Brown stated that
Administration is currently evaluating the request from Mr. Richey as well as the legality of the closure of another street near the
WSU campus. A traffic study has been performed on Taylor Avenue and it does not appear the traffic volume is as high as
residents have observed, but she will follow up with Mr. Richey directly on those matters.

Council member Comments
          Council member Hyer thanked the residents that have participated in tonight’s meeting; the level of their preparedness
and the manner in which they expressed their viewpoints was very impressive and he is very appreciative of their involvement.
          Council member Choberka referenced a recent community meeting at Lester Park where she heard from residents about
their happiness about the neighborhood they live in; sometimes that area, specifically the vicinity referenced by Ms. Bramwell,
gets a ‘bad rap’, but many people are very fulfilled and have made great friendships and relationships with people who live near
them.
          Council member Graf thanked Mr. Richey for his input regarding the traffic on Taylor Avenue; all residents who live on
his street share his feelings and he applauded the neighborhood for pursuing the public process that will help them to preserve the
quality of life in their neighborhood.
          Council member Blair echoed Mr. Richey’s comments about Taylor Avenue as well; he lives in that area and recalled
promises that the residents have received from the City regarding options for controlling speed of vehicles on the road. None of
those promises have come to fruition, though other neighborhoods have benefitted from the efforts of that neighborhood. These
residents have been patient and respectful of the City, but they are losing their patience, and something needs to be done
regardless of the outcome of the traffic study cited by Ms. Brown. He concluded he loves WSU but is concerned by the danger of
his neighborhood due to the increase in college traffic on Taylor Avenue. He thanked Mr. Richey for his efforts and implored
Administration to look more closely at the matter and determine a compromise that works for all parties.

Closed Session
       The Council chose not to hold the closed session.


     There being no further business to come before the Council, COUNCIL MEMBER MYERS MOVED THE
MEETING ADJOURN AT 8:41 P.M. MOTION WAS SECONDED BY COUNCIL MEMBER CHOBERKA, ALL
VOTING AYE.
