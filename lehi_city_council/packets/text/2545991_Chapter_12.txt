Section 04.060. Criteria for Approval.
No amendment to this Code, or the Zoning District Map(s), may be recommended for approval by the Commission
nor approved by the City Council unless such amendment or conditions thereto are consistent with the General Plan.
In considering a Code amendment, or Zoning District Map(s) amendment, the applicant shall identify, and the City
Staff, DRC, Commission, and the City Council may consider the following factors, among others:

    1.   The effect of the proposed amendment on the character of the surrounding area;
    2.   Consistency with the goals and policies of the General Plan.
    3.   Consistency and Compatibility of the proposed zone with the General Plan land uses of nearby and adjoining
         properties.
    4.   The suitability of the properties for the uses requested and their suitability for the existing uses identified by
         the General Plan;
    5.   Whether a change in the uses allowed for the affected properties will unduly affect the uses, or proposed uses
         for nearby and adjoining properties;
    6.   The gain to the public health, safety and welfare from the existing classification to the proposed amendment;
         and
    7.   The overall community benefit of the proposed amendment.


  REPORT ANALYSIS

The applicant requests review and recommendation of a proposed amendment to Chapter 12 of the Development Code
on fence height and requirements. The current code classifies all fences over six feet in height as a conditional use.
This has caused confusion on whether all these fences have to be approved or not.

The proposed amendment changes fences over six feet in height to an exception. It allows staff level approval for
fences over six feet in height that an applicant requests because of grade or safety issue concerns. Any other reason
for a taller fence and all fences over eight feet in height would require review and approval by the Planning Commis-
sion with a public hearing. These would still be considered an exception not a conditional use. This puts the burden
of proof that the taller fence is needed on the applicant and allows the Planning Commission to deny the request if
sufficient reasons are not provided.

The DRC reviewed the proposed amendment and had two comments. Both of these comments were implemented in

of eight feet of height as an exception Staff can approve. The other comment related to providing access to power
equipment. This comment relates to all fences regardless of height.


  SCOPE OF APPROVAL

Development Code Amendments are legislative, discretionary items. This means that they do not have to be approved
and the Planning Commission and City Council can require any changes as part of their approval.

The public can speak to these items. They can encourage the Planning Commission to give a positive or negative
recommendation. They also can ask for changes to be made. For changes to be made they would need to be included
in the motion made by the Planning Commission or City Council.


  POSSIBLE MOTIONS AND FINDINGS

The Planning Commission may move to recommend approval, approval with modifications or denial of the pro-
posed amendments to the Lehi City Development Code. Any motion should include findings. Possible findings
include:

    1.   The proposed Development Code Amendment adds clarity that fences over six feet in height do not have to
         be approved.
    2.   The proposed Development Code Amendment codifies the requirement for access to power equipment.
    3.   The proposed Development Code amendment is/is not in conformance with the purposes, intent, and provi-
         sions of the General Plan and its various elements.
    4.   Additional findings based upon information presented during the public hearing.
