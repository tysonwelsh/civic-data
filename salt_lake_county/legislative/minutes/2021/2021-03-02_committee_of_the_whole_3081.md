3.3 Legislative Update 21-0229
Attachments: Staff Report
Presenter: Kara Trevino, Legislative Director, Council (Approx. 1 hr). 
Discussion/Direction
 
Ms. Kara Trevino , Intergovernmental Relations Manager, Council Office, 
reviewed the following legislative bills:
- SB 212 Health Departments Review Task Force
Ms. Trevino stated this bill creates a task force to review the powers, duties, organization 
and activities of the Utah Department of Health and Local Health Departments, request 
audits, develop recommendations for legislative action, improve the effectiveness and 
efficiency of the health departments.  This bill has passed through committee.
- SB 214 Official Language Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill amends a previous bill, which made English the official 
language of Utah.  This bill would allow government agencies to print information in 
languages other than English. 
Mayor Jennifer Wilson stated this bill provides flexibility, which will help the 
Page 2 of 12
Committee of the Whole Meeting Minutes March 2, 2021
underserved communities.   
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member 
Stringham, to support SB 214.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- SB 237 Animal Shelter Revisions
Ms. Trevino stated this bill addresses the methods by which an animal shelter may 
euthanize an animal.  It requires animal shelters to adopt a euthanasia policy.
A motion was made by Council Member Bradshaw, seconded by Council Member 
Snelgrove, to support SB 237.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- SB 240 County Recreational Area Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill relates to the Mountainous Planning District and Mountainous 
Planning Commission.  It removes the sunset provision, allows the County to use general 
funds for fire, paramedic, and police protection services, and allows the County to 
determine the membership of the Mountainous Planning Commission. 
A motion was made by Council Member Theodore, seconded by Council Member Alvord, 
to support SB 240.  The motion carried by a unanimous vote.
- HB 433 Amendments Related to Infrastructure Funding
Ms. Trevino stated this bill authorizes the issuance of $ 1,400,000,000 in bonds for 
specified transportation and transit projects. 
• One-time appropriation: $862,900,000 
• $621 M state roads 
• $107.5 M for state parks/rec 
• $101.6 for transit 
• $32.5 M for active transportation
• Local match drops from 40% to 20% 
• Follows project prioritization process (Transportation Commission, Unified Plan)
• Double track Frontrunner, Salt Lake County Midvalley BRT, $ 5M for an environmental 
study at the Point of the Mountain area, two lanes on U -111 from Herriman Parkway to 
11800 South

Ms. Trevino updated the Council on the following bills:
- 4th Sub SB 18 Property Tax Exemption Amendments
Ms. Trevino stated this bill passed in the Senate with the recommendations from the 
County included.  The bill was then substituted in Committee with amendments the 
County did not support.  It is her understanding that a 5th substitution will be adopted, 
which will change the bill back to what the County would support.  She will keep a close 
eye on this bill to make sure that happens.
- 2nd Sub SB 52 Property Tax Deferral Modifications
Mr. Wayne Cushing , County Treasurer, stated the Council originally opposed this bill 
because of the impact it would have on the County ’s revenue.  The bill was amended to

income level below twice the circuit breaker amount, and property valued at less than the

- 1st Sub SB 65 Community Reinvestment Agency Amendments

- 2nd Sub SB 155 988 Mental Health Crisis Assistance
Ms. Trevino stated this bill will be studied over the interim.
- 3rd Sub SB 195 Emergency Response Amendments
Mr. Mitchell Park , Legal Counsel, Council Office, outlined the following four 
provisions of this bill: 1) it defines what Orders of Constraint are and clarifies how that 
relates to a public health emergency, 2) it includes an Order of Restriction as it relates to a 
pandemic public health emergency, 3) it gives power to the State Legislature to terminate a 
public health emergency that has been effect for more than 30 days.  It also gives power 
to the County Council to terminate the public health emergency at any time, and 4) it 
provides a change in the way emergencies are handled.  He stated this is a very 
complicated bill.
Mayor Jennifer Wilson stated she wanted to go on record at the state level of opposing 
this bill.  The bill would apply to all emergencies, not just health emergencies.  Time is 
very important with all emergencies.  
Ms. Catherine Kandor , Deputy Mayor of Regional Operations, stated there usually is 
not enough time to wait 24 hours before declaring an emergency.  It could result in a loss 
of life.
