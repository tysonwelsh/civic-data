5. Public Hearing, Proposed General Plan Amendment, to consider Union Station Framework Plan.
  Mr. Rypien stated the Union Station Framework Plan has been prepared in response to the Make
  Ogden Plan and the Planning Commission is to review it and determine whether it meets the standards
  of the Make Ogden Plan. The plan encompasses the area between Wall Avenue and the railyard
  bounded by 22nd Street and 27th Street. Areas to the north and south contain either vacant property or
  underutilized property. The plan provides land use options and height limitations for new construction
  in this area with a mix of residential and commercial uses as well as parking structures. He discussed
  the benefits of the plan in that it will protect historic features of the building and its surroundings, will
  encourage reinvestment, and revitalize the downtown area. Staff recommends the proposed plan be
  adopted as it appears to be in compliance with both the General Plan and the Make Ogden Downtown
  Plan.
  Commissioner Aaberg asked about the existing parking for the Frontrunner station and whether
  adequate parking is proposed to replace stalls which would be lost through development. Mr. Rypien
  responded indicating new parking structures will be constructed which would serve both the
  FrontRunner as well as other development, including residential and commercial uses as they are
  developed. It is anticipated there will be adequate parking for the station.

6. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment, to allow short-term rentals in downtown
  Mr. Brierley explained while some recent revisions were recommended for approval to clarify standards
  for residential vacation rentals, the City has been approached by Visit Ogden to allow short-term
  rentals in the downtown area. He stated the new downtown code defines a hotel/motel as having at
  least 15 rooms for overnight stay for less than 90 days while a short-term rental limits overnight stay to
  less than 30 days. Visit Ogden has expressed concern there is a shortage of overnight dwelling
  options in the downtown area, particularly during special events. It is their feeling the downtown is the
  more desirable place for visitors as entertainment and restaurants are available and would increase the
  vitality of the downtown. While hotels are allowed downtown, there is a requirement to check in at the
  lobby desk while there is typically no management for short-term rentals. Staff is proposing adding
  short-term vacation rentals as a permitted use in both the C-ENT and the H25 zones where property is
  already developed as a dwelling unit. He felt some standards should be established to protect existing
  long-term residents in the downtown, suggesting a building could contain either apartments or vacation
  rentals but not both at the same time. The proposed amendment would not require additional parking
  to be created, but minimum parking for the use must be satisfied. While the original intent is to service
  visitors while on vacation, there also are some short-term contractors working in the area who desire
  overnight accommodations for up to six months but do not want to pay the fee for hotels for that length
  of time. Concern has also been raised relating to investors using properties as short-term rentals, and



                                                      5
  creation of too many short-term rentals reduces viable housing stock in the community. One solution
  recommended is to require a building be owned for a minimum of three years prior to allowing it to be
  used as a short-term rental. Staff recommends the amendment be approved to allow both owner-
  occupied and non-owner-occupied vacation rentals in the downtown so long as property is owned for a
  minimum of three years before short-term rentals cam be established and that both apartments for
  long-term stay and short-term rentals not be allowed within the same building at the same time.
  Commission asked about existing apartment buildings or loft apartments, indicating mixing the use for
  long-term and short-term rentals may not be a problem based on the size of the buildings or the
  number of dwelling units. Mr. Brierley responded some clarification language might be added to allow
  a mix of uses for loft apartments where the ground floor is commercial or office space, but felt should
  not be allowed for larger buildings or for newly constructed buildings as a means to fill tenant space.
  He also clarified those currently operating and licensed as a short-term rental would not be affected by
  the three-year ownership requirement. Commissioner Shinoda noted there are a few existing single-
  family homes in the C-ENT zone and asked whether they would have the same regulations. Mr.
  Brierley responded under current regulations one could become a short-term rental while the proposed
  language would require all or none be used as short-term rentals. Commissioner Blaisdell noted the
  definition of a short-term rental is up to 30 days and asked if and how that is enforced. Mr. Brierley
  stated a short-term rental can be up to 30 days by definition, but there are no restrictions limiting
  whether a contract could be extended. Staff is suggesting the code regulating the downtown limit
  overnight stays to 90 days.
    Mr. Steve Lister, 1918 Chocktaw Ridge, stated he owns the District Lofts at 2236 Washington
    Boulevard which houses four loft units that have been used as short-term rentals for several years. He
    expressed concern with the six-month time limitation, stating he has had engineers working at Hill Air

He stated there is a need for executive housing for between three to eighteen months with a full-service

  Mr. Jared Allen, 541 26th Street, stated he had requested a parking reduction for the Windsor Hotel

7. Public Hearing, Proposed Subdivision Ordinance Amendment, relating to subdivision amendment and
   lot modifications.
  Mr. Brierley indicated Staff is proposing some minor amendments to the Subdivision Ordinance in an
  effort to simplify processes, better comply with the General Plan and State code regulating
  subdivisions. The proposed language particularly considers parcel combinations, lot combinations and
  lot line adjustments as well as subdivision amendments and vacations. He stated it also eliminates
  discrepancies and makes it easier to understand and locate regulations. The proposed amendment
  would acknowledge lots which show up on County records as being more than one lot with arrows
  identifying as being combined with one single parcel number as legally combined lots so long as they
  are under the same ownership. It also eliminates the need for a subdivision plat for the combination of
  subdivided lots, and clarifies standards and procedures for parcel amendments, vacations and lot line
  adjustments and allows Mayoral approval of minor amendments. It also addresses recent changes in
  State law regulating these actions and provides opportunity for public meeting process when required.
  Neighbors within 300’ would be notified by mail and invited to attend the public meeting. Staff feels the
  proposed amendments are consistent with the General Plan, and with State law and recommends
  approval.
