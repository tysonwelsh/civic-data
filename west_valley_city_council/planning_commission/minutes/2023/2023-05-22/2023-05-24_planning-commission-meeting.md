GP-1-2023
Russell Platt
1251 West 3300 South
General Plan change from Mixed Use with High Density Residential
to Mixed Use with Very High Density Residential
13.0 Acres

Russell Platt is requesting a change to the General Plan from Mixed Use with High Density
Residential (12 to 20 units/acre) to Mixed Use with Very High Density Residential (over 20
units/acre) on 13.0 acres of property located at 1251 West 3300 South. This application also
includes a request to replace a development agreement for this property that was entered into
back in 2002. The subject property is currently zoned MXD (Mixed Use). Surrounding zoning
includes C-2 (General Commercial) to the west, north, and portion of the east and RMH
(Residential Mobile Home) to the south and the remaining east portion. Surrounding uses include
vacant land owned by the City’s Redevelopment Agency to the north, a used car lot to the west,
and mobile home parks to the east and south.


The property is currently developed as the Village at River’s Edge Apartments which include
244 apartments and 21,054 square feet of commercial space on the ground floor of four of the
buildings on the north end of the property. In the southeast corner of the property is a 40-unit
condo building that is not included with this application. The current density of the apartment
development is 18.8 units/acre.
Development Proposal
The applicant is proposing two significant changes to the existing Village at River’s Edge
development that are highlighted on the attached concept plan. The first proposed change is to
remodel buildings A1-A4 (buildings in blue on the concept plan) to replace the existing
commercial space with 30 new apartments. The second change is to fill the wetland area along
the east side of the property and develop 17 rental townhome units within 4 new buildings
(buildings in purple on the concept plan). The addition of 47 new units increases the density
from 18.8 units/acre to 22.4 units/acre. This is why the General Plan change is sought. Included
with this report are renderings for the proposed townhomes as well as renderings showing how
the existing mixed use buildings would be remodeled to convert the ground floor commercial
space into apartments. Also included are proposed standards for a new development agreement.
The existing development agreement is included for reference.

Apartments
The applicant is proposing three different types of new apartment units – 12 studio units, 6 1-
bedroom units, and 12 2-bedroom units. The studio units would be 475 sq. ft, the 1-bedroom
units would be 725 sq. ft., and the 2-bedroom units would be 912 sq. ft. The minimum areas in
the Zoning Ordinance for stacked units are 400 sq. ft. for studio units, 600 sq. ft. for 1-bedroom
units, and 850 sq. ft. for 2-bedroom units.

The new units will have 9-foot ceilings, solid surface countertops, two tone paint, 96% efficient
furnaces, and vinyl plank flooring in all rooms except the bathrooms. The applicant is also
proposing to fix stucco cracks and repaint all of the existing apartment buildings, the clubhouse,
and the adjoining condo building which is not under the same ownership. In addition, the
applicant has included management standards with his proposal.

The applicant provided the following reasons for converting the commercial space to apartments:

       “The retail space on the first floor of four of the buildings has not been successful. There
       has been an unfortunate amount of public disturbance requiring police attention. By
       converting the retail space to new residential units it will mitigate the need for police
       monitoring, making the apartment community and extended communities safer places.”

Townhomes
Each of the 17 rental townhomes will include a 2-car garage, 2,000 sq. ft. of finished area,
rooftop decks, 9-foot ceilings, solid surface countertops, 96% efficient furnaces, and vinyl plank
flooring in all rooms except the bathrooms.

The applicant provided the following reasons for filling the wetland area and adding rental
townhomes:

       “The wetlands area has been a destination place for the homeless community to camp, by
       cleaning it up and using the area for townhomes and new amenities the area will be more
       easily monitored and maintained.”

Amenities
The existing apartment development includes an outdoor swimming pool, hot tub, walking paths,
covered picnic/BBQ area, 50% open space, a playground, 60 garage spaces, covered carport
a parking, and a clubhouse. The clubhouse includes a kitchen and gathering room, a fitness
center with changing rooms and showers, and a business center.
Proposed new amenities include an additional playground, an additional picnic area, two
pickleball courts, and new landscaped open space where part of the wetland was. The applicant
is also proposing new pedestrian walkway site lighting identified on the concept plan.

Issues

Business Turnover
According to the applicant, the commercial space in the first floors of the mixed use buildings
has not been successful. To help the Planning Commission evaluate this statement, staff has
included with this report a spreadsheet of the license history for the commercial space on the
subject property that is sorted based on the start date of the license. The current licensed
businesses on the property, which are highlighted in yellow in the spreadsheet, include a medical
office, cell phone store, dental office, a hair salon with permanent cosmetics, a laundromat, a nail
salon with permanent cosmetics, and a barber shop. Since the project was completed there have
been 35 business licenses terminated or closed and 9 with a delinquent status, meaning they have
not renewed their license. In some cases, individuals working in hair salons, nail salons, and
permanent cosmetic establishments have obtained their own licenses. Most of the prior licensed
uses were similar to the current licensed uses.

Crime
In the applicant’s justification for the changes, he mentioned that the commercial space led to
public disturbances that required police attention. He also mentioned that the wetland area
attracted transient persons.

The table below compares the 2021 volume of police calls for service for large (150 or more
units) apartment developments within the City. At first look, the Village at River’s Edge
development has the highest call volume along with E Gate. However, a few factors should be
considered to put this number in context.

First, the Village at River’s Edge numbers include calls to the adjoining condos as well as the
commercial space. The other apartment developments don’t include condos or commercial
space. When the Police Department enters the address for a call on an apartment, they sometimes
but not always indicate the unit number of the apartment. If you sum up all of the calls to
buildings with commercial use that do not have an apartment unit number in the address, the
total was 203. So, it appears that a significant number of calls were in response to the
commercial uses.

Second, the Village at River’s Edge apartments are the closest apartments within the City to the
homeless shelter just east of the City’s border in South Salt Lake. Ever since the homeless shelter
opened in South Salt Lake, the City has seen a significant increase in transient activity
particularly in commercial areas closest to the shelter.

repair/enhance the existing landscaping; repair and paint the stucco exteriors of the apartments,
clubhouse, and adjoining condos; add additional site lighting; and adhere to management

BACKGROUND

Scott Godfrey is requesting preliminary plat approval for the Godfrey Industrial Park Subdivision
No. 3. The proposed subdivision will also amend lots 1 and 2 of the Godfrey West Subdivision.
The property is located at approximately 2400 South 5900 West and is bordered by property zoned
Manufacturing.

ISSUES:

At the present time, the subdivision boundary consists of a number of parcels and two recorded
lots within the Godfrey West Subdivision. The proposed subdivision is being requested to
consolidate these parcels and existing recorded lots into a new 4 lot subdivision to be known as
the Godfrey Industrial Park Subdivision No. 3.

As mentioned earlier, two lots in the Godfrey West Subdivision will be amended by this plat. The
reason these are being included in this subdivision is to benefit from a new street proposed to be
located on the north side of these lots. An existing building on lot 1 has already been constructed,
and staff is working the site plan for the second building adjacent to Beagley Road and 5900 West.

Access to the subdivision will be gained by Beagley Road, 5900 West, the SR-201 South Frontage
Road and from a new cul-de-sac extending west from 5900 West at 2400 South. All roads except
the new cul-se-sac are existing. The cul-de-sac will be a dedicated right-of-way having a width of
66 feet and a length of just over 700 feet. The road will consist of curb and gutter on both sides
and sidewalk on the north side. The only portion of the subdivision lacking improvements is along
the south side of the SR-201 South Frontage Road. The developer will need to coordinate
improvements at this location with the Engineering Division. Said improvements will join up with
improvements installed as part of the Lee Creek Subdivision to the east.

The subdivision plat will be subject to FEMA Flood Zone requirements. The developer is working
with the Engineering Division and Salt Lake to resolve and establish guidelines related to those
requirements. In addition, portions of the subdivision are located in a perceived wetland area.
Future studies will need to be done with the Army Corps of Engineers to determine if these
wetlands are jurisdictional. While these identified areas do not affect the subdivision plat, they
may affect future buildings. This item will be better addressed during permitted and/or conditional
use applications as lots within the subdivision develop.

C-9-2023
Hunt Electric (Outside Storage)
1784 West 2300 South
M Zone (1.02 acres)

The applicant, Hunt Electric, is requesting conditional use approval for outside storage at 1784
West 2300 South. This property is zoned M, manufacturing and the West Valley City General
Plan designates this area as Light Manufacturing. The surrounding zone is Manufacturing on all
sides and the surrounding uses include warehouse/light industrial.

Hunt Electric operates a satellite warehouse facility here in one of the four existing suites. They
have inventory that comes and goes on a regular basis as well as vehicles that they would like to
keep outside in a secured area. Therefore, they have submitted an application for approval of
outside storage at this site. The storage area is located behind the building in a 60’ x 24’ area that
is paved and enclosed with a chain link fence.

A site review was conducted and there are several landscaping and site maintenance violations.
The building was originally built in 1972. There are some plans on file however, there is not a
detailed landscape plan. Staff conducts a site inspection when a new business license application
is submitted, which is the case for this application. Also, when sites have multiple tenants the site
issues are typically resolved with the property owner and not the tenant. The maintenance issues
will be addressed with the property owner, however, in this situation, since there is not a detailed
landscape plan on file, Staff recommends that some of the landscape issues be addressed and
reviewed with this conditional use.

Photos have been provided and show the current condition of the site. Staff recommends that a
minimum of (3) three trees be planted in the setback along 2300 South in the area between the
access points. For the remainder of the site all landscaped areas need to be maintained free of
weeds and contain 50% live plant material as outlined in the City Code.

Staff Alternatives:

Approval, subject to the resolution of any concerns raised at the public hearing as well as the

           1. Outside storage is only permitted within the fenced area and located on the

           2. All landscaped areas need to be maintained free of weeds and contain 50% live
              plant material as outlined in the City Code.

           3. There shall be a minimum of (3) three trees be planted in the setback along 2300
              South in the area between the access points.

Continuance, to allow for the resolutions of any issues raised at the public hearing.

C-10-2023
Castlewood Development
2940 West 3650 South
Conditional use approval for a 330-unit apartment development
3.02 acres

The Planning Commission approved a conditional use permit for this project on June 8, 2022.
However, due to financing issues, the developer did not complete the conditional use approval
process with staff and did not begin the project. Since conditional use permits automatically
terminate within 12 months of Planning Commission approval when a building permit has not
been obtained, the applicant is reapplying for the conditional use permit before the prior approval
is terminated.

Castlewood Development is requesting conditional use approval to redevelop 3.02 acres at 2940
West 3650 South into a 330 unit, 2 to 7 story, Class A apartment development. The proposed
unit mix for the project is 42 studio units, 201 one-bedroom units, and 87 two-bedroom units.
The majority of the property (2.94 acres) is zoned CC (City Center) and the balance (0.08 acres)
is zoned R-2-8 (Two Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet). The entire
building is within the CC zone. The property is designated as very high density residential (over
20 units/acre) or office for most of the property and promenade/plaza for the remaining north
portion in the Fairbourne Station Vision, which is part of the City’s General Plan. The property is
currently vacant as the office development previously on the property was demolished in 2014.
According to the Public Works Department, 3650 South will be improved between 3200 West
and 2700 West in 2024 or 2025. Planned improvements include consistent curb, gutter, and
sidewalk along both sides of the street as well as a left turn lane in the middle of the street. Once
these improvements are made, no on-street parking will be allowed.

Project Review
The first section below outlines the purposes in the CC zone and how this development
accomplishes these purposes. The second section addresses the main ordinance standards (it is
not a comprehensive list) applicable to this development.

City Center Zone Purposes
The following is a list of purposes from the CC Zone (underlined) as well as a brief description
of how the apartment development meets the stated purpose:

      Create a recognizable center or downtown for West Valley City. At 7 stories, this
       apartment development will add to the unique, urban environment in Fairbourne Station.
      Encourage and direct development that supports transit. High density residential
       development is one of the best land uses for supporting transit.
      Encourage infill and redevelopment near the future transit station by City Hall. The
       apartment building replaces an office development demolished in 2014 and is
       approximately 650’ from the West Valley Central TRAX station and 400’ from the bus
       hub.
      Reinforce the use of public transportation by locating higher-intensity development,
       including employment-oriented businesses and higher density residential uses, adjacent to
       transit stops. This project is creating higher intensity development by replacing a low
       intensity, 1 to 2 story office development with 330 residential units.
      Encourage mixed-use development to reduce automobile dependency and roadway
       congestion by combining trips and locating destinations within walking and biking
       distances – all interconnected with transit. This is the third residential building in
       Fairbourne Station that will complement the hotel, medical office building, police
       headquarters, and professional office building.
      Provide a mix of housing types, costs and densities. This project will provide a variety of
       studio, 1-bedroom, and 2-bedroom layouts with varying rents. At 109.3 units/acre, the
       proposed density of this project exceeds that of Phase 1 of the Residences at Fairbourne
       Station at 78.7 units/acre and of Phase 2 of the Residences at Fairbourne Station at 69.8
       units/acre.
      Promote architectural and site design treatments that enhance the visual appearance of
       development within the Zone. After a minor change and clarifying notes are added, the
       proposed elevations meets or exceeds the City’s architectural requirements.

family residential from 2 spaces per unit to 1.3 spaces per unit for studio units, 1.5 spaces per
unit for one-bedroom units, and 1.75 spaces per unit for two-bedroom units if the Commission
finds that the reduction is not detrimental to the surrounding area and will enhance the proposed
development. The CC Zone also allows a 10% reduction to parking requirements. The applicant
has submitted the attached parking study in the form of a memo from Hales Engineering to
support the proposed amount of parking.

Amenities
For a project of this size, the ordinance requires a minimum of five amenities from the list in the
ordinance with at least three of the five being major amenities. This project has six major
amenities (outdoor swimming pool, courtyards, clubhouse/community room, rooftop patio,
fitness room, and community game room/recreation room) and seven minor amenities (outdoor
fire pit/fireplace, hot tub, building lobby, package locker system, indoor bicycle storage, electric
vehicle charging stations, and outdoor covered pavilion with tables and a grill).

Unit Sizes
The ordinance requires a minimum of 400 square feet for studio units, 600 square feet for 1-
bedroom units, and 850 square feet for 2-bedroom units. The proposed minimum unit sizes are
520 square feet for studio units, 604 square feet for 1-bedroom units, and 930 square feet for 2-
bedroom units. Given the mix of unit layouts, many units will exceed these proposed minimums.

Landscaping and Recreation Space
The ordinance requires a minimum of 10% of the site area as landscaping and a minimum of
40% of the site area as landscaping and recreation space. The landscaping percentage for this
project is 10.9% and the combined landscaping and recreation space percentage is 54.8%.

Architecture
After reviewing the elevations and renderings, staff had a few minor comments and clarifying
questions for the applicant. Once these comments and questions are addressed, the elevations and
renderings will meet or exceed the City’s design standards.

Other Commitments

In addition to addressing the architectural comments/questions mentioned above, the applicant
has committed to the following improvements:
      Fencing will be installed around all ground floor patios and will include a gate to provide

      The existing chain link fence along the west side of the property shall be replaced with a

      The applicant will work with the City’s Parks Department to align the grade of the site

      To avoid having pets relieve themselves on the City’s promenade park, the applicant has

The applicant also secured an easement from the adjoining condo association to the west to allow

   2. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the
