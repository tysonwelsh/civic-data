    1. WEBER COUNTY BOARD OF CANVASS – 2015 ELECTION

         Ryan Cowley, County Elections Director, referred to the election results before the commissioners for
         Proposition 1 regarding the transportation tax stating that it passed by 56.7%. His overview included
         voter turnout statistics, by mail summary, and reasons for not counting. Mr. Cowley noted the great
         lengths that the Elections office went to in order to resolve those ballots that had a signature issue. He
         said that about 90% of the voters returned their ballot by mail, which is significant. The only other time
         that vote-by-mail was conducted was in 2013 for the library bond. The turnout county-wide for this
         year’s election was about 37%. He noted that Farr West City chose to conduct a traditional election
         whereby voters had to go to their polling places to vote. This gave a good sample of data. Their turnout
         came in at about 23%. There was a significant voter increase by the cities that utilized vote-by-mail. He
         expressed thanks to everyone for the tremendous job in conducting the election and Commissioner Ebert
         also expressed thanks.
         Commissioner Ebert moved to approve the 2015 County Board of Canvass; Commissioner Bell seconded.
         Commissioner Ebert – aye; Commissioner Bell – aye; Chair Gibson – aye

    2. PUBLIC HEARING TO TAKE INPUT ON A PROPOSED INTERFUND LOAN BETWEEN THE
       TRANSPORTATION DEVELOPMENT FUND AND THE PUBLIC WORKS FUND FOR THE PURPOSE OF
       COMPLETING CAPITAL PROJECTS

       Scott Parke, County Comptroller, stated that there is a significant amount of money in the Transportation
       Development Fund and there are plans to spend that money, however, it will be several years before
       construction and the funds are expended. This money is earning minimal interest. The county has the
       opportunity to continue working on some of the flood-control projects but additional funds are needed.
       The solution is to borrow internally and this will be repaid over 10 years from the money earmarked for
       stormwater projects.

    3. PUBLIC HEARING TO TAKE INPUT ON THE WEBER COUNTY 2016 TENTATIVE BUDGET

       Scott Parke, County Comptroller, gave a slide presentation noting that the county has quite a diverse
       economy, which lends to a more stable economy. The market has not rebounded much since the 2008
       downturn. His presentation included the new property value growth, the sales tax (increasing at about 4%
       annually over the last several years), General Fund revenues (expected at $70.2 million) and General Fun
       expenditures (at $69.7 million). The county spent almost $6 million on healthcare in 2010 and budgeted
       almost $9 million for 2016. Weber County is one of the few counties that pays 100% of the premiums for
       health insurance for every employee. Sick leave and other post employment benefits will become a
       significant expenditure in the next few years. He also stated that, while the county’s benefit package is
       high, the county is finding it difficult to attract people to employment positions because the cash benefit is
       not as high as that of the nearby counties.

       The county just received a high bond rating from Standard & Poor’s Rating Services due to its strong
       management with good financial practices, strong budgetary flexibility, etc. Mr. Parke stated that the
       county needs to review how employees are compensated and figure out how to accomplish other goals to
       become more efficient. Commissioner Ebert said that the county is working on these items.

       Mr. Parke addressed the Special Revenue Fund revenues ($40.6 million) and expenditures (70.8 million)
       noting that the county built up a significant amount of money in the Transportation Development Fund
       intentionally and is now spending down that fund balance for transportation projects. He presented the
       five-year forecast for the Municipal Services Fund, the Library Fund and the Health Fund. The voters
       just approved the transportation tax, which will significantly increase the ability to complete various
       transportation/maintenance projects and maintain a healthy fund balance in the Municipal Services Fund.
       Regarding the Enterprise Funds, Mr. Parke stated that the Transportation Station is budgeted to exceed
       spending by $414,919. The final 2016 budget is to be adopted on 12/22/2015.

         Commissioner Bell noted that there are no COLA or merit increases in this budget; there were also no

         Jan Fullmer, of Eden, is retired and her profession was in the healthcare industry. She stated that
         healthcare costs for insurance premiums have far exceeded the cost of living. She said that many
         companies that negotiate healthcare contracts with insurance companies prefer to cut back on paying for
         the gold/platinum policies for employees in order to increase their salaries. She asked if the county can
         be penalized for paying 100% of the healthcare premiums because that is considered a Cadillac plan
         under the Affordable Care Act. Mr. Parke believes the county’s plan is grandfathered and stated that the
         County Human Resources Director is addressing it.
