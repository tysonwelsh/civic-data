Pre-Council, 5:30 p.m.
1. Welcome and Invocation
Mayor Johnson welcomed everyone and noted that all Councilmembers were present. Councilor
Albrecht gave the invocation.

2. Presentations and Reports
2.1 Water Restrictions Discussion
Gary Thomas and Matt Dalton, with the Water Department, suggested some minor changes to
the Water Conservation Plan. They also recommended going to Phase 3, the severe water
shortage phase on June 15th. The biggest change will be that residents can only water 2 days per
week.

2.2 Parks Funding Discussion
Steve Marchbanks, Parks Manager, informed the Council that the bids for Monument Park
construction came back at 30 percent over budget and expressed concern for the high
construction costs. He said that they can make a budget adjustment or do a phased approach to
construction where they might add the amenities at a later date. The Council agreed that the
Shadow Ridge and Monument Parks should be constructed and completed this year.

3. Agenda Questions
Mayor Johnson addressed some of the funding requests from the Council. He said that they can
use 5 million to construct a portion of Family Park. $500,000 can be allocated to pave Vet’s
Baseball Park, and $15,000 for a new disk-golf course. The Mayor agreed to hire a par time grant
writer for a 1-year trial period. The library was given an additional $10,000. There was discussion
regarding how the city could do cost sharing with property owners for downtown sidewalk
installation, and that the City could establish a program next year. Mayor Johnson expressed
concern with adding a Resource Officer due to the ongoing costs. Council Condie felt strongly that

8. Consideration of the Micron Area Plan Concept, changing the Land Use Map of the
   existing Micron Area Plan.
   Scott Bishop with DR Horton was representing the applicant. He stated that the Micron Area
   Plan was approved in 2012 and discussed the current approved plan, which has a lot of office
   space. He knows that there are concerns with what was approved as park space versus their
   new proposed plan. He stated that the schools chose to build in an area not designated on the
   area plan so the densities needed to shift.

    There was discussion about the open space calculations and how the old plan and new plan
    compared.

    Councilor Hancock stated that it’s better not to have all the office space. He was concerned
    with the use of the triangle piece, he would also like to see the higher density closer to SR92,
    and add a buffer along the active adult zone. There was discussion about what the triangle
    piece use could be. Suggestions included a park with pickleball courts, natural landscaping and
    smaller programed amenities. Councilor Hancock would like to see more clarity with the open
    space and restricted open space calculations and amenities.

    The Mayor stated that having the Area Plan in 3 different cities can be difficult and asked the

    space.

    Another Lehi resident also preferred the open space.

    Ab Ford stated that they were expecting to have a large park in this area.

    Lindsay Gamin presented the Council with a document containing comments from other
    residents. She said that adding more residential units will have an impact and the Council needs
    to be prepared for that.

    Meg Ford stated that she bought her property with the understanding that there would be a park
    next to her home.

    Teresa Park stated that they were expecting a park behind their home and noted that if it’s not
    going to be a park, then they need a buffer between the active adult community and current
    residents.

    Erin Ethington stated that they were expecting open space on the triangle piece.

    Rob Ludlow stated that they haven’t met the minimum open space requirement for
    programable space and expressed other concerns with the area plan.

    Caleb Frushtick stated that he hopes Lehi and Highland can work together and maintain the
    character of the area near Highland.

    Pam Neer expressed concerns with traffic and the egress to Highland Boulevard.

    John Spencer stated that he agrees with the other comments and expressed concerns with the
    open space calculation.

10. Consideration of Ordinance #22-2021, a General Plan amendment on 3-acres located at
    2029 West 900 North, changing the designation from VLDRA (very low density
    residential agriculture) to Commercial.
    Jacob Hansen was the applicant. He stated that there is a large interest for tennis and pickleball
    in the area and there is a need for indoor facilities. The amenities would serve all Lehi residents
    and the surrounding communities. They plan to offer lessons, leagues, and competitive
    tournaments.

    The Council expressed concerns with changing the land use, and although they like this use, it
    could open the door for other uses that may not fit the character of the area. There was
    discussion of creating a development agreement for the proposed use. Councilor Koivisto
    stated that she’s hesitant to change the zone.

    Mayor Johnson invited the public to comment.

    Lindsey Gamin stated that she is in support of this proposal.

    Stephanie Jones was also in support.

    There was discussion of how to best allow for the proposed use. It was suggested that the zone
    could remain the same and then create a development agreement to allow for the use.

    Motion:     Councilor Condie moved to table the item and directed staff to draft a development
                agreement for the proposed use. Councilor Koivisto seconded the motion.
