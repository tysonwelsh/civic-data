  City Recorder Lisa Brough presented the Roderick Nephi Business Park Annexation petition to the
council. The petition is for approximately 29 acres of property located south of Owens Corning and is

   Ms. Brough updated the council that the south end of parcel XB00-2592-112 owned by Sandra

PROPERTY BOUNDARY DISPUTE RESOLUTION POLICY ADOPTED:

   Ms. Brough explained to the council that as the city receives various zone change requests and/or
annexation petitions, a common problem has been the misalignment of parcel boundaries. These
misalignments may come in the form of an overlap, underlap, or a disputed property line. The boundary
problems lead to delays in a development application later. To resolve this problem before a
development application is proposed, the staff would like the council to consider adopting a Property
Boundary Dispute Resolution Policy that simply states:
         “Before the Planning Commission or City Council will consider a zone change request or before
an annexation petition will be certified, all boundary line disputes, including but not limited to underlaps
and overlaps, shall legally be resolved by all affected property owners. Proof of resolution of boundary
line disputes must be provided by the zone change requester or annexation petitioner(s) to the Planning
Commission and City Council.”
   Travis Ludlow a local developer stated that he thought this policy would delay their ability to develop
timely. A discussion ensued about the importance of having all the property boundary disputes resolved
before a development is submitted. It can cause a lot of extra time and headache for the city staff to
having to keep going back and forth with the developer.
   Councilor Worwood moved to approve the Property Boundary Dispute Resolution Policy as
presented. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The motion passed on a unanimous vote.

WORK SESSION:

    1. The council reviewed a master plan of the city owned property in the southwest portion near
       the south freeway exit. It is named after the previous owners and called the Greenhalgh
       property. The city paid for a conceptual master plan to help visualize the property for future
       plans and to look for ways to responsibly place utilities and roads. The masterplan showed
       property dedicated for a police station, a quad-plex for ball fields or a large retail pad, and
       approximately 25 lots of varying sizes available for commercial or light industrial usage. The
       council discussed several different options on how best to develop the property. The council
       agreed that it would be best to sell most of the property and to have some say in how it is to be
       developed through a development agreement.

    2.    City Planner Shauna Talbot updated the council about the ideas and revisions the Planning
          Commission has been working on for the Combined Use Zone.

    3. Ms. Talbot reviewed with the council the Planning Commission’s ideas for a new industrial zone
       for light industrial uses (ID-3). The ID-3 zone would be patterned after the traditional industrial
       zone with a restriction on uses. The proposed zone has some minor requirements for
       landscaping and would allow for 75% of the open space to be xeriscaping, consistent with the
       goals of the city’s water conservation plan. He council discussed allowing outside storage for
       the businesses located in this new zone.

    4. City Attorney Kasey Wright gave an update on the construction progress of the Hive. The council
       scheduled a tour of the facility for next work session at 6:00 p.m. on March 22, 2022.

    5. Councilor Worwood moved to adjourn the meeting. Councilor Ostler seconded the motion. The
       motion passed on a unanimous vote.


Meeting adjourned at 8:53 p.m.
