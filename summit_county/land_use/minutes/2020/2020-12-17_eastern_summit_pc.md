Findings of Fact:  
1. The applicant proposes to install an underground water transmission system to replace the 
open ditch irrigation system currently in place in the East Peoa Bench area.  
2. The proposed transmission line will run through multiple properties beginning at the end of 
Stevens Lane in Oakley City.  
3. The proposed transmission line will be entirely within the Agricultural 10 Acre (AG-10) zone.  
4. Underground water transmission systems are a conditional use in the AG-10 zone.  
5. The proposed transmission line will follow existing property lines, fences, and existing access 
roads, with the goal of creating a smaller impact on farmable ground.  
6. The transmission line will be 30” in diameter at its largest point with smaller lines running to 
individual properties.  
7. The proposed pipeline is approximately 17,700 feet long. 13,700 feet of the project is within 
Oakley City limits; the remaining 4,000 feet is in unincorporated Summit County.

8. There are no known wetlands or natural streams along the pipeline corridor.  
9. The application was routed to affected county departments, state agencies and service 
providers.  
10. Currently land along the canal is irrigated through flood irrigation.  
11. The proposed pipeline will change the irrigation system to a sprinkler system.  
12. Sprinkler systems are more water efficient that flood irrigation.  
13. The applicant has received and recorded easements for the pipeline along all affected 
properties.  
 
Conclusions of Law:  
1. The proposed Conditional Use as conditioned complies with all requirements of the Eastern 
Summit County Development Code.  
2. The use is appropriately located with respect to public facilities, it is adjacent to an existing 
county road, and has adequate water.  
3. The Conditional Use as conditioned is consistent with the standards established in chapter 2 of 
the Eastern Summit County Development Code.  
4. The use is not detrimental to public health, safety and welfare, as the roads and public services 
in the area are sufficient to accommodate the increase in intensity of the use.  
5. The effects of any differences in use or scale have been mitigated through careful planning.  
 
Conditions of Approval:  
1. All disturbed areas shall be regraded and revegetated per the requirements of the engineering 
standards in the County Code.  
2. All necessary permits must be obtained, and fees shall be paid prior to the commencement of 
any activity, including but not limited to the Summit County Engineering, Summit County 
Health and the Summit County Building Departments.

2. Discussion regarding the Eastern Summit County General Plan amendments. – Ray 
Milliner, Principal Planner 
 
Director Putt shared the background for this discussion. Starting in January the Planning 
Commission will kick off the work program of prioritized Code Amendments and fold in the 
review of the General Plan. it has been almost eight years since the General Plan was looked 
at. 
Planner Milliner shared the rankings of most concern for the Planning Commission. First 
ranked is affordable housing, which would require a new chapter of the Code. Next priority 
is the creation of a mixed use/multi-family zone. Third priority is clarification of when and 
how a Development Agreement is used. Fourth priority is a review and update of the Use 
Table. Fifth priority is the Junk Ordinance. Sixth priority is a Neighborhood Commercial 
(NC) Zone, which would be located near residences. The next priorities include contractor 
yards, the Recreational Commercial (RC) Zone, campgrounds, and riparian protections of 
stream and water corridors.  
Director Barnes stated the Planning Commission is being asked to read the General Plan 
to find what is missing, and what needs to be changed, kept, or discarded. A future land use 
map or plan is typically included to show where development should be encouraged. 
Should specific statements about affordable housing be included? General observations 
about the General Plan should be shared at the next meeting.
