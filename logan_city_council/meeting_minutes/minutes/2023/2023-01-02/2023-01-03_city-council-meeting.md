Councilmember Jensen said that nationally and on a state level there is a teacher
shortage. What is our situation in regard to teacher retention.

Mr. Schofield answered the benefit of being close to a university, is there are plenty of
(university students), who do their student teaching with us. As a result, we can bring
them in as teachers. However, the difficulty we have with retention is that many teachers
are not here long-term for more than a few years. The other part is there is a 25% staff
shortage who have non-traditional degrees meaning their primary profession is not
teaching. We have been fortunate not to have experienced severe teacher shortages. Some
of the largest districts such as Salt Lake started the year (in Special Education) with 20 to
30 positions empty. The biggest challenge and shortages are teacher assistants, classroom
aids, etc. who help provide supplemental services.

Councilmember Jensen expressed sympathy and concern regarding the tragic violence
that has occurred on school campuses. He asked, what has the Logan School District
done to help prevent, protect, and provide security on campus.

Mr. Schofield responded all of the schools have single entry points. All the doors are
locked during the day except for the main entrance. There are secure vestibules that have
been placed at each of the schools. No one can enter the school unless the office staff
buzzes them in. They have done this to have greater control over who enters a school

building rather than through random points of access. They regularly run drills and
training with the Logan Police Department and Cache County's Sherriff's office. They
have an app called "Duress" that allows any staff member to initiate an alert in an
emergency. The app allows first responders to have access to building maps, where the
emergency is to permit them to respond in a targeted manner. He expressed his gratitude
for their partnership with the police and sheriff's office.

Mr. Schofield emphasized that the next step in building security is if the legislature
provides a one-time fund for building security. The funds if granted will be used to put
locking sensors on all exterior doors in a school building. Currently, the doors all
automatically lock, but the current system does not indicate if a door fails to close
(accidentally is left ajar). The Uvalde tragedy also had the same system, but one of the
doors was accidentally left ajar. The sensors once put in place will the school know if in
fact the door is secure.

The final objective is to create a positive school environment where they can catch and
aid students who are in distress. They are working to create an environment where a
school does not become a target. They are creating an environment where they feel
connected to the school, peers, teachers, etc. In doing so, they are able to decrease the
likelihood of the school becoming a target, since the school becomes a source of positive
connections.

Vice Chair A. Anderson inquired what is the School District’s intention for Hillcrest and
the anticipated need to bond for additional construction for the Logan School District (for
example the middle school).

Mr. Schofield answered the old Hillcrest renovations are temporarily postponed as they
finish other minor projects. It is anticipated that by June, they will depart from Hillcrest
and make plans for demolition. The hope is to finish demolition before winter, but it is
unlikely sodding will be done before school starts. Next spring, there will be an available
open field again.

In terms of future construction projects long-term, they will need to bond again. This is a
few years out. All of their facilities are in good working shape except for Adams and
Wilson Elementary which have some unreinforced masonry which are in the process of
being repaired. They want to expand the early childhood center to bring in a general
special education and preschool population in a single building. They also want to
upgrade the MLMS facilities, both in the parking and drop-off. Currently, the cost of
building a new elementary school is $15 million.

State of the City Address – Mayor Holly Daines

State of the City Address, January 3, 2023

Good evening! I’m delighted to present a State of the City message. I have just
completed my fifth year as Mayor. Our council members have served well and we have
accomplished a great deal in that time! Tonight, I would like to especially recognize and
thank them for their support and their thoughtful comments and input, as well as thank
our outstanding Executive Team and all the employees at the city. Running the city is
truly a team effort. We have a well-seasoned group who work collaboratively, and we
continue to invest in our city and make decisions with long term benefits for our citizens.
We have a shared vision, communicate well, work hard and get things done!

As you know, one of the city council’s primary responsibilities is approving the annual
budget. Starting next month, the Finance Director and I begin working with department
heads on that all-important budget process for the fiscal year that begins July 1, with a
plan to hold budget workshops with the council members in May and move through the
approval process in June, and we welcome their input.

After the stresses of Covid, 2022 was a banner year for the city in many ways. As always,
our first priority is fiscal responsibility. In partnership with the Council, we always
balance our budget, keep significant reserves in all critical funds in case of an
emergency, and invest appropriately in capital and infrastructure projects.

The Finance Department serves all departments city-wide. They keep us on track with
annual budgets, quarterly financial statements, audits, helping develop long term plans

for capital improvements, infrastructure investments, and in building and maintaining
reserves. They again received a Certificate of Achievement, which is, and I quote: “the
highest form of recognition in the area of governmental accounting… for excellence in
preparing the city’s Annual Comprehensive Financial Report demonstrating a
constructive spirit of full disclosure to clearly communicate its financial story.” We take
our stewardship of taxpayer dollars, city revenue and expenditures very seriously.
Transparency is extremely important and we are always happy to answer questions about
how city funds are used. We also appreciate our IT department which is part of Finance.
We continue to right-size our city operations given the significant growth in Logan City
and Cache County. Three years ago our Fire and EMS services were challenged to
respond to the volume of calls generated outside Logan City where we’d historically
served, and we moved ahead with a planned separation. This year, the number of Fire
and EMS calls in the Logan service area, which includes North Logan, Providence and
River Heights, has increased 10% and will equal the total number of calls countywide
before we separated. Because the area we serve is not nearly so spread out, we are
keeping up with our current staffing levels, but that was clearly a good decision given our
growth. The county has increased their capacity as well, and we back each other up as
needed.

The other service we are in the process of right-sizing is garbage collection. For the past
60 years, Logan has provided county-wide garbage collection and landfill service, the
only city in the entire state to do that. Because of growth it was becoming difficult to
manage current and future garbage collection as more of our collection service was
outside Logan City vs. inside Logan. We were dispatching trucks all over the county, on
multiple days, stretching our capacity and increasing our costs and risk. We were
becoming a large, for-profit garbage business rather than serving Logan citizens and
that was not our intent. Change is difficult, and there was initial angst among other
cities, but we have continued to assist them through the process as they determine the
best path forward for them individually or collectively. We will shortly sign an MOU
with the county to formalize our promise to continue to provide garbage collection until
June 2025, if needed, as they transition to other collection options. Logan will continue
to operate the transfer station and green waste site at the Environmental Center and own
and operate the North Valley Landfill for the use of all citizens county-wide.

Another long-standing tradition needs re-evaluation. You heard earlier from
Superintendent Schofield in his report on the school district that after 46 years of a great
partnership at the Rec Center between the city and school district, needs and conditions
have changed and we are mutually discussing a phased plan that will eventually allow
the school district, which owns the property underneath the Rec Center, to utilize the
entire facility to meet their growing needs. The city is discussing how to provide future
recreation services for its citizens, particularly our youth.

Our biggest concern at the present time for Light and Power is the supply of baseload
power available to purchase. A number of factors have combined to create a perfect
storm in the western united states of decreased baseload power and increased cost for
that power. For the past 20 years, power rates have been relatively stable. That is
changing dramatically for a number of reasons. First, demand for power has increased
significantly with population and business growth, a large increase in electric vehicles –
which draw a lot of power – and the switch of many newly constructed housing units to
nearly all electric power for furnaces, water heaters and appliances. The switch to
electric vehicles and electric appliances in homes does improve air quality, which is a
benefit to all. But it requires more power.

hydro power from Lake Powell has been cut in half. Our baseload coal resource has

to our portfolio, but during our summer peak, solar power drops off about the time our
peak demand is kicking in, from 5-9 pm when folks get home from work and turn on air
conditioners, appliances, etc. And we still need to have baseload power to back up our
renewable energy because it is not available all the time. Both residential and
commercial customers have already seen surcharges on their power bills because costs
have gone up significantly. Given the law of supply and demand, the cost for power will
continue to rise for everyone.

We believe new, cleaner technology to provide power will likely come on line in the next
ten years with nuclear power, improvements in battery storage to increase the reliability
of solar power, etc. However, the other challenge for many cities in Utah is the
transmission system. Right now, most of the power statewide comes over the Rocky
Mountain Power transmission system. Any new projects are waiting 5-6 years to get in
line for transmission capacity. In the meantime, we as a city are looking seriously at
purchasing and installing natural gas turbines to produce about 10 MW of power
“behind the meter” as they say. It would be adjacent to one of our substations so we
could control when we use it, and it doesn’t need to go over the transmission lines.
Although we can’t predict natural gas prices, the turbines would at least be a significant
resource which we control as guaranteed baseload power.

In our Community Development department, efforts to streamline government included
moving both landlord and business licensing on-line, better code enforcement tracking
and reporting, and with the construction boom of 2022, staff has provided many, many
design reviews, plan reviews, building permits and inspections, special event permits, as
well as a number of updates to various standards to keep them current and relevant.
These standards are crafted by staff, after lots of research, then vetted through the
Planning Commission then reviewed and approved by the council. That list includes new
standards for Mixed Use Development, Accessory Dwelling Units, Short Term Rentals,
Micro-mobility (otherwise known as e-bikes, scooters, one-wheel devices, etc.), and
parkstrip landscaping in an effort to be more waterwise. They also implemented the
Public Art Master Plan with projects in two different parks.
