2.       A presentation regarding an update from Valley Visioning. (20-111) (1:47:10)

Cameron Martin, Ari Bruening, and Ryan Beck presented. They highlighted background details
on the Valley Visioning process. The process began in 2018 and was a planning tool for the Utah
Valley area to articulate the community’s vision for the future. There has been a robust public
process to involve the public, as well as organizations and other entities in the area which were
major contributors in the community. Presentation only.

3.       A discussion regarding ranked-choice voting. (20-113) (2:02:22)

Stan Lockhart presented. He shared details about ranked-choice voting, which was used in Utah
party conventions in 2020. He highlighted the experience of using RCV in Payson and Vineyard,
which piloted its use in municipal contexts in Utah County. He contrasted the demographics of
the two cities, Vineyard a new and fast-growing community, while Payson is an old legacy city
with pioneer roots. Both cities have very different voting populations, but both had very
successful experiences with RCV. Mr. Lockhart highlighted other benefits of RCV, including
shorter and less expensive campaigns. Presentation only.

4.       A discussion regarding face masks in public with regards to COVID-19. (20-114)
         (0:15:12)

Council Chair George Handley introduced this item, which had been requested by Councilor
David Sewell, due to concerns shared by several Councilors. Mr. Handley welcomed Drs. Ben
Abbott and Chantel Sloan, two researchers from BYU. Dr. Sloan is an associate professor in the
BYU Department of Health Sciences. Both Drs. Sloan and Abbott were collaborating with
several other professionals in the BYU community on modeling data during the pandemic. They
shared details of their research and their primary concerns for the BYU and Provo community.

Dr. Abbott explained that masks were highly effective at reducing the spread of COVID-19.
There are no serious side effects to wearing masks and there is no scientific basis for such
claims. Dr. Sloan emphasized that BYU and Provo needed to be partners in reducing COVID-19
spread. A university community is so interconnected; they expect major outbreaks regardless of
large gatherings. Most outbreaks will occur among people who are compliant, but simply
because of the way viruses work by using the interconnectedness of social networks and within
households. Student housing is unique because it is so dense, and because students who live

Many BYU faculty or staff have children in local schools, spouses who work in the community,
and both students and faculty patronize local businesses. A mask requirement would have a

Brian Jones, Council Attorney, reviewed the powers delegated to City officials by the State. He
noted that the existence of a local health department did not exclude cities from regulating
health-related issues, such as during a pandemic. Mr. Jones noted that the state legislature had
enacted special COVID-19 provisions, one of which stated that cities could not be more
restrictive than the state in their COVID-19 legislation or executive action. The current executive
order stated that cities must obtain prior approval from the Utah Department of Health if a city
desired to adopt a mandatory face covering requirement. Mr. Jones briefly reviewed what
measures other cities in Utah have taken. Councilors discussed the possibilities and what type of
parameters and enforcement would be appropriate.

Councilors asked questions and discussed the matter. Their comments included:
    In response to Councilors’ questions on enforcement and the cost-benefit tradeoff, Drs.
      Sloan and Abbott explained that signage makes an impact. Even without proper
      enforcement, a mandate reduces pockets of non-compliance which perpetuate outbreaks.
      Data has also shown that with a mandate, there is about a 15-20% increase in compliance.
    Several Councilors expressed concern that the City has not acted soon enough and felt that
      the City needed to act as soon as possible. Both Councilors Shannon Ellsworth and Bill
      Fillmore thought an emergency meeting was appropriate under the circumstances; the
      health and safety of its citizens was a primary concern of local government. Councilors
      George Handley and David Harding felt more of a sense of urgency given that BYU and
      public schools were reopening in a matter of weeks. Councilors also felt that other areas of
      the county were looking to Provo as an example and doing nothing set a bad precedent.
    Mayor Michelle Kaufusi shared a prepared statement summarizing her comments. She
      indicated that she was not amenable to enacting a mandate, unless one came from the
      governor or Utah County Health Department, in collaboration with BYU leadership.
    Police Chief Rich Ferguson felt that enforcement was not a realistic means of achieving
      compliance with any mask mandates. He felt education was a better approach.
    Mr. Jones outlined several possible options the Council could pursue. Councilors
      supported issuing a Council statement and scheduling a special meeting.
Motion:       Bill Fillmore moved to schedule a special Council Meeting for Thursday, August
              20, 2020, at which the Council would further discuss and possibly vote on final
              action in the form of a resolution or ordinance regarding masks, and to authorize
              staff to post on Open City Hall a topic requesting community feedback on the
              issue. Seconded by David Harding.
Vote:         Approved 6:0, with David Sewell excused.
