               SUMMARY: These proposed amendments to the Taylorsville City Code address an existing
                        conflict within the code concerning the appeal process for non-administrative
                        conditional use permits and brings consistency to certain terms used in the
                        code.
           BACKGROUND: Recently it was discovered that there is a conflict in the Taylorsville City Code
                       regarding the process for appeals of non-administrative conditional use
                       permits (i.e. conditional uses heard by the Planning Commission). Chapter
                       13.03 Officers, Boards, and Commissions and Chapter 13.34 Appeals and
                       Variances both state that appeals of non-administrative conditional use
                       permits are to be heard by the “appeal authority” (currently identified as a
                       “hearing officer” appointed by the Mayor with the advice and consent of the
                       City Council). Chapter 13.33 Administrative and Non-Administrative
                       Conditional Use Permit, however, states that non-administrative conditional
                       use appeals will be heard by the City Council. In order to bring consistency
                       within the code, staff is proposing to amend Section 13.33.110 Non-
                       administrative Conditional Use Appeals to state that non-administrative CUPs
                       will be heard by the appeal authority.

                             Additionally, Section 13.33.100 identifies the appeal authority for
                             administrative conditional uses (i.e. conditional use permits delegated by code
                             to staff) as the “administrative law judge” rather than “appeal authority” or
                             “hearing officer”, although they are all one in the same. In order to bring
                             consistency to the verbiage utilized in the code, staff proposes to change
                             references to the “administrative law judge” in 13.33.100 to “appeal
                             authority”.

                             Findings:
                             1. Current Taylorsville Code contains contradictions in the required process
                                 for appeals of non-administrative conditional use permits.
                                  2.
                                 Amending Section 13.33.110 to identify the appeal authority as the
                                 appellant for non-administrative conditional use permits will bring
                                 consistency within the code concerning appeals of non-administrative
                                 conditional use permits.
                            3. Altering Section 13.33.100 to change the term administrative law judge
                                 to appeal authority will bring consistency to the verbiage utilized in
                                 Chapter 13.33 regarding conditional use appeals.
      STAFF RECOMMENDATION: Staff recommends approval of Ordinance #20-13 based on the above
                            findings.

13.33.100 ADMINISTRATIVE CONDITIONAL USE APPEALS:
A.   All appeals from decisions of the director regarding administrative conditional use permits
     shall be reviewed by an administrative law judge the appeal authority. The administrative
     law judge shall be appointed by the mayor with the advice and consent of the city council.
B.   An appeal shall be filed in writing with the city recorder within ten (10) calendar days of
     the date that the director issues his written decision.
C.   Except as provided in subsection D of this section, review of decisions of the director shall
     be confined to the record developed by the director.
D.   The administrative law judge appeal authority may hold a public hearing or permit an
     evidentiary review outside the record to determine whether:
     1. An alleged procedural irregularity has occurred that does not appear in the record; or
     2. The proposed use would:
       a. Influence patterns of growth adverse to the integrity of the general plan as
          implemented by this code;
       b. Have a long term detrimental impact on city resources available for capital
          improvements or urban services; or
        c. Undermine the health, safety, or welfare of the surrounding neighborhood or
          community.
If the administrative law judge appeal authority determines that the use does not comply with the
general standards or that the conditions proposed or imposed do not substantially mitigate or
eliminate all anticipated detrimental impacts or effects, it shall then determine if there are
additional or substitute conditions that may be proposed or imposed.
If the administrative law judge appeal authority determines that the detrimental effects of the
proposed conditional use cannot be substantially mitigated or eliminated by the proposed or
imposed conditions in order to achieve compliance with the standards, it may deny the
conditional use. The administrative law judge appeal authority may also remand the appeal to the
director for further consideration. The administrative law judge appeal authority shall be
governed by the general standards set forth herein in making its determination.
E.   Any decision by the administrative law judge appeal authority, except a remand to the
     director, shall be final and subject to the conditions imposed by the director. The
     development code shall not be construed to vest a right to any conditional use except upon
     complete and continued compliance with the conditions finally approved.
F.   Ex parte communication between the administrative law judge appeal authority serving in
     an official capacity in a proceeding regarding an administrative conditional use appeal and
     any party to the appeal is prohibited. (Ord. 12-15, 7-11-2012)


13.33.110: NONADMINISTRATIVE CONDITIONAL USE APPEALS:
A.   All appeals from decisions of the planning commission regarding nonadministrative
     conditional use permits shall be reviewed by the city council appeal authority.
B.   An appeal shall be filed in writing with the city recorder within ten (10) calendar days of the
     date of the planning commission decision at which the permit was approved.
C.   Except as provided in subsection D of this section, review of decisions of the planning
     commission shall be confined to the administrative record developed by the commission.
D.   The city council appeal authority may hold a public hearing or permit an evidentiary review
     outside the planning commission record to determine whether:
     1. An alleged procedural irregularity has occurred that does not appear in the record; or
     2. The proposed use would:
       a. Influence patterns of growth adverse to the integrity of the general plan as implemented
          by this code;
       b. Have a long-term detrimental impact on city resources available for capital
         improvements or urban services; or
       c. Undermine the health, safety, or welfare of the surrounding neighborhood or community.
E.   Hearings may be held by the city council itself or by any council member, hearing examiner,
     or agent appointed by the council.
F.E. The city council appeal authority shall review the record of the planning commission,
     together with additional evidence when received, to determine if the decision was correct.
     If the city council appeal authority determines that the use does not comply with the general
     standards or that the conditions proposed or imposed do not substantially mitigate or
     eliminate all anticipated detrimental impacts or effects, it shall then determine if there are
     additional or substitute conditions that may be proposed or imposed.
     If the city council appeal authority determines that the detrimental effects of the proposed
     conditional use cannot be substantially mitigated or eliminated by the proposed or imposed
     conditions in order to achieve compliance with the standards, it may deny the conditional
     use.
     The city council appeal authority may also remand the appeal to the planning commission
     for further consideration. The city city council appeal authority shall be governed by the
     general standards set forth herein in making its determination.
G.F. Any decision by the city council appeal authority, except a remand to the planning
     commission, shall be final and subject to the conditions imposed by the council appeal
     authority. The development code shall not be construed to vest a right to any conditional use
     except upon complete and continued compliance with the conditions finally approved.
H.F. Ex parte communication between members of the city council appeal authority serving in an
     official capacity in a proceeding regarding an appeal of a nonadministrative conditional use
     appeal and any party to the appeal is prohibited. (Ord. 12-15, 7-11-2012)
