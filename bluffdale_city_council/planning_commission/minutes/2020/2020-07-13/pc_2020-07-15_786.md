4.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on a City
       Initiated Text Amendment Application to Amend Titles 11-2 and 11-22 of the Bluffdale
       City Land Use Ordinances, Revising the City's Sign Code, along with Associated
       Defmitions and Related Administrative Provisions. City of Bluffdale, Applicant.
       (Application 2020-12). Staff Presenter, Grant Crowell.

City Planner/Economic Development Director, Grant Crowell, presented the staff report and stated
that the City Council adopted a Temporary Land Use Ordinance in March 2020. It restiicted new
sign applications for six months, with some exceptions. The reshiction would give the City time to
work on a new Sign Code. Some of the reasons for the development of a New Sign Code included
the fact that the current ordinance would allow 25-foot pole signs as a basic commercial standard.
They would also be allowed every 100 feet. Mr. Crowell reported that other communities use lower
monument-style signs with some architectural aesthetics incorporated into the design. It was
recognized that an area such as 2700 West could become full of tall pole signs if no regulations
were established.

Mr. Crowell stated that the proposed text amendments are substantial in length and scope because
much of the City Code is decades old and, as a result, is antiquated and incomplete. The Utah
Constitution stipulates that with regard to content-based issues, only time, place, and maimer can be
regulated. Therefore, an ordinance needs to be content-neutral. There were also concerns with
economic development, aesthetics, and fairness . Decisions need to be made relative to freeways
and electronic message centers.

The text amendments under consideration constitute a new base model because the previous
ordinance was too out of date. The base model contains text to be reviewed and considered and
contains such things as application procedures, application requirements, administration,
enforcement, and definitions. Mr. Crowell reported that the temporary ordinance expires on
September 11 , 2020. Consequently, if the City does not adopt a new ordinance, it will revert to the
old ordinance.

Mr. Crowell reviewed the proposed ordinance as contained in the meeting packet. Mr. Crowell
noted that five years prior, the City went through a lengthy process to update its billboard
regulations. They are referred to in the City Code as 11011-appmienant signs, billboards, or off-
premise advertising. Mr. Crowell wanted to go on the public record as stating that no changes were
made to the billboard regulations because the previous changes were made in collaboration with
that paiiicular industry. Any changes would entail the legal requirement of returning to the industry
to notify them of the changes. The City felt that the billboard regulations were appropriate, as
currently established.

Electronic signs that do not fall within the scope of non-appurtenant signs will be referred to as
"electronic message centers." He noted that some billboards have been converted into electronic
LED signs.

Mr. Crowell explained that the new chapter is still numbered 11-22. He identified and explained
some of the terms used in the proposed text amendments and provided a brief overview of the

Mr. Crowell reported that he previously dealt with an individual regarding a home occupation sign.
The cun-ent City Code allows for two square feet. Some businesses are legally located in a
residential zone, but they cmTently are not allowed to have a business sign. This is an issue that

Mr. Crowell next addressed the section on temporary signs and banners, which still need to have a
pennitting process. It was noted that political signs have different types of protections.
Mr. Crowell noted that the COVID-19 crisis has led many communities to loosen enforcement of
sign regulations to help businesses remain solvent.

Mr. Crowell reported that special areas of town need to be identified. One such location is Pony
Express Road, which would allow for a 50-foot sign because traffic moves fast along the freeway.
Another location to consider would be Bangerter Highway. In that case, the City could set
standards for height, area, and distance from Bangerter Highway or 2700 West. Consideration also
needed to be given for master-planned sites, such as Woodbury's Gateway Redwood Project.

Mr. Crowell noted that the use of electronic message centers is a huge issue. Decisions need to be
made regarding where they can be located and how they can be designed, including illumination
level and size. There are already some electronic message centers in Bluffdale, some of which are
permitted and some that may not have been permitted. He stated that inconsistencies need to be
reconciled.

Mr. Crowell reviewed the types of signs that would not require a permit. The allowed size of
flagpoles and flags also needs to be detennined as well as window signs. A-frame signs need to be
safe and not obstruct rights-of-way. Signs on delivery trucks also need to be addressed. The City
Attorney would need to give his opinion with regard to free speech rights. Holiday decorations, for
sale, or lease signs on residential properties are exempt. Political signs have considerable latitude.

5.     City Council Reports.

Mr. Crowell reported that the July 8 City Council Meeting was canceled. At the June 24 meeting,
the City Council finalized the budget. They adopted the funeral home text amendment to include
cremation facilities within the existing funeral home definition. They also approved the change
from 20 feet to 10 feet between accessory buildings and dwellings, as recommended by the
Plmming Commission. The City Attorney has also been working on a pending ordinance on
residential facilities for elderly or disabled persons. Work was also being done on home
occupations and business licensing both of which will eventually come to the Pla1U1ing Commission
for review .

In response to C01mnissioner Walston's question regarding the impact of COVID-19 on the City's
budget, Mr. Crowell stated that there are often data lags, so the full impact was still unknown. He
did confirm, however, that the revenue projections are lower. Sales tax collection was also
significantly impacted. Communities with restaurants, movie theaters, live entertaimnent venues,
hotels, travel, and tourism-based businesses were hit especially hard.

Concerning the City budget, the City Council had to use some fund balance. In addition, some
positions have not been filled or were not converted from part-time to full-time. It was noted that
there is still a lot of building permit activity. Mr. Crowell explained that the long-term impact is
impossible to predict.
