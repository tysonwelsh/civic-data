Proposal 
 
The applicant is requesting a Conditional Use Permit for the Run-A-Muk Trailhead 1 & 2. The 
Conditional Use Permit would bring the existing Run-A-Muk Trailhead 1 into conformance. As 
part of the Conditional Use Permit, the applicant is also requesting to improve an additional 
Run-A-Muk Trailhead 2 parking area (30 parking stalls) which would include a restroom 
facility. The new parking area would be located ~1,000 feet east of the existing Run-A-Muk 
parking area.  
 
In addition to the Run-A-Muk Trailheads, the applicant is also requesting a Conditional Use 
Permit Amendment for the previously approved RTS Trailhead (across the street from the  
existing Run-A-Muk Trailhead). The CUP Amendment would allow for the installation of 
restroom facilities.

Background 
 
The Run-A-Muk off-leash dog park, trail system and trailhead (15 parking stalls) were improved 
in ~2014. The existing trailhead was improved without a land use permit as part of the 
community wide trail improvements for the Run-A-Muk trail system. While Community Wide 
Trails are Allowed uses per the Snyderville Basin Development Code, Trailheads, Major (more 
than 10 parking stalls) are reviewed through a Conditional Use Permit. Since the original Run-A-
Muk Trailhead did not receive land use approval, the applicant is requesting a Conditional Use 
Permit for both the existing and proposed Run-A-Muk Trailheads.  
 
According to the applicant; “In 2020, RAM, accounted for 70% of all hiking in the District. RTS 
consists of an extensive and popular trail system serving both hikers and MTB. While there is 
access to 2 smaller parking areas, these existing parking lots are often full, prompting some trail 
users to park on the shoulder of Olympic Parkway BLVD during peak usage. This project may 
help reduce on street parking and provide increased opportunity for the recreational benefits of 
the community. SBSRD has been awarded a RAPREC grant for the construction of this project if 
approved.  
 
The (2) vault toilets would be a much-needed addition in this area and eliminate the portable 
toilet that is located at the RTS trailhead. The proposed restrooms are precast concrete vault 
toilet of approximately 84 SQFT. Details provided in attached brochure.

Hours of operation for the trail head is from sunrise to sunset. This would eliminate the need for 
additional lighting in the area.  
 
Snow storage would be provided outside of the parking stalls.  
 
The surface area of the parking stalls would be asphalt. Storm water retention would be 
provided as required by Summit County Engineering Dept.  
 
There will be no changes in existing landscaping other than removal of vegetation in proposed

species mix.”   
 
The existing RTS Trailhead was approved through a Conditional Use Permit in 2012. The

To whom it may concern, 
 
This is a request for a conditional use permit (CUP) for property located in the Snyderville Basin area.  
The property is owned by Summit County but managed by Snyderville Basin Special Recreation District 
(SBSRD).  This project has received partial funding from the RAP/REC grant program. 
  
This proposal includes a parking area with restroom (vault toilet) at Run a Muk (RAM) and an additional 
restroom (vault toilet) at the existing RTS trailhead, which is currently serviced by a portable restroom.  
These properties are located along Olympic Parkway Blvd in Kimball’s Junction. 
 
The proposed project would include a new paved 30 stall parking area/trailhead in RAM, accessed from 
Olympic Parkway Blvd.  A restroom would be located at this new parking area.  A new hiking only 
adaptive trail would be created to connect to the RAM existing trails.  There will be an additional 
restroom (vault toilet) installed at the existing RTS parking area, replacing the current portable 
restroom.  
  
The RAM hiking only trail system and off lease dog area is SBSRD’s most visited public area in the 
district’s boundaries.  Currently, limited parking areas are often overwhelmed by trail users and can 
creates issues with street parking along Olympic Parkway Blvd.  This proposed parking could help 
eliminate those issues. 
 
In conversations with The Utah Athletic Foundation, these parking areas would be of benefit to the 2034 
Winter Olympics by providing a base for a larger overall transportation plan for the events. 
 
Hours of operation would be from sunrise to sunset eliminating the need for addition lighting at the 
trailhead(s). 
 
Snow storage would be provided at each end of the new paved parking area.  
  
Storm water retention/detention will be provided on as required by Summit County Engineering Dept.

Proposal 
 
The applicant Todd Hansen, on behalf of Park City School District, is requesting approval for the 
Trailside Elementary School Expansion Conditional Use Permit (CUP), a ~5,700 sq. ft. addition to 
the Trailside Elementary School, located at 5700 Trailside Dr (Parcel SS-42-C-X), Trailside 
Neighborhood, Summit County, UT. The proposal includes but is not limited to additional 
classroom and support space for prekindergarten functions, wraparound community service 
spaces, and mechanical/electrical work to support the improvements. The proposal also 
includes a ~4,300 sq. ft. interior remodel to accommodate additional prekindergarten space. 
Lastly, the proposal includes revising drop-off/pick-up patterns and revising the back-of-house 
access to improve student safety.

Background 
 
Trailside Elementary School was constructed in 2002 and is ~78,650 sq. ft. in size.  
  
Analysis and Findings 
 
State Code Requirements and Exemptions 
 
Utah Code 17-27a-305(1.a) states: 
 
Each county, municipality, school district, charter school, local district, special service 
district, and political subdivision of the state shall conform to any applicable land use 
ordinance of any county when installing, constructing, operating, or otherwise using any 
area, land, or building situated within a mountainous planning district or the unincorporated 
portion of the county, as applicable. 
 
Title 17, Section 27a-305(2) of the Utah Code allows a county to:  
 
• Subject a school to standards within each zone pertaining to setback, height, bulk and 
massing regulations, off-site parking, curb cut, traffic circulation, and construction staging  
• Impose regulations upon the location of a project that are necessary to avoid 
unreasonable risks to health or safety.

Under Title 17, Section 27a-305(3), a county may not: 
 
• Impose requirements for landscaping, fencing, aesthetic considerations, construction 
methods or materials, additional building inspections, county building codes, building use 
for educational purposes, or the placement or use of temporary classroom facilities on 
school property. 
• Require school to participate in the cost of any roadway or sidewalk, unless reasonably 
necessary for the safety of the children or to connect to existing rights of way.  
• Require the school to pay for county building inspectors.  
 
Summit County Process and Required Permits 
 
Section 10-3-1 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code states: No development activity may 
be undertaken within the unincorporated area of the Snyderville Basin unless all development 
permits applicable to the proposed development or subdivision of land are issued in accordance 
with the provisions of this title.  
 
Per Section 10-2-10 of the Snyderville Basin Development Code, schools are processed through 
a Conditional Use Permit in the Rural Residential (RR) Zone.  
 
Section 10-2-18(B.3) of the Snyderville Basin Development Code requires that any new public or 
quasi-public project with ten thousand (10,000) square feet or more of gross floor area go 
through the Master Planned Development (MPD) process.  
 
Since this request for the expansion is less than ten thousand (10,000) square feet only the 
Conditional Use Permit process is required, and the Snyderville Basin Planning Commission is 
the Land Use Authority.

Site Design Narrative - Trailside Elementary School  
  
a. Project Neighborhood Connectivity 
Trailside Elementary is an existing school built in 2001-2002 and is 78,650 SF. Trailside 
Elementary School is a Pre-K-5 elementary school serving approximately 370 students, 37 
teachers and 6 support staff. It is located at 5700 Trailside Drive west of US 189. 
Neighborhoods it serves include: Powderwood, Kimball Junction, Spring Creek, Black Hawk, 
*Silver Creek *(Silver Creek Village is not within the boundaries of Park City School 
District, and instead falls within South Summit School District) Canyon Creek, Bitner Road, 
Goshawk Ranch, Redstone, Silver Summit, Highland Estates, Park Ridge Estates, and part of 
Old Ranch Road. The proposed project includes a 5,743 SF addition to the existing school and 
will be located at the west end of the school, attaching directly to the existing west wing.  
Extensive work has been performed by Park City School District over the past 12 years to 
evaluate the adequacy of the educational facilities within the district. As part of that process a 
Master Plan was developed addressing 6 schools within the district. The Master Plan included 
identification of critical needs which correlated to the overall educational goals of the district. 
This result of this work identified the need for additional classroom and support space for Pre-
kindergarten functions, Wraparound community service spaces, improved drop off patterns, 
rework back of house access to improve student safety and mechanical/electrical work to 
support the improvements. 
 (1) The property fronts Trailside Drive and is accessed by both vehicular and pedestrian 
traffic, from Silver Summit Parkway to the east Highland Drive and North Old Ranch 
Road to the west to Trailside Drive. Access to the school will remain on the existing 
southwest parking lot and southwest student drop off. The existing southwest parking lot 
and delivery access will be modified to accommodate the new building addition and 
added Pre-kindergarten playground. Students drop off for the Pre-kindergarten addition 
will remain in the southwest parking lot and designated drop-off lanes accessed off 
Trailside Drive. 
(2) The are no new streets proposed for this redevelopment and addition to the existing 
school.  
(3) Vehicle, pedestrian, and bicycle routes will remain unchanged from the existing 
access to the school from Trailside Drive. The existing sidewalk from the subdivision 
north of the school will remain unchanged. Hardscape will be extended between the 
existing school parking/drop off and an additional entrance added for the new Pre-
kindergarten. The primary school entrance will remain at the west and a secondary entry 
to the preschool will be located on the southwest side accessed from the existing 
parking /drop off.   
b. Availability of Neighborhood Facilities and Services 
The Park City area and surrounding Summit County has over 40 miles of multi-use pathways. 
There are existing pathways that connect Trailside Elementary School to virtually all retail, trail 
heads and parks. This building addition does not affect or change the existing connecting 
pathways in and out of the school.

1.  DIG OR TRENCH A FOUR INCH WIDE BY SIX INCH DEEP TRENCH,  THE LENGTH OF THE SILT FENCE
2.  ROLL OUT SILT FENCE MATERIAL ALONG THE FRONT OF THE TRENCH SUCH THAT THE STAKES WILL BE ON THE DOWNSTREAM SIDE

3.  STARTING AT ONE END, DRIVE THE FIRST STAKE AT LEAST 10 INCHES INTO THE GROUND.  NOTE THAT THE STAKE MUST BE DRIVEN
FAR ENOUGH INTO THE GROUND SO THAT 8 - 12 INCHES OF MATERIAL REMAINS IN THE GROUND.
4.  AT THE NEXT STAKE, PULL THE MATERIAL TAUT BEFORE DRIVING THE SECOND STAKE INTO THE GROUND.  NOTE THAT THE STAKE
MUST BE DRIVEN FAR ENOUGH INTO THE GROUND SO THAT 8 - 12 INCHES OF MATERIAL REMAINS IN THE GROUND.
5.  REPEAT STEP 4 UNTIL THE STAKES ARE DRIVEN INTO THE GROUND.
6.  WHEN ATTACHING TWO LENGTHS OF FENCE TOGETHER, DO THE FOLLOWING:
6.1 PLACE THE END POST OF THE SECOND FENCE INSIDE THE END POST OF THE FIRST FENCE.
6.2 ROTATE BOTH POSTS AT LEAST 180 DEGREES IN A CLOCKWISE DIRECTION, TO CREATE A TIGHT SEAL WITH THE FENCE
MATERIAL.
6.3 DRIVE BOTH POST INTO THE GROUND SO THAT 8 - 12 INCHES OF MATERIAL REMAINS IN THE GROUND.

FEATURES & SPECIFICATIONS
 INTENDED USE 
Common architectural look, with clean rectilinear shape, of the WDGE LED was designed to 
blend with any type of construction, whether it be tilt-up, frame or brick. Applications include 
commercial offices, warehouses, hospitals, schools, malls, restaurants, and other commercial 
buildings.
 CONSTRUCTION 
The single-piece die-cast aluminum housing integrates secondary heat sinks to optimize 
thermal transfer from the internal light engine heat sinks and promote long life. The driver 
is mounted in direct contact with the casting for a low operating temperature and long life. 
The die-cast door frame is fully gasketed with a one-piece solid silicone gasket to keep out 
moisture and dust, providing an IP66 rating for the luminaire.
 FINISH 
Exterior painted parts are protected by a zinc-infused Super Durable TGIC thermoset 
powder coat finish that provides superior resistance to corrosion and weathering. A tightly 
controlled multi-stage process ensures a 3 mils thickness for a finish that can withstand 
extreme climate changes without cracking or peeling. Standard Super Durable colors 
include dark bronze, black, natural aluminum, sandstone and white. Available in textured 
and non-textured finishes.
 OPTICS 
Well crafted reflector optics allow the light engine to be recessed within the luminaire, 
providing visual comfort, superior distribution, uniformity, and spacing in wall-mount 
applications. The WDGE LED has zero uplight and qualifies as a Nighttime Friendly™ 
product, meaning it is consistent with the LEED® and Green Globes™ criteria for 
eliminating wasteful uplight.
 ELECTRICAL 
Light engine consists of high-efficacy LEDs mounted to metal-core circuit boards to 
maximize heat dissipation and promote long life (up to L91/100,000 hours at 25°C). The 
electronic driver has a power factor of >90%, THD <20%. Luminaire comes with built in 
6kV surge protection, which meets a minimum Category C low exposure (per ANSI/IEEE 
C62.41.2). Fixture ships standard with 0-10v dimmable driver.
 INSTALLATION 
A universal mounting plate with integral mounting support arms allows the fixture to 
hinge down for easy access while making wiring connections. The 3/8” Architectural Wall 
Spacer (AWS) can be used to create a floating appearance or to accommodate small 
imperfections in the wall surface. The ICW option can be used to mount the luminaire 
inverted for indirect lighting in dry and damp locations. Design can withstand up to a 1.5 G 
vibration load rating per ANSI C136.31.
 LISTINGS 
CSA certified to U.S. and Canadian standards. Luminaire is IP66 rated. PIR options are 
rated for wet location. Rated for -40°C minimum ambient. DesignLights Consortium® (DLC)

be DLC Premium qualified or DLC qualified. Please check the DLC Qualified Products List
