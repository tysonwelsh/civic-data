        1.2      Mayor’s Report

      Mayor Kristie Overson reported that she had just come from a ribbon-cutting for the Utah
Hispanic Chamber of Commerce which was now located in Taylorsville. It was a great opportunity
to support local Hispanic businesses.

Another new business had recently opened in Taylorsville which was Smart Staffing Solutions.
Their business was matching people to jobs. The mayor explained that it was a very dynamic
business with other locations in Utah. She was excited that they had chosen to add a site in
Taylorsville.

The ChamberWest gala had been held the previous week where Hidden Peaks Coffee of
Taylorsville won the Small Business of the Year award. She greatly appreciated the city’s
partnership with ChamberWest.

A quarterly staff meeting was held the previous month and city staff learned about cybersecurity
in order to keep city data secure. It was a great learning experience for everyone involved.

Salt Lake County Mayor Jenny Wilson had recently invited Mayor Overson and other female
mayors in the Salt Lake Valley to attend an Elizabeth Smart Foundation seminar. It was a very
hands-on presentation of self-defense techniques which the mayor found to be both enlightening
and intimidating.

Seventeen new sculptures were recently unveiled at Centennial Plaza as part of the Plaza + Art
program. Artists essentially lent the pieces to the city to display for one year. She appreciated the
Council seeing the wisdom of providing funding to bring art to the community.

A local church group had recently invited Mayor Overson to attend a pancake breakfast and answer
questions about city activities. She enjoyed these opportunities to have informal dialogue about
the city with interested residents.

The Annual Volunteer Appreciation event was held the previous week and she explained it was a
wonderful opportunity to mingle and celebrate volunteerism in Taylorsville.

The Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) was holding their annual conference that week at
the Salt Palace Convention Center. Mayor Overson and several councilmembers were attending
the three-day event, and it was a great opportunity to network with their counterparts in other cities.

There were only two more Starry Nights on the Plaza events this season and she encouraged
everyone to stop by the following Friday where there would be a kids market and a Touch-a-Truck
event.

Mayor Overson explained that the city administration was concerned about parking in the city, and
she knew that the city council had concerns as well. She felt that it was time to have a serious, in-
depth discussion about potential solutions with the police department, code enforcement, the
Council and city administration. She asked the councilmembers to review the code and
contemplate potential solutions in anticipation of that discussion.

The mayor invited Howard Wilson to step forward and meet her at the podium. She presented Mr.
Wilson with a plaque expressing the city’s appreciation for his many years of service on the Arts
Council.

       3.4       West Valley Animal Services – Melanie Bennett

       West Valley Animal Services Director Melanie Bennett updated the Council on shelter
activity over the second quarter of 2023.

She explained that 735 animal licenses had been issued in the city thus far in 2023, in addition to
131 Millrace dog park tags. Each of the three office technicians had assisted more than 600 people
per month from January through March and over 700 per month during April through July.
Response times had continued to improve once officers were given specific areas of
responsibilities.

A total of 333 animals had been impounded in Taylorsville during the 2nd quarter as compared to
161 impounded during the 1st quarter. Animal adoptions totaled 159 during the quarter. Ms.
Bennett pointed out that the standard of care for a healthy animal (and not all in their custody were
healthy) was fifteen minutes per animal per day. Their monthly average was 165.10 animals and
there were typically three technicians on duty during the day working ten-hour shifts. That number
of animals required 13.76 hours per day for care but again, the technicians only worked ten-hour

5.      PLANNING MATTERS

        5.1      Discussion Item – Review of the Taylorsville Land Development Code Update
                 Project (Part 1) – Jim Spung

       Senior Planner Jim Spung reviewed the development standards in Chapter 13.04—
specifically, parking and loading, landscaping and buffering, and fences and walls. He reminded
the councilmembers that the goals of this project were to:
    • Streamline development review procedures and increase predictability;
    • Consider the needs of the City both now and in the future;
    • Align the Land Development Code with General Plan and State Statutes;
    • Enable great design and a diverse mix of land uses; and
    • Improve user-friendliness through logical structure, graphics, tables and illustrations.
