          1. 2   Mayor' s Report


         Mayor Kristie Overson reported that over 300 members of the Utah chapter of the American
Planning Association attended a conference next door at the Mid Valley Performing Arts Center
with some time also spent at the Centennial Plaza campus. A close eye was kept on the parking

situation during this two-day event, but everyone was able to locate a place to park.

Salt Lake County had also held a press conference on the steps of the Centennial Plaza
Amphitheatre, signing a proclamation encouraging the business community, local civic groups,
and community institutions to make Salt Lake County a welcoming place. She was delighted that
the County had chosen Taylorsville from which to deliver the message.

The final Movie in the Park for the year was held the previous Saturday in the new amphitheater.
Mayor Overson expressed appreciation to the Youth Council and the Parks and Recreation
Committee for their work on the event.


September 11th had been a day of service around the valley, and she was aware that many people
participated in a variety of projects.

         5. 1    Ordinance No. 21- 13 An Ordinance of the City of Taylorsville approving a text
         amendment       to Taylorsville Municipal Code Sections 13. 07.020, 13. 08. 020, 13. 09.030,

           13. 10. 020 and 13. 11. 010 related to Accessory Dwelling Units ( ADUs), extended living
          areas, guesthouses, and corresponding definitions— Wayne Harper




          Economic and Community Development Director Wayne Harper explained that recent
legislation had changed how Accessory Dwelling Units ( ADUs) were defined in State code. He
clarified that these were only units that were contained within an existing single-family dwelling.
It did not include tiny homes, mobile homes, or anything else detached from the existing structure.

The new State code would go into effect on October 1, so time was of the essence to remain
compliant with the law. He explained that:
          ADUs were not for short-term rentals ( Airbnb, for example)

          Property owner was required to live on-site
          Business license was required

          Notice must be recorded at the County
          City could still prohibit ADUs on lots smaller than 6,000 square feet and prohibit
          separate utility meters


Mr. Harper explained that the proposed ordinance was being used as a model for other
municipalities. Requiring that a notice be recorded at the Salt Lake County Clerk' s office would
ensure that if/when the property was sold in the future, the new property owner would be made
aware that there had been an ADU permit in place but that they would need to apply for a new
permit if they wished to continue the ADU status.

A conditional use permit process would no longer be required, nor would there be a maximum
amount of floor space.         The cost of the permit would be reduced for those property owners who
were     taking   part   in the Good Landlord program. No permit would be required if the ADU was
occupied by family members. There were two extensive changes to the current process which
were that 1) the definition and everything dealing with extended living areas had been repealed
and folded into the state definition of ADU which would make compliance much simpler; and 2)
the definition of" guesthouse" had been modified and limited.
                                                            The Planning Commission had
forwarded a unanimous recommendation for the City Council to approve Ordinance 21- 13 and
staff recommended the Council' s approval as well.


Regarding the prohibition against short-term rentals, Councilmember Armstrong asked what was
considered short term. He also wished to know if an ADU required a separate
                                                                       r    entrance from that
of theprimary dwelling. Mr. Harper responded that anything less than a 30- day stay was short
term and that a separate entrance was not a requirement under the proposed ordinance.


Councilmember Armstrong inquired about grandfathering in existing non-conforming properties.
Mr. Harper responded that it was hoped those individuals would be willing to become compliant.
His department was currently working with Code Enforcement to identify those properties.

City Administrator John Taylor indicated there would need to be some internal discussions about
doing everything possible to make it easy for property owners to apply for the permit — even
potentially waiving licensing fees. However, potential penalties for non- compliance would need

In response to an inquiry from Councilmember Harker, Mr. Harper explained that a single
bedroom would not be considered an ADU.          There would need to be a separate bathroom and
kitchen facilities to meet that standard as well as an additional parking spot on site. Therefore, an
inspection would be necessary to ensure that all conditions were met.          Additionally, a single
bedroom could not be rented out.


Chair Cochran asked where tiny homes or trailers fit into this situation. Mr. Harper responded that
the proposed ordinance only addressed living space within an existing residential structure. There
were some ( although very few) lots within the city where guest houses were permitted, but they
could still only be occupied by family and could not be rented out.

Mayor Overson wished to reiterate that what Mr. Harper and his department had done was to
ensure that Taylorsville remained compliant with the new action taken by the state and now we
must  figure out how to work together to help people be compliant. This was not a hammer but
instead, a tool. Various city departments would be working together to ensure these situations
were handled appropriately.
