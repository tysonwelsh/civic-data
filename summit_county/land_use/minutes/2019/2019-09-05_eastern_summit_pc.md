Chair Clyde stated this application has not really drawn any public attention.

 Findings of Fact:  
1. The property is in the Agriculture -80 (AG-80) zone.  
2. The applicant, Echo Divide Wind LLC is requesting a conditional use permit for a Wind 
Power Generation Facility Greater Than 45 Feet in Height.  
3. Section 11-3-16 note 1 of the Eastern Summit County Development Code states “Wind power 
generation facilities greater than 45 feet in height are exempt from the ridgeline prohibition 
provisions in subsections 11-2-4I and J of this title provided it meets all of the conditional uses 
permit requirements in section 11-4-7 of this title.”  
4. The use is a conditional use permit in the AG-80 zone.  
5. The applicant is requesting a 100-megawatt wind power generation facility that would house 
up to 39 turbines.  
6. The proposed location is on a 5,884-acre site along the Utah/Wyoming boarder south of 
Interstate 80 approximately 7 miles from Evanston.  
7. Each turbine would be approximately 500 feet tall, measured from the apex of each blade and 
344 feet to the top of the turbine.  
8. The radius of each windmill is approximately 400 feet.  
9. The total electrical output would be approximately 100 megawatts, which is enough electricity 
to power nearly 22,000 homes per year.  
10. Access to the site will come from existing roadways and entry points.  
11. There is a network of roads throughout the property which the applicant will expand to 
accommodate the construction equipment necessary to build the turbines, then reclaim and 
revegetate once construction is over (reclaim to their original width/state).  
12. The applicant conducted a public open house for the project, independent from the normal 
County noticing requirements. It was held on June 27, 2019, in the Ledges Building in Coalville.  
13. Notice for the open house was sent to all property owners within a 2-mile radius of the site, 
and advertisements were placed in the Park Record, Uintah County Herald (Evanston 
newspaper) and the Summit County News. The open house was attended by approximately 20 
people.  
14. On July 18, 2019 the Planning Commission conducted a work session and provided the 
applicant with feedback regarding the proposed use.  
15. The Eastern Summit County General Plan states, “Work with appropriate public agencies to 
permit and approve development of alternative energy.”  
16. The proposed use is the first such use in Summit County. It is consistent with the stated 
sustainability goals of the County Council and the goals to promote energy conservation in the 
Eastern Summit County General Plan.  
17. There are wetlands and natural streams on the property.  
18. This proposal is conditioned to require the applicant to stay out of stream and wetland areas.  
19. After the initial construction phase, the use will neither hinder nor eliminate any existing 
agricultural operations in the area.

20. The use will not hinder the existing irrigation systems in the area.  
21. The site has adequate septic and water capabilities to handle the use.  
22. The impact of the addition on County infrastructure as it relates to traffic volume, and other 
public services will be minimal.  
23. No changes to County roads and infrastructure are anticipated as part of this application.  
24. The application was routed to affected county departments, state agencies and service

Conclusions of Law:  
1. The proposed Conditional Use as conditioned complies with all requirements of the Eastern 
Summit County Development Code.  
2. The use is appropriately located with respect to public facilities, it is adjacent to an existing 
county road, and has adequate water and septic.  
3. The modification as conditioned is consistent with the standards established in chapter 2 of the 
Eastern Summit County Development Code.  
4. The use is not detrimental to public health, safety and welfare, as the roads and public services 
in the area are enough to accommodate the increase in intensity of the use.  
5. The effects of any differences in use or scale have been mitigated through careful planning.  
 
Conditions of Approval:  
1. Final approval of the location and number of wind turbines on the site shall be delegated to 
staff. If there are any issues or conflicts between staff and the applicant relating to the final 
approval of the location and number of turbines on the site, final approval shall be by the 
Planning Commission.  
2. The maximum number of turbines allowed on the property is 39.  
3. All wind turbines shall be located within the areas featured on the map titled “Echo Divide 
Wind Energy Project Site Plan” on page 30 of the applicant package.  
4. No modifications to this approved Conditional Use Permit are permitted without prior 
approval by the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission. 
5. All necessary permits must be obtained, and fees paid prior to the commencement of any 
construction activity, including but not limited to any Summit County Engineering Department 
and Summit County Building Department permits.  
6. After the turbines are installed, all grading and associated cut and fill areas shall be 
revegetated and contoured to blend into the natural terrain.  
7. A weed management plan shall be submitted as part of the re-vegetation process.  
8. Transmission lines shall be located along existing roads to the greatest extent possible, to limit 
grading and site disturbance.  
9. Lighting of the turbines shall be limited to safety requirements of Federal and State agencies. 
No lighting above what is required by these agencies is allowed.  
10. Approval of this Conditional Use Permit shall expire one (1) year following the date of the 
Planning Commission’s written approval, unless the conditional use permit activity has 
commenced or unless the applicant submits a written request for an extension prior to expiration, 
and the extension is granted.

Findings of Fact:  
1. The Promontory Development Agreement was approved on January 2, 2001 and was 
subsequently amended in August 2005. It provided for 885 estate lots as part of a base density 
and 716 incentive density lots.  
2. Promontory Investments LLC and Promontory Development LLC are the owners of Parcels 
NS-1 (250.27 acres), NS-2 (298.83 acres), NS-3 (199.05 acres), and SS-23 (363.30 acres).  
3. The applicant, Shawn Potter representing Promontory Development LLC, is requesting to 
receive approvals for six (6) plats for the Promontory Pinnacle Subdivision.  
4. Phase 1 accounts for 16 single family lots, Phase 2 accounts for 12 single family lots, Phase 3 
accounts for 9 single family lots, Phase 4 accounts for 8 single family lots, Phase 5 accounts for 
8 single family lots, and Phase 6 accounts for 11 single family lots, totaling 64 lots.  
5. The applicant is also proposing to plat an ancillary parcel in Phase 1 which will accommodate 
a future clubhouse for the Pinnacle Subdivision.  
6. The subject development is located on Portions of Parcels NS-1 (250.27 acres), NS-2 (298.83 
acres), NS-3 (199.05 acres), and SS-23 (363.30 acres), with 126.91 acres being used towards the 
single family lots at an average size of 1.98 acres. 3.83 acres are being used towards the 
clubhouse parcel.  
7. On February 14, 2019, the applicant requested a Development Agreement Amendment which 
would have allowed for increasing of the home size from 8,000 sq. ft. to 22,000 sq. ft. on Lots 1-
36 of the Pinnacle Subdivision. The proposal also included allowance of Accessory Dwelling 
Units which was previously prohibited by the Promontory Design Guidelines.  
8. The applicant withdrew the Development Agreement Amendment request on August 1, 2019 
and now proposes to conform with the existing Promontory Development Agreement and Design 
Guideline requirements.  
9. The density is established by the Promontory Development Agreement pool of density.  
10. Lots 1-36 are proposed to be Estate Base Density lots and lots 37-64 are proposed to come 
out of the Incentive density pool.  
11. The development parameters for this project are specifically set forth in the Promontory 
Development Agreement.  
12. On May 2, 2019, the Planning Commission performed a general visit of the Promontory 
Development which included the Pinnacle Subdivision.  
13. During the Planning Commission discussions regarding the now withdrawn Development 
Agreement Amendment, specific lots were identified as having potential to ridgeline.  
14. The applicant has included additional restriction notes on the plat to identify these lots. The 
developer proposed to limit heights to 28 feet for any structure that may ridgeline.

15. Slopes over 30% have been identified in this subdivision.  
16. The applicant has identified building pads that generally avoid slopes over 30%.  
17. The applicant has included the following slope restriction note on the plats; Section 11-2-
4(F) of the Eastern Summit County Development Code prohibits development on natural grade
