  APPLICABLE DEVELOPMENT CODE REGULATIONS

Section 06.010 Purpose and Intent.
The intent of the Planned Community Zone and this Chapter is to:

A. Allow the City to encourage and facilitate more detailed and specific planning and analysis for certain areas of
   the City.
B. Establish provisions and requirements which enable the City to address unique areas of the City or where other
   characteristics exist that warrant a comprehensive set of land use policies and standards which will encourage
   an efficient and imaginative development pattern.
C. Provide an opportunity for flexibility from the constraints of traditional zoning and allow for development on a
   performance basis. Higher density and intensity of uses may be achieved as higher quality development and
   performance standards are implemented within the zone.
D. Provide a consolidated and structured framework for the processing of preliminary and final subdivision
   plat(s), preliminary and final site plans, and other permits and licenses required by this Code.

An application for approval of a Planned Community Zone is a request by the applicant for additional flexibility
beyond that allowed by the traditional zones within the City. It is the sole responsibility and burden of the appli-
cant to convince the Planning Commission and City Council that the proposed Planned Community Zone is prefer-
able to traditional zoning. Approval for use of the Planned Community Zone lies at the discretion of the City
Council.

Section 06.020 Planned Community Description.
Each Planned Community Zone should include a cohesive mixture of land uses, including commercial and/or business
park uses, which will provide services and employment opportunities to residents within the community. Residential
uses should provide a range of housing types and densities to accommodate a variety of age and income groups and
residential preferences, so that the community’s demographic diversity is maintained. Commercial uses should focus on
retail services for the residents of the community and must include a unifying design theme which compliments the
residential areas. Residential and commercial land uses should be integrated to promote a walkable, sustainable com-
munity.
Development should promote creative lot configuration with usable public and private recreation areas, parks, trails and
open space with assurance of maintenance. Preservation of open space and critical environmental areas is also highly
encouraged through the use of clustering and other innovative development options where a standard lot pattern is not
practical or desirable due to physical constraints.

Section 06.030 Design Compatibility.
Development within a Planned Community Zone should promote attractive, unified architectural design. Development
should be planned as one complex land use with a common architectural design theme that provides variety within a
context of architectural compatibility, rather than an aggregation of individual, unrelated buildings located on separate,
unrelated lots. In order to ensure that development within the Planned Community Zone is compatible and meets the
purposes of this Chapter, the Planning Commission may recommend and the City Council may require that certain de-
sign guidelines be employed as part of the approval of an Area Plan for a PC Zone. The design guidelines may include,
but are not limited to, architectural controls, colors, materials, building mass, innovative design of buildings and access,
and any other features deemed appropriate by the City Council. As part of the design guidelines for any Planned Com-
munity Zone each single family detached or attached dwelling shall include, as a minimum standard, an enclosed one
(1) car garage.


  REPORT ANALYSIS

Gardner-Plumb is requesting an amendment to the Skye Area Plan Table of Uses. The Table lists various permitted,
conditional and unapproved uses for the area, although “Grocer” and “General Merchandise Store” are not currently
included. The applicant proposes to explicitly define and permit “Grocer” and “General Merchandise Store” as ap-
proved uses.

This amendment provides greater clarity for prospective tenants and the City regarding permitted uses, although these
uses would previously have been permitted under Retail Sales, General. These uses align with the city’s vision for the
area and provide valuable services to the community.

While these uses may still be permitted without this clarification, the applicant desires to encourage the construction of
these facilities in the area by explicitly mentioning them in the Table.

The Development Review Committee (DRC) had no redline comments regarding this request.


  SCOPE OF APPROVAL

Area Plan Amendments are a legislative item with a large amount of discretion by the Planning Commission. They
may consider if these changes will align with the City’s vision and the goals of the Area Plan. In this case it is not
changing the uses that would be allowed, just explicitly stating them.

The public can comment on the proposed Area Plan Amendment. Comments directed at potential impacts of the
change and how the Area Plan could address those concerns are the most helpful.


  POSSIBLE MOTIONS AND FINDINGS

The Planning Commission may move to recommend approval or denial of the proposed area plan. Any motion
should include findings. Possible findings include:

    1.   The proposed amendment to the table of uses will not change the uses that are permitted in the Skye Planned
         Community.
    2.   The proposed area plan does/does not meet the requirements of the Lehi City Development Code in regards to
         area plan approval.
    3.   Other findings based upon information presented during the public hearing.

   1)
          a) Gardner-Plumb proposes an amendment to the current Skye Area Plan to
             explicitly define and permit “Grocer” and “General Merchandise Store” as
             approved uses. This clarification aims to prevent future confusion and provide
             greater assurance to potential tenants.

              Grocer: Facilities that sell fresh and packaged food, beverages, dairy, meat,
              produce, and dry goods. May include household essentials, personal care items,
              and over-the-counter pharmaceuticals.

              General Merchandise Store: Facilities that sell a broad range of consumer
              products, which may include clothing, footwear, and accessories; household
              goods and furnishings; electronics and appliances; health and beauty products;
              toys, games, and recreational items; and, in some cases, groceries and
              packaged foods.

          b) This amendment strengthens the area plan by ensuring greater clarity for
             prospective tenants and the city, which ultimately increases the likelihood that
             grocery stores and general merchandisers will establish locations within the area.
             These uses align with the city’s vision for the area and provide valuable services
             to the community.

          c) As mentioned above Gardner-Plumb believes that both uses (grocery store and
             general merchandise) are aligned and suitable with the existing and future uses
             of the area plan.

          d) In addition to providing valuable services to the community, grocery stores and
             general merchandisers often serve as anchor tenants, attracting other desirable
             businesses and enhancing the overall vibrancy and welfare of the community.

   2)
       The proposed amendment does not significantly change the land use or density of the
area plan.
