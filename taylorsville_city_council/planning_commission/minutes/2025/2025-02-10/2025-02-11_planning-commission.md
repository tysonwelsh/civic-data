                                        GENERAL PLAN AMENDMENT


  3.           Public Hearing and Review of a Comprehensive                Update to Taylorsville' s
               General     Plan. ( File 3G23 — GPLAN- 000440- 2024 /         City- wide application;
               Mark McGrath, Long- Range Planner)



            Long- Range Planner Mark McGrath reminded the commissioners of the nine
chapters within the updated General Plan:
       1-   Introduction
       2-   Community Character
       3-   Land Use
       4-   Mobility
       5-   Economic       Development
       6-   Parks and Recreation
       7-   Neighborhoods
       8-   Moderate Income Housing
       9-   Environmental (       to include water conservation)


Each chapter would begin with an introduction followed by an explanation of current
conditions on that particular topic. Each chapter would then address the city' s objectives,
strategies and recommendations                 regarding the nine areas.

 The guiding principles throughout the document were to balance diversity and social
 equity;        stewardship ( leaving Taylorsville            better   than   we   found   it);   resilience (   to both
 economic         and natural     occurrences);
                                                    civic duty and character; and health/ wellness. Mr.
 McGrath explained that these were the common threads that should be wound around
 every element of the General Plan.


 Four fundamental strategies had been identified which were to: Strengthen Community
 Identity and Character; Create Vibrant Economic Centers and Mixed- use Neighborhoods;
 Maintain and Create Distinguishable, Stable, and Desirable Neighborhoods; and Create
 a Multi- Modal Community.

 Mr. McGrath explained that more than anything, this plan was being driven by population
 growth, which was happening at a very rapid rate. Between 2010 and 2020, Utah had
 been the fastest growing state by percentage in the U. S. and the majority of that growth
 took place in Salt Lake County which was projected to increase by 500, 000 people over
 the next 35 years. Utah County was projected to increase by over 670, 000 people during
 the same time period. This growth would have a very serious impact on transportation
 needs in both counties.


 Despite the anticipated increase in population, households were expected to go down in
size which meant even more housing units would be needed. But despite this, there was
very little buildable land left in Salt Lake County.
Taylorsville'      s
                       strategy was to contain      90% of the city' s future
                                                                              growth to 5%               of the city' s
footprint. Growth would therefore be intense in those areas but limited in the majority of
existing neighborhoods. It was believed that approximately 4000 housing units could be
built in the Taylorsville Expressway corridor. A little over 1000 new units were already
planned for Summit Vista. In the West Point District where Volta was located, about 650
units    had already been            approved,
                                                   plus further redevelopment was being proposed
elsewhere in that district. There was some new interest in residential redevelopment in
the Center Pointe District and the Crossroads of Taylorsville which could be the home of
several hundred residential units.


The mercantile district would likely be somewhat less dense but could be a good location
for redevelopment, as could 4700 South between 1- 215 and 2700 West. In total, over
7, 000 new dwelling units could be accommodated in the city which would now be reflected
in the general plan.

A public hearing regarding the Station Area Plan on Taylorsville Expressway was planned
for March 25th, and that plan would be added as an addendum to the General Plan.
Commissioner Wright asked how best for planning commissioners to promote the
General Plan. Mr. McGrath responded that his only recommendation was to share it with
whomever           was     willing     to   listen.   Commissioner Wright felt this document was very
necessary and expressed his appreciation for Mr. McGrath' s work on it.
Chair Russell inquired about a rough schedule for BRT/ Midvalley Express. Mr. McGrath
responded that Utah Transit Authority was currently predicting completion by the end of
2026. He suspected it might be finished a bit earlier.

 Chair Russell opened the public hearing. However, there were no members of the public
 in attendance, so no one expressed a desire to speak. The Chair then closed the public
 hearing.
