RECOMMENDATION:  Staff has reviewed the application for compliance with all standards 
in the Eastern Summit County Development Code and has found that it meets the requirements 
for approval. Staff recommends that the Eastern Summit County Planning Commission (ESCPC) 
review the proposed plat amendment, conduct a public hearing, and vote to approve the plat 
amendment pursuant to the findings of fact, conclusions of law and conditions of approval 
found in this Staff Report.   
 
Project Description 
Project Name:   Elk Meadows Subdivision Lot 4 Plat Amendment 
Applicant(s):   Harwood Homes, LLC 
Property Owner(s): Harwood Homes, LLC 
Location:   2225 E Elk Meadow Circle, Oakley, UT 84055 
Zone District:   AG-10 
Parcel Number and Size: EM-4-3AM (6.68 acres) 
Type of Process:  Administrative 
Final Land Use Authority: Planning Commission 
  
Proposal 
The applicant is proposing to reconfigure the building pad on Lot 4 of the Elk Meadows 
Subdivision Amended Lots 4 & 5 by changing the shape and location of the buildable area 
(Exhibit A).  The proposed building pad is the same square footage as the original building area 
at 44,100 square feet. 
 
 
 
 
 
5
2 
Vicinity Map 
 
 
 
Background 
The Elk Meadows Subdivision was originally platted in 1997 and consisted of 5 lots, ranging 
from 4.82 acres to 9.09 acres. In 1998 the Elk Meadows plat was amended to further subdivide 
the remaining acreage to include lots 6-14 (EXHIBIT B).   
 
Between 2000 and 2004, lots Lot 5, 7, 9 and 13 were amended to relocate the building pads to 
avoid existing irrigation ditches and improve the location of the building pads (EXHIBIT C). 
 
On the 1998 plat, each of the 14 lots has a designated “building area.”  Lots 6-14 also include a 
label for “open space area,” though this area is not specifically defined.  Lots 1-5 do not include 
this label.   
 
Lot 4 is flat and does not contain any critical lands or irrigation canals.  The original size of lot 4 
was 9.09 acres.  In 2015, lots 4 and 5 were amended to reduce the size of lot 4 to 4.406 acres.  
At this time, the building pads on lots 4 and 5 were also adjusted (Exhibit D).  In 2023, lots 4 
and 5 were amended again, resulting in what is seen today where lot 4 is 6.68 acres (Exhibit E).    
 
Research suggests that one intention behind the inclusion of building pads on lots 6-14 was to 
accommodate irrigation canals that ran through a few of the lots (some of which still exist).  
Research has not revealed any other reason for the inclusion of building pads.    
 
6
3 
Analysis and Findings 
Section 11-4-5(E.10) of the Eastern Summit County Development Code sets the following 
grounds for vacating or amending a subdivision plat not involving a private or public road: 
 
1. If the Land Use Authority is satisfied that the public interest will not be materially 
injured by the proposed vacation, alteration or amendment, and there is good cause for 
the vacation, alteration or amendment, the Land Use Authority, may vacate, alter or 
amend the final subdivision plat, or any portion of the final subdivision plat. 
 
Analysis: The applicant is proposing an amended shape and location of the 
building pad but proposes to maintain the existing size.  There is no evidence to 
suggest that the proposed alteration will materially impact the public interest. 
COMPLIES 
 
2. No subdivision plat amendment shall be approved which results in an increase in density 
unless the requirements set forth in subsection E6 of this section for a re-subdivision 
have been satisfied.  
 
Analysis: The amendment adjusts the building pad on an existing lot within a 
platted subdivision.  No new density is being created. COMPLIES
