GPZ-1-2023
Russ Nelson
4543 and 4547 S 4000 W
General plan change from Non-Retail Commercial to General Commercial
and zone change from RB to C-2
0.73 acres

This application was continued by the Planning Commission at the applicant’s request during the
public hearing on March 22, 2023.

Russ Nelson has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for two parcels totaling 0.73
acres located at 4543 and 4547 S 4000 W. Both properties are currently zoned RB (Residential
Business) with a General Plan designation of Non-Retail Commercial. The proposed zone is C-2
(General Commercial) and the proposed General Plan designation is General Commercial.

Surrounding zones include C-2 to the south, RB to the west, R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling
Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet) to the north, and R-4 (Four Unit Dwelling
Residential) to the east. Surrounding land uses include a fast-food restaurant to the south, a home
to the west, a mobile home park to the north, and a fourplex to the east. The subject property is
currently licensed as a day care.

A previous General Plan/zone change application (GPZ-3-2022) made by a different applicant
where the requested zone was C-2 was denied by the City Council at the applicant’s request.
Originally, the applicant had hoped to have an access onto 4000 West. When the applicant
understood that an access onto 4000 West would not be approved, she attempted to secure a
shared access with the property owner to the south. Since, the applicant was unsuccessful in
securing the shared access, she ultimately asked that the General Plan/zone change be denied.
The City Council voted to deny the application on 5/3/2022.

Section 7-5-101 of the Zoning Ordinance states the following: “If the City Council denies an
application to change the Zoning of a property, no application to change the Zoning of that
property to the same zone as proposed in the denied application shall be submitted until at least
one year has passed unless the Planning Commission finds that circumstances have changed in
such a manner as to merit consideration of a new application.” While the previous application on
this property was submitted 13 months before this application, the denial occurred less than a
year ago. However, given the new use for this application and the unique circumstances
surrounding the last application, the Planning Commission could find that circumstances have
changed enough to consider this application.

If this latest application is approved, the applicant plans to build a new Quick Quack car wash on
the subject property. The existing structures on the property would be demolished. Included with
this report is a concept plan for the proposed car wash.

In the C-2 zone, a car wash, which is considered automobile service, is reviewed as a conditional
use when the property is adjacent to residential use or residential zoning. Hence, if this
application is approved, the applicant would need to apply for a conditional use permit to pursue
the car wash development.

During the Commission’s first study meeting on this application, a question was raised about the
proposed car washes’ hours of operation. The facility will be open every day from 7 AM to 7 PM
in the winter months and from 7 AM to 9 PM in the summer months.

Staff shared the concept plan with the Public Works Department for preliminary comment.
Public Works indicated that the curb cut along 4000 West was too close to the curb cut to the
south. As with the previous application, the applicant was directed to work with the landowner to
the south to share the existing access. The applicant was not successful in securing a shared
access with the adjoining landowner. For this reason, the applicant is working on a new concept
plan and has requested that this application be continued to the Planning Commission meeting on
May 10th.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Continuance. As requested by the applicant, this application should be continued to the
      Planning Commission meeting on May 10th.

   2. Approval. This application should be approved.

   3. Denial. The RB zone should remain.

ZT-3-2023
HH-Salt Lake, LLC
Amendments to Sections 7-1-103 and 7-6-301 of the zoning ordinance to
Remove the definition for Lingerie Store and to remove Lingerie Store
From the Commercial and Manufacturing Use Table
This application was continued by the Planning Commission during the meeting on April 12,
2023.

Scott Sabey, who represents HH-Salt Lake, LLC, has requested ordinance amendments to
Sections 7-1-103 and 7-6-301 of the zoning ordinance to remove the definition for Lingerie Store
and to remove Lingerie Store from the Commercial and Manufacturing Use Table. The reasons
for the requested amendments are outlined in the attached document from the applicant.

Section 7-1-103 defines Lingerie Store as “a retail establishment meeting any of the following
criteria:
         a. Over five percent of the floor area is devoted to the sale of lingerie, adult novelties,
         and adult marital or sexual aids;
         b. Lingerie, adult novelties, and adult marital or sexual aids account for five percent or
         more of gross sales; or
         c. The establishment is marketed as a Lingerie Store or adult establishment.”

Section 7-6-301 allows Lingerie Stores in the MXD (Mixed Use), C-2 (General Commercial), C-
3 (Transitional Commercial), and M (Manufacturing) zones. Lingerie Stores are prohibited in the
BRP (Business/Research Park), CC (City Center), C-1 (Neighborhood Commercial), and LI
(Light Industrial) zones as well as within the Decker Lake Station, Jordan River, Bangerter
Highway, and 5600 West Overlay Zones. There are just under 9,000 acres of property within the
City where Lingerie Stores are allowed. This represents 46% of property (excluding roads) in the
City.

The term “Lingerie Store” was first introduced in the zoning ordinance with the adoption of
Ordinance 15-03 in 2015. This ordinance expanded the list of prohibited uses within the CC
zone. The term “Lingerie Store” was later defined in Section 7-1-103 and added to the
Commercial and Manufacturing Use Table in Section 7-6-301 with the adoption of Ordinance
17-10 in 2017.

The City has long used overlay zones to prohibit certain uses within areas where other uses are
preferred. For example, within the Decker Lake Station Overlay Zone located east of I-215 and
generally between 3100 S and 3500 S, the City has encouraged entertainment uses like the
Maverik Center and Harmon Hall, restaurants, and hospitality uses.

In addition to Lingerie Stores, there are several other uses that are prohibited in all of the overlay
zones mentioned above. These uses include: Agricultural Business and Industry, Alternative
Financial Service Provider, Used Automobile Sales, Automobile Service, Bail Bonds Dealer,
Blood Plasma Center, Cannabis Production Establishment, Cargo Container, Self-Storage
Facility, Sexually Oriented Business, and Tattoo Establishment.

S-2-2023
Grove South Industrial Park Subdivision
5551 South U-111
M Zone
2 Lots
14 acres


BACKGROUND:

Grey Garza is requesting preliminary plat approval for the Grove South Industrial Park
Subdivision. The subdivision plat will dedicate a new road that will extend east from U-111 to the
east property line of the subdivision to provide future access to properties to the east, north and
south. Properties to the east, north and south are currently vacant zoned manufacturing.

ISSUES:

The proposed application is being requested in order to create a 2 lot subdivision in the
manufacturing zone. It is anticipated that future uses will be light manufacturing and warehouse.
The property owner is proposing 2 lots at the present time, but depending upon future interest and
land uses, but could amend the plat to accommodate smaller lots if needed.

Lot 1 will consist of 6.5 acres with lot 2 comprising 5.7 acres. Both lots will gain access via a new
right-of-way within the subdivision. The new road will be 66 feet in width and will consist of
curb, gutter, asphalt and sidewalk on one side of the road. The road for the time being will
terminate with a cul-de-sac, but will extend to the east property line to provide future access and
or connections with properties to the east, north and south as they develop. It is not anticipated at
this time that any improvements will be required along U-111.

The property sits just south of an old rail line operated by Kennecott Copper. It was recently
abandoned and the overpass at U-111 removed. It is staff’s position that if at all possible, the rail
line right-of-way be absorbed in this subdivision and/or Mr. Garza’s property to the north at some
point in time. Staff understands that discussions have taken place between Kennecott and the
applicant about this very issue. A fair amount of remediation is needed before the property could
be utilized within a development application, but progress is being made and staff will monitor
this matter moving forward. Staff did learn that resolving the right-of-way issue is a top priority
for Kennecott.

At a future date, staff will review either conditional use and/or permitted use applications. At that
time, staff and agency comments will be more thoroughly addressed as part of the site plan review
process. The subdivision plat will contain easements and other information applicable to the
division of property.

STAFF ALTERNATIVES:
