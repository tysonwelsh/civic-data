4.     PUBLIC HEARING, CONSIDERATION, AND RECOMMENDATION on a City-
       Initiated Text Amendment Application to Amend Title 11, Chapters 11.20, 11.90,
       11.120, 11.150, 11.160, 11.220 et seq. of the Bluffdale City Code Regarding Widening
       Required City Commercial Site Building, and Landscaping Standards, in the GC-1
       Zone and City-Wide, along with Other Related Administrative Provisions.
       (Application 2020-54)- Staff Presenter, Grant Crowell.

Mr. Crowell led the discussion on this agenda item for the fourth time since the March 17, 2021,
meeting. Mr. Crowell noted that the public hearing remained open. This agenda item was the result
of the City Council's adoption of a Pending Ordinance in October 2020 to address design concerns
along the 2700 West corridor. During the review, it became apparent to staff that there were many
design and landscaping codes that were important City-wide.

Thus far, the majority of the concerns came from people associated with the 2700 West conidor
with regard to the cost ramifications of the proposed text amendments, particularly with the use of
bricks.

Since the last Planning Commission Meeting, Mr. Crowell had looked at window percentages,
which have been a controversial issue. Accordingly, he modified the percentage from 50% to 40%.
The question to be addressed was how the building should look from the street. The next issue of
concern was to determine the areas of visibility, particularly what constitutes the "back" of a
building. The distinction between "institutional" buildings and "commercial" buildings was another
point of consideration. The revised language eliminates the requirement of a buffer between an
institutional building and a residential area.

Mr. Crowell also studied landscaping requirements since the last meeting. He noted that there is a
difference between aspirational and practical requirements. It is impossible to predict exactly how
Bluffdale will build out and have a bona fide City Center or just be a bedroom community. He
explained that the block on 2700 West is surrounded by four roads consisting of Bangerter
Highway, 2950 West, 14000 South, and a future 13900 South. Assertions people may make need to
be verifiable, particularly with regard to the visibility of the different areas in a site plan.

With regard to landscaping, Mr. Crowell reported that he had not changed much in the proposed
text. Water conservation is a priority that has become more pronounced, especially with the water
provider as it applies to water efficiency standards and how they will be applied. The Engineering
Department was working to address the issue. The model to build all sod and hire landscape
mowing companies was considered outdated and cannot be utilized going forward . It will be
necessary to have 20% sod with zones and irrigation types that are good for the climate. The
situation will require a Landscape Architect, which admittedly will incur a cost to the developer.
The use of trees in a commercial site plan was something Mr. Crowell advocated.

       Use Code, CW Franz, Applicant. (Application 2021-11)      Staff Presenter, Ellen

Ms. Oakman presented the staff report and stated that the applicants want to convert a home into a
detached garage. The subject propetiy is located at 14564 South 3200 West and is .98-acre in size
in the R-1-43 zone. Ms. Oakman identified the location of the existing home on a map displayed.
They plan to build a new home and would like to keep the foundation of the existing home for a
detached garage/workshop. They do not want to have to demolish the existing home after the new
home is built. In order to build the new home, they would still need to live in the existing home
during constmction. Doing so would require a Temporary Use Agreement.

Commissioner Loumis explained that City Code does not allow detached garages to have bedrooms.
Ms. Oakman noted that accessory dwelling units are not allowed in a detached structure and must
be within the home and have a legal pennit. Ms. Oakman referenced the Temporary Use Code
provided in the packet and stated that instead of requiring the home to be demolished once the new
home is completed, the proposed text amendment allows for the building to be converted by
removing the kitchen and sleeping facilities. Additional details were included. Ms. Oakman
displayed photos of the existing home to illustrate how it would fit on the property with the new
home.

Ms. Oakman explained that the City Code allows detached garages to have kitchens and bathrooms
and does not restrict room types. However, Accessory Dwelling Units ("ADU") are patently
prohibited as detached stmctures. Commissioner Walston stated that technically, the applicants
would not be required to take out the kitchen. They want to make sure that it is clear that the
detached garage will be a workshop and not give the impression of it being a dwelling unit. Chair
Cragun suggested that the proposed text amendments might be better crafted to match the current
specifications of a detached garage by allowing the applicants to keep the kitchen facility and
remove the sleeping facilities.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Flynn, Ms. Oakman explained that detached
structures and main homes are allowed on a residential lot. Ms. Oakman clarified that the
applicants are asking for a detached garage. In response to Cmmnissioner Walston's request for
clarification on Accessory Dwelling Units, Ms. Oakman explained that they must be enclosed as
part of the home and not connected by a breezeway. Senior City Planner, Jennifer Robison,
clarified the legal requirements.

The applicant, CW Franz, gave his address as 14564 South 3200 West. He explained that the
existing home is located on the back comer of the property and is an ideal location for a workshop.
It also has concrete walls and a concrete floor. The converted shop will meet the setback
requirements for an accessory dwelling, however, that would not be the case if he attached the new
home to the existing home. The new home would meet the setback requirements. In addition, he
would not want to have to demolish the existing home within 10 days once the new home is built,
only to put in a new shop in a few years. He clarified that he does not need a kitchen in the shop but
would like to keep the bathroom.

Ms. Oakman explained that there is no distinction between a "detached garage" and a "detached
structure." The City Code does not have a specific definition for "garage". Commissioner Loumis
stated that the existing home would make a great garage, especially since all of the building
materials are already in place. Mr. Franz stated that the home has a cement floor and no basement.
If it had wood floors, the situation would be much different. Mr. Franz stated that he plans to use
the shop for woodworking and automotive work. A garage door will make it look like a shop rather
than a home. Mr. Franz confinned that he would have no problem not using the shop as a
secondary dwelling unit.

Ms. Oakman read the definition of "detached garage," which is a building that is intended for the
storage of personal property. It is only allowed for uses such as home theater, trophy room, den,
recreation room, and other similar uses for the residents of the main dwelling. It could also be used
for a home-based occupation and office. An "accessory structure" is subordinate and secondary to
the primary building. Chair Cragun thought it was odd that the definition of a garage did not
mention the parking of automobiles. Mr. Franz was amenable to having the proposed text
amendment use the tenn "garage".

Mrs. Robison noted that the Land Use Table for the R-1-43 zone allows fo r accessory buildings, so
it was not necessary to use the tenn "garage".

Chair Cragun opened the public hearing. Mr. Crowell received no emails. There were no fmiher
public comments. Chair Cragun closed the public hearing.

Commissioner Loumis felt Mr. Franz should be allowed to keep the kitchen and bathroom and
move the other walls so that it is not a residence. It was noted that Mr. Franz would also be
required to meet with the City Attorney.

Steve Walston moved to forward a positive recommendation to the City Council for the Text
Amendment Application 2021-11, with the deletion of the removal of the kitchen and sleeping
facilities and convert it into a detached accessory structure. It would also be consistent with
the City's other regulations. Ulises Flynn seconded the motion. Vote on motion: Johnny
Loumis, Jr.-Aye, Steve Walston-Aye, Kory Luker-Aye, Ulises Flynn-Aye, Debbie Cragun-Aye.
The motion passed unanimously.
