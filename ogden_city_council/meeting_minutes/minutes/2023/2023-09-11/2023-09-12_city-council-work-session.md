and O-1 to C-3/GO; AND
Proposed Street Vacation — River Drive east of Washington Boulevard
Deputy Planning Manager Simpson stated a multi-family residential and commercial development located at
approximately 18" Street and Washington Boulevard is being proposed which would contain up to 115 units in two 5- story
residential buildings and a small commercial building where the Pioneer Heritage building (Piccadilly’s building) is located. The
overall project approvals include a rezone to C-3/CO, a street vacation of River Drive east of Washington Boulevard, and property ,
sale and/or transfer from the City, Redevelopment Agency (RDA), and Utah Division of Transportation (UDOT). The project area |
has a combination of several zones that would need to be consolidated into a single zone. The current zones include CP-2, R-2,
and O-1. The zoning classification proposed is the C-3/CO or Regional Commercial/Conditional Overlay. The conditional overlay
element requires the approval of a development agreement that includes concept plans, timelines for development, standards for
design, materials and site improvements, and reversionary clauses if the project is not completed as proposed or on the agreed
upon schedule. The conditional overlay also acts to limit the uses to only those which are included in the development agreement
and would not allow other permitted and conditional uses listed in the C-3 zone to be approved without an amendment to the
development agreement. In order to make the site work for the proposed development, the petitioners have requested the vacation
of the River Drive right-of-way east of Washington Boulevard. River Drive itself continues east of the remaining dedicated right-
of-way and connects to Adams Avenue just south of 17th Street. The portion between the dedicated right-of-way and Adams
Avenue was previously vacated and is currently owned by Ogden City. The request is to vacate the remaining dedicated right-of-
way, which is about 140 feet in length and about 40 feet in width. Mr. Simpson presented several images of the conceptual site
plan, and summarized the components of the project as follows:

Along Washington Boulevard, the western most part of the vacated River Drive area would become an active plaza
creating a strong connection between the south residential building, the commercial building, and the parkway trail. It will be
visible from Washinton Boulevard but set back and screened by planters. It will also be visible from the fitness/yoga room to the
north and café roof deck to the south and seamlessly connected to indoor café seating via roll-up garage door. It will be shaded
throughout the day by the commercial building to the south and will be inviting and open to the public. It will wrap around the
northeast corner of the commercial building and can be regularly used for cafe seating and other gatherings.

General discussion among the Council and Mr. Simpson centered on the relationship between the subject property and
surrounding properties/land uses; proximity of the subject property to downtown Ogden; the history of the various properties that
have been assembled for this project; the site plan/project layout; the efforts of the developer to enhance the public experience in
the vicinity of the project area; the amount of open space reserved for trail improvements and publicly accessible open space to be
included in the project; terms of the development agreement; the potential for the zoning of the property to revert to the current
zoning if the project is eliminated in the future for some reason.

Chair Choberka noted one question that has arisen about this project relates to the City’s request for the developer to
consider the redevelopment of additional properties as part of their project. She asked for the rationale behind not publicly
advertising the availability of those properties through a public request for proposal (RFP) process. Mr. Simpson stated the
rationale is that the property that the City controls is so small and there are not many development options for the site. The
developer approached the City with an unsolicited proposal, and it would be inappropriate to advertise their concept for public
proposals and bids.

Chair Choberka invited input from the applicants, Joanna and Paul Droubay. She stated by thanking them for engaging
the neighborhood in discussions of this project, as well as engaging with the Council regarding their proposal. Ms. Droubay
identified the section of the road that would be subject to the proposed street vacation and emphasized that they are not trying to
acquire that road in order to increase their unit count on the site, but they want to provide a seamless connection between their
development and the Ogden Parkway trail. They will also landscape it in an appropriate manner that compliments the trail and the
river and will also maintain the area. Mr. Droubay also discussed the timeline of the project; they are working to finalize the ‘
financing for the project, which is dependent upon the demolition of the Pioneer Days property. They are also continuingto work =| }
with UDOT to finalize the transfer of ownership of all of the properties that are part of the project.
