     DISCUSSION – Annual Sewer Report

Mr. Winterton presented information regarding the City's Municipal Wastewater Planning
Program (MWPP) Annual Report. The city worked hard to reduce or eliminate system incidents
or violations of discharges to the Powell Slough. In response to a question from Mr. Macdonald,
Mr. Winterton said line maintenance and line replacements kept the system in working order. The
City had invested in monitoring systems for early detection as well as lining collection pipes and
sewer conveyance pipes, which helped protect the pipes from root intrusion and extend the life of
the system.

Mr. Winterton summarized the report and provided information about water meter replacement
with updated technology, especially automated meters that allowed consumers to view their
personal usage amounts with regularly updated information. There was continued discussion
regarding updated water meter infrastructure throughout the city. Mr. Winterton spoke about utility
rates for the upcoming fiscal year, saying they would not increase for Fiscal Year 2020-2021. They
were going to evaluate which needed capital projects could still move forward without increases.
Mr. Peterson asked for clarification on the water rates, and the benefits of moving to a new meter
infrastructure, which Mr. Winterton provided.

There was continued discussion on capital projects for water reclamation, in particular the water
tank planned to be built at Community Park. Mr. Davidson said the tank project would cost
somewhere between $10 million to $13 million, so the City would look to finance that over time.
The water rate structure had been put in place in part to assist with the cost of the tank. Lewis
Young Robertson & Burningham (LYRB) had been working with staff to look at future rates and
determine the best way forward for financing large capital infrastructure projects.

Mr. Spencer expressed particular concern for senior citizens living below the poverty line and their
ability to pay increasing water rates. Mr. Davidson said that was a consideration specifically
discussed with LYRB, to see if adjustments could be made. Mr. Davidson said the reality was that
the project had to be paid for; as payment requirements were modified for one group of rate payers,
the financial responsibility was shifted to another group of rate payers. They wanted to be
thoughtful in defining which rate payers would qualify for relief.

Mr. Winterton concluded his report, and particularly thanked his staff. He said while many jobs
could be done from home, those working water reclamation were still needed at their posts to keep
the system operational. The Mayor and Council voiced their gratitude to staff for the work they
did in ensuring that even during these uncertain times Orem residents could rely on clean, running
water to their homes.


        DISCUSSION – Reducing Credit Card Fees

Mr. Wolfley presented information on reducing Orem’s credit and debit card fee liability. He said
an innovation team (i-Team) of employees from different departments was assembled to study the
issue and share solutions. The objectives were to find ways to hold water and sewer rates even for
Fiscal Year 2020-2021 (FY21), to reduce escalating costs, to address the unsustainable trend, and
follow best practices from other utilities. Mr. Wolfley shared a graph which showed credit card
fees and transaction by year. He said over the past five years the two lines had grown further and
further apart, with fees outpacing transactions at a significant rate. This had to do with the kind of
credit cards that were used to make payments, as credit card companies regulated themselves and
could adjust fees whenever they wanted.

Mr. Wolfley said every time a credit card was run, there was a fee associated with that. He said
the City’s elastic services were those that residents could easily find at other cities or high
competition services, including recreation, library, passport, fingerprint, and events. Because of
their elastic nature, they did not recommend additional fees or surcharges for these services.
Inelastic services included city utilities, court and police fees, building/permit fees, and business

fees. Mr. Wolfley defined surcharges versus fees, and clarified that surcharges could not be applied
to utilities payments per card company agreement policies, but convenience fees could be applied.

Mr. Wolfley reviewed adjustments already in place. These included:
   • $1 minimum to use a credit card to pay Library fees
   • $5,000 cap per transaction through Development Services
   • Working with merchant service providers for best practices and compliance
   • Increased online security to obtain lower fees (requiring residents to add additional

   • Quick pay for utility bills using Automated Clearing House (ACH)

He then presented the i-Team’s recommendations for a go-forward plan. The City would establish
a cap and convenience fee for utility bill payments; the cap would be $500 and the convenience
fee was proposed for $3.95. Any amount exceeding $500 would be split into multiple payments,
with the $3.95 convenience fee applied to each transaction. They would directly contact everyone
who paid with credit or debit cards and notify them of their options and ability to avoid the fee if
they paid their utility bill through ACH transactions.

There was continued discussion on the proposal. In response to Council questions, Mr. Wolfley
said the large majority of utility customers had bills under the $500 amount. Typically the accounts
with the higher bills were commercial accounts. Mr. Wolfley said they would communicate the
changes and options to utility customers through multiple means, including emails and information
leaflets sent with printed utility bills.

Members of the City Council were generally favorable of the proposal, and believed residents
would expect and understand the change. Mr. Spencer said it may be worth exploring a different
fee for those large commercial accounts, as they were the main cause for concern. Mr. Wolfley
said they hoped to have the new policy effective October 1, 2020.


     DISCUSSION – CDBG COVID-19 Funding

Mr. Downs said additional funds had been appropriated by the federal government to prevent,
prepare for, and respond to the coronavirus pandemic in our community and to mitigate the impact
of COVID-19. The City’s allocation was $411,983 and was only for the approved uses, not to
offset any revenue shortfall. Ms. Mathews said they had met with the other entitlements and
discussed opportunities for possible joint projects. Those included public services for mental health
and domestic violence, a business stabilization fund, funds to the jail, and first responder and health
care provider support. Ms. Mathews said some possible Orem-only projects were public services,
rental/mortgage assistance, police and fire overtime and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), and
CDBG administration. She mentioned that the other entitlements received more money than Orem
had.

The Mayor and Council generally stated their preference that the funding go toward Orem-only
projects. Mayor Brunst said Utah County received funding from other sources. Some members of
the Council felt the money should be used to support first responders and their families during this
time. In response to a question from Mr. Sumner, Ms. Mathews said the money Utah County

        BUDGET DISCUSSION – Operations, Capital Improvement Projects, Fleet & IT

Mr. Nelson presented a Fiscal Year 2020-2021 (FY21) revenue and expenditure overview. He said
his team had to revise their budget documents to consider the effect of the COVID-19 pandemic
on the economy. He emphasized that the information he would present included conservative
estimates based on most current data available. Mr. Nelson shared General Fund revenue changes,
both increases and decreases, with estimated totals showing a deficit of approximately $1.45
million.

Mayor Brunst said previous budget discussions had included the possibility of increasing rates and
holding a Truth in Taxation hearing, but he felt the pandemic had caused unforeseen problems and
he was concerned about what residents were suffering through. He said this might not be the year
to consider that increase. Mr. Sumner added that he agreed with Mayor Brunst’s comments. Mr.
Nelson said the Council could adjust the tentative budget as they saw fit. Mr. Davidson said the
decreases in revenue they were anticipating were not 20% across the board. They were working
with the Utah League of Cities and Towns (ULCT) to have a third party review Orem’s data and
overlay that with current projections. This would provide multiple perspectives.

Mr. Nelson continued reviewing major enterprise fund fees, including water, water reclamation,
storm water, cemetery, street lighting, impact fees and more. He said the requests for General Fund
operational expansions totaled approximately $67,700 and covered requests from Development
Services, Finance, Fire, Public Works, and City Manager’s departments. Mr. Nelson then
explained the requests from other funds, including water, water reclamation, storm water, roads,
and IT, totaling approximately $447,500. He said the IT request was tied to the Water AMI (smart
meters) Network equipment rotation and maintenance. Discussion ensued regarding the proposals.

Mr. Nelson invited Reed Price and Thayne Carter to present information for FY21 fleet
replacement. Mr. Price reviewed the vehicles in need of replacement or repurposing across the
City. He said Orem had received a grant from Volkswagen to reimburse some costs toward five
fleet vehicles, including the passenger bus for the Senior Center. In response to a question about
repurposing, Mr. Price said one requirement of the Volkswagen grant was to completely disable
the old vehicle once it was replaced. Mr. Macdonald asked about leasing a vehicle for police
investigations rather than purchasing; Mr. Price said leasing was a better option for a high-mileage
vehicle like this. Investigations vehicles typically covered 20,000+ miles per year.

projects (CIP). Mr. Kelly summarized the costs and split out the amounts from each fund, totaling

     COMMUNICATION ITEMS

Mr. Davidson gave an update on Governor Herbert’s recent press conference in which the
Governor stated that Utah would move from the red-high risk category relating to COVID-19 to
the orange-moderate risk category, effective 12:01 a.m. on May 1, 2020. The details were outlined
in the State’s “Utah Leads Together 2.0” plan and related addenda, and included some changes to
city operations. Mr. Davidson said this differed from the previous directive because it was
considered an order from the Governor’s office. He said many employees would continue to
stagger work shifts, masks would be worn in city buildings and entry to facilities like the City
Center, Library, and Senior Center would be metered and watched. Other city operations would
be done by appointment only to help control numbers and exposure risks.

Mrs. Lauret asked about employees that had tested positive for COVID-19. Mr. Davidson said at
present three employees had tested positive, and each was asymptomatic at the time of the meeting.
The City buildings had been sanitized by a third party company, and staff was working together
on create ways to discourage “crowds” from building up, like removing lobby furniture. Mr.
Davidson said they wanted to be smart in how the reopened services to the public.

Mr. Spencer asked about group sport seasons, in particular club baseball and soccer. Mr. Davidson
said the state’s order prohibited team sports at this time, and seasons would likely be canceled. Mr.
Hirst said he had been in contact with representatives from the cosponsored groups.
