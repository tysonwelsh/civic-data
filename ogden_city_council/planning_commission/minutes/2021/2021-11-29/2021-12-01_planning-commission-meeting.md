3. Public Hearing, Petition to Rezone, from R-1-6 to C-2/CO to allow the Children’s Justice Center at
   approximately 1845 Jackson Avenue. (Attachment 3)

  Mr. Rod Layton explained the role of the Children’s Justice Center, stating while it is a non-profit, it is
  mandated by the State of Utah and regulated by the Attorney General’s office. The desire is to provide
  a home-like setting so children are comfortable being interviewed in domestic violence situations.
  They work closely with the local law enforcement and the County Attorney in providing services for
  children. While a residential setting is desirable, the use is not residential as there are no overnight
  accommodations, and commercial zoning is required. The existing facility has been outgrown and as it
  is a historic resource, the ability to expand at that location is limited. The Children’s Justice Center has
  been looking at new locations for a few years, and as they desire to be centrally located and near the
  courts, they are now considering leasing property owned by the Ogden School District and desire to
  construct a new residential-style building at this location. He provided photos of similar existing
  facilities throughout the State as well as a proposed architectural style for this building.
  Mr. Brierley stated this request is to rezone a portion of the Ogden School District property from the
  existing R-1-5 and O-1 zones to become commercial C-2 in order to accommodate the use as the
  Children’s Justice Center building. Staff is recommending the conditional overlay zone also be
  attached so that in the future other commercial uses cannot be allowed. He reviewed the General
  Plan, Community Policies and the East Central Community Plan, indicating this property had been
  identified as a location of a sports field and aquatic center. While a sports field has been built to the
  north, the School District is no longer considering an aquatic center, and other property is available
  where this could be established in the future if their plans change. The surrounding area is residential
  in character, and the Ogden School District offices are a permitted use in the residential zone. The
  style of building desired by the Children’s Justice Center is residential in character and would fit with
  the surrounding area. The proposed C-2/CO zone would include a development agreement which
  would regulate the proposed use as well as architectural style and other details. Staff is also
  recommending a provision which requires the property to revert to the existing R-1-5 zoning in the
  event the Children’s Justice Center no longer occupies the building, which would allow other uses
  including residential, educational, religious, day care or public building at that time. Staff recommends
  the Planning Commission recommend approval of the rezoning request subject to the conditional
  overlay zone and development agreement to include the architectural style being residential and blend
  with the character of the neighborhood, and that if vacated by the CJC the zoning would return to R-1-
  5. Approval also should be subject to this lot being divided to become a separate parcel and the zone
  boundaries limited to that new parcel.
  Commission asked if there is a time period for development in the event funds are not available for the
  new construction. Mr. Brierley indicated the development agreement has a performance clause
  requiring construction to begin within two years and if no work has begun, the zoning would revert back
  to R-1. Chair Graf asked how much of the property is currently in the O-1 zone as well as whether the
  existing Naval Reserve building is being used. Mr. Brierley responded the total property is 1.9 acres
  with about .87 acres on the hillside being zoned O-1. He is unaware of any use of the Naval Reserve
  building, but it is owned by the School District.

4. Request to Amend Nonconforming Use, to revise improvement requirements for Upholstery Shop at
   1150 22nd Street. (Attachment 4)
  Mr. Adam Alfaro stated his father had purchased this building and had received approval for a change
  of nonconforming use to allow an upholstery shop at which time the Planning Commission approved a
  site plan showing landscaping in the public right-of-way and the removal of existing angle parking
  stalls. He stated the back parking lot is cumbersome and difficult for delivery trucks to maneuver.
  Customers also prefer parking in front of the building as the main access to the building is in the front.
  He felt the business is a benefit to the surrounding community, and cited other locations where angle
  parking in the right-of-way is allowed, indicating it is similar to their previous location. He asked the
  Commission to approve a site plan allowing the existing three parking stalls to remain in front of the
  building.
  Ms. Corbridge indicated the applicant had received approval for a change of use at this location in
  2018 at which time a site plan was approved showing completion of the front improvements, including
  the removal of parking spaces and adding landscaping and trees in the right-of-way area. While
  landscaping has been installed in front of the building, the right-of-way improvements have not been
  made. The owner is now asking for an exception to allow the parking spaces to remain in order for
  customers and delivery vehicles to load and unload furniture near the front building entrance. The
  previous location of the upholstery shop and other buildings in the East Central community contain
  similar nonconforming parking spaces in front of the commercial-style buildings. She explained when
  the Planning Commission considers a change of nonconforming use, the Ordinance indicates the site
  is to meet all development requirements as much as is physically possible, and that parking should be
  designed so vehicles do not back into the public right-of-way. Because adequate parking can be
  provided in the rear parking lot, and there is a building entrance near the rear of the building, Staff
  recommends denial of the request.
  Commission asked what work would be required according to the approved site plan. Ms. Corbridge
  indicated the existing curb cut would be required to be relocated to line up to the driveway and
  landscaping and trees would be installed. Parking would not be allowed within the driveway area.
  Mr. Allen Alfaro stated he likes this building and its location and has put much work into making it work
  for his business. He stated improvements have been made to the site and the building and the
  business is an asset to the community. He stated he measured the street and indicated due to its

  width, those parking in front of the building do not back into the travel lane. He felt if the front parking
  were to be removed, he would lose many customers and may be forced to close the business.
  Commissioner Safsten asked if there is a rear entrance to the building where customers could bring

6. Public Hearing, Proposed Ordinance Amendment 15-13-39, to consider changes to standards for
   Accessory Dwelling Units. (Attachment 6)
  Mr. Montgomery explained the State Legislature recently changed the regulations for accessory
  dwelling units indicating cities cannot regulate the size of these units in single-family homes. As
  the existing ordinance limits the unit size to 40% of the floor area, the City needs to eliminate that
  clause. He also indicated other items are in the Ordinance but have found to be impractical, such as
  requiring a business license. He reviewed items which can and cannot be regulated by the City,
  stating these remain as single-family homes, but are allowed to have an accessory dwelling unit.

                                                                                                     Page | 4
  Language also is proposed to require all sleeping rooms to comply with emergency egress
  requirements which is allowed by State law. Staff recommends approval of the Ordinance language as
  proposed. He clarified these are approved with a land use permit, at which time City Staff assures the
  building is owner-occupied, assures two existing legal parking spaces are available on site, and
  identifies where sleeping rooms are located at assures egress windows exist before these permits are
  issued. Discussion continued relating to duplex units in relation to ADU’s as well as varying
  requirements for egress windows for rentals. Mr. Montgomery stated as ADU’s are limited to being
  owner occupied, the requirement for egress windows is applicable for these where it may not always
  be applicable for duplexes. He also clarified an accessory dwelling unit may or may not have a
  separate entrance, but any separate entrance is required to be on the side or rear of the home.
As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Safsten to close the public
  hearing. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell and passed unanimously.
 MOTION:       A motion was made by Commissioner Sandau to recommend approval of the proposed
               ordinance language based on the findings it is consistent with the General Plan and will
               meet State law requirements and community needs for accessory dwelling units. Motion
               was seconded by Commissioner Safsten and passed unanimously with Commissioners
               Blaisdell, Garner, Safsten, Sandau, Schade, Stoker and Graf voting aye.

7. Public Hearing, Proposed Plan Amendment, for Ogden On Board Master Plan. (Attachment 7)
 Mr. Montgomery explained the Ogden On Board Plan was developed during 2018-2019 in an effort to
 explore the BRT route and define the future vision along the corridor. It contains goals relating to transit
 stops with one of the main stops at the Intermodal Hub at 23rd & Wall. Since that time, two additional
 plans have been created, including potential use of the railyards created by a group of USU students,
 and the Make Ogden Master Plan which was adopted last year which both recommend more focus on
 the Union Station campus. The proposed amendment would expand the plan area to the south to
 include the Union Station and identify existing parking to the north of the Intermodal Hub as potential
 Transit-Oriented Development. Staff recommends approval of the amendments as proposed.
 Commissioner Schade noted 23rd Street is identified as a “green street” and also would accommodate
 the BRT route, which would limit its use to being a one-way street. He asked about the use of a parking
 structure at 23rd & Wall as it would be difficult to access if 23rd Street is not available as an east-west
 street. He also expressed concern with the vision for the use of Wall Avenue as it is the only remaining
 viable north-south corridor in the City, and the BRT will affect traffic patterns. Mr. Montgomery
 explained the phasing plan would eventually move the focus of traffic from 23rd Street, and that it is
 desirable to slow traffic in the downtown area, stating the main congestion areas are 20th/21st streets
 and 30th/31st Streets.
 Mr. Jordan Swain, UTA, stated the intent of the proposed plan is to maximize development potential,
 and felt it can be done in a way which will calm traffic along Wall Avenue. He stated this plan can then
 be used by UTA as it seeks development of adjacent property to the north, and is a guide for future
 development, but will likely change over the course of development. Mr. Montgomery acknowledged
 while there are some constraints with this site, the City is supportive of a Transit-Oriented Development
 project at that location. Commission further discussed other options for access to Wall Avenue,
 including circulating around Union Station and enter at 28th or 29th Street.
 As there were no public comments, a motion was made by Commissioner Sandau to close the public
 hearing. Moton was seconded by Commissioner Garner and passed unanimously.
 MOTION:        A motion was made by Commissioner Schade to recommend approval of the proposed
                amendments to pages 69-73 of the Ogden On Board Master Plan based on the findings it
                is consistent with the General Plan. Motion was seconded by Commissioner Blaisdell
                and passed unanimously with Commissioners Blaisdell, Garner, Safsten, Sandau,
                Schade, Stoker and Graf voting aye.

Review of Meeting
Mr. Montgomery expressed appreciation of the options provided by legal staff relating to the change of
nonconforming use for the upholstery shop, stating there is no wrong answer so long as findings are
identified to support the motion. Chair Graf stated the Commission needs to be cautious in responding to
emotional pleas of property owners. Mr. Stratford stated his intent was to indicate that when it was
approved in 2018, there was an expectation that the site plan with rear parking only would work, but now it
has found to be unfeasible, and the site is limited. Mr. Montgomery explained while the Commission
desires to be consistent in its decision-making, policies over the years have changed, and there was a
time period where front yard parking for commercial uses was allowed so long as the building is not on a
corner. There are examples of both these being removed to meet current standards as well as being
allowed to continue. The main intent is to preserve these older buildings for the benefit of the community
and allow businesses to be viable. Mr. Stratford cautioned the Commission relating to pleas to eliminate
landscaping requirements as there are options for water conscious landscaping available.

Mr. Montgomery stated there will be a work session on December 15th with one agenda item and an
introduction to the consultants working on the rewrite of the code governing the downtown in an effort to
include standards of the Make Ogden Plan.

As there was no additional business before the Commission, the meeting was adjourned at 7pm.
