Wasatch Commercial Developers
4890-5090 West Lake Park Blvd.
General plan change from Large Lot Residential to Business Park and
zone change from A and A-2 to B/RP
39.5 acres


Wasatch Commercial Developers has submitted a General Plan/zone change application for five
parcels totaling 39.5 acres located at 4890-5090 West Lake Park Blvd. The property is currently
zoned A (Agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre) and A-2 (Agriculture, minimum lot size 2 acres)
with a General Plan designation of Large Lot Residential (2 to 3 units/acre). The proposed zone is
B/RP (Business/Research Park) and the proposed General Plan designation is Business Park. The
current General Plan also shows the need for a park in this general area.

Surrounding zones include M (Manufacturing) to the north, east, west, and a portion of the south
and R-1-8 (Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 square feet) for the
remaining south portion. To the west, the M zone only covers the adjoining waterway. The
property west of the waterway is zoned A-2. Surrounding land uses include the Riter Canal and
the Lake Park Commerce Center industrial development to the north, the Stonebridge Golf
Course to the east, the Lake Park Corporate Center office development and vacant land entitled
for residential to the south, and a waterway and vacant land to the west. The property is currently
vacant.

Proposed Development

If this application is approved, the applicant plans to build a biomedical research and development
campus of 7 buildings totaling approximately 580,000 square feet (690,000 square feet when
including the mezzanines) of industrial/office flex space. Included with this report is a concept
plan and building renderings for the property along with a letter from the applicant that supports
this application.

General Plan and Zoning History

Back in 2015, the subject property was included in a City initiated General Plan/zone change that
involved 741 acres in various locations throughout the City. With one exception of Non-Retail
Commercial, the General Plan was changed on all 741 acres to Large Lot Residential and the
zoning was changed to either A or A-2. At the same time, the City also increased its housing
standards for single unit dwellings and created the RE (Residential Estate) zone. The Council was
concerned about the shrinking amount of available land for residential development and the lack
of higher end housing in the City. These changes were made to encourage larger homes on larger
lots. The previous zoning on the subject property was M.

At the time, the proximity of the golf course; the Highbury amenities like trails and waterways;
the nearby services like the Instacare, Target, restaurants, etc.; the existing nearby mix of
residential; and the canal buffer were reasons for the residential designation. The relatively large
area of the property also created an opportunity for a good sized RE zone subdivision.

In 2018, the City also amended the Major Street Plan to remove a planned extension of Corporate
Park Drive between Lake Park Blvd. and 2400 South which would have bisected the subject
property. At the time, the City did not want truck traffic driving through what was planned as a
residential development.

Reference Information

The basic question for this application is should the property be residential as proposed in the
City’s General Plan or should it be developed as proposed by the applicant. To help the
Commission evaluate this proposal, this report provides information on anticipated traffic, taxable
value, employment, zoning regulations, and transit for the Commission’s reference.

       Traffic
       While not required for a General Plan/zone change, the applicant submitted the attached
       traffic impact study for the proposed development. According to this study, the proposed
       development is anticipated to generate approximately 6,432 weekday daily trips, including
       600 trips in the morning peak hour, and 570 trips in the evening peak hour. The study also
       indicates that nearby intersections would operate at acceptable levels of service upon
       completion of the proposed project and that no traffic mitigations would be needed. For
       comparison, a large lot residential development would result in roughly 100 units and
       would generate 1,000 daily trips.

       According to the City’s Public Works Department, the Monticello Academy located at
       2782 South Corporate Park Drive does create a surge in traffic and on-street parking
       during drop-off and pick-up times.

       Taxable Value
       Taxable value is the assessed value of property the County Assessor uses to calculate
       property tax. In the case of residential property, taxable value is set at 55% of assessed
       value. In the case of commercial property, taxable value is set at 100% of assessed value.

       According to the applicant, the taxable value of the proposed biomedical project is
       estimated at $160 million. Using the average taxable value of homes in the Newton Farm
       Subdivision for reference, the taxable value of a large lot residential development would
       be about $42 million.

       Employment/Population
       According to the applicant, an estimated 1,500 to 2,100 persons would be employed at the
       proposed biomedical campus. These employees would earn an average of $65,000 per
       year with salary and wages ranging from $60,000 - $200,000 per year.
       In contrast, a large lot residential development would result in about 100 homes with an
       estimated population of 380 persons.
       Zoning Regulations
       The requested B/RP zone is different from the M zone found on adjoining properties in
       several ways. First, the B/RP zone is more restrictive than the M zone when it comes to
       allowed land uses. For example, the B/RP zone prohibits uses like heavy truck sales and
       service, heavy industrial, outside storage, self-storage facilities, towing and impound
       yards, truck transfer companies, and vehicle recycling facilities while the M zone allows
       these uses. For reference, a copy of the commercial and manufacturing zone use table is
       included with this report. Second, the B/RP zone requires a minimum of 15% landscaping
       coverage while the M zone only requires 5%. Third, the front setback is 30’ in the B/RP
       zone and 20’ in the M zone.

       Transit Access
       Lake Park Blvd. is served by UTA bus route 513 which offers very limited weekday
       service in the early morning and late afternoon terminating at the Fairbourne Station bus
       hub to the south and the Salt Lake Central Station to the north. The Wasatch Front
       Regional Council’s Regional Transportation Plan (RTP) includes a future bus route that
       would begin at 5600 West and Lake Park Blvd., travel east along Lake Park Blvd. and
       Parkway Blvd. to Redwood Road, and eventually end at the Central Pointe TRAX station
       on 2100 South between 300 West and West Temple.

Other Potential Considerations

In addition to the points raised above, there are other potential considerations that the
Commission may want to weigh when evaluating this application. These other potential
considerations are addressed below.

       Opportunity Zone Designation
       In 2018, the State designated the area between SR-201 and 3100 South and from 5600
       West and 3600 West as an Opportunity Zone. The Opportunity Zone Program is a federal
       program designed to incentivize capital investment through tax incentives offered to
       investors that hold their investments with the greatest benefit going to those investors who
       hold their investment for 10 or more years. Hence, developers have an added incentive
       within this area to build and hold commercial development as opposed to homes that are
       developed and sold.

       Nearby Biomedical Uses
       There are two biomedical uses either under construction or planned within Lake Park.
       Nusano, a manufacturer of radioisotopes, is currently under construction at 4061 West
       Parkway Blvd. The applicant is also currently working with a medical manufacturing
       company on the parcel at 2778 South Leadership Ct. in Lake Park that specializes in the
       design, development, and manufacturing of medical ultrasonic components.

       Prohibition on Private Streets
       As the Commission may recall, the City recently amended the Zoning Ordinance to
       prohibit private streets. If this application is approved, this prohibition could potentially
       affect the layout of the development.
Buffering from Industrial Uses
The new industrial buildings to the north of the subject property are separated from the
subject property by the Riter Canal, landscaping on the north bank of the canal, a 6’ tall
masonry wall, additional landscaping north of the masonry wall, and parking (not truck
parking). The setback of these industrial buildings from the north property line of the
subject property varies from about 120’ to 260’. In addition, there are no truck loading
docks along the south side of any of the industrial buildings.

Undeveloped Land within Lake Park Corporate Center
Within Lake Park Corporate Center there are approximately 53 acres of undeveloped land
that could potentially be developed as biomedical uses. In addition, the large parking lots
within Lake Park are currently underutilized given the increase in remote work. Should
this trend continue, there may be potential for additional infill development within Lake
Park.

During the study session, the Commission asked if the applicant had considered acquiring
the remaining vacant property within the Lake Park Corporate Center. Staff contacted the
applicant and learned that they did reach out to the owners of the remaining vacant
properties to acquire them. However, these owners were either not interested in selling or
not willing to sell at a price that was acceptable to the applicant.

Bridges
There were two bridges installed to connect the north and west sides of the subject
property with the adjoining properties across the waterways. If this application is
approved, these bridges may no longer be needed.

Assurance of Biomedical Use
While the B/RP zone requested by the applicant is more restrictive than the M zone that
exists on some of the neighboring properties, the B/RP zone still allows uses like general
light industrial and warehousing that aren’t related to biomedical uses. There are
potentially several benefits to the City of a biomedical campus over just general
warehousing and/or general light industrial. These include significantly less semi-truck
traffic, higher paying jobs, a higher taxable value, and synergy with other biomedical uses.
However, if this application is approved without a development agreement commitment
for biomedical uses, the property could be developed as just general light
industrial/warehouse.

Architecture
The applicant included renderings of the proposed buildings in the application. These
renderings show large window areas, large roof overhangs, and other features. If this
application is approved, staff recommends a development agreement that incorporates
these renderings.

Industrial Development in the Larger Area
The applicant provided a map, which is included with this report, that highlights industrial
       development that has occurred in the larger vicinity since 2015 when the subject property
       was zoned A and A-2.

       Future Residential Development Nearby
       The vacant property to the south of the subject property includes a development agreement
       that entitled the ground for 69 townhomes and 89 small lot single unit dwellings.

       School District Property
       Staff contacted Granite School District to see what their latest plans are for the parcel west
       of the subject property. The District intends to continue holding this property and monitor
       student generation rates.

Development Agreement

If the Commission is supportive of this application, staff recommends a development agreement
to address two of the issues raised earlier in this report. First, to ensure that the project is not a
distribution warehouse development, staff recommends that the overall project be limited to a
total of 18 semi-truck docks and that a minimum of 40% of the total building area be used for
office, research space, lab space, and clean rooms with the balance being manufacturing and
storage space. For reference, a typical distribution warehouse facility with the same building area
would have around 90 semi-truck docks. Also, staff generally sees about 20% of large tilt-up
concrete buildings used as office with the balance being warehousing or other industrial uses.

Second, staff recommends that the renderings submitted by the applicant be included in the
development agreement as a standard for the architecture. In addition, staff also recommends that
the development agreement make clear the City’s commercial design standards would apply to
this development, meaning further architectural enhancements may be required.

Staff Alternatives:

   1. Approval. This application should be approved subject to a development agreement that
      incorporates the standards recommended in this report.

   2. Continuance. This application should be continued for reasons determined during the
      public hearing.

   3. Denial. This application should be denied as residential development is more appropriate
      at this location.

GPZ-8-2022
Bryan and Chantel Bradford
3816 South 3200 West
Zone change from A to R-1-8
0.69 acres

Bryan and Chantel Bradford have submitted a General Plan/zone change application for two
parcels totaling 0.69 acres located at 3816 South 3200 West. The applicants reside at the subject
property. The property is currently zoned A (Agriculture, minimum lot size ½ acre) with a
General Plan designation of Large Lot Residential (2 to 3 units/acre). The proposed zone is R-1-8
(Single Unit Dwelling Residential, minimum lot size 8,000 sq. ft.) and the proposed General Plan
designation is Low Density Residential (3 to 4 units/acre). As a reminder, City ordinance only
allows zone changes to R-1-8 on properties less than 2 acres.

Surrounding zones include R-1-8 to the north and east and A to the south and west. The property
is surrounded by single unit dwellings except for one duplex on the east side of 3200 West.

Development Proposal

The subject property includes a home that was built in 1941. According to the applicants, they
would like to subdivide the property into three lots, keep the existing home on one of the lots, and
build two new homes on the other two lots. The applicants have also indicated that they plan to
move the current detached garage closer to the existing home and improve the appearance of the
structure. In addition to the relocated garage, they plan to add a new driveway with the ability to
park 4 cars in the driveway.

Development Agreement

A development agreement is required for this project. Section 7-11-213 of the West Valley City
Zoning Ordinance states: “All new Subdivisions involving a rezone of property, or a PUD, shall
participate in a Development Agreement that addresses housing size, quality, exterior finish
materials, Streetscapes, Landscaping, etc.” For the development agreement, the applicant is
proposing to use the same standards that were in place up until July of last year along with a few
additional standards included by the applicant. These standards are attached for reference.

After a site visit, staff identified some items that we would recommend addressing in the
development agreement. These items are described below:
    There are weeds growing between the curb and the sidewalk and between the stamped
        concrete parkstrip and the sidewalk.
    The existing garage is in poor condition, doesn’t meet current setbacks, and would no
        longer be on the same lot as the existing home.
    The existing chain link fence along the front and side of the property is damaged.
    The landscaping on the property is in poor condition and in need of maintenance and/or
        replacement.
    The exterior of the existing home is mostly a grey colored brick that is discolored in some
        areas.
      If the property is subdivided as proposed, the rear (west side of house) setback for the
       existing home would technically be around 12’. Normally, the rear yard setback in the R-
       1-8 zone is 20’; however, for homes with a two-car garage built prior to July 8, 1986, the
       rear yard setback may be reduced to 15’.
      It appeared from the exterior of the home that only window mounted A/C units were
       available for cooling.

Staff Alternatives:

      Approval. The application should be approved. The recommendation for approval is made
       subject to a development agreement including the following standards:
          o The two new homes shall follow the standards proposed by the applicant.
          o All weeds within the right-of-way and behind the curb shall be removed.
          o The existing detached, two-car garage shall be demolished and replaced with a
               new garage placed on the lot with the existing home in a location that meets the
               City’s setback requirements.
          o The existing chain link fence shall be removed, repaired, or replaced.
          o All dead and weedy landscaping shall be replaced with landscaping that complies
               with City standards.
          o The brick and wood portions of the exterior of the existing home shall be painted.
          o The rear yard setback for the existing home may be reduced to a minimum of 10’.
          o The window mounted A/C units in the existing home shall be replaced with central
               A/C.

      Continuance.

      Denial. A larger lot zone like R-1-12 or RE would comply with the existing General Plan
       designation and allow the two existing lots to be reconfigured to allow one new building
       lot.
