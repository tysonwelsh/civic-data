I.    Welcome – Mayor Pro-Tem Gray.

II.   Pledge of Allegiance.
Mayor Dahle led the Pledge of Allegiance.

III.     Public Comments.
Lynn Pace thanked the City Council for their service to the community. He noted that many of
the things discussed while he was on the City Council have since been achieved. The City Council
is doing a marvelous job and has created a wonderful community. Mr. Pace next shared a concern
with the Council. There used to be a freshly cut Christmas tree each year, but now there is a
pyramid of strung lights. The lights do not create a sense of place and he suggested that there be
a real tree instead. He reported that a previous Master Plan for the plaza had called for a festival
tree. When there used to be a real tree lit, visitors would go out of their way to drive through the
City, because the tree created an atmosphere in the Village Center. The string of lights hung in
the shape of a pyramid does not create a sense of community or encourage people to visit. Mr.
Pace asked the City Council to do something more so that the City is a wonderful place to be
during the holidays. He was willing to donate a live tree to the community for everyone to enjoy
this holiday.

IV.      Public Hearing on Proposed Amendments to Title 7 – Administrative Code Enforcement
         Program.

additional amendments in the future. For instance, ensuring that there are no conflicts of interest.
Council Member Brewer explained that the role of Code Enforcement is important Code
Enforcement Officers interface closely with residents on various issues. He commended the Code
Enforcement Officers for their professional behavior. The law is not perfect, and one size does
not fit all, so he stressed the importance of fostering a positive culture. It is important to encourage
one another to be good citizens. Additionally, it is always beneficial to attempt to work things out

VII.    Consideration of Ordinance 2025-19 – Amending Title 13.86.020 to Add 2052 East
        Arbor Lane to Holladay’s Historic Designation List.
Council Member Durham was grateful that there are residents in the community who are
committed to historic preservation, especially when it involves their own private property. The
designation process is voluntary and involves a request from someone who cares about the historic
nature of their home. He appreciates the homeowners for choosing to make this request. Council
Member Brewer echoed those comments and commended the homeowners for their work.

Council Member Quinn moved to ADOPT Ordinance 2025-19 – Amending Title 13.86.020 to
Add 2052 East Arbor Lane to Holladay’s Historic Designation List. Council Member Brewer
seconded the motion. Vote on Motion: Council Member Gray-Yes; Council Member Quinn-Yes;
Council Member Fotheringham-Yes; Council Member Durham-Yes; Council Member Brewer-
Yes; Mayor Dahle-Yes. The motion passed unanimously.

VIII. City Manager Report – Gina Chamness.
City Manager Gina Chamness shared an update with the City Council. She reported that there is
a potential donation from the Knudsen Family. There is a proposed donation of an aluminum sign
that would stretch from one monument to the other. This would create more of an entrance to the
park. The Knudsen Family is proposing to donate that metal sign to the City and the value is
approximately $18,000. This was an idea that was considered during the planning process for

Knudsen, but as the budget for that project was finalized, the sign was eliminated. The Knudsen
Family explored the idea of cost-sharing with the City, but because it was not a priority, the family
returned with the idea to donate the money for the sign in its entirety. The City would be
responsible for some engineering costs to make sure the sign was secure, but she does not have a
cost estimate for that. She assumes that it would cost less than $5,000 for engineering costs related
to the sign. Before the City accepts this generous donation, she wanted to ask the City Council for

Council Member Gray expressed support for the aluminum sign. She was grateful for the
generosity of the Knudsen Family. Council Member Brewer asked if the City would be obliged
to maintain the sign if the name of the park ever changed in the future. Ms. Chamness believed
this would need to be addressed through some sort of agreement. That should be fairly simple to
do.

Council Member Fotheringham noted that in the past there were budgetary constraints that
prevented the City Council from being able to add the sign. It was wonderful that the Knudsen
Family has now expressed a willingness to donate the money for the sign. Ms. Chamness thanked
Council Members for the feedback and reported that the City will move forward with this and
accept the donation.

IX.      City Council Reports and District Issues.
Council Member Gray shared information about her experience with the Central Wasatch
Commission (“CWC”) where she represents the City of Holladay. This was her first meeting as a
representative of the City. She informed the Council that there have been discussions about a
potential Millcreek Canyon shuttle. There is a Feasibility Study being conducted for the shuttle.
There was a public comment period held over the summer, and another comment period is now
open. There seems to be a lot of public support for a Millcreek Canyon shuttle, but the study will
ensure that a shuttle could accommodate dogs, ski equipment, picnicking items, and so on. There
are some practical concerns that will be addressed through the study. Something else to be aware
of is that there have been discussions about increased bus service in the canyons for the 2025/2026
ski season. Utah Transit Authority (“UTA”) has locked in its schedule, but other options are being
explored.

Council Member Gray reported that Wasatch Front Waste and Recycling is going to move to
monthly billing starting in January. She informed those present that there have been some
complaints and concerns from people in the Heughs Canyon neighborhood. Residents are
wondering whether the City can provide doggie bags for the people who are taking their dogs on
that trail.

Council Member Quinn reported that she touched base with the Utah Department of Transportation
(UDOT”) about the crosswalk across 4500 South over to the park. She wanted an update on their
schedule and was informed that there is still a plan for that work to move forward. Originally, it
was stated that it would be constructed this fall, but she has now been informed that the work will
take place at the beginning of the year. Despite the slight delay, the work is still planned to occur.

Council Member Fotheringham shared information about Holladay Arts-related events. There is
