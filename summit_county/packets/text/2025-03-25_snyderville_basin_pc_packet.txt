Proposal 
 
The applicant is requesting a Master Planned Development (MPD) for the Outlets at Park City, a 
19-building mixed use redevelopment project located at 6699 N Landmark Dr (Parcel FSE-1), 
Kimball Junction, Summit County, UT.  The applicant is proposing to Rezone Parcel FSE-1 from 
the Town Center Zone to the Neighborhood Mixed-Use 1 (NMU-1) Zone. 
 
The project includes the redevelopment of the upper portion of the property into a residential 
project while the lower portion of the property would be developed with additional infill 
development.  
 
The upper portion of the property is currently developed with ~98,000 sq. ft. of retail space. 
The applicant is proposing to demolish ~82,000 sq. ft. of the existing retail and replace it with a 
10-building residential project which includes 325 units (313 apartments and 42 townhomes) 
and a 7,200 sq. ft. amenity/clubhouse space within one of the three apartment buildings. One 
of the buildings is proposed to be mixed use and would contain 7,300 sq. ft. commercial. The 
applicant is also proposing two parking structures in the upper portion of the property. 
 
The lower portion of the property is currently developed with ~230,000 sq. ft. of retail space. 
The applicant is proposing to infill the parking area with a 9-building mixed-use project with 
ground floor commercial (~54,000 sq. ft.) and upper floor residential (77 apartments).

improving transit, and encouraging trip reductions strategies that may further reduce trips beyond 
what the analysis estimates.

ITE Parking Demand Rates  
Hales Engineering referred to the Institute of Transportation Engineers (ITE) Parking Generation 
(6th Edition, 2023) to identify parking demand rates for the study land uses. ITE has gathered 
actual parking demand counts at various land uses and identified average, 85 th percentile, and 
maximum rates. The 85 th percentile rate represents a demand that is higher than 85 percent of 
study sites. The industry standard is to apply this rate. Hales Engineering calculated the 
anticipated parking demand based on the 85th percentile rates. Since Saturdays have the highest 
rates for the proposed land uses, these numbers were used in the analysis. The number of stalls 
needed for each phase based on these rates is shown in Table 4. As shown, ITE would suggest 
that the parking demand for the proposed project will be 1,115 stalls at full build. 
Table 4: ITE Parking Generation 
 
Since the ITE rates represent actual parking demand, it is common to provide a parking supply 
beyond what the anticipated demand is to accommodate occasional surges in demand and to 
reduce the need for drivers to circle the parking lot to find an open stall. For commercial areas, 
Hales Engineering recommends providing 10% additional stalls beyond the anticipated demand, 
and for residential areas, an additional 5% (except for the townhomes with private garages). 
Based on this, a baseline supply of 1,199 stalls would be recommended for the project based on 
ITE data without considering shared parking.

Local Parking Demand 
A site visit was made to confirm the existing number of parking stalls and count the number of 
occupied stalls per hour on Saturday, July 10, 2021, from 11:30 am – 6:30 pm. There are currently

hour. Based on the occupied amount of retail square footage, the demand rate was found to be 
2.91 parking stalls per thousand square feet. With a 10% increase to determine the recommended 
supply, a parking supply rate of 3.20 was calculated for the existing commercial at the Outlets.

Background 
 
The existing Rocky Mountain Summit Park Substation located on Parcel S-59 has been serving 
the Summit Park neighborhood area for ~50 years. 
 
Summit County is a designated Fire High Consequence Area (FHCA), and the purpose of the 
project is to rebuild and expand the existing Summit Park Substation as part of an ongoing 
effort for wildfire mitigation. According to the applicant, the substation and associated 
equipment will be rebuilt to the up-to-date national electric safety codes and avian safe 
standards. The upgrade will also accommodate existing and future electric power needs for 
Summit County.  
 
The upgrade from 46 kilowatts (kV) to 138kV on the transmission line and the substation 
doubles the capacity of the Summit Park Substation. It goes from a 5 megavolt-amperes (MVA) 
transformer to a 10 MVA transformer. The project’s primary purpose is to mitigate wildfire 
risks. Another benefit of the project is the increased reliability and lifespan of the equipment.  
 
The additional growth and demand for electrical services requires upgrades to equipment to 
meet the additional demand. Electrical facilities provide services that support health and safety 
throughout the Snyderville Basin.
