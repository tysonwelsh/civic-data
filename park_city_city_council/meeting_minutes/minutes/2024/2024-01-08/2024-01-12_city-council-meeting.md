HOUSING AUTHORITY DISCUSSION    
 
1. Potential Regional Housing Authority Discussion  
Jeff Jones, Summit County Economic Development and Housing Director, began the 
meeting by reviewing the staff report with Park City Housing Director, Jason Glidden, 
who added goals of the Housing Authority. Mayor Worel asked about different funding 
streams available other than voucher programs and Glidden believed there were other  JOINT PARK CITY AND SUMMIT COUNTY COUNCIL MEETING

 types of federal funding available for housing authorities, but stated they would be 
 geared towards individual developments and programming.  
   
 City Council Member Parigian asked for examples of other funding programs and Jones 
 cited a Housing and Urban Development (HUD) grant last year called "Pathways to 
 Removing Obstacles to Housing" which was well funded because housing was a 
 countrywide focus now. Utah was focused on housing also and he was confident there 
 would be more money going forward.    
   
 County Council Member Hanson thanked everyone involved and asked for clarification 
 between core services and enhanced services. Glidden distinguished that core services 
 were essential things that needed to be done now, such as marketing, one-stop shop 
 for applications, compliance, and education. To go into the development side of things, 
 additional staff, expertise, and resources would be needed at the next level, which 
 required an enhanced skill set.    
   
 City Council Member Ciraco asked what the hurdles would be and whether the housing 
 authority would be geared to address those, such as zoning or land use issues. Glidden 
 concurred there would be challenges regarding land use and zoning, resources, and 
 political will, i.e. community support. Jones chimed in land and construction costs, 
 higher interest rates, regulatory environment, and balancing between meeting future 
 demand and protecting what was desirable and valued in the community were all 
 hurdles. The housing question was complex, and he pondered whether that would be 
 best solved with a committee or a housing authority.  
   
 Chair Stevens inquired if the housing authority could manage the current deed 
 restrictions in the County and City in addition to whatever else was worked on, and 
 Glidden conceded it was the intention to have the housing authority take over all the 
 affordable units within the County and City that required compliance checks.  
   
 Council Member Dickey doubted the housing authority was the right tool and cautioned 
 that it would introduce risks and costs without much benefit now. It would add another 
 layer that would be more duplication and impediment rather than truly independent.  
 Glidden clarified that the City housing department wouldn't disappear right away but 
 there would be a transition period with staff reduction and cost savings being transferred 
 to the authority over time. He agreed that the housing authority needed to be 
 independent. Glidden used the City's Clark Ranch property as an example of the City 
 Council still retaining control and ownership but possibly partnering with the authority to 
 develop the site. He indicated this would be an enhanced service level, and that 
 additional budget would be needed, but for the subsidy to the housing authority to be 
 reduced, it would need to generate additional revenue from development projects.      
   
 City Council Member Toly asked whether the County was comfortable giving direction 
 with two members absent and Chair Stevens assured that she could give direction

 based on the comments from the last meeting and conversations she had with them. 
 City Council Member Toly asserted there was a big housing hole and if this wasn't the 
 way to fill it then they should use their resources a different way because there had 
 been a lot of intense time spent on this so far.    
   
 City Council Member Parigian asserted there were some benefits in the core services 
 such as sharing experts in the field and collaboration, but he felt uncertain about the 
 enhanced services and didn't believe in being half in and half out. He was not 
 supportive now. City Council Member Toly reiterated the housing authority was the way 
 to go and disagreed that a committee plus would accomplish anything.    
   
 Mayor Worel pointed out that Summit County and Park City received bad press 
 regarding affordable housing. She noted there was a lot of good synergy between the 
 Councils, however she wondered if there was another model that would be appropriate 
 as one resource where citizens could go for information and applications. She 
 suspected there were many things that could be done together but she was not 
 convinced it must be a regional housing authority.    
   
 City Council Member Rubell placed a lot of weight on the recommendation by the 
 housing team at both the County and City, but the timing was difficult for him.  He 
 suggested the City and County come up with a collaborative project with a committee 
 model first, and then entertain a formal authority when there was a better idea of what 
 giving up control would mean. He did not support deciding if it was all or nothing now.  
 Down the road, perhaps other municipalities would want to join the City and County 
 once they noticed how Park City and Summit County worked together.  
   
 County Council Member Robinson indicated the County had not developed affordable 
 housing on its own, but the County acquired land. To create the authority with $1.5 
 million for core services wouldn't build anything, it was only to start the authority. He 
 desired to see the same results in the County as the City had with delivering units. He 
 didn't believe this model would do that in the most efficient way. He added that the full 
 Council should decide unanimously on something this major. If the collective wisdom 
 was to go ahead with this then he wouldn't stand in the way.  
   
 County Council Member Hanson asserted she was all in. The City was doing great 
 work. Right now, the County was doing and spending nothing, so the County needed 
 help. She didn't hear anything on the table to accomplish affordable housing other than 
 the housing authority.      
   
 City Council Member Ciraco echoed the concerns of Mayor Worel, and Council 
 Members Dickey, Robinson, and Rubell. He reasoned there needed to be a focus on 
 making more happen. The City had done a lot without a housing authority therefore he 
 doubted creating a housing authority would make a difference in the context of 
 partnering with the County. He surmised it was less a question of money but more

 about land use, zoning, and political will, and he doubted the housing authority would 
 address those issues. He speculated the County should lead the formation of a housing 
 authority and then it might be easier to justify the cost to further that effort.  
   
 Chair Stevens acknowledged that at every other meeting, the opinion for forming a 
 housing authority had flip-flopped. Taking politics out and creating a separate body was 
 what appealed to her. She acknowledged both organizations had done housing in 
 different ways with different philosophies. The County focused on inclusionary zoning 
 and working with developers to provide more affordable housing whereas the City had 
 been the developer. She wanted to know how they could establish an effective body 
 given that they had been so inconsistent with their opinions and direction to staff.    
   
 City Council Member Rubell noted this had been a learning process for them, and the 
 additional information influenced opinions along the way. City Council Member Toly 
 questioned if they could do better housing if they allowed experts to do it rather than 
 elected officials, which was why she appreciated this solution because the government 
 was taken out of it.    
   
 County Council Member Hanson clarified that this was the only path for the County to 
 develop their own housing. She concurred with Council Member Toly to give the 
 expertise to the experts. City Council Member Dickey repeated that he didn't want 
 duplication, and this wasn't the right tool at this time. He didn't see the housing authority 
 as an accelerator. City Council Member Parigian appreciated that they were responsive 
 to citizen voices. Council Member Robinson proposed a business plan for the housing 
 authority that stipulated $2 million from each entity with the goal to build a specific 
 housing goal in the next five years. He noted he only supported it if everyone else did 
 too.  
   
 Chair Stevens offered time at the next meeting for absent Council members to voice 
 their comments and instructed staff not to do any more work on this in the meantime, 
 and then a final decision could be made. Mayor Worel agreed this was an important 
 topic and it was critical that each Council could speak with one voice, so she requested 
 that Park City Council have their own conversation about a housing authority and return 
 with a united voice at the next meeting.  
   
 County Council Member Robinson gauged the interest in a business partnership plan 
 and City Council Member Toly reminded each Council of the table of six or seven 
 different options presented at the November meeting that they could consider as they 
 decided if something else made more sense. City Council Member Ciraco looked 
 forward to the point when they would have a chance to do something transformational 
 and suggested they pick a project, do a letter of intent to work together, and that would 
 inform how they would form a future housing authority.

 SUNDANCE FILM FESTIVAL PREVIEW  
 Park City Special Events Manager Jenny Diersen remarked that they were excited to 
 welcome the 40th Annual Sundance Film Festival and indicated the economic and 
 cultural opportunities would be countless. This would be an 11-day in-person festival. 
 They were anticipating pre-pandemic numbers for attendance. She thanked the 
 departments in the County and the City that had collaborated on this effort. She 
 highlighted that the City had four priorities in terms of transportation: public safety, 
 reducing residential impacts, transit, and drop off/loading. The City would be running 
 transit from 5:30 a.m. to 2:05 a.m. to make sure people would have opportunities not to 
 be in their cars. In addition, Sundance worked with Deer Valley and Park City Mountain 
 Resort to allow people to keep cars parked in the lots at night. She acknowledged that 
 community outreach to residents had been extensive. No comments or concerns were 
 voiced by any Council members.    
   
 SEASONAL WORKFORCE HOUSING COMMITTEE UPDATE
