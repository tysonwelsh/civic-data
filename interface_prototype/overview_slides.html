A project in progress

Utah Civic Data

Every level of Utah government — cities, counties, regional planning, and the Legislature — in one place, and askable in plain English.

The problem

Growth decisions are made in hundreds of separate rooms.

Each publishes its own records, in its own format, on its own website. Following one project across all of them takes weeks. Nobody can see across.

What you can ask it

Questions that used to take weeks

  • How did my council member vote on housing — every time?
  • Which cities approve the most density, and which reject it?
  • Where did a council overrule its own planning commission?
  • Who funded this campaign — and how did that official later vote?
  • What's actually funded to get built near me?
Why this is possible now

Three things that couldn't be done before

  • One structure across every government Forty-four governments publishing in forty-four formats, federated into a single shape — so a question can follow a rezone from the neighborhood, to the regional plan, to the state law behind both. Connections that exist nowhere else
  • Records no machine could read Handwritten disclosures. Sideways scans. Spreadsheets stapled to forms. Read by sight, transcribed, and checked against the totals printed on each page. Now countable — and answerable in plain language
  • A harvester built for every city Every government publishes differently, so each needs its own retrieval program — thirty-one cities, eight counties, the Legislature. Written with AI in days rather than years. Previously prohibitive, one city at a time
