4. Discussion of Ordinance Amending Code Chapter 2.56 Community Districts and
Community Councils

Mayor Silvestrini said he wanted to bring the attention of the Council to the need to look at
code regarding the community councils. He said the substance of the ordinance would not
change with this ordinance, but when the City brought code over from the County there was
some language lost in translation. This is a clarifying ordinance that the community councils
will review before it is approved. Council Member Jackson noticed language that said
planning issues would be sent to the Chair, but packets should go to the whole council. John
Brems said he will adjust the language accordingly.

5. Discussion on Engineering Department Budget Adjustments

b. Scott’s Avenue Storm Drain Emergency Repairs

John Miller said the budget from last year has line items for capital improvement projects. He
has moved around the amounts in the budget. He said there was a discretionary line item that
was taken out, so the funds were eliminated to bring it down to $1.2 million. Next year’s
budget will increase by $300,000, to $1.5 million.

Miller said there are 4 projects that require a change. The first is for an emergency storm
drain repair on Scott’s Avenue. The drain has rusted out on the bottom and collapsed in a
section, so it needs to be repaired. The cost will be approximately $40,000.

The second is an ongoing project on 2300 E. on the west side. He said last year some
sidewalks and park strips were corrected and curb and gutter were raised. A driveway was
missed in the correction and is now too steep. The City needs to have the driveway fixed and
a bid was received from Beck for $7,000. Mayor Silvestrini asked if there is a warranty issue.
Miller said there is not a warranty issue because everything was built to design, but the
design was bad. He said that since this is an existing project, the County controls the funding
on the project. Instead of delaying the project and waiting on a transfer of money to
Millcreek, the City will pay for it then get reimbursed by the MSD Board.

Miller said the third is a modification with JUB Engineering on the transportation master
plan studies. The original assumption was a hybrid model, but it was determined that it will
not be sufficient, so the budget change is for a change order to that contract.

The last project update is to the Upland Dr. sidewalk. The current estimates are at $450,000,
so roughly $250,000 of the budgeted funds will be able to be reallocated, but they will
remain intact until the project bids come back.

Mayor Silvestrini brought up the status of the engineering interlocal agreement with the
County. The City tried to renegotiate the contract because costs seemed excessive, but
ultimately all services were terminated effective March 31“. The City is hiring a consultant
firm for plan reviews and an additional staff member. He gave assurance to the public and
Council that the engineering work is being done and it makes more sense for the City
financially to bring the services in-house.

The Mayor has asked the MSD Board to transfer current project funds to Millcreek to finish
the projects. Council Member Marchant thanked the Mayor for looking out for the City. The
Mayor said he relied on the recommendations of John Miller and John Geilmann on the
contract.

Miller said that the City’s baseline budget is $1.5 million with $2.4 million in grants and
other funding; $4 million worth of capital improvement projects.

1. Environmental Crimes Task Force District Attorney’s Office Presentation; Lt. Alex
Huggard

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 9 April 2018 Page 3 of 9

Ron Lund with Salt Lake County Health Department and Lt. Alex Huggard with Salt Lake
County’s District Attorney’s Office said they wanted to give a presentation on what they
could do for the City. They went through case examples of illegal dumping, meth in
businesses, etc. that negatively impact the environment. Council Member Marchant asked if
they regulate noise ordinances. The Mayor added to the question by asking if they work with
cities on their own noise ordinances. He said it is up to the City for enforcement of their
ordinances. Lund said the County’s noise standard is based on data and measurement.
Council Member Marchant asked what problems they see in Millcreek. They see transient
camps, business’ and other discharge, and dumping. Council Member Catten asked about air
pollution. Lund said they work with the State but there is a solid fuel burning regulation.
Lund said the County has an environmental task force that meets every month. Entities on the
task force include the health department, code enforcement, district attorney’s office, the
EPA, storm water, fire departments and the like. They coordinate information and share
resources. They have an administrative process with warning letters, notice of violation and
permit suspension and a criminal process. They require the violators to create a plan to
prevent their actions from taking place again. Each year they do a cleanup project on Earth
Day in partnership with Murray City. They hope to protect the environment through
education and enforcement, reinvest in partners, and learn from others through the
multicounty task force. They also provide agency trainings.

2. Discussion on Reinvestment Areas; Economic Development Director

Mike Winder said the newly created Community Reinvestment Agency will help the City
create reinvestment areas. Winder is looking at 4 areas of focus that he identified as: West
Millcreek Urban Renewal Area, Millcreek City Center known as “the wedge” between
Highland Dr. and 1300 E., Canyon Rim Crossings around 2300 E. and 3300 S., and Olympus
Hills. The West Millcreek Urban Renewal Area already exists, and the City will be asking
the County to transfer the administration of it to Millcreek.

Mayor Silvestrini said staff has been talking about development interests in all the
development areas with businesses. Winder said the next step is a proposed survey area for
each of the development areas. Mayor Silvestrini said there will be a public process before
the City gets going. Winder sent out a letter to all the businesses in Millcreek asking how the
City can support them.

3. Discussion on Firework Boundaries; Fire Marshal Brad Larsen

Mayor Silvestrini said the new legislation he advocated for about restricting fireworks
boundaries passed. He said the Council needs to decide on a restriction area for discharging
fireworks and will start with the same areas as last year. Chief Larsen said the local
legislative bodies must provide a map to their county by May Ist. He does not anticipate
recommending any additional areas be restricted in Millcreek. The counties must publish the
map by June 1“ and distribute them to the fireworks distributors. He said the Salt Lake
County Emergency Management office draws up the maps.

The Council reviewed the existing map on the Unified Fire website. Mayor Silvestrini said
he thinks 200ft. from the Jordan River is sufficient. The Council considered the boundary
being 700 West but decided to extend it from the Jordan River to 500 West. Mayor
Silvestrini feels the other boundaries are well defined. Council Member Jackson questioned
including Big Cottonwood Park. The Council considered the area between 900 East and 1100

RM and Adoption of a Development Agreement for a 22-unit Townhome Planned
Unit Development; ZM-17-001, located at approx. 1200 E. 4500 S. by applicant

Francis Lilly said this item went before the Planning Commission in November and
March. The applicant would like to build a 22-unit townhome development. He said the
community council has also seen the application twice in addition to there being a
neighborhood meeting. The application consists of 2-bedroom units with a two-story
height limit. An issue that came up previously is that the property owner would own all
the units and the development would be treated as a rental. The only access will be
located near the corner of 1200 E. and 4500 S.

The development agreement has a new exhibit B that was distributed by Lilly. Lilly read
over the changes in the exhibit, which are that the project will not exceed 31 feet in
height, the brick or stone veneer will compose at least 17 percent of front and side
elevations, and the project will not utilize aluminum or vinyl siding except of use as soffit
or fascia material. The narrative in the exhibit matches the drawings with the amended
version. The owner will be dedicating property to UDOT but will landscape and maintain
the property until UDOT uses it. Mayor Silvestrini clarified that the reason to have the
project comply with the PUD code is that it would give the land owner the option at some
point to sell the units off individually rather than renting them and if it was not a PUD,
the property would not be able to be separated. Lilly informed the Mayor that he is
correct, and the PUD gives the City more leverage. Council Member Marchant asked if
there will be signage on the street that prohibits parking on the street as listed on #6 in the
exhibit. Lilly said he spoke with residents about the possibility of creating a residential
parking district. Council Member Marchant is worried about visitors parking on 1200 E.
Lilly said the Planning Commission did recommend approval subject to the development
agreement. The community council recommended denial of the application because of
parking impacts and concerns about density.

Applicant Kasey Kershaw said he is open to suggestions and is willing to notify residents
about parking and enforce it. Council Member Uipi asked how long the public process
would be with creating a parking district. Francis said it would be a few months long with
public hearings. Kershaw said the advantage to this development’s design is less
bedrooms, so less cars, and a smaller footprint with a driveway.

Mayor Silvestrini asked if there was any public comment on the matter.

Pam Samuelson, 4528 S. 1200 E., addressed the Council about the inconveniences this
development will cause. She raised concern with access, more traffic, and that it will be
harder to turn left on 4500 S. She asked for 6 parking signs on 1200 E. with residential
permit parking. Council Member Jackson referenced the Grandeur Peak parking permits
in that the residents only get 2 passes, which will affect her too. Council Member Uipi
said the Grandeur Peak parking situation was different. Samuelson said the residents on
1200 E. bought their houses for the quiet street and asked the Council to consider that.

Linda Allen, 1249 E. Sophomore Circle, asked at what point the existing neighborhoods
matter. She read a list of statements she heard from the Planning Commission and the
developer. She said if the Council approves the rezone, the density will be greater with

2.2 Discussion and Consideration of Ordinance 18-23 and Ordinance 18-24, Rezone
from R-1-21 to R-1-15 and Adoption of a Development Agreement for a Two-Lot
Subdivision; ZM-18-001, located at approx. 3821 E. Parkview Dr. by applicant
Robert and Alison Renza

Lilly said the application is for a rezone so that the property can be subdivided into 2 lots.
The subdivision would not be possible in the current zoning. Lilly said surrounding lots
range from a quarter acre to almost an acre. One of the issues that staff encountered was
that the R-1-15 Zone is not subject to the Residential Compatibility standards, so staff is
proposing a development agreement that puts some of those standards on the subdivision.
Lilly read through the development agreement considerations.

Development Agreement Considerations

Millcreek City Council Meeting Minutes 9 April 2018 Page 7 of 9

In order to ensure compatibility with the surrounding neighborhood, staff is recommending
that the rezone be subject to a development agreement that addresses, at a minimum, the
following:

1. The applicant will subdivide the lots on or before 9 August 2018.

2. The maximum height of a home on proposed lot 1 will be 30 feet above natural grade, up

to an elevation of 5,005 feet.

3. The maximum height of a home on proposed lot 2 will be 30 feet above natural grade.

4. The homes on both lots shall comply with the building envelope requirements of the City’s

Residential Compatibility Overlay Zone, specifically section 19.71.030 (B)(6).

5. The maximum lot coverage for any structure on proposed lots 1 and 2 shall not exceed 28
percent of either lot. Lot coverage is defined in section 19.71.060 (C) of the Millcreek
Code.

6. The home proposed for lot 1 shall front Parkview Drive. There shall be a minimum 30-foot

setback from the property line along Parkview Drive and any proposed home.

Lilly said staff and the Planning Commission support the agreement and zone change.
The community council’s recommendation was favorable, but there was some concern
that the development could be out of scale. Council Member Uipi asked how the 30ft
setback was determined. Lilly said that it is the setback in the R-1-15 Zone.

Robert Renza said he started the rezone process in October. He feels that he has
addressed all the concerns that have been raised.

Joan Haven, representing the Mt. Olympus Community Council, said the Council is in
favor of the rezone subject to the condition that it is subdivided into two lots. She said the
lot is large and out of character from the surrounding smaller lots in the neighborhood.
She said approving the rezone without the development agreement is not ok.

Mayor Silvestrini asked if there was any public comment on the application; there was
none. He said he attended the Mt. Olympus Community meeting when this was
discussed, and residents were concerned about the size of houses in the area. He said that
subdividing the property will help minimize the size of what can be built on the property.
The Mayor thanked Mr. Renza on working with the community.

Council Member Jackson moved to approve item 2.2, Ordinance 18-23 and Ordinance 18-
24. Council Member Uipi seconded. Mayor Silvestrini called for the vote. The motion
passed unanimously.
