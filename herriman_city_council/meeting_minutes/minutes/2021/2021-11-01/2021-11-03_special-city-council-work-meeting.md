                                     SPECIAL CITY COUNCIL
                                    WORK MEETING MINUTES
                                     Wednesday, November 3, 2021
                                     Approved December 8, 2021

The following are the minutes of the City Council Meeting of the Herriman City Council. The meeting was held on
Wednesday, November 3, 2021, at 5:00 p.m. in the Herriman City Hall Ft. Herriman Conference Room, 5355
West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the
City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

Presiding: Mayor David Watts

Councilmembers Present: Jared Henderson (via phone), Sherrie Ohrn, and Steve Shields.

Staff Present: City Manager Nathan Cherpeski, Assistant City Manager Wendy Thomas, Assistant City Manager
Tami Moody, City Recorder Jackie Nostrom, Finance Director Alan Rae, City Attorney Chase Andrizzi,
Communications Manager Jonathan LaFollette, City Planner Michael Maloy, Planning Manager Clint Spencer, and
Community Development Director Blake Thomas.

Councilmembers Excused: Clint Smith.

5:00 PM:
1.     Call to Order
Mayor David Watts called the meeting to order at 5:10 p.m. and welcomed those in attendance. He excused
Councilmember Clint Smith from the meeting.

2.      Administrative Reports
         2.1. Discussion relating to the draft General Plan – Michael Maloy, City Planner
City Planner Michael Maloy thanked the Council for the opportunity to present and said he wanted to get feedback
on the new draft General Plan, and explained he would go over each chapter of the plan briefly. He said they would
also overview the future land use plan map and elaborated the Planning Commission had recommended a few changes
and now looked to the Council for their input. He said the last thing needed was to establish their timeline. He
November 3, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                                          Page 2 of 9
requested direction from the Council about each chapter, to gain a better understanding of the concerns shared by
the Council. He then introduced Consultant Susie Pethoram to continue the presentation.

Consultant Susie Pethoram said chapter one was overall just an introduction and asked if there were any questions or
concerns. There were none. Consultant Pethoram said chapter two covered where Herriman was currently, and where
it was projected to go. She said it framed the General Plan as a document, and asked if there were any questions about
the chapter. Councilmember Steve Shields spoke about item 2.2, which discussed the vested development rights and
anticipated population of Olympia Hills. Consultant Pethoram said this would incorporate the information from the
2020 census and agreed the actual population of Herriman was slightly higher than what had been reported in the
most recent census.

Councilmember Steve Shields asked what the projections were for buildout, and City Planner Michael Maloy relayed
his belief it was around 120,000 to 150,000. Consultant Pethoram said overall, household size was increasing, which
reflected some young adults were moving back home.

Consultant Pethoram gave an overview of the community vision of chapter three, and said it gave a sense of what the
City’s priorities were. She asked if there were questions or concerns. Councilmember Steve Shields asked about a
passage in the chapter which referred to the “heart of Herriman.” Councilmember Steve Shields recited from the
text: “growing wisely will ensure the heart of Herriman – the Towne Center – will continue to evolve and become the
community’s housing, employment, social, and civic center. By directing the majority of growth into areas of existing
and planned infrastructure and services the City can preserve open spaces of natural and scenic value while also
providing housing diversity, enhanced economic development, social and cultural amenities, and civic services.” He
asked Consultant Pethoram to explain the meaning of the passage, and she replied the original plan for Herriman was
to have several community and neighborhood centers, and she spoke about the difference between what had been
intended for Herriman with the amount of time it took for those plans to be achieved. Councilmember Steve Shields
said even if they filled the surrounding area with more housing, it would still be an overall small residential area and
would primarily be a civic center more so than a neighborhood. Consultant Pethoram said this passage could be
updated to say there would be multiple centers in the town. Councilmember Steve Shields relayed his thought the
passage was great, and he did not want to nit-pick the document too much. City Planner Michael Maloy noted
Councilmember Steve Shields had a good point, and it was not uncommon for a City to start out with one main center
and then become more dispersed over time. He agreed there should be an update to the language in the document
for additional clarity. Mayor David Watts noted it would be more accurate to say there were several commercial cores
of Herriman, and not one distinct town center.

City Planner Michael Maloy spoke about the introduction of chapter three, item 3.1. He said the document stated
the City’s core community values were open spaces, active lifestyles, the outdoors, recreation, and spectacular views.
He asked if this was a complete list, or if more could be added. Councilmember Steve Shields voiced the opinion
those were elements of the community rather than values and thought there were other great values held by the
residents of Herriman. Consultant Pethoram agreed the passage needed more wordsmithing and referred to those
things as elements which people loved about Herriman. Mayor David Watts agreed this passage should be rephrased
November 3, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                                             Page 3 of 9
and suggested to not specify “spectacular views” was a value as this could get pushback in the future if a business or
building was constructed which could disrupt an individual’s view.

Consultant Pethoram asked if there were any questions about the initiatives. Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn said the
Herriman vision used to be to “promote industry and the history of the community and preserve its agricultural and
rural roots.” She was concerned about this being stated as the vision as it was not reflected in the plans for
development outlined in the plan. Consultant Pethoram replied they were trying to balance the industrial growth with
the preservation of agriculture. Councilmember Steve Shields agreed it was important to capture elements of the
history of Herriman in the document and not give lip service to the vision. He said it should celebrate and promote
the history of the town, and while they did not need to stay agrarian, that was where the identity of the town had
come from, and the city needed to honor this perspective. Consultant Pethoram said the events had been put on in
April of which had shown Herriman was unique and learned many people had moved to the area for agriculture so
she agreed that they would link the action items to the vision statement to make specific points about how they would
act on the vision of Herriman. Mayor David Watts said while they did have agricultural roots, the City had grown a
lot since then and the demographic of Herriman had shifted. The drive to stay tightly held to an agricultural society
had waned. He said the idea needed to be revisited that Herriman was an agricultural town, and Consultant Pethoram
pointed out it was more about honoring the history of the town as an agricultural center, not preserving it as an
agricultural town. Councilmember Steve Shields pointed out while Salt Lake City was now a huge economic center,
there were still parks, art, and other things which paid homage to its roots and history. He said similarly, there were
things in Herriman, such as Dansie Historical Park, which acknowledged the roots of the town.

Consultant Pethoram went over chapter four, and said it was a policy guide. She elaborated it was a newer edition and
incorporated the priorities which had been determined in the planning stage of the document. She said there were six
priorities which had been determined: Bringing in Economic Development Opportunities, Preserving and Enhancing
Community Character, Providing Community Amenities, Providing Safe Transportation Choices, Facilitating and
expanding Employment Opportunities, and Supporting & Maintaining a Mix of Housing Types. Susie Pethoram
asked if the Council would like to further discuss any of those priorities. Councilmember Steve Shields said number
four, providing safe transportation choices, had to have an element of efficiency for transportation, and he felt
efficiency was just as important as safety. Mayor David Watts spoke about number two, preserving community
character, and said he thought the phrasing of “conventional, older Herriman” would not be received well by older
residents. He did not want the residents to feel the Council did not care about the older residents of the town and
needed to find ways to protect those residents as the town developed and changed. He spoke about the need to
balance the needs of their existing residents while still incentivizing growth and development. He noted there was a
very stark difference between the newer areas of the town and the older, agricultural areas, and it was an abrupt change
when one walked or drove through the town. He added they needed to find ways to include older parts of the town,
rather than developing without them.

Mayor David Watts asked why the document specified the want to “achieve a balance of mid-size and small size lots
in the neighborhoods,” and inquired about the omission of large lots. He said he foresaw developers using the wording
to their advantage, and suggested the wording be changed so that it was clearer for residents. Consultant Pethoram
agreed this language could be altered. Councilmember Steve Shields said there were still many people in Herriman
November 3, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                                                      Page 4 of 9
who had a desire for a small estate feel, and he thought they needed to incorporate that into their planning. Mayor
David Watts cautioned when it came to density, they needed to make it clear the desire to mix small, medium, and
large lots, so it was better to reference all the lots together to give a better sense of their intent. City Manager Nathan
Cherpeski noted they did not even use the phrase “mid-size lot” anymore since it had no clear definition and was thus
subjective. He suggested they create an infographic which would show the breakdown of lots in Herriman. There was
a brief discussion about the subjectivity of lot sizes, depending on where people were from and the average lot size
they were used to. It was determined to not leave things open to interpretation and be specific when lot sizes were
referenced.

Councilmember Steve Shields said the phrase “affordable housing” was often used as a replacement phrase for low-
income housing. He said the City needed to think about housing across the entire economic spectrum and needed to
have a full spectrum of homes available so people could live their entire lives in Herriman. Consultant Pethoram said
this was a great point and relayed the need to have larger homes available so people could upgrade when it was in
their budget, which would also open smaller homes for younger families. She relayed the desire of the residents to be
able to start, grow, and advance through the life cycle.

City Planner Michal Maloy asked if the Council would want to include a statement or comment about single family
cottage developments. He noted he had spoken with Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn about this and explained in a
limited form there may be a need for this type of housing. He said the cottages were large, and there may be an
opportunity to have those available in Herriman. Councilmember Ohrn said the cottages could be very spacious, and
since they still went on sizable lots, people still had a large front and back yard. She added the cottages could be a
good option for people who could not afford a home but needed more space than would be available in an apartment.
She thought it was okay to have smaller homes, especially if they still had a good amount of outside space. Consultant
Pethoram offered some examples which supported this view and said it would change the feel of the neighborhood
when there were small houses on larger lots.

Mayor Watts talked about community amenities. He felt the residents had a right to open space, and he wanted the
right to be expressed in a clearer way as they held this discussion. He said he came from a place where the nearest
park had not been within walking distance from his house, and he felt that was unreasonable. He wanted the Council
to focus on ensuring parks were located close to the neighborhoods and suggested parks were important.
Councilmember Shields agreed residents had a right to open space and added the need for green spaces would only
increase as they continued to develop. He opined the City needed to conserve and promote green recreation and open
space for residents. Mayor Watts indicated parks should be close to low-income housing areas as well.

                          Councilmember Jared Henderson was excused from the meeting at 6:00 p.m.

Councilmember Ohrn commented she did not go to the park when she was younger as she played in the backyard.
She said if zoned appropriately, and had restrictions on house and lot sizes, the City could ensure residents had enough
green space on their lots. She pointed out budget parameters would impact how much could be done with the parks,
and the Council should review zoning restrictions as well to make sure house sizes did not take up too much of a lot.
She added everyone in the community must pay for the parks, and so the Council needed to ensure accessibility.

Councilmember Shields stated he was enthusiastic about the idea of cottage developments. He said many of the most
desirable neighborhoods in the City had large yards and tree lined avenues, but the actual house size was small. He
thought concepts which had a nice lot size and small home, would serve Herriman well. Mayor Watts agreed with
Councilmember Sherrie Ohrn, but said his main concern was the State might tell them that they could not mandate
a certain lot size.

Councilmember Shields thought units which were built with an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) would have a bigger
application in the future, since it made housing more affordable by allowing someone to own a home and then rent
out the ADU for additional income. He said he felt the Council was more interested in creative solutions to problems
than pushing a political agenda. Mayor Watts agreed.

Consultant Pethoram said the more specific the General Plan could be, the better for both the Council and residents,
and said the detailed feedback was helpful. The City needed to have clear policies and guidelines, so application
processes were simple for residents. Councilmember Ohrn added there was little land left in Herriman, which was
not titled, which required the City to focus on how to develop the remaining land and make sure the General Plan
was condensed so it was easy for people to read.

Mayor Watts said he wanted the General Plan to emphasize safe transportation choices, and specifically have safe
walking routes to be called out in the General Plan. Councilmember Ohrn asked what role the Council had in terms
of safe walking, and Mayor Watts replied that they needed to make sure there was adequate budget for the
development, although some of it fell under the jurisdiction of the Traffic Committee. Mayor Watts stated he wanted
to plan things out, so the Fire Department had clear access to all parts of the City. He spoke about the importance of
Public Safety in Herriman, especially regarding transportation. City Planner Maloy said the City did have a separate
Public Safety Master Plan, but said staff could incorporate parts of the document into the overall General Plan.
Consultant Pethoram agreed, and thought it was okay to be repetitive when it came to key issues.

Councilmember Shields said the General Plan did not touch on elements of technology, and he thought this should
be incorporated. He thought things such as smart cities, response time, and transportation would all be impacted by
technology advancements. He noted UTA was being regularly active in upgrading their methods of transportation.
He thought technology needed to be leveraged and included in the General Plan. Mayor Watts agreed on the
importance.

Mayor Watts asked if there was anything about employment opportunities in the General Plan, and Consultant
Pethoram said the document stated they were looking for ways to expand job opportunities in Herriman. Mayor Watts
commented most of the time the Council would have to revisit the General Plan was when it came to issues of density
and development, but it was important to remember it touched on other things as well. Councilmember Shields added
if a business moved to the City, he would want both the CEO of the company and an entry level employee to be able
to live comfortably in Herriman.

Consultant Pethoram spoke about the Action Plan, and the way it was set up. She explained the team had gone
through these items in detail with the Planning Commission and asked the Council if there was a desire to include
any additional information in the Action Plan.

Mayor Watts asked clarifying questions about the map included in the General Plan draft, and Consultant Pethoram
gave an overview of the map in section six of the document. Mayor Watts made some suggestions about how the
map could be clarified and asked what was meant by “developing neighborhood plans.” Consultant Pethoram replied
it allowed them to take a closer look at specific neighborhoods, and address things which needed to be changed or
updated in a certain part of the city. She elaborated it could address simple things, such as what type of tree would be
allowed on the sidewalks of a neighborhood or could address more complex things which would preserve the
neighborhood’s character. Mayor Watts requested to include the phrase “as needed,” since the passage could imply
all neighborhoods in Herriman would have the plan, although not all neighborhoods necessarily needed one.

Mayor Watts then asked how it would keep their design standards updated. He said consistency needed to be
maintained, but still set aside time to review and keep their designs current. He suggested this review should be
conducted every other year and thought it should be clear to not have a permanent design standard and would review
them at regular intervals.

Councilmember Shields asked about a fiscal impact calculator, and Consultant Pethoram said the calculator would
help estimate how tax revenue would change based on land use decisions, particularity rezones. Councilmember
Shields said it was like how private businesses estimated their costs before decisions were rendered and thought the
calculator should be incorporated into land use changes in the future. Consultant Pethoram commented South Jordan
had used the fiscal impact calculator for the development and management of residential areas, along with the General
Plan. Councilmember Shields said he would be interested in this type of calculation because he guessed the analysis
would not be a simple “S” curve where there was one best place in the city to optimize on, but it would indicate
multiple areas where it would be profitable to build.

Mayor Watts asked Consultant Pethoram about the update to the zoning ordinance. He liked the document referring
to the “missing middle,” and confirmed the quote “medium single lots detached around .01 to .02 acres” referred to
medium size lots. Consultant Pethoram responded it was not a medium size lot necessarily, but it was the “missing”
lot size in the sense most units in Herriman were either larger or smaller than the range discussed in the passage. She
said there were not a lot of lots this size, and most lots in Herriman were a quarter acre or more. Mayor Watts asked
for the passage be clarified, to make it obvious Herriman did not consider .01 acre lots to be “medium” sized. He
suggested to show in the infographic to indicate there were not many lots of this size in Herriman. He clarified the
intent to show the aim to expand the range of lot sizes.

Councilmember Shields liked the idea to include senior communities and neighborhoods in the development plans
and asked if there was data to support the idea senior only neighborhoods were good for communities. He pondered
if there was a benefit to putting senior living communities close together and clarified he was not opposed to having
senior living communities grouped together, but just wanted to understand the rationale behind it, and understand if
there was a specific reason or if it was only personal preference. City Planner Maloy explained it had to do with the
November 3, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                                               Page 7 of 9
idea people could live their entire life cycle in Herriman, and said it was helpful to have a senior community so all
amenities could be nearby. Councilmember Shields said he wanted to see decisions and policies driven by data, not
personal preference.

Mayor Watts said the item about establishing the transfer of development rights gave him pause. He said in the past,
developers had claimed density on both sides of the argument where they had “double dipped” on density. He thought
this needed to have a policy in place to address the concern but felt the transfer of rights made him nervous. He
thought if they allowed developers to transfer those rights to a third party, the Council would lose jurisdiction over
the development. He understood if it was allowed, it would be easier for the developer to sell the project, but he was
still concerned the Council would lose the ability to influence new developments in Herriman. City Planner Maloy
agreed on the need to have additional conversations. Mayor Watts said additional research and discussions needed to
be conducted before the establishment of the transfer of development rights. He suggested this should be considered
after the legislative session. Councilmember Ohrn expressed concern about the issue as well. Mayor Watts said it
could easily end up in a situation where it would cause everyone to go through decade-old development agreements
and clarify what the intention of a previous Council had been. He thought the transfer of development rights was a
good thing idea but needed to have a larger conversation prior to establishment.

Councilmember Shields commented on the need to understand if the transfer of development rights impacts the value
of the city by changing the population and overall density. City Planner Maloy thought this was a good point to
consider. Mayor Watts said criteria should be established to define the goal for overall density, and thought these
conversations needed to happen with greater context. Councilmember Shields said it needed to get specific about
what kind of businesses they wanted to bring into Herriman, and what the tax impact of those businesses would be.
He suggested those goals should be updated.

Councilmember Shields commented it would have been helpful to have had these tools ten or twenty years ago. He
stated the numbers and percentages of single- and multi-family homes had been known for buildout. Mayor Watts
recalled when the Council had determined all unzoned land should be commercial, and noted this decision made
people upset as there was not a lot of data to support the decision at the time. He said there is more information
available now which could be used in an advantageous manner to help the City become more sustainable.

Consultant Pethoram mentioned the conservation design ordinance and zoning overlay, and recalled it being
previously proposed. Councilmember Shields asked where it would apply. Consultant Pethoram responded it could
be applied to almost any new subdivision. If it had an overlay and development rights, it could be developed as open
space having unique landmark features open to people outside of the development. Councilmember Shields clarified
it was like what had been considered prior to recreation zones. Mayor Watts iterated the FR zone had been updated
because it did not allow for housing to be clustered. City Planner Maloy said the FR zone was another tool to allow
the City to receive and maintain open space without having to purchase land. Mayor Watts acknowledged areas which
would be desirable for public use at the discretion of Council. He did not want the developers dictate as to what parts
of the City would be set aside for open space. Consultant Pethoram said the zoning overlay would help them keep
that local control.

Consultant Pethoram continued to discuss ADUs and asked for the feedback. Mayor Watts wanted to allow new
development to include ADUs to show the desire of the City to allow the use. He liked the idea of incorporating an
ADU to new developments, since it could add things like separate driveways and additional parking spaces to
accommodate.

Mayor Watts said it would take several years to really address all the action items. Councilmember Shields commented
some of the action items had been brought to their attention needed to be discussed in much greater detail before
moving forward with a plan. Councilmember Ohrn said in Youth Council interviews, the applicants were asked what
changes the desired, the most common answer was to create more spaces in the City where teenagers could
congregate. She liked the General Plan had made a point to include more parks and open space for the residents.

City Planner Maloy stated it needed to collaborate with the Council to determine a policy about what to do with the
right-of-way inventory. Mayor Watts noted the map had been made without any reference to the curb and gutter, and
needed to be fixed.

Councilmember Ohrn thought the connection to the trust should be a priority action item, and she thought it needed
to be aggressive about ensuring there was clear access to trailheads. Consultant Pethoram commented the action items
were intended to serve the needs of the existing neighborhoods in Herriman and were geared toward continued
maintenance and development.

City Planner Maloy said it had good walkability in residential areas, but when a commercial site came through, the
sidewalk connection plan became very convoluted, and needed to have pedestrian routes in commercial areas. Mayor
Watts asked if City Planner Maloy could help wordsmith how the language would sound. Councilmember Ohrn
requested to also incorporate conservation language in the document as well, as the gateways served no purpose. She
thought it should be updated and redesigned to reflect new water conservation standards.

Mayor Watts asked if there were any parks in Herriman which had a lot of trees and walking paths. The Rosecrest
trail system was mentioned. The Council discussed the desire to not want to pay for irrigation to be installed but could
do things to avoid wasting water on turf. Councilmember Ohrn recalled some residents had asked if they could plant
trees in the parks. Mayor Watts suggested a City-wide tree walk, where residents could pay for a tree which would be
donated to the City and get a small plaque or marker at the base of the tree.

Consultant Pethoram felt they had gone through the chapters in sufficient detail and summarized the Council’s general
direction about future land use. City Planner Maloy explained that they had identified several areas of the draft the
Council might direct staff to change prior to the public hearing and explained there were areas where the City’s
development partners had different ideas than what had been suggested by the Planning Commission. He added a
web version of the document had been given to the consultant.

Consultant Pethoram showed the neighborhood commercial node, and explained it was the lower intensity of the
retail, where a Costco had been intended to be constructed. City Planner Maloy explained the request was to change
the front to a more traditional commercial strip, and the back would be a medium density residential area. He said
November 3, 2021 – Special Herriman City Council Minutes                                           Page 9 of 9
they were not sure what the depth should be and asked the Council for direction. Councilmember Shields thought
the land use calculator would be helpful in this situation. Mayor Watts felt opposed to the proposed changes, and
Councilmember Shields agreed as his inclination was to say no, although he acknowledged he did not have enough
data to make an informed decision. Councilmember Ohrn agreed and wanted to see more information before turning
the development over. She voiced concerns about long term planning and said while some developments might sell
well right now and be easy to develop, they might not have the longevity. City Planner Maloy indicated commercial
could be tough, and the deeper into the site and the further away one got, the harder it became to lease.
Councilmember Shields asked if there was a way to preserve frontage and suggested to make the development in an
“L” shape and add more access roads so all the parts abutting the road could be commercial. Mayor Watts replied the
parcel was cut in half, and commercial developments followed parcel lines. Councilmember Shields indicated the
Council should not make any decisions pre-emptively as they did not have sufficient data.

City Planner Maloy referenced a certain area which had a separate owner and was supposed to be commercial, and
explained it was a technical correction. He said the rest of the site was contaminated, and there had been discussions
about moving the contaminated soil to the auto mall and making the area residential. He said the developers wanted
to get commercial in the area, but he recalled the residents had been unhappy when the land had been changed to
commercial, and they might prefer to make it residential again. Councilmember Shields mentioned a conceptual layout
for the area had been created, and he really liked the concept depicted therein. However, he added he was unsure if
more commercial space was the right thing for the City.

3.       Adjournment
Councilmember Shields moved to adjourn the Herriman City Council work meeting at 7:31 p.m. Councilmember Ohrn seconded the
motion, and all present voted aye.
