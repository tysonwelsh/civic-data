2.2 Legal Update- Ryan Wood
Mr. Wood distributed a memo on the definitions of adjacent and contiguous and how they should
be applied in City Code in relation to the Ivory Homes Parkview Cottages application. In city code,
the term “contiguous” is consistent with the definition found in state code. The term “adjacent”
however, is problematic. City code uses the term “adjacent” differently depending on the context.
He stated that it seems appropriate to apply the more specific definition found in Chapter 39, and
it is consistent with the way Section 17.040(L) has been applied in every other PUD in the City. It
is also consistent with the purpose of this section of the code which is to provide a transition
between a PUD and adjacent/contiguous residential development, while still preserving the
flexibility allowed in a PUD. Mr. Wood’s recommendation is that the city enter into a development
agreement with the applicant for clarification of how to apply the code. This also ensures fairness
to the applicant who was directed to submit an application consistent with this historical
interpretation. There was also discussion of how a developers agreement would solidify some other
requests from the city if the applicant is willing to enter into the agreement. Mr. Struthers will
follow up with the applicant. Mr. Struthers noted that they will amend the development code
definitions for clarity.

2.3 Tiny Homes Discussion
Mr. Struthers stated that Sierra Pierson conducted research on tiny homes and how the code in
various cities is applied to accommodate them. Mr. Struthers stated that the philosophy of tiny
homes is the ability to have a more simplistic lifestyle. Most tiny homes are defined by 400 square
feet or less. Some can be placed permanently, and others are on wheels and can easily be
transported. Ms. Pierson stated that Eagle Mountain, and most other cities in Utah, allow for tiny
homes, but only as an accessory dwelling unit. Some cities have communities of tiny homes where
they share a common area outside of the home. She found regulations that required the
homeowners to connect to utilities and others are more like a campground with utility hookups.
Mr. Struthers stated that there are many things to consider if they want to pursue this. Councilor
Hancock stated that he would like to wait for now and see how other communities react to and
apply some of the regulations. He stated that it’s good to stay engaged and apprised of the needs
of the community.

2.4 Discussion of General Plan Land Use Goals and Policies.
Mr. Struthers stated that this is a follow up discussion from the Joint Work Session on January
14th. He stated that the goals and strategies are what we are trying to achieve as a City and it gives
staff direction on how to proceed with planning and development. Some of the goals and policies
included the following: Need to be aware on low densities in environmentally sensitive areas;
minimize hard surfaces; actively pursuing conservation easements so that city property can always
remain open space; make Lehi a place where people want to live; develop strong communities
with a variety of housing types, transportation, environment, parks and open space, infrastructure
and public safety; create some really good mixed use developments; focus on economic
development; include a historic downtown district and build on that heritage. Mr. Struthers stated
that he would like the Council to provide input and feedback before these are adopted as part of
the General Plan update.

5. Mayor and Council Reports
Mayor Johnson asked Chris Bleak to attend and give a report on the current legislative session.
He stated that there was concerns with transportation funding due to the new tax reform bill that
was recently repealed.

Chris Bleak reported that he tracks the bills that may affect Lehi, especially with transportation.
He is committed to help find funding for Lehi’s transportation. Mr. Bleak noted that there is
concern about where the road funding will now come from. He stated that with the repeal of the
tax reform bill, there is no longer funding for transit and other transportation. It’s also less likely
that the State will bond for road projects this year. He also noted that the new I-15 interchange is
on the funding list, so he will be lobbying to have that project accelerated.

Mayor Johnson encouraged the Council to sign up as a team for the Environmental Sustainability
Committee challenge for cleaner air.
