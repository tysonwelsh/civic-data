I.     Welcome – Mayor Dahle.
Mayor Dahle called the meeting to order at approximately 6:00 p.m. He read into the record the
Order on Public Meetings of the City Council of the City of Holladay.

II.     UFA Update – Neff’s Canyon Fire – Captain Dan Brown.
Capt. Dan Brown and Patrick Costin provided an update on the Neff’s Canyon Fire. The fire has
been burning since September 22 and has burned 60 acres with 42% containment. Approximately
200 firefighters were currently assigned to the fire. Smoke was expected to be visible for another
four to five weeks. Council Member Fotheringham asked if a determination of cause had been made.
Mr. Costin stated that the fire was caused by a lightning strike.

III.   Public Comments.
There were no public comments.

IV.    Consideration of Ordinance 2020-13 Amending the City Zoning Ordinance to Create a New
       Residential Infill (RI) Zone.
Council Member Gibbons moved to table Ordinance 2020-13 Amending the City Zoning Ordinance
to Create a New Residential Infill (RI) Zone. Council Member Fotheringham seconded the motion.

There was discussion regarding the meaning of tabling a motion and that it may give staff the ability
to bring the matter back to the Council at a later date. A timeline for the tabling of the motion was
discussed and it was agreed that if the Ordinance did not pass by that date, it would die.

Council Member Durham amended the motion to table a vote for a period of up to nine months, after
which, in the event Ordinance 2020-13 is not approved, would terminate. The Council roll call vote
was as follows: Council Members Gibbons, Fotheringham, Petersen, Quinn, Durham and Mayor
Dahle in favor. Ordinance 2020-13 was tabled unanimously.

Manager Chamness recommended the appointment of Martin Banks to the Council, he will serve in

Durham and Mayor Dahle in favor with Council Member Fotheringham opposed. Resolution 2020-
28 was approved by a 5-to-1 vote.

VII. City Manager Report – Manager Chamness.
Ms. Chamness updated the Council on Clover Lane and stated that it will be added to the Pavement
Management Plan, however, the paving will likely not occur until June. Cumberland will also be
paved next spring.

Ms. Chamness reported on the study being conducted on the Holladay Canal Trail. Staff was in the
process of working with its consultant to conduct six open houses and was seeking public input for
the area along the City-owned canals. Public input ranged from positive feedback to concerns about
crime, parking, and privacy.

Ms. Chamness reported on her attendance at the Utah League of Cities and Towns (“ULCT”) and
stated that the League will be conducting a public engagement survey on community policing
beginning in October.

VIII. Council Reports and District Issues.
Council Member Gibbons commented on the recent passing of two City of Holladay residents who
made contributions to the City. He also mentioned that a couple residents contacted him regarding
voting questions. City Recorder, Stephanie Carlson, reported on voting locations within the City,
which were to be posted.

Council Member Quinn appreciated the update on the Neff’s Canyon Fire and identified concerns
with construction on 3900 East and challenges with traffic. Ms. Chamness would continue to work
with staff on traffic alleviation. Council Member Quinn also asked for an update on Wander Lane.
Ms. Chamness reported that the work was still underway.

Council Member Petersen reported that she received several comments from parents thanking the
City for its efforts in acquiring better lighting along 2300 East.

consent of the Council.

IX.    Recess City Council to a Work Meeting.
       a.      Discussion on Proposed Storm Water Ordinance.
Mayor Dahle reminded Council that the objective of this meeting was to ensure that the Council is
comfortable with the Storm Water Ordinance moving forward. City Attorney, Todd Godfrey,
addressed the current edits to the Ordinance. He reviewed the edits and mentioned that references to
“storm sewer” or “stormwater sewer” were removed and replaced with “stormwater system”.

City Engineer, Jared Bunch, provided a background on the Storm Water Ordinance and Equivalent
Residential Units (“ERUs”). There was discussion regarding various aspects of the proposed Storm
Water Ordinance language.

Mayor Dahle reported that Mr. Godfrey will finalize wordsmithing edits, the Facebook Live Event
would be forthcoming, and the public hearing period would open on October 15.

       b.     Discussion on Proposed Holladay Crossroads Zone.
Mayor Dahle advised the Council that he did not want to engage any substantive discussion on the
Holladay Crossroads Zone until the public hearing had occurred. Ms. Chamness reviewed the
proposed timeline for the project with Council.

       c.      Discussion on Proposed Sidewalk Policy.
Ms. Smith provided an overview of the proposed Sidewalk Policy and stated that recent reviews are
intended to address liability issues and updates to conform with current case law. The proposed
Sidewalk Policy addresses three categories including repair and maintenance, new development, and
sidewalk gaps and fill-ins. Mr. Godfrey provided additional background. Discussion regarding
determining sidewalk ownership ensued.

Ms. Smith polled the Council on various topics to develop policy direction. The Council supported
the City of Holladay in continuing to provide snow and ice removal. It was proposed that the City
invest in a Grant Fund to assist property owners with sidewalk repairs. Staff and the Council engaged
in discussion of various policy aspects including sidewalk assessments, use restrictions, corridor use,
curb and gutter options, and the use of fees for alternative facilities.

Staff was directed to draft language based on the discussion and return to the Council within the next
couple of months.

       d.       Budget Overview and Discussion Budget Amendments.
Ms. Chamness provided a brief update on the conclusion of fiscal year 2020 and provided details on
additional expenses and revenues anticipated for fiscal year 2021. She reported that Property and
Sales Tax revenues were strong while Franchise Tax revenue was trending lower. Overall revenue
was close to the budgeted projections. Expenses similarly were close to budgeted projections. She
reported that the 2020 fiscal year-end results were much stronger than anticipated.

Ms. Chamness reviewed distributions of Coronavirus Aid, Relief and Economic Security (“CARES”)
Act funds for COVID-19 received by the City. Salt Lake County will be disbursing an additional
allotment of CARES Act funds to the City of Holladay in the same amount as the first CARES Act
fund disbursement of $900,000. Guidance on fund utilization was updated to include utilizing funds
for first responders. More information would be forthcoming on how CARES Act fund distributions
to the City will impact the General Fund.

It was reported that the City of Holladay may also qualify for additional federal funding from the
Federal Emergency Management Agency (“FEMA”) related to recent storm clean-up efforts.

Lastly, Ms. Chamness asked for direction on a budget amendment to address bridge repair and
maintenance. Mr. gave an overview of the various funding scenarios and mentioned that staff is
seeking approval of approximately $150,000 to be added to the Capital budget in a subsequent budget
amendment. The need for the funds will depend on how revenues change with the possible federal
funding distributions previously mentioned. The Council generally approved of the budget
amendment in theory.
