GOAL Foundation 2020 Annual Report
          The City Council has requested to receive information from local foundations and nonprofit organizations that have a
contractual agreement with Ogden City. These presentations help the Council to become more familiar with the objectives and
efforts of the organizations. The foundation will describe their organizational mission and goals, successes and challenges over the
past year and any future plans for their organization.
          GOAL Foundation Executive Director/ChiefExecutive Officer Eric Bauman used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to
provide the GOAL Foundation 2020 Annual Report. He discussed the history of the GOAL Foundation, which was incorporated
as a 501(c)(3) in February of2001; it started with supporting events and building a volunteer database and advanced into an event
production entity with the production of the 2006 Ogden Marathon. He presented a calendar of upcoming 2021 events, including
the winter race circuit, Ogden marathon, youth summer camps, and the El Doce race. For summer camps, GOAL has partnered
with Arcadia Guided Outdoor Education to expand camp offerings; GOAL will also continue to offer mountain bike camps and
indoor climbing camps. In 2020, GOAL produced several world-class events, both in person and virtual. This includes the winter
race circuit, sunshine kids run, summer circuit, 20,000-mile challenge, and last chance challenge. There were also several meet
ups, including the rise and run event, run and shop local event, and yoga in the park. There were a total of 36 weeks of events
aimed at helping people to stay active throughout the COVID-19 pandemic. The year 2021 marks 20 years of the Ogden
Marathon, or "20 Years of Running"; the marathon will be virtual rather than in person, but there is already great excitement about
the event. Ogden Marathon 2021 events will take place from May 9 through 16, 2021; there are already over 2,300 registered
athletes and there will be a focus on spotlighting the amazing Ogden community and downtown businesses.
          Chair Blair thanked Mr. Bauman and the entire Foundation for their efforts to continue to provide quality programming
and events throughout the COVID-19 pandemic.

     Classification, Compensation, and Benefits study
               Human Resources Manager Judkins reported City Administration contracted with Evergreen Solutions, LLC (Evergreen)
     to complete a Classification, Compensation, and Benefits Study. The initial intent was to have a fmal report that could inform
     compensation decisions for the Fiscal Year (FY) 2021 Budget. However, in early 2020, the administration directed Evergreen to
     delay the fmal report until the economic impacts of Covid-19 could be ascertained. She introduced Dr. Linda Recio, President of
     Evergreen Solutions, and invited her to present the fmdings of the Classification, Compensation, and Benefits Study.
               Dr. Recio used the aid of a PowerPoint presentation to discuss the following:
                    •   Study Initiation and Outreach.
                    •   Assessment of Current Conditions.
                    •   Job Assessment Tool.
                    •   Market Salary Survey.
                    •   Market Benefits Survey.
                    •   Recommendations.
                    •   Implementation Options and Costs.
               Dr. Recio noted the study was initiated in August of20 19 and started with data collection regarding employees, current
     salary schedules, and benefits. The outreach summary took place from the 16th to the 18th of September 2019, which included four
     orientation sessions with approximately 400 associates and a video link provided for employees unable to attend in person. There
     were 39 focus groups conducted with about 340 employees. The assessment indicated the City has 645 employees- 601 full time
     and 44 part time- with 209 unique classification titles in three employee groups: merit service, non-merit service, and sworn
     public safety. Assessment fmdings relative to the compensation structure indicated the structure of pay plans are generally orderly
     with fairly constant range spreads and midpoint progression. Relative to salary distribution, the majority of salaries are clustered
     above the respective pay range midpoint for merit and non-merit service employees. However, for police employees, salaries are
     clustered below respective midpoints and for fire employees salaries are clustered at both ends of the respective pay ranges. A job
     assessment tool (JA T) was given to employees to allow them to provide data on roles and responsibilities of their respective
     classification. All employees were asked to complete the JAT, which produced classification scores based upon five factors:
     leadership, working conditions, complexity, decision making, and relationships. There were multiple verification steps, which
     were combined with market survey data allowing for calculation of market values and updating of City job descriptions. The JA T
     was launched on Thursday, September 26, 2019 and closed Wednesday, October 14, 2019; 589 of the City's 649 employees
     completed the JAT and 563 were reviewed by supervisors. Additionally, 33 individuals completed the Management Issues Tool
     (MIT). During this same time frame, data was collected from 19 market peers. Sixty-one employment classifications were
     benchmarked, which yielded 533 data points, or 8.7 per benchmarked classification. The results of this survey are as follows:
                    •   Overall, 5.5 percent below the market minimum, 10.3 percent.
                    •   Below the market midpoint, and 13.7 percent below the market maximum of salary ranges of benchmark
                        positions.
                    •   Below the market average range spread-with an average across all surveyed positions of 47.7 percent,
                        compared with the 36.6 percent spread found across most City pay ranges.
               Overall, the City was found to have a competitive benefits package; strengths include: high-deductible health plan,
     additional forms of compensation, and paid holidays. Weaknesses include: PPO health plan, vision insurance, and vacation leave
     policies. Evergreen makes the following recommendations based upon this data:
               Classification:
                    •    Sixty-nine (69) classification title changes;
                    •   Updates to FLSA and job descriptions.
               Compensation:
                    •   Combine Merit Service and Non-Merit Service pay plans into a General Employee Pay Plan; and
                    •   Make adjustments to Police and Fire plans.
               General:
                    •   39 open pay ranges, each with a minimum and maximum;
                    •   A minimum annual salary of$26,000 (Grade G01);
                    •   A constant range spread of 50 percent;
                    •   A constant midpoint progression of four percent.
               Police:
                    •   Four pay grades-Police Officer (PO), Police Sergeant (PS), Police Lieutenant (PL), and Police Division
                        Commander (PDC);
                    •   Nine steps for Grade PO and six for the other three grades;

                 •   An overall range spread of 50.5 percent for Grade PO and 33.8 percent for the other three grades; and
                 •   An accelerated step progression plan with higher percentage increases between earlier steps.

         Fire:
                 •   Six pay grades-Firefighter (FF), Firefighter/Paramedic (FFP), Fire Captain (FC), Deputy Fire Marshall
                     (DFM), Battalion Chief (BC), and Fire Marshall (FM);
                 •   Eight steps for Grades FF and FFP and five for the other four grades;
                 •   An overall range spread of 45.4 percent for Grades FF and FFP and 27.4 percent for the other four grades; and
                 •   An accelerated step progression plan with higher percentage increases between earlier steps.
            Implementation options include the following:
                 •   Bring to New Minim urn -Maintains current employee pay unless existing pay falls below the newly assigned
                     pay grade's minimum. When this occurs, the employee's pay is raised to the new minimum.
                 •   Bring to Closest Step- Each employee's salary is brought to the next highest/closest step in the newly
                     proposed pay plan. Pay increases will always be less than a full step increase, as the intent of this option is to
                     simply place the employees on a step in the new plan. Note: This option applies only to Police and Fire
                     employees.
                 •   Class Date Parity- Realigns an employee's salary within their recommended range based on how long they
                     have been in their current role (classification). This is done on the basis of30-year career trajectory, where an
                     employee with 15 years of classification experience would be placed at the midpoint, whereas an employee
                     with 30 or more years would be at maximum.
                 •   Hire Date Parity- This option functions similarly to the Class Date Parity option, but instead the calculation is
                     based upon the employee's overall tenure with the City.
                 •   Current Range Penetration - A calculation is performed to determine the percentage through the current
                     range an employee's salary falls. The employee's recommended salary calculation will place him/her at the
                     same percentage through the proposed range.
                 •   Current Range Penetration 2- This is a customized, hybrid option proposed only for General employees. If
                     an employee has been with the City for less than one year, the Bring to New Minimum option applies.
                     Otherwise, the Current Range Penetration option applies; full salary adjustments are made up to ten percent and
                     only half of any remaining adjustment past ten percent is made.
            Dr. Recio then presented a chart illustrating the costs associated with each of the six options, after which she noted there
are many other options, but these are the six that Evergreen felt were best suited for Ogden City's situation. Additional
recommendations include the following:
                 •   Evaluate, develop, and implement clear methods of employee pay progression;
                •    Conduct small-scale salary surveys as needed;
                •    Conduct a comprehensive classification and compensation study every five to seven years; and
                 •   Review and revise, as appropriate, existing pay practice guidelines.
            Council member Hyer then inquired as to who the City's peers were for purposes ofbenchmarking employment data. Dr.
Recio stated that peers were selected based upon geography, the size of City, and demographic/ethnicity information. The survey
was sent to 25 peer groups, with 19 of them responding. Council member Hyer stated he understands that the City is located near
Salt Lake City, but their economy is much different than Ogden's and he asked if that was taken into account. Dr. Recio answered
yes; a cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) is implemented to offset benchmark data based upon United States Department ofLabor
statistics.
           Chair Blair asked if there were any peers outside the State of Utah, to which Dr. Recio answered no; she generally opts to
only rely upon in-state peers for the purposes of benchmarking wages. Ms. Judkins added that she can provide the Council with a
list of the cities that were considered to be market peers.
           Discussion then centered on the fiscal impact of each of the implementation options that were presented; Dr. Recio stated
Evergreen offers cost estimates based upon multi-year implementation rather than a one-year implementation because projects like
this can be very expensive.
           Ms. Judkins stated that as additional questions arise the Council is welcome to reach out to her or directly to Dr. Recio to
gather further information. Dr. Recio thanked the Council for their time this evening.

        Proposed Rezone of 1752 Pacific Avenue
                  Planning Manager Montgomery explained Sam Hunter has submitted a petition to rezone three parcels at approximately
        1752 Pacific Avenue from R-1-5 to M-1 for the construction of several small manufacturing shops. The properties were included
        in the 2016 Gibson Community Plan update and were rezoned from M-1 to R-1-5 in 2019 in accordance with that plan. The
        Planning Commission has forwarded a recommendation of denial of the proposed rezone with a vote of 6-2. The Council
        reviewed the proposal at its March 9, 2021 work session. At that meeting, several Council members expressed interest in having
        an ordinance drafted to consider the rezone. In addition, there was discussion about future developments in the area and Council
        members wanted to receive additional information on that topic. Mr. Montgomery presented an aerial linage identifying the
        existing land use and development patterns in the area directly surrounding the subject property. He also identified land owned by
        the City's Redevelopment Agency (RDA) and the bike path; and drew the Council's attention to a property that has been slated for
        a large residential development in the area. He noted one of the main topics of discussion tonight should be the appropriate zoning
        designation that will create a clear boundary while also serving as transitional uses between different land uses.
                  Council member Hyer referenced single-family homes located south of the subject property and inquired as to their age.
        Mr. Montgomery stated they were likely built in the 1940's; some have been well maintained while others have not. Council
        member Hyer asked if there is the potential for those properties to be purchased and redeveloped for a different use; the
        development proposed for the area south of the subject property seems to have connectivity to Gibson A venue, but does not need
        access to Pacific Avenue. He questions whether the subject property will ever be developed for a residential use and if this zone
        change is not approved, it may remain vacant. Mr. Montgomery stated that the entire street upon which the subject property is
        located has been developed for residential use; however, the subject property was zoned for manufacturing uses in the past and it
        was never developed in that fashion. That is one of the reasons the zoning was changed to residential in 2019, but it still has not
        been developed. He stated that he feels the only way the use of the other residential properties would be converted is if current
        owners move from the home or pass away and a new owner desires to change the properties use. There was brief discussion about
        a 118 residential unit project mentioned in the March 9, 2021 work session meeting, after which Council Deputy Director Symes
        indicated he has worked with the City's legal counsel to have an ordinance drafted that would allow for the Council to approve the
        requested zone change; that ordinance would be considered at a future City Council meeting if the Council is comfortable moving
        forward. No objections from the Council were voiced. Mr. Symes indicated he will work with the petitioner to get the matter
        scheduled.
