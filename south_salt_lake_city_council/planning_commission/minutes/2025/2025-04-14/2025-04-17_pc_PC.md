1.      PUBLIC HEARING
        An Application for Preliminary Subdivision Plat to Create a Four-Lot Subdivision located
        at 2280 South State Street.
        ACTION ITEM
        Applicant: Blaser Ventures
        Address: 2280 South State Street

Senior City Planner, Spencer Cawley, presented the Preliminary Subdivision Plat for the Market Center
located at 2280 South State Street. Representatives from Blaser Ventures and McNeil Engineering
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                              3
April 17, 2025


were present and available to answer questions. Mr. Cawley stated that the application is for the parcel
located along State Street just South of Winco. It is a 5 ½-acre vacant metes and bounds parcel. The
applicant is proposing to create a four-lot subdivision for a multi-family mixed-use development. The
subject property is in the Station Subdistrict of the Downtown Zoning District. Details were provided
showing how each lot is to be subdivided and the acreage of each lot. Each of the new lots will have
individual addresses that have been reviewed by the County. No issues were identified. Currently, the
City is using some of the space to advertise and display the mural on the property. As part of the
application, the applicant provided the Preliminary Site Plan, which is conceptual and subject to
technical review. Staff highlighted each lot to provide a visualization of where they are located.

Mr. Cawley reported that the Preliminary Plat is a part of the attachment to the Staff Report. The
request has been reviewed by the Planning, Fire, and Engineering Departments as well as the County.
A few items were still outstanding and need to be resolved through the Conditions of Approval before
recordation. Part of the analysis of the Staff Report was to look specifically at how the General Plan
relates to each of the land use applications that come before the Planning Commission. Specifically,
they are looking at the land use, neighborhood, and housing goals of the General Plan. This application
will eventually lead to 478 new residential units with a minimum of 400 being affordable. That lends
itself to the first goal of land use in neighborhoods, which is to welcome new residents into the City.
Land Use and Neighborhood Goal #3 is to enhance the streetscape and design of the City. The review
of the Conditional Use Permit (“CUP”) will show how the applicant intends to improve the streetscape.
The request will impact Main Street, Central Point Place, State Street, and Haven Avenue.

Mr. Cawley explained that Housing Goal #2 is to connect the housing of various densities to appropriate
services. Lots 2, 3, and 4 will have a commercial or retail element. They are not only open to the
residents of the development but those in the surrounding area as well. The proposed Conditions of
Approval were reviewed and discussed.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the Market Center Preliminary Subdivision
Plat to create four lots located at 2280 South State Street based on the Findings of Fact, Conclusions of
Law, and Conditions of Approval in the Staff Report and enumerated in the Staff Report.

Chair Carter opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
closed.

3.     PUBLIC HEARING
       An Application for Preliminary Subdivision Plat to Amend an Existing Subdivision Plat
       located at 3535 South State Street. The Applicant is Mark Miller Subaru.
       ACTION ITEM
       Applicant: Mark Miller Subaru
       Address: 3535 South State Street

Deputy Community Development Director, Eliza Ungricht presented the Staff Report and stated that
the request was before the Commission last year when the applicants did a consolidation. They now
want to consolidate more property into the existing plat on Winslow Avenue. There were two single-
family homes with one currently under contract. The other was located further west on Winslow
Avenue and has been demolished. The applicants wish to consolidate it into their property. Views
from various vantage points on the property were presented. The intent was to expand the dealership
showroom, which is currently under construction. At the time the home was purchased, it had already
been contaminated with Meth and a lot of money would have had to be put into it. The home that was
remodeled was not contaminated and was being sold. It was to remain residential in nature.

Ms. Ungricht reviewed the zoning and stated that the property is currently zoned Residential Multiple.
The rest of the property is Commercial Corridor. The Code specifies that if the majority of a property
is in one zone, a rezone is not necessary. For that reason, there was no rezone request before the
Commission. It was noted that once a property is consolidated it can act as Commercial Corridor by
default.

The proposed Site Plan showed that the applicants have closed some curb cuts, which was a previous
requirement. The shaded portion shown was the existing property that was to be consolidated. The
request will meet the 15% landscaping requirement. That condition put in place last time specified that
any time they expand or repave the property they will have to meet the 15% requirement. The current
Site Plan includes a Condition of Approval that the requirement be met at the time of Building Permit
issuance. In addition, the applicants will be adding a sidewalk and a park strip along Winslow Avenue,
which was not required previously. The change will benefit everyone who lives on Winslow Avenue.
It was noted that there is no sidewalk on the south side of Winslow Avenue but there is on the north
side near America First Credit Union and it dead ends there.

The Preliminary Plat includes an 11½-foot dedication and the continuation of the 10-foot Public Utility
Easement (“PUE”), which is required by the Code. In terms of the General Plan considerations, the
applicants meet two. Mark Miller Subaru is a long-standing business in South Salt Lane and what is
proposed will help expand the business and enhance the health, vitality, and integrity of the
neighborhood by adding the sidewalk and park strip. Staff recommended that the Planning Commission
approve the Preliminary Subdivision Plat to consolidate two properties onto one lot at 3535 South State
Street based on the information submitted by the applicant, the General Plan considerations, the
ordinance analysis with the Findings of Facts, Conclusions of Law, and Conditions of Approval set
forth in the Staff Report.
South Salt Lake City Planning Commission Regular Meeting                                                 8

Chair Carter asked where the sidewalk terminates and how it will interface with the property to the
east. He asked if there were any plans for the City to develop the sidewalk further in so that the residents
who live on the south side will have access without having to step off the street and back onto the
sidewalk. Ms. Ungricht stated that there are currently no plans for the City to continue the sidewalk.
If it were continued, it would involve the City taking the 11½ feet from all of the residents on Winslow
Avenue. She explained that plats are required to put in that improvement. Unfortunately, when
development occurs the result is small portions of sidewalk here and there, which creates a dead end of
sidewalks throughout the City.

Commissioner Southey requested additional information on the historical context. She recalled that
there were three houses that the applicant wanted at which time a compromise was reached. The
compromise would leave the street feeling like a residential street with a business on the end rather
than a few houses added onto a monolithic block of cars. She asked what had changed. Ms. Ungricht
explained that it involves the rezone application, which is a legislative request. In this case, the City
does not have a say in what the property owner does with the home. The three houses involved a
compromise that took place 1½ years ago. Since then, there has been movement and the plans changed
slightly. Commissioner Pechmann recalled that the directive was that the applicants fix up the homes,
sell them, and maintain the residential acreage on the street.

Ms. Ungricht confirmed that the property will remain residential. She explained that the zoning map
will not change because there will not be a zone change. The map also will not change and the parcel
will include two zones.

The applicant, Josh Goldsmith, identified himself as the Chief Operating Officer for Mark Miller
Subaru. He reported that when they first bought the two homes, both were tested for Meth. The one
that is currently under contract was updated. They invested $60,000 to $70,000 in the home to make it
saleable. They were excited to see it going back into the community. The last time they were before
the Commission, they also discussed making sure that they created a buffer for the neighborhood. In
response, they installed eight-foot Trex fencing along the border. Unfortunately, both of the homes
were tested for Meth in 2023. The home that is currently under contract did not come back with any
trace levels. The home that was razed came back at .27. In the State of Utah, the legal limit to be able
to occupy a home is .1. As the applicants looked at different options for repairing the home they
discovered that there were major structural issues that would have made remediation inadequate and
would have resulted in the home needing to be torn down and rebuilt. At that point, given what had
already been spent, it did not make sense to proceed.

Commissioner Pechmann asked Mr. Goldsmith what the lot would be worth in today’s market.
Mr. Goldsmith was unsure but stated that it would be worth a significant amount as a parking lot.
Because it is the nearest property to the neighborhood, a small dog park was built on the northwest
corner of the dealership for customers and guests who bring their dogs to the store. They hoped to
transition it to create a 20’ x 40’ dog park. They were also looking for ways to give back to the
community and create a community hub. They have come to realize that there are not many Level 3
chargers in South Salt Lake. The intent was to add Level 3 chargers in the future that would be open
to the public on the property.

Commissioner Pechmann commented that residents have expressed a desire for communal space such
as a small play structure around the marketing office. Another suggestion was that the dog park be
accessible to members of the community. Mr. Goldsmith stated that it will be open 24 hours per day,
however, in the case of a playground, the liability would be too great. A dog park was considered more
realistic. Commissioner Slifka asked if a dog park would require approval from the City. Ms. Ungricht
confirmed that it would require City review and approval.

Chair Carter opened the public hearing. There were no public comments. The public hearing was
closed.

In response to a question raised by Commissioner Slifka about the dog park, Deputy City Attorney,
Brianne Brass stated that the level of review and approval will depend on the factual scenario. The
applicants have a CUP so if they expand certain areas, the issue would likely need to be addressed since
it would be an accessory use. Commissioner Slifka did not want to burden the residents with noise
issues. Ms. Brass stated that staff would have to conduct an analysis.

Commissioner Southey felt like the compromise left the balance of the block still feeling residential in
nature. What is proposed makes it evenly split. She recognized Mark Miller Subaru as an important
part of the community that brings a great deal to the City. She noted that they cannot force them to
build a house on the property.

Chair Carter stated that compromise is needed. He was pleased that they were able to put one of the
homes on the market. The home that was torn down was problematic and is no longer a nuisance.

enhance the City with the addition of a new sidewalk and park strip. It was clarified that the Code
mentions expanding a CUP; however, this does not meet that definition. To expand a CUP requires it

existing CUP. She recalled that an eight-foot fence was installed rather than a 10-foot fence. She

recommended that the Planning Commission approve CUP C25-00003 for Mark Miller Subaru with
the new conditions enumerated in the Staff Report.

property and comply with all of the conditions approved 1½ years ago. Three new conditions would
be added. She noted that the applicants have come into compliance with all of the other conditions.
No complaints had been received by staff since the CUP was put in place.
