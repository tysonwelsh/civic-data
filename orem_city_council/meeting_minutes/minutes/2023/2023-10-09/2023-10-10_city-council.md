Water Tank Project Tour: Presenter: Lane Gray, Capital Projects Manager

Those in attendance: Brandon Nelson, Tom Macdonald, Dave Spencer, Keri Rugg, Ryan
Clark, Pete Wolfley, Lane Gray, Chris Tschirki, Reed Price, Jason Bench, Brenn Bybee, Mayor
Dave Young, D. Jacob Summers, Bryce Merrill and LaNae Millett.

The water tank project is a 10 million gallon tank across from Orem Elementary. The
project was started at the end of January 2023. It took 3 months to dig the large pit and 80,000
cubic yards of landfill was removed. Currently the construction company is working on the

Fire Prevention Home Survey: Presenter: Derek Spencer, Fire Division Chief, and Marc
Sanderson, Fire Chief

Chief Sanderson introduced Division Chief Spencer. Division Chief Spencer explained
that the fire department is always looking for ways to educate the public about fire safety. There
is a virtual program that will be rolling out to the public, it's called Virtual CRR. A short video
was shown to the council. Division Chief Spencer continued that this program is non-intrusive
and the home survey can educate the community. The survey will help the fire department know
which neighborhoods they need to focus on. Homes should have smoke detectors, carbon
monoxide detectors, fire extinguishers and an emergency plan.

The plan is to send this out over social media and get the community to complete the
survey. The city council suggested using a drawing to encourage residents to participate. Chief
Sanderson assured the council that the information will be used for statistical purposes and will
not be used for violations at homes. He also spoke about the potential of the program.

Women’s & Children’s Memorial: Presenter: Becca Driggs, Jill Barrick, Sara Reed, Julie Allen,
Steve Nelson and John Baxter

Ms. Barrick gave a printed proposal to each of the City Council. She introduced the
proposal before them, a memorial for women and children on the site where two pioneer children
were reported to be buried. She had attended a symposium where they discussed the impact that
female immigrants had on the area and they told a story about two pioneer children who were
buried around 1600 North in Orem.

Ms. Driggs, in preparation for this presentation, went to the lot where these pioneer
children were buried. She found the area was not being used and was in poor condition.

A video of Julie Allen, professor of Scandinavian Studies at BYU, and Sarah Reed,
assistant professor in the History department at BYU. Ms. Allen works extensively on
Scandinavian Immigration to Utah in connection with conversion to the Church of Jesus Christ
of Latter Day Saints. She explained that Utah County was a hotbed of Scandinavian
Immigration. She finds this project important because it brings to mind those settlers and their
sacrifices. This knowledge can enrich our community. Ms. Reed explained that many of the
early settlers were polygamist families and when the government made that illegal it forced
families to go into hiding. This made women very vulnerable and it was difficult for them to get
the help they needed. Ms. Allen expressed an interest in honoring their memory.

Ms. Driggs spoke about Eva Charlotta Andersson. Eva was born in Sweden on March
31, 1852. She was baptized into the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints in March 1881.
She immigrated to Utah June 1884 and married William Bjork August 1885. She died at the age
of 49 on December 20, 1901. Two sons of William Bjork were buried in Orem. Eva was the
mother and not mentioned in the article.

Ms. Driggs continued to explain that from 1860 to 1880s about half of Utah’s population
were in polygamous households. Congress passed various laws prohibiting polyamy an unlawful
cohabitation. The federal government enforced these laws with raids and thousands of arrests.

This resulted in families being separated and many had to go into hiding. More than 40% of
mothers in the 19th century Utah lost one or more children.

The Gravesite is located at 411 W 1600 N in Orem. The property is vacant and owned by
the City of Orem. This group would like to make this site a memorial to the children and other
pioneer women and children. They would like the City to give the property a historical
designation. A BYU team of undergraduate and graduate geology and archaeology students are
willing to perform LIDAR/Radar testing of the site. They believe that this site is a good place to
put a Women’s and Children Memorial. Many women today deal with the stigma and shame of
losing a child. This memorial is not just for the women who lost children but for the women who
will lose children in the future.

Ms. Barrick spoke about the value of the project. First there are two pioneer children that
were buried on this site. There is a team of professional BYU geologists who have offered their
services. The property is owned by Orem. This is a singular and rare opportunity to do
something special with this property.

Mr. Summers anticipates that the council would approve a resolution to express their
intent to have a memorial garden put in. This would allow the staff to move forward. Mr.
Peterson would like to see the resolution expanded to other areas in Orem that could have a
memorial.

Short Term Rental and Nuisance Code: Presenter: D. Jacob Summers, Deputy City Attorney

Mr. Summers is following up with a discussion from August 2023 about Short Term
rentals. The Utah State Legislature has not passed any prohibitions on municipalities enacting
ordinance to regulate the Short-Term Rental industry. The City of Orem does not have any city
code specific to Short Term Rentals but we do have zoning ordinances that can be enforced for
violations outside of “Single Family” authorized uses.

Mr. Summers continued to explain the current efforts of the City of Orem. City staff are
responsive to resident complaints. City does not use short term rental websites to look for short
term rentals, rather the city responds to resident complaints. These complaints go against the
owners, not the renters.

Also other Utah counties were presented. Mr. Summers spoke about regulations that other cities
have put into place.

Mr. Summers asked the question where do we go from here. The Council will need to

Resident complaints would be the process of discovering Short-Term Rentals, using the online

Administrative Judge.

In God We Trust Ordinance: Presenter: D. Jacob Summers, Deputy City Attorney

Mr. Summers is proposing a resolution in support of displaying the United States
National Motto in the City Council Chambers. Mr. Summers went over the history of the
National motto “In God We Trust.” The Star Spangled Banner in the fourth verse which reads
“And this be our motto - In God is our trust”. In 1864 the motto was put on coins. On July 30,
1956 Congress passed a joint resolution declaring INGOD WE TRUST the national motto.

The motto has been displayed in many government offices. The motto was placed in
schools and on license plates. The motto has been settled legally that it is acceptable to post it in
government buildings. The purpose of the motto being displayed is it solemnizes public
occasions, expresses confidence in the future and it fosters patriotism. Mr. Summers said it
makes sense to adopt this and display it in our new council chambers.

Mr. Spencer wondered if we should put it on the back or our city flag. Mr. Bybee and
Mayor Young said that discussions have been that the motto should also be incorporated into our
medallion and in the Freedom Plaza.

Mr. Summers said the first step is to pass the resolution, then the city could work on a
mock up of ideas on how to display it.

Resolution to Adopt the updated City of Orem Transportation Master Plan and amending

the Orem City General Plan to include the updated City of Orem Transportation Master
Plan in Appendix F, Presenter: Sam Kelly, City Engineer

Mr. Kelly has given each council member a copy of the resolution and the resolution
reflects the changes requested by the city council. Mr. Peterson has asked what is different from
the planning commission recommendation. Mr. Kelly went over those changes. He explained
that they modified some language to item 1.1 which are comments towards future BRT and Light
Rail improved transit. Other comments that were stricken were those pertaining to the actively
lobbying efforts to secure funding for the transit system. The long range plan for Front Runner
was removed since Orem is not currently part of the plan.

Mayor Young wants the connectivity on State Street to be strongly encouraged. Mr.
Clark explained that using the term ‘may’ means we can ask them to do it and the term 'must'
means they will have to do it. Mr. McGinn suggested that instead of using must or may it could
say should and end with when feasible.

Mr. Macdonald affirmed that the TMP could be adjusted in future plans. Ms. Millett
reminds the group that these are done every 5 years and adjustments can always be made.

Mr. Kelly spoke about 800 West and the plan is to leave it the plan for the area of State
Street and 400 North. This would be studied before any action is taken. Part two of the
resolution speaks to the ongoing discussion of updating the bicycle and pedestrian master plan.
Mr. Kelly has submitted for some MAG funding to go forward with connectivity of UVU and
Orem as well as Orem Center Street to Geneva Road. Section 5 is to pursue funding for
connectivity and beautification on State Street. Ms. Millett highlighted that we are interested in
coordinating with UTA to provide an adequate and improved bus system for our residents. Mr.
Peterson is concerned about widening roads and the effects it has on neighborhoods. He is also
concerned about the Stronger Together PIC and that many past mayors and council members are
supporting certain candidates for change. Mr. Macdonald does not agree with Mr. Peterson’s

political statement.

Mr. Peterson moved to adopt by resolution the updated City of Orem Transportation
Master Plan and amending the Orem City General Plan to include the updated City of Orem
Transportation Master Plan in Appendix F. Ms. Millett seconded the motion. Those voting yes:
David Young, David Spencer, Terry Peterson, Tom Macdonald, and LaNae Millett. The motion
passed.
