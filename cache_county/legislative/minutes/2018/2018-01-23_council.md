OV New Hires — The new Human Resources Director, Amy Adams, will begin employment
February 5, 2018.

Daniel Pete began work yesterday and was introduced as the Events Center
Coordinator. Pete has lived in Cache County for five years and said he is looking

e Resolution No. 2018-02 — Hyrum Southeast Annexation — Chris Harrild observed that
staff had originally recommended a protest; however, Hyrum City has signed an
agreement worked out with the County for maintenance on the roads impacted by the
annexation. Harrild informed the Council it is now their decision whether to accept the
annexation with unincorporated islands and/or peninsulas.

Council member Merrill asked if the county had an annexation process, would the state
override it. Harrild replied he doesn’t believe so; the state code is vague concerning
annexations. The county presently lacks an actual process in the code of what needs to
come before the Council and what does not. This will be addressed in the proposed
code coming to the Council shortly.

The Council expressed concerns over future road maintenance and the “scattered”
properties involved in the annexation.

(Attachment 4)

ACTION: Motion by Vice Chairman Ward to waive the rules and approve Resolution No.
2018-02-Hyrum Southeast Annexation. Tidwell seconded the motion. The motion
passed, 5 aye - Merrill, Tidwell, Ward, Worthen & Zilles and 1 nay — Erickson. White
absent.

Schedule — Director Nelson explained this refers to the rates associated with the parcel

records online accessible format his department has been developing. The Assessor,
Recorder and Treasurer were all involved in the project which will facilitate realtors’ and
title companies’ as well as individual citizens’ information searches while still maintaining
confidentiality. Nelson went over projected costs and benefits to the county and invited
the Council to a “sneak preview” of CORE on January 30, 2018 at 9:30 a.m. in the
Council Chambers.

(Attachment 5)

ACTION: Motion by Council member Zilles to waive the rules and approve Resolution
No. 2018-03-Adopting Cache Online Records Express (CORE) Rate Schedule. Worthen
seconded the motion. The vote was unanimous, 6-0. White absent.

2. Parcel 08-091-0014 (5.0 ac.) was divided from 08-091-0003 (5.0 ac.) without Land Use Authority

C. Cache Countywide Comprehensive Plan

7. The Cache Countywide Comprehensive Plan (“Plan”) was adopted in January 1998 with the
primary purpose of the plan is to recommend orderly future patterns of land use (Plan, pg.1).
The Plan recognizes that industrial development is a major portion of the economy of the
County but states industrial development should be limited to existing zones where possible
and should not be located in areas where they would create land use conflicts (Plan, pg. 31).
Further, the Plan identifies two impacts of industrial development - impacts on adjacent
property and the transportation system. Impacts on adjacent property include noise, smell,
light, and other and it is because of these impacts that general plans try to resolve by
segregating non-compatible land uses. The Plan does recognize that most of the industrial
development has occurred in existing municipalities and along the principal highway system
(US Highways 89/91) (Plan, pg. 82). Finally, the Plan lists objectives for industrial
development that include encouraging development close to areas where people work, shop
and live and encourage infill development instead of leap frog development (Plan, pg. 116).

D. Access—16.04.040 [A], 16.04.080 [E], Road Manual

a. Private Road: No commercial or industrial development shall be permitted.
11. A basic analysis of private road (7800 North) is as follows:
a. 7800 North is a private road that provides access to the existing single-family residence on
the subject property and adjacent properties.
b. Any industrial use on the subject property would be prohibited from using the private road.
12. A basic analysis of U.S. Highway 91 is as follows:
a. Is a state highway that provides access to the general public and is under the jurisdiction

requires UDOT approval. The applicant must meet with UDOT to determine UDOT’s
position on the industrial use proposal.

CONCLUSION

Based on the findings of fact noted herein. the Jenkins Storage Parking Rezone is hereby
recommended for denial to the County Council as follows:

1. The location of the subject property is not compatible with the purpose of the Industrial (1)

Zone as identified under §17.08.030[A] of the Cache County Code as:

a. It is located immediately adjacent to residential and agricultural properties and does not
meet the stated purpose of the Industrial (1) Zone for industrial uses that are to be located in
areas where the use can be carried on with minimum conflict or deleterious effect upon the
surrounding properties.

b. An industrial development as allowed in the Industrial (1) Zone is incompatible with the
existing adjacent residential and agricultural uses and conflicts with the stated purpose of
the Agricultural (A10) Zone to promote and protect agricultural uses and activities where
farming is a viable component of the local economy.

2. It is not consistent with the Cache Countywide Comprehensive Plan’s vision for industrial
development outside already established industrial zones as it impacts adjacent non-industrial
land uses and establishes a leap frog development pattern instead of infill development.

CACHE COUNTY
RESOLUTION 2018-04

RESOLUTION ENCOURAGING THE CACHE WATER DISTRICT
TO HIRE A FULL TIME MANAGER

WHEREAS, from April through June of 2016, Cache County and the municipalities of Cache County passed
resolutions indicating a desire to establish a water conservancy district to serve all of Cache County. Subsequently,
during the November 2016 general election, the voters of Cache County voted in support of the creation of the Cache
Water District: and

WHEREAS, the Utah Lieutenant Governor issued a Certificate of Incorporation for the District on December
22, 2017 pursuant to Utah Code Ann. § 67-1a-6.5, and the Certificate of Incorporation and other required documents.
were duly recorded in the office of the Cache County Recorder on August 14. . 2017; and

WHEREAS, as the newly formed Cache Water District has begun to operate. Cache County has provided
funding. services, and assets to the Cache Water District with the understanding that a water manager would be hired
to protect the interests of Cache County water users; and

WHEREAS; a water manager would provide consistency and understanding of the current uses and future
needs of Cache County water resources, give local credibility and authority on a state level, and would provide a
stronger voice for Cache County on water legislation issues; and

WHEREAS; a water manager could apply for grant funding to facilitate strategic planning for the future of
Cache County water: and

WHEREAS; the Cache County water master plan indicates the municipal water systems in Cache County
will have shortages in the future and a water manager can manage funds and water resources dedicated to providing
water for future generations; and

WHEREAS: the Bear River Development Act apportions the remainder of the Bear River between Cache
County, Bear River Water Conservancy District, Weber Basin Water Conservancy District, and Jordan Valley Water
Conservancy District. A water manager could attend meetings and provide input to make sure that water allocation
and water rights in Cache County are protected; and

THEREFORE, the Cache County Council finds as follows:

i, That a water manager is needed to carry out the objectives of the Cache County Water Master Plan
and the Cache Water District: and

2. The Cache Water District should hire a water manager to carry out the objectives set forth in the

recitals of this resolution;

THEREFORE, the Cache County Council passes this resolution encouraging the Cache Water District to hire
a water manager in a timely manner. This resolution takes effect immediately upon adoption.

Dated this_23rQlay of January, 2018.
