The following are the minutes of the Planning Commission Meeting of the Herriman Planning Commission.
The meeting was held on Thursday, February 4, 2021 at 6:30 p.m. in the Fort Herriman Conference Room
and the Herriman City Council Chambers, 5355 West Herriman Main Street, Herriman, Utah. Adequate notice
of this meeting, as required by law, was posted in the City Hall, on the City’s website, and delivered to members
of the Council, media, and interested citizens.

and Joy Kaseke

Chair Berbert called the meeting to order at 6:32 p.m.


Assistant City Planner Maloy stated City Council approved the five-acre Valhalla rezone with Commission
recommendations to one acre with zoning condition of four lots.
City Planner Maloy reminded the Commission all three agenda items were Administrative with no public
hearing and signed plats required Commission approval. Commissioner Palmer asked if neighbors were aware
a welding business was being added to the area. City Planner Maloy replied he was not aware, but the applicant
could be asked at the regular meeting. The current operator had things in place such as thick walls and dust
capture to minimize impact on the neighborhood.


FFKR Susie Petheram presented the general plan draft update. She reviewed the feedback from the latestJoint
CC/PC meeting. The adoption process and options for communicating the draft plan, and clarification of
existing versus proposed future land use categories were still to be worked on. Agricultural lands were
incorporated into a new category. She presented options for presenting the plan to the public, such as online
soundbites or town hall meetings. The final approval process would inlcude work meeting presentations to
Council and the Commission, followed by public hearings, and finally adoption by Council. City Planner Maloy
hoped to create more than a map. Ideally, policy goals, objectives and text would impact conversations with
development partners. Chair Berbert asked when it would go public. He proposed having City Council review
the plan, followed by requests for public comment. Bike lanes and paths were important. Developers should
choose elements to include in projects. Commissioner Bradford hoped the update would be well publicized
with lots of opportunities for public feedback. She recommended Council feedback prior to public input, then
make the final draft available for public review. Commissioner Rypien recommended including information in
the newsletter. Commissioner Palmer thought meetings by district would reach the most residents and provide
the most feedback. Commissioner Jacobson recommended documenting changes, and reasons for changes, as
they were made. Chair Berbert thought specific goals should be identified to lead the General Plan in the right
direction. City Planner Maloy requested for the Commissioners to identify neighborhood insights and share
them with FFKR Susie Petheram.

The work meeting adjourned by consensus at 7:02 p.m. and will reconvene after the regular meeting.

City Planner Michael Maloy explained an adjacent property was recently approved. The unit location, parking
lot and floor plan for welding equipment were displayed. The users in the project area were a mixture of light
manufacturing and indoor recreation.

Applicant Braiden Rindlisbacher was present online. He stated the neighboring tenant had been notified and
had no issues. Equipment was placed to have the least impact on nearby businesses.

Chair Berbert asked what comes out of the vent on top of building. Efrain Garcia replied it vented the heat from
the powder coating machine. Commissioner Ferguson requested the rating of the demising wall. The applicant
responded it was a two by six steel stud demising wall fully insulated with two layers of Type X drywall and fire
caulking.

Recommendations:
   1.  Receive and agree to the recommendations from other agencies.
   2.  Applicant installs dust collection and air filtration for all processes that produce odor and dust.
   3.  Consideration of workstation placement to reduce sound migration to neighboring tenants.
   4.  No outdoor storage of material is permitted.
   5.  All work and/or fabrication must be contained within the building.
   6.  No signs are approved with this request; a separate application will be required. No signs are
       allowed on public property. All signs must be located on private property.

Commissioner Jackson Ferguson MOVED to approve item 3.1 file number C2021-003 Conditional Use Approval for a Light

City Planner Michael Maloy explained the project extended across multiple parcels and displayed the parcel map
and intent for future amenities. Proposed phase one amenities included parking stalls, lighting, pump track, stubs
for electrical car charging stations, a pavilion with picnic tables and many others. The traffic impact and parking
study were included in the packet. Open houses have been held in person and virtually. Commissioner Jacobson
asked if nearby school boards had been consulted. Landscape Architect Shegrud sent them a letter but had not
heard back. She offered to reach out to them again. Trail connections under Mountain View Corridor were
included in the plan. The lower trailhead and pump track locations were provided. Furniture, play elements and
obstacles were displayed and staff recommendations were reviewed.

Landscape Architect Shegrud introduced Matthew Winward and Joseph Wing, Consultants from G. Brown
Design, Inc. They explained the have worked with the City for 1.5 years and attended lots of public meetings.
They were hopeful the budget would allow for all the proposed amenities and they offered to work as cost
effectively as possible. The multi-use asphalt trail would be 2.1 miles long, the circuit training trail was 1.9 miles
long and the mountain bike loop was 2-miles long. Each trail use would be identified to minimize conflict.
Parking was calculated by Hales Engineering. They used traffic counts, looked at similar sites, and traffic planning
guidelines. Peak demand was outlined to be 60 vehicles per day. Eighty stalls were proposed to meet the 2030
traffic projections. Commissioner Palmer mentioned the reservoir needed more parking. Traffic study
recommendations, potential parking, traffic, and pedestrian needs were outlined. More parking could be added
with future trailheads and amenities. Overflow parking locations were discussed. Commissioners requested trail
connectivity information. Interim City Manager Wendy Thomas responded these trails connect with existing
trails and would connect with future trails, including the Welby Jacobs trail.

Commissioner Lorin Palmer moved to adjourn the meeting at 7:46 p.m. and all voted aye.

Chair Berbert recommenced the work meeting at 7:46 p.m.



Commissioners shared goals that should be part of the general plan. Chair Berbert emphasized trail connectivity
and a preference for every road to have bike paths. Commissioner Rypien expressed central gathering areas
should be standard requirements in each neighbourhood. He also thought dead ends and cul-de-sacs should
be reduced to increase connectivity between neighbourhoods. Commissioner Palmer offered the opinion that
many requirements were already in the general plan but there was a disconnect with enacting measures to create
the general plan vision. Developers should be encouraged to meet the general plan goals. Commissioners
recommended adding a minimum lot size for each zone, and the inclusion of overlay zones. Commissioner
Ferguson said the plan represented vision for the most part, except for Olympia Hills section, which was not
part of Herriman City yet. He thought the Commission should let the public know Olympia Hills had been
forced on the area. Chair Berbert recommended guiding the streets to and from Olympia hills along commercial
areas and not through neighbourhoods. City Planner Maloy mentioned the addition of a standard
recommendation to require general plan conformity. They discussed how specific the general plan should be.
City Planner Maloy replied goals and policies could be tied to specific neighbourhoods. Commissioners thought
developers should be encouraged to build wider streets with more trees. Commissioners agreed public
engagement was very important and Councilmembers should present general plan information at district
meetings. The entire plan could be overwhelming and should be broken down into smaller topics and specific
persons should be identified to answer questions in each district. Commissioners discussed ways to connect
more with the City Council. FFKR Susie Petheram presented an overview and proposed Future Land Use
(FLU) categories. Commissioners requested the FLU category spreadsheet be emailed to them for closer
review. FFKR Susie Petheram requested for Commissioners to drop comments into the PDF file or email
comments to her. Commissioners agreed the draft should be presented to the City Council.

The Planning Commission work meeting was adjourned by consensus at 8:31p.m.
